                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution enshrines the principle that public office is a public trust,
requiring all public officials and employees to serve with accountability, integrity, and
transparency. Consistent with this mandate, the State must ensure that public funds
and resources are managed, disbursed, and accounted for prudently. Central to this
responsibility is the Commission on Audit (COA), the country's supreme audit
institution, which is constitutionally mandated to examine, audit, and settle all
accounts pertaining to government revenues, expenditures, and the use of public
funds and property.

       To operationalize this constitutional mandate, Presidential Decree No. 1445,
otherwise known as the Government Auditing Code of the Philippines, was enacted in
1978. While it has served as the principal legal framework for government auditing
for decades, rapid advances in technology, digital governance, public financial
management, and international auditing standards have rendered several of its
provisions outdated and inadequate to address the evolving demands of public
accountability.

       Recognizing the need for reform, President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr., in his 2023
State of the Nation Address, urged Congress to enact a new Government Auditing
Code "to make auditing more attuned to these changing times." In response, this bill
seeks to enact the Revised Government Auditing Code of the Philippines.

       The proposed measure modernizes the country's government auditing
framework by recognizing electronic records and digital government transactions as
valid subjects of audit and institutionalizing the use of electronic collections, receipts,
payments, and other digital audit trails. It likewise strengthens the Commission on
Audit's authority to require the production of records and information necessary for
the conduct of audits, reinforces compliance with audit recommendations, authorizes
the adoption of modern audit methodologies, and enhances internal control systems

to safeguard public resources against loss, waste, fraud, and abuse. Ultimately, the
measure promotes greater efficiency, transparency, accountability, and public trust in
government institutions.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 6          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State that all resources
 7   of the government shall be managed, expended, or utilized in accordance with law,
 8   rules, and regulations, and safeguarded against loss or wastage through illegal or
 9   improper disposition in order to ensure efficiency, economy, and effectiveness in the
10   operations of government. The responsibility to take care that such policy is faithfully
11   adhered to rests directly with the chief or head of the government agency concerned.

34         SEC. 4. Fundamental Principles. Financial transactions and operations of
35   any government agency shall be governed by the fundamental principles set forth
36   hereunder:
37
38         (a) No money shall be paid out of any public treasury or depository except in
39         pursuance of an appropriation law, appropriation ordinance, or other specific

42

44         purposes;

47         was created, or the funds received;

24          SEC. 5. Composition of the Commission on Audit, Qualifications, Term
25   and Salary of Members. The Commission shall be headed by a Chairperson and
26   two (2) Commissioners, who shall be natural-born citizens of the Philippines and, at
27   the time of their appointment, at least thirty-five (35) years of age, either certified
28   public accountants with at least ten (10) years of auditing experience, or members of
29   the Philippine Bar who have been engaged in the practice of law for at least ten (10)
30   years, and must not have been candidates for any elective position in the elections
31   immediately preceding their appointment. At no time shall all Members of the
32   Commission belong to the same profession.
33
34          The Chairperson and the Commissioners shall be appointed by the President of
35   the Philippines, subject to the confirmation of the Commission on Appointments, for a
36   term of seven (7) years without reappointment. Appointment to any vacancy shall be
37   only for the unexpired portion of the term of the predecessor. In no case shall any
38   member be appointed or designated in a temporary or acting capacity.
39
40         In case of vacancy pending the appointment of a Chairperson, the senior
41   member, as to appointment, shall perform the functions of the Chairperson until a
42   new one is appointed and assumes office.
43
44          No member of the Commission shall, during their tenure, hold any other office
45   or employment in the government. They shall neither be in the practice of any
46   profession or in the active management or control of any business which in any way

38          SEC. 52. Definition of Government Auditing. Government auditing is a
39   systematic, independent, and objective process that involves obtaining and evaluating
40   evidence in accordance with the prevailing International Standards for the Supreme
41   Auditing Institutions (ISSAIs) for the purpose of assessing whether the financial
42   information, transactions, programs, operations, accounts, and reports of any
43   government agency are in accordance with the applicable international accounting



                                              22
 1   framework. Government audits cover three audit streams: financial audits, compliance
 2   audits, and performance audits which are guided respectively by separate ISSAIs.
 3
 4          SEC. 53. Principles of Government Auditing. The auditor shall be guided
 5   by the general principles and the prescribed international standards related to the
 6   audit process:
 7
 8         (a) Comply with the relevant ethical principles of integrity, independence and
 9         objectivity, competence, professional behavior, and confidentiality and
10         transparency;
11
12         (b) Maintain appropriate professional behavior by applying professional
13         skepticism, professional judgment, and due care throughout the audit;
14
15         (c) Perform the audit in accordance with professional standards on quality
16         control;
17
18         (d) Possess collectively the knowledge, skills, expertise, and ability and
19         experience to exercise professional judgment necessary to successfully
20         complete the audit;
21
22         (e) Manage the risks of providing a report that is inappropriate in the
23         circumstances of the audit;
24
25         (f) Consider materiality throughout the audit process, in relation to stakeholder
26         concerns, public interest, regulatory requirements and consequences for
27         society;
28
29         (g) Prepare audit documentation that is sufficiently detailed to provide a clear
30         understanding of the work performed, evidence obtained, and conclusions
31         reached; and
32
33         (h) Establish effective communication throughout the audit process.

21   or not it is in actual custody of such accountable officer.

24   resulting from the unlawful deposit, use, or application thereof and for all losses

28   accountable officer shall be relieved from liability by reason of acting under the

31   the accountable officer notified the superior officer in writing that the expenditure is
32   illegal, irregular, excessive, extravagant, unnecessary, and unconscionable. The officer
33   directing any illegal payment or disposition of the funds or property and the
34   accountable officer who fails to serve the required notice shall be jointly and solidarily
35   liable.

31          SEC. 100. Administrative Disciplinary Action. Any unjustified failure by
32   the public officer concerned to comply with any requirement imposed in this Act shall
33   constitute neglect of duty or grave misconduct, as the case may be, and shall be a
34   ground for this Commission to file appropriate administrative disciplinary action
35   against the said public officer who, upon being found guilty thereof after hearing, shall
36   be meted out such penalty commensurate with the degree of guilt in accordance with
37   the civil service law, without prejudice to criminal prosecution whenever the evidence
38   warrants.
39
40          The auditor shall report to the concerned Director any violation of the preceding
41   paragraph for referral of the case to the 0MB, or other government disciplining
42   authorities.

 1          Sec. 101. Penal Provisions. - Any person who shall commit any of the
 2   following acts shall, upon conviction, suffer imprisonment of not less than six (6)
 3   months and one (1) day but not more than six (6) years or a fine of not less than One
 4   hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than One million two hundred
 5   thousand pesos (PI,200,000.00) or an imprisonment of six (6) years and one (1) day
 6   to twelve (12) years, or both at the discretion of the court:
 7
 8          (a) Refusing or neglecting to render or settle an account as required by law or
 9          regulation after demand by the Commission or its authorized representatives;
10
11          (b) Denying access to databases or information technology systems, or failing
12          to produce documents, records, or papers under custody within fifteen (15)
13          days after demand by the Commission or its authorized representatives in
14          connection with the conduct of audit;
15
16          (c) Destroying or concealing documents, records, or papers which are officially
17          entrusted to such person, pertaining to the collection, receipt, expenditure,
18          or disbursement of public funds and utilization or disposal of government
19          property in connection with the conduct of audit;
20
21          (d) Failing or refusing to submit documents required in audit without justifiable
22          cause shall constitute a ground for disallowance of the claim under
23          examination, assessment of additional levy or government share, or
24          withholding or withdrawal of government funding or donations through the
25          government, as well as for administrative or penal sanctions; or
26
27          (e) Committing other similar acts that impede, frustrate, or delay the efficient
28          conduct of audit. If a violation of this Act constitutes an offense or crime
29          punished under Act 31 No. 3815, otherwise known as the Revised Penal Code,
30          as amended, or other special laws which imposes a higher penalty, the latter
31          shall prevail.
32
33          If a violation of this Act constitutes an offense or crime punished under Act No.
34   3815, otherwise known as the Revised Penal Code, as amended, or other special laws
35   which imposes a higher penalty, the latter shall prevail.
