                         AN ACT
EXTENDING THE ZERO BALANCE BILLING POLICY TO ALL

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution provides: “The State shall
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them.” Article XIII, Section 11 further provides: “The State shall adopt an
integrated and comprehensive approach to health development which shall endeavor
to make essential goods, health and other social services available to all the people
at affordable cost. There shall be priority for the needs of the under-privileged, sick,
elderly, disabled, women, and children. The State shall endeavor to provide free
medical care to paupers.”1 These constitutional mandates provide the foundation for
measures that make essential healthcare financially and geographically accessible to
the Filipino people.

     At present, the Zero Balance Billing (ZBB) Policy is implemented principally
in DOH-run hospitals. However, many Filipinos—particularly those in rural,

municipal, and other government health facilities. The absence of uniform ZBB
coverage in these facilities may expose patients to out-of-pocket expenses, delay
necessary treatment, and limit their access to medicines, laboratory tests, procedures,
and other essential services. Government records showing that approximately 2.76
million patients benefited from ZBB from July 2025 to May 2026 demonstrate the
substantial demand for accessible healthcare.2 This bill therefore seeks to extend
ZBB nationwide, enabling more government facilities to provide comprehensive
covered services without additional charges, regardless of a patient’s place of
residence or the government entity operating the facility.

       The bill provides automatic ZBB coverage for PhilHealth members admitted
to designated ZBB Wards or obtaining covered services from government primary
care providers. It requires government hospitals outside the DOH system to
designate at least sixty percent (60%) of their authorized bed capacity as ZBB
Wards, without diminishing the basic or ward accommodations required under
existing law. It also establishes uniform benefits and safeguards, provides for annual
assessment of bed allocation, directs the DOH and PhilHealth—together with other
concerned agencies—to issue the implementing rules, and requires the necessary
appropriations to be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      By establishing a uniform nationwide framework, this measure will make
essential healthcare more accessible, strengthen financial protection, and ensure that
the benefits of Zero Balance Billing are not determined by a patient’s location or
choice of government facility.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Expanded Zero
Balance Billing Act.”

      Sec 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health, ensure equitable access to quality healthcare services,
and protect every Filipino from financial hardship arising from illness.
      Toward this end, the State shall extend the Zero Balance Billing Policy not
only to DOH-run hospitals but also to all other government hospitals and
government primary care providers nationwide, ensuring that no qualified patient is
required to pay for covered healthcare services.


      Sec 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:


      (a) Basic or Ward Accommodation refers to accommodation consisting of a
      bed in a shared room, regular meals, fan ventilation, and shared toilet and bath,
      as defined under Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal
      Health Care Act”;


      (b) Covered Healthcare Services refer to the entire hospital bill for medically
      necessary care rendered during confinement in basic or ward accommodation,
      including professional fees, room and board, laboratory tests, operating room
      services, and medicines available at the hospital pharmacy;


      (c) DOH-run Hospital refers to any hospital owned, controlled, managed, or
      directly operated by the DOH;


      (d) Government Hospital refers to any hospital operated and maintained,
      wholly or partly, by the National Government, any local government unit,
      government department, bureau, office, agency, instrumentality, government-
      owned or controlled corporation, or state university or college.
      (e) Government Primary Care Provider refers to any government-owned
      or operated health facility accredited or qualified to provide primary care
      services, including rural health units, city and municipal health offices, health
      centers, infirmaries, and other similar facilities; and


      (f) Zero Balance Billing Policy refers to a policy of PhilHealth and the
      Department of Health under which all PhilHealth members, regardless of
      income or membership category, are automatically entitled to covered
      healthcare services without additional charges when admitted to basic or ward
      accommodation. No further financial classification shall be required.


      Sec 4. Nationwide Coverage. – The Zero Balance Billing Policy shall apply
not only to DOH-run hospitals but also to all other government hospitals and
government primary care providers throughout the Philippines.


      It shall cover:


      (a) All PhilHealth members, regardless of income or membership
      classification, who are admitted to a designated Zero Balance Billing Ward in
      a government hospital; and


      (b) All PhilHealth members who avail themselves of covered healthcare
      services from a government primary care provider.


      Coverage shall be automatic, and no further financial assessment or
classification shall be required.
      Sec 5. Zero Balance Billing Ward. – DOH-run hospitals shall maintain the
existing application of the Zero Balance Billing Policy to their basic or ward
accommodations.


      All other government hospitals shall designate at least fifty percent (50%) of
their authorized bed capacity as Zero Balance Billing Wards. The Department of
Health and PhilHealth shall annually assess whether the percentage may be
increased based on utilization, hospital capacity, and funds provided under the
General Appropriations Act.


      Nothing in this section shall reduce the minimum basic or ward
accommodation required under Republic Act No. 11223. Beds not designated for
Zero Balance Billing may be maintained as other basic or non-basic
accommodations in accordance with existing laws.


      Sec 6. Safeguards. – The governing principles, benefits, coverage limits, and
safeguards under the Zero Balance Billing Policy in DOH-run hospitals shall apply
equally to all covered facilities.


      Sec 7. Appropriations. – The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be determined during the preparation and deliberation of the annual General
Appropriations Act and included in the appropriations of the concerned agencies.


      Sec 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health and PhilHealth, in coordination
with other concerned government agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for its effective implementation.


          Sec 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.


          Sec 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to or
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


          Sec 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
