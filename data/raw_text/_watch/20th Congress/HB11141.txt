                          AN ACT
  INCORPORATING SCAM PREVENTION EDUCATION IN THE SENIOR
   HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR,
                 AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Over the years, fraudsters and scammers have become more
dangerous and have proliferated even in the digital space, especially during
the pandemic. Various scams and economic fraud have threatened not only
wealthy businesses but also individual citizens. Those who do not have
access to education or enough information on their existing modus operandi
are vulnerable to these crimes.

       The continued expansion of digital services in the Philippines has been
accompanied by persistent cyber-enabled fraud, phishing, identity theft, and
other online scams. Enforcement activities and advisories from the
Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group, the Anti-Money
Laundering Council, the Department of Information and Communications
Technology, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, and telecommunications
providers demonstrate the continuing need for stronger prevention,
reporting, investigation, and victim-support mechanisms. Globe Telecom, for
example, reported blocking 71 million spam and phishing messages and
deactivating 5,670 confirmed spam and phishing numbers in 2021,
illustrating the scale of fraudulent communications directed at mobile users.

       Children and adolescents are likewise increasingly exposed to online
risks through smartphones, social-media platforms, online games,
messaging applications, digital-payment services, and e-learning platforms.
Their limited experience in recognizing phishing, impersonation, fraudulent
offers, and improper requests for personal information may make them
particularly vulnerable to scams and identity theft. There is therefore a need
to strengthen digital-safety education and preventive measures for school-
aged children, while promoting cooperation among schools, families,
government agencies, telecommunications providers, financial institutions,
and online platforms to protect minors and assist victims of cyber-enabled
offenses.

      While scams typically target adults, the recent increased exposure and
access of school-aged children to the internet have also made them prey to
fraudulent schemes.

       The growing threat of fraud in society not only stresses the need to
protect young people from scams. It also points to the alarming fact that
students are exposed to the dangers these fraudsters create. It is therefore
vital for schools to promote anti-scam awareness throughout society as well
as equip students with the knowledge so that they can report cases once
they encounter an incident of potential fraud or scam.

       This bill seeks the protection of students against fraud through the
integration of scam prevention education in the school curriculum of senior
high schools. It is the hope of this measure to empower the next generation
of Filipinos with the critical thinking skills to be able to protect themselves
and their families from exploitative scammers.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Scam Prevention
Education Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The Constitution declares that it is
the state’s policy to serve and protect the people, to prioritize education in
order to promote patriotism and nationalism, and to recognize the vital role
of communication and information in nation-building. Towards this end, this
Act aims to stem, if not eliminate, different types of scams by inculcating in
our youth and/or students the value of critical thinking. This will prevent
them from falling prey to scams as they immerse themselves daily in the
online world.

SECTION 3. Inclusion of Scam Education in the Senior High School
Curricula. – Scam Education shall be part of the Senior High School
curriculum in public and private schools.

The Department of Education (DepEd), in consultation with the Department
of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), Philippine National
Police (PNP), National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), and other concerned
agencies shall immediately formulate necessary steps and measures to
achieve the objectives of this Section.
SECTION 4. Appropriation. – The amount necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the appropriation of the
Department of Education (DepED), under the current General
Appropriations Act. Such amount as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations - The DepEd, in
consultation with the DICT, PNP, and NBI, among others, shall have the
joint responsibility of adopting the implementing rules of this Act within
sixty (60) days from its approval.

SECTION 6. Non-Impairment Clause. – Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as to diminish, impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or
made available to disadvantaged, marginalized, or specific sectors under
existing laws.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. - If any part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, administrative orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with or
contrary to the provisions of this Act are deemed amended, modified or
repealed accordingly.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2)
newspapers of general circulation.
