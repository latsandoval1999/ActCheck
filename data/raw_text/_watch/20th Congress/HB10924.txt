                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

                  The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed term of office for barangay officials.
           Congress therefore possesses the authority—and the corresponding responsibility—to
           establish a term that promotes democratic accountability while allowing barangay
           officials sufficient time to govern effectively.

                      Republic Act No. 12232 presently fixes the term of elected barangay and
_________NOVFFFFFFF




           Sangguniang Kabataan officials at four (4) years. This measure proposes to increase the
           term to five (5) years in order to provide greater stability, continuity, and effectiveness in
           barangay governance.

                 The barangay is the government institution closest to the people. It is the primary
           point of contact for residents seeking basic services, public assistance, dispute resolution,
           disaster response, peace-and-order intervention, and access to government programs.
           Under the Local Government Code, the barangay serves as the basic political unit and the
           principal planning and implementing unit of government policies, plans, programs,
           projects, and activities within the community.

                  Despite the breadth of these responsibilities, barangay officials are often required to
           perform their duties within a governing period significantly shortened by administrative
           transition and electoral preparation.

                  The initial year of a term is commonly devoted to organizing the new
           administration, constituting committees, preparing or updating development and
           investment plans, familiarizing officials with budgeting, accounting, procurement, and
           audit requirements, and coordinating proposed programs with municipal, city, provincial,
           and national government agencies. Projects involving infrastructure, livelihood, health,
           sanitation, disaster preparedness, peace and order, and social welfare may require several
           stages of planning, funding, procurement, implementation, and evaluation.

                  Before these programs can fully mature, officials may already be compelled to
           prepare for the next election. Political organizations must be maintained, consultations
           intensified, campaign resources assembled, and electoral challenges addressed.
           Consequently, the actual period during which barangay officials can devote their full
           attention to governance may be considerably shorter than their nominal term.

                  This frequent return to electoral activity can encourage short-term and highly visible
           projects at the expense of programs whose benefits require sustained implementation.
           Essential but less immediately visible initiatives—such as drainage maintenance, disaster-
           risk mapping, nutrition programs, community-based peace-and-order systems,
           digitization of records, financial controls, and long-term development planning—may be
           neglected because their results do not always coincide with the electoral calendar.

                 A five-year term would provide barangay and Sangguniang Kabataan officials with
           a more practical governing cycle. It would allow sufficient time for institutional
           organization and comprehensive planning, followed by sustained implementation, project
           completion, evaluation, accountability, and an orderly transition. More importantly, the

FIXING THE TERM OF OFFICE OF BARANGAY OFFICIALS AND
MEMBERS OF THE SANGGUNIANG KABATAAN TO FIVE (5) YEARS,
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 12232 OR “AN ACT SETTING THE TERM
OF OFFICE OF THE BARANGAY OFFICIALS AND MEMBERS OF THE
SANGGUNIANG KABATAAN, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”



Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Section 1 of Republic Act No. 12232 or “An Act Setting the Term of
Office of Barangay Officials and Members of the Sangguniang Kabataan, and for Other
Purposes” is hereby amended to read as follows:

                      “SECTION 1. Term of Office. – The term of office of all elected barangay
                      and Sangguniang Kabataan officials shall be [four (4)] FIVE (5) years. No
                      elective barangay official shall serve for more than [three (3)] TWO (2)
                      consecutive terms in the same position. No elective Sangguniang Kabataan
       official shall serve for more than one (1) term in the same positions.
       Voluntary renunciation of office for any length of time shall not be
       considered as an interruption in the continuity of service for the full term
       for which the elective official was elected.”

       Sec. 2. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 12232 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

       “SEC. 2. Date of Election. – The next regular barangay and Sangguniang
       Kabataan elections shall be held on the first Monday of November [2026]
       2028 and every [four (4)] FIVE (5) years thereafter.”

       Sec. 3. Section 5 of Republic Act No. 12232 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       “SEC. 5. Transitory Provision. – Incumbent elective barangay officials
       SHALL BE REGARDED AS HAVING COMPLETED ONE TERM
       IN THEIR RESPECTIVE POSITIONS, CONSIDERING THE
       EXTENSION OF THEIR TERMS BY TWO (2) YEARS.
       INCUMBENT OFFICIALS serving their third consecutive term in the
       same position shall [not be eligible] BE INELIGIBLE to run for the same
       position in the November [2026] 2028 Barangay and Sangguniang
       Kabataan Elections.”

       Sec. 4. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions of
this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act.

      Sec. 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       Sec. 6. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, issuances,
and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to and inconsistent with this
Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

        Sec. 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
