                             AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR MEDICAL PAROLE TO QUALIFIED PERSONS DEPRIVED OF
LIBERTY (PDL) AND SETTING RULES ON THE GRANT THEREOF

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 11 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State values
the dignity of every human person and guarantees full respect for human rights.”
Although imprisonment serves the legitimate purpose of punishment, deterrence, and
rehabilitation, it does not justify the unnecessary deprivation of basic rights and
inhumane conditions for persons deprived of liberty (PDLs), especially those suffering
from terminal illnesses or an incapacity that renders them incapable of managing their
own affairs.

         At present, the deplorable conditions within our prisons are undeniable.
Approximately 70% of the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP) jails are
reportedly congested.1 Congestion rate is at 500%, where 200,000 people are held in
facilities that can only accommodate 41,000.2 This leads to malnutrition and limited
access to adequate and proper healthcare among PDLs. Such reality is evidenced by
an increasing mortality rate among PDLs, 2.5 times higher than the general
population.3 More people are unnecessarily dying within our prisons.

       In light of these realities backed by statistics, this bill seeks to institutionalize
granting parole based on medical humanitarian grounds.

       A medical parole refers to the conditional release of a prisoner from a
correctional institution, provided they are suffering from a terminal illness or a
permanent or irreversible incapacity. A medical parole not only allows for more humane
treatment for medically suffering PDLs, but also rationalizes the correctional practice
by allowing the conditional release of PDLs who are no longer capable of serving their
sentences within the correctional facilities due to their medical conditions.

         Under this bill, qualified PDLs shall serve out their sentences under the care of
their families and shall be allowed to seek better medical care outside correctional
facilities. Furthermore, the bill institutionalizes the mandate of the Board of Pardons
and Parole (BPP) to grant medical parole to PDLs, providing for the standardized
guidelines and requirements for its grant. Finally, the bill specifically requires that the
grant of medical parole presupposes that the conditional release of a PDL shall not
constitute a threat to public order and safety, and clearly provides for a mechanism for
citizens and interested parties to oppose any application.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Javier Benitez,
Eric Yap, and Edvic Yap, and Senator Erwin Tulfo. This bill fully adopts this
Representation’s Senate Bill No. 1146 in the 18th Congress.

       Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Medical Parole Act
 2   of 2026.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Grant of Medical Parole. – The Board of Pardons and Parole (BPP)
 5   may, upon its own initiative or upon application as provided in this Act, grant medical
 6   parole to a Person Deprived of Liberty (PDL) who suffers from the following conditions:
 7
 8             a. Terminal illness, which is defined as having a prognosis of having a life
 9                expectancy of one (1) year or less, due to an incurable illness, disease,
10                or condition; or
11             b. Permanently and irreversibly incapacitated by a physical or medical
12                condition that is not terminal, but which prevents the PDL from having
13                sufficient understanding or capacity to competently manage his or her
14                own affairs.
15
16          SEC. 3. Medical Parole Application. – PDLs or their authorized representatives
17   under Section 4 of this Act may apply with the BPP for a medical parole. Their
18   application must include a certification under oath by a licensed government physician
19   containing the following:
20
21             a) That the licensed government physician conducted due examination of
22                the PDL;


                                               1
23               b) That based on said examination, there is a determination that the PDL
24                  suffers from a terminal illness, which is defined under this Act as having
25                  a prognosis of having a life expectancy of one (1) year or less due to an
26                  incurable illness, disease, or condition; or is permanently and irreversibly
27                  incapacitated by a physical or medical condition that is not terminal, but
28                  which prevent the PDL from having sufficient understanding or capacity
29                  to competently manage his or her own affairs;
30               c) A detailed description of the physical condition, disease, or syndrome
31                  suffered by the PDL and/or the PDL’s incapacity; and
32               d) A prognosis addressing the likelihood of the prisoner’s recovery from the
33                  condition, disease, or syndrome and the extent of any potential recovery.
34
35         Consideration for medical parole shall be in addition to any other parole for
36   which a PDL may be eligible.
37
38          SEC. 4. Authorized Representatives. – An application for medical parole may
39   be submitted by the PDL, or on their behalf by a parent, grandparent, child, or sibling.
40   The application must be in writing and addressed to the Chairman or Executive
41   Director of the BPP.
42
43          SEC. 5. Opposition to the Grant of Medical Parole. – The BPP shall regularly
44   publish, online and in conspicuous places within the city or municipality where the PDL
45   is serving sentence, any pending proceedings for the grant of medical parole. Any
46   person may object, through the filing of a verified opposition, to the grant of medical
47   parole on the following grounds:
48
49               a) The condition, disease, syndrome, or incapacity does not exist, or its
50                  severity does not qualify the PDL for medical parole;
51               b) The PDL’s release will constitute a threat to public safety; or
52               c) The PDL is likely to commit an offense while on medical parole.
53
54          The person who filed an opposition under the first ground shall pay for the cost
55   of a second medical examination and accompanying diagnostic tests. The second
56   medical examination shall be conducted by a different licensed government physician
57   than the one who issued the original medical certification.
58
59          SEC. 6. Period to Resolve Application. – The BPP shall resolve the application
60   in a summary proceeding within one (1) month from application by the PDL: Provided,
61   That, this period may be extended to two (2) months if there are oppositions.
62
63            SEC. 7. Eligibility. – A PDL is eligible for medical parole if both the following
64   apply:



                                                  2
 65             a) The BPP determines, based on the PDL’s medical condition and the
 66                written diagnosis, that the PDL does not constitute a threat to public
 67                safety and is not likely to commit an offense while on medical parole; and
 68             b) The BPP prepares a medical parole plan that ensures appropriate
 69                supervision and placement of the PDL.
 70
 71          SEC. 8. Conversion to Non-Medical Parole. – If the PDL who was granted
 72   medical parole recovers from the condition, disease, syndrome, or incapacity, the BPP
 73   may convert the medical parole to non-medical parole. BPP may impose any
 74   additional terms to ensure that the interests of society will be protected.
 75
 76         SEC. 9. Periodic Examinations. – The BPP may require as a condition of
 77   medical parole that the PDL agrees to periodic examinations and diagnoses at the
 78   PDL’s expense. The examining physician shall submit reports of each examination
 79   and diagnosis to the BPP.
 80
 81          SEC. 10. Release Credit Limit. – The BPP shall not grant medical parole sooner
 82   than one (1) year before the PDL’s parole or earned release credit date unless, based
 83   on medical information furnished to it, the BPP determines that the physical condition
 84   of the PDL is so serious that the PDL would not be reasonably expected to live for
 85   more than one (1) year.
 86
 87          SEC. 11. Additional Requirement. – Notwithstanding any other law, a PDL who
 88   has served ten (10) consecutive years or more in prison shall not be paroled until the
 89   BPP first receives a report as to the PDL’s mental condition and ability to adjust to life
 90   outside the prison from a licensed psychiatrist or psychologist of the National Center
 91   for Mental Health (NCMH).
 92
 93          SEC. 12. Non-Disqualification. – A grant or denial of medical parole does not
 94   affect a PDL’s eligibility for non-medical parole.
 95
 96           SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The BPP, in consultation with
 97   relevant governmental entities and non-profit organizations, shall promulgate rules
 98   and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days from its
 99   effectivity.
100
101           SEC. 14. Mandatory Evaluation and Reporting. – Every three (3) years, from
102   its effectivity, the BPP shall conduct a comprehensive review of the implementation of
103   this Act. A report of its findings and recommendations shall be submitted, within thirty
104   (30) days from the completion of the review, to the House Committee on Justice and
105   the Senate Committee on Justice and Human Rights.
