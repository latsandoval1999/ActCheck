                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Every Filipino child knows the sampaguita. We string it into garlands. We
give it to our mothers, to our teachers, and to the Santo Niño. We call it our
national flower. Here is something most of us were never told: the sampaguita
did not originally come from the Philippines. It was brought in from across the
sea. Meanwhile, in our own forests and mountains, there are multiple species of
jasmine that grow nowhere else in the world, and only few are aware of their
names.

      Our country is home to more than nine thousand two hundred fifty (9,250)
native plants.1 At least one out of every three is found only here, and nowhere
else on Earth. Of our one thousand (1,000) orchid species, roughly seven out of
ten grow only in these islands.2 By any honest measure, we are one of the most
flower-rich countries on the planet. And yet most are not aware of this
wonderous flora diversity. Our children grow up admiring the tulips of Holland,
the cherry blossoms of Japan, and the lavender fields of France, places far from
the realities and ecosystems of our own islands, while the waling-waling, the
tayabak, and the Rafflesia bloom quietly in our own provinces, unvisited,
unphotographed, and underappreciated.

      Recently, the Senate passed a measure declaring the waling-waling our
national orchid.3 The harder truth is that the waling-waling is vanishing from
the wild. We are naming our flowers faster than we are saving them. A title on

     AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL SYSTEM OF NATIVE FLOWER
PARKS, CREATING FOR THIS PURPOSE THE NATIONAL NATIVE FLOWER
  PARKS COUNCIL AND THE NATIVE FLOWER PARKS DEVELOPMENT
 FUND, PROMOTING THE CONSERVATION, PROPAGATION, AND PUBLIC
  APPRECIATION OF PHILIPPINE NATIVE AND ENDEMIC FLOWERING
         PLANTS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Native
Flower Parks Act”.

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful
ecology in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature, to conserve and
promote the nation’s biological diversity as part of the national patrimony, and
to foster the preservation, enrichment, and dynamic evolution of a Filipino
national flower culture.

      SECTION 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

      (a) Accredited Native Flower Park refers to an existing park, garden,
          arboretum, or similar facility, whether established and managed by a
          national government agency, an LGU, a state university or college
          (SUC), a private entity, a PO, or an NGO, which upon its own
          application is recognized by the Council as meeting the standards
          under this Act, without transfer of ownership, management, or
          jurisdiction over such facility;
     (b) National Native Flower Parks Council refers to the council created
         under Section 4 of this Act;
     (c) Fund refers to the Native Flower Parks Development Fund created
         under Section 8 of this Act;
     (d) Endemic Flower Species refers to a native flowering plant species
         whose natural distribution is restricted to the Philippines, or to a
         defined biogeographic region, island, or locality within the Philippines,
         and which occurs naturally nowhere else;
     (e) In Situ refers to the conservation of ecosystems and natural habitats
         and the maintenance and recovery of viable populations of species in
         their natural surroundings;
     (f) Ex Situ refers to the conservation of components of biological diversity
         outside their natural habitats, including propagation and maintenance
         in nurseries, botanic gardens, arboreta, seed and germplasm banks,
         and tissue culture facilities, undertaken to support and never to
         substitute for in situ conservation;
     (g) Invasive Alien Species refers to a species introduced outside its
         natural past or present distribution, whose introduction,
         establishment, or spread threatens ecosystems, habitats, or species
         with economic or environmental harm;
     (h) Native Flower Park refers to a defined area of land or water, whether
         publicly or privately owned, designated or accredited under this Act and
         developed, managed, and maintained principally for the in situ or ex
         situ conservation, propagation, display, study, and public appreciation
         of Philippine native and endemic flowering plants, together with their
         pollinators and associated habitats;
     (i) Native Flower Species refers to a flowering plant species that occurs
         naturally within the Philippines, or within a particular locality thereof,
         having arrived and established itself there without direct or indirect
         human introduction, whether or not it also occurs naturally in other
         countries;
     (j) Protected Area refers to an identified portion of land and/or water set
         aside by reason of its unique physical and biological significance,
         managed to enhance biological diversity and protected against
         destructive human exploitation, as defined under Republic Act No.
         11038; and
     (k) Threatened Species refers to a species or subspecies categorized as
         critically endangered, endangered, vulnerable, or other threatened
         species under Republic Act No. 9147 and the pertinent issuances of the
         Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), including
         the updated national list of threatened Philippine plants.

       SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Native Flower Parks Council. –
There is hereby created a National Native Flower Parks Council, hereinafter
referred to as the Council, which shall be attached to the DENR for policy and
program coordination, and which shall serve as the principal policy-making,
standard-setting, and coordinating body for the national system of Native Flower
Parks.

      (a) Composition. – The Council shall be composed of the following:
             1) The Secretary of the DENR, as Chairperson;
             2) The Secretary of the Department of Tourism (DOT), as Co-
                 Chairperson;
             3) The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology
                 (DOST), or a permanent representative;
             4) The Secretary of the Department of Agriculture (DA), or a
                 permanent representative;
             5) The Secretary of the Department of Interior and Local
                 Government (DILG), or a permanent representative;
             6) One (1) representative of the LGUs, jointly nominated by the
                 League of Provinces of the Philippines, the League of Cities of the
                 Philippines, and the League of Municipalities of the Philippines;
             7) One (1) representative of the Bangsamoro Government,
                 designated by the Chief Minister through the ministry having
                 jurisdiction over environment and natural resources, whenever
                 the Council deliberates on any matter affecting the Bangsamoro
                 Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM);
             8) Three (3) representatives from accredited NGOs, CSOs, or POs
                 with demonstrated experience in plant conservation, community-
                 based natural resource management, or community-based
                 tourism, at least one (1) of whom shall come from a PO based
                 outside the National Capital Region (NCR);
             9) Two (2) representatives from the academe or the scientific
                 community, drawn from SUCs or research institutions with
                 programs or recognized expertise in botany, plant taxonomy,
                 forestry, or horticulture; and
             10) One (1) representative of ICCs/IPs, selected by ICCs/IPs
                 through their own customary processes and consistent with
                 Republic Act No. 8371.
      (b) Government representatives shall be permanently designated and shall
          hold a rank not lower than Assistant Secretary or its equivalent. Other
          representatives shall serve a term of three (3) years, renewable once,
          and shall be selected through a transparent and publicly announced
          process prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations (IRR). At
          least forty percent (40%) of the total membership of the Council shall
          be women, consistent with Republic Act No. 9710, otherwise known as
          the “Magna Carta of Women.”

      SECTION 5. Functions of the Council. – The Council shall:

      (a) Formulate and adopt, within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act,
          a ten (10)-year National Native Flower Parks Development Plan, to be
          reviewed at least once every three (3) years, harmonized with the
          Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan, the National Tourism
          Development Plan, and the Philippine Development Plan;
      (b) Prescribe the standards, criteria, and procedures for the designation of
          Native Flower Parks and for the accreditation of existing facilities under
          Section 5 of this Act;
      (c) Designate Native Flower Parks and accredit Accredited Native Flower
          Parks, and suspend or withdraw such designation or accreditation for
          cause, after notice and hearing;
      (d) Establish and maintain a publicly accessible National Registry of Native
          Flower Parks;
      (e) Administer the Fund and prescribe the guidelines for its disbursement,
          subject to existing accounting and auditing rules;
      (f) Accredit NGOs, CSOs, POs, SUCs, and research institutions for
          purposes of representation, partnership, and technical assistance
          under this Act;
      (g) Provide technical assistance, training, and planting materials to LGUs,
          POs, and host communities undertaking native flower conservation and
          propagation;
      (h) Monitor and evaluate the performance of Native Flower Parks, and
          require the submission of annual reports from their respective
          management bodies; and
      (i) Perform such other acts as may be necessary to carry out the purposes
          of this Act.

      The Council shall not exercise any power that would diminish, supersede,
or duplicate the statutory mandates of the DENR, the DENR-BMB, any Protected
Area Management Board (PAMB), the NCIP, or any LGU. The Council shall not
own, expropriate, or assume the management of any land, park, or facility not
voluntarily placed under its designation or accreditation in accordance with this
Act.

       The DENR-BMB shall serve as the Secretariat of the Council. Personnel
shall be drawn from the existing plantilla of the DENR and its attached agencies,
and no new permanent positions shall be created by reason of this Act.

      The Council shall submit to the President and to both Houses of Congress,
within sixty (60) days from the close of each fiscal year, an annual report on its
programs, accomplishments, disbursements, and the status of each Native
Flower Park. The report shall be published on the official websites of the DENR
and the DOT and shall be available in printed form upon request.

      Members of the Council shall serve without additional compensation, but
may receive reasonable per diems, travel, and other necessary expenses, in
accordance with existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules. Non-
government members and representatives of ICCs/IPs shall be entitled to
reimbursement of transportation, accommodation, and subsistence expenses to
ensure their effective participation.
      SECTION 6. Designation and Phased Establishment of Native Flower
Parks. – Within five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act, the Council shall
cause the establishment or designation of at least one (1) Native Flower Park in
each administrative region of the country.

      (a) In determining the sequence of establishment, the Council shall give
          priority to:
             1. Regions and provinces with the highest documented richness
                 and endemism of native flowering plants;
             2. Provinces and municipalities belonging to the fourth, fifth, and
                 sixth income classes;
             3. Provinces recording the lowest tourism arrivals and receipts;
             4. Geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, and island and
                 upland communities; and
             5. Localities with existing community-based, LGU-led, or academe-
                 led native plant conservation initiatives that may be supported or
                 accredited instead of duplicated.
      (b) No Native Flower Park shall be designated unless the following have
          been secured and made publicly available:
             1. A resolution of the Sanggunian of the host LGU expressing
                 consent to the designation;
             2. FPIC obtained in accordance with Republic Act No. 8371 and the
                 rules of the NCIP, where the proposed site is within or
                 immediately adjacent to an ancestral domain or ancestral land;
             3. A site suitability, ecological, and multi-hazard assessment;
             4. A species and propagation plan certified by the DENR-BMB,
                 identifying the native and endemic species appropriate to the
                 biogeographic region and the sources of planting materials;
             5. Where required, an Environmental Compliance Certificate issued
                 pursuant to Presidential Decree No. 1586; and
             6. Not less than two (2) public consultations conducted in the host
                 community, in a language and at times and venues accessible to
                 its residents.
      (c) Any existing park, garden, arboretum, nursery, or similar facility may
          apply for accreditation under this Act. Accreditation shall be voluntary,
          shall not transfer ownership, management, or jurisdiction, and shall
          entitle the facility to technical assistance and to access the Fund on the
          same terms as a designated Native Flower Park.
      (d) The establishment, designation, and accreditation of Native Flower
          Parks within the BARMM shall be undertaken in coordination with, and
          with the concurrence of, the Bangsamoro Government, consistent with
          Republic Act No. 11054.
      SECTION 7. Support for the Council. – The agencies enumerated in this
Section shall provide sustained technical, scientific, logistical, and program
support to the Council, within their respective mandates and existing
appropriations.

      The DENR, through the BMB, shall provide scientific and regulatory
support, including species identification, permitting, threatened species
clearances, and the supply of planting materials from its nurseries.

      The DOT, through the Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone
Authority (TIEZA) and the Tourism Promotions Board, shall provide tourism
planning, infrastructure, standards, and promotional support.

      The DOST, through its relevant councils and institutes, shall provide
research, propagation technology, and tissue culture support.

      The DA, through the Bureau of Plant Industry, shall provide horticultural
and plant health support.

      The DILG shall assist in mobilizing LGUs. The DepEd and the Commission
on Higher Education shall integrate native flower education into curricula and
co-curricular programs.

       The NCIP shall ensure compliance with FPIC and the protection of the
rights of ICCs/IPs.

      The NCCA and the National Museum of the Philippines shall provide
cultural, historical, taxonomic, and documentation support.

      The Department of Public Works and Highways shall, upon request,
program access roads and related facilities.

       There is hereby created a Native Flower Parks Development Fund, which
shall be held in trust and administered by the Council, with the DENR acting as
depository agency, and which shall be subject to audit by the Commission on
Audit.

      SECTION 8. Native Flower Parks Development Fund. – There is hereby
created a Native Flower Parks Development Fund, which shall be held in trust
and administered by the Council, with the DENR acting as depository agency,
and which shall be subject to audit by the Commission on Audit.

      The Fund shall consist of:
      (a) initial appropriation and subsequent annual appropriations provided
          under Section 15 of this Act;
      (b) Such amounts as may be released from the share of the TIEZA in travel
          tax collections pursuant to Section 72 of Republic Act No. 9593,
          particularly the portions earmarked for the development of ecotourism
          sites in depressed provinces and for the development of historic,
          cultural, religious, and heritage sites and prime tourist destinations,
          subject to the approval of the TIEZA Board and to existing rules;
      (c) Income generated from the operation of Native Flower Parks established
          or supported through the Fund;
      (d) Grants, donations, bequests, and official development assistance from
          domestic and foreign sources, accepted in accordance with law; and
      (e) Such other sources as may be authorized by law.

      Not less than sixty percent (60%) of the total amount disbursed from the
Fund in any fiscal year shall be applied to Native Flower Parks located outside
the NCR. The Council shall report compliance with this requirement in its annual
report, and any shortfall shall be explained in writing and corrected in the
immediately succeeding fiscal year.

      Not less than fifty percent (50%) of the income generated by a Native Flower
Park shall be retained for its operation, maintenance, and host community
programs, and shall not be remitted to the national treasury, except as otherwise
required under Section 11 of this Act for parks situated within protected areas.

       No portion of the Fund shall be used for the creation of new permanent
positions in the national government, for the payment of salaries of national
government personnel, or for the purchase of motor vehicles for the use of
national officials.

      SECTION 9. Environmental Sustainability and Climate Resilience. – Every
Native Flower Park shall be planned, sited, constructed, and operated so as to
advance the conservation of Philippine native and endemic flowering plants and
to minimize harm to the ecosystems in which it is located. Only native flowering
plants shall be planted, displayed, or propagated in the collection and display
zones of a Native Flower Park. Priority shall be given to species native or endemic
to the biogeographic region in which the park is located. The Council, upon the
recommendation of the DENR-BMB, shall issue and regularly update a list of
species prohibited in Native Flower Parks.

      The introduction, propagation, or maintenance of invasive alien species
within a Native Flower Park is prohibited. Every park shall implement measures
for the early detection, containment, and eradication of invasive alien species.
No plant material shall be collected from the wild except pursuant to a permit
issued under Republic Act No. 9147 and, where applicable, the clearance of the
concerned PAMB. Priority shall be given to materials sourced from community
nurseries, accredited seed and germplasm banks, DENR nurseries, ethical
propagation programs, and plants legitimately salvaged from areas already
cleared under lawful authority.

      No Native Flower Park shall be sited in a manner that requires the
conversion of old-growth or primary forest, the reclamation or filling of wetlands,
mangroves, or seagrass beds, or the alteration of a critical habitat declared under
Republic Act No. 9147, except with the prior written clearance of the DENR-BMB
and, where applicable, the concerned PAMB.

       Park facilities shall employ climate-appropriate and, where practicable,
locally sourced and traditional materials and designs; shall be powered by solar
or other renewable energy where technically and economically feasible,
consistent with Republic Act No. 9513; shall provide for rainwater harvesting
and water conservation; shall comply with Republic Act No. 9003 and observe a
policy of avoiding single-use plastics; and shall minimize impermeable surfaces
and night lighting harmful to pollinators and nocturnal wildlife.

      Every Native Flower Park shall maintain habitat for native pollinators. The
use of pesticides and herbicides known to be harmful to pollinators shall be
avoided, and integrated pest management shall be practiced.

      Park facilities shall observe the principles of universal design and shall
comply with Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 (Accessibility Law) and its implementing
rules, so that senior citizens, persons with disabilities, pregnant women, and
children may enjoy the park with dignity and independence.

      Native Flower Park shall undergo annual ecological monitoring, the results
of which shall be published. The Council shall coordinate with the Philippine
Statistics Authority and the DENR for the inclusion of Native Flower Parks in the
ecosystem and natural capital accounts maintained under Republic Act No.
11995.

       SECTION 10. Local Economic Incentives and Culturally Sensitive
Approaches. – Every Native Flower Park shall be operated so that the greater part
of the employment, income, and opportunity it generates remains with the host
community, including local hiring, consultation with local knowledge holders,
and respect for ancestral domains.

      Concessions and enterprises within the native flower parks shall prioritize
awarding to local micro, small, and medium enterprises, cooperatives, PO-run
enterprises, and women and youth organizations of the host community.

      SECTION 11. Protected Areas. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed as
amending, repealing, modifying, diminishing, or in any manner impairing the
provisions of Republic Act No. 11038, Republic Act No. 7586, as amended, or
Republic Act No. 9147, or the powers, duties, and jurisdiction of the DENR, the
DENR-BMB, or any PAMB.

      Where a proposed or existing Native Flower Park is located within a
protected area or its buffer zone, its establishment and operation shall require
the prior approval of the concerned PAMB, shall conform to and be integrated
into the approved protected area management plan, and shall be treated as a
component thereof. The PAMB shall retain full jurisdiction and management
authority over the area, and the role of the Council shall be limited to the
provision of technical assistance, funding, standards, and promotion.

      The Council shall not designate any Native Flower Park whose
establishment would require the reclassification of a protected area, the
diminution of its boundaries, the disestablishment of any portion thereof, or the
grant of any tenurial instrument inconsistent with Republic Act No. 11038.

       Where a Native Flower Park is proposed within or adjacent to a locally
managed marine protected area or a coastal or wetland area, the Council shall
coordinate with the concerned LGU, the PAMB where applicable, and the Bureau
of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources.

      In case of any conflict or inconsistency between this Act and Republic Act
No. 11038 or Republic Act No. 9147, the latter shall prevail.

      SECTION 12. Linkages to Existing Projects, Initiatives, and Similar Parks.
– This Act shall integrate and strengthen existing efforts and shall not duplicate
or displace them. The Council shall not assume jurisdiction over, or the
management of, any park or facility already administered by the National Parks
Development Committee, an LGU, an SUC, the National Museum of the
Philippines, a religious or civic organization, or a private person. Participation in
the national system shall be by voluntary application for accreditation.

        Before authorizing the establishment of a new Native Flower Park, the
Council shall first determine, and shall state in writing, whether an existing
facility within the same region may instead be accredited, rehabilitated, or
expanded to serve the purposes of this Act. The Council shall coordinate with
the National Parks Development Committee, and shall harmonize its programs
with the Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan, the National Tourism
Development Plan, the Philippine Development Plan, the Enhanced National
Greening Program, and the relevant programs of the DepEd, the Commission on
Higher Education, and the NCCA.

      Nothing in this Act shall impair the authority of an LGU under Republic
Act No. 7160 to establish, operate, regulate, or promote its own parks, gardens,
and flower festivals, or to legislate on matters within its competence. Existing
LGU flower festivals, gardens, and related cultural events are hereby recognized,
and the Council shall, upon request, extend technical and promotional
assistance to them. The national system established under this Act shall support
and give effect to the national floral symbols of the Republic as declared by law
or by proclamation, including through their in situ and ex situ conservation,
propagation, and public display.

      The Council shall maintain open data-sharing arrangements with the
National Museum of the Philippines, the DENR-BMB, the Philippine Clearing
House Mechanism for Biodiversity, SUCs, herbaria, and recognized international
biodiversity databases, subject to the protection of sensitive locality data for
threatened species and to Republic Act No. 10173.

     SECTION 13. Public Awareness Campaign. – The Council, together with
the DENR, the DOT, the DepEd, the Commission on Higher Education, the
NCCA, the National Museum of the Philippines, and the Presidential
Communications Office through the Philippine Information Agency, shall design
and sustain a nationwide public awareness campaign on Philippine native and
endemic flowering plants.

      (a) The campaign shall cultivate agency, intentionality, ownership, and
          pride in native flower species, and shall move public tribute to
          Philippine flowers beyond symbol and slogan into experiences that
          Filipinos can see, smell, touch, and reach.
      (b) The second week of June of every year is hereby declared as Linggo ng
          mga Pilipinong Bulaklak, to be observed nationwide in coordination with
          Philippine Environment Month.
      (c) The DepEd and the Commission on Higher Education shall integrate
          instruction on Philippine native and endemic flowering plants into the
          basic and higher education curricula and into co-curricular activities,
          using materials developed with the DENR-BMB and the National
          Museum of the Philippines.
      (d) Admission to Native Flower Parks shall be free for students, teachers,
          senior citizens, persons with disabilities, solo parents, and bona fide
          residents of the host LGU. Any admission fee charged to others shall
          be reasonable and shall be set in consultation with the host LGU.
      (e) Native Flower Parks shall progressively provide Filipino Sign Language
          interpretation, Braille and tactile signage, scent and sensory gardens,
          audio guides, and accessible pathways, so that the experience of the
          park is not denied to any Filipino by reason of disability.
      (f) The Council shall conduct mobile and pop-up native flower exhibits,
          school caravans, and community propagation drives in geographically
          isolated and disadvantaged areas, island municipalities, and localities
          without a Native Flower Park.
      (g) Campaign materials shall be produced in Filipino, in English, and in
          the major regional and indigenous languages of the areas where they
          are disseminated.
      (h) The Council shall maintain a free, publicly accessible online platform
          featuring the National Registry of Native Flower Parks, the National
          Inventory of Philippine Native and Endemic Flowering Plants,
          educational materials, and virtual tours.
      (i) No part of the campaign, and no signage, structure, or material funded
          under this Act, shall bear the name, image, likeness, or initials of any
          incumbent public official, or be used for any partisan political purpose.

      SECTION 14. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. – The following acts are hereby
prohibited:

      (a) Collecting, uprooting, cutting, destroying, or removing any plant within
          a Native Flower Park without authority from its management body;
      (b) Introducing, propagating, or releasing any invasive alien species within
          a Native Flower Park;
      (c) Representing any park, garden, or facility as a Native Flower Park or an
          Accredited Native Flower Park without the corresponding designation
          or accreditation;
      (d) Using the Fund, or any property acquired through the Fund, for any
          purpose contrary to this Act.

      Any person who commits any of the foregoing acts shall, upon conviction,
suffer the penalty of a fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos
(P100,000.00) nor more than Two Hundred Fifty Thousand pesos (P250,000.00),
at the discretion of the court. Where the act involves a threatened species, or is
committed by a public officer or employee, or by a juridical person through its
responsible officers, the maximum penalty shall be imposed, without prejudice
to administrative liability and to prosecution under Republic Act No. 3019.

       Where the same act is punishable under Republic Act No. 9147, Republic
Act No. 11038, or any other law imposing a higher penalty, the higher penalty
shall be imposed.

      Nothing in this Section shall be construed to penalize the customary,
subsistence, ceremonial, or other non-commercial use of plants by members of
ICCs/IPs or of host communities in accordance with their traditions, customary
laws, or any applicable management or benefit-sharing agreement.

      SECTION 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the DENR. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act, including the annual replenishment of the
Fund, shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act under the
budget of the DENR, as a separate line item identified as the Native Flower Parks
Development Fund.
       SECTION 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 18. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved,
