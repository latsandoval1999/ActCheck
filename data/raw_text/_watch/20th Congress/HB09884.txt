                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Under the Government Service Insurance Act, entitlement to a lifetime
retirement pension from the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS)
requires at least fifteen years of service, and the duty to contribute ends
upon separation from the service.1 This requirement does not fit the
structure of elective office. Local elective officials at the provincial,
municipal, and barangay levels may serve only three consecutive terms, a
total of nine years, before a mandatory interruption. These officials form the
backbone of local governance, often serving their communities across
multiple offices and multiple levels of government without ever
accumulating the uninterrupted service that the current law rewards.
       Completing the fifteen year requirement converts an official who
would otherwise receive only a refund into the holder of a lifetime pension.
To ensure that this imposes no cost on the public and no strain on the fund,
the bill requires that the voluntary contribution be set at the amount
actuarially determined by the GSIS to fully fund the additional benefit,
borne entirely by the official, with no government share charged to any
public appropriation. This measure gives them a fair and fully self funded
opportunity to earn the security that the law already extends to those in
continuous service.
       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

   AN ACT PROVIDING FOR VOLUNTARY GSIS CONTRIBUTIONS BY
PROVINCIAL, MUNICIPAL, CITY, AND BARANGAY ELECTIVE OFFICIALS
    TO COMPLETE THE MINIMUM SERVICE REQUIREMENT FOR
RETIREMENT, AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8291, AND FOR OTHER
                          PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:​
​
​     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the Local
Elective Officials’ GSIS Voluntary Contribution Act.​
​
​      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to
reward long and faithful service in local governance with a secure retirement
and to ensure that the social insurance system responds to the realities of
local elective office. The State recognizes that local elective office at the
provincial, municipal, city, and barangay levels is exercised through fixed
and term-limited terms under the Local Government Code which interrupt
continuous service and prevent many local elective officials from completing
the minimum service required for a pension. Toward this end, and on the
strict conditions that the option be fully funded by the official and remain
neutral to the public treasury and to the actuarial solvency of the
Government Service Insurance System, the State shall allow qualified local
elective officials who have rendered at least nine years of actual elective
service to complete the minimum service requirement through voluntary
contributions.​
​
​     SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
      (a) Local elective official refers to a person who holds or has held a
      public office by virtue of election at the provincial, municipal, city, or
      barangay level under Republic Act No. 7160, the Local Government
      Code of 1991, and who, while in office, was a compulsory member of
      the GSIS receiving fixed monthly compensation. This includes
      governors, vice governors, and members of the Sangguniang
      Panlalawigan; mayors, vice mayors, and members of the Sangguniang
      Panlungsod or Sangguniang Bayan; and punong barangays and
     members of the Sangguniang Barangay. This Act does not extend to
     members of the House of Representatives, Senators, or any other
     national elective official.
     ​
     (b) Qualifying elective service refers to actual service rendered as a
     local elective official for which premium contributions were paid to
     and received by the GSIS, amounting to at least nine (9) years or three
     (3) full consecutive terms under Republic Act No. 7160, whether
     served in the same office or in different local elective offices at the
     provincial, municipal, city, or barangay level, and whether served
     consecutively or with interruptions, provided that each period is
     supported by a verified service record.​
     ​
     (c) Full term refers to a complete term of office as fixed by Republic
     Act No. 7160, actually served from assumption to expiration, and does
     not include any portion of a term served by succession or
     appointment.
     (d) Voluntary contribution refers to the contribution paid under this Act
     by a local elective official who is no longer in active service, or who
     continues to serve beyond the mandatory retirement age under
     Section 13(b) of Republic Act No. 8291, as amended.​
     (e) Periods with paid premiums refers to the periods for which
     premium contributions were actually paid to and received by the
     GSIS, used in computing the amount of benefits.
     (f) Incremental benefit refers to the additional benefit that becomes
     payable by reason of the completion of the fifteen (15)-year
     requirement through voluntary contributions, over and above the
     separation benefit the member would otherwise have received.

       SECTION 6. Voluntary Contributions at Actuarially Determined
Full Cost. – A local elective official who avails of the option under Section
13(b) of Republic Act No. 8291, as amended by this Act, shall pay a
voluntary contribution in the amount actuarially determined by the GSIS to
fully fund the incremental benefit. Such amount shall in no case be less
than the sum of the personal share and the government share prescribed
under Republic Act No. 8291 and its implementing rules. No part of the
voluntary contribution shall be charged against the appropriations of the
national government, any province, municipality, city, or barangay, or any
agency or instrumentality thereof, and the government share obligation
shall not apply to any period of voluntary contribution. It is the intent of this
Section that the option impose no cost on the public treasury and remain
neutral to the fund.​
​
​     SECTION 7. Prospective Application. – Voluntary contributions
under this Act shall apply to current and future periods only. No period
during which contributions were not actually paid may be bought back,
reinstated, or retroactively credited, and this Act shall not be construed to
authorize the purchase of past service credit.
       SECTION 8. Computation of Benefits on Periods With Paid
Premiums. – The benefits of a local elective official who completes the fifteen
(15)-year requirement through voluntary contributions shall be computed
strictly on the basis of the periods with paid premiums, including the
periods voluntarily paid under this Act, and no benefit shall be granted for
any period without paid premiums. The GSIS shall ensure, through the
contribution level set under Section 6 of this Act, that the present value of
the voluntary contributions is at least equal to the present value of the
incremental benefit.
      SECTION 9. Effect of Prior Receipt of a Separation Benefit. – A
local elective official who has previously received a separation benefit for
prior service may have that prior service credited toward the fifteen (15)-year
requirement only upon refund to the GSIS of the separation benefit received,
with interest at the rate prescribed by the GSIS. In the absence of such
refund, only the periods with paid premiums that have not been settled by a
separation benefit shall be credited.
      SECTION 10. Periodic Review and Authority of the GSIS. – The
Board of Trustees of the GSIS, upon the recommendation of its Actuary,
shall prescribe the contribution base, ceilings, schedules, and conditions for
the option under this Act, shall review the contribution level at least every
three (3) years, and may adjust or suspend the option when necessary to
preserve the actuarial solvency of the fund. The exercise of this authority
shall not impair any contribution already paid or any benefit already
accrued. ​
​
​      SECTION 11. Integrity of Service Records. – Any benefit obtained
under this Act through misrepresentation or material non-disclosure of the
service record shall be void, and the GSIS shall recover any amount paid,
without prejudice to administrative, civil, or criminal liability under existing
laws. ​
      ​
​     SECTION 12. Segregation of Records and Audit. – The GSIS shall
record and report voluntary contributions under this Act separately from
compulsory contributions, and shall maintain the controls necessary to
ensure that no government share is charged to public funds for such
periods. The records shall be subject to audit by the Commission on Audit.
       SECTION 13. Coordination with the Civil Service Commission and
the Department of the Interior and Local Government. – The GSIS shall
coordinate with the Civil Service Commission and the Department of the
Interior and Local Government to ensure that the periods of qualifying
elective service credited under this Act are consistent with the documented
record of local elective service, verified against official records of the
Commission on Elections and the relevant provincial, municipal, city, or
barangay government units.​
​
​     SECTION 14. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to diminish the retirement and separation benefits
already enjoyed under the laws existing at the time of retirement.
​
​      SECTION 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the GSIS, in coordination with the
Civil Service Commission, the Department of Budget and Management, and
the Department of the Interior and Local Government, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary for the proper implementation of this Act,
with particular attention to the verification of qualifying elective service
records at the provincial, municipal, city, and barangay levels.
      SECTION 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this
Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.
      SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof that are
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.
       SECTION 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
