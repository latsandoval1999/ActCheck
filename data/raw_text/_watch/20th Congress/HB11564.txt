HOUSE BILL No. 11564

Introduced by Representative EDGAR R. ERICE

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Lupus is a serious and lifelong illness that affects many Filipinos, yet it remains poorly
understood by the general public. Its symptoms can differ from one person to another and may
resemble those of other illnesses. As a result, some patients spend considerable time and
resources seeking medical attention before their condition is properly identified.

For an ordinary Filipino family, this delay has real consequences. Repeated consultations,
laboratory tests, medicines, and continuing treatment can become a significant financial
burden. More importantly, when the disease is not detected and properly managed early, the
patient's condition may worsen and affect his or her ability to study, work, earn a living, and
care for a family.

Government intervention should therefore focus on a simple objective: help Filipinos recognize
lupus earlier and make proper care more accessible and affordable.

This bill seeks to establish a comprehensive national program for lupus under the Department
of Health. It provides for greater public awareness, early detection and proper referral, training
of health workers, improved access to appropriate treatment and medicines, patient and family
support, and the collection of reliable information necessary for government planning and
research.

The measure also recognizes that detecting an illness is only the beginning. A person diagnosed
with lupus may require continuing care and regular monitoring. Thus, the bill directs the

Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) to provide appropriate coverage for
lupus screening and early detection and to develop and periodically review an appropriate
benefit package for medically necessary diagnostic procedures, laboratory monitoring,
treatment, and other health services for persons living with lupus.

Access to proper care should likewise not depend on where a Filipino lives or how much a
family earns. The bill therefore strengthens the referral system among primary care facilities,
government hospitals, specialists, and other health facilities, while giving particular attention
to indigent and financially incapacitated patients and those living in underserved communities.

No Filipino suffering from lupus should have to endure years of uncertainty before receiving
proper care, nor should a family be left to face the financial and emotional burden of the disease
on its own. Through greater awareness, earlier detection, accessible treatment, and continuing
support, government can help persons living with lupus lead healthier and more productive
lives.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

HOUSE BILL No. 11564

AN ACT
INSTITUTING A COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL HEALTH PROGRAM FOR THE
PREVENTION, EARLY DETECTION, DIAGNOSIS, TREATMENT, AND
MANAGEMENT OF SYSTEMIC LUPUS ERYTHEMATOSUS (SLE), PROVIDING
SUPPORT FOR PERSONS LIVING WITH LUPUS AND THEIR FAMILIES, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SEC. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive Lupus Prevention
Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.

Towards this end, the State shall adopt an integrated, comprehensive, and patient-centered
approach to the prevention, early detection, diagnosis, treatment, and management of Systemic
Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), hereinafter referred to as lupus.

The State shall promote public awareness of lupus, strengthen the capacity of health care
providers for its early recognition and proper management, improve access to appropriate
diagnostic and treatment services, encourage scientific research, establish reliable health

SEC. 3. National Program for Lupus Prevention, Early Detection, Treatment, and
Management. The Department of Health (DOH) shall establish and implement a
comprehensive National Program for Lupus Prevention, Early Detection, Treatment, and
Management, which shall form part of and be integrated into the country's Universal Health
Care system.

The Program shall include, but shall not be limited to, the following:

(a) Nationwide information, education, and awareness campaigns on lupus, its signs and
symptoms, risk factors, possible complications, and the importance of timely medical
consultation;

(b) Programs for the early recognition, screening, and appropriate diagnosis of lupus;

(c) Development and implementation of evidence-based clinical practice guidelines and
appropriate standards of care for lupus;

(d) Establishment and strengthening of referral pathways among primary care providers,
hospitals, specialists, and other health facilities capable of diagnosing and managing lupus:

(e) Access to appropriate treatment, medicines, laboratory monitoring, rehabilitation, and other
medically necessary services;

(f) Continuing education and capacity-building programs for physicians, nurses, barangay
health workers, and other health care providers involved in the recognition, referral, treatment,
and management of lupus;

(g) Development and strengthening of support networks and counseling programs for persons
living with lupus and their families;

(h) Promotion and support of scientific and clinical research relating to lupus, including its
prevalence, risk factors, diagnosis, treatment, and long-term outcomes among Filipinos;

(i) Establishment and maintenance of an information and monitoring system on lupus; and
(j) Such other programs and activities as may be necessary to achieve the objectives of this Act.

In implementing the Program, the DOH shall coordinate with local government units,
government hospitals, health care provider networks, medical professional organizations,
academic and research institutions, patient organizations, and other relevant public and private
institutions.

SEC. 4. Early Detection and Referral. The DOH shall develop appropriate protocols for the
early recognition and referral of persons who exhibit signs and symptoms consistent with lupus.

Primary care facilities and health care provider networks shall, as far as practicable. be
equipped with appropriate information and referral protocols to facilitate timely evaluation and
diagnosis.

Persons suspected of having lupus shall be referred, whenever medically necessary. to
appropriate specialists or health facilities with the capability to conduct confirmatory
diagnostic procedures and provide appropriate treatment.

Particular attention shall be given to ensuring access to early detection and referral services for
indigent and financially incapacitated patients and persons residing in geographically isolated
and disadvantaged areas and other underserved communities.

SEC. 5. Center for Lupus and Autoimmune Diseases. The DOH shall designate or establish,
within an appropriate DOH hospital or medical center, a Center for Lupus and Autoimmune
Diseases, which shall serve as the national technical, training, research, and referral center for
lupus and other related autoimmune diseases.

The Center shall:

(a) Provide specialized diagnostic, treatment, and referral services;

(b) Develop and disseminate evidence-based clinical guidelines and standards of care;

(c) Provide technical assistance and training to health care professionals and health facilities;
(d) Conduct, coordinate, and support research relating to lupus and autoimmune diseases;

(e) Assist in the development of a national referral network for lupus patients; and

(f) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to support the implementation of this Act.

The DOH may designate qualified regional hospitals or medical centers as regional referral
centers for lupus to improve accessibility of specialized services outside the National Capital
Region.

SEC. 6. Training of Health Care Providers. The DOH, in coordination with relevant medical
societies, professional organizations, academic institutions, and other concerned agencies, shall
develop and implement appropriate training and continuing education programs for health care
professionals and other health workers on the early recognition, diagnosis, referral, treatment,
and management of lupus.

Training programs shall particularly emphasize early recognition and appropriate referral at the
primary care level to reduce delayed diagnosis and misdiagnosis.

SEC. 7. Information and Monitoring System on Lupus. The DOH shall establish and maintain
an Information and Monitoring System on Lupus to generate reliable and relevant data
necessary for health policy, program planning, resource allocation, research, and evaluation.

The system shall, as far as practicable, contain epidemiological and other relevant information
concerning the prevalence, incidence, demographic characteristics, diagnosis, treatment,
complications, and outcomes of lupus in the Philippines.

The collection, processing, storage, sharing, and use of personal and health information under
this Section shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data

Privacy Act of 2012,” and other applicable laws and regulations on patient confidentiality and
health information.

Information used for statistical, policy, or research purposes shall, whenever appropriate. be
anonymized or de-identified.

SEC. 8. PhilHealth Coverage. The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth). in
coordination with the DOH and consistent with Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as
the “Universal Health Care Act,” shall ensure appropriate benefit coverage for lupus screening
and early detection services.

PhilHealth shall further study, develop, and periodically review an appropriate benefit package
for medically necessary diagnostic procedures, laboratory monitoring, treatment, and other
health services for persons diagnosed with lupus, subject to applicable laws, clinical guidelines,
health technology assessment when required, and actuarial sustainability.

In designing and reviewing such benefit package, PhilHealth shall take into consideration the
chronic nature of lupus and the need for continuity of care and regular monitoring.

SEC. 9. Access to Medicines and Health Services. The DOH shall endeavor to ensure the
availability and accessibility of medicines, diagnostic services, and other health interventions
necessary for the appropriate management of lupus in government health facilities, consistent
with existing laws, national formularies, clinical guidelines, and applicable procurement and
health technology assessment requirements.

Qualified indigent and financially incapacitated patients may likewise avail themselves of
existing government medical assistance programs, subject to applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

Section 10. Patient and Family Support. The DOH, in coordination with local government
units, health facilities, patient organizations, and other relevant institutions, shall encourage the
establishment and strengthening of support programs for persons living with lupus and their
families.

Such programs may include patient education, counseling, peer-support networks, information
on available health and social services, and assistance in navigating appropriate referral and

Participation in any support, treatment, research, or other program established pursuant to this
Act shall respect the dignity, privacy, autonomy, and informed consent of the patient.

SEC. 11. Research and Development. The DOH, in coordination with the Department of
Science and Technology, academic and research institutions, medical societies, and other
appropriate entities, shall promote and support research on lupus, including research on its
epidemiology, causes and risk factors, diagnosis, treatment, management, complications,
quality of life, and socioeconomic impact in the Philippine setting.

Research conducted pursuant to this Act shall comply with applicable ethical standards, data
privacy requirements, and regulations governing health research involving human participants.

SEC. 12. National Lupus Awareness Week. The first week of May of every year is hereby
declared as “National Lupus Awareness Week.”

The DOH shall lead appropriate activities during National Lupus Awareness Week to promote
public understanding of lupus, encourage early consultation and diagnosis, disseminate
information regarding available services, and combat misinformation, stigma, and
discrimination affecting persons living with lupus.

All government agencies, local government units, public health facilities, schools, and other
appropriate institutions are encouraged to support activities in observance thereof.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth and other concerned government
agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 14. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and other concerned agencies.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 15. Separability Clause. \f any provision or part of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions or parts not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and
other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended. or

SEC. 17. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
