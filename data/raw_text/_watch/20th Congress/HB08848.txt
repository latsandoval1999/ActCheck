                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIV, Section 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that the State
shall conserve, promote, and popularize the nation’s historical and cultural heritage and
resources, as well as artistic creations. It also mandates that the State recognize the role
of culture in fostering national identity and strengthening the nation’s social, political,
and economic development.

       Local government units (“LGUs”) play a vital role in advancing cultural
governance at the grassroots level. Under the Local Government Code of 1991, LGUs are
empowered to enact measures and implement programs that promote the general
welfare of their constituents, including the preservation of cultural heritage, support for
local artists and cultural workers, and the promotion of cultural tourism and creative
industries. Through local initiatives such as cultural mapping, heritage conservation,
festivals, and community-based cultural programs, LGUs serve as primary stewards of
the country’s rich and diverse cultural heritage.

       Existing national policies further reinforce the importance of cultural
development. Republic Act No. 7356 created the National Commission for Culture and
the Arts (NCCA) as the primary government agency responsible for formulating policies
and coordinating programs for the preservation, development, and promotion of
Philippine culture and the arts. Meanwhile, Republic Act No. 10066, or the National
Cultural Heritage Act of 2009, provides for the protection and conservation of cultural
property and heritage resources. More recently, the Philippine Development Plan and
related cultural policy frameworks recognize culture and creativity as key drivers of
inclusive development and economic growth.

      Despite these policy foundations, the level of institutional support for cultural
governance among LGUs remains uneven. Many local governments have demonstrated
exemplary practices in safeguarding heritage, supporting cultural workers, and
integrating culture in development planning, while others continue to face capacity and
resource constraints in advancing cultural initiatives.

       To encourage excellence in cultural governance and incentivize LGUs to
strengthen their cultural programs and institutions, this measure seeks to establish the
Seal of Good Cultural Governance (SGCG). Modeled after successful governance
recognition mechanisms such as the Seal of Good Local Governance established under
Republic Act No. 11292, the SGCG will recognize LGUs that demonstrate exemplary
performance in promoting cultural heritage, supporting cultural workers and creative
industries, and integrating culture into local governance and development.

       Through a performance-based recognition and incentive system administered by
the National Commission for Culture and the Arts, in coordination with relevant
government agencies and LGU leagues, the SGCG aims to foster a culture-responsive
governance framework nationwide. It will encourage local governments to adopt
sustainable cultural policies, strengthen local cultural institutions, and promote inclusive
cultural participation while safeguarding the diverse heritage and identities of Filipino
communities.

       Ultimately, the establishment of the Seal of Good Cultural Governance will help
strengthen cultural stewardship, empower cultural workers, and advance culture and
creativity as pillars of national development.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE SEAL OF GOOD CULTURAL GOVERNANCE FOR
    LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS, PROVIDING INCENTIVES THEREFOR,
    AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Seal of Good Cultural
Governance Act".

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Principles and State Policy. - It is hereby declared the
policy of the State to conserve, promote, and develop the nation’s cultural heritage,
cultural expressions, and creative industries, and to ensure that culture is integrated into
national and local development.

       Towards this end, the State shall encourage local government units (“LGUs”) to
institutionalize cultural governance mechanisms, support cultural workers and artists,
safeguard tangible and intangible cultural heritage, and implement programs that
strengthen cultural identity and creativity within their communities.

       The State shall establish a recognition and incentive system that promotes
excellence in cultural governance among LGUs.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

          (a) “Creative Industries” refers to trades involving persons, whether natural or
              juridical, that produce cultural, artistic, and innovative goods and services
              originating in human creativity, skill, and talent and having a potential to
              create wealth and livelihood through the generation and utilization of
              intellectual property. Creative industries include those directly or indirectly
              involved in the creation, production and manufacturing, performance,
              broadcasting, communication and exhibition, or distribution and sale of
              works and other subject matter, in accordance with existing laws, rules and
   regulations on intellectual property rights protection. Creative industries
   include the following domains, with a non-exhaustive list of industries
   belonging under each: (a) Audiovisual Media Domain; (b) Digital
   Interactive Media Domain; (c) Creative Services Domain; (d) Design
   Domain; (e) Publishing and Printed Media Domain; (f) Performing Arts
   Domain; (g) Visual Arts Domain; (h) Traditional Cultural Expressions
   Domain; (i) Cultural Sites Domain; and (j) Other domains and industries as
   the Philippine Creative Industries Development Council may determine,
   subject to guidelines and criteria it may issue as defined in Sec. 3, R.A. 11904,
   Philippine Creative Industries Development Act of 2022)

(b) “Cultural governance” refers to the systems, policies, institutions, programs,
    and mechanisms adopted by national and local government units to
    protect, promote, and develop cultural heritage, cultural expressions, and
    creative industries, and to ensure that culture is integrated into
    development planning and governance.

(c) “Cultural heritage” refers to the totality of cultural property preserved and
    developed through time and passed on to posterity, including tangible and
    intangible heritage such as historic sites and structures, cultural landscapes,
    artifacts, traditions, performing arts, rituals, languages, knowledge
    systems, and other expressions of Filipino identity consistent with the
    provisions of existing heritage laws.

(d) “Cultural workers” refer to individuals and groups engaged in the creation,
    preservation, promotion, management, research, education, and practice of
    culture and the arts, including artists, performers, artisans, cultural
    managers, curators, heritage practitioners, scholars, and other professionals
    working in cultural and creative fields.

(e) “Intangible cultural heritage” refers to the practices, representations,
    expressions, knowledge, skills, and associated instruments, objects,
    artifacts, and cultural spaces that communities, groups, and individuals
    recognize as part of their cultural heritage, including oral traditions,
    performing arts, social practices, rituals, festive events, traditional
    craftsmanship, and knowledge systems transmitted from generation to
    generation.

(f) “Local cultural development plans” refer to strategic plans adopted by local
    government units that outline policies, programs, projects, and activities
    aimed at preserving cultural heritage, supporting cultural workers,
    promoting local creative industries, and integrating culture into local
    development planning.
       SEC. 4. Seal of Good Cultural Governance. - There is hereby established a Seal of
Good Cultural Governance (“SGCG”), which shall be conferred upon local government
units that demonstrate exemplary performance in the protection, promotion, and
development of culture and the arts.

      The Seal shall recognize LGUs that have established effective cultural policies,
programs, and institutional mechanisms that support cultural development and heritage
preservation.

      SEC. 5. Coverage. - The Seal shall be open to all provinces, cities, municipalities,
and barangays that meet the standards and criteria set forth under this Act and its
implementing rules and regulations.

      SEC. 6. The Council of Good Cultural Governance. - There is hereby established a
Council of Good Cultural Governance (the “Council”), which shall oversee the
implementation, evaluation, and conferment of the Seal of Good Cultural Governance.

The Council shall be composed of the following:

          (a) The National Commission for Culture and the Arts (“NCCA”) who shall
              serve as Chairperson of the Council;
          (b) The Department of the Interior and Local Government (“DILG”);
          (c) The Department of Tourism (“DOT”);
          (d) The Department of Education (“DepEd”);
          (e) The Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (“DEPDev”);
          (f) The National Historical Commission of the Philippines (“NHCP”);
          (g) The National Museum of the Philippines (“NMP”);
          (h) The Cultural Center of the Philippines (“CCP”);
          (i) One (1) representative from the League of Provinces of the Philippines;
          (j) One (1) representative from the League of Cities of the Philippines;
          (k) One (1) representative from the League of Municipalities of the
              Philippines; and
          (l) One (1) representative from the Liga ng mga Barangay.
          (m)        Two (2) representatives from the cultural and creative sectors, to be
              appointed by the NCCA from among nationally recognized artists,
              cultural workers, or heritage experts.

      The NCCA shall serve as the Secretariat of the Council.

      SEC. 7. Powers and Functions of the Council. - The Council shall act as a policy-
making and advisory body to ensure the proper implementation of the SGCG. It shall
perform the following powers and functions:

          (a) Develop and promulgate the performance indicators that shall serve as
              standards for evaluating the LGUs' compliance and/or satisfaction of
   each of the criteria prescribed herein: Provided, That the Council shall
   designate each Council member to lead the development of performance
   indicators in their area of jurisdiction or expertise and recommend the
   approval of such performance indicators by the Council: Provided, further,
   That in the development and approval of the performance indicators, the
   Council shall take into consideration that LGUs may not be similarly
   situated and that one (1) or more indicators may be peculiarly
   inapplicable or extremely difficult to meet given the inherent limitations
   or circumstances of the LGUs;

(b) Review and/or revise the performance indicators consistent with the
    long-term development plans of the National Government. The
    development of indicators should reflect outcomes that are performance-
    based, encouraging outputs that reflect concrete benefits to the
    community in terms of policy, rules, regulations, behavior, skill
    competencies, knowledge, or attitude;

(c) Promote the integration of cultural governance in local development
    planning and governance systems;

(d) Conduct an evaluation of the impact of this Act on the performance of the
    LGUs for purposes of determining the need for enhancing or continuing
    the SGCG, rolling out the SGCG in barangays, and recommending any
    amendatory legislation. For this purpose, the Council shall institute an
    effective feedback mechanism where the concerns of LGUs and other
    stakeholders on the implementation of the SGCG are appropriately
    considered;

(e) Create technical working groups, upon the recommendation of the
    Council member concerned, composed of experts from government
    agencies, and other sectors, to assist the Council members in the
    performance of their respective functions as provided for in paragraph (a)
    of this section;

(f) Submit to the President of the Philippines, the President of the Senate, and
    the Speaker of the House of Representatives, on an annual basis, copies of
    the performance indicators approved by the Council together with the
    performance indicators developed and recommended by the lead Council
    member, and the result of the impact assessment conducted pursuant to
    paragraph (d) of this section; and

(g) Perform such other functions as are necessary or incidental to properly
    carry out the purposes of this Act.
       SEC. 8. Criteria for the Grant of the Seal. - The grant of the SGCG shall be based
on the demonstrated performance of local government units in promoting and
strengthening cultural governance within their respective jurisdictions.

      In evaluating LGUs for the SGCG, the Council shall adopt performance indicators
based on, but not limited to, the following areas:

          (a) Cultural Policy and Institutional Development, including the establishment
              of mechanisms and institutions that support cultural governance at the
              local level;
          (b) Protection and Conservation of Cultural Heritage, including initiatives that
              safeguard tangible and intangible cultural heritage;
          (c) Support for Cultural Workers and Creative Industries, including programs
              that encourage artistic production, cultural participation, and the growth of
              local creative sectors;
          (d) Cultural Programs and Community Participation, including activities that
              promote cultural awareness, cultural education, and community
              engagement in cultural initiatives;
          (e) Integration of Culture in Local Development, including the incorporation
              of cultural considerations in local development planning, tourism
              strategies, and creative economy initiatives.

        The specific indicators, evaluation parameters, and performance standards for the
SGCG grant shall be developed and periodically reviewed by the Council and prescribed
in the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

       SEC. 9. Implementing Agency. - The National Commission for Culture and the
Arts (NCCA) shall be the implementing agency of this Act. It shall, in consultation and
coordination with partner agencies or sectors, be responsible for the assessment and
evaluation of each LGU relative to its compliance with the criteria:

          (a) Develop the evaluation framework and indicators;
          (b) Conduct assessments and validation of LGU applications;
          (c) Confer the Seal to qualified LGUs; and
          (d) Provide technical assistance and capacity-building to LGUs seeking to
              improve their cultural governance systems.

      The NCCA may coordinate with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Department of Tourism (DOT), the National Historical
Commission of the Philippines (NHCP), and other relevant agencies or sectors, as it may
deem necessary, to assist in carrying out its responsibilities under this Act.

      SEC 10. Requisites to Qualify for SGCG. — An LGU which, according to the
assessment of the National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), complies with
and satisfies all assessment criteria provided in Section 8 of this Act shall be conferred
and awarded the SGCG and granted the corresponding incentives as provided under
Section 11 of this Act.

     SEC. 11. Incentives for Awardees. - LGUs awarded with the Seal of Good Cultural
Governance may be entitled to the following incentives:

   1. Priority access to cultural grants and funding programs administered by the
      NCCA and other national government agencies;

   2. Eligibility for technical assistance programs for cultural development;

   3. National recognition and citation during annual cultural governance ceremonies;
      and

   4. Additional incentives that may be provided by the national government, subject
      to existing laws and regulations.

     SEC. 12. Assessment and Conferment. — The NCCA shall conduct a periodic
assessment of LGUs for purposes of determining their eligibility for the SGCG. The
conferment of the SGCG shall be made every three (3) years through a national
recognition ceremony honoring LGUs that demonstrate exemplary cultural governance.

     SEC 13. The SGCG Incentive Fund. — There is hereby created a special account
under the General Appropriations Act (GAA) called the "SGCG Fund". The Fund shall
be utilized solely to pay out the incentives granted to LGUs which qualify for the SGCG.

     The Council established under Section 6 of this Act shall have the authority to
determine the monetary incentives for LGUs based on the number of awardees. It shall
also have the authority to re-evaluate and increase the amount of SGCG incentive to be
awarded to LGUs: Provided, That, the increase shall be subject to the sufficiency of the
Fund at the time the increment is implemented.

       SEC. 14. Utilization and Limitations on Expenditures of the Incentives under
 the SGCG Fund. — The use of the incentives paid out of the SGCG Fund shall be
 governed by the policies governing the utilization of the twenty percent (20%) of the
 annual National Tax Allotment (NTA) for local development projects, the Annual
 Investment Program (AIP) and the Local Development Investment Program (LDIP).
        The incentives paid out of the SGCG Fund shall not be used for any of the
 following:
          (a) Financing micro credits and loans;
          (b) Travel expenses, whether domestic or foreign, except when the purpose
              is in furtherance of the purposes of this Act;
          (c) Administrative expenses of the LGU including, but not limited to, cash
              gifts, bonuses, food allowances, staff uniforms, communication bills,
              utilities, transportation costs and the like;
          (d) Purchase, maintenance or repair of any motor vehicles or motorcycles not
              directly used for the SGCG undertaking;
          (e) Salaries, wages, emoluments, per diems or overtime pay of employees;
          (f) Construction, repair, or refurbishing of administrative offices; and Loan
              guarantee.
        SEC. 15. Technical Assistance for Capacity-Building. — The NCCA shall
 release the cultural governance assessment report, which shall identify the
 governance gaps and areas for improvement of the respective LGUs.

      Relevant national government agencies, in coordination with the Council, shall
provide technical assistance and capacity-building support to address the identified gaps
of LGUs which have not qualified for the SGCG award.

       SEC. 16. Management and Administration of the SGCG Fund. — The SGCG
 Fund established under Section 13 of this Act shall be managed and administered by
 the NCCA under the supervision of the Council.
       Appropriation to replenish the amount paid out from the Fund during the year
 and/or to augment the Fund shall be proposed by the NCCA for inclusion in its
 annual budget under the General Appropriations Act.
        SEC. 17. SGCG Operating Fund. - The activities and operational expenses,
 other than the incentive grants, related to the implementation of this Act shall be
 funded from the appropriations of the National Commission for Culture and the Arts.
     Thereafter, the NCCA shall include in its annual budget proposal, to be
incorporated in the General Appropriations Act (GAA), the item for the Seal of Good
Cultural Governance (SGCG) Operating Fund in an amount equivalent to two percent
(2%) of the SGCG Incentive Fund, to cover program administration, monitoring and
evaluation, capacity-building activities, and other related operational requirements.

     SEC. 18. Guarantee of Local Government Autonomy. - The autonomy of local
government units (LGUs) shall be respected at all times. Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as limiting the authority of LGUs to formulate and implement policies,
programs, and projects in accordance with their powers under existing laws, including
the general welfare clause.

      Participation in the SGCG Program shall be voluntary and shall not diminish
LGUs' discretion to pursue cultural, social, economic, and environmental initiatives
responsive to the needs of their respective communities.
       This Act shall not be interpreted as restricting LGUs from adopting innovative
cultural policies and programs designed not only to qualify for the SGCG award but also
to promote cultural development, safeguard heritage, support local artists and cultural
workers, and ensure the enjoyment by their constituents of fundamental rights, including
the rights to life, liberty, property, health, education, and a balanced and healthful
ecology.

      SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the NCCA, in consultation with the Council and relevant
government agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

     SEC. 20. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional and invalid, all other parts or provisions hereof which are not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

      SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with any provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

    SEC. 22. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
