                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines possesses a rich and diverse cultural heritage expressed through indigenous
knowledge systems, traditional crafts, oral traditions, music, dance, rituals, and other
community-based cultural practices. These living traditions are vital expressions of Filipino
identity and serve as an important link between generations. However, many of these traditions
are now at risk of disappearing due to rapid modernization, migration, declining interest among
the youth, and the absence of sustainable mechanisms for transmitting knowledge from cultural
bearers to younger generations.

The Schools of Living Traditions (SLTs) were established as community-based learning spaces
where traditional knowledge and skills are passed down directly from cultural masters and
elders to the youth. Through this system, communities are empowered to preserve and continue
their unique cultural practices while ensuring that these traditions remain relevant in
contemporary society.

Despite their importance, many SLTs currently operate without a clear institutional framework,
adequate funding, or consistent government support. Some initiatives rely heavily on
temporary projects, grants, or limited local efforts, which often leads to irregular
implementation and the eventual discontinuation of programs. The lack of institutionalization
also limits the ability of cultural agencies and local governments to provide long-term support,
training, and monitoring for these initiatives.

Republic Act No. 10066, or the National Cultural Heritage Act of 2009, recognizes the
importance of safeguarding intangible cultural heritage. However, it does not sufficiently
establish a structured and sustainable system for the nationwide implementation and support
of Schools of Living Traditions.

This bill seeks to institutionalize the Schools of Living Traditions (SLTs) as a formal and
sustained mechanism for safeguarding and transmitting intangible cultural heritage across the
country. By amending Republic Act No. 10066, the measure provides a clear policy framework
for the establishment, operation, and support of SLTs in partnership with cultural communities,
cultural agencies, and local government units.

Through this measure, the government will ensure consistent funding, proper documentation
of traditional knowledge, capacity-building for cultural masters, and increased participation of
the youth in heritage preservation. Institutionalizing SLTs will strengthen community-led
cultural education and protect endangered traditions from disappearing.

By providing long-term support and recognition to these community-based learning systems,
this bill affirms the State’s commitment to safeguarding the nation’s living heritage for present
and future generations.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC. 2. Appropriations. – The initial amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act shall
be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the Commission. Thereafter, such amount
necessary to effectively carry out the provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

The concerned LGUs shall likewise allocate and charge the amounts necessary for the implementation
of this Act against their respective local funds.

SEC. 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Commission, in consultation with other agencies
and representatives of indigenous and local communities shall, within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, promulgate the rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions of
this Act.
SEC. 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional, the remainder
not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, letters of instruction,
proclamations, administrative orders, rules, or regulations that are inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
