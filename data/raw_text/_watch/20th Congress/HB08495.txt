                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The international armed conflict primarily involving the United
States, Israel, and Iran has far-reaching consequences on the global peace
and order situation, and a devastating impact on the global energy supply
chain.

       The armed conflict has directly resulted in the de facto closure of the
Strait of Hormuz on March 1, 2026, severely disrupting the passage of
freight carrying critical global commodities, including crude oil, aluminum,
and other strategic resources.1 Such supply shocks have historically
triggered inflationary pressures and economic slowdowns in oil-importing

      While the Philippines generally sources its products such as processed
gasoline from neighboring Asian countries, refining countries such as
Japan, South Korea and Singapore source their crude oil from the Middle
East, which is currently at the focal point of the ongoing military action.
Ninety-seven percent (97%) of liquid petroleum products and ninety-one
percent (91%) of liquid petroleum gas are imported by these Asian refineries
directly from the Middle East. 3 Moreover, about 98% of the Philippines’
crude oil imports are sourced directly from the same region, underscoring
the country’s significant exposure to geopolitical developments affecting
Middle Eastern energy supply.4

      Given the indispensable role of petroleum in transportation,
agriculture, industry, and other essential economic activities, the risk of
supply disruptions has already contributed to a sharp increase in global and
domestic fuel prices. As a matter of fact, the Department of Energy already
announced a price increase per liter of gasoline by P7.00 to P13.00, diesel
by P17.50 to P24.25, and kerosene by P32.00 to P38.50, with projections of
even higher increases should the conflict persist.5

      These developments threaten to significantly raise the cost of
transportation, food production, and basic goods, placing substantial
economic pressure on Filipino households and sectors most sensitive to
energy price fluctuations. Rising fuel costs are expected to severely affect

      At the same time, the escalation of hostilities in the Middle East may
necessitate the repatriation and reintegration of large numbers of Overseas
Filipino Workers (OFWs), which will require substantial emergency
government support. According to the Department of Migrant Workers
(DMW), there are more than 1.113 million land-based Overseas Filipino
Workers (OFWs) deployed across the Middle East. These workers face both
direct and indirect risks to their safety, livelihood, and well-being amid
escalating regional tensions.6 As a matter of fact, on March 5, 2026, reports
indicated that around 300 Filipinos were affected by the ongoing tensions
in the Middle East due to the United States-Iran conflict arrived in the
Philippines     underscoring        the     vulnerability       of    OFWs       to    sudden
displacement.7

      Despite the seriousness of the situation, existing government
resources are insufficient to meet the scale of the crisis. Current available
funding for transport fuel subsidies is limited to roughly ₱2.5 billion in
continuing funds, an amount that is inadequate to cover the projected cost
of a full transport subsidy program should oil prices continue to rise.8

      Similarly, available emergency resources for other affected sectors—
including agriculture and OFW repatriation and reintegration—are far

           Moreover, if the situation worsens in the Middle East, the

repatriate and support returning OFWs through reintegration programs.
This is a far cry from the available ₱1.76 billion emergency funds of the

           In view of these urgent circumstances, this measure seeks to
appropriate            Fifty-Two        Billion     Eight       Hundred         Million      Pesos
(₱52,800,000,000.00) as supplemental appropriations for Fiscal Year 2026.
The proposed emergency funding will provide targeted fiscal protection to
the sectors most vulnerable to the economic shock caused by the Middle East
crisis. The proposed allocation includes:

     a.    ₱12 billion for transport fuel subsidies to assist public utility vehicle
           drivers and other transport workers affected by rising fuel prices;

     b.    ₱2.8 billion for agricultural subsidies to cushion farmers against
           rising fertilizer and production costs; and

     c.    ₱38 billion for emergency repatriation, assistance, and reintegration
           programs for OFWs who may be displaced by the conflict.

      In times of global instability, the government must act decisively to
protect the welfare of its citizens and safeguard the stability of the national
economy. This proposed supplemental appropriation provides a targeted,
responsible, and necessary fiscal response to mitigate the economic shock
brought about by the ongoing Middle East crisis.

3    Affected by the Crisis in the Middle East. - The amount appropriated herein
4    will be used exclusively to augment aid, relief, repatriation, rehabilitation,
5    reintegration, and livelihood programs and projects implemented by

7    indirect effects of the conflict in the Middle East. The amount shall consist

16            repatriation of Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) affected by the

18         d. Twenty Billion Pesos (₱20,000,000,000.00) for reintegration
19            programs for returning and repatriated OFWs affected by the
20            conflict in the Middle East.

15         Any amount appropriated under this Act that remains unobligated as
16   of December 31, 2026 shall revert to the General Fund.

25   allotments to the unappropriated surplus of the General Fund.

26         b. Excess revenue collections arising from higher-than-assumed
27   petroleum product prices under the FY 2026 revenue program are hereby
1    earmarked for the appropriations under this Act, subject to certification by

4    regional development councils, is authorized to rationalize discretionary
