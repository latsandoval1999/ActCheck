                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that "The State
shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them."

For decades, the devastating health and economic impacts of tobacco
consumption and exposure to secondhand smoke have been well-established. To
combat this, the Philippines enacted Republic Act No. 9211, or the "Tobacco
Regulation Act of 2003." More recently, in response to the rapid rise of electronic
nicotine and non-nicotine delivery systems (ENDS/ENNDS) and heated tobacco
products (HTPs), Republic Act No. 11900, or the "Vaporized Nicotine and Non-
Nicotine Products Regulation Act," was passed into law.

While these laws laid the groundwork for regulating smoking and vaping in the
country, both contain crucial loopholes that continue to compromise public
health. Specifically, both RA 9211 and RA 11900 allow for the establishment of
Designated Smoking Areas (DSAs) and Designated Vaping Areas (DVAs) within
enclosed public places and workplaces.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United States Centers
for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), there is no risk-free level of exposure
to secondhand smoke and secondhand aerosols. Ventilation systems and
physical separation do not eliminate the hazards of exposure. Toxins and
carcinogens from both combustible tobacco and vaporized products still seep into
supposedly smoke-free and vape-free zones, endangering the lives of workers,
commuters, and the general public.

As a Party to the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC), the
Philippines is obligated under Article 8 to adopt effective legislative measures
providing for protection from exposure to tobacco smoke in indoor workplaces,
public transport, and indoor public places. This standard must equally apply to
vaporized nicotine and non-nicotine products to prevent the re-normalization of
smoking behaviors and protect the youth and non-users from toxic emissions.
This bill seeks to amend RA 9211 and RA 11900 by strictly mandating a 100%
smoke-free and vape-free environment in all enclosed public places, workplaces,
and public conveyances. It eliminates indoor designated smoking and vaping
areas, ensuring that the fundamental right of every Filipino to breathe clean air
is prioritized over the privileges of the tobacco and vape industries.

In view of the paramount importance of public health, the swift passage of this
bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT MANDATING A SMOKE-FREE AND VAPE-FREE ENVIRONMENT IN
       ALL PUBLIC AND WORKPLACES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
     REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9211 AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11900, AND FOR
                              OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Comprehensive
Smoke-Free and Vape-Free Environment Act."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
The State recognizes that there is no safe level of exposure to secondhand smoke
and secondhand aerosols. In accordance with its obligations under the World
Health Organization Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (WHO FCTC),
the State shall absolutely protect the public from exposure to tobacco smoke and
emissions from vaporized nicotine and non-nicotine products in all enclosed
public places, workplaces, and public conveyances.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act:

   1. Public Place refers to all places, fixed or mobile, that are accessible or open
      to the public or places for collective use, regardless of ownership or right to
      access, including but not limited to schools, workplaces, government
      facilities, establishments that provide food and drinks, accommodation,
      merchandise, professional services, entertainment or other services,
      outdoor spaces where facilities are available for the public or where a crowd
      of people would gather.
   2. Workplace refers to any place used by people during their employment or
      work, whether done for compensation or voluntarily, including all attached
      or associated places commonly used by the workers in the course of their
      work.
   3. Public Conveyance refers to modes of transportation servicing the general
      population, such as, but not limited to, elevators, airplanes, buses,
      taxicabs, ships, jeepneys, light rail transits, tricycles, and similar vehicles.

SEC. 4. Prohibition on Smoking and Vaping in Public Places and Workplaces. –
Section 5 of Republic Act No. 9211 is hereby amended to read as follows:

"SEC. 5. Smoking AND VAPING Ban in Public Places. - Smoking AND THE USE
OF VAPORIZED NICOTINE AND NON-NICOTINE PRODUCTS shall be
ABSOLUTELY PROHIBITED in all ENCLOSED public places, WORKPLACES,
AND PUBLIC CONVEYANCES, [whether enclosed or outdoors] INCLUDING BUT
NOT LIMITED TO THE FOLLOWING:

a. Centers of youth activity such as playschools, preparatory schools, elementary
schools, high schools, colleges and universities, youth hostels and recreational
facilities for persons under eighteen (18) years old;

b. Elevators and stairwells;

c. Locations in which fire hazards are present, including gas stations and storage
areas for flammable liquids, gas, explosives or combustible materials;

d. Within the buildings and premises of public and private hospitals, medical,
dental, and optical clinics, health centers, nursing homes, dispensaries and
laboratories;

e. Public conveyances and public facilities including airport and ship terminals
and train and bus stations, restaurants and conference halls, except for
OUTDOOR separate smoking AND VAPING areas; and

f. Food preparation areas."

SEC. 5. Abolition of Indoor Designated Smoking and Vaping Areas. – To ensure a
100% smoke-free and vape-free indoor environment, Designated Smoking Areas
(DSAs) and Designated Vaping Areas (DVAs) shall only be allowed in completely
open, outdoor spaces that are at least ten (10) meters away from entrances, exits,
windows, and ventilation air intakes of enclosed public places and workplaces.

Consequently, Section 15 of Republic Act No. 11900 is hereby amended to read
as follows:

"SEC. 15. Use in Public Place. - NO DESIGNATED VAPING AREAS SHALL BE
PERMITTED WITHIN ANY ENCLOSED PUBLIC PLACE OR WORKPLACE.
DESIGNATED VAPING AREAS SHALL ONLY BE ALLOWED IN OUTDOOR
SPACES. [The use of Vaporized Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products and Novel
Tobacco Products shall be prohibited in all indoor public places except in
Designated Vaping Areas, or in point-of-sale of Vaporized Nicotine and Non-
Nicotine Products and Novel Tobacco Products...]

ALL DESIGNATED OUTDOOR VAPING AREAS MUST NOT BE LOCATED
WITHIN TEN (10) METERS FROM ENTRANCES, EXITS, OR ANY PLACE
WHERE PEOPLE PASS OR GATHER."

SEC. 6. Duties of Persons in Charge. – All owners, proprietors, operators,
possessors, managers, or administrators of public places, workplaces, and public
conveyances shall be strictly responsible for ensuring compliance with this Act.
They shall:

   1. Prominently post "No Smoking and No Vaping" signages in the most visible
      locations of the establishment or vehicle;
   2. Remove all ashtrays and vaping receptacles from enclosed areas; and
   3. Ensure that individuals smoking or vaping in prohibited areas are directed
      to cease the act and, if necessary, be escorted out of the premises.

SEC. 7. Penalties. – Any person who violates the provisions of this Act shall suffer
the following penalties:

   •   First Offense: A fine of not less than Two thousand pesos (PhP 2,000.00)
       but not more than Five thousand pesos (PhP 5,000.00).
   •   Second Offense: A fine of not less than Five thousand pesos (PhP 5,000.00)
       but not more than Ten thousand pesos (PhP 10,000.00).
   •   Third and Subsequent Offenses: A fine of not less than Ten thousand pesos
       (PhP 10,000.00) but not more than Twenty thousand pesos (PhP 20,000.00)
       or imprisonment of not more than one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of
       the court.

For business establishments failing to implement this Act, revocation or
cancellation of their business permits and licenses may be ordered upon the third
offense.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – The relevant sections of Republic Act No. 9211
(Tobacco Regulation Act of 2003) and Republic Act No. 11900 (Vaporized Nicotine
and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act) pertaining to indoor designated
smoking and vaping areas, as well as all other laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
