                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Unmanned systems, commonly known as drones, have become increasingly
accessible and are now widely used for recreational, commercial, agricultural,
scientific, media, and government purposes. Their rapid advancement has
significantly contributed to innovation, improved efficiency, and economic
development across various sectors.

       However, the proliferation of drone technology has also brought about serious
concerns relating to public safety, national security, privacy, aviation safety, and the
protection of critical infrastructure. Incidents involving unauthorized drone
operations near airports, military installations, government facilities, densely
populated areas, and public events have highlighted the need for a comprehensive
legal framework governing their possession and operation.

      At present, while existing regulations issued by the Civil Aviation Authority of
the Philippines (CAAP) provide operational guidelines for unmanned aircraft, there
remains no comprehensive legislation that establishes uniform standards for the
registration, possession, operation, accountability, and enforcement mechanisms
applicable to unmanned systems. Likewise, there is a need to strengthen
coordination among concerned government agencies in monitoring, regulating, and
addressing violations involving drone operations.

     This proposed measure seeks to establish a comprehensive regulatory
framework governing the possession and operation of unmanned systems. It aims to
ensure that drones are utilized responsibly while safeguarding public safety,
protecting individual privacy, preserving national security, and promoting the
orderly integration of unmanned systems into Philippine airspace. The bill likewise
provides appropriate penalties for unlawful possession and operation, unauthorized
surveillance, interference with lawful activities, and other prohibited acts, while
recognizing the legitimate use of drones for commercial, educational, research,
humanitarian, agriculture, disaster response, and other lawful purposes.

     By striking a balance between technological innovation and public welfare, this
measure promotes the responsible development of the unmanned systems industry
while ensuring that emerging technologies are utilized in a manner consistent with
the Constitution, existing laws, and the best interests of the Filipino people.

   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT REGULATING THE POSSESSION AND OPERATION OF
 UNMANNED SYSTEMS, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS
            THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


                    ARTICLE I: GENERAL PROVISIONS
SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Unmanned Systems
Regulation and Safety Act of 2026"
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to promote and
govern the use of technology for national development while safeguarding the life,
liberty, property, and security of its citizens. The State recognizes the immense
potential of drones, formally known as Unmanned Systems (US), for commercial,
recreational, scientific, and public service applications. However, the state also
acknowledges the inherent risks they pose to public safety, national security, and
individual privacy.
      Therefore, the State shall establish a comprehensive regulatory framework to
ensure that the importation, manufacture, sale, ownership, and operation of drones
are conducted responsibly and safely. This framework shall balance innovation with
security, empower law enforcement agencies, and prescribe clear penalties to deter
unlawful use and exploitation.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:
a. Agricultural Operation - refers to the use of an Unmanned System for
agricultural purposes, including but not limited to, crop dusting, seeding, pest
control, crop health monitoring, and livestock management.
b. CAAP Regulations - Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines rules,
including Civil Aviation Regulations (CAR) Part 11 and Part 14 and CAAP
Memorandum Circular No. 29-15, governing UAS operations.
c. Commercial Operation - refers to any authorized use of a drone for work,
business, or in connection with any form of compensation or hire.
d. Controlled Airspace - refers to airspace of defined dimensions within which
Air Traffic Control service is provided.
e. Critical Infrastructure - refers to assets, systems, and networks so vital to
the Republic of the Philippines that their incapacitation or destruction would
have a debilitating impact on national security, economic stability, public
health, or safety.
f. Drone Operator or Remote Pilot - refers to any natural or juridical person
who commands, controls, operates, and manipulates the flight controls of
Unmanned System.
g. Drone Owner - refers to the natural or juridical person in whose name the
drone is registered.
h. Law Enforcement Agency (LEA) - refers to the Philippine National Police
(PNP), National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), Philippine Drug Enforcement
Agency (PDEA), and other government agencies authorized by law to conduct
law enforcement functions.
i. Law Enforcement Operation - refers to the broad range of activities
undertaken by government agents to enforce the law. This encompasses the
practical, on-the-ground duties and responsibilities performed by the PNP and
other law enforcement agents to maintain public order, prevent and detect
crime, and apprehend offenders.
j. Modification - refers to any alteration to a drone's hardware or software that
changes its flight capabilities, payload capacity, or operational range from its
original manufacturer specifications.
k. Prohibited or No-Fly Zone - refers to a designated area over which drone
flight is strictly prohibited for national security or public safety reasons.
      l. Public Safety and Security Operation - refers to the authorized use of an
      Unmanned System by a government entity for public service purposes, such
      as but not limited to search and rescue, law enforcement, reconnaissance,
      tactical operations, disaster response, and other purposes deemed necessary to
      promote law and order, safety, and security.
      m. Recreational Operation - refers to the authorized use of an Unmanned
      System for personal interest, hobby, or amusement purposes, with no
      commercial or professional intent.
      n. Restricted Zone - refers to a designated area over which drone flight is
      subject to specific conditions or limitations.
      o. Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) - refers to an unmanned system designed
      for flight in the atmosphere or aerial surface.
      p. Unmanned Ground-Based System (UGS) - refers to an unmanned system
      designed for operation on land surfaces.
      q. Unmanned Maritime System (UMS) - refers to an unmanned system
      designed for operation on the surface of or underneath the water, including
      submersible and semi-submersible vehicles.
      r. Unmanned System (US) - refers to any remotely, automatically, or
      autonomously controlled vehicle and its associated control systems, operating
      in the air, on land, or in water, without a human pilot or operator on board.
      s. Visual Line of Sight (VLOS) - refers to the operator's ability to always see
      the drone with their own eyes (without visual aids like binoculars) during
      flight.
      t. Weaponized Drone - refers to any Unmanned System equipped with or used
      to deploy any firearm, explosive, chemical, biological, radiological, or any
      other hazardous payload intended to cause harm, injury, or death.
SEC. 4. Categories and Classifications of Drones. All drones shall be classified
based on their Maximum Take-Off Weight (MTOW) and/or operational risk as
follows:
   a. Based on Weight:
      1. Category 1 (Micro): Less than 250 grams. Exempt from registration if used
      recreationally.
      2. Category 2 (Small): 250 grams to 7 kilograms. Requires registration and
      licensing.
      3. Category 3 (Standard): Over 7 kg but not exceeding 25 kg. Requires stricter
      certification.
      4. Category 4 (Large): Over 25 kg. Regulated similarly to manned aircraft.
   b. Based on Operational Risk:
      1. Class 1 (Low-Risk): Recreational flights in approved areas.
      2. Class 2 (Medium-Risk): Commercial operations within VLOS, such as
      aerial photography or mapping.
      3. Class 3 (High-Risk): Specialized operations like Beyond Visual Line of
      Sight (BVLOS) flights, nighttime operations, or drone deliveries, which
      require special permits.
       ARTICLE II: GOVERNANCE, REGISTRATION, AND LICENSING
SEC. 5. Inter-Agency Council on Unmanned Systems (IAC-US). - To ensure a
holistic and harmonized regulatory approach, an Inter-Agency Council on
Unmanned Systems is hereby created. It shall be chaired by the Secretary of the
Department of Transportation (DOTr) and shall be composed of the heads of the
following agencies: from the DND, DILG, DTI, DICT, DOST, DA, NSC, CAAP,
AFP, PNP, BFP, and PCG. The IAC-US shall be the primary body responsible for
policymaking and overseeing the implementation of this Act.
SEC. 6. Mandatory Unmanned System Registration. All Unmanned Systems,
except for UAS weighing less than 250 grams used for recreational purposes, must
be registered in the Philippine Unmanned Systems Registry. The IAC - US shall
establish this central registry. The CAAP shall manage the registration of UAS. The
PCG shall manage the registration of UMS. The DOTr, in coordination with the PNP,
shall manage the registration of UGS. Registration shall be renewable every three
(3) years.
SEC. 7. Mandatory Operator Licensing. No person shall operate an unmanned
system covered by mandatory registration without a valid Remote Operator's
License. The IAC-US, through its member agencies, shall establish license
categories based on the domain, class, and risk level of the operation. Applicants
must pass knowledge examinations and practical skills assessments relevant to their
intended domain of operation.
SEC. 8. Accreditation of Importers, Manufacturers, and Sellers. Any entity
engaged in the importation, manufacturing, assembly, sale, or lease of drones in the
Philippines must secure accreditation from the Department of Trade and Industry
(DTI) in coordination with the CAAP. Accredited entities are required to:
      a. Maintain a record of all sales, including the buyer's name, address, and the
      drone's serial number.
      b. Provide a point-of-sale safety briefing or leaflet to buyers, outlining the key
      provisions of this Act.
      c. Submit a quarterly sales report to the CAAP and the Philippine National
      Police (PNP).


                       ARTICLE III: RULES OF OPERATION
SECTION 9. General Operating Rules. All unmanned system operations shall
adhere to the following unless otherwise authorized:
      a. Operate within prescribed operational boundaries.

      b. Maintain continuous visual line of sight (VLOS) with the system, unless
         specifically authorized for BVLOS or autonomous operations.

      c. For UAS, fly at a maximum altitude of 400 feet (122 meters) above ground
         level.

      d. Do not fly over crowds, schools, hospitals, or public gatherings.

      e. Do not operate a drone from a moving vehicle.

      f. Yield the right-of-way to all manned vehicles, vessels, or aircraft.

      g. Do not operate over crowds, critical infrastructure, or designated public
         gatherings without authorization.

      h. The operator must not be under the influence of alcohol or prohibited
         substances.

SEC. 10. Prohibited Acts. The following acts are declared unlawful:

      a. Operating a drone without a valid registration and/or operator's license.

      b. Import, manufacture, lease, and sell drones, parts, and their peripherals
         without accreditation.
      c. Sell or lease a drone to any individual without a valid Drone Operator's
         License.

      d. Weaponizing a drone or equipping it with any dangerous or hazardous
         material, including firearms, explosives, biological agents, or corrosive
         substances.

      e. Using a drone for the surveillance, harassment, or violation of the right to
         privacy of any individual as defined under Republic Act No. 10173 or the
         "Data Privacy Act of 2012."

      f. Using a drone to aid or commit any crime, including but not limited to
         smuggling, illegal drug trafficking, and reconnaissance for criminal or
         terrorist activities.

      g. Interfering with the operations of law enforcement, emergency response,
         or disaster relief personnel.

      h. Hacking, spoofing, jamming, or illegally interfering with the command,
         control, or navigation signal of a registered drone.

      i. Operating a drone while under the influence of alcohol or prohibited drugs.

      j. Similar and analogous acts using drones in the commission of illegal
         activities.


SEC. 11. Prohibited and Restricted Zones. It is strictly prohibited to operate a
drone:

      a. Within a 10-kilometer radius of any airport or airfield.

      b. Over Critical Infrastructure and designated national security areas,
      including but not limited to military bases, Malacañang Palace, and major
      government installations.

      c. Over areas where a Temporary Flight Restriction (TFR) is in effect, as
      declared by the CAAP for emergencies, major events, or public safety and
      security operations.
SEC. 12. Protection of Privacy. The use of a drone for surveillance, eavesdropping,
or capturing images, videos, or audio of any person or private property without
explicit consent is strictly prohibited and shall be punishable under this Act, in
addition to penalties provided under Republic Act No. 10173, or the "Data Privacy
Act of 2012”.

SEC. 13. Regulation on Drone Modification. Any modification to a drone that
alters its flight performance, weight, or communication systems must be declared
and approved by the CAAP. The weaponization of a drone, by attaching firearms,
explosives, biological agents, hazardous materials, or any lethal or non-lethal
weapon, is absolutely prohibited.

  ARTICLE IV: ROLE OF GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND SPECIAL
                       OPERATIONS

SEC. 14. Role of Local Government Units. - Consistent with the Local
Government Code, LGUs may enact ordinances to regulate the operation of drones
in areas of local jurisdiction, such as public parks, plazas, and heritage sites. Such
ordinances shall be supplementary to this Act and its IRR and must not contradict
the national policies and standards set by the CAAP and other national government
agencies.

SEC. 15. Use for National Security and Law Enforcement. - Duly constituted
government agencies, including the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) and the
Philippine National Police (PNP), may operate unmanned systems for national
security, intelligence, law enforcement, border control, and counter-terrorism
operations. The use of such systems, including the employment of counter-
unmanned system technology, shall be governed by their existing rules of
engagement and protocols established in the IRR.

SEC. 16. Use for Disaster and Emergency Response. - Government agencies such
as the NDRRMC, PHIVOLCS, BFP, NAMRIA, and PCG are authorized to deploy
and operate unmanned systems as a critical component of disaster risk assessment,
monitoring, search and rescue, and post-disaster recovery operations. They may
apply for exemptions from standard operating rules during a state of calamity,
provided safety is ensured through coordination with the IAC-US.

SEC. 17. Use for Research and Development. - The State shall encourage the use
of unmanned systems for scientific research and technological development. The
Department of Science and Technology (DOST), in coordination with the IAC-US,
shall:
      a. Establish designated special-use areas for safe testing and experimentation.

      b. Develop a streamlined process for issuing experimental or research permits
      to accredited academic and scientific institutions.

      c. Promote partnerships between the government, academe, and industry to
      advance local unmanned systems technology.

SEC. 18. Use of Drones for Agricultural Purposes. The use of drones for
agricultural activities is hereby recognized as a vital tool for enhancing food security
and modernizing the agricultural sector. Such use shall be governed by the
following:

      a. Authorization and Scope. The State shall support and regulate the use of
      agricultural drones for purposes including, but not limited to:

             1) Precision Dispensing in applying fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides,
             seeds, or water.

             2) Crop Management in conducting crop health monitoring, soil
             analysis, irrigation assessment, and yield estimation through
             multispectral or thermal imaging

             3) Livestock Monitoring to oversee livestock location, health, and
             grazing patterns.

      b. Special Permit and Operator Certification. The operation of a drone for
      dispensing any chemical, biological, or agricultural substance shall require a
      Special Agricultural UAS Permit from the Civil Aviation Authority of the
      Philippines (CAAP).

      c. Operators must obtain a Special Agricultural Drone Operator Certificate in
      addition to their Remote Pilot License. This certification requires specialized
      training in the safe handling and application of agrochemicals, spray drift
      mitigation, and the operation of drone-mounted dispersal systems, conducted
      by training organizations accredited by the Department of Agriculture (DA)
      and the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority (FPA).

      d. Operational Safety and Coordination. All agricultural drone operations
      involving chemical dispensing must adhere to safety protocols established by
      the FPA and the DA, which shall include:
             1) Buffer Zones: Maintaining mandatory buffer zones from residential
             areas, schools, bodies of water, and other sensitive areas to prevent
             contamination.

             2) Public Notification: Providing prior notification to the local
             government unit or barangay before conducting large-scale spraying
             operations.

             3) Extended Visual Line of Sight (EVLOS): To facilitate operations
             over large plantations, flight operations beyond the direct sight of the
             remote pilot may be allowed, provided that trained visual observers are
             utilized to keep the drone in sight and maintain communication with the
             pilot, subject to CAAP approval.

SEC. 19. Enforcement Authority. The PNP and other deputized law enforcement
agencies are hereby authorized to enforce the provisions of this Act. They shall have
the authority, but not limited to:

      a. Demand the presentation of the drone's registration and the operator's
      license during an inspection.

      b. Order the immediate landing and grounding of any drone believed to be
      operating in violation of this Act.

      c. Seize, confiscate, and impound any drone operated in violation of this Act
      or used in the commission of a crime without prejudice to the handling and
      processing of the drone and all data saved therein of evidentiary value.

      d. Employ counter-drone technology in situations where a drone poses an
      imminent threat to public safety, national security, or the security of a high-
      value installation or event, subject to existing rules of engagement protocols
      and standard operational procedures.

SEC. 20. Counter-Drone Measures. The PNP, the Armed Forces of the Philippines
(AFP), and other authorized national security agencies are hereby authorized to use
reasonable and necessary force, including approved counter-drone technologies, to
detect, identify, disable, or neutralize any drone that poses an imminent threat to
public safety, critical infrastructure, or national security. The use of such measures
shall be governed by protocols to be established in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR).
                     ARTICLE V: PENAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 21. Fines and Penalties. Any natural or juridical person found in violation of
the provisions of this Act shall be subject to the following penalties. The PNP and
other deputized law enforcement agencies are hereby empowered to impose the
following administrative fines and sanctions for violations of its regulations
promulgated in accordance with this Act:

      a. Any person operating an unregistered drone required to be registered, or
         for failure to mark the drone with its registration number, shall be fined
         from Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000) to One Hundred Thousand Pesos
         (P100,000) and temporary suspension of the operator's license.



      b. Any person operating a drone without a valid Remote Pilot License shall
         be fined One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000) to Two Hundred
         Thousand Pesos (P200,000) and disqualified from applying for a license
         for a period of one (1) year.



      c. Any person violating the General Operating Rules under Section 9 shall be
         fined Twenty Thousand Pesos (P20,000) to Fifty Thousand Pesos
         (P50,000) per violation.


      d. Any person who failed to comply with the accreditation requirements for
         importers, manufacturers, and sellers shall be fined from Three Hundred
         Thousand Pesos (P300,000) to One Million Pesos (P1,000,000) and
         suspension or revocation of the entity's business permit and accreditation.

      e. Any person who willfully operates a drone in a Prohibited Zone or in a
         manner that violates the conditions of a Restricted Zone shall be punished
         by prisión correccional in its medium and maximum periods and a fine of
         Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000) to Five Hundred Thousand
         Pesos (P500,000).

      f. Any person who uses a drone to commit acts of surveillance or recording
         in violation of Section 10 shall be punished by prisión correccional in its
         minimum and medium periods and a fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos
         (P100,000) to Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (P300,000). This is without
         prejudice to any separate liability under Republic Act No.10173 (Data
         Privacy Act) or Republic Act No. 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act).

      g. The act of attaching any firearm, explosive, chemical, biological,
         radiological, or any other deadly weapon or hazardous material to a drone
         shall be punished by reclusión temporal in its maximum period to reclusion
         perpetua and a fine of One Million Pesos (P1,000,000) to Two Million
         Pesos (P2,000,000).

      h. Any person who uses a drone to intentionally interfere with the operations
         of law enforcement officers, firefighters, or other emergency responders
         shall be punished by prisión correccional in its maximum period and a fine
         of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000).

      i. Any person who uses drone to unlawfully gather, collect, record, or
         transmit information, images, or electronic data from any military
         installation, critical infrastructure, or government facility for purposes of
         espionage or to aid any foreign power or entity hostile to the Republic of
         the Philippines shall be punished by reclusión perpetua and a fine of Five
         Million Pesos (P5,000,000) to Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000).

      j. If the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalty of perpetual
         absolute disqualification from public office shall be imposed in addition to
         the imprisonment and fine.

      k. If the offender is a foreign national, they shall be prosecuted accordingly
         and deported immediately after serving the sentence without further
         proceedings.

SEC. 22. Use of a Drone as a Special Aggravating Circumstance. In any crime
punishable under the Revised Penal Code or any special penal law, the use of a drone
in the planning, commission, facilitation, or concealment of said crime shall be
considered a special aggravating circumstance. When this circumstance is present,
the penalty to be imposed shall be the maximum period of the penalty prescribed by
law for the offense committed.

           ARTICLE VI: IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS
SEC. 23. Authority to Promulgate Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).
The Department of Transportation (DOTr), through the Civil Aviation Authority of
the Philippines (CAAP), as the lead implementing agency, is hereby mandated to
formulate, in public consultation with relevant stakeholders, the rules and
regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

The IRR shall be prepared in coordination with the following government agencies:

a. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

b. Department of National Defense (DND);

c. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);

d. Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT);

e. Department of Agriculture (DA);

f. Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR);

g. National Privacy Commission (NPC);

h. Philippine National Police (PNP);

i. Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP); and

j. Other agencies as may be deemed necessary.

SEC. 24. Scope and Content of the IRR. The IRR shall be promulgated within
ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act and shall provide detailed procedures
and standards for the following matters:

      a. Registration and Marking: The procedure for drone registration, renewal,
      transfer of ownership, de-registration, fees, and the specific requirements for
      displaying registration markings.

      b. Licensing and Training: The curriculum, standards, and procedures for the
      aeronautical knowledge examination, practical skills assessment, and
      issuance of Remote Pilot Licenses for various categories and classes of
      operations. It shall also establish standards for accredited drone pilot training
      organizations.
      c. Airspace and Flight Rules: The precise definition and publication of
      Prohibited and Restricted Zones, and the process for establishing Temporary
      Flight Restrictions (TFRs) for emergencies or special events.

      d. Special Operations Permits: The application process and safety
      requirements for Class 3 (High-Risk) operations, including Beyond Visual
      Line of Sight (BVLOS), nighttime operations, and drone delivery services.

      e. Law Enforcement Protocols: The rules of engagement and standard
      operating procedures for the PNP and other law enforcement agencies in the
      inspection, seizure, and disposition of drones, as well as the authorized use of
      counter-drone technologies.

      f. Governmental Use Protocols: The specific guidelines for drone use by the
      BFP, PNP, AFP, and other government agencies, including inter-agency
      coordination during joint operations.

      g. Privacy and Data Protection: The specific guidelines to ensure compliance
      with the Data Privacy Act, particularly for drones equipped with advanced
      surveillance and data-capturing technologies.

      h. Accreditation and Sales Monitoring: The detailed requirements and
      procedures for the accreditation of drone importers, manufacturers, and
      sellers, and the mechanism for reporting sales data.

      i. Fines and Penalties: A detailed schedule of administrative fines and a clear
      procedure for the adjudication of administrative offenses.

SEC. 25. Retroactivity Clause. This Act shall have retroactive effect. All drone
owners who have not yet registered their drones or obtained permits to operate their
drones are given six (6) months from the effectivity of the Implementing Rules and
Regulations of this Act to register and obtain permits without fear of sanction.

SEC. 26. Separability Clause. If for any reason, any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, such parts not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 28. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in any two (2) newspapers of general
circulation in the Philippines.
