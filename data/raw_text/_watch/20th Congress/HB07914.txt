                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Political parties are the cornerstone of a functioning and robust democracy, yet
in the Philippines, they have long remained among its weakest and least
institutionalized actors.

        A political party is an organized group committed to the promotion and defense
of a defined set of political ideals in government. Political parties play a crucial role in
a democratic system. They aggregate diverse and competing citizen interests into
coherent policy platforms through negotiation and compromise, allowing voters to
make informed choices among alternative governance agenda. By structuring
electoral competition around programs rather than isolated preferences, parties
enable collective political participation, accountability, and meaningful influence over
public policy. They formulate and refine policy proposals, mobilize and integrate
citizens into the political process, and recruit, train, and support leaders for public
office1.

        In the Philippines, however, political parties have historically fallen short of this
ideal. Rather than being programmatic and ideology-driven formations, they have
largely evolved into patronage-based, personality-oriented organizations, often
utilized merely as convenient vehicles to mount electoral campaigns during election
periods.2 The political party landscape in the Philippines has remained weakly
institutionalized, characterized by persistent intra-party factionalism that reflects
structural fragmentation rather than coherent ideological alignment.3 Empirical studies
indicate that factional splits and multi-factional competition have been enduring
features across different eras of Philippine party politics, contributing to instability and
low levels of party institutionalization.4

         Another manifestation of this institutional instability is the prevalence of so-
called “political butterflies” or turncoats, politicians who frequently switch party
affiliations with little to no regard for ideology or party programs. Empirical studies
show that major parties such as the Liberal Party (LP), Lakas-Christian Muslim
Democrats (CMD), and Partido Demokratiko Pilipino-Lakas ng Bayan (PDP-Laban)
experience sharp membership shifts tied to the political fortunes of their leaders,
particularly the incumbent President. This bandwagoning behavior weakens checks
and balances, concentrates power within the ruling party, and undermines democratic
accountability.5 Political opportunism and turncoatism should never be encouraged,
much less tolerated, as they sully the values of honor, integrity, and accountability
essential to public service.

       Despite numerous attempts throughout the country’s history to reform the
orientation and behavior of political parties, these initiatives have failed to produce
transformative and sustained change. A principal reason for this failure is the absence
of a comprehensive and institutionalized legal framework to govern, regulate, and
support the Philippine political party system. Without such framework, political parties
remain structurally weak, excessively dependent on private financing, and vulnerable
to undue influence and corruption.

        Thus, this proposed measure.

       This bill seeks to address these systemic deficiencies by introducing
comprehensive reforms to the political party system. It establishes clear standards for
the registration and accreditation of national political parties, mandates internal
democracy and ethical governance, and institutionalizes transparent, encouragement
of establishing political think-tanks, merit-based processes for the nomination and
selection of candidates. It likewise introduces firm measures to discourage political

         Central to this measure is the creation of a State Subsidy Fund to augment the
operating budgets of qualified and accredited national political parties. Moreover, this
bill integrates robust reporting, auditing, and performance monitoring requirements to
ensure accountability in the use of public funds.

       Ultimately, this proposed measure seeks to professionalize political parties and
transform them into stable, programmatic, and accountable institutions that serve as
genuine instruments of development and good governance. By strengthening political
parties as pillars of democracy, this measure advances constitutional principles of
transparency, accountability, and meaningful political participation, and reinforces
public trust in the political and electoral process.

       A similar measure seeking to strengthen political parties and address political
turncoatism was filed by this Representation in the 18th Congress as Senate Bill No.
620. In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Gloria Macapagal
Arroyo, Edgar Erice, and Raymond Adrian Salceda.

 6           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall
 7   mean:
 8               a) Accredited National Political Party – refers to a national political party
 9                  qualified to receive subsidy for party development and campaign
10                  purposes, and accredited for this purpose by the Commission on
11                  Elections (COMELEC) based on the criteria provided under this Act.
12
13               b) Candidate – refers to any person aspiring for or seeking an elective
14                  public office, who by themself or duly nominated by an accredited
15                  political party, aggroupment, or coalition of parties, has filed a certificate
16                  of candidacy with the COMELEC.
17
18               c) Campaign Contribution – refers to any form of donation to any
19                  candidate, political party, aggrupation, or coalition thereof. It includes
20                  any gift, donation subscription, loan, advance or deposit of money, or
21                  anything of value, or those arising from contract, pledge, or agreement
22                  to contribute, made for the purpose of influencing the results of the
23                  elections, but shall not include services rendered without compensation
24                  by individuals volunteering a portion or all of their time in behalf of a
25                  candidate or political party. It also includes the use of office space,
26                  facilities, equipment, office supplies, and other materials and fixtures
27                  voluntarily donated by other persons, or allowed their use for free, the
28                  monetary value of which shall be assessed based on market rates
29                  prevailing in a particular area.
30
31               d) Campaign Expenditure – refers to any type of expense incurred,
32                  regardless of source, amount, and purpose, that relates, directly or
33                  indirectly, to the conduct of an electoral campaign. It includes all
34                  payments of money or anything of value, or a contract, promise, or
35                  agreement to spend, for the purpose of influencing the results of the
36                  election. It includes the use of office space and facilities personally
37                  owned by the candidate, the monetary value of the use of which shall
38                  be assessed based on the market rates prevailing in a particular area.
39
40               e) Coalition Agreement – refers to an agreement between different political
41                  parties who are cooperating to achieve a particular aim.

11           SEC. 4. Registration as a National Political Party. – Any organized group of
12   persons seeking registration as a national political party may file with the COMELEC
13   a verified petition, attaching thereto its constitution and by-laws, platform, principles,
14   policies, and general program of government, a verified list of its national officials,
15   members of the executive board, or its equivalent, and the heads of its regional,
16   provincial, and city chapters, annual statement of accounts, and such other relevant
17   information as may be required by the COMELEC.
18
19           The COMELEC shall, after due notice and hearing, resolve the petition within
20   ten (10) days from the date it is submitted for decision. National Political Parties
21   already registered as such with the COMELEC prior to the effectivity of this Act are
22   not required to register anew, but are required to submit their intent to continue
23   participating in elections within a period of six (6) months after the effectivity of this
24   Act.
25
26          SEC. 5. Policy Agenda and Program of Governance. – National Political Parties
27   are mandated to craft a clear policy agenda and program of governance consistent
28   with their party philosophy and ideals. The members of the National Political Party
29   shall endeavor to act in accordance with the defined party platform and pursue
30   programs to fulfill party commitments.
31
32           SEC. 6. Party Ethics. – Accredited National Political Parties, as provided for in
33   Section 21 of this Act, shall institute internal control mechanisms to promote
34   accountability and transparency within the party. Accredited National Political Parties
35   shall likewise develop and enforce an internal code of conduct and ethical standards
36   that will guide party members in upholding the values and standards of public life, and
37   toward this end shall formulate and implement disciplinary procedures for party
38   members: Provided, That said program for internal controls, ethical standards, and
39   disciplinary procedures shall be duly submitted to the COMELEC and made available
40   to the public.
41
42           SEC. 7. Selection of Candidates. – The selection process for candidates of
43   National Political Parties shall be democratized through the adoption of a process that
44   is fair, open, and transparent, and which promotes optimal participation from the

 1   members of the party. Toward this end, every National Political Party is mandated to
 2   formulate and adopt a merit system for nomination and selection of candidates who
 3   must be members of the party. Every National Political Party shall submit to the
 4   COMELEC its rules governing the merit system on nomination and selection of
 5   candidates not later than one hundred and eighty (180) days before the election day
 6   following the effectivity of this Act.
 7
 8           Any aggrieved member of an accredited National Political Party may file a
 9   verified complaint with its Grievance and Arbitration Committee, not later than ten (10)
10   days after the party convention, for violation of the rules governing the merit system
11   on the nomination and selection of candidates. Every National Political Party shall hold
12   conventions or meetings to nominate their official candidates not earlier than fifteen
13   (15) days before the start of the election period and shall submit to the COMELEC not
14   later than the start of the election period the names of the officials of the party
15   authorized to nominate their official candidates.
16
17           No political party shall nominate more candidates than the number of persons
18   required to be voted for in an elective position, nor shall any candidate be allowed to
19   accept nominations from more than one registered political party, except in cases of

23   filed not later than the last day for filing of the certificates of candidacy as determined
24   by the COMELEC.

26          SEC. 8. Certificate of Nomination and Acceptance. – The certificate of

36          SEC. 9. Changing Political Party Affiliation. – Any member of a National Political
37   Party who changes party affiliation after being nominated by the party shall be deemed
38   to have committed Political Turncoatism.

40          SEC. 10. Penalties for Political Turncoatism. – Political Turncoats shall be:
41
42              a) Deemed to have forfeited their elective office if they change their
43                 political party affiliation within one (1) year prior to the next election or
44                 within one (1) year after the immediately preceding election;

                                                 5
 1                b) Prohibited from being appointed or from holding any position in any
 2                   public or government office for three (3) years after the expiration of the
 3                   current term or office;
 4                c) Prohibited from assuming any executive or administrative position in the
 5                   new political party; and
 6                d) Directed to refund any and all amounts from the political party which
 7                   were received by, or utilized for the beneficial use of the political
 8                   turncoat, plus a fifty percent (50%) surcharge thereon.

10           SEC. 11. Petition for Disqualification. – Any citizen of voting age, or any
11   candidate, political party, aggroupment, or coalition thereof, may file with the
12   COMELEC, upon the filing of the certificate of candidacy and before proclamation, a
13   verified petition to disqualify a candidate on the ground of political turncoatism as
14   defined in this Act.

16          SEC. 12. Disqualification of National Political Party. – Any National Political

19   disqualification shall continue until such time that the political turncoat shall be
20   removed from the party after providing the COMELEC with facts to the contrary.

 6          SEC. 33. Punishable Acts. – The following acts shall be punishable:
 7
 8              a) Misuse of funds received by National Political Parties, both from the
 9                 Fund and from campaign contributions;
10              b) Giving and/or receiving of campaign contributions which go beyond the
11                 allowable limits and violate the prohibitions set under Section 14 of this
12                 Act and other existing laws;
13              c) Inability to account for all incoming contributions from whatever source;
14              d) Failure to submit pre-election as well as post-election disclosure
15                 statements to the COMELEC. Any submission past the late submission
16                 period set in Section 29(c) will be deemed as a failure to submit, hence,
17                 punishable under existing laws;
18              e) Failure to submit expenditure reports related to campaigns, and
19                 financial reports on party development activities, under Section 29 (b)
20                 and (d), respectively; and
21              f) False reporting or any misrepresentation in the financial statement
22                 reports.

24          SEC. 34. Penalties. –
25
26              a) Any candidate or official of any National Political Party who violates
27                 Section 33 (a), (c), and (e) of this Act shall be punished with

29                 (12) years, or a fine ranging from One Hundred Thousand Pesos
30                 (P100,000.00) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), or
31                 both. The said candidate or official shall likewise be disqualified to hold
32                 public office. Any National Political Party that violates Section 33(a), (c),

14           SEC. 35. Appropriations. – The amount of Three Hundred Fifty Million Pesos
15   (P350,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated out of the funds of the National Treasury,
16   effective immediately upon the approval of this Act. Every year thereafter, there shall

18   Hundred Fifty Million Pesos (P350,000,000.00) for the implementation of this Act. All
19   such amounts appropriated pursuant to this Act shall go to the State Subsidy Fund,
