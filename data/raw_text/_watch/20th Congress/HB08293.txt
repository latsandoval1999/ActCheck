                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is a basic service and a necessary input to modern economic and
social life. However, the cost of electricity in the Philippines remains among
the highest in the region, partly due to the tax burden on fuels used in power
generation and the heavy dependence on oil-based generation in off-grid and
missionary areas.

Under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, fuels and
electricity are generally subject to the value-added tax (VAT), with only limited
exemptions and zero-rating for specific energy products and renewable power.

VAT on fuels used as primary inputs for power generation forms part of the
cost base that is ultimately recovered from consumers through generation
charges or from the public through subsidies such as the universal charge for
missionary electrification.

In Small Power Utilities Group (SPUG) and other missionary or off-grid areas,
electricity supply is typically provided by diesel and oil-fired generators
operated by the National Power Corporation–SPUG and qualified private
operators. The logistical difficulty and high cost of transporting fuel to remote
islands and isolated grids result in generation costs that may be several times
higher than those in the main grids, requiring significant subsidies to keep
tariffs at reasonable levels.

Fuel cost is the dominant component of generation cost in these areas, such
that VAT and other indirect taxes on diesel and oil-based fuels effectively
increase both the end-user tariff and the fiscal burden of subsidies.

The structure of the power sector, the regulatory pass-through of costs, and
the presence of subsidies for missionary electrification mean that VAT on
fossil fuels for generation may not function as an efficient environmental price
signal but instead contributes to high tariffs and fiscal pressures, especially
in remote and poorer communities.

This bill therefore proposes to expressly exempt from VAT:
       (a) The sale and importation of fossil fuels—coal, petroleum oils, and
natural gas—when proven to be primarily used for the generation of electricity
in the Philippines; and
       (b) The sale and importation of diesel, heavy fuel oil, and other oil-based
fuels used exclusively for power generation in SPUG areas, missionary
electrification areas, and other off-grid or isolated grids under the
government’s missionary electrification program.

The proposed exemption is narrowly targeted, conditional, and supported by
safeguards. By reducing the VAT cost embedded in fuels used for power
generation, especially in SPUG and missionary areas, this measure is
expected to moderate electricity generation costs, contribute to more
affordable tariffs, and alleviate the subsidy burden on the national
government and on-grid consumers who pay the universal charge for
missionary electrification.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
