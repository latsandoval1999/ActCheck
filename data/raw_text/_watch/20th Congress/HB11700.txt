AN ACT AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NUMBER 9510, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“CREDIT INFORMATION SYSTEM ACT”, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

A strong and reliable credit information system is essential to economic growth and financial stability.
As the Philippines continues to strengthen its financial infrastructure, it must ensure that its credit
reporting framework meets internationally recognized standards. The World Bank’s General
Principles for Credit Reporting recognize that an effective credit registry is a critical tool for
safeguarding the soundness of the financial system. These principles highlight the need for: (i) a
strong regulatory and enforcement framework; (ii) high-quality and reliable data; (iii) secure and
efficient systems and technology; and (iv) sound governance and adequate financial resources. A
well-functioning credit information system enables lenders to make informed credit decisions,
supports regulators in identifying and addressing systemic risks, and expands access to fair and
affordable credit for individuals and businesses. It also enhances market discipline, promotes
responsible lending, and reduces information asymmetry between borrowers and creditors. Viewed
through the lens of financial inclusion, prudent regulation, and evidence-based policymaking, this
measure is both timely and necessary.

Republic Act No. 9510, or the Credit Information System Act (CISA), was enacted to establish a
comprehensive and centralized credit information system for the collection and dissemination of fair
and accurate information arising from credit and credit-related activities in the financial system. To
carry out this mandate, the law created the Credit Information Corporation (CIC) as the central
repository of credit information, tasked with receiving and consolidating basic credit data and
providing access to reliable and standardized information on the credit history and financial condition
of borrowers. The CIC is a government-owned and controlled corporation (GOCC) subject to the
oversight of the Governance Commission for GOCCs (GCG), including the review of its policies,
operations, and major decisions, pursuant to Republic Act No. 10149, or the GOCC Governance Act
of 2011.

In the course of implementing the CISA, the CIC has encountered operational and institutional
challenges that affected the pace of its development. During its early years, the CIC relied
significantly on National Government support as it worked toward financial sustainability. It also
faced challenges in enforcing compliance among submitting entities, particularly those outside its
direct regulatory oversight, as well as operational constraints relating to information technology
infrastructure and data processing capacity. These experiences underscored the need to strengthen the
legal and institutional framework governing the country's credit information system. They likewise
highlighted the limitations of maintaining a credit registry independent of the country’s central
monetary authority, whose existing regulatory powers and supervisory reach are essential to ensuring
comprehensive credit data submission and effective enforcement of reporting obligations.

===== [page 2] =====
This bill seeks to amend the CISA by converting the CIC into a subsidiary of the Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas (BSP), thereby institutionalizing the establishment and maintenance of a centralized credit
information system under the BSP. The bill also transfers primary regulatory, supervisory, and
rulemaking authority from the Securities and Exchange Commission to the BSP, including
responsibility to issue implementing rules, prescribe accreditation standards, regulate access and fees,
enforce compliance, and oversee the transition and reorganization of the CIC. The proposed transfer
recognizes the BSP’s unique position as the country’s central monetary authority, bank supervisor, and
overseer of the payments and financial system. The BSP already exercises supervisory and regulatory
authority over banks and other supervised institutions, maintains extensive financial sector databases,
and possesses the technical expertise, institutional independence, and cybersecurity capabilities
necessary for the effective operation of a national credit information system. Aligning the credit
registry with the BSP’s financial stability mandate will promote more timely access to reliable credit
information, improve regulatory oversight, and support the development of a more efficient and
resilient credit market.

With this bill, the Philippines stands to benefit from embracing a globally tested institutional model. A
substantial majority of countries surveyed by the World Bank have entrusted their public credit
registries to the stewardship of their central banks or financial supervisory authorities. Moreover, in a
paper issued in 2011 by the World Bank entitled “General Principles for Credit Reporting,” the World
Bank posits that credit registries provide supervisors with an additional offsite tool for systemic risk
concentration monitoring and assessing overall portfolio quality, to identify discrepancies in borrower
ratings among banks or to identify trends in lending. It also notes that, with a very few exceptions,
credit registries are commonly owned and operated by central banks or other financial supervisors.
This international practice reflects the close relationship between credit information systems and the
broader objectives of financial stability, prudential supervision, and risk monitoring. Placing the
credit registry under the central bank enables supervisory authorities to more effectively identify
credit concentrations, emerging vulnerabilities, and interconnected risks within the financial system,
while ensuring that credit information remains subject to robust governance and data protection
standards.

This global consensus reflects the recognition that central banks, by virtue of their institutional
independence, technical expertise, and mandate to safeguard financial stability, are uniquely
positioned to oversee credit information systems in a manner that ensures security, neutrality, and
effective utilization of data for the public interest. By integrating the CIC under the control and
supervision of BSP, the BSP would gain direct access to comprehensive and reliable credit data,

strengthening its supervisory oversight, risk assessment capabilities, and evidence-based
policymaking.

At its heart, this bill is about enabling a future where every Filipino has the opportunity to participate
in a credit system that is fair, accessible, and trustworthy. By entrusting the CIC to the BSP, we pave
the way for a more inclusive financial ecosystem where data becomes a tool for empowerment rather
than exclusion, and where responsible credit becomes a call to upliftment rather than a barrier.
