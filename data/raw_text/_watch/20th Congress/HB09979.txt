                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
The integrity of the Philippine capital market is a vital engine for economic growth,
anchored by the Initial Public Offering (IPO) process. As a rigorous gatekeeper, the IPO
ensures that only financially stable and transparent companies solicit public investment.
However, the rise of "Backdoor Listings”, which occurs when private entities bypass
these rigors by acquiring dormant shell companies, presents an opportunity to modernize
standards and better safeguard small investors from asset stripping and market volatility.

The “Capital Market Integrity and Backdoor Listing Prevention Act of 2026” aims to
uphold transparency and accountability across the market. This Bill requires backdoor
listings to meet the same strict compliance standards and/or undergo the exact same strict
vetting process as a standard IPO. It likewise protects small investors by allowing them a
veto vote, also called the "majority-of-the-minority" vote, and compels acquiring entities
to reveal their true owners. In addition, it implements market safeguards, such as
immediate trading suspensions and strict asset-stripping bans, to guarantee that listing on
the Exchange remains a merit-based privilege.

In light of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT PRESCRIBING RIGID STANDARDS FOR CORPORATE
RESTRUCTURING RESULTING IN BACKDOOR LISTINGS, AMENDING FOR
 THE PURPOSE THE SECURITIES REGULATION CODE, AND PROVIDING
             PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Capital Market Integrity and
Backdoor Listing Prevention Act of 2026.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the investing
public and ensure the transparency of the capital markets. The State recognizes that while
corporate acquisitions are vital to the economy, the use of "shell companies" to
circumvent the rigorous disclosure and track-record requirements of an Initial Public
Offering (IPO) undermines market integrity.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall be
understood to mean:

   a. Acquiring Entity – refers to the non-listed person, partnership, or corporation that,
      through a transaction or series of transactions, gains control of a listed
      corporation.
   b. Backdoor Listing – occurs when a listed corporation undergoes a transaction
      resulting in a change of control in favor of a non-listed entity, a substantial change
      in business or assets, and the failure of the surviving entity to meet standard IPO
      track-record requirements at the time of the transaction.
   c. Exchange – refers to the Philippine Stock Exchange or any other organized
      market for the trading of securities.
   d. Majority of the Minority – refers to a voting requirement where a transaction must
      be approved by the majority of the shares held by stockholders other than the
      controlling interest and the acquiring entity.
   e. Re-Compliance – the process by which a corporation must prove its suitability for
      listing after a change in control or business.
   f. Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) – refers to the natural person(s) who ultimately
      owns or controls the acquiring entity.
SECTION 4. Definition of Backdoor Listing. – For purposes of this Act, a "Backdoor
Listing" occurs when a listed corporation, directly or indirectly, undergoes a transaction
or series of transactions that result in:

   1.​ A change in control of the listed corporation in favor of a non-listed person or
       entity;
   2.​ A substantial change in the primary business or assets of the listed corporation;
       and
   3.​ The surviving entity failing to meet the suitability and track-record requirements
       prescribed for an IPO at the time of the transaction.

SECTION 5. Mandatory Re-Compliance. – Any transaction deemed by the Securities
and Exchange Commission (SEC) or the Exchange as a Backdoor Listing shall be subject
to Re-Compliance Requirements, which shall include:

   a.​ Track Record: The acquiring private entity must demonstrate a minimum of three
       (3) years of profitable operations and cumulative earnings equivalent to the
       requirements of the Main Board of the Exchange.
   b.​ Public Offering Requirement: Within six (6) months of the closing of the
       transaction, the entity must conduct a public offering of at least 20% of its issued
       and outstanding capital stock to ensure price discovery and liquidity.
   c.​ Approval Threshold: The transaction must be approved by a vote of at least
       three-fourths (3/4) of the outstanding capital stock, with a mandatory "majority of
       the minority" vote requirement to protect small-scale investors.

SECTION 6. Anti-Shadow Governance Measures. –

   1.​ Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) Disclosure: No Backdoor Listing shall be
       approved without the full disclosure of the UBO of the acquiring entity to the
       third degree of consanguinity or affinity.
   2.​ Director Disqualification: Any director or officer of the acquiring entity who has
       been involved in a failed or fraudulent listing within the last ten (10) years shall
       be disqualified from holding a board seat in the surviving listed corporation.

SECTION 7. Trading Suspension. – The Exchange shall automatically impose a trading
suspension on the shares of the listed corporation upon the announcement of a
memorandum of agreement or any definitive document that may result in a Backdoor
Listing. Such suspension shall only be lifted upon the SEC’s certification that the entity
has met all "Re-Compliance" standards.

SECTION 8. Moratorium on Asset Stripping. – For a period of twenty-four (24) months
following the backdoor entry, the surviving corporation is prohibited from disposing of
more than 10% of its total assets as declared at the time of listing, unless such disposal is
in the ordinary course of business or approved by the SEC.

SECTION 9. Penalties. – Any person or corporation found to have intentionally
bypassed the IPO process through structured "shell" acquisitions to avoid disclosure shall
be liable for:

   1.​ Administrative Fines: Up to Five Hundred Million Pesos (₱500,000,000.00).
   2.​ Delisting: Permanent revocation of listing status.
   3.​ Criminal Liability: Imprisonment of seven (7) to fifteen (15) years for responsible
       officers if fraud or market manipulation is proven.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The SEC, in coordination with
the Philippine Stock Exchange, shall issue the IRR within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, or any part thereof, which is inconsistent with any of the
provisions of this Act is hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

Approved.
