                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise known as the "Paternity Leave Act of 1996," was
enacted nearly three decades ago to recognize the vital role of fathers in providing
support to their spouses during the period of recovery and the care of their
newborns. It granted a modest seven (7) days of paid leave, restricted strictly to
married male employees and limited only to the first four (4) deliveries.

Since then, our understanding of shared parental responsibilities has evolved
significantly. The enactment of Republic Act No. 11210, or the "105-Day
Expanded Maternity Leave Law," highlighted the state's commitment to maternal
and child health, but it also underscored the wide disparity in parental leave
allocations. While a mother may voluntarily transfer up to seven (7) days of her
leave to the father, the standalone benefit allocated directly to fathers remains
structurally insufficient at just one week.

Early infant care and the postpartum recovery of a mother require active,
sustained partnership. Studies consistently demonstrate that increased paternal
involvement during the neonatal stage leads to better developmental outcomes
for the child, reduces the incidence of postpartum depression in mothers, and
fosters stronger family units.

Furthermore, our laws must adapt to the shifting realities of Filipino households
by expanding protections to cover long-term cohabiting partners without the
benefit of marriage, ensuring that no child or father is penalized based on civil
status.

This Bill seeks to amend Republic Act No. 8187 by increasing the paid paternity
leave to fifteen (15) working days, removing the four-delivery cap, and extending
eligibility to common-law spouses. By investing in our fathers, we strengthen the
foundational core of our society—the Filipino family.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                    AN ACT
RECOGNIZING THE INTERTWINED ROLE OF PARENTS IN CHILD-REARING
 BY EXPANDING THE PATERNITY LEAVE BENEFITS, AMENDING FOR THE
      PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8187, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
                      "PATERNITY LEAVE ACT OF 1996"
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Expanded
Paternity Leave Act."

      SEC. 2. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 8187 is hereby amended to read as
follows:
      "SEC. 2. Notwithstanding any law, rules and regulations to the contrary,
every [married] male employee in the private and public sectors, REGARDLESS
OF EMPLOYMENT OR CIVIL STATUS, shall be entitled to a paternity leave of
[seven (7) days] FIFTEEN (15) WORKING DAYS with full pay for [the first four (4)
deliveries] ALL DELIVERIES of the legitimate spouse with whom he is cohabiting,
OR THE PARTNER WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY COHABITING IN A
COMMON-LAW RELATIONSHIP WITH NO LEGAL IMPEDIMENT TO MARRY;
PROVIDED, THAT IN THE CASE OF COMMON-LAW RELATIONSHIPS, THE
EMPLOYEE AND HIS PARTNER HAVE BEEN LIVING TOGETHER AS HUSBAND
AND WIFE FOR AT LEAST ONE (1) YEAR PRIOR TO THE DELIVERY.

      The male employee applying for paternity leave shall notify his employer of
the pregnancy of his legitimate spouse OR COMMON-LAW PARTNER and the
expected date of such delivery. For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include
childbirth, [or any] miscarriage, OR ABORTION."
      SEC. 3. Section 3 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "SEC. 3. Definition of Term. – For purpose of this Act, Paternity Leave refers
to the benefits granted to a [married] male employee allowing him not to report
for work for [seven (7) days] FIFTEEN (15) WORKING DAYS but continues to earn
the compensation therefor, consisting of basic salary and all allowances, on the
condition that his spouse OR COMMON-LAW PARTNER has delivered a child, [or]
suffered a miscarriage, OR AN ABORTION for purposes of enabling him to
effectively lend support to his [wife] PARTNER in her period of recovery and/or in
the nursing and care of the newly-born child."

       SEC. 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within thirty (30) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Labor and Employment, the
Chairperson of the Civil Service Commission, and the Secretary of Health, in
consultation with relevant stakeholders, shall review and amend the existing
rules and regulations necessary for the proper implementation of the provisions
of this Act.

      SEC. 5. Penal Provision. — Section 5 of the same Act is hereby amended to
increase the fine from Twenty-five thousand pesos (P25,000) to ONE HUNDRED
THOUSAND PESOS (P100,000) or imprisonment of not less than thirty (30) days
nor more than six (6) months, at the discretion of the court.

      SEC. 6. Non-Diminution Clause. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed
to reduce, diminish, or eliminate any existing paternity leave benefits of any form
granted under existing laws, decrees, executive orders, or any contract, collective
bargaining agreement, or policy between employer and employee.


       SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, presidential
issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
