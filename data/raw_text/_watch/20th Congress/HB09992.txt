                               EXPLANATORY NOTE
The 1987 Philippine Constitution explicitly guarantees that in all criminal prosecutions,
the accused shall be presumed innocent until the contrary is proved (Article III, Section
14(2)). This constitutional shield protects individuals from the power of the State until
guilt is proven beyond a reasonable doubt in court.

Yet, law enforcement agencies routinely parade unarraigned suspects before media
cameras in handcuffs and detention vests. This practice inflicts irreversible public
humiliation, damages reputations, and creates a trial by publicity that prejudices the
administration of justice. Even if a suspect is later cleared or acquitted, the public
vindication rarely undoes the initial televised damage.

While reporting on crime is necessary, it must not turn unconvicted citizens into a public
spectacle. This Act explicitly prohibits law enforcement from showcasing arrested
suspects to the media and public. This measure protects the constitutional rights and
human dignity of individuals under custody by imposing strict criminal, civil, and
administrative penalties for violations.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROHIBITING THE PUBLIC PARADING AND PRESENTATION OF
UNCONVICTED ARRESTED SUSPECTS TO THE MEDIA, PRESERVING THE
CONSTITUTIONAL PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE, AND PROVIDING
PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Suspect Media Protection
Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to value the
dignity of every human person and guarantee full respect for human rights.
Concomitantly, the State upholds the constitutional right of every accused to be presumed
innocent until proven guilty. Toward this end, the State shall prohibit any police or law
enforcement practices that subject unconvicted, arrested persons to public degradation,
premature public judgment, and trial by publicity through orchestrated media
presentations.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   a. "Arrested Suspect" refers to any person taken into custody by law enforcement
      officers via a warrant of arrest, or through a valid warrantless arrest under Rule
      113, Section 5 of the Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure, who has not yet been
      formally arraigned before a competent court.
   b. "Law Enforcement Officer" refers to any member of the Philippine National
      Police (PNP), the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), the Philippine Drug
      Enforcement Agency (PDEA), the Bureau of Customs (BOC), the Bureau of
      Immigration (BI), and all other government agencies tasked with law
      enforcement, investigative, and arrest capabilities.
   c. "Media Presentation or Parading" refers to the act of showcasing, presenting,
      staging, or positioning an arrested suspect to be photographed, filmed, videotaped,
      or interviewed by journalists, media networks, or the general public, including the
      broadcasting of such images on official social media channels.

SECTION 4. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any law enforcement officer,
public official, or employee to:
   1.​ Present, parade, or stage an arrested suspect before television, print, radio, or
       digital media cameras during press conferences, briefings, or organized media
       events;
   2.​ Compel, induce, or allow an arrested suspect to wear clothing, vests, or markings
       that explicitly label them as a "criminal," "suspect," or "detainee" in plain view of
       public media prior to arraignment;
   3.​ Publish, upload, or broadcast the mugshots, full names, or videos of an
       unarraigned suspect on official law enforcement social media pages or websites,
       unless such publication is strictly necessary for public safety or to facilitate the
       capture of a fugitive from justice; and
   4.​ Allow members of the media to conduct live or recorded interviews with an
       arrested suspect while under custody, unless the suspect, with the active assistance
       and written consent of independent counsel of their own choice, explicitly
       requests such an interview.

SECTION 5. Permissible Disclosures. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed as
restricting the right of the public to legitimate information regarding crime and public
safety. Law enforcement agencies may issue factual written press releases or conduct
verbal briefings containing:

   1.​ The legal name, age, and hometown of the arrested individual;
   2.​ The specific legal charges filed or to be filed against the individual;
   3.​ A factual narrative of the circumstances surrounding the arrest; and
   4.​ An inventory of items or contraband seized, provided that such items are
       photographed and presented independently, without positioning the suspect
       alongside the seized items for media documentation.

SECTION 6. Penalties. –

   A.​ Any law enforcement officer or public official who violates Section 4 of this Act
       shall, upon conviction, suffer the penalty of prision correccional in its minimum
       period (6 months and 1 day to 2 years) or a fine ranging from One Hundred
       Thousand Pesos (PHP 100,000.00) to Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP
       300,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.
   B.​ Independent of any criminal action, a violation of this Act shall constitute Grave
       Misconduct and shall be a ground for immediate suspension or dismissal from the
       government service, including the forfeiture of retirement benefits.
   C.​ The law enforcement officer or agency responsible for the unlawful presentation
       shall be liable for civil damages to the aggrieved party for injuries to reputation,
       mental anguish, and degradation of dignity under the framework of the Civil Code
       of the Philippines.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
in coordination with the Department of Justice (DOJ), the National Police Commission
(NAPOLCOM), and representatives from human rights civil society organizations, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.
SECTION 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, rules, regulations, or
circulars inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, including specific internal manuals
or operational guidelines of law enforcement agencies, are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
