                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Higher education has become an increasingly important driver of economic
competitiveness, innovation, workforce development, and international
cooperation in an interconnected global environment. Around the world,
countries have adopted comprehensive internationalization strategies to
strengthen the quality of their higher education systems, expand academic and
research collaboration, attract international students and scholars, and position
education as a strategic export sector that contributes to national development.

       The Philippines possesses significant strengths that can support a more
globally engaged and internationally competitive higher education sector. The
country's widespread use of English, strong academic traditions, growing
research capabilities, cultural openness, and strategic location within the Asia-
Pacific region provide substantial opportunities for Philippine higher education
institutions to participate more actively in international academic networks,
transnational education initiatives, and global knowledge economies. Despite
these advantages, internationalization efforts across Philippine higher education
institutions remain uneven, fragmented, and largely dependent on individual
institutional initiatives. There is currently no comprehensive statutory framework
that provides sustained national direction, dedicated financing, and coordinated
institutional support for higher education internationalization and education
export.

       At the same time, international education has emerged as a major economic
sector in many countries, generating significant investments, employment
opportunities, research collaboration, and foreign exchange earnings. The
Philippines is well-positioned to participate in this growing global market.
However, maximizing these opportunities requires deliberate public policy
interventions that strengthen institutional capacities, improve international
visibility, expand academic mobility, promote transnational education, and
ensure that international engagement contributes directly to quality enhancement
and national development objectives.

       This measure seeks to institutionalize a National Internationalization
Framework for Higher Education to provide strategic direction and continuity to
the country's internationalization efforts. It promotes a whole-of-sector approach
that integrates international, intercultural, and global dimensions into instruction,
research, extension, governance, and student development while safeguarding
academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and national interest.

       Ultimately, this proposed legislation recognizes that higher education
internationalization is not merely an academic undertaking but a strategic national
investment that can enhance educational quality, strengthen research and
innovation, improve workforce competitiveness, expand opportunities for
Filipino learners and educators, and contribute to long-term economic growth and
global engagement
       .
       In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Higher
Education Internationalization Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize higher education as a strategic national asset in an increasingly
interconnected and competitive global environment, and to actively promote the
internationalization of Philippine higher education institutions as a means of
advancing national development, social mobility, innovation, cultural exchange,
and global engagement. Towards this end, the State shall:

   a. Promote relevant, inclusive, globally competitive, and future-ready higher
      education that equips Filipino learners with internationally transferable
      skills, intercultural competence, and global perspectives, while remaining
      firmly anchored in national priorities, values, and identity;
   b. Institutionalize internationalization as a core, long-term, and strategic
      function of higher education institutions (HEIs), integrated into
      institutional governance, academic planning, quality assurance, research
      agendas, and student development, consistent with academic freedom and
      institutional autonomy;
   c. Strengthen the capacity of Philippine HEIs to engage in international
      partnerships,    academic    mobility,    transnational    education,   and
      collaborative research, ensuring that such engagements result in
      measurable improvements in teaching quality, research productivity, and
      institutional capability;
   d. Develop and position Philippine higher education as a competitive
      education export sector, capable of attracting international learners,
      scholars, researchers, and institutional partners, generating sustainable
      foreign exchange, expanding global academic networks, and enhancing the
      international reputation of Philippine education;
   e. Ensure that the benefits of internationalization and education export are
      broadly accessible to public and private HEIs, students, faculty, and
      administrators through structured public investment, capacity-building,
      and differentiated support mechanisms; and
   f. Advance internationalization at home by embedding global, intercultural,
      and comparative perspectives within domestic curricula, pedagogy,
      research, and campus life.

SEC. 3. Objectives — This Act aims to:

   a. Establish a coherent, comprehensive, and forward-looking National
      Internationalization Framework for Higher Education that provides
      strategic direction and continuity to internationalization and education
      export efforts across the higher education sector;
   b. Build    institutional   leadership,   governance    systems,    professional
      competencies, and organizational capacity for internationalization,
      including the ability of HEIs to design, manage, and sustain
      internationally marketable academic programs and partnerships;
   c. Support structured, reciprocal, and outcomes-oriented faculty, student,
      and researcher mobility programs that enhance learning quality, research
      collaboration, and the global visibility of Philippine higher education;
   d. Promote the development, recognition, and sustainability of joint, dual,
      and transnational academic programs that meet international quality
      standards and respond to global and regional workforce and skills
      demands;
   e. Enhance the global reputation, credibility, and competitiveness of
      Philippine HEIs as preferred destinations for international study, research,
      and academic collaboration; and
   f. Strengthen the contribution of higher education internationalization to the
      country’s education export strategy, innovation ecosystem, workforce
      development agenda, and long-term economic resilience.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms — As used in this Act:

   a. Academic Mobility refers to the movement of students, faculty members,
      researchers, or academic professionals across borders for purposes of
      study, teaching, research, training or academic collaboration;



                                                                                  3
b. Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) refers to digitally
   enabled, faculty-led, and curriculum-integrated international teaching and
   learning activities;
c. Dual or Double Degree Program refers to an academic program jointly
   delivered by partner institutions in different countries, resulting in the
   conferment of two separate but coordinated qualifications;
d. Education Export refers to the provision, marketing, and delivery of
   Philippine-based higher education services to international learners,
   institutions, governments, and organizations through cross-border supply,
   consumption abroad, commercial presence, and presence of natural
   persons, including student mobility, transnational higher education
   arrangements, joint and dual degree programs, collaborative research,
   digital and virtual learning, and other recognized modes of international
   education services;
e. Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) refer to public and private
   institutions duly recognized by the Commission on Higher Education;
f. Internationalization at Home (IaH) refers to a deliberate set of
   institutional policies and practices that embed international and
   intercultural learning outcomes within domestic curricula, pedagogical
   approaches, co-curricular activities, research agendas, and campus
   environments, ensuring inclusive global exposure regardless of physical
   mobility;
g. Internationalization of Higher Education refers to the intentional,
   systematic, and sustained integration of international, intercultural,
   transnational, and global dimensions into the purpose, governance, core
   functions, delivery modalities, and outcomes of higher education,
   including, but not limited to, instruction, research, extension, student

      services, quality assurance, institutional management, and external
      engagement;
   h. Joint Degree Program refers to an academic program jointly designed,
      delivered, and quality-assured by two or more higher education institutions
      in different countries, leading to the conferment of a single qualification;
      and
   i. Transnational Higher Education (TNHE) includes all types and modes
      of delivery of higher education study programs, sets of courses of study,

      State where a TNHE provider operates or programs which may operate

      located in a country different from the one where the awarding institution
      is based.



SEC. 5. National Internationalization Framework for Higher Education —
The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall institutionalize and
implement a National Internationalization Framework for Higher Education,
which shall serve as the central, authoritative, and continuing policy, planning,
coordination, and governance instrument governing all internationalization and
education export efforts across the Philippine higher education sector.

The Framework shall articulate a clear national vision for the global engagement
of Philippine higher education and shall guide public and private higher
education institutions in integrating international, intercultural, and global
dimensions into their core functions of instruction, research, extension, and
institutional governance, while safeguarding academic freedom, institutional
autonomy, and national interest.

The National Internationalization Framework for Higher Education shall:

   a. Define      strategic    national       objectives      for    higher    education
      internationalization and education export, aligned with national
      development priorities, workforce and skills strategies, research and
      innovation agendas, and the country’s broader economic, social, and
      diplomatic goals, including the enhancement of academic quality, global
      competitiveness, and institutional capacity;
   b. Identify priority markets, regions, and partner countries, as well as priority
      disciplines, programs, and fields of study with strong international

      collaboration and education export, without compromising domestic
      access, equity, and quality;

      institutional readiness, program effectiveness, and sector-wide impact,
      including indicators on international student enrollment, academic
      mobility, joint and dual degree offerings, international research
      collaboration, faculty global exposure, and education export outcomes,

   d. Promote       differentiated   and   inclusive       institutional   pathways   for

      colleges,     and    private   higher     education      institutions   to   pursue

   e. Set   governance        standards    and     institutional     arrangements     for
      internationalization, including guidance on leadership roles, internal

SEC. 6. Internationalization at Home — The Commission on Higher Education
shall actively promote and support Internationalization at Home (IaH) as a core,
inclusive, and sustainable pillar of higher education internationalization and as a
foundational strategy for strengthening institutional readiness for education
export, global engagement, and international academic collaboration.

IaH shall be pursued as a quality-enhancement, access equalizing, and system-
wide approach, ensuring that all learners, regardless of their capacity for physical

                                                                                   7
mobility, benefit from international and intercultural learning experiences within
domestic learning environments.

For this purpose, Internationalization at Home initiatives shall include, but not be
limited to, the following:

   a. Curriculum internationalization, including the integration of international,
      comparative, regional, and intercultural perspectives into program
      outcomes, course content, teaching strategies, assessment methods, and
      learning resources, consistent with outcomes-based education and quality
      assurance standards;
   b. International and intercultural learning outcomes, embedded across
      academic programs to develop global competencies, cross-cultural
      communication skills, ethical awareness, and international professional
      readiness among students;
   c. Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) and virtual exchange
      programs, including digitally enabled, faculty-led, and curriculum-
      integrated learning activities conducted jointly with partner institutions
      abroad, to expand global exposure without requiring physical travel;
   d. Joint, dual, and collaborative degree programs delivered within the
      Philippines, designed and implemented with foreign partner institutions,
      allowing students to earn internationally recognized credentials while
      studying locally, subject to applicable quality assurance, accreditation, and
      regulatory requirements;
   e. Engagement of international scholars, visiting professors, researchers, and
      practitioners, whether on-site or through digital platforms, in teaching,
      research, mentoring, curriculum development, and academic discourse, in
      accordance with applicable immigration, labor, and regulatory laws;
   f. Development of multicultural and intercultural campus ecosystems,
      including globally oriented student organizations, multilingual activities,
                                                                                   8
      international student support services, cross-cultural dialogue platforms,
      and inclusive campus programming that foster mutual understanding and
      global citizenship;
   g. Faculty capacity-building for internationalized teaching and learning,
      including training on intercultural pedagogy, international curriculum
      design, digital collaboration, and global assessment practices;
   h. Integration of Internationalization at Home into institutional quality
      assurance and academic planning, ensuring that international and
      intercultural dimensions are reflected in program review, accreditation,
      faculty evaluation, and institutional performance assessment; and
   i. Strategic linkage of Internationalization at Home initiatives to education
      export objectives, by strengthening the international credibility,
      recognition, and market readiness of Philippine academic programs,
      enhancing institutional branding, and improving the overall attractiveness
      of Philippine higher education to international learners and partners.

In implementing this section, CHED shall ensure that Internationalization at
Home initiatives are inclusive, academically grounded, quality-driven, and
aligned with national interest, and that such initiatives complement, rather than
substitute for, physical academic mobility and other forms of international
engagement.



SEC. 7. Internationalization Support Fund — There is hereby created an
Internationalization Support Fund, which shall form part of the Higher Education
Fund (HEF) and shall be administered by the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), for the purpose of supporting and strengthening the internationalization
capacity of Philippine higher education institutions and advancing the
development of the country’s education export sector: Provided, That the Fund

                                                                                9
shall serve as a dedicated and sustainable financing mechanism for programs,
projects, and activities on internationalization and education export development,
guided by the principles of equity, quality, accountability, transparency, and
strategic impact;

Provided, Further, That the Internationalization Support Fund shall be sourced
from the Higher Education Fund derived from travel taxes collected pursuant to
existing laws, consistent with Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the
Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (UAQTEA), and other
applicable laws, rules, and regulations governing the Higher Education Fund;

Provided, Finally, That at least ten percent (10%) of the annual Higher Education
Fund shall be earmarked and utilized for internationalization and education
export programs, projects, and activities authorized under this Act, which
earmarking shall be programmatic in nature, subject to the availability of funds
and existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations, and
shall be implemented without prejudice to the mandatory, entitlement-based, and
priority obligations of the Higher Education Fund under Republic Act No. 10931.

the following:

   a. Leadership development and governance capacity-building, including
      training, certification, and professional development programs for higher
      education institution presidents, vice presidents, deans, international
      relations officers, and academic leaders on internationalization strategy,

SEC. 9. Prioritization Framework for Fund Allocation— The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) shall adopt a prioritization and scoring framework for
the allocation of resources from the Internationalization Support Fund to ensure
transparency, equity, strategic alignment, and measurable impact. In establishing
such a framework, CHED shall be guided by the principle that fund allocation
shall support quality enhancement, institutional capacity-building, inclusive

                                                                                  12
participation, and the responsible development of education export, rather than
institutional size, reputation, or prior international exposure alone.

The prioritization and scoring framework shall include, but not be limited to, the
following criteria:

   a. Alignment with National Priorities — the degree to which the proposed
      program,        project,   or   activity   is    aligned   with   the   National
      Internationalization       Framework       for   Higher    Education,   national
      development plans, workforce and skills strategies, research and
      innovation priorities, and education export objectives;
   b. Academic Quality and Learning Outcomes — the extent to which the
      proposal demonstrably contributes to improved teaching and learning,
      curriculum quality, student competencies, faculty development, or
      research outputs, consistent with quality assurance and outcomes-based
      education standards;
   c. Institutional Capacity-Building Impact — the potential of the proposal to
      strengthen institutional systems, governance structures, human resource
      capacity, and long-term internationalization readiness, particularly for
      emerging or resource-constrained higher education institutions;
   d. Inclusivity and Equity Considerations — the extent to which the proposal
      promotes broad participation of students, faculty, and institutions,
      including state universities and colleges, local universities and colleges,
      regional institutions, and underserved or developing academic disciplines;
   e. Education Export Potential and Global Engagement — the degree to
      which the proposal enhances international visibility, joint or transnational
      program offerings, international partnerships, research collaboration, or
      the global market readiness of Philippine higher education programs,
      without compromising domestic access and quality;


                                                                                    13
   f. Sustainability and Replicability — the likelihood that the initiative can be
      sustained beyond initial funding, leveraged through partnerships,
      institutionalized within the HEI, or replicated by other institutions or
      sectors;
   g. Cost-Effectiveness and Fiscal Responsibility — the reasonableness of
      proposed costs in relation to expected outcomes, availability of counterpart
      funding or in-kind contributions, and efficient use of public resources; and
   h. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Risk Management — the presence of clear
      implementation plans, performance indicators, monitoring mechanisms,
      risk mitigation measures, and compliance with applicable laws, rules, and
      regulations.

CHED is hereby authorized to assign relative weights to the foregoing criteria,
prescribe minimum eligibility thresholds, and adopt differentiated scoring
methodologies based on institutional typology, program classification, or stage
of institutional or programmatic development, as may be provided in the
implementing rules and regulations.

In the implementation of this section, CHED shall ensure that the prioritization
and scoring framework is publicly disclosed, uniformly applied, and subject to
periodic review and refinement, taking into account evolving national priorities,
developments in the international higher education environment, and evidence
derived from program monitoring and evaluation.

Nothing in this section shall be construed as limiting or diminishing the
regulatory authority of the Commission on Higher Education, impairing
academic freedom or institutional autonomy, or creating any vested right,
entitlement, or proprietary interest in favor of any applicant or beneficiary.

SEC.    11.      Inter-Agency   Coordination     on   Education     Export     and
Internationalization — The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall,
within its mandate, coordinate with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)
and the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) to advance higher education
internationalization, strengthen the country’s education export sector, and
promote the global engagement of Philippine higher education institutions:
Provided, that such coordination shall respect the respective mandates,
authorities, and institutional competencies of the concerned agencies.

For this purpose, inter-agency coordination shall include, but not be limited to,
the following:

   a. Policy and Strategic Alignment. — CHED, DTI, and DFA shall endeavor
       to align higher education internationalization initiatives with national
       services export strategies, trade and investment promotion policies, and
       foreign policy objectives, including the identification of priority markets,
       regions, and modalities for education export and academic cooperation;
   b. International Promotion and Market Development. — DTI, in
       coordination with CHED, shall support the integration of higher education
       services into national and sectoral export promotion programs, including
       the provision of market intelligence, participation in international
       education fairs, and linkage with foreign partners, where applicable;
                                                                                 17
c. Diplomatic and International Engagement. — DFA, through Philippine
   foreign service posts and diplomatic channels, shall assist in promoting
   Philippine higher education institutions abroad, facilitating academic and
   research    partnerships,   and   supporting   government-to-government
   education cooperation initiatives, consistent with existing laws, protocols,
   and international commitments;
d. Facilitation of Academic Mobility and Partnerships. — The concerned
   agencies shall, within their respective mandates, support the mobility of
   students, faculty members, researchers, and academic professionals, and
   facilitate institutional partnerships, joint programs, and collaborative
   research arrangements, subject to applicable immigration, labor, and
   regulatory requirements;
e. Coordination Mechanisms. — CHED may establish appropriate
   coordination mechanisms, including technical working groups, focal
   points, or consultative platforms, thereto ensuring regular information
   exchange, policy coherence, and effective implementation of joint
   initiatives under this Act; and
f. Safeguards and Limitations. — Nothing in this section shall be construed
   as creating a new inter-agency body, mandating automatic funding
   obligations, impairing academic freedom or institutional autonomy, or
   diminishing the regulatory authority of CHED or the primary mandates of
   DTI and DFA. CHED shall ensure that inter-agency coordination efforts
   are strategic, complementary, and results-oriented, and that education
   export and global engagement initiatives contribute to academic quality
   enhancement, institutional capacity-building, and the broader national
   interest.




                                                                            18
SEC. 12. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting — The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) shall establish a monitoring and evaluation
framework and submit an annual report to Congress on internationalization and
education export outcomes, including enrollment, partnerships, and research
collaboration.



SEC. 13. Sunset Review and Impact Assessment — Five (5) years after the
effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall
conduct a comprehensive sunset review and impact assessment to evaluate the
implementation, effectiveness, and continuing relevance of this Act, including
the utilization and governance of the Internationalization Support Fund, the
achievement of intended outcomes in education export and global engagement,
the reach and impact of Internationalization at Home initiatives, consistency with
the objectives and safeguards of Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as
the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (UAQTEA), and the
responsiveness of the Act to emerging global trends, regional competition, and
developments in the international higher education environment.



SEC. 14. Appropriations — The amounts necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Commission on Higher Education, subject to the availability of funds and
applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

      Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act, including the operationalization, programming, and
utilization of the Internationalization Support Fund, shall be sourced from the

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall,
in consultation with relevant government agencies, higher education institutions,
and other stakeholders, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.



SEC. 16. Separability Clause — If any provision is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.



SEC. 17. Repealing Clause — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.



SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
