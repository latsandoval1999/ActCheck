                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      When the four-day people power uprising culminated in the ouster of
Marcos Sr. on 25 February 1986, hopes were feverishly high for the dawning
of an authentic new society. Forty years later today, these hopes have been
dashed to near extinction, dahil ang tunay na bagong lipunan ay ni hindi pa
rin nasisilayan.

     But hope springs eternal from a collective faith in the people’s capacity
to make history and effect change. Government’s failure over the decades to
genuinely and meaningfully memorialize the historic EDSA uprising has
depreciated and almost obliterated the significance of the people’s struggle
for liberation from an autocratic and oligarchic martial law regime that
exacerbated poverty and inequality.

     Indeed, we failed to institutionalize the memorialization of the EDSA
people power events. Compounding this historical blunder is President
Marcos Jr.’s downgrading of February 25 from a special non-working to a
special working holiday. Compare this with Rwanda, where the 1994
genocide is commemorated for 100 days every year. Or with Argentina,
which held a five-year trial that utilized arenas where the past was re-
enacted, serving as basis for legal judgment. Or with East Timor, where the
final report of the Timor-Leste Commission for Reception, Truth and
Reconciliation documenting human rights violations from 1975-1999 was
published in a five-volume compilation.

      Memorialization is not only a powerful, tried and tested instrument in
inculcating lessons from the past that provides a better understanding of the
currently prevailing problematic national situation.

     Memorialization also pays tribute to those who deserve to be honored.

      It is well to remember that while the EDSA people power uprising has
been lauded for being peaceful and nonviolent, the road to EDSA had been
indisputably and tragically paved with the blood and sweat of heroes and
martyrs and countless victims of gross human rights violations many of
whom remain unnamed and unrecognized. Memorialization gives them due
recognition – they who were persecuted, tortured, disappeared, incarcerated
or killed for us to navigate and utilize whatever democratic space we gained
from the EDSA uprising.

       Considering the historical importance of February 25, 1986, a law
declaring February 25 of every year as a regular national public nonworking
holiday is imperative. The law will (a) perpetuate the commemoration of the
Filipinos’ triumph against the despotism and profligacy during the darkest
era in Philippine history; (b) memorialize the heroism and sufferings of the
victims and survivors of the martial law period; (c) promote historical truth
and help guarantee non-repetition of violations of human rights; and (d)
forego the varying degrees of celebrations decreed by Philippine presidents
to the extent of ambivalence and negation.

      Declaring February 25 of every year a regular nonworking holiday is
actually a pittance. We Filipinos owe ourselves, the shoulders we stand on,
and the next generations so much more.

     Verily, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

                          AN ACT
DECLARING FEBRUARY 25 OF EVERY YEAR AS A REGULAR NATIONAL
PUBLIC NON-WORKING HOLIDAY IN COMMEMORATION OF THE
CULMINATION OF THE PEACEFUL EDSA PEOPLE POWER UPRISING,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


      SECTION 1. Culmination Day of the Peaceful EDSA People Power
Uprising. – February 25 of every year is hereby declared a regular national
public non-working holiday to commemorate, institutionalize and
memorialize the culmination of the peaceful four-day EDSA People Power
Uprising.

      SEC. 2. EDSA Commission. The EDSA Commission created pursuant
to Executive Order No. 82 (s. 1999) is hereby reconstituted to plan and
implement appropriate nationwide ceremonies for the observance of
February 25 as the culmination day of the peaceful EDSA People Power
Uprising.

      As reconstituted, the EDSA Commission shall be composed of the
following:

a.   The Executive Secretary as Chairperson;

b.   As Members, the:
        ● Chairperson of the National Historical Commission of the
          Philippines (NCHP);
        ● Chairperson of the Commission on Human Rights;
        ● Chairperson of the Human Rights Violations Victims’ Memorial
          Commission;
        ● Secretary of the Department of Interior and Local Government;
        ● Secretary of the Department of Education;
        ● Secretary of the Department of Labor and Employment;
        ● Chairperson of the Philippine Alliance of Human Rights Advocates
          (PAHRA);

     SEC. 3. Appropriations. The amount of at least Ten Million Pesos
(P10,000,000.00) annually shall be appropriated in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) and allocated to the EDSA Commission for its
operation, together with private donations. Funds accruing to and expenses
made by the EDSA Commission shall be subject to the usual accounting
rules and procedures and audit by the Commission on Audit.

      Pending inclusion in the GAA of the appropriate funds for the EDSA
Commission, its operation shall be funded from the savings of the Office of
the President and/or from the contingent fund under the control of the
President.

       SEC. 4. Separability Clause. – If any part, section or provision of this
Act is held invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 5. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 6. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
