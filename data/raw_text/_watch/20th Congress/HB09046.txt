                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE



The municipal fisheries sector is the cornerstone of Philippine food security and coastal
resilience, yet it remains among the most vulnerable and marginalized in the archipelago.
Despite the critical role of municipal fisherfolk in feeding the nation, data from the
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA, 2021)1 reveals a staggering poverty incidence rate
of approximately 30.6% among fisherfolk—a figure significantly higher than the national
average. These communities are currently caught in a systemic "triple threat" cycle of
resource depletion, climate-induced disasters, and widespread displacement due to
coastal reclamation, tourism expansion, and large-scale infrastructure projects.

The Necessity and Urgency

The precipitous decline in fisherfolk productivity underscores the necessity for legislative
intervention. Recent reports indicate that the average daily catch per fisher has
plummeted from a sustainable 10–20 kg/day in previous decades to a mere 2.5 kg/day
due to overfishing and illegal commercial encroachment within municipal waters. 2
Compounding this economic crisis is the physical insecurity of these communities;
thousands of families reside in high-risk "coastal danger zones" without legal tenure,
leaving them uniquely vulnerable to the intensifying typhoons and rising sea levels
associated with the Philippine climate.

Displacement, often framed as "development," frequently strips fisherfolk of their
proximity to traditional fishing grounds and landing sites. Without the "Kasaganaan sa
Baybayin Act," these families remain in a state of perpetual precarity, unable to invest in
their homes or livelihoods due to the constant threat of eviction. There is an immediate
and urgent need to institutionalize "Fisherfolk Settlement Areas" (FSAs) to provide secure
tenure and ensure that coastal development does not come at the expense of our nation's
food producers.

Constitutional and International Basis

This proposed legislation is firmly anchored in both the supreme law of the land and
established international human rights and development frameworks:

        Constitutional Mandate: The bill draws its authority from Article XIII, Section 7 of
         the 1987 Philippine Constitution, which mandates that the State shall protect the
         rights of subsistence fisherfolk to the preferential use of local marine and fishing
         resources. This Act operationalizes the principle that "those who have less in life
         should have more in law" by ensuring that coastal land-use policies prioritize the
         housing and livelihood needs of the municipal fishing sector.
        International Alignment: The measure aligns with the Food and Agriculture
         Organization (FAO) Voluntary Guidelines for Securing Sustainable Small-Scale
         Fisheries (SSF Guidelines), which urge states to ensure tenure security, prohibit
         forced evictions, and facilitate equitable access to resources to promote
         sustainable small-scale fishing practices (FAO, 2015).3
        Global Sustainability: This Act advances the United Nations Sustainable
         Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 14 (Life Below Water), which
         advocates for access for small-scale artisanal fishers to marine resources, and
         SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities), which promotes inclusive, resilient,
         and safe human settlements (United Nations, 2015).4

Key Legislative Mechanisms

To address these challenges, the Kasaganaan sa Baybayin Act introduces several critical
innovations:

   1. Certificate of Compliance (COC): A mandatory legal requirement for any project—
      public or private—impacting coastal communities, ensuring that displacement is
      not arbitrary and that livelihood transition is fully funded.
   2. Inter-Agency Committee on Fisherfolk Settlement Areas (IACFSA): An
      institutional mechanism that ensures national policy coherence across the DA-
      BFAR, DHSUD, DENR, and other key agencies, with direct participation from the
      fisherfolk themselves.
   3. Climate-Resilience Standards: The bill mandates that all settlements must be
      designed for climate-adaptation, including elevated structures, coastal green
      buffer zones (mangroves), and disaster-resilient evacuation infrastructure.

Conclusion

The "Kasaganaan sa Baybayin Act" is not merely a housing initiative; it is a human rights
imperative. By integrating secure land tenure with climate-resilient infrastructure and
mandated livelihood protections, the State provides a mechanism for long-term
community empowerment. This Act ensures that municipal fisherfolk—the guardians of
our marine heritage—are granted the legal protection to remain on their land, secure in
their right to continue their traditional livelihoods, and resilient against the existential
threats posed by climate change.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Kasaganaan sa Baybayan Act.”

 2   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to fulfill
 3   its social justice mandate by ensuring the security, dignity, and economic stability of
 4   municipal fisherfolk through the following measures:

 5   (a) Institutionalization of Preferential Rights: The State shall explicitly recognize and
 6   protect the statutory preferential rights of municipal fisherfolk to the use and
 7   management of municipal waters and coastal resources, ensuring that any competing
 8   commercial, industrial, or tourism-related use remains legally subordinate to the
 9   subsistence and livelihood requirements of local fisherfolk, in accordance with Section
10   7, Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution.

11   (b) Guarantee of Secure and Resilient Tenure: The State shall establish a permanent,
12   climate-resilient, and sustainable housing framework that guarantees legal security of

                                                                                                  1
 1   tenure for municipal fisherfolk, prioritizing their protection against natural hazards
 2   through the mandatory integration of disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation
 3   standards in all housing projects, consistent with the right of every citizen to adequate
 4   housing and the State's duty to provide for the vulnerable.

 5   (c) Mandatory Safeguards Against Arbitrary Displacement: The State shall prohibit the
 6   displacement of fisherfolk communities from their ancestral and customary landing
 7   sites and settlements due to reclamation, tourism, commercial expansion, or
 8   conservation zoning, unless such relocation is proven to be for a compelling public
 9   interest and is accompanied by a legally binding, transparent, and participatory
10   relocation and livelihood restoration plan that is demonstrably superior to their original
11   living conditions, in line with the prohibition of forced evictions under R.A. No. 7279.

12   (d) Integrated Planning and Resource Management: The State shall mandate the
13   seamless integration of Fisherfolk Settlement Areas into all local land-use plans,
14   Comprehensive Land Use Plans, coastal resource management programs, and national
15   housing development agendas, ensuring that residential settlements are strictly
16   proximal to primary fishing grounds and essential support infrastructure to prevent the
17   degradation of traditional livelihoods.

18   (e) Participatory Governance: The State shall institutionalize the compulsory
19   participation of accredited fisherfolk organizations and Fisheries and Aquatic
20   Resources Management Councils at every stage of the planning, design, monitoring,
21   and implementation of settlement policies, acknowledging their role as primary
22   decision-makers and stewards of their own communities and aquatic domains.

23   (f) Sustainable Development and Climate Action: The State shall adopt a holistic
24   approach to coastal development that harmonizes economic growth with ecological
25   integrity, ensuring that all fisherfolk settlements incorporate ecosystem-based
26   management and climate-resilience strategies to mitigate the impacts of sea-level rise
27   and extreme weather events, as part of the country’s commitment to the United Nations
28   Sustainable Development Goals.

29   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms

30   For this Act, the following terms are defined as follows:

31   (a) Municipal Fisherfolk refers to persons—including women and marginalized gender
32   groups—who are directly or indirectly engaged in the entire ﬁsheries value and supply
33   chain. This encompasses the taking, culturing, post-harvest handling, ﬁsh vending,
34   marketing, and further processing of ﬁshery or aquatic products. To be recognized
35   under this deﬁnition, such individuals must reside in the municipality or city and be duly
36   registered in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry (FishR) as maintained by the local
37   government unit, pursuant to the inclusive standards set by the Bureau of Fisheries and
38   Aquatic Resources.

                                                                                                  2
 1   (b) Fisherfolk Settlement Areas (FSAs) refer to contiguous areas of public land,
 2   government-owned property, or reclaimed land specifically designated, zoned, and
 3   developed for the permanent residential use of municipal fisherfolk, which must feature
 4   direct, unobstructed physical and legal access to the shoreline, traditional fishing
 5   grounds, and necessary fisheries-related infrastructure.

 6   (c) Fish Landing Site refers to a functional coastal infrastructure or designated segment
 7   of the shoreline where fishing vessels are permitted to dock, and where catch is
 8   unloaded, weighed, sorted, and distributed. This site shall be recognized as a public
 9   utility and protected from any obstruction, privatization, or unauthorized conversion that
10   would impede the traditional docking rights of municipal fishers.

11   (d) Security of Tenure refers to the legal right of a beneficiary to occupy and reside in a
12   designated Fisherfolk Settlement Area, which is established and protected through
13   formal legal instruments—including but not limited to, usufruct agreements,
14   stewardship contracts, community mortgage programs, or cooperative housing titles—
15   that protect against arbitrary eviction, dispossession, or displacement.

16   (e) Resettlement refers to the government-mandated, planned, and managed transfer of
17   fisherfolk households from their current location to an alternative site, which shall only
18   be permitted when the current site is certified as a hazard-prone or danger zone by the
19   Mines and Geosciences Bureau or equivalent authority, or when the continued
20   occupancy is rendered legally or physically impossible, provided that such relocation
21   complies with the participatory, livelihood-preserving standards mandated by this Act.

22   (f) Just Transition Measures refers to a comprehensive suite of social protection,
23   technical training, and financial assistance programs designed to provide immediate
24   relief and long-term economic stability to fisherfolk households whose livelihoods are
25   negatively impacted by spatial reallocation, infrastructure development, or
26   environmental conservation measures. These measures must include, at a minimum,
27   income replacement, compensation for capital assets, and the facilitation of alternative
28   or enhanced fishing-related employment.

29   (g) Municipal Waters include not only streams, lakes, inland bodies of water and tidal
30   waters within the municipality which are not included within the protected areas as
31   deﬁned under Republic Act No. 7586 (The NIPAS Law), public forest, timber lands, forest
32   reserves or ﬁshery reserves, or part of ancestral domain as deﬁned under Republic Act
33   8371 (IPRA), but also marine waters included between two (2) lines drawn
34   perpendicular to the general coastline from points where the boundary lines of the
35   municipality touch the sea at low tide and a third line parallel with the general coastline
36   including offshore islands and ﬁfteen (15) kilometers from such coastline, subject to
37   the following rules:
38      1. For cities or municipalities with generally straight coastlines, the distance shall
39         be reckoned from the low-water line along the mainland shoreline;

                                                                                                   3
 1      2. For cities or municipalities composed wholly or partly of islands, islets, or with
 2         irregular coastlines, the distance shall be reckoned from the coastline of the
 3         outermost island or islet, applying the archipelagic principle; and
 4      3. Where two (2) municipalities are so situated on opposite shores that there is less
 5         than thirty (30) kilometers of marine waters between them, the third line shall be
 6         equally distant from opposite shore of the respective municipalities.
 7   This delineation is for ﬁsheries governance and local administrative purposes and shall
 8   not affect national maritime baselines.

 9   SECTION 4. Beneficiaries. The primary beneficiaries of this Act shall be registered
10   municipal fisherfolk households, as evidenced by their active entry in the Municipal
11   Fisherfolk Registry maintained by the Local Government Unit (LGU). The Municipal
12   Fisherfolk Registry shall be a public document, updated annually, and subjected to a
13   mandatory community validation process involving the Municipal/City Fisheries and
14   Aquatic Resources Management Council (M/C-FARMC) to ensure the accuracy and
15   integrity of the list.

16   Priority in the allocation of housing and tenure support shall be strictly granted to the
17   following households:

18   (a) Households currently occupying coastal areas formally classified as "danger zones"
19   or "high-risk areas" by the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) or the appropriate
20   national disaster risk management authority;

21   (b) Households experiencing actual or threatened displacement due to state-sanctioned
22   infrastructure projects, private coastal developments, tourism expansion, or the
23   implementation of protected area/conservation zoning;

24   (c) Households currently possessing no legal security of tenure, including those living
25   as informal settlers on public or private land within the municipal coastal zone;

26   (d) Members of organized fisherfolk associations, cooperatives, or federations duly
27   accredited by the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Cooperative
28   Development Authority (CDA), or the Department of Agriculture-Bureau of Fisheries and
29   Aquatic Resources (DA-BFAR).

30   In the implementation of this Section, all Local Government Units are mandated to
31   ensure that registration and tenure documentation are gender-responsive and
32   accessible. This includes the recognition of both husband and wife, or the primary
33   fisher-household head regardless of gender, as co-tenants or co-beneficiaries in all
34   tenure instruments. Any form of gender-based discrimination, including the exclusion of
35   women fishers who engage in post-harvest or processing activities, shall be strictly
36   prohibited. Any violation of the beneficiary identification protocols herein shall be

 3   SECTION 5. Establishment of Fisherfolk Settlement Areas (FSAs). The DA-BFAR, in
 4   close coordination with the DHSUD, the DENR, and the concerned Local Government
 5   Unit (LGU), shall identify, validate, and designate suitable public lands as Fisherfolk
 6   Settlement Areas (FSAs) within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of
 7   this Act. If no suitable public land is available within the municipality, the LGU, with the
 8   assistance of the DHSUD and DA-BFAR, shall pursue land acquisition, land banking, or
 9   lease-to-own arrangements specifically for the purpose of establishing FSAs.

10   All designated FSAs shall comply with the following mandatory standards:

11   (a) Proximity and Access: The settlement must be located within a radius that
12   guarantees rapid, safe, and unobstructed physical access to traditional fishing grounds
13   and designated fish landing sites. The LGU and the DA-BFAR shall certify that no
14   infrastructure or private enclosure shall impede the fisherfolk's daily transit between the
15   settlement and the sea.

16   (b) Provision of Essential Services and Infrastructure: The State shall ensure the
17   provision of basic services, including potable water, sanitation, electrification, and
18   connectivity. Furthermore, each FSA shall be equipped with integrated livelihood
19   support facilities, such as cold storage, fish drying areas, and boat repair centers, to
20   promote the economic viability of the community.

21   (c) Security of Tenure and Protection from Displacement: All residents within the
22   designated FSAs shall be granted secure, legally recognized tenure instruments. The
23   settlements shall be protected from any form of arbitrary displacement, demolition, or
24   land-use conversion. Any modification to the settlement status shall require a prior,
25   transparent, and participatory public hearing with the residents and the Municipal/City
26   FARMC.

27   (d) Environmental Sustainability and Climate Resilience: All FSAs shall be designed
28   and developed in strict adherence to climate-resilient engineering standards. This
29   includes the mandatory avoidance of high-risk coastal hazard zones, the
30   implementation of elevation strategies for housing structures, the establishment of
31   coastal green buffers, and the integration of ecosystem-based disaster risk reduction
32   measures, such as mangrove reforestation and coral reef protection, to mitigate the
33   impacts of sea-level rise and extreme weather events.

34   SECTION 6. Right to a Secured Settlement. Qualified beneficiaries shall be granted
35   secure tenure through recognized legal instruments, including usufruct agreements,
36   stewardship contracts, community mortgage programs, cooperative housing
37   agreements, or other tenure arrangements recognized under housing and agrarian


                                                                                                    5
 1   reform laws. In addition to the right to secure tenure, municipal fisherfolk shall be
 2   entitled to the following rights:

 3   (a) Protection Against Arbitrary Displacement: Municipal fisherfolk shall not be
 4   arbitrarily displaced from their settlements or traditional fishing grounds. Any project—
 5   whether public or private—that impacts these communities must satisfy the following:

 6      1. Conduct of a mandatory, free, prior, and informed consultation with the affected
 7         community and the local FARMC;
 8      2. Proof of exhaustion of all alternatives to displacement;
 9      3. Provision of a legally binding relocation plan that ensures livelihood continuity
10         and accessibility to fishing resources.

11   (b) Right to Remain in Established Settlements: No municipal fisherfolk shall be
12   uprooted from their established settlements or denied access to their customary fishing
13   areas without a lawful, participatory, and transparent procedure. Any displacement
14   undertaken in violation of this provision shall be deemed void ab initio. Responsible
15   officials and private proponents shall be subject to administrative, civil, and criminal
16   liability, without prejudice to the rights of the affected individuals to immediate
17   restitution, reinstatement, or just damages.

18   (c) Safeguarding of Customary Access in Spatial Reallocation: Any spatial reallocation
19   within municipal waters for tourism, reclamation, or conservation zoning must maintain
20   customary access areas for registered municipal fisherfolk. Such access shall only be
21   restricted if strictly required for safety or ecological conservation, and only upon the
22   prior implementation of "Just Transition Measures." These measures must include, at a
23   minimum, sustainable livelihood restoration, alternative landing site infrastructure, and
24   fair economic compensation.

25   (d) Prohibition of Forced Evictions: Forced eviction from settlements, fish landing sites,
26   or traditional fishing areas is strictly prohibited, except in the specific cases authorized
27   by Republic Act No. 7279 and other applicable statutes. Where eviction or relocation is
28   deemed lawful and unavoidable, the State shall guarantee:

29      1. The provision of adequate, suitable, and safe resettlement sites located within a
30         reasonable distance from primary fishing grounds;
31      2. The delivery of just, fair, and transparent compensation and rehabilitation
32         assistance;
33      3. Meaningful participation of the affected fisherfolk in all stages of the planning
34         and implementation of the relocation;
35      4. The preservation of continued access to fishing resources to the maximum
36         extent possible.

10   (LGUs) shall mandatorily integrate designated Fisherfolk Settlement Areas (FSAs) into

13   Coastal Resource Management (CRM) overlay. The LGU, through its Local Planning and

18   SECTION 9. Role of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management Councils
19   (FARMCs). The Municipal and City FARMCs shall serve as the primary institutional
20   partner of the LGU in all stages of FSA implementation. Their responsibilities include,
21   but are not limited to:

22   (a) Conducting independent validation of the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry to confirm
23   beneficiary eligibility;

24   (b) Providing formal recommendations for the selection and identification of potential
25   settlement sites;

26   (c) Monitoring the construction and development of settlements to ensure adherence to
27   standards; and

28   (d) Leading community-based efforts in the protection, conservation, and management
29   of traditional fishing grounds and marine protected areas (MPAs) adjacent to the FSA.

30   SECTION 10. Climate-Resilient Fisherfolk Settlements. All FSAs shall be designed and
31   constructed to comply with the National Building Code and the latest climate-
32   adaptation standards. Mandatory measures include:

33   (a) Site Selection: Absolute prohibition against siting settlements in areas identified by
34   the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) as having high susceptibility to storm surges,
35   tsunamis, or liquefaction;


                                                                                               9
 1   (b) Structural Integrity: Housing units shall be typhoon-resilient, designed for elevated
 2   construction to account for projected sea-level rise;

 3   (c) Ecosystem-Based Protection: LGUs shall implement a mandatory coastal green
 4   buffer zone, including the restoration of mangroves and beach forests to serve as
 5   natural storm-surge barriers;

 6   (d) Emergency Infrastructure: Every FSA shall be equipped with dedicated, accessible,
 7   and disaster-resilient evacuation centers or multi-purpose halls designed to withstand
 8   super-typhoon wind speeds.

 9   SECTION 11. Settlement Planning Standards. The IACFSA shall issue the "Minimum
10   Planning and Design Standards for Fisherfolk Settlements" within one hundred eighty
11   (180) days of this Act’s effectivity. These standards shall cover:

12   (a) Suitability and Accessibility: Proximity standards that mandate settlement location
13   within a five (5) kilometer radius of the primary landing site or, where terrain dictates
14   otherwise, the provision of a dedicated shuttle/access route;

15   (b) Land Allocation: Minimum square-meterage requirements per household that
16   account for gear storage, vessel maintenance, and home-based post-harvest
17   processing;

18   (c) Cultural and Structural Appropriateness: Housing designs that prioritize cross-
19   ventilation, flood-resilience, and adequate space for the storage of fishing equipment,
20   constructed using materials compliant with local hazard profiles.

21   SECTION 12. Fisheries Support Facilities. Each FSA shall be integrated with dedicated
22   fisheries support infrastructure to ensure economic viability. The LGU and DA-BFAR
23   shall ensure the provision of:

24   (a) Standardized fish landing, docking, and mooring facilities;

25   (b) Sanitary fish drying, processing, and solar-tunnel dryer areas;

26   (c) Cold storage and ice production plants to reduce post-harvest losses;

27   (d) Secured boat storage and maintenance workshops;

28   (e) Controlled fish trading centers that provide fair-market access to fisherfolk,
29   preventing monopolistic middleman practices.

30   SECTION 13. Community Infrastructure. The State, in partnership with the LGU, shall
31   ensure the "Progressive Realization of Community Infrastructure." Each FSA shall be
32   provided with:

 1   (a) 24/7 access to potable water and centralized sanitation/sewage treatment systems;

 2   (b) All-weather access roads linking the FSA to primary municipal arteries;

 3   (c) Functional community health centers and primary education facilities within a

 5   (d) Sustainable energy solutions, including off-grid solar micro-grids where traditional

 7   SECTION 14. Congressional Oversight. The IACFSA shall submit an annual report to
 8   Congress through the Committees on Fisheries and Aquatic Resources and Housing of

10   development; number of beneficiary households; funding utilization; challenges and

12   SECTION 15. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. The following acts are hereby prohibited
13   and shall be subject to the corresponding penalties:

14   (a) Violation of the Right to a Secured Settlement: Any person or entity that violates the
15   security of tenure of a qualified municipal fisherfolk beneficiary, as defined in Section 6,
16   shall be punished by an administrative fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (PHP
17   50,000.00) but not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP 100,000.00), and
18   imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years. In addition, the offender shall pay a penalty
19   equivalent to double the administrative fine imposed.

20   (b) Large-Scale Displacement of Fisherfolk: Any person or entity that causes the
21   unlawful displacement of ten (10) or more fisherfolk households, or the obstruction of
22   access to traditional fishing grounds for an entire barangay or community, shall be
23   liable as follows:

24      1. Administrative Penalty: A fine of Five Million Pesos (PHP 5,000,000.00) to Ten
25         Million Pesos (PHP 10,000,000.00).
26      2. Criminal Penalty: Imprisonment of six (6) to twelve (12) years, and a criminal fine
27         equivalent to twice the administrative fine imposed.
28      3. Juridical Liability: If the offender is a corporation, partnership, or other juridical
29         person, the penalty of cancellation of the registration and permit to operate shall
30         be imposed. The officers, directors, or representatives who authorized the act, as
31         well as the individuals who participated in its execution, shall be held personally,
32         criminally, and solidarily liable.
33      4. Civil Liability: The offender shall be liable for damages to life and property of the
34         displaced communities, and shall shoulder the full cost of environmental
35         restoration or rehabilitation of the damaged fishery resources based on the
36         Philippine Ecosystem and Natural Capital Accounting System (PENCAS)
37         standards.

                                                                                                11
 1   (c) Unlawful Closure of Access: The obstruction or blockage of established fish landing
 2   sites, traditional boat routes, or common access points by any person, public or private,
 3   without a valid permit or legal order, is prohibited. Violators shall be punished by an
 4   administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PHP 50,000.00) to One Hundred Thousand
 5   Pesos (PHP 100,000.00), and imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years. The offender
 6   shall also be liable for the total cost of removing the obstruction. This penalty is without
 7   prejudice to separate criminal liability if the act is attended by physical injury,
 8   intimidation, or harassment.

 9   (d) Falsification or Manipulation of Registry Data: Any person who willfully falsifies,
10   tampers with, or manipulates the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry to secure benefits or
11   deprive a qualified beneficiary of their rights under this Act shall be punished by an
12   administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PHP 50,000.00) to One Hundred Thousand
13   Pesos (PHP 100,000.00), and imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years. A fine equivalent
14   to twice the administrative fine shall also be imposed.

15   SECTION 16. Moratorium on the Resettlement of Municipal Fisherfolk

16   (a) General Prohibition: Upon the effectivity of this Act, a national moratorium is hereby
17   imposed on the forced relocation or resettlement of municipal fisherfolk from their
18   existing settlements, traditional landing sites, or customary fishing grounds. No such
19   resettlement shall be undertaken during the period of implementation of this Act, except
20   in cases where a formal certification from the Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB) or
21   the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS) identifies the
22   current site as being in a state of imminent environmental danger or high-risk for man-
23   made disasters, rendering the area completely uninhabitable.

24   (b) Suspension of Pending Projects: All ongoing or pending development projects—
25   whether public or private—that would result in the displacement of municipal fisherfolk
26   are hereby suspended by operation of law. This suspension shall remain in effect until
27   the project proponent secures a Certificate of Compliance (COC) from the IACFSA,
28   certifying that the project has fully integrated the protections, tenure security, and just
29   transition measures mandated by this Act.

30   (c) Requirements for Resumption: Proponents of suspended projects may seek the
31   lifting of the moratorium only by submitting a formal "Undertaking of Compliance" to the
32   IACFSA. This undertaking must include:

33      1. A validated, participatory relocation plan that identifies a new site with equal or
34         better access to traditional fishing grounds;
35      2. A financial guarantee, in the form of a performance bond, to cover the costs of
36         livelihood transition, relocation assistance, and infrastructure development at the
37         new site;



                                                                                               12
 1      3. Proof of written consent and a signed agreement with the affected fisherfolk
 2         households and the local FARMC, obtained through an independent, third-party
 3         verified consultation process.

 4   SECTION 17. Appropriations. The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
 5   charged against existing appropriations of implementing agencies. Thereafter, funding
 6   shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

 7   SECTION 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within one hundred eighty
 8   (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the IACFSA, as the primary policy-making
 9   body, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to ensure the effective
10   implementation of this Act.

11   (a) Drafting Committee: The IACFSA shall form an IRR Drafting Committee chaired by
12   the DA-BFAR and the DHSUD, with the legal counsel of the Department of Justice (DOJ)
13   serving as an ex-officio member to ensure consistency with existing statutes. The
14   Secretariat of the IACFSA shall serve as the primary administrative support for the
15   drafting process.

16   (b) Multi-Stakeholder Consultation: In the formulation of the IRR, the Committee shall
17   conduct at least three (3) regional consultative workshops and one (1) national summit.
18   The Committee is mandatorily required to engage the following stakeholders:

19      1. The National Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management Council (NFARMC);
20      2. Accredited provincial and municipal federations of fisherfolk organizations;
21      3. Civil society organizations (CSOs) specializing in human rights, land tenure, and
22         coastal resource management;
23      4. Representatives from the League of Municipalities of the Philippines (LMP); and
24      5. Experts from the academe and the scientific community regarding climate-
25         resilient coastal engineering.

26   (c) Mandatory Contents of the IRR: The IRR shall, at a minimum, prescribe:

27      1. Standardized procedures for the identification, validation, and awarding of
28         Fisherfolk Settlement Areas;
29      2. Detailed technical specifications for "climate-resilient" coastal housing and
30         support infrastructure;
31      3. Uniform guidelines for the "Just Transition Measures" and livelihood restoration
32         packages;
33      4. Simplified, accessible, and gender-responsive protocols for the registration of
34         beneficiaries in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry; and
35      5. Procedures for the exercise of the IACFSA’s quasi-judicial powers in resolving
36         disputes and adjudicating claims for violations of this Act.

 3   SECTION 19. Rule of Statutory Construction. Any doubt in the interpretation,
 4   application, or implementation of any provision of this Act shall be resolved in favor of
 5   the municipal fisherfolk, consistent with the constitutional mandate of social justice and
 6   the protection of the preferential rights of subsistence fisherfolk to the use of marine
 7   and fishing resources. The provisions of this Act shall be liberally construed to ensure
 8   the realization of the State’s duty to provide security of tenure and livelihood protection
 9   to the sector.

10   SECTION 20. Separability Clause. If any provision, section, or part of this Act is declared
11   unconstitutional, invalid, or inconsistent with the Constitution or other existing laws by a
12   court of competent jurisdiction, such judgment shall not affect, impair, or invalidate the
13   remainder of this Act. The remaining provisions not affected by such declaration shall
14   continue to be in full force and effect.

15   SECTION 21. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
16   proclamations, rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the
17   provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly. Provided
18   that the provisions of Republic Act No. 8550 (The Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998), as
19   amended, and Republic Act No. 7279 (Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992)
20   shall remain in force, except to the extent that they are inconsistent with the specific
21   protections and mandates provided under this Act.

22   SECTION 22. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
23   publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation
