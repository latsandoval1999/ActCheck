EXPLANATORY NOTE

‘In health, there is freedom. Health is the first of all liberties,” said Swiss
philosopher Henri Frédéric Amiel. It is a woeful reality for some persons deprived of
liberty (PDLs) that they are shackled not only by their being offenders serving sentence
or as detainees awaiting trial for an offense they may not have at all committed, but also
doubly restrained by severe health condition.

This bill seeks to legislate Compassionate Early Release (CER) to afford PDLs
with life-limiting illness, significant functional impairment, complex medical care needs
that cannot be effectively addressed by the penitentiary or correctional institution
particularly the elderly, women and juveniles a chance to return to their family and
community and live productive and meaningful lives or spend their remaining days with
dignity in the company of their loved ones.

CER is imperative as it is a measure of compassion consistent not only with the
constitutionally declared policy on the value of dignity and full respect for human rights
but also with the Universal Health Care Act that seeks to improve access to appropriate,
affordable and quality health care services for every Filipino. The law does not
discriminate against institutionalized populations including PDLs who are more
vulnerable to health service deprivation due to inherent security and legal barriers. No
hapless Filipino rendered helpless by severe health and medical status deserves to

languish in jail.

Significantly as well is the fact that CER would contribute to solving the critical
problem of congestion in jails and correctional institutions.

Overcrowding has long hounded the country’s penitentiary system. The average
congestion rate of all correctional facilities stands at 321% of the facilities’ capacity or
more than three (3) times their budgeted capacity. There is only one (1) correctional
officer to twenty-four (24) PDLs in the Bureau of Corrections. The already meagre
health care budget is further constrained by the huge number of PDLs.

===== [page 2] =====
Congestion necessarily restricts mobility and productivity which negatively
impacts the mental health and overall well-being of PDLs. The dire situation likewise
escalates the infection risk for communicable diseases and heightens the risk of
exacerbation of disease for those with non-communicable diseases due to the poor
living conditions and lack of access to regular health care. This multiplies mortality or
morbidity of PDLs that can be thirty (30) times higher than that of the non-PDL
population.

Verily, decongesting places of deprivation of liberty through CER is beneficial to
both the released PDLs and those who remain in the penal or correctional facility.

Reducing the inordinately large number of PDLs would help government cut
costs on food, water, health and medical care and allied services, and expend the freed
funds to improve the living condition of the PDLs left behind.

Highlighting the value of CER, former Justice Secretary Jesus Crispin Remulla
posits that the mechanism “...reaffims our commitment to justice, human dignity and
rehabilitation...”

Mr. Remulla’s message brings to light the distinction between CER and medical
parole. The two processes, although both mostly based on health and medical grounds,
are not identical. The proposed CER which is heavily anchored on human rights and
dignity, is based solely on the PDLs current health and medical condition as well as the
corresponding prognosis regardless of the status of serving sentence or length of
detention or deprivation of liberty. Whereas parole is the conditional release of a PDL
before the full completion of a sentence on the undertaking of good behavior or non-
commission of a crime after the release. In the case of a PDL on CER, they are unlikely
to return to lawbreaking as they have been effectively or fully rehabilitated or too ill to
have the capacity or energy to reoffend.

The proposed law underscores appropriate, holistic, and inclusive rehabilitation
of PDLs as it is guided by studies’ findings that inmates who undergo rehabilitation
programs are less likely to recommit crimes and go back to prison compared to those
who do not participate in the program.

Congress should not temporize in actualizing deliverance of deserving PDLs
through CER.

Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR COMPASSIONATE EARLY RELEASE TO QUALIFIED
PERSONS DEPRIVED OF LIBERTY

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Compassionate Early
Release Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State values the dignity of all human
persons and guarantees full respect for all human rights. Among these equal and
inalienable rights is the right to health. Its enjoyment shall be guaranteed through timely
access to quality and affordable health care services regardless of the socio-economic,
political, or legal status of the beneficiaries. Needless to stress, these include persons
deprived of liberty (PDLs) particularly those suffering from terminal illness and physical
or mental disability, specially among the elderly, women, and juveniles.

The State is committed to apply and implement the Nelson Mandela Rules or the
United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, specifically
Section 24 thereof, which states that “The provision of health care for prisoners is a
Stafe responsibility. Prisoners should enjoy the same standards of health care that are
available in the community and should have access to necessary health care services
free of charge without discrimination on the grounds of their legal status.” Also
according to the World Health Organization, “the State has a fundamental duty of care
fo ensure the health and well-being of Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDLs)”. This
responsibility requires providing consistent and adequate health and psychosocial
support throughout detention and confinement ensuring a smooth transition to
community-based services after release. Such continuity is essential not only for

===== [page 4] =====
protecting the rights and dignity of PDLs but also for supporting their reintegration into
society and upholding broader public health and human rights obligations.

Consistent with Article 40 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the
Child, the State establishes a comprehensive juvenile justice and welfare system that
promotes holistic rehabilitation which upholds the child’s dignity and worth as a human
person. Pursuant to the United Nations Rules for the Protection of Juveniles Deprived of
Liberty (Havana Rules), the State acknowledges that “..Deprivation of the liberty of a
juvenile should be a disposition of last resort and for the minimum necessary period and
should be limited to exceptional cases. The length of the sanction should be determined
by the judicial authority, without precluding the possibility of his or her early release.”

The State recognizes the differential impact of incarceration on women,
particularly in situations of extreme vulnerability or crisis. In line with international
human rights standards, including the United Nations Rules for the Treatment of
Women Prisoners and Non-custodial Measures for Women Offenders (Bangkok Rules),
the State shall adopt gender-responsive and equitable measures in the implementation
of compassionate early release (CER). The CER mechanism shall take into account
women’s reproductive and caregiving roles, their specific health needs, and the unique
socio-economic and psychological pathways that often lead to their involvement in the
criminal justice system such as experiences of gender-based violence, coercion,
poverty, and discrimination.

A rights-based approach shall guide all CER proceedings, ensuring that the
mechanism is not only a matter of procedural justice but also an instrument of
restorative and inclusive justice. Special consideration shall be given to women who are
pregnant, primary caregivers of young children, elderly, terminally ill, or survivors of
abuse, in accordance with the principles of proportionality, non-discrimination, and
human dignity.

In general, in implementing CER the State shall adhere to standards under
various international human rights instruments such as but not limited to the:

a) International Bill of Rights

b) United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child

c) Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women

d) Basic Principles for the Treatment of Prisoners

e) United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners (Nelson
Mandela Rules)

f) United Nations Rules for the Treatment of Women Prisoners and Non-custodial
Measures for Women Offenders (Bangkok Rules)

g) United Nations Rules for the Protection of Juveniles Deprived of Liberty (Havana
Rules)

SEC. 3. Construction in Favor of Persons Deprived of Liberty. — All
provisions of this Act including its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) shall be
construed to achieve its objectives. All doubts in the interpretation and implementation
of these provisions shall be resolved in favor of the person deprived of liberty.

Compassionate Early Release (CER) refers to the early release of a person
deprived of liberty (PDL) from detention or penal or rehabilitative confinement
based on humanitarian grounds or a set of medical criteria set forth in this Act
such as terminal or severe illness, permanent disability or incapacitation
which renders continued detention or confinement unnecessary or even
harmful prior to completing their sentence.

Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDL) refers to adult offenders confined and
children in conflict with the law who are placed in a public custodial setting
from which they are not permitted to leave at will by order of any judicial or
administrative authority.

Child or Juvenile refers to a person under the age of eighteen (18) years.

Child in Conflict with the Law (CICL) refers to a child who is alleged as,
accused of, or adjudged as having committed an offense under Philippine
laws.

Concemed Authorities refer to those public officials or employees designated
or mandated to implement the provisions of this law and those who, without
direct involvement in the decision-making process of granting CER, have
knowingly caused delay, directly or indirectly, in the approval and/or denial of
the application for CER.

Chronic IlIness refers to a long-standing and debilitating health condition that
persists over time or recurs frequently and substantially limits daily activities.

Debilitating Health Condition refers to a condition or disease that is severe,
incurable, or progressive which significantly impairs one’s ability to function. It
also afflicts those who, due to disability or severe injury, are unable to care for
themselves.

Disability refers to any condition that impedes, obstructs or prevents the
completion of daily tasks using traditional methods because of physical or
mental impairment.

Terminal Iliness refers to an incurable and irreversible condition caused by injury,
disease, or illness that would cause death within one year or less or a period of
time in accordance with accepted medical standards and where the application of
life-sustaining treatment can no longer prolong life expectancy.

Mental Health Condition (as defined in the Mental Health Law) refers to a
neurologic or psychiatric condition characterized by the existence of a
recognizable, clinically-significant disturbance in an_ individual's cognition,
emotional regulation, or behavior that reflects a genetic or acquired dysfunction
in the neurological, psychosocial, or developmental process underlying mental
functioning.

or mobilizing the offender, the victim and the community in crime or conflict

b)

SEC. 9. Post-release Interventions. — All PDLs granted CER shall receive post-
release interventions.

a) Post-release interventions include, but are not limited to, social services,
healthcare, rehabilitation, and support for reintegration into society.

b) Relevant government agencies, headed by the Department of Social Welfare and
Development, in coordination with NGOs and community support systems, shall
develop guidelines and provide comprehensive programs to ensure the welfare

===== [page 9] =====
and successful reintegration of these individuals. Such guidelines shall clearly
require: the exact address where the PDL will reside, the name, address and
contact number of the person/s in-charge of the care of the PDL; an undertaking
that the family and the concerned government agency shall take the
responsibility for the released PDLs to have access to health services including
palliative care. Reportorial requirements shall also be specified.

c) Post-release interventions shall be designed to address the specific needs of
PDLs, particularly those who do not have family or social support, ensuring that
they are not left vulnerable or without assistance upon their release.

SEC. 10. Opposition to the Grant of CER. — Upon receipt of the application, the
ICERRB shall immediately publish, online and in conspicuous places within the city or
municipality where the PDL is serving sentence, any pending proceedings for grant of
CER. Any person may object, through the filing of a verified opposition to the grant of
CER based on the following grounds:

a) The condition, disease, syndrome, or incapacity does not exist or its severity
does not qualify the PDL for CER; or

b) The health professional/s that personally examined the PDL is not licensed under
the laws of the Philippines.

Any opposition to the petition for CER shall be filed not later than thirty (30) days from
the date of publication of the notice of the application. In case of an opposition, the
applicant PDL shall be subjected to a second medical examination which shall be
conducted by a licensed government physician different from the physician who first
examined the PDL and subsequently issued the original medical certification.

The nature of the offense committed or the length of the sentence being served shall
not, by itself, be a ground to deny the application for CER, provided that the health and
humanitarian considerations presented are sufficiently established and verified.

SEC. 11. Decision-making Process. — The ICERRB shall comprehensively and
judiciously review applications and shall make a decision within thirty (30) days from the
receipt of the applications. In case there are oppositions, this period may be extended to
sixty (60) days. The decision, whether approval or denial of the application, shall be
based on the PDL’s health condition, life expectancy, and reintegration potential. The
decision shall be furnished the PDL; in case the PDL is not the applicant, their
authorized representative who made the application; the PDL’s family; and the detention
facility's jail warden.

SEC. 12. Appeal. — A PDL or their legal representative or an oppositor may file an
appeal directly with the Secretary of Justice within 15 days from receipt of the decision.
Appeal shall be decided on within fifteen (15) days from receipt thereof. The decision of
the Secretary of Justice shall be final and executory.

SEC. 17. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this

SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the

public.

SEC. 20. Separability Clause. — If for any reason, any section or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, such other sections or provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SEC. 22. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
