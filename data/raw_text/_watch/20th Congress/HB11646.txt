                           EXPLANATORY NOTE
       The Constitution recognizes the vital role of the youth in
nation-building and commits the State to full employment and equality of
employment opportunities for all.1 Before their first paycheck, they must pay
for applications, fares, medical examinations, and work attire, costs that
existing fee waivers do not cover 2 and that can cut a job search short.3 In
July 2026, the youth employment rate fell to 81.4 percent.4
      This bill extends to every fresh Filipino graduate a one-time transition
grant of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP 5,000.00), claimable within one (1) year
from graduation. The Commission on Higher Education leads its
implementation, with the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority serving technical-vocational completers.
      Should funds fall short, the poorest and most marginalized graduates
are served first, every region receives its fair share, and public funds are
shielded from fraud and political use.
       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE “TULAY SA HANAPBUHAY PROGRAM”, PROVIDING
  FRESH FILIPINO GRADUATES OF PHILIPPINE HIGHER EDUCATION
AND TECHNICAL-VOCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS AND PROGRAMS WITH A
ONE-TIME CASH GRANT OF FIVE THOUSAND PESOS (PHP 5,000.00) TO
      SUPPORT THEIR TRANSITION FROM SCHOOL TO WORK, AND
                   APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Tulay sa
Hanapbuhay Act."
      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy
of the State to promote the welfare of the youth and equality of employment
opportunities for all. Toward this end, the State shall ease the transition of
young Filipinos from education to employment by defraying the initial costs
of seeking work, with preference for the poor and marginalized when
resources are limited.
      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
(a) Fresh Filipino Graduate refers to a Filipino citizen who has completed a
bachelor's or associate degree program in a higher education institution
recognized by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), or a program
registered with the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA) leading to a National Certificate or its equivalent;
(b) Combined Annual Gross Family Income refers to the total gross income of
the applicant's parents or legal guardians in the preceding year or, for a
self-supporting applicant, the applicant's own gross income; and
(c) Special Equity Group refers to Indigenous Peoples under Republic Act No.
8371; persons with disabilities under Republic Act No. 7277, as amended;
solo parents and their dependents under Republic Act No. 8972, as

amended; persons living in poverty under Republic Act No. 11291;
underprivileged and homeless citizens under Republic Act No. 7279; and
graduates who are the first in their immediate family to complete a degree or
National Certificate program.​
​
​     SECTION 4. One-Time Cash Grant. – Every Fresh Filipino Graduate
shall be entitled to a one-time cash grant of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP
5,000.00) to defray reasonable pre-employment and initial employment
expenses, including job applications, transportation, documentation,
medical examinations, and work attire: Provided, That the grant may be
availed of only once, regardless of the number of programs completed:
Provided, further, That the grant shall be in addition to, and shall not
diminish, any scholarship, subsidy, or benefit under existing laws.​
​
​      SECTION 5. Implementing Agencies. – CHED shall be the lead
implementing agency and shall administer the grant for graduates of higher
education institutions, while TESDA shall administer the grant for
completers of technical-vocational programs. CHED and TESDA shall jointly
maintain a single registry of grantees, verify graduation or completion
electronically from institutional records whenever practicable, and verify
identity through the Philippine Identification System. The Department of
Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall provide the data needed to
implement Section 6.​
​
​     SECTION 6. Order of Priority When Funds Are Insufficient. –
Whenever appropriations are insufficient to cover all qualified applicants in
a fiscal year, available funds shall first be allotted among the regions in
proportion to their number of fresh graduates in the preceding year. Within
each region, grants shall be awarded in the following order of priority:
      (a) members of households under the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino
      Program or identified as poor in the DSWD household targeting
      system, and former grantees of the Tertiary Education Subsidy; ​
      ​
      (b) members of a Special Equity Group; and ​
      ​
      (c) all other qualified applicants. Within each class, applicants with
      lower Combined Annual Gross Family Income shall be preferred.
​
The implementing rules shall allow alternative proof of income for
households in the informal sector and for self-supporting applicants.​
​
​     SECTION 7. Disbursement, Replacement, and Reconsideration. –
The grant shall be released within thirty (30) days from approval of the
application, directly and without charge to an account in the grantee's name
with the Land Bank of the Philippines or another authorized government
channel. Unclaimed or forfeited grants shall go to the next qualified
applicants in the order of priority. A denied applicant may seek
reconsideration within fifteen (15) days from notice, to be resolved within

fifteen (15) days.​
​
​     SECTION 8. Non-Partisan Administration. – No elective official shall
take part in the selection of grantees or the distribution of grants. No grant
shall be conditioned on attendance at any event or bear the name or image
of any public official. Releases shall comply with the Omnibus Election
Code.​
​
​     SECTION 9. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. – Any person who
obtains or attempts to obtain the grant through misrepresentation, falsified
documents, impersonation, or any other fraudulent means shall refund the
amount received and pay a fine of Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP 10,000.00) to
Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000.00). A public officer who knowingly
approves a fraudulent claim or violates Section 8 shall suffer imprisonment
of six (6) months to two (2) years, or a fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP
50,000.00) to Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP 200,000.00), or both, and
perpetual disqualification from public office, without prejudice to liability
under other laws.​
​
​      SECTION 10. Monitoring, Reporting, and Transparency. – CHED
and TESDA shall report annually to the President, Congress, and the
Department of Budget and Management; publish grants released per region,
institution, and priority class; and, within three (3) years from the first
release, submit to Congress an independent evaluation of the grant's effect
on employment. Personal data shall be processed in accordance with
Republic Act No. 10173.​
​
​     SECTION 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement
this Act shall be included in the budgets of CHED and TESDA in the annual
General Appropriations Act, based on the projected number of fresh
graduates.​
​
​    SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – CHED and
TESDA, with the DSWD and the Philippine Statistics Authority, shall jointly
promulgate the implementing rules within ninety (90) days from effectivity.​
​
    ​ SECTION 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in force.​
​
 ​    SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.​
​
​      SECTION 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
​
​       Approved.
