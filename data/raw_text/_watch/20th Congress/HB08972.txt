                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to formally establish a National Telemedicine Council and
institutionalize the use of telemedicine as an avenue to deliver essential healthcare
services across the Philippines. It ensures that Filipinos, especially those in remote
communities, have access to clinical specialists in their region and to that of the
public hospitals nationwide, within the safety and comfort of their own homes.

       Teleconsultation services have become increasingly accessible through mobile
applications, hotlines, and official platforms of the Department of Health (DOH), as
well as private providers. However, there is a disconnect between existing policy
frameworks and actual ground experience. While Joint Administrative Order No.
2021-0001, through the DOH, Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), provides the
guidelines on the implementation of telemedicine in the delivery of individual-based
health services1, its intended benefits remain unevenly realized in geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas due to lagging digital infrastructure.

       This measure strengthens the delivery of essential healthcare services in
remote areas and enables the strategic use of information and communication
technology (ICT) during emergencies, disasters and states of calamity. It further
mandates the education and training of health personnel to ensure competence in
telemedicine systems and establishes the necessary regulatory oversight to ensure
compliance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act
of 2012”.

      In     view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A NATIONAL TELEMEDICINE SYSTEM IN THE
PHILIPPINES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Telemedicine Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall protect and promote the right to
health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. Hence, it is the
policy of the State to utilize innovative information and communication technology
(ICT) to ensure healthcare reaches all Filipinos, particularly those farm far-flung and
underserved rural areas.

        It shall likewise be the policy of the State to protect the fundamental human
right to privacy of communication while ensuring the free flow of information to
promote health and wellness, especially in times of emergencies and calamities, in
accordance with Republic Act (RA) No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy
Act of 2012”.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Telemedicine, also referred to as telehealth or electronic healthcare,
       refers to a system of delivering healthcare services through the use of
       information and communications technology as a complement to traditional
       healthcare systems. It includes the total management of a patient's health care
       from consultation, diagnosis, and treatment to prescription of medicine and
       record-keeping. It likewise includes the education and training of health
       personnel to capacitate them using telemedicine systems as well as the
       development of applications and programs for the furtherance of health; and

          b. Information and Communications Technology refers to a broad range
       of systems and tools used for managing and communicating information,
       which includes but is not limited to:

                                            1
                1.​ Digital interactive video, audio, and/or data transmission;

                2.​ Video or web-conferencing technology;

                3.​ Still image capture or asynchronous store and forward;

                4.​ Modern medical devices for patient care and diagnosis.

      SEC. 4. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are:

          a.​ To create an innovative, unified, and comprehensive policy framework
      for the practice and development of telemedicine in the country;

          b.​ To provide healthcare services, including consultation, diagnosis,
      treatment and prescription of medicines especially in areas that have
      inadequate or are far from traditional healthcare institutions and
      professionals;

         c.​ To allow the use of information and communication technology in the
      delivery of healthcare services in time of emergencies and disasters; and

         d.​ To strengthen the health system and infrastructure of the country that
      will enable it to serve all Filipinos and withstand any unforeseen events.

       SEC. 5. National Telemedicine Council. – A National Telemedicine Council,
hereinafter referred to as “Council”, is hereby established and composed of the
following:

         a.​ Secretary of Health, who shall serve as Chairperson;

         b.​ Secretary of Information and Communication Technology;

         c.​ Secretary of Science and Technology;

         d.​ Privacy Commissioner of the National Privacy Commission (NPC);

         e.​ President of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation;

         f.​ Director-General of the Food and Drug Administration;

         g.​ Presidents of healthcare professional associations such as the
             Philippine Medical Association, Philippine Nurses Association, and
             the Philippine Pharmacists Associations; and

         h.​ Director of the Office for Telemedicine.

       SEC. 6. Functions of the Council. – The Council, with regard to telemedicine in
the Philippines, shall:

         a.​ Establish guidelines and regulations on the practice and provision of
      services;

         b.​ Create a national roadmap for the achievement and furtherance of the
      objectives of this Act;

          c.​ Establish a   national   integrated database system for medical
      information;

         d.​ Establish and provide for a mechanism of accreditation for electronic
      healthcare providers;

          e.​ Establish a system for electronic prescription of medicine;

         f.​ Capacitate and train existing and new healthcare professionals on the
      provision of health services;

          g.​ Ensure interoperability of the national telemedicine database with the
      existing Health Information Exchange (HIE) systems; and

          h.​ Set quality standards for systems, professionals and services.

       The Council shall convene as frequently as necessary to perform its mandated
functions.

       SEC. 7. Implementing Agency and Secretariat. – The Department of Health shall
be the lead agency in implementing this Act. For this purpose, an Office of
Telemedicine shall be created under the Department which shall serve as the
Secretariat for the Council. The Office for Telemedicine shall be headed by a
Director.

       SEC. 8. Practice of Medicine. – The provision of Telemedicine services shall be
limited to licensed healthcare professionals acting within the scope of their
respective professions. A telemedicine consultation shall be deemed to establish a
professional-client relationship, carrying the same legal responsibilities as an
in-person consultation.

      SEC. 9. Emergency and Disaster Response. – The Council shall establish
protocols for the enhanced utilization of Telemedicine during declared states of
calamity or public health emergencies.

       SEC. 10. Privacy and Data Security. – All telemedicine providers shall comply
with the provisions of RA 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”.
All electronic health records and communications shall be encrypted and stored in
accordance with the guidelines set by the NPC and the Department of Information

and Communications Technology. Any unauthorized breaches of confidentiality
shall be penalized under RA No. 10173, and RA No. 10175, otherwise known as the
“Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012”.

     SEC. 11. Standard of Care. – The standard of care for telemedicine shall be the
same as that required for in-person healthcare services. No practitioner shall be
exempt from liability for professional negligence due to the medium of
communication used.

        SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be taken from the current appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, the
amount needed for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the
annual appropriations of the DOH as a distinct and separate item.

        SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Council, in coordination
with the NPC and PRC, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

        SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall continue to be in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders,
issuances, rules and regulations, or part thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
likewise repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
