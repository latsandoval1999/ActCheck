                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Parents and caregivers often bring babies and young children with them when they
go to government of ices, hospitals, transport terminals, restaurants, and other public
places. Yet many of these places still do not have basic facilities like safe diaper-
changing stations or proper seating for infants. As a result, caregivers are forced to
change diapers in unsafe or unsanitary areas or struggle to care for their children in
ways that are uncomfortable and undigni ied.

This bill addresses a simple but important need. It requires government of ices and
covered public establishments to provide basic infant-friendly facilities that are clean,
safe, and accessible. Caring for a baby is part of everyday life, and families should not
be discouraged or disadvantaged when they access public services or conduct daily
transactions. Just as the law already requires lactation stations to support
breastfeeding mothers, providing diaper-changing stations and child-friendly seating
is a logical and necessary step to protect infant health and family welfare.

Some establishments already offer these facilities, but access remains uneven and
depends largely on location or management choice. This leaves many families,
especially low-income caregivers who rely on public of ices and terminals, without

cleanliness, and dignity, and helps ensure that public spaces in the Philippines are
truly family-friendly.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Infant Care Facilities in
Public Places and Workplaces Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote public
health, child welfare, and family-friendly public services by ensuring that parents,
guardians, and caregivers have access to safe, sanitary, and accessible infant care
facilities in government of ices and public-facing establishments, including diaper-
changing stations and child-friendly seating, subject to reasonable space and safety
standards.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) “Government Of ices” refer to national government agencies, constitutional
       commissions, of ices of local government units, and government-owned or -
       controlled corporations that transact with the public.


                                                                           3|Page
   (b) “Covered Establishments” shall refer to:

      1. Major Establishments, as de ined in paragraph (c); and
      2. Food Service Establishments meeting the seating threshold in paragraph
         (d).

   (c) “Major Establishments” refer to malls, shopping centers, public
       transportation terminals, theaters and cinemas, convention centers, sports
       arenas, auditoriums, cultural complexes, exhibition halls, permanent
       amusement parks, hospitals and other health facilities, gasoline stations with
       public restrooms, and other establishments open to the public with total gross
        loor area or occupancy load meeting thresholds to be determined in the IRR.

   (d) “Food Service Establishment” refers to a restaurant, cafeteria, fast-food
       outlet, or similar business primarily engaged in selling food or beverages for
       on-premises consumption, including those located within malls or similar
       structures; Provided, that establishments which by policy do not admit
       persons below eighteen (18) years of age are excluded.

   (e) “Common Seating” refers to the actual number of customer seats regularly
       available for on-premises dining.

   (f) “Child-Friendly Seating” refers to a high chair, booster seat, or other seating
       equipment designed for infants or young children to be seated safely at tables
       or counters, meeting safety standards set by the DOH in coordination with
       relevant agencies.

   (g) “Diaper-Changing Station” refers to a ixed or foldable surface or table
       located in or near a restroom, designed for safe diaper changing, with required
       safety features (including stable mounting and load rating), sanitation
       features, and access to hand hygiene facilities or a nearby lavatory, consistent
       with standards prescribed by the DOH.

   (h) “Infant or Young Child” refers to a child from birth up to thirty-six (36)
       months of age.

SECTION 4. Minimum Infant Care Facilities Required. — Covered Establishments
and Government Of ices shall provide:

   (a) Diaper-changing stations accessible to caregivers; and

   (b) Child-friendly seating in covered food service establishments, as provided in
       Section 6.

SECTION 5. Diaper-Changing Stations; Location and Accessibility. —

   (a) General Rule. Every Major Establishment and Government Of ice shall
       provide at least one (1) diaper-changing station that is safe, sanitary, and
       reasonably accessible to the public.

   (b) Gender Accessibility. Diaper-changing stations shall be:

      1. Available in both men’s and women’s restrooms; or
      2. Located in a gender-neutral/family restroom accessible to all genders;
         or
      3. Located in a common area adjacent to restrooms that provides privacy and
         safety, as may be allowed under the IRR.

   (c) Per Floor Requirement. Where practicable, at least one (1) diaper-changing
       station shall be provided per loor occupied by the establishment or of ice and
       open to the public; Provided, that the IRR may allow equivalent compliance for
       buildings with centralized restrooms.

   (d) Minimum Standards. The diaper-changing station shall not be located inside
       a toilet cubicle if placement creates sanitation or safety risks. It must comply
       with DOH standards on cleanliness, ventilation, and equipment.

SECTION 6. Child-Friendly Seating in Food Service Establishments. —

   (a) Duty to Provide on Request. Covered food service establishments shall make
       available child-friendly seating to patrons with an infant or young child,
       subject to availability and safety.

   (b) Minimum Ratio. Covered food service establishments with ten (10) or more
       common seats shall provide at least one (1) child-friendly seat for every ten
       (10) common seats, or a fraction thereof, up to a maximum to be set by the
       IRR based on reasonable space and demand.

   (c) Sanitation. Child-friendly seating shall be maintained in clean and sanitary
       condition and shall be inspected regularly.

SECTION 7. Exemptions and Alternative Compliance. —

   (a) Structural Impossibility / Space Constraints. Existing establishments may
       be granted alternative compliance (e.g., a centrally located diaper-changing
       station within the building) if strict compliance is not feasible due to structural
       constraints, subject to DOH standards and prior approval.

   (b) Restaurant Exception (Central Facility). A restaurant with seating capacity
       of ifty (50) or more may be exempted from installing its own diaper-changing
       station if a centrally located diaper-changing station is available within a
       reasonable walking distance to be set in the IRR, provided such facility is open
       during operating hours and is accessible to restaurant patrons.

SECTION 8. Implementing Agency; Standards; Inspections. —

   (a) Lead Agency. The Department of Health (DOH) shall be the lead agency for
       setting minimum public health and sanitation standards for facilities required
       under this Act, in coordination with DILG, DTi (as appropriate for business
       compliance), DPWH (for building standards interface), and LGUs.

   (b) Consistency with Existing Public Health Facilities Standards. The DOH
       shall ensure that standards under this Act are consistent with the
       requirements for clean, private, and sanitary facilities in public places,
       consistent with analogous public health facility mandates.

   (c) Inspection and Compliance. LGUs, through their licensing and inspection
       functions, and local DOH of ices, as applicable, shall conduct periodic
       inspections and enforce compliance as part of business permit renewal and
       government of ice facility compliance.

   (d) No Overreach in IRR. The IRR shall be con ined to implementing this Act and
       shall not create prohibitions or penalties beyond what this Act provides,
       consistent with jurisprudence.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with DILG and other relevant
agencies and after consultation with stakeholders, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations.

SECTION 10. Transition Period. — Covered Establishments and Government Of ices
existing at the time of effectivity shall have one (1) year to comply. New
establishments and newly occupied government of ices shall comply prior to opening
to the public or commencement of operations, as may be provided in the IRR.

SECTION 11. Penalties and Administrative Sanctions. —

   (a) Notice to Comply. For irst-time inspection indings, the enforcing of ice shall
       issue a written notice specifying de iciencies and giving a cure period of not
       less than thirty (30) days, unless the violation poses an immediate health or
       safety risk.

   (b) Fines. Failure to comply after the cure period shall subject the establishment
       or of ice to an administrative ine of Two thousand pesos (PHP 2,000.00)
       per day of continuing violation, until compliance.

   (c) For Business Establishments. Continued refusal to comply may be a ground
       for non-renewal of business permits, subject to due process and applicable
       local ordinances.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
