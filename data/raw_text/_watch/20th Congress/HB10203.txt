                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Many Filipino families continue to face preventable health risks due to
limited access to regular screening, early diagnosis, and primary healthcare
services, particularly in underserved and geographically isolated communities.
As a result, illnesses such as hypertension, diabetes, malnutrition, mental health
conditions, and other preventable diseases often remain undetected until
complications arise.

      This bill seeks to institutionalize a Whole Family Health Program that
provides free annual preventive health check-ups for Filipino households through
barangay health stations, rural health units, mobile health teams, and public
healthcare facilities. The measure adopts a family-centered and preventive
healthcare approach focused on early detection, nutrition, mental health,
reproductive health, child development, and disease prevention.

      The proposed program strengthens the implementation of the Universal
Health Care Act by improving access to primary healthcare services, enhancing
continuity of care, and promoting coordinated health interventions at the
  community level. It also provides mechanisms for digital health records, mobile
  healthcare delivery, referral systems, and local government participation to
  ensure efficient and inclusive implementation.

         By prioritizing prevention, early intervention, and community-based
  healthcare, this measure aims to improve health outcomes, reduce long-term
  healthcare costs, and strengthen the overall well-being and resilience of Filipino
  families.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Whole Family
Health Program Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of all Filipinos through a preventive,
family-centered, community-based, and integrated healthcare approach.


Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize accessible and regular preventive
health services for Filipino households, particularly vulnerable and underserved
sectors, with emphasis on nutrition, non-communicable diseases, mental health,
reproductive health, child development, and early disease detection consistent
with the Universal Health Care Act and existing public health policies.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:
   a) “Family Unit” refers to individuals living together as one household and
      registered under the barangay household or family profiling system,
      regardless of legal or biological relationship.
   b) “Preventive Health Check-Up” refers to barangays or municipalities
      classified as rural by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), National
      Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), or other authorized
      government agencies.
   c) “Barangay Health Station (BHS)” refers to the primary healthcare facility
      operating at the barangay level under the supervision of the local
      government unit and the Department of Health.
   d) “Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs)” refer to
      communities with limited access to healthcare services due to geographic,
      socioeconomic, or security-related barriers, as defined by the Department
      of Health.


SEC. 4. Annual Preventive Family Health Check-Ups. – All registered family
units shall be entitled to one (1) free annual preventive health check-up through
the nearest Barangay Health Station (BHS), Rural Health Unit (RHU), public
health facility, or authorized mobile health team.


The annual health check-up may include:
   a) Basic medical, dental, and physical examination;



                                                                                2
   b) Nutritional assessment, including body mass index (BMI), growth
      monitoring, and food insecurity screening;
   c) Screening for non-communicable and lifestyle-related diseases including
      hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular disease risks, and high cholesterol;
   d) Mental health screening, psychosocial assessment, and brief counseling
      services;
   e) Reproductive health consultation, maternal health services, family
      planning counseling, and sexually transmitted infection (STI) risk
      screening, where appropriate and consistent with existing laws;
   f) Child and adolescent health and developmental screening;
   g) Health education on disease prevention, nutrition, sanitation, vaccination,
      and available government health programs.


Priority shall be given to indigent households, senior citizens, persons with
disabilities, pregnant women, children, indigenous peoples, and residents of
GIDAs.


SEC. 5. Implementation by the Department of Health. – The Department of
Health (DOH) shall serve as the lead implementing agency and shall:
   a) Develop national standards, protocols, and clinical guidelines for
      preventive family health check-ups;
   b) Deploy mobile health units, family health teams, and telehealth support
      services in underserved and geographically isolated areas;
   c) Coordinate with local government units for family registration, scheduling,
      referral systems, and community health education;
   d) Establish and maintain interoperable digital health records and reporting
      systems consistent with the Data Privacy Act and the Universal Health
      Care Act;

   e) Ensure integration of the Program with existing national public health
      initiatives and primary healthcare systems.


SEC. 6. Responsibilities of Local Government Units. – LGUs shall:
Local government units, through their Provincial, City, or Municipal Health
Offices, shall:
   a) Conduct family profiling, registration, and scheduling of annual check-
      ups;
   b) Mobilize barangay health workers (BHWs), nutrition workers, and
      community volunteers for implementation and health promotion activities;
   c) Facilitate referrals and continuity of care for individuals requiring further
      medical attention or specialized treatment;
   d) Submit periodic accomplishment and financial reports to the DOH;
   e) Ensure adequate local support, facilities, and logistical assistance for the
      Program.


Barangays may establish Family Health Committees composed of barangay
officials, health workers, civil society representatives, and community
volunteers.


SEC. 7. Referral System and Continuity of Care. – annual check-up shall be
referred to the appropriate public healthcare facility, specialist, or government
support program.


Indigent and financially vulnerable patients shall be entitled to available benefits
and financial protection mechanisms under Republic Act No. 11223 or the
Universal Health Care Act, PhilHealth programs, and other applicable laws.

SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual appropriations of the Department of Health
and participating agencies under the General Appropriations Act.


Local government units may allocate counterpart funding, facilities, personnel,
equipment, or logistical support for the implementation of the Program.


SEC. 9. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Transparency. – The DOH, in
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
shall:
   a) Develop a monitoring and evaluation framework to assess program
         implementation,    service coverage, health outcomes, and referral
         compliance;
   b) Submit annual reports to Congress detailing implementation progress,
         budget utilization, disease trends, nutrition indicators, and service gaps;
   c) Ensure compliance with transparency, accountability, and public health
         data protection standards;
   d) Conduct periodic independent impact assessments and program reviews.


SEC. 10. Incentives and Accountability Mechanisms. – a) Local government
units demonstrating exemplary implementation and achieving high program
coverage rates may be eligible for performance-based health grants, incentives,
or recognition programs subject to DOH and DILG guidelines.


b) Failure of responsible officials to implement the Program without justifiable
cause, or misuse of funds allocated under this Act, shall subject such officials to
administrative, civil, or criminal liability under existing laws.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with concerned government
agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
