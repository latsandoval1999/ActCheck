                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The Philippines continues to face a housing challenge. Although estimates vary, studies
consistently indicate that the number of informal settler families (ISFs) has long
exceeded one million and continues to rise annually. The UN-Habitat estimated that the country had
3.7 million ISFs in 2022.1 In Metro Manila alone, 596,308 ISFs occupied 3,447.93 hectares of land in
2024.2

        This persistent challenge is been compounded by the housing backlog and limited access
to affordable housing. As of December 2022, the country’s unmet housing needs had reached 6.66
million units.3 A 2022 study by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) found
that low-income households cannot afford housing at 30% of their income.4

        Moreover, the PIDS study underscored that housing policies and projects catering to the poor
are more evident in LGUs with functioning Local Housing Boards (LHBs), particularly those that
uphold more responsive representation, transparency, and openness in addressing the housing needs
of the marginalized families. The study recommended that, to be fully functional and
effective, LHBs must be provided financial support through a socialized housing fund. It further
emphasized the need for LHBs to be involved not only in addressing informal settlements, but in
the broader decision-making process for city-wide shelter and land use planning.

       The 1987 Philippine Constitution recognizes the invaluable role of the private sector in public
governance. It encourages the participation of non-governmental, community-based, and sectoral
organizations, while emphasizing the State’s duty to facilitate adequate consultation mechanisms.5

        Consistent with the cited studies, policy recommendations, and constitutional principles, this
bill seeks to institutionalize LHBs in all local government units (LGUs) to ensure a coordinated,
responsive, and inclusive approach to housing. By defining their composition, powers, and funding
mechanisms, the measure empowers LGUs to plan, regulate, and implement housing programs and
services consistent with national and local development frameworks, while safeguarding the rights of
informal settler families and strengthening multi-stakeholder participation in the housing sector.

                            AN ACT
 INSTITUTIONALIZING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF LOCAL HOUSING BOARDS
   IN ALL LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS, PROVIDING FOR ITS POWERS AND
    FUNCTIONS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160,
    OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991, AS
                           AMENDED

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Local Housing Board Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It shall be the policy of the State to establish a local
      government platform that shall ensure a timely and efficient response to the housing needs of
      the people.

      In pursuit of this policy, the State shall:

   a. Adopt a just and humane socialized housing program that is aligned with the National Housing
      and Urban Development Plan 2040 and integrated into the Comprehensive Land Use
      Plans (CLUPs) of cities and municipalities;
   b. Safeguard poor and marginalized families against evictions or demolitions carried out in
      violation of the law; and
   c. Promote and institutionalize representation of all stakeholders in the housing sector, including
      the private and public sectors, non-governmental, community-based, and sectoral
      organizations.

      SECTION 3. New Sections. New sections denominated as Sections 105-A, 105-B, 105-C,
      105-D, 105-E, 105-F, and 105-G under a new Title denominated as Title V-A are inserted
      after Section 105, Title V of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local
      Government Code of 1991, as amended, to read as follows:

     Board shall be constituted within sixty (60) days after the official assumption of local

    Development (DHSUD);

    Northwestern Mindanao;

10. Three (3) private sector representatives (PSRs), subject to Section 105-B hereof.
11. Regional Director of the Department of Social Welfare and Development.

        LGUs with existing local housing boards shall continue to operate and shall incorporate the
provisions of this Title into their existing mandate.

        SECTION 105-B. Private Sector Representatives (PSRs). The PSRs shall be
        selected from organizations created or formed by the private sector for the purpose of socio-
        economic      development,        including people’s    organizations and    non-government
        organizations, subject to the following qualifications:

   a. The organization must be registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission for the
      purpose of bestowing legal personality.

   b. The organization must be in operation for at least five (5) years and has substantially
      completed programs and projects within the LGU. These programs and projects include
      research, consultancy, charities, and/or hard physical projects involving housing.

   c. The organization must be organized through private initiative and must not have any
      government official or employee as a member. The organization must not be funded through
      government or any government-related financial support.
   d. For the purpose of encouraging technology transfer, the LGU may invite international
      organizations as members, provided that all of the above three criteria are satisfied, and the
      organization/s vision aligns with the objectives of the approved Local Shelter Plan (LSP).

        Upon selection, the PSRs shall represent not their private sector organization but the
interests of the LGU’s housing sector, as a whole.

      The term of office of the PSRs shall coincide with the regular term of local elective officials.
The DILG shall issue guidelines on the selection of PSRs.

       SECTION 105-C. Powers and Functions. The Provincial Local Housing Board shall have
       the following powers and functions:

   a. Formulate, develop, and recommend to the Sanggunian policies, directives, rules, and
      regulations, consistent with law, as may be necessary, regarding the provision of socialized
      housing programs and the protection of the rights of informal settlers to just
      and humane evictions and demolitions;
   b. Consolidate the housing data of all cities and municipalities under its jurisdiction, based on
      LSPs, for submission to the DHSUD Regional Office;
   c. Designate at least one (1) representative to the local development council: Provided,
      That such representative is not already a member of the council;
   d. Recommend to the Sanggunian the sites for socialized housing that comply with the
      requirements prescribed under Section 8 of Republic Act No. 7279, or the Urban
      Development and Housing Act of 1992 (UDHA), as amended, and the guidelines issued for
      the purpose;
   e. Advise the Sanggunian on matters of local taxation that may affect local government housing
      programs or projects, including, but not limited to, the formulation of a socialized housing
      tax, and idle land tax;
   f. Recommend schemes for the acquisition and disposition of lands within their localities for
      socialized housing purposes, subject to Sections 9 to 14 of RA No. 7279 and its implementing
      guidelines: Provided, That such schemes ensure that the valuation of lands for socialized
      housing purposes is within levels affordable to prospective beneficiaries, including those
      below the poverty threshold;
   g. Recommend partnership arrangements with the national government on the provision of
      decent and affordable shelter;
   h. Act on matters necessary to address the high housing needs of cities/municipalities within
      their jurisdictions, particularly those with housing needs, provided that they coordinate with
      DHSUD Regional Office on large-scale housing needs requiring multi-LGU action;
   i. Ensure timely and accurate survey estimates of informal settler families living within their
      jurisdiction, and submission of a complete report on the ISF estimates by concerned LGU
      offices to the DHSUD and DILG regional and provincial offices;
   j. Maintain a running list of contractor performances in past and ongoing housing projects
      implemented within the LGU’s jurisdiction, to be used as basis for evaluating project bidders
      during contractor procurement subject to RA 9184 procurement guidelines;
   k. Through the local chief executive, submit to the President and the Congress of the Philippines
      an annual report, as provided by Section 41 of RA No. 7279, as amended;
   l. Coordinate with the Regional Development Councils on aligning priority local housing
      programs and projects with the overall regional development thrusts, and on matters relating
      to the housing sector, particularly those requiring action from multiple government agencies
      or offices;

   m. Coordinate with the Local Project Monitoring Committees on the monitoring and evaluation
      of housing projects, and provide decisions, when necessary, as recommended by its members;
   n. Coordinate with the Urban Poor Affairs Office, or its equivalent, to ensure the accurate and
      timely updating of the registration of informal settler families, and to integrate the list of
      beneficiaries into priority local housing projects; and
   o. Perform other related functions as may be established by law or ordinance, that are necessary
      to implement the mandate of LGUs under the UDHA.

        Boards of HUCs, independent component cities, component cities and municipalities shall
have the same above-mentioned powers and functions of the Provincial Housing Boards, except for
items (b) and (h). In addition, they shall undertake the following:

   a. With the assistance of the DHSUD and other concerned government agencies, prepare the
      mid-term and long-term LSP, which shall form part of the bases of the Comprehensive
      Development Plan (CDP): Provided, That such plan shall be consistent with the city/municipal
      CLUP: Provided further, That public hearings shall be conducted for the purpose;
   b. Ensure proper disposition of socialized housing projects in compliance with R.A. No. 7279, as
      amended;
   c. Review and endorse for approval of appropriate office/s preliminary and final subdivision
      schemes and development plans of all economic and socialized housing projects, including
      joint LGU-developer projects, and projects that require individual or group building
      occupancy permits covered by Batas Pambansa Blg. 220 and its implementing rules and
      regulations; and
   d. Recommend to the Sanggunian necessary sets of standards and/or requirements for all
      housing types, including socialized, economic, medium, and open market, in order to ensure
      that housing projects are aligned with the city/municipality’s long-term development
      objectives and land use, and to promote the city/municipality’s cultural identity through
      placemaking.

       SECTION 105-D. Secretariat. The local planning and development office shall serve as the
       secretariat of the Board. LGUs may create, as necessary, a unit within their Planning and
       Development Office for the purpose of providing Secretariat functions to their LHBs.

       The Secretariat shall have the following functions:

   a. Provide overall administrative, technical, and operational support to the Board;

   b. Prepare the agenda and discussion materials of the Board’s meetings;

   c. Disseminate Board resolutions to concerned agencies, offices, private organizations,
      and/or other government instrumentalities and ensure these are implemented/acted upon;

   d. Conduct the selection of PSRs based on the guidelines prescribed by the DILG;

   e. Determine the annual funding requirements of the Board, and ensure timely submission to
      the Local Finance Committee for inclusion in the Local Expenditure Program which will be
      submitted to the Sanggunian for approval;



                                                                                                  7
   f. Upon request of the concerned barangay, provide technical and administrative assistance in
      addressing housing concerns;

   g. Serve as the Board’s property custodian; and

   h. Perform such other functions as may be required by the Board.

       SECTION 105-E. Funds for the Operation of the Local Housing Board and Creation
       of Local Shelter Fund. The LGU shall allocate a portion of its regular funds to cover the
       expenses for the meetings and regular operations of the Board.

        The Board shall establish a Local Shelter Fund to finance its activities beyond its
meetings, including advocacies, consultations, and other essential undertakings for the housing
sector. The LGU shall allocate 0.5% of the combined income from Real Property Tax and idle lands
tax for the Local Shelter Fund. The Board shall be responsible for managing the Local Shelter
Fund. The DHSUD, in consultation with the DILG, shall formulate guidelines for the utilization
of the Local Shelter Fund.

       SECTION 105-F. Generation and Mobilization of Resources. For the purpose of
       advising the Local Development Council (LDC) on matters relating to the sourcing of funds
       for socialized housing, the Board may recommend the following schemes
       for financing socialized housing projects:

   a. Build-operate-transfer and other related schemes;
   b. Bond flotation and other credit financing arrangements;
   c. Availment of foreign or local grants: Provided, That LHBs are hereby authorized to receive
      foreign and local grants, which shall be remitted to the local treasury, and be credited to the
      socialized housing account of the concerned LGU;
   d. Loan packages from government financing/lending institutions;
   e. Joint venture projects with private sector groups or developers, as well as with NGOs and
      POs; and,
   f. Pooling of resources among LGUs, the private sector, NGOs, and POs.

        SECTION 105-G. Meetings and Quorum. The Board shall hold meetings at least once
every quarter, and as often as may be deemed necessary. The presence of the Chairperson and a
majority of the members of the Board shall constitute a quorum. Special meetings may be called by
the Chairperson or by a majority of the members when warranted. Decisions shall be reached by
the Board through a simple majority: Provided, That at least one (1) of the member PSRs is present.

       A written notice stating the date, time, place, and agenda of the meeting shall be sent to each
member of the Board at least one (1) week prior to the scheduled meeting, or at least three (3)
days before, if it is a special meeting.

       SECTION 105-H. Socialized               Housing     Program.     Based     on     the LDC-
       approved Local Shelter Plan, the Board shall formulate the Local Housing Program, which
       shall include a list of projects that the LGU will implement within three (3) years.

SECTION 4. Transitory Provisions. Local chief executives of provinces, cities, and
first- to third-class municipalities shall constitute their respective Boards within ninety (90)
days from the promulgation of the implementing rules and regulations of this Act. Local chief
executives of fourth- to sixth-class municipalities shall be given a period of one (1) year from
the promulgation of the implementing rules and regulations of this Act to constitute their
Boards. Failure to constitute the Board within this prescribed period shall be deemed prima
facie evidence of unjustified failure or refusal to do so.

Cities and municipalities with existing LHBs or similar entities are hereby given ninety (90)
days from the promulgation of the implementing rules and regulations of this Act to conform
with the provisions of this Act: Provided, That nothing herein shall
preclude provinces, cities, and municipalities from designating additional functions and/or
appropriating additional funds to achieve the objectives of this Act.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days after the
approval of this Act, the DHSUD and the DILG, in consultation with the concerned
government agencies, the private sector, POs, and NGOs, shall formulate the appropriate
rules and regulations necessary to effectively implement any or all the provisions of this Act.

SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules
and regulations, and other issuances, or part or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SECTION 7. Separability Clause. If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions of this Act shall continue to be in force
and effect.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
