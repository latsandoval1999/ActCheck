                          AN ACT
 MANDATING THE DISPLAY OF NUTRITIONAL INFORMATION ON THE
              MENU OF FOOD ESTABLISHMENTS
                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Good nutrition has been attributed to healthier food choices and,
generally, a healthier diet. The presence of nutritional information on the food
labels helps people maintain their healthy diet and nutrition because of
informed choices. This, in turn, helps prevent the rise of various diseases
attributed to an unhealthy diet.

      The World Health Organization indicates that 80 percent of premature
heart disease, stroke, and Type II Diabetes and 40 percent of cancers can be
prevented through a healthy diet. Thus, consumers have now become more
discerning in their choice of food, always checking the nutritional information
and calorie count on food labels. Filipinos, however, are not as informed when
dining in restaurants. They are often unaware of the calorie and nutritional
content of a meal, leading them or misleading them into an unhealthy eating
pattern.

      This bill aims to mandate the disclosure and display of the nutrition
content information on each standard menu in all types of restaurants,
including fast food chains, in order to enable and empower consumers with
the right to choose the food they want to eat without compromising their
health.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
 MANDATING THE DISPLAY OF NUTRITIONAL INFORMATION ON THE
              MENU OF FOOD ESTABLISHMENTS
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Nutrition
Information Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right of the people to good health. Towards
this end, the State hereby mandates the disclosure and display of the
nutrition content information of food on the menus and menu boards of
restaurants and fast-food chain establishments to help Filipinos make
healthier food choices.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

   (a) Food establishment - any food retail establishment that is part of an
       affiliation of ten (10) or more food retail establishments, doing business
       under the same name, regardless of its type of ownership, and selling
       substantially the same menu items in standard content and portion
       servings.

   (b) Food items - a unit serving of a ready-to-eat food or beverage for sale in
       a restaurant or food retail establishment.

   (c) Food-on-display - food offered for sale that is visible to a customer before
       making a selection and needs no further preparation before
       consumption.

   (d) Standard menu item - many individual food items or combination of food
       items that is routinely listed on a menu or menu board, or routinely
       offered for sale by a restaurant or fast-food chain establishment.
   (e) Menu - a printed list or pictorial display of food items or combination of
       food items and their prices posted in and visible within a fast-food chain
       as defined in this Act, including menus distributed or provided outside
       of the establishment.

   (f) Menu board - any list or pictorial display of food items and their prices
       posted in and visible within or outside a fast-food chain as defined in
       this Act, including drive-thru menu boards.

   (g) Self-service food - food and beverages that the consumers serve
       themselves, including food offered for sale at a salad bar, buffet line,
       cafeteria line, or similar self-service facility.

SECTION 4. Disclosure of Nutrition Information. - All food establishments,
as defined in this Act, are hereby required to disclose and display the nutrient
content information of each standard menu item on their menus and menu
boards as usually prepared and sold to the public. For food establishments
offering self-service food and food on-display, the nutrient content information
per item or serving shall be posted on a sign adjacent to such food.

The nutrition information shall indicate the amount of each of the following:

   (a) Total fat;
   (b) Saturated fat;
   (c) Cholesterol;
   (d) Sodium;
   (e) Total carbohydrates;
   (f) Complex; carbohydrates;
   (g) Sugars;
   (h) Dietary fiber; and
   (i) Total Protein.

Disclosure and display of nutrition information shall not apply to custom
orders on food that is part of a customary market test or temporary menu
items.

SECTION 5. Calorie Content. - The disclosure of nutrition content
information by food establishments, as required in the preceding section,
shall indicate the calories contained in a standard menu item to the nearest
five-calorie increment up to and including fifty (50) calories, to the nearest
ten-calorie increment above fifty (50) calories.

For foods that have fewer than five (5) calories, the declaration may be
expressed as zero (0).

SECTION 6. Disclosure Compliance Certificate. - As an additional
requirement for their operation within six (6) months from the effectivity of
this Act, food establishments are hereby directed to apply for and secure a
Nutrition Disclosure Compliance Certificate (NDCC) from the Department of
Health (DOH). The NDCC issued by the DOH shall be valid for two (2) years
from the date of issuance and may be renewed at least thirty (30) days before
its expiration. The DOH may refuse to renew the NDCC of any restaurant or
fast-food chain establishment found to be in violation of this Act.

Failure of any restaurant of fast-food chain establishment to secure a NDCC
shall be a ground for the suspension or revocation of its license to operate.

SECTION 7. Penalties. - A fine of not less than twenty thousand pesos (PHP
20,000) but not more than one hundred thousand pesos (PHP 100,000) shall
be imposed upon any person operating a food establishment found to have
failed to comply with the mandatory nutrition information regarding the
number of calories or nutrients contained in their standard menu items.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, DOH shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - Should any provision herein be declared
unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity of the other provisions
of this Act.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause - All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
regulations or other issuances or parts inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
publication in the Official Gazette or in any two (2) newspapers of general
circulation in the Philippines.
