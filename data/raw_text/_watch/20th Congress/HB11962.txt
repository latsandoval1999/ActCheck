                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Rabies prevention and control remain critical public health priorities in the
Philippines, particularly in ensuring equitable access to timely post-exposure
prophylaxis (PEP) and reducing preventable human rabies fatalities.

       This bill seeks to institutionalize the establishment and operation of certified
Animal Bite Centers (ABCs) in all Rural Health Units (RHUs) nationwide to
strengthen the delivery of rabies exposure management services at the primary
healthcare level. It mandates the provision of essential services, including wound
management, risk assessment, anti-rabies vaccination, rabies immunoglobulin
administration, patient monitoring, and referral services, in accordance with
standards prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH).

       To ensure the sustainability and operational readiness of RHU-based ABCs,
the measure establishes requirements for facility certification, personnel
qualifications, continuing professional development, and the adequate supply of
vaccines, biologics, and medical equipment. It further provides for a phased
implementation framework, national and regional buffer stock systems, and
telemedicine support to address operational and resource constraints, particularly in
underserved communities. Moreover, it mandates expanded PhilHealth coverage for
PEP services and provides access safeguards to ensure that indigent and other
vulnerable patients are not denied treatment due to financial incapacity.

        The proposed measure complements the National Rabies Prevention and
Control Program by strengthening primary healthcare delivery, institutionalizing
local treatment capacity, and establishing mechanisms for surveillance, monitoring,
and accountability. Through the nationwide availability of certified ABCs, the
government can facilitate timely medical intervention, improve treatment
completion rates, and address disparities in access to rabies prevention and
treatment. These interventions are likewise consistent with the country's
commitment to eliminating dog-mediated human rabies by 2030.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
ESTABLISHING AN ANIMAL BITE CENTER IN ALL RURAL HEALTH UNITS
IN THE COUNTRY, STRENGTHENING THE NATIONAL ANTI-RABIES
PROGRAM, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “ABC Nationwide Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of all Filipinos by ensuring timely,
accessible, equitable, and affordable post-exposure prophylaxis for individuals
bitten or otherwise exposed to potentially rabid animals. Toward this end, the State
shall expand the availability of Animal Bite Centers through the Rural Health Units
in the country. The State likewise upholds a One Health approach, recognizing the
interdependence of human health, animal health, and environmental factors in the
prevention and control of rabies, in coordination with the international commitment
to eliminate dog-mediated human rabies by 2030.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. ﻿Animal Bite Center (ABC) refers to a health facility or designated unit
       within a health facility duly certified by the Department of Health (DOH), to
       provide comprehensive rabies exposure management services, including
       wound cleansing, post–exposure prophylaxis, and rabies immunoglobulin
       administration, in accordance with national standards;​

          b. ﻿Anti-Rabies Vaccine refers to any World Health Organization
       (WHO)-prequalified cell culture or embryonated egg-based rabies vaccine
       registered with the Food and Drug Administration and approved for use in
       post-exposure prophylaxis;​
         c.​ ﻿Category of Exposure refers to the WHO’s classification of animal bite
      exposures into Category I (no exposure), Category II (minor exposure), and
      Category III (severe exposure), which determines the required course of
      post-exposure prophylaxis;​

         d.​ ﻿Cold Chain Management refers to the system of handling, storing, and
      transporting vaccines within required temperature ranges to maintain their
      efficacy from manufacture to administration;​

         e.​ Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Area or GIDA refers to a
      community physically and socio-economically separated from mainstream
      society as defined under DOH Administrative Order No. 2012-0012 and
      Republic Act (RA) No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Act”;​

         f.​ ﻿One Health refers to the collaborative, multisectoral, and
      transdisciplinary approach, working at the local, regional, national, and
      global levels, recognizing the interconnection between humans, animals,
      plants, and their shared environment;​

         g.​ ﻿Post-Exposure   Prophylaxis (PEP) refers to the comprehensive
      management of a person exposed to a potentially rabid animal, which
      includes wound treatment, anti-rabies vaccination, and, where indicated,
      administration of rabies immunoglobulin;​

         h.​ Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) refers to either human (HRIG) or
      equine (ERIG) rabies immunoglobulin administered as part of PEP for
      Category Ill exposures, to provide immediate passive immunity;​

         i.​ Rural Health Unit (RHU) refers to the primary healthcare facility
      established and operated by a municipality or city government, staffed by a
      municipal or city health officer; and​

         j.​ Suspect Rabid Animal refers to any animal displaying behavior
      consistent with rabies infection or any animal that bites a person without
      provocation, whether or not the animal is subsequently confirmed to be
      rabid.

        SEC. 4. Mandatory Establishment of Animal Bite Centers in All Rural Health
Units. – All Rural Health Units nationwide shall establish, operate, and
continuously maintain a duly certified Animal Bite Center within their respective
facilities. Each RHU-based ABC shall provide the following services, in accordance
with standards prescribed by the Department:

         a.​ ﻿Immediate wound cleansing, debridement,           and   clinical   risk
      assessment based on the category of exposure;​
         b.​ ﻿Administration of anti-rabies vaccines following the complete
      prescribed regimen;​

         c.​ Administration of rabies immunoglobulin for Category III exposures;​

         d.​ ﻿Appropriate referral of severe, complicated, or immunocompromised
      cases to higher-level health facilities;​

         e.​ ﻿Systematic surveillance and reporting of animal bite incidents and
      rabies exposures to the DOH and relevant agencies;​

         f.​ ﻿Observation, follow-up, and completion of vaccination schedules;​

        g.​ ﻿Coordination with local veterinary services for the observation and,
      where warranted, the testing of suspect animals;​

         h.​ ﻿Sustained community education on rabies prevention, responsible
      animal ownership, and the importance of completing the full vaccination
      regimen; and​

          i.​ ﻿Such other services as may be prescribed by the DOH through
      subsequent administrative orders, circulars, and issuances under the
      National Rabies Prevention and Control Program, including any updated
      clinical protocols, surveillance requirements, and biologics standards
      consistent with prevailing WHO recommendations and the One Health
      approach.

      SEC. 5. Phased Implementation. – The establishment of ABCs in all RHUs shall
be implemented in three (3) phases, as follows:

         a.​ ﻿Phase I: Within three (3) years from effectivity, all RHUs in highly
      urbanized cities and component cities, as defined under RA No. 7160,
      otherwise known as the “Local Government Code”, shall establish operational
      ABCs. ​

         b.​ ﻿Phase II: Within two (2) years from effectivity: All RHUs in
      municipalities and cities not classified as GIDA but without an existing
      DOH-certified ABC within a ten (10)-kilometer radius, or without accessible
      transport linkages to an existing ABC, shall establish operational ABCs.​

          c.​ Phase III; Within three (3) years from effectivity: All remaining RHUs
      nationwide, including those in municipalities and cities classified as GIDAs,
      as well as those located in provinces with a rabies incidence rate above the
      national average, shall establish and operate compliant ABCs. The extended
      timeline for this phase recognizes the additional personnel, infrastructure,
      and procurement requirements necessary to establish fully operational ABCs
      in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.
       The DOH, in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), shall issue and maintain an updated master list of RHUs and
their compliance status, made publicly available through DOH’s official website and
the DILG’s LGU portal.

       SEC. 6. Standards and Certification. – All RHU-based ABCs shall comply with
the standards, protocols, and certification requirements established by the DOH
under the National Rabies Prevention and Control Program. The DOH shall issue
the necessary certifications upon finding that an RHU-based ABC satisfies
minimum standards for facilities, equipment, cold chain, staffing, and service
delivery. Certification shall be valid for a period of three (3) years, subject to
periodic monitoring, unannounced inspections, and renewal.

      The DOH shall provide technical assistance, training, and capacity-building
support to RHUs to facilitate compliance.

        SEC. 7. Licensing and Accreditation. – No ABC established under this Act shall
operate without a valid License to Operate and corresponding certification issued
by the DOH. Continued operation shall be subject to periodic inspection and
compliance audits. Failure to renew certification, material non-compliance with
applicable standards, or substantiated reports of substandard care shall constitute
sufficient ground for the suspension or revocation of authority to operate, without
prejudice to applicable administrative and criminal liability under existing law.

        SEC. 8. Staffing Requirements. – Each RHU-based ABC shall maintain
minimum staffing adequate to ensure continuous, effective service delivery, which
shall include:​

         a.​ At least one (1) physician trained in rabies exposure management and
      post-exposure prophylaxis who shall serve as the ABC-in-charge;​

         b.​ At least one (I) nurse or midwife trained in vaccine administration and
      adverse event monitoring; and​

         c.​ At least one (1) health worker trained in vaccine handling and cold
      chain management.​

       In municipalities where physician staffing is limited, the DOH may
authorize, through issuance of appropriate guidelines, trained nurses or midwives
to perform rabies exposure management under physician teleconsultation protocols,
subject to applicable scope-of-practice regulations under RA No. 9173, otherwise
known as the “Philippine Nursing Act of 2002”; and RA No. 7392, otherwise known as
the “Philippine Midwifery Act of 1992”. LGUs may augment staffing levels as
necessary to meet service demands.

      SEC. 9. Continuing Professional Development. – All personnel assigned to
RHU-based ABCs shall undergo mandatory training prior to assignment and
periodic recertification not less than once every three (3) years thereafter, covering
rabies exposure management, post-exposure prophylaxis protocols, vaccine
administration, cold chain management, adverse event monitoring and reporting,
and rabies surveillance. The DOH, in coordination with the Professional Regulation
Commission and relevant professional regulatory boards, shall develop and accredit
continuing education units for such training.

       SEC. 10. Supply of Vaccines, Biologics, and Equipment. – The DOH shall ensure
the continuous, adequate, and equitable supply of anti-rabies vaccines, rabies
immunoglobulin, wound care supplies, personal protective equipment, and cold
chain equipment necessary for the uninterrupted operation of all RHU-based ABCs.
Procurement shall be governed by RA No. 9184, otherwise known as the
“Government Procurement Reform Act”, and shall prioritize quality-assured and
internationally prequalified products. The DOH shall coordinate with the
Department of Budget and Management to ensure timely release of procurement
funds to prevent stock-outs.

       SEC. 11. National and Regional Buffer Stock System. – The DOH shall establish
and maintain a national buffer stock system, with corresponding regional buffer
depots, for anti-rabies vaccines and rabies immunoglobulin. Buffer stocks shall be
maintained at a level sufficient to supply all RHU-based ABCs for a minimum
period of ninety (90) days, and shall be replenished continuously to prevent
depletion. RHU-based ABCs shall maintain minimum facility-level stock levels
based on projected utilization rates as determined by the Department. All stock
movements shall be recorded in a real-time vaccine inventory management system
accessible to the Department and relevant LGU health offices.

       SEC. 12. Telemedicine and Remote Clinical Support. – The DOH shall establish
and maintain a telemedicine and teleconsultation support system accessible to all
RHU-based ABCs, with priority for facilities in GIDAs and remote areas, to facilitate
realtime clinical guidance on rabies exposure management, complex cases, and
adverse event response. This system shall be integrated into the DOH’s existing
digital health infrastructure under RA No. 11223 and the National Digital Health
Ecosystem. Internet connectivity gaps in GIDAs shall be coordinated with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) for resolution
under applicable connectivity programs.

       SEC. 13. PhilHealth Benefit Coverage. – The Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth) shall, within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this
Act, revise or expand its benefit packages to ensure comprehensive coverage for
post-exposure prophylaxis services, including the complete series of anti-rabies
vaccinations and rabies immunoglobulin, whether availed at RHU-based ABCs or at
higher-level health facilities. Coverage shall apply to all PhilHealth members and
their qualified dependents. Consistent with the Universal Health Care Act, no
indigent or financially incapacitated patient shall be denied access to treatment on
account of inability to pay. PhilHealth shall report compliance to Congress within
one hundred eighty (180) days from effectivity of this Act.
        SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
operationalization of this Act, including the construction or renovation of ABC
facilities, procurement of equipment and initial vaccine stocks, and training of RHU
personnel, shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH under
the National Rabies Prevention and Control Program. Thereafter, such sums as may
be necessary for the continued implementation, operation, and monitoring of
RHU-based ABCs shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act
under the DOH.

        LGUs shall provide counterpart funding for the infrastructure, staffing
augmentation, logistics, operations and maintenance, and community outreach
activities of RHU-based ABCs from their respective local health funds, including the
Health Facilities Enhancement Program allocations, Special Education Fund where
applicable, and other available local fiscal resources. Provinces, cities, and
municipalities may enter into inter-LGU agreements to jointly establish and operate
shared ABCs in areas where demand or resources do not justify independent
facilities.

       No service fee shall be charged to indigent patients, children below eighteen
(18) years of age, or senior citizens aged sixty (60) years and above. Reasonable
cost-recovery fees may be imposed on other patients, provided that no patient shall
be denied emergency post-exposure prophylaxis on account of inability to pay.
RHU-based ABCs shall maintain a revolving fund from such fees, subject to the
usual accounting and auditing rules and regulations.

       SEC. 15. Role of Local Government Units. – LGUs, in coordination with their
respective local health boards, shall support the establishment and operation of
RHU-based ABCs. They shall:

         a.​ Provide adequate, appropriately located space within or immediately
      adjacent to the RHU premises;​

         b.​ Enact and enforce local ordinances on responsible animal ownership,
      mandatory dog registration, and stray animal management, in coordination
      with the Department of Agriculture Bureau of Animal Industry (DA-BAI);​

         c.​ Support community-based rabies prevention activities, including mass
      dog vaccination campaigns, in partnership with barangay health workers
      and the local veterinary office;​

         d.​ Coordinate with barangay officials for timely surveillance, case
      reporting, and tracing of suspect animals; and​

         e.​ Include rabies prevention and the sustainability of RHU-based ABC
      operations in their annual investment plans for health submitted to the DOH.

      SEC. 16. Compliance, Monitoring, and Administrative Accountability. – The DOH
and the DILG shall jointly monitor LGU compliance with the mandates of this Act.
The failure of any LGU to establish or maintain a compliant, operational, and
certified ABC within the period prescribed in Section 5 herein shall be reported to
the Office of the Ombudsman and to the Sangguniang Panlalawigan or
Sangguniang Panlungsod with jurisdiction, and shall subject the responsible local
chief executive and health officer to the following consequences:

         a.​ ﻿A notice of non-compliance, requiring submission of a written
       Compliance Action Plan within thirty (30) days;​

           b.​ ﻿Technical intervention and supervised implementation by the DOH
       and the DILG, at the expense of the non-compliant LGU, upon continued
       failure after sixty (60) days from notice; and​

           c.​ Administrative sanctions in accordance with RA No. 7160, the Civil
       Service Law, and other applicable statutes, upon a finding of willful or
       grossly negligent non-compliance, without prejudice to other civil or criminal
       liability under existing law.

     The DILG shall include ABC compliance indicators in the annual Seal of
Good Local Governance (SGLG) assessment and other LGU performance evaluation
frameworks.

       SEC. 17. Integrated One Health Rabies Surveillance System. – The DOH, in
coordination with the DA-BAI and relevant LGUs, shall establish and maintain an
integrated human-animal rabies surveillance system consistent with the One Health
approach. All RHU-based ABCs shall submit standardized exposure reports,
vaccination records, and adverse event notifications through the electronic
Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) system or such successor
system as the DOH may designate. The surveillance system shall enable real-time
tracking of animal bite incidence, vaccine utilization, PEP completion rates, and
post-vaccination adverse events, and shall serve as the evidence base for policy
adjustments, resource allocation, and the phased implementation updates required
under Section 5 herein.

       SEC. 18. Private Sector Participation. – Private hospitals, clinics, and Animal
Bite Treatment Centers (ABTCs) are encouraged to complement the public
RHU-based ABC system by participating in the National Rabies Prevention and
Control Program. The DOH may enter into memoranda of agreement with private
health facilities for the referral, treatment, and follow-up of animal bite cases, subject
to applicable PhilHealth reimbursement arrangements. Private sector facilities shall
comply with the same DOH standards and reporting requirements applicable to
RHU-based ABCs. Tax incentives for private facilities that participate in
underserved and GIDAs shall be subject to applicable provisions of the Tax Code
and applicable investment promotion laws.

      SEC. 19. Access and Equity Safeguards. – No individual bitten or exposed to a
potentially rabid animal shall be denied access to post-exposure prophylaxis by
reason of inability to pay, citizenship status, geographic location, or any other
ground. The following shall receive priority access and, where applicable, free
services at all RHU-based ABCs:

          a.​ ﻿Children below eighteen (18) years of age;​

          b.​ Senior citizens, persons with disabilities, and pregnant women;​

         c.​ ﻿Indigent patients as determined by the Department of Social Welfare
      and Development (DSWD); and​

          d.​ ﻿Patients with Category Ill exposures, regardless of socioeconomic
      status.

       The DOH shall ensure that all RHU-based ABCs maintain sufficient buffer
stock of vaccines and RIG to service patients unable to pay, through the buffer stock
system established under Section 11 of this Act.

       SEC. 20. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. – All patient records, exposure
reports, vaccination data, and surveillance information collected under this Act shall
be treated with strict confidentiality and protected in accordance with RA No. 10173,
otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012” and its implementing rules. The
National Privacy Commission shall be consulted by the DOH in establishing data
governance protocols for the integrated surveillance system. No patient information
shall be disclosed to third parties, including insurance companies, employers, or law
enforcement, without the patient’s informed written consent, except as required by
applicable law for public health reporting purposes.

      SEC. 21. Monitoring and Performance Evaluation. – DOH shall establish a
comprehensive monitoring and evaluation framework with measurable key
performance indicators, including but not limited to:

         a.​ ﻿Number and percentage of RHUs with certified, operational ABCs,
      disaggregated by phase, region, and LGU income class;​

         b.​ Timeliness of first-dose PEP administration following exposure within
      twenty-four (24) hours,​

          c.​ ﻿PEP completion rates per cohort;​

          d.​ ﻿Vaccine and RIG stock-out days per facility;​

          e.​ Reported adverse events and response timeliness;​

         f.​ ﻿Annual human rabies case fatality rates, disaggregated by age group
      and region; and​

          g.​ ﻿LGU compliance rates per phase target.
       The DOH shall submit an annual implementation report to the President and
to both Houses of Congress, and shall make such report publicly accessible through
the Department’s official website, not later than the thirty-first (31st) of March of the
following calendar year. The Commission on Audit shall conduct a performance
audit of the program not later than three (3) years from the effectivity of this Act.

        SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the DA-BAI, the DILG,
PhilHealth, relevant professional societies, patient advocacy groups, and other
stakeholders, shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)
necessary for the effective implementation of this Act. The IRR shall be published in
the Official Gazette and in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation, and
shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication.

       Pending the issuance of the IRR, existing administrative orders and
guidelines of the DOH governing ABCs shall remain in effect insofar as they are not
inconsistent with this Act.

       SEC. 23. Transitory Provisions. – All existing ABCs operating within Rural
Health Units as of the effectivity of this Act shall be deemed provisionally compliant
for a period not exceeding one (1) year, within which they shall apply for, and
obtain, the corresponding certification under this Act and its IRR. RHU-based ABCs
that have already obtained DOH certification under prior administrative issuances
shall be credited toward Phase I compliance under Section 5 of this Act, subject to
verification by the Department.

       SEC. 24. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as
repealing or diminishing the provisions of RA No. 9482, otherwise known as the
“Anti-Rabies Act of 2007”, RA No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government
Code”, or RA No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care Act”, except
insofar as they are inconsistent herewith.

      SEC. 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
