                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines, importing over 90% of its oil primarily from the Middle East, faces acute
vulnerability to global oil shocks, as evidenced by consecutive weeks of fuel price hikes through
early March 2026, with diesel and gasoline increases totaling several pesos per liter. These surges,
driven by geopolitical tensions and supply disruptions, have escalated domestic energy, transport,
and Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) costs, contributing to inflation pressures, particularly in
housing or utilities (up to 3.3% in January 2026) and secondary effects on food and agriculture
comprising 36% of the Consumer Price Index (CPI).

Data by GMA Integrated News Research shows that this is the ‘first time in Metro Manila that the
price of diesel has exceeded Php 100 per liter.’ One jeepney driver interviewed by GMA News
also mentioned that ‘it is his first time to experience such an oil price in the three decades he has
been jeepney driver.’

Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), which account for 99.6% of businesses
nationwide, are among those most affected. Operating on thin margins, even modest increases in
logistics and input costs can significantly strain their finances and, in many cases, threaten their
ability to remain operational. The situation is further complicated by the weakening peso, which
reached ₱60 to $1 for the first time, and projections that economic growth could be trimmed by
about 0.2–0.3% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP). At the household level, residential renters also
face mounting pressure, as some landlords have recently raised rents by about 2–4%, increasing
the risk of evictions. This adds to the financial strain on families in an economy where fuel costs
indirectly affect roughly 28% of consumer prices.

This bill provides immediate economic relief by imposing a temporary freeze on increases in
residential and MSME commercial rental rates, allowing the deferment of housing loan payments
without penalties, authorizing emergency loans from the SSS and GSIS and declaring a
moratorium on the accrual of interest on government loans. The measure aims to stabilize markets
and protect vulnerable sectors from the adverse effects of rising fuel costs.
Moreover, this measure authorizes government agencies and its subsidiaries to partner with duly
registered transport providers to deliver free public transportation during national or local
emergencies, ensuring continued mobility for the riding public. It likewise mandates the DTI and
DOE, in coordination with relevant agencies, to keep prices of petroleum products, basic
commodities and utilities including telcos and internet service providers remain reasonable by
preventing unjustified increases, with authority to impose or recommend price ceilings, curb
hoarding and profiteering, and implement necessary interventions such as requiring price
justification, subsidies, or buffer stocking to stabilize supply and protect consumers.

Grounded in data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and the Department of Energy
(DOE), the proposed interventions are designed to help temper inflationary pressures, projected to
exceed four percent (4%) while providing regulatory clarity and enforceability through the
appropriate government agencies.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROVIDING IMMEDIATE ECONOMIC RELIEF MEASURES IN RESPONSE
                      TO FUEL PRICE HIKES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Crisis Relief Act of 2026”
SEC 2. Declaration of State Policy— It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
vulnerable households from the adverse effects of sudden fuel price hikes, which exacerbate
inflation and strain disposable incomes. Toward this end, the State shall provide temporary but
immediate relief, while ensuring fiscal sustainability and equitable burden-sharing.

SEC. 3. Coverage Period.— The relief measures under this Act shall apply for six (6) months
from its effectivity, or until the President certifies via proclamation that national average fuel prices
(as reported by the Department of Energy) have stabilized below P60 per liter for gasoline and
diesel, whichever comes first.

SEC 4. Relief Measures.— The following temporary measures are mandated:
(a) Freeze on Increase of Residential Rental Rates. —Residential rental rates for dwelling units
shall be frozen at their current levels for the duration of the coverage period provided in this Act.
No landlord, lessor, or property manager shall impose any increase in rental payments during such
period.

(b) Freeze on Increase of Commercial Rental Rates for MSMEs.—Rental rates for commercial
establishments, specifically those operated by micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs),
shall likewise be temporarily frozen, and landlords shall be prohibited from increasing lease
payments during the coverage period of this Act.

(c) Deferred or Staggered Payment of Housing Loan Amortizations.—Borrowers with housing
loans under the PAG-IBIG (Pagtutulungan sa Kinabukasan: Ikas, Bangko, Industriya at
Gobyerno), Home Development Mutual Fund (HDMF) are allowed temporary deferment or
staggered payment of mortgage amortizations during the coverage period, without the imposition
of penalties, surcharges, or additional interest, subject to implementing guidelines.

(d) Emergency Loans from SSS and GSIS. — The SSS and GSIS shall provide emergency loans
of up to Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) to eligible members at a concessional interest rate not
exceeding five percent (5%) per annum, repayable in up to twenty-four (24) monthly installments.
Eligibility requires active contribution status and proof of impact from fuel price surges;
applications shall be processed within fifteen (15) days via digital or agency channels.

(e) Moratorium on Interest Accrual for Government Loans. — No interest shall accrue on the
principal balances of loans extended by government financial institutions, including the Social
Security System (SSS), Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), Pag-IBIG Fund, and Land
Bank of the Philippines, for qualified borrowers during the coverage period. Qualified borrowers
must be in good standing as of the effectivity of this Act and certify economic hardship due to fuel
price hikes, subject to guidelines issued by the Department of Finance (DOF) in coordination with
the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).

SEC.5. Public–Private Transport Partnership for Free Public Transportation. —
During periods of national or local emergency as may be declared by the President or the
appropriate Local Chief Executive, all National Government Agencies (NGAs), Government-
Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GOCCs), and Local Government Units (LGUs) are hereby
authorized and directed to enter into contracts, memoranda of agreement, or other lawful
arrangements with duly registered transport service providers, including but not limited to public
utility vehicles (PUVs), transport cooperatives, and Transport Network Vehicle Service (TNVS)
operators, for the purpose of providing free transportation services to the riding public.

SEC. 6. Price Stabilization and Anti-Profiteering Measures During Emergencies. — The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Energy (DOE) and the National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC), shall ensure that the prices of petroleum products, basic
commodities and utilities including telcos and Internet Service Providers (ISPs) remain
reasonable and free from unjustified increases, and may impose or recommend price ceilings when
necessary. Upon the declaration of a national or local emergency, the State shall protect the public
by directing the DTI, DOE, and other relevant agencies to prevent hoarding, profiteering,
cartelization, and other anti-competitive practices; require justification for price increases when
warranted; and implement appropriate measures, including subsidies or buffer stocking, to
stabilize supply and prices, consistent with existing laws.

SEC. 7. Prohibited Acts.— It shall be unlawful for any person or business entity to:

       (a) Refuse implementation of relief measures or impose prohibited increases/fees.
       (b) Evict tenants/lessees for non-payment during grace periods, absent abandonment or
           non-use.
       (c) Charge interests, penalties and surcharges on deferred/staggered payments under this
           Act.
       (d) Charge interest, penalties, or surcharges on moratoriums, emergency loans, or
           staggered electricity payments under this Act;
        (e) Deny eligible applications for emergency loans or staggered payments without just
            cause.
        (f) Demand, solicit, collect, or receive any fare, fee, donation, or any form of compensation
            from passengers for the use of free transport services provided under this Act.
        (g) Any unjustified increase in prices or violation of this Section shall be subject to
            penalties in accordance with existing laws, including but not limited to Republic Act
            No. 7581, as amended, otherwise known as the “Price Act,” and other applicable laws
            and regulations.

SEC. 8. Penalties.— In addition to existing laws, violations shall incur imprisonment of two (2)
to six (6) months, a fine of P10,000 to P1,000,000, or both, at the court’s discretion. Corporate
offenders’ responsible officers are jointly liable.

SEC. 9. Enforcement and Monitoring.— The Department of Trade Industry (DTI), in coordination
with Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), Bureau of Internal
Revenue (BIR), Department of Finance (DOF), Department of Energy (DOE), Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas (BSP), Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), Social Security System (SSS),
Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) and Local Government
Units (LGUs), shall monitor compliance, handle complaints via hotlines/portals, and submit
quarterly reports to Congress.

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).— Within thirty (30) days from effectivity
of this Act, the SSS, GSIS, ERC, DTI, DOF, BIR, DHSUD, BSP, DOE, and Pag-IBIG shall issue
joint IRR, including eligibility forms and awareness campaigns.

SEC. 11. Appropriation.— Necessary funds shall be sourced from agency budgets or
unprogrammed appropriations under the Office of President.

SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid,
the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
