       PROMOTING AWARENESS OF CHRONIC FATIGUE SYNDROME

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Myalgic encephalomyelitis or chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a
complex, chronic illness characterized by debilitating fatigue, post-exertional
malaise, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive difficulties, and other symptoms that
substantially limit daily activities. No definitive diagnostic test or universally
established cure currently exists, and prevalence estimates vary because of
differences in diagnostic criteria.1

      Management generally focuses on individualized medical care,
symptom monitoring, sleep support, and energy management to prevent
worsening after physical or mental activity. Patients may also experience
stigma because the illness is often invisible and poorly understood, resulting
in delayed diagnosis, inadequate treatment, and social isolation. 2

       In the Philippines, research and official data specifically concerning
myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) remain
limited. The absence of reliable national prevalence data highlights the need
for local epidemiological studies, clinical guidelines, health-worker training,
and improved reporting and referral systems. International research describes
ME/CFS as a disabling, multisystem illness characterized by persistent
fatigue, post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive difficulties

evidence that the condition is uncommon. Limited recognition, the absence of
a definitive diagnostic test, and the possibility of misdiagnosis may contribute
to underreporting. Accordingly, this measure seeks to promote awareness,
strengthen clinical recognition, reduce stigma, support research, and improve
access to appropriate assessment, treatment, rehabilitation, mental-health
support, and social services for persons with ME/CFS.

                         AN ACT
     PROMOTING AWARENESS OF CHRONIC FATIGUE SYNDROME

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Chronic Fatigue
Syndrome Awareness Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS) is
widely misunderstood such that patients are viewed as malingering, wanting
attention or feigning CFS-like syndrome. The lack of recognition of the disease
and support for CFS patients affect them psychologically thereby prolonging
their recovery from the disease. It is in accord with the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them that awareness of CFS be raised to dispel the
social stigma associated with the disease so that the patients may attain early
recovery and restoration to a productive and fulfilling life.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the term:

      a) “DOH” refers to the Department of Health;

      b) “Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS)” refers to CFS is an energy deficit
         disorder, the impact primarily is on the immune system, the brain,
         the muscles and endocrine system; and

      c) “PIA” refers to the Philippine Information Agency.

SECTION 4. National Public Awareness Campaign. – The Department of
Health (DOH) in cooperation with the Philippine Information Agency (PIA)
shall carry out a national campaign to increase public awareness and
knowledge of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome.
Activities under the national campaign shall include:

      1. The dissemination of information on recent findings about CFS, its
         probable causes, prevention, diagnosis, and available treatments.

      2. Information and workshops on how to provide the necessary
         emotional support to a family member, relative, or friend suffering
         from CFS.

SECTION 5. Implementing Agency. – The Department of Health (DOH), in
cooperation with the Philippine Information Agency (PIA), is hereby authorized
and directed to adopt a system to implement and carry out the provisions of
this Act. The DOH shall, within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
promulgate rules and regulations to carry out and enforce the provisions of
this Act.

SECTION 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act is hereby authorized to be appropriated from the annual
budget of the Department of Health.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the
other provisions hereof.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, and issuances,
or portions thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are
hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
