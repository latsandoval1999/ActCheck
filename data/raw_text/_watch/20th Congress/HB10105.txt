                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to reinstitute absolute divorce as an additional legal remedy for
marriages that are beyond repair. It does not diminish the institution of marriage;
rather, it recognizes that in exceptional circumstances, preserving individual dignity,
personal safety, and the best interests of children requires a lawful mechanism for
the dissolution of marriage.

       Under existing law, the dissolution of marriage is generally limited to
annulment, declaration of nullity, or legal separation. These remedies are often
expensive, time-consuming, and available only under limited legal grounds. Legal
separation, moreover, does not dissolve the marital bond, leaving spouses unable to
remarry despite prolonged separation or the complete breakdown of the marital
relationship. As a result, many Filipinos remain trapped in failed marriages,
exposing themselves and their children to continued emotional, psychological, and
economic hardship.

       Comparative experience likewise demonstrates that the Philippines remains
one of the few jurisdictions that does not generally recognize absolute divorce for its
citizens. The absence of this remedy has created legal inequities, particularly for
spouses who have long been separated or who are victims of domestic violence,
abandonment, or repeated marital misconduct. The availability of divorce in other
jurisdictions has shown that legal dissolution, when accompanied by adequate
judicial safeguards and child protection measures, provides parties with legal
certainty while promoting accountability and orderly settlement of family relations.

       This proposed measure provides specific and judicially determinable grounds
for divorce, ensures due process before the Family Courts, and establishes
safeguards against fraud, coercion, and collusion. It likewise preserves the
legitimacy of children, protects their custody and support, provides for spousal
support where warranted, governs the equitable distribution of property, and
imposes penalties for non-compliance with court-ordered obligations. It further
institutionalizes counseling, reconciliation mechanisms, legal assistance, and
oversight measures to ensure that divorce remains a remedy of last resort while
safeguarding vulnerable family members.
       Rather than the perceived weakening of marriage, this bill establishes a
comprehensive legal framework for addressing exceptional cases where the marital
relationship has irretrievably failed while ensuring that the rights of spouses,
children, and third parties remain fully protected.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
REINSTITUTING ABSOLUTE DIVORCE AS AN ALTERNATIVE MODE FOR
THE DISSOLUTION OF MARRIAGE

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Absolute Divorce Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect marriage as an inviolable social institution and the foundation of the family,
while recognizing that certain marriages may have irretrievably broken down
beyond reconciliation. Further, the State shall provide a judicial remedy for absolute
divorce under clearly defined grounds and procedures, consistent with due process
of law, to protect the rights and welfare of the spouses and their children, and allow
qualified parties to remarry in accordance with the law.

       To this end, the State shall adopt a policy on absolute divorce consistent with
the fundamental freedoms guaranteed under the Constitution, the rights guaranteed
under Republic Act (RA) No. 9710, otherwise known as the “The Magna Carta of
Women”, the provisions of the United Nations Universal Declaration of Human
Rights, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, the Convention on
the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, the Convention on
the Rights of the Child, and other international human rights instruments to which
the Philippines is a party.

        SEC. 3. Guiding Principles. – The implementation of this Act shall be guided by
the following principles:

          a. Absolute divorce shall be granted only upon a judicial determination
       that the marriage has irretrievably broken down under the grounds and
       procedures prescribed in this Act;

          b. Proceedings for absolute divorce shall be accessible, affordable and
       expeditious, while ensuring due process and the protection of the rights of all

parties;​

   c. Nothing in this Act shall preclude either spouse from availing of the
remedies of legal separation, annulment of marriage, or declaration of nullity
of marriage under Executive Order No. 209, otherwise known as “The Family
Code of the Philippines”, as amended;​

   d. The rights and welfare of spouses, particularly those subjected to
abuse, violence, coercive control, or other forms of marital misconduct, shall
be protected throughout the proceedings;​

   e. A sixty (60)-day cooling-off period shall be instituted to afford the
parties an opportunity for reconciliation before the proceedings continue;​

    f. Every decree of absolute divorce shall include provisions for the care,
custody, and support of children, protection of their legitime, termination and
liquidation of the conjugal partnership of gains or the absolute community,
and alimony for the offended spouse; and​

   g. The State shall continue to strengthen marriage and family life through
responsive pre-marital, marital, and family support programs, including
counseling and other appropriate interventions.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

    a. Abandonment refers to the act of a spouse leaving the conjugal home
without justifiable cause and with a deliberate intention of creating a
perpetual separation. The desertion amounts to an absolute cessation of
marital relations, rights, and duties. The abandonment may also be physical
estrangement or financial desertion;​

    b. ﻿Absolute divorce refers to the judicial dissolution of a marriage or the
termination of the bond of matrimony where the spouses return to their
status of being single with the right to contract marriage again;​

   c. ﻿Alcoholism refers to excessive consumption of alcoholic beverages at a
level that interferes with physical or mental health, and social, family, or
occupational responsibilities;​

   d. ﻿Alimony refers to spousal support adjudged in the divorce decree by
the proper Family Court in favor of the offended spouse against the offending
spouse;​

   e. ﻿Bigamous marriage refers to a second or subsequent marriage
contracted before the former marriage has been legally dissolved, or before
the absent spouse has been declared presumptively dead by means of a
judgment rendered in the proper proceedings;​

   f. ﻿Child refers to a common child of the spouses below eighteen (18)

adoption or one whose custody and care is judicially decreed to be given to a
guardian. In the proper cases, a child also refers to an offspring of one of the
spouses;​

   g. ﻿Child custody refers to the parental authority and care of a child as
decreed by a competent court when the parents of the child are divorced;​

    h. ﻿Child support refers to a method of compensating a parent needing
financial support for raising and sheltering a child or children by the other
parent who has the means to pay for the expenses associated with child
support. The proper Family Court shall determine the amount of support,
based on the financial capacity of the parent giving support and the needs of
the child or children requiring support pursuant to the provisions of The
Family Code of the Philippines;​

   i. ﻿Chronic gambling refers to an impulse control disorder in which a

disrupt or damage family, work and social relations, and vocational pursuits;​

   j. Competent court refers to the proper Family Court established under
RA No. 8369, otherwise known as the “Family Courts Act of 1997”, which shall
exercise jurisdiction both over the absolute divorce proceeding and the
persons of the petitioner and respondent spouses;​

   k. Court-assisted petitioner refers to a petitioner-spouse who has

other which may be committed through:​

          1. ﻿Physical violence;

          2. ﻿Psychological and emotional violence;

          3. Sexual violence; or

          4. Economic abuse;

   m. ﻿Drug addiction refers to habitual dependence on a prohibited drug or
drugs;

       SEC. 5. Grounds for Absolute Divorce. – A petition for absolute divorce may be
filed on any of the following grounds:

          a. Any of the following acts or circumstances:

                1. ﻿Physical violence or grossly abusive conduct directed against
             the petitioner, a common child, or a child of the petitioner;​

                2. ﻿Physical violence or moral pressure to compel the petitioner to
             change religious or political affiliation;​

                3. Attempt by the respondent to corrupt or induce the petitioner, a
             common child, or a child of the petitioner to engage in prostitution, or
             connivance in such corruption or inducement;​

               4. ﻿Final judgment sentencing the respondent to imprisonment of
             more than six (6) years, even if pardoned;​

                5. Drug addiction, habitual alcoholism, or chronic gambling of the
             respondent;​

                6. The respondent’s persistent refusal or inability to fulfill the
             essential obligations of marriage arising from sexual incompatibility or
             concealment existing prior to or during the marriage;​

               7. ﻿Contracting by the respondent of a subsequent bigamous
             marriage, whether in the Philippines or abroad;​

                8. ﻿Marital infidelity, sexual perversion, or having a child with
             another person other than one’s spouse during the subsistence of the
             marriage, except where a child is conceived through assisted
             reproductive technology with the mutual agreement of the spouses, or
             where the wife bears a child after being a victim of rape;​

                9. Attempt by the respondent against the life of the petitioner, a
             common child, or a child of the petitioner;​

                 10. Abandonment of petitioner by the respondent without
             justifiable cause for more than one (1) year; or




                                         5
                 11. Other forms of domestic or marital abuse as defined under
              Section 4 of this Act.

          b. The existence of any of the following circumstances:

                  1. A decree of legal separation has been final and executory for at
              least two (2) years;

                 2. The spouses have lived separately in fact for at least five (5)
              years immediately preceding the filing of the petition, and
              reconciliation is highly improbable;

                 3. Psychological incapacity of either spouse under Article 36 of
              The Family Code of the Philippines, whether such incapacity existed at
              the time of the celebration of the marriage or arose thereafter;

                 4. One (1) spouse undergoes sex reassignment surgery or legally
              transitions to a gender different from that assigned at birth, resulting in
              the irretrievable breakdown of marriage; or

                 5. Irreconcilable differences as defined in Section 4 of this Act.

          c. Any of the grounds for annulment of marriage under Article 45 of The
       Family Code of the Philippines.

       The grant of absolute divorce based on domestic or marital abuse or violation
of RA No. 9262, otherwise known as the “Anti-Violence against Women and their
Children Act of 2004”, shall be without prejudice to the prosecution of the offending
or errant spouse under the said Act.

       SEC. 6. Civil Recognition of a Valid Foreign Divorce. – A valid foreign divorce
secured by either the alien or Filipino spouse has the effect of a divorce under this
Act without going through the judicial process. Said foreign divorce must be
authenticated by the Philippine Consul in or proximate to the foreign country where
it was secured, and subsequently registered with the proper Civil Registry Office in
the Philippines or the Office of the Philippine Consul abroad where the Filipino
spouse is residing.​

       SEC. 7. Valid Canonical or Church Dissolution, or Declaration of Nullity of
Marriage. – A marriage which is nullified or dissolved by the proper matrimonial
tribunal of the Roman Catholic Church or any other recognized religious sector
denomination may be recognized by the proper Family Court upon verification that
the requirements of this Act have been complied with.

        SEC. 8. Prescription of Action. – A petition for absolute divorce shall be filed
with the proper Family Court by the petitioner or joint petitioners within ten (10)
years from the occurrence or discovery of the cause for divorce or from the
effectivity of the Absolute Divorce Act, whichever comes later.


                                           6
      SEC. 9. Procedure for Obtaining Absolute Divorce. – The following rules shall
govern petitions for absolute divorce:

          a. The filing of a verified petition before the proper Family Court by
      either the petitioner-spouse or joint petitioner-spouses, as the case may be,
      shall commence the proceedings for absolute divorce.​

          b. Except as otherwise provided in this Act, the procedures governing
      petitions for legal separation, annulment of marriage, and declaration of
      nullity of marriage under The Family Code of the Philippines, as amended,
      and the applicable Rules of Court shall apply suppletorily; Provided, That they
      are consistent with this Act.​

         c. The court shall determine the existence of the grounds for absolute
      divorce based on competent and credible evidence. No decree of absolute
      divorce shall be granted solely on the basis of a stipulation of facts or a
      confession of judgment.

         d. Upon approval of an application to litigate as a court-assisted
      petitioner, the Family Court shall:

                 1. Waive the payment of filing fees and other litigation costs;

                 2. Appoint a counsel de officio; and

                3. When        necessary,    assign    accredited social workers,
             psychologists, psychiatrists, or other qualified professionals to assist
             the petitioner, the court, and the children of the parties; without cost to
             the petitioner.

          e. ﻿The petition shall identify all known creditors of the spouses,
      including creditors of the absolute community of property or conjugal
      partnership of gains and the separate creditors of either spouse. Such
      creditors shall be notified of the proceedings, and the court shall adopt
      measures to protect their lawful interests.

          f. The public prosecutor shall investigate whether the petition is
      attended by collusion between the spouses or whether either spouse has been
      coerced into filing or consenting the petition, and shall submit a report to the
      proper Family Court within sixty (60) days from the filing of petition.

       SEC. 10. Overseas Filipinos. – Petitions filed by overseas Filipinos shall be
given priority in the scheduling of hearings. The proper Family Court shall set the
reception of evidence, upon the availability of the petitioners, for not more than two
(2) consecutive days.

       SEC. 11. Summary Judicial Proceedings. – The following grounds for absolute
divorce may be subject to summary judicial proceedings as defined in Section 4 of
this Act:

          a. ﻿When the spouses have been separated de facto for at least five (5)
       years;​

          b. When one of the spouses has contracted a bigamous marriage;

           c. When the spouses have been legally separated by judicial decree for at
       least two (2) years;​

           d. When one of the spouses has been sentenced to imprisonment for at
       least six (6) years, even if subsequently pardoned; or​

          e. When one of the spouses has undergone a sex reassignment surgery or
       has transitioned into another sex.

      A petition under this section may be filed individually or jointly by the
concerned spouses.

       SEC. 12. Mandatory Sixty-Day Cooling-Off Period. – Except for grounds under
summary judicial proceedings or those involving acts of violence against women
and their children under RA No. 9262, or an attempt against the life of the other
spouse, a common child, or a child of the petitioner, the proper Family Court shall
observe a mandatory sixty (60)-day cooling-off period from the filing of the petition
to afford the parties an opportunity for reconciliation.

       If the parties fail to reconcile upon the expiration of the cooling-off period, the
court shall immediately proceed with the trial and render judgment within one (1)
year from the expiration.

       SEC. 13. Waiver of Expert Testimony. – Expert testimony shall be dispensed
with, unless the proper Family Court decides that such testimony is indispensable in
deciding the petition for divorce.

        SEC. 14. Effects of Absolute Divorce. – A decree of absolute divorce shall have
the following effects:

          a. The marriage is dissolved and the divorced spouses shall have the
       right to contract marriage again;​

           b. The proper Family Court shall determine the custody of the minor
       children, visitation rights, child support, and parental authority in accordance
       with the best interests of the child and the pertinent provisions of the Family
       Code of the Philippines. In no case shall custody be awarded to the offending
       spouse when the divorce is granted on any of the grounds under Section 5(a)
       (1), (2), (4), (5), (7), (8), (9), and (10) of this Act; Provided, That in a subsequent


                                              8
divorce of a previously divorced spouse, the custody of the children may be
granted to the spouse with lesser number of children, subject to the discretion
of the Family Court;​

    c. ﻿Unless otherwise agreed upon in a marriage settlement, the conjugal
partnership of gains or the absolute community shall be dissolved and
liquidated in accordance with The Family Code of the Philippines. The Court
shall order the delivery of the presumptive legitime of the common children
and ensure the protection thereof in accordance with law. The partition and
distribution of the properties of the spouses, including the delivery of the
children’s presumptive legitime, shall be recorded in the appropriate civil
registry and registry of deeds and shall not prejudice third (3rd) persons acting
in good faith;​

   d. During the pendency of the divorce proceedings, the spouses shall
remain solidarily liable for obligations incurred for family expenses;​

    e. In addition to the equal share in the assets of the absolute community
or conjugal partnership, the offended spouse who is not gainfully employed
shall be entitled to spousal support or alimony from the offending spouse in
accordance with this Act and the pertinent provisions of The Family Code of
the Philippines. The amount and duration of support shall be determined by
the court after considering the needs of the obligee and the financial capacity
of the obligor: Provided, That such support shall cease upon the remarriage of
the recipient or upon such other grounds as may be provided by law;​

    f. The offended spouse may revoke donations made in favor of the
offending spouse, as well as the designation of the latter as a beneficiary in
any insurance policy, even if such designation be stipulated as irrevocable.
Such revocation shall not prejudice the rights of third (3rd) persons acquired in
good faith;​

   g. The effects of the decree on intestate succession, testamentary
disposition, donations, and insurance beneficiaries in accordance with The
Family Code of the Philippines and other applicable laws shall be observed;​

    h. The legitimate or adopted children of divorced parents shall retain
their legal status and legitimacy. A child conceived or born within three
hundred (300) days after the filing of a petition for absolute divorce shall be
considered legitimate, unless the ground for divorce is the marital infidelity
of the wife; and​

   i. After the filing of the petition for divorce, the spouses shall be entitled
to live separately. The court shall appoint a competent third party as
administrator to manage the absolute community or conjugal partnership
property during the pendency of the divorce proceedings in order to prevent
dissipation of the common properties. Prior to the discharge of the
administrator's functions and duties, the administrator shall post a surety


                                    9
      bond approved by the proper Family Court. The administrator shall have the
      same powers and duties as those of a guardian under the Rules of Court.

       SEC. 15. Support for Children. – The proper Family Court shall determine and
award child support pursuant to the pertinent provisions of The Family Code of the
Philippines, taking into primary consideration the best interests of the child.

       In determining the amount of support, the proper Family Court shall
consider the needs of the child and the financial capacity of the parents. Upon
application of either party, the proper Family Court may order an increase or
decrease in the amount of support upon showing of a substantial and material
change of circumstances.

      When the obligor is an overseas Filipino, the court shall furnish the
Department of Migrant Workers, where applicable and permitted by law, and the
overseas Filipino’s employer with a copy of the order awarding child support for
appropriate action.

       SEC. 16. Custody and Support Pendente Lite. – During the pendency of the
petition and in the absence of adequate provisions in a written agreement between
the spouses, the proper Family Court, upon application of either party, issue orders
for:

          a. Spousal support;

          b. Custody and support of the common children; and

          c. Visitation rights of the noncustodial parent.

      In issuing such orders, the court shall give the paramount consideration to
the best interests of the children, as well as their choice of the parent with whom
they wish to remain, in accordance with The Family Code of the Philippines.

       SEC. 17. Award for Damages. – The proper Family Court may, as warranted by
evidence, award actual, moral, and exemplary damages as provided for under the
Civil Code of the Philippines to the offended spouse against the offending spouse.

       SEC. 18. Conversion of Pending Petitions. – Parties who have pending petitions
under Articles 36, 45, and 55 of The Family Code of the Philippines may convert
their petitions to an action for absolute divorce under this Act without paying filing
fees.

       SEC. 19. Reconciliation During the Pendency of the Divorce Proceedings. – If the
spouses agree to reconcile during the pendency of the divorce proceedings or before
the finality of judgment of divorce, a corresponding joint manifestation under oath
duly signed by them shall be filed with the same court where the petition for
absolute divorce was filed.



                                          10
       Upon hearing of the joint manifestation and the court finds it in order, the
petition for divorce shall be dismissed.

       SEC. 20. Reconciliation After the Finality of a Divorce Decree. – After a divorce
decree has become final and executory, the former spouses may reconcile and restore
their marital relationship only by contracting a new marriage in accordance with
The Family Code of the Philippines, as amended. Such reconciliation shall not affect
the finality of the divorce decree, nor the rights and obligations that have accrued
thereunder.

      SEC. 21. Guidelines for Reconciliation. – Reconciliation under this Act shall be
governed by the following:

           a. ﻿Any reconciliation during the pendency of the divorce proceedings or
       after the finality of the divorce decree shall uphold the best interests of the
       children and protect the rights and welfare of the spouses;​

           b. ﻿The proper Family Court shall determine whether to approve a joint
       manifestation of reconciliation after considering all relevant evidence and
       ensure that such reconciliation is voluntary and free from fraud, force,
       intimidation;

           c. In petitions involving acts of violence against women and their
       children under RA No. 9262, or an attempt against the life of the offended
       spouse, a common child, or a child of the offended spouse, the court shall
       exercise due discretion in determining whether reconciliation serves the best
       interests of the parties and their children;​

          d. The reconciling spouses shall submit a verified plan of reconciliation
       under oath which shall include the following:​

                  1. ﻿The arrangements for the custody, support, and welfare of their
              children, where applicable;​

                 2. ﻿The settlement of their property relations, if necessary; and

                3. ﻿The names and addresses of all known creditors whose interests
              may be affected by the reconciliation;​

          e. ﻿The joint manifestation and verified plan of reconciliation shall be
       furnished to the creditors identified therein, and the court shall ensure that
       their lawful interests are adequately protected; and

          f. Any order approving the reconciliation shall be registered in the proper
       registry of deeds, without prejudice to the rights of creditors or third (3rd)
       persons not notified of the proceedings.​




                                          11
       SEC. 22. Penalties. – A spouse who is a party to a petition for absolute divorce
and who is found by the court to have used threats or coercion to compel the other
spouse in filing the petition, and spouses who are guilty of collusion, shall be
punished with imprisonment of five (5) years and a fine of Three hundred thousand
pesos (Php 300,000.00).

      Any offending spouse in default of providing the required spousal support or
alimony and child support under this Act shall be charged with contempt of court
and be imposed a fine of:

          a. Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) for the first default;

         b. One hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) for the second default;
      and​

         c. Two hundred thousand pesos (Php 200,000.00) each for the third and
      succeeding defaults.

       The offending spouse found guilty of indirect contempt of court shall be
punished with imprisonment thereupon until the obligation for spousal and/ or
child support has been paid.

       SEC. 23. Options. – The concerned spouse shall have the option to file a
petition for dissolution of marriage, annulment of marriage, or legal separation
under Articles 36, 45, and 55, respectively, of The Family Code of the Philippines or a
petition or joint petition for absolute divorce under this Act.

       SEC. 24. Appeals. – Within fifteen (15) calendar days from receipt of the decree
of absolute divorce, any of the spouses may file an appeal with the Court of Appeals
under the pertinent provisions of the Rules of Court.

      A motion for execution pending appeal may be issued by the Court of
Appeals upon application of the appellee. Moreover, the Court of Appeals may
dismiss an appeal forthwith once it finds the same frivolous.

        SEC. 25. Community-Based Pre-Nuptial and Post-Matrimonial Programs and
Activities. – The National Government shall, through the DSWD, implement
community-based pre-nuptial, reconciliatory phase or cooling-off counseling, and
post-matrimonial programs and activities aimed at strengthening the marital and
family life of Filipinos.

       These social welfare interventions, which will protect the stability of Filipino
families, shall be instituted by the DSWD in provinces, cities, and clusters of
municipalities in the entire country, while observing the local cultures and customs
that are prevalent in each locality.

      SEC. 26. Community-Based Women’s Desk. – The frontline desk in every
barangay established under RA No. 9710, otherwise known as “The Magna Carta of


                                          12
Women”, shall be readily available and easily accessible to provide support and
assistance to victims, especially women, of all forms of abuse and acts of violence
identified under the said Act and those provided for under RA No. 9262.

       SEC. 27. Attorney’s Fees. – In petitions with no court-assisted petitioner, the
attorney’s fees, inclusive of acceptance fee, appearance fees, and success fee, shall
not exceed Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) for the entire proceedings.
However, the petitioner and counsel can mutually agree on a higher rate of
attorney’s fees.

       SEC. 28. Creation of More Positions of Public Attorneys and Family Courts. – The
Department of Justice (DOJ) is authorized to create the necessary additional
positions for public attorneys under the Public Attorney’s Office to advocate for
court-assisted petitioners.

      In the discretion of the Supreme Court, more Family Courts may be created to
adjudicate petitions for absolute divorce.

       SEC. 29. Congressional Oversight Committee on the Absolute Divorce Act. – There
is hereby created a Congressional Oversight Committee (COC) composed of five (5)
members each from the Senate and the House of Representatives. The members of
the Senate and the House of Representatives shall be appointed by the Senate
President and the Speaker, respectively, with at least one (1) member representing
the Minority.

      The COC shall be headed by the respective Chairpersons of the Committee on
Health and Demography of the Senate and the Committee on Population and
Family Relations of the House of Representatives. The Secretariat of the COC shall
come from the existing Secretariat personnel of the Senate and the House of
Representatives committees concerned.

        The COC shall monitor and ensure the effective implementation of this Act,
recommend the necessary remedial legislation or administrative measures, and
conduct a review of this Act every five (5) years from its effectivity. The COC shall
perform such other duties and functions as may be necessary to attain the objectives
of this Act.

        SEC. 30. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be sourced from the savings of the DSWD for the current fiscal year,
and the subsequent amounts necessary for the full implementation of this Act shall
be included in the subsequent annual General Appropriation Act.

       SEC. 31. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DSWD shall, as lead
agency, together with the DOJ, the Philippine Commission on Women (PCW), and
the National Youth Commission (NYC), in consultation with at least two (2)
representatives from women’s organizations with proven track record in women
and gender rights advocacy to be identified by the PCW, and two (2) representatives
from children’s organizations with proven track record in child rights advocacy to

be identified by the NYC, promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
within sixty (60) days after the effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 32. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is

       SEC. 33. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 34. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
