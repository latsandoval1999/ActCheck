                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Article XII, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall
promote a more equitable distribution of opportunities, income, and wealth, and
shall regulate the acquisition, ownership, use, and disposition of property to
promote the common good. Article XIII, Section 4 further mandates the State to
undertake an agrarian reform program founded on the right of farmers and
regular farmworkers to own the lands they till, and to provide support services
to agriculture. These constitutional directives underscore the State’s duty to
protect farmers and strengthen agricultural productivity as pillars of national
development and food security.

       Filipino farmers continue to confront escalating fertilizer prices driven by
global supply volatility, exchange rate pressures, and domestic distribution
costs. Fertilizer constitutes a substantial portion of production expenses for rice,
corn, and other strategic crops. When input costs rise sharply, smallholder
farmers, who comprise the majority of agricultural producers, are compelled to
reduce application rates, incur debt, or operate at a loss.

      The consequences extend beyond the farm. Reduced fertilizer uses leads
to lower yields, diminished harvest quality, and an unstable domestic supply of
staple crops such as rice and corn. In turn, constrained production contributes
to upward pressure on food prices, exacerbating inflation and burdening Filipino
consumers. The vulnerability of the agricultural sector to input cost shocks,
therefore, directly affects both farmer welfare and national food price stability.

        While Republic Act No. 11203 (Rice Tariffication Law) and Republic Act No.
11598 provide certain financial assistance mechanisms, these measures are
limited in scope and do not establish a permanent, responsive system to address
fertilizer price volatility across crops and regions. Regulatory oversight under
Republic Act No. 11321 ensures fertilizer quality control but does not address
affordability concerns.
       This bill seeks to institutionalize a targeted and transparent Fertilizer
Subsidy Program to assist qualified smallholder farmers during periods of
significant input cost increases. The measure aims to stabilize agricultural
production, protect farmer incomes, mitigate inflationary pressures on food
commodities, and strengthen national food security.

     By establishing a structured and accountable support mechanism, the
proposed measure advances the State’s constitutional duty to support farmers
and promote sustainable agricultural development.


     It is for the foregoing premises that the approval of this Bill is earnestly
sought.

                               AN ACT
 INSTITUTING A FERTILIZER SUBSIDY PROGRAM AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                              THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Fertilizer and
Pesticide Subsidy Act”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State to
protect and assist the agricultural sector as an indispensable component of national
growth and development. As such, the State shall foster an enabling environment for
farmers and provide effective assistance for them to acquire a reasonable profit and
increase local production.

      SECTION 3. National Fertilizer and Pesticide Subsidy Program (NFPSP). - The
Department of Agriculture (DA) shall implement a National Fertilizer and Pesticide
Subsidy Program which shall benefit qualified farmers.

       The DA shall provide fertilizer subsidy to qualified farmer beneficiaries according
to the following scheme:
    a) Per hectare, six bags of fertilizers or subsidy vouchers not lower than Php
       6,000.00 for those who plant inbred seeds;
    b) Per hectare, seven bags of fertilizers or subsidy vouchers not lower than Php
       7,000.00 for those who plant hybrid seeds.

       The DA shall similarly provide pesticide subsidy vouchers to qualified farmer
beneficiaries. The DA shall determine the amount of subsidy vouchers not lower than
Php 2,000.00 per hectare annually, depending on the current agricultural situation and
prevailing market prices.

      SECTION 4. Eligibility for the NFPSP. – To be eligible for the fertilizer and pesticide
subsidy, beneficiaries must meet the following criteria:
   a) A bona fide Filipino citizen who is a member of the agricultural sector;
   b) Must be at least eighteen (18) years old at the time of registration;
   c) Must be a farmer, farm laborer/worker or agriyouth based on the Registry System
      for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) Definition of Memebrs of the Agriculture
      and Fisheries Sector.

        Qualified farmers registered under the RSBSA shall be automatically included as
eligible beneficiaries of the NFPSP.

      SECTION 5. Program Report. – The DA shall annually submit to the agricultural
committee of both houses of Congress a report assessing the fertilizer and pesticide
subsidy program Each report required shall include for the one year period ending on
March of the year in which the report is submitted

   a) The number of farmers that participated in the program;
   b) Information regarding the demographic characteristics of the farmer
      beneficiaries;
   c) Description of the steps taken by the Department to help the farmer beneficiaries;
   d) Overall production performance by the farmer-beneficiaries;
   e) Assessment of its impact in the National Food Security Plan.

        SECTION 6.      Appropriations. – Such amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation for
the concerned agencies. Thereafter, the funds necessary for this Act’s continuous and
effective implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DA, in coordination with
other relevant government agencies, shall formulate and promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations and other issuance as may be necessary to ensure the effective
implementation of the provisions of this Act within sixty (60) days from the approval
thereof. The implementing rules and regulations issued pursuant to this section shall
take effect (30) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or any newspaper of
general circulation.

      SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
proclamations, rules, and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act, are
hereby amended or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

      SECTION 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
