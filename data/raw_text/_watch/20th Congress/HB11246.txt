                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The rapid growth of digital financial services has expanded access to loans and credit
facilities, but has likewise given rise to abusive debt collection practices through text
messages, social media, mobile applications, and other digital platforms. Reports of
threats, intimidation, public shaming, unauthorized disclosure of personal information,
and coercive communications have caused serious harm to the dignity, privacy, and
security of borrowers.

       While existing laws and regulations provide remedies against certain unlawful
collection practices, there remains a need for a clear and comprehensive framework
specifically addressing threatening, harassing, coercive, and abusive debt collection
conducted through digital and electronic means.

      This measure seeks to establish fair and lawful standards for digital debt collection,
prohibit specified abusive practices, protect borrowers from harassment and misuse of
personal information, strengthen compliance and regulatory oversight, and provide
appropriate administrative, civil, and criminal remedies, without prejudice to liabilities and
penalties under other applicable laws.

      The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           “AN ACT
     PROHIBITING THREATENING, HARASSING, COERCIVE, AND
     ABUSIVE DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES BY ONLINE LENDING
     APPLICATIONS, DIGITAL CREDIT PROVIDERS, FINANCING
     COMPANIES, LENDING COMPANIES, AND OTHER SIMILAR
     ENTITIES, PRESCRIBING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AND FOR
     OTHER PURPOSES”

           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of
     the Philippines in Congress assembled:

            SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the
     "Anti-Digital Debt Collection Harassment Act."

           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes
     the vital role of digital technology and financial innovation in
     promoting financial inclusion and expanding access to credit and
     other financial services. While lending companies, financing
     companies, online lending applications, digital credit providers,
     and similar entities possess the legitimate right to collect debts and
     enforce lawful obligations, such right must be exercised in a
     manner consistent with the Constitution and the laws of the
     Republic.

           It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
     borrowers from threatening, coercive, harassing, abusive, and
     unethical debt collection practices, particularly those perpetrated
     through digital and electronic means.

            The State shall safeguard the dignity, privacy, reputation,
     security, and personal information of individuals against
     intimidation, public humiliation, unauthorized disclosure of
     personal data, and other forms of abuse arising from debt
     collection activities. Toward this end, the State shall promote
responsible lending, ethical collection practices, accountability
among financial service providers, and the responsible use of
technology, while ensuring that advancements in digital finance
contribute to economic development without compromising the
fundamental rights and welfare of consumers. Furthermore, the
State shall establish effective mechanisms for regulation,
enforcement, and redress to ensure that debt collection activities
are conducted fairly, transparently, and with due respect for
human dignity and the rule of law.

       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this
Act:

       (a) Debt Collection refers to any act intended to secure
           payment, settlement, or recovery of a financial obligation.

       (b) Digital Communication refers to communication
           conducted through text messages, emails, social media
           platforms, instant messaging applications, mobile
           applications,   voice-over-internet communications,
           automated messaging systems, artificial intelligence
           systems, and similar technologies.

       (c) Harassment     refers     to   repeated,    unwanted,
           unreasonable, or abusive communications that, under
           the circumstances, are intended or reasonably likely to
           annoy, intimidate, embarrass, alarm, or coerce a
           borrower.

       (d) Threatening Communication refers to any statement,
           message,    image,     audio    recording,   video,   or
           communication that directly or indirectly threatens
           physical harm, legal action without basis, imprisonment,
           public humiliation, reputational damage, employment
           consequences, disclosure of personal information, or
           other unlawful adverse consequence, when made for the
           purpose of coercing payment.

       (e) Digital Lending Platform refers to any application,
           website, software, or electronic system through which
           loans, credit facilities, or cash advances are offered or
           administered.

       (f) Borrower refers to any individual who obtains or applies
           for a loan, credit facility, or cash advance.
     (g) Regulatory Authority refers to the Securities and
         Exchange Commission, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas,
         National Privacy Commission, Department of Information
         and Communications Technology, or any other agency
         having jurisdiction over covered entities.

     SECTION 4. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to:

     (a) All lending companies;

     (b) Financing companies;

     (c) Online lending applications;

     (d) Digital credit providers;

     (e) Buy-now-pay-later service providers;

     (f) Loan servicing entities;

     (g) Third-party debt collection agencies;

     (h) Collection agents and representatives acting on behalf of
         any covered entity; and

     (i) Any natural or juridical person engaged in the collection
         of debts through electronic or digital means.

      SECTION 5. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are
prohibited:

     (a) Sending messages that threaten arrest, imprisonment,
         detention, criminal prosecution, deportation, or other
         legal consequences when such threats are false,
         misleading, baseless, or intended primarily to intimidate;

     (b) Threatening physical harm, violence, injury, or damage to
         property;

     (c) Using profane, obscene, discriminatory, degrading,
         insulting, humiliating, or abusive language in a manner
         intended to intimidate, demean, or coerce a borrower;

     (d) Publicly shaming borrowers through social media,
         websites, online forums, group chats, or any digital
         platform;
(e) Threatening    to    publish,    distribute,   or   disclose
    photographs, videos, personal information, financial
    records, contact lists, or other private information, except
    when such disclosure is authorized by law;

(f) Contacting family members, relatives, friends, employers,
    co-workers, clients, classmates, or other third parties for
    the purpose of embarrassing, humiliating, or coercing a
    borrower, except where such contact is authorized by law
    or reasonably necessary to communicate with a person
    who is a co-maker, guarantor, surety, or authorized
    representative of the borrower;

(g) Misrepresenting oneself as a government official, law
    enforcement officer, lawyer, court personnel, or
    representative of any public agency;

(h) Using caller identification spoofing, fabricated legal
    notices, fake court orders, or simulated government
    documents for the purpose of deceiving, intimidating, or
    coercing a borrower;

(i) Sending communications designed to create a false
    impression that criminal proceedings have already been
    initiated;

(j) Making repeated or excessive communications that,
    considering their frequency, timing, manner, content, and
    persistence, constitute harassment;

(k) Using automated systems, artificial intelligence tools,
    bots, or mass messaging technologies to send or facilitate
    threatening or harassing communications;

(l) Accessing, processing, or using a borrower's contact list,
    photographs, messages, or other personal data for debt
    collection purposes without lawful authority;

(m) Threatening adverse employment consequences,
   academic sanctions, immigration consequences, or
   professional disciplinary actions without legal basis;

(n) Creating social media accounts, websites, or digital
    content intended to ridicule, shame, or expose borrowers;
    and
     (o) Engaging in any other collection practice that is
         substantially similar in nature and effect to the acts
         expressly prohibited under this Section and that is
         intended to threaten, coerce, intimidate, humiliate, or
         harass a borrower.

      SECTION 6. Fair Debt Collection Standards. - All entities
covered under Section 4 of this Act shall:

     (a) Conduct debt collection in a professional, respectful, and
         lawful manner;

     (b) Identify themselves truthfully in all communications;

     (c) Provide accurate information regarding the debt;

     (d) Maintain records of collection communications for at least
         five (5) years;

     (e) Establish internal mechanisms for receiving and resolving
         consumer complaints; and

     (f) Adopt safeguards against abusive debt collection
         practices.

       SECTION 7. Borrower's Rights. - Borrowers shall have the
right to:

     (a) Be treated with dignity and respect;

     (b) Receive truthful and accurate information regarding their
         obligations;

     (c) Be free from threats, intimidation, harassment, and
         public humiliation;

     (d) File complaints before appropriate regulatory authorities;

     (e) Obtain copies of collection records relating to their
         accounts; and

     (f) Seek damages and other legal remedies for violations of
         this Act.
      SECTION 8. Duty to Implement Compliance Programs. -
All entities covered under Section 4 shall establish compliance
programs, employee training systems, monitoring mechanisms,
and consumer protection policies to ensure compliance with this
Act.

      SECTION 9. Administrative Liability. - Without prejudice
to civil and criminal liability, the regulatory authority having
jurisdiction over the covered entity or activity may, after notice and
opportunity to be heard and in accordance with applicable law,
impose:

      (a) Suspension of operations;

      (b) Revocation of licenses, registrations, or accreditations;

      (c) Administrative fines ranging from Five Hundred
          Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) to Ten Million Pesos
          (₱10,000,000.00);

      (d) Permanent disqualification from engaging in lending or
          financing activities; and

      (e) Blacklisting of offending entities and responsible officers,
          subject to applicable rules and regulations.

      The administrative sanctions imposed under this Section
shall be proportionate to the nature, gravity, frequency, and
circumstances of the violation.

       SECTION 10. Criminal Liability. - Any person who willfully
commits any of the prohibited acts under Section 5 in the course
of, or for the purpose of, debt collection shall suffer:

      (a) Imprisonment of six (6) months to six (6) years; and

      (b) A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
          (₱500,000.00) but not more than Five Million Pesos
          (₱5,000,000.00).

       Where the offender is a corporation, partnership,
association, or other juridical entity, the responsible directors,
officers, managers, or employees who willfully authorized, directed,
knowingly tolerated, or participated in the commission of the
offense shall be held liable.
      The penalties provided under this Section shall be without
prejudice to prosecution under other applicable laws when the
same act or omission constitutes a distinct offense thereunder,
subject to the Constitution and applicable rules on double
jeopardy.

      SECTION 11. Civil Remedies. - A borrower who suffers
injury as a result of any violation of this Act may institute a civil
action to recover:

      (a) Actual damages;

      (b) Moral damages;

      (c) Exemplary damages;

      (d) Attorney's fees and litigation expenses; and

      (e) Such other relief as may be warranted.

      The remedies provided under this Act shall be without
prejudice to any other rights and remedies available under existing
laws.

      SECTION 12. Joint Regulatory Oversight. - The Securities
and Exchange Commission shall serve as the lead implementing
agency, without prejudice to the jurisdiction and regulatory
authority of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, National Privacy
Commission, Department of Information and Communications
Technology, Department of Trade and Industry, and other
concerned agencies over entities and activities within their
respective mandates.

      The concerned regulatory authorities shall coordinate, as
appropriate, in the investigation and enforcement of violations of
this Act.

      SECTION 13. Whistleblower Protection. - Any employee,
contractor, agent, or individual who reports violations of this Act in
good faith shall be protected against retaliation, dismissal,
discrimination, harassment, or other adverse actions.

      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. -
Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the
Securities and Exchange Commission, in coordination with the
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, National Privacy Commission,
Department of Information and Communications Technology,
Department of Trade and Industry, and other concerned agencies,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

      SECTION 15. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this
Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining
provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees,
executive orders, rules and regulations, ordinances, and other
issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, diminish, or
otherwise affect rights, remedies, powers, authorities, liabilities, or
penalties available under other applicable laws, except to the
extent of actual inconsistency with this Act.

      SECTION 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen
(15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a
newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved.
