                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIII, Section 12 of the 1987 Constitution provides that “the State shall
establish and maintain an effective food and drug regulatory system and undertake
appropriate health, manpower development, and research, responsive to the country’s
health needs and problems.” Crucial in the efficient delivery of the health needs of the
country is also the advancement and protection of the health and welfare of public health
workers. Thus, on March 26, 1992, the Magna Carta for Public Health Workers (Republic
Act No. 7305) was enacted. Unfortunately, for over 33 years now, the law remains as it
is without any amendment to keep abreast to the challenges and needs of the present
time.

       While numerous bills sought to revise or strengthen its provisions from the 16th to
the 19th Congress, none have been successfully passed into law. The Magna Carta for
Public Health Workers was designed to promote the welfare and well-being of the public
health workers by providing various benefits and allowances. However, the monetary
values of these benefits have remained unchanged over three decades, failing to keep
pace with inflation and evolving economic conditions. Added to these are the growing
clamor for more humane conditions of work for health care professionals especially the
nurses and doctors.

       According to the Human Resources for Health by Ateneo de Manila University
researchers, the Philippines has a physician-to-population ratio of 7.92 per 10,000
people, which falls below the international minimum standard of 10 per 10,000. In
addition, the country is facing a shortage of at least 127,000 nurses. This shortage is
further aggravated by the ongoing "brain drain" phenomenon, where many healthcare
workers leave the country in search of better pay and working conditions abroad. Almost
4,500 positions in public hospitals across the Philippines remain unfilled, as many
licensed Filipino nurses are unwilling to take these jobs due to low salaries, extended
working hours, and insufficient benefits as noted by the Asia Pacific Foundation of
Canada. Based on the findings of Woo, et.al., burnout symptoms are also widespread
among healthcare professionals, leading to serious repercussions such as resignations,
career shifts, and eventual migration to other countries in search of better working
conditions.
       The Filipino Nurses United (FNU) consultations with nurse administrators from
various healthcare facilities revealed that nurses are burdened by severe understaffing,
often managing between 20 to 50 patients per shift while working 12 to 16 hours without
receiving overtime pay. In addition, approximately 36,000 government-employed nurses
hold contractual positions under schemes such as the Job Order system or the Nurse
Deployment Program (NDP), leaving them with limited job security and benefits. In
addition, physicians report long working hours, inadequate compensation, delayed
allowances, and job insecurity. Many are also tasked with administrative duties due to
lack of support staff, further increasing their workload and stress levels.

        It is the declared policy of the State to safeguard and advance the right to health
of every Filipino and to foster greater health awareness among the population. Under
Article II, Section 18 of the 1987 Constitution, the State affirms labor as a primary social
economic force. It shall protect the rights of workers and promote their welfare. It is thus
become imperative to the State to ensure better, fair, and safe working conditions for
public health care workers.

       To effectively address the aforementioned challenges and fulfill the mandate of the
Constitution, this bill aims to amend certain provisions in the Magna Carta for Public
Health Workers by expanding the benefits and allowances of health care workers,
regulate working hours to better support their welfare so they can provide a better quality
service to our fellow Filipinos. It also seeks to hold government-owned hospitals,
administratively liable for willful non-compliance with, or deliberate evasion of, the
provision of benefits due to Public Health Workers.

      To ensure that the proposed amendments are both responsive and fiscally feasible
and sustainable, this bill further incorporates provisions on safe staffing standards,
workforce regularization, mental health protection programs, continuing professional
development support and rural and geographically isolated area service incentives.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
 AMENDING CERTAIN PROVISIONS OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7305, OTHERWISE
   KNOWN AS “THE MAGNA CARTA OF PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS”, BY
STRENGTHENING SAFE STAFFING, WORKFORCE REGULARIZATION, MENTAL
HEALTH PROTECTION, PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, RURAL DEPLOYMENT
             INCENTIVES, AND RETENTION MECHANISMS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Expanded Magna Carta
of Public Health Workers Act of 2025.”

      SEC. 2. Section 15 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “SEC. 15. Normal Hours of Work. - The normal hours of work of any public health
      worker shall not exceed eight (8) hours a day or forty (40) hours a week. IN THE
      EXIGENCY OF SERVICE, HOWEVER, THE WORKING HOURS MAY BE
      EXTENDED PROVIDED THAT IT SHALL NOT BE MORE THAN FIFTEEN (15)
      HOURS A DAY. AN EMPLOYEE REQUIRED TO RENDER SERVICE FOR 15
      HOURS SHALL BE GIVEN AN UNINTERRUPTED REST DAY OF ONE DAY
      WITHIN THE WEEK WHEN SUCH SERVICES WERE RENDERED.

      WHEN EXIGENCY OF SERVICE WOULD REQUIRE PHYSICIANS TO RENDER
      WORK HOURS BEYOND THE 15 HOURS ALLOWED UNDER THIS ACT, THEY
      SHALL BE GIVEN A MANDATORY TIME OFF OF ONE DAY IMMEDIATELY
      AFTER THE SERVICES WERE RENDERED.

      PROVIDED, THAT THE FOREGOING LIMITATION ON MAXIMUM DUTY
      HOURS MAY BE EXCEEDED DURING DECLARED EMERGENCIES,
      DISASTERS, PUBLIC HEALTH EMERGENCIES, MASS CASUALTY
      INCIDENTS, OR OTHER CIRCUMSTANCES AS MAY BE AUTHORIZED BY
      THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH, SUBJECT TO APPROPRIATE
      COMPENSATION AND MANDATORY REST PERIODS THEREAFTER.

      Hours worked shall include: a) all the time during which a public health worker is
      required to be on active duty or to be at a prescribed workplace; and b) all the time
      during which a public health worker is suffered or permitted to
      work: Provided, That, the time when a public health worker is placed on "On Call"
      status shall not be considered as hours worked but shall entitle the public health
      worker to an "On Call" pay equivalent to [fifty percent (50%)] SIXTY PERCENT
      (60%) of his/her regular wage. ‘On Call’ status refers to a condition when public
      health workers are called upon to respond to urgent or immediate need for
      health/medical assistance or relief work during emergencies such that he/she
      cannot devote the time for his/her own use.

      SEC. 3. Section 16 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SEC. 16. Overtime Work. - Where the exigencies of the service so require, any
      public health worker may be required to render service beyond the normal eight
      (8) hours a day BUT NOT MORE THAN 15 HOURS A DAY, EXCEPT DURING
      DECLARED EMERGENCIES, DISASTERS, PUBLIC HEALTH EMERGENCIES,
      MASS CASUALTY INCIDENTS, OR OTHER CIRCUMSTANCES AS MAY BE
      AUTHORIZED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH. In such a case, the workers
      shall be paid an additional compensation [in accordance with existing laws and
      prevailing practices.] EQUIVALENT TO THE HEALTH WORKERS’ REGULAR
      WAGE PLUS THIRTY PERCENT (30%) OF IT.


       SEC. 4. Section 18 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

      SEC. 18. Night-Shift Differential. -

      a.    Every public health worker shall be paid a night-shift differential of [ten
      percent (10%)] TWENTY PERCENT (20%) of his/her regular wage for each hour
      of work performed during the night-shifts customarily adopted by hospitals,
      BIRTHING CLINICS AND OTHER HEALTH FACILITIES OPERATING IN A 24/7
      SHIFT AND DURING CALAMITIES, DISASTERS AND PUBLIC HEALTH
      EMERGENCIES.

      b.     Every health worker required to work on the period covered after his/her
      regular schedule shall be entitled to his/her regular wage plus the regular overtime
      rate and an additional amount of [ten percent (10%)] TWENTY PERCENT (20%)
      of such overtime rate for each hour of work performed between ten (10) o'clock in
      the evening to six (6) o'clock in the morning.


      SEC. 5. Section 21 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

       SEC. 21. Hazard Allowance. –PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS WHO ARE
       EXPOSED TO HEALTH HAZARDS AND OCCUPATIONAL RISKS SHALL
       CONTINUE TO BE ENTITLED TO HAZARD ALLOWANCE COMPUTED UNDER
       A PERCENTAGE-BASED STRUCTURE, IN NO CASE LOWER THAN THE
       RATES PROVIDED UNDER EXISTING LAWS, SUBJECT TO A GRADUAL
       INCREASE OF RATES AS MAY BE PRESCRIBED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF
       HEALTH (DOH) IN COORDINATION WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET
       AND MANAGEMENT (DBM).

       THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH, IN COORDINATION WITH THE
       DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT AND THE CIVIL SERVICE
       COMMISSION, SHALL PRESCRIBE CLEAR CRITERIA FOR DETERMINING
       THE DEGREE OF HAZARD EXPOSURE AND SHALL PROVIDE FOR
       PERIODIC ADJUSTMENTS OF HAZARD ALLOWANCE RATES BASED ON
       INFLATION, ACTUAL OCCUPATIONAL RISK, AND THE FISCAL CAPACITY
       OF THE NATIONAL GOVERNMENT AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS.

       SEC. 6. A new Section 21-A is hereby inserted to read as follows:

       SEC. 21-A. COMPREHENSIVE HEALTH BENEFITS. —ALL PUBLIC HEALTH
       WORKERS SHALL BE ENTITLED TO BASIC AND EMERGENCY MEDICAL
       SERVICES WHICH SHALL INCLUDE, BUT ARE NOT LIMITED TO: SERVICES
       OF HEALTH CARE PROFESSIONALS, DIAGNOSTIC, LABORATORY,
       DENTAL, AND OTHER MEDICAL SERVICES, AND PRESCRIPTION DRUGS.

       SEC. 7. Section 22 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

       SEC. 22. Subsistence Allowance. – [Public health workers who are required to
       render service within the premises of hospitals, sanitaria, health infirmaries, main
       health centers, rural health units and barangay health stations, or clinics, and other
       health-related establishments in order to make their services available at any and
       all times, shall be entitled to full subsistence allowance of three (3) meals which
       may be computed in accordance with prevailing circumstances as determined by
       the Secretary of Health in consultation with the Management-Health Workers'
       Consultative Councils, as established under Section 33 of this Act: Provided, That
       representation and travel allowance shall be given to rural health physicians as
       enjoyed by municipal agriculturists, municipal planning and development officers
       and budget officers.] ALL PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS SHALL BE ENTITLED
       TO FULL SUBSISTENCE ALLOWANCE EQUIVALENT TO TWO HUNDRED
       (PHP 200.00) PER DAY FOR RENDERING ACTUAL SERVICES WITHOUT
       PREJUDICE TO INCREASE OF THIS AMOUNT AS PRESCRIBED BY THE
       SECRETARY OF HEALTH.
      SEC. 8. Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SEC. 28. [Compensation for Injuries.] LOSS OF EARNINGS BENEFIT– Public
      health workers shall be protected against the consequences of employment
      injuries, ILLNESS OR QUARANTINE, in accordance with existing laws. Injuries
      OR ILLNESS incurred while doing overtime work shall be presumed work-
      connected.

      PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS OR THEIR FAMILIES, AS MAY BE APPLICABLE,
      SHALL BE ENTITLED TO AN ENHANCED DEATH OR DISABILITY
      COMPENSATION PACKAGE, INCLUDING A ONE-TIME DISABILITY OR
      DEATH COMPENSATION PACKAGE, IN CASE OF DEATH, PERMANENT
      TOTAL DISABILITY, DISMEMBERMENT, PERMANENT LOSS OF SIGHT,
      HEARING, OR SPEECH, WHEN THE INJURY OR ILLNESS OCCURRED IN
      THE PERFORMANCE OF THE HEALTH WORKER’S DUTY.

      THE AMOUNT, QUALIFYING CONDITIONS, AND PAYMENT MECHANISM
      SHALL BE DETERMINED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND THE
      DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT, IN COORDINATION WITH
      THE GOVERNMENT SERVICE INSURANCE SYSTEM, TAKING INTO
      ACCOUNT FISCAL FEASIBILITY AND SUSTAINABILITY, AND THE EXISTING
      BENEFITS AVAILABLE UNDER APPLICABLE LAWS.

      A NATIONAL PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS DEATH AND DISABILITY
      BENEFITS TRUST FUND MAY BE ESTABLISHED TO SUPPORT ENHANCED
      DEATH AND DISABILITY BENEFITS UNDER THIS SECTION. BENEFITS
      UNDER THIS SECTION SHALL BE WITHOUT PREJUDICE TO, AND SHALL
      NOT DIMINISH, BENEFITS DUE UNDER GSIS AND OTHER EXISTING LAWS.

SEC. 9. New Sections 29-A to 29-F are hereby inserted to read as follows:

      SEC. 29-A. “Mental Health Leave. —In recognition of the critical importance
      of mental health and the unique stresses faced by public health workers, all
      public health workers shall be entitled to a five (5)-day paid mental health
      leave per year, separate from their existing sick and vacation leaves.

      THE MENTAL HEALTH LEAVE MAY BE USED FOR THE PURPOSE OF
      MENTAL HEALTH RECOVERY, COUNSELING, THERAPY, OR ANY OTHER
      MENTAL HEALTH-RELATED NEEDS AS CERTIFIED BY A LICENSED
      MENTAL HEALTH PROFESSIONAL.

      THIS LEAVE SHALL NOT BE DEDUCTED FROM THE EXISTING SICK OR
      VACATION LEAVE CREDITS OF THE HEALTH WORKER AND SHALL BE
      GRANTED WITHOUT PREJUDICE TO THEIR EMPLOYMENT STATUS OR
      BENEFITS.”

      IN ADDITION TO THE MENTAL HEALTH LEAVE, ALL GOVERNMENT
HOSPITALS, HEALTH CENTERS, RURAL HEALTH UNITS, AND OTHER
HEALTH-RELATED ESTABLISHMENTS SHALL INSTITUTIONALIZE MENTAL
HEALTH PROTECTION PROGRAMS, INCLUDING ANNUAL MENTAL
HEALTH ASSESSMENTS, ACCESS TO FREE PSYCHOLOGICAL AND
COUNSELING SERVICES, BURNOUT PREVENTION AND WELLNESS
PROGRAMS, AND PEER SUPPORT AND RESILIENCE-BUILDING
INITIATIVES.

SEC. 29-B. SAFE STAFFING STANDARDS. —THE DEPARTMENT OF
HEALTH SHALL ESTABLISH, PERIODICALLY REVIEW, AND ENFORCE
SAFE STAFFING STANDARDS FOR GOVERNMENT HOSPITALS, HEALTH
CENTERS, RURAL HEALTH UNITS, AND OTHER HEALTH-RELATED
ESTABLISHMENTS. THE STANDARDS SHALL INCLUDE, AT A MINIMUM,
THE FOLLOWING GUIDELINES: GENERAL WARD: ONE (1) NURSE FOR
EVERY TWELVE (12) PATIENTS, MAXIMUM; HIGH-RISK WARD: ONE (1)
NURSE FOR EVERY SIX (6) PATIENTS; INTENSIVE CARE UNIT: ONE (1)
NURSE FOR EVERY ONE (1) TO TWO (2) PATIENTS; AND EMERGENCY
ROOM: STAFFING BASED ON PATIENT VOLUME, ACUITY, AND OTHER
PARAMETERS AS MAY BE PRESCRIBED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF
HEALTH. IMPLEMENTATION IN HOSPITALS OPERATED AND MANAGED BY
LGUS SHALL BE SUBJECT TO THE STAGGERED COMPLIANCE PLAN
UNDER SECTION 12 OF THIS ACT, UNTIL SUCH TIME THAT SUFFICIENT
FUNDS ARE AVAILABLE FOR FULL COMPLIANCE.

SEC. 29-C. REGULARIZATION OF LONG-TERM CONTRACTUAL PUBLIC
HEALTH WORKERS. —PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS ENGAGED UNDER JOB
ORDER, CONTRACT OF SERVICE, NURSE DEPLOYMENT PROGRAM, OR
SIMILAR CONTRACTUAL ARRANGEMENTS WHO HAVE RENDERED
CONTINUOUS AND SATISFACTORY SERVICE FOR THREE (3) TO FIVE (5)
YEARS SHALL BE PRIORITIZED FOR APPOINTMENT TO PLANTILLA
POSITIONS, MERIT AND FITNESS REQUIREMENTS, QUALIFICATION
STANDARDS, AND APPLICABLE BUDGETARY RULES.

SEC. 29-D. CONTINUING PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT SUPPORT. —
PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS SHALL BE PROVIDED SUPPORT FOR
CONTINUING PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, INCLUDING ANNUAL CPD
ALLOWANCE, STUDY LEAVE PRIVILEGES, SCHOLARSHIP AND TRAINING
OPPORTUNITIES,      SPECIALTY   COURSES,      CERTIFICATIONS,
CONFERENCES, AND CAREER ADVANCEMENT PATHWAYS, BY THE
DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH IN COORDINATION WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF
BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT AND OTHER CONCERNED AGENCIES.

SEC.  29-E. INCENTIVES  FOR    RURAL, UNDERSERVED,  AND
GEOGRAPHICALLY ISOLATED AND DISADVANTAGED AREAS. —TO
PROMOTE     EQUITABLE    DISTRIBUTION  OF    HEALTHCARE
PROFESSIONALS, PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS ASSIGNED TO RURAL,
UNDERSERVED,     AND   GEOGRAPHICALLY    ISOLATED   AND
      DISADVANTAGED AREAS SHALL BE GRANTED APPROPRIATE
      INCENTIVES, WHICH MAY INCLUDE RURAL HARDSHIP ALLOWANCE,
      HOUSING ASSISTANCE, TRANSPORTATION ALLOWANCE, EDUCATIONAL
      ASSISTANCE FOR DEPENDENTS, AND ADDITIONAL SERVICE INCENTIVES.

      SEC. 29-F. RETENTION INCENTIVES. —THE GOVERNMENT SHALL
      INSTITUTIONALIZE  RETENTION   INCENTIVES    TO   ENCOURAGE
      EXPERIENCED PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS TO REMAIN IN GOVERNMENT
      SERVICE. SUCH INCENTIVES MAY INCLUDE A FIVE-YEAR RETENTION
      BONUS, TEN-YEAR LOYALTY INCENTIVE, ADDITIONAL LEAVE CREDITS,
      AND ENHANCED RETIREMENT SUPPORT OF THE NATIONAL
      GOVERNMENT AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS.

      SEC. 10. Section 39 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SEC. 39. Penal Provision. - Any person who shall willfully interfere with, restrain or
      coerce any public health worker in the exercise of his/her rights or shall in any
      manner commit any act in violation of any of the provisions of this Act, upon
      conviction, shall be punished by a fine of not less than Twenty thousand pesos
      (P20,000.00) but not more than Forty thousand pesos (P40,000.00) or
      imprisonment of not more than one (1) year or both at the discretion of the court.

      If the offender is a public official, the court, in addition to the penalties provided in
      the preceding paragraph, may impose the additional penalty of disqualification
      from office.

      “ALL HOSPITALS, SANITARIA, HEALTH CENTERS, RURAL HEALTH UNITS
      AND OTHER HEALTH-RELATED ESTABLISHMENTS OWNED AND
      OPERATED BY GOVERNMENT OR ITS POLITICAL SUBDIVISIONS WITH
      ORIGINAL CHARTERS THAT WILLFULLY CIRCUMVENT OR VIOLATE THE
      LAW TO EVADE PROVISION OF BENEFITS TO PUBLIC HEALTH WORKERS
      WILL BE HELD LIABLE. THE PERSON DIRECTLY RESPONSIBLE AS WELL
      AS THE HEAD OF AGENCY WILL BE SANCTIONED UNDER
      ADMINISTRATIVE LAW AND OTHER APPROPRIATE LAWS.

      PROVIDED, THAT HOSPITALS OPERATED AND MANAGED BY LGUS
      COVERED BY AN APPROVED STAGGERED COMPLIANCE PLAN SHALL
      NOT BE DEEMED TO HAVE WILLFULLY CIRCUMVENTED OR VIOLATED
      THIS ACT SOLELY BY REASON OF STAGERRED IMPLEMENTATION.


SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Health (DOH)
shall, in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), Civil
Service Commission (CSC), Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), Department
of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), both Houses of Congress, local government
units, and other concerned agencies, and in consultation with public health worker
representatives, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the proper
implementation of this Act not later than ninety (90) days from its effectivity. The
implementing rules shall include mechanisms for hazard exposure classification, periodic
benefit adjustment, safe staffing monitoring, regularization priorities, mental health
programs, CPD support, rural and GIDA incentives, retention benefits, and staggered
compliance by hospitals operated and managed by LGUs, available-funds safeguards,
and fiscal sustainability safeguards.

SEC. 12. STAGGERED COMPLIANCE BY HOSPITALS OPERATED AND MANAGED
BY LGUS. – HOSPITALS OPERATED AND MANAGED BY LGUS SHALL BE GIVEN
AMPLE TIME TO COMPLY WITH THE REQUIREMENTS OF THIS ACT THROUGH A
STAGGERED COMPLIANCE PLAN, TAKING INTO CONSIDERATION THE FISCAL
CAPACITY AND AVAILABLE FUNDS OF THE CONCERNED LGU, UNTIL SUCH TIME
THAT SUFFICIENT FUNDS ARE AVAILABLE FOR FULL COMPLIANCE.

THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH, IN COORDINATION WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF
BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT, THE CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION, THE
DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT, BOTH HOUSES OF
CONGRESS, AND THE CONCERNED LGUS, SHALL ISSUE THE NECESSARY
GUIDELINES FOR THE PROGRESSIVE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS ACT.


SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, rules and
regulations, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
