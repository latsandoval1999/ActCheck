                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care (UHC) Act, and Republic Act No.
11959, or the Regional Specialty Centers Act, laid the foundation for an integrated and
people-centered health system. However, gaps remain in addressing the developmental
and educational needs of children with disabilities, particularly during early childhood and
school age when timely intervention is most critical.

Public schools are the most accessible institutions for Filipino families, yet the
establishment of Special Education (SPED) Centers remains uneven and discretionary.
The absence of a nationwide mandate and sustainable funding mechanism continues to
limit access to properly resourced SPED services. Challenges such as the shortage of
trained SPED teachers, limited specialization programs, inadequate compensation, and
weak coordination between health and education systems persist.

This measure seeks to institutionalize the establishment of SPED Centers in public
elementary and secondary schools nationwide, prioritizing underserved and marginalized
areas. It provides sustained funding for SPED teacher training, specialization,
scholarships, and professional development, alongside additional compensation and
incentives for SPED personnel and allied professionals.

Further, the bill strengthens collaboration among the Department of Education,
Department of Health, local government units, and barangays to integrate developmental
and therapeutic services into the mandates of the UHC Act and the Regional Specialty
Centers Act. Importantly, it also emphasizes parental involvement through consultations
and school-based activities, ensuring that families are active partners in the education
and holistic development of children with disabilities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING SPECIAL EDUCATION (SPED) CENTERS IN
   PUBLIC SCHOOLS, PROVIDING FUNDS FOR SPED TEACHER TRAINING,
 SPECIALIZATION, AND SUPPORT, AND EXPANDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11223
 (UNIVERSAL HEALTH CARE ACT) AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11959 (REGIONAL
 SPECIALTY CENTERS ACT) TO INCLUDE COMPREHENSIVE DEVELOPMENTAL
      AND SPECIALIZED EDUCATIONAL SERVICES FOR CHILDREN WITH
                            DISABILITIES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Inclusive Education and
Development Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of every child to quality and inclusive education, health, and holistic
development. In recognition of the interdependence of health and education, and in
fulfillment of its obligations under the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons
with Disabilities (UNCRPD), the State shall institutionalize Special Education (SPED)
Centers in public schools and integrate developmental and therapeutic services into the
public education and healthcare systems.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. –

   a. Child – A person below eighteen (18) years of age, or those over eighteen (18)
      who are unable to fully take care of themselves or protect themselves due to
      disability or condition.
   b. Children with Disabilities – Those with long-term physical, mental, intellectual,
      developmental, or sensory impairments.
   c. Private Entities – Corporations, partnerships, cooperatives, and sole proprietors
      engaged in business or non-profit operations.
   d. SPED Center – A facility within a public school providing special education
      services, individualized instruction, developmental assessments, therapeutic
      interventions, and related services.
   e. Allied Professionals – Includes speech and language pathologists, occupational
      therapists, physical therapists, developmental pediatricians, behavioral therapists,
      psychologists, and guidance counselors.
   f. Inclusive Education – A system of education that accommodates all children
      regardless of condition.

SEC. 4. Establishment of SPED Centers in Public Schools - DepEd, in coordination with
LGUs and barangays, shall establish at least one (1) SPED Center in every public
elementary and secondary school, prioritizing underserved and marginalized areas. Each
center shall have appropriate infrastructure, facilities, learning materials, diagnostic tools,
and assistive technologies. Staffing shall include at least one qualified SPED teacher per
ten (10) children with disabilities, supported by allied professionals. Schools may serve
as satellite hubs for therapy and diagnostic services in partnership with DOH Regional
Specialty Centers.

SEC. 5. Integration into UHC and Regional Specialty Centers - DOH shall ensure
developmental and therapeutic services for children with disabilities are included in
service packages under the UHC Act and Regional Specialty Centers Act. Partnerships
with DepEd SPED Centers shall deliver early diagnosis, referrals, therapy, and
developmental monitoring within schools, integrated with barangay health centers.

SEC 6. Training, Specialization, and Professional Development. - DepEd, in collaboration
with CHED, TESDA, and accredited institutions, shall develop SPED training and
specialization programs. Scholarships, grants, and continuing education incentives shall
be provided. DepEd shall establish Centers for SPED Excellence as hubs for research,
innovation, training, and technical assistance.

SEC 7. Compensation and Incentives for SPED Personnel. - SPED teachers and allied
professionals shall receive additional compensation of at least fifteen percent (15%) of
their basic salary, hazard pay, continuing education allowances, and access to mental
health support. Career advancement pathways specific to SPED teachers shall be
established.

SEC. 8. Role of Barangays and LGUs. - Barangays shall designate a SPED Focal Person
to coordinate with schools and health centers. LGUs shall allocate funds for SPED
Centers, including infrastructure, transportation, and honoraria for allied professionals.
Barangay Development Councils shall include SPED and inclusive education in annual
investment plans. LGUs and barangays shall promote parental involvement in SPED
programs through consultations and school-based activities.
SEC. 9. Inter-Agency Council on Inclusive Education and Development (IACIED) - An
IACIED shall be created, composed of:

   •   Secretary of Education (Chair)
   •   Secretary of Health (Co-Chair)
   •   Secretary of Social Welfare and Development (Member)
   •   Chairperson of the Commission on Human Rights (Member)
   •   Representative from NCDA
   •   Representative from the League of Provinces/Cities/Municipalities
   •   Three (3) representatives from SPED professional organizations and parent
       groups

The Council shall convene quarterly and report annually to Congress.

SEC. 10. Appropriations. - Funds necessary shall be charged against current
appropriations of DepEd and DOH, and thereafter included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, DepEd, in coordination with DOH, DSWD, and LGUs, shall promulgate rules
and regulations.

SEC. 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision is held invalid, the rest shall remain valid.

SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. - All inconsistent laws, decrees, issuances, rules, and
regulations are repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 14. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication
in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
