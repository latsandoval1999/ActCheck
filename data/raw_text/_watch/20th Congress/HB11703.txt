                       AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE CLASSIFICATION OF BARANGAYS,
ESTABLISHING A SYSTEM OF FISCAL EQUALIZATION AMONG
BARANGAYS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The barangay is the basic political unit of government and the primary
planning and implementing unit of government policies, plans, programs,
projects, and activities in the community. As the level of government closest to
the people, it is called upon to provide and maintain basic services and facilities
that directly affect the daily life of its inhabitants.

       Barangays, however, differ considerably in their financial capacity,
population, land area, economic base, geographic conditions, and requirements
for basic services and facilities. A highly urbanized barangay serving tens of
thousands of inhabitants cannot reasonably be treated in the same manner as a
small rural barangay. Neither can an economically active barangay with
substantial regular revenues be placed on the same financial and administrative
footing as an isolated barangay with limited sources of income.

       The system of local government already recognizes these differences in the
case of provinces, cities, and municipalities. Income classification serves as an
important measure of their financial capacity and carries corresponding
consequences in matters of fiscal administration, personnel, compensation,
financial assistance, and the implementation of development programs and
projects. Barangays, notwithstanding the differences in their circumstances and
financial capacity, are not covered by a comparable general system of
classification.

       This measure seeks to address this gap by establishing five (5) classes of
barangays based principally on their average annual regular income, with due
consideration given to population, land area, locally generated revenues, poverty
incidence, geographic conditions, and requirements for basic services and
facilities.

       Classification, however, should not merely attach a designation to a
barangay. It should enable government to recognize differences in capacity and
respond accordingly. Barangays with greater financial and administrative
capacity may be accorded greater flexibility in organization, personnel,
procurement, financial administration, and the undertaking of programs and
projects. Barangays with lesser capacity should, in turn, receive greater assistance
to enable them to discharge the functions entrusted to them.

       The measure therefore establishes a Barangay Equalization Fund. The
principle underlying the Fund is simple: while barangays should endeavor to
become self-reliant and maximize the use of their own resources and revenues,


                                                                                   2
no community should be unable to provide adequate basic services and facilities
merely because the barangay in which it is situated has a limited revenue base.

Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays shall accordingly be regularly entitled to
participate in the Fund, with Fifth Class Barangays receiving preferential
assistance. Third Class Barangays may participate only under exceptional
conditions where, notwithstanding their classification, their resources are shown
to be inadequate by reason of high poverty, extraordinary population or service
requirements, geographic isolation, exposure to calamities, peace and order
conditions, or other analogous circumstances. First and Second Class Barangays
shall not be entitled to an Equalization Share.

The proposed Fund does not diminish the regular share of barangays in national
taxes or any other fiscal entitlement. Equalization is intended to be additional
assistance. It recognizes the long-established principle that the National
Government or the next higher level of local government may augment basic
services and facilities where those available at the lower level are inadequate to
meet the requirements of the inhabitants.

The proposal likewise builds upon the existing policy of providing growth and
equity assistance to local governments with limited fiscal capacity. What this
measure seeks to accomplish is to establish a permanent, transparent, and
predictable system specifically responsive to the financial condition and service
requirements of barangays.

Classification must ultimately serve development. A Fifth Class Barangay should
not remain permanently dependent upon equalization assistance. The system
should provide it with the means to strengthen its administration, improve basic
services, develop its local economy, increase its own revenues, and eventually
graduate to a higher class. In the same manner, improvements in local revenue
                                                                                  3
collection should be encouraged rather than penalized by an immediate
withdrawal of assistance.

By recognizing differences among barangays while ensuring that communities
with fewer resources are not left behind, this measure seeks to strengthen
barangay government, promote greater fiscal responsibility, and enable every
barangay to become a more effective, responsive, and self-reliant community.


      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                       AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE CLASSIFICATION OF BARANGAYS,
ESTABLISHING A SYSTEM OF FISCAL EQUALIZATION AMONG
BARANGAYS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Barangay
Classification and Equalization Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote genuine and meaningful local autonomy and to enable barangays to
attain their fullest development as selfreliant communities and effective partners
in the attainment of national goals.

      Towards this end, the State shall establish a system for the classification of
barangays which shall take into account their financial position and their capacity
to provide basic services and facilities and discharge the functions and
responsibilities vested upon them by law.

       The State shall likewise provide for an equitable system of assistance to
barangays which, by reason of limited resources, population, poverty, geographic
location, or other circumstances, require additional resources to provide adequate
basic services and facilities and promote the general welfare of their inhabitants.

       The classification and equalization systems established under this Act shall
seek to strengthen barangay government, promote accountability and fiscal
responsibility, encourage barangays to generate and maximize the use of their
resources and revenues, and reduce disparities in their capacity to provide basic
services and facilities.

SEC. 3. Classification of Barangays. — All barangays shall be classified
according to their financial capacity and ability to provide basic services and
facilities, as follows:

   a. First Class Barangays;
   b. Second Class Barangays;
   c. Third Class Barangays;
   d. Fourth Class Barangays; and
   e. Fifth Class Barangays.

   The classification herein provided shall be used in determining the financial
and administrative capacity of barangays and, whenever appropriate, the extent
of powers, authority, responsibilities, resources, and assistance that may be
granted or made available to them.

   The classification of a barangay shall not be construed as a measure of the
quality of governance or performance of its officials.
                                                                                  2
SEC. 4. Criteria for Classification. — The classification of a barangay shall be
based principally on its average annual regular income for the three (3) fiscal
years immediately preceding the general reclassification.

      For purposes of this Act, average annual regular income shall consist of
revenues and receipts which regularly accrue to the general fund of the barangay,
as may be determined by the Department of Finance: Provided, That special
funds, nonrecurring receipts, borrowings, proceeds from the sale of assets,
extraordinary grants and aids, disaster assistance, and other receipts of a similar
character shall not be included.

      In determining the proper classification of a barangay, consideration shall
likewise be given to the following:

   a. population;
   b. land area;
   c. locally generated revenues;
   d. poverty incidence;
   e. geographic location and accessibility;
   f. the nature and extent of basic services and facilities required by its
      inhabitants; and
   g. such other circumstances which materially affect the financial position of
      the barangay or the cost of providing basic services and facilities.

   The Department of Finance, in consultation with the Department of Budget
and Management, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, the
Philippine Statistics Authority, and the Liga ng mga Barangay, shall prescribe the
income ranges and such reasonable adjustments as may be necessary for the
proper classification of barangays.


                                                                                  3
SEC. 5. Income Classes. — The Secretary of Finance shall prescribe the average
annual regular income corresponding to each class of barangay: Provided, That
the income ranges shall reflect substantial differences in the financial capacity of
barangays and shall be adjusted periodically to take into account changes in the
value of money, the cost of government operations, and the revenues accruing to
barangays.

       In prescribing such income ranges, the Secretary of Finance shall take into
account the distribution of barangay income throughout the country and the need
to maintain reasonable and meaningful distinctions among the several classes.

       The income ranges shall be reviewed at the time of every general
reclassification.

SEC. 6. General Reclassification. — The classification of all barangays shall be
reviewed and updated once every three (3) years on the basis of the three (3)
immediately preceding fiscal years.

       The Secretary of Finance shall, upon completion of every general
reclassification, issue a schedule showing the classification of every barangay.

       The classification of a barangay shall remain in force until the next general
reclassification unless corrected on account of manifest error or material omission
in the data upon which such classification was based.

       The schedule of classification and the principal data used in determining
the classification of each barangay shall be made available to the public.




                                                                                   4
SEC. 7. Newly Created Barangays. — A newly created barangay shall be
provisionally classified within six (6) months from the commencement of its
corporate existence on the basis of its estimated regular income, population, land
area, resources, and capacity to provide basic services and facilities.

      Such provisional classification shall continue until the barangay is included
in the next general reclassification.

SEC. 8. Purposes of Classification. — The classification of barangays may serve
as basis for:

   a. the determination of financial and administrative capacity;
   b. the establishment of organizational structures and staffing patterns;
   c. the rates of compensation, honoraria, allowances, and other benefits
      authorized by law;
   d. limitations on expenditures for personal services;
   e. financial, accounting, budgeting, and auditing requirements;
   f. procurement procedures and expenditure thresholds;
   g. the establishment and operation of public utilities and other economic
      enterprises;
   h. counterpart requirements for programs and projects financed wholly or
      partly by the National Government or another local government unit;
   i. the grant of financial assistance, subsidies, and other forms of aid;
   j. the authority to undertake such programs, projects, services, and activities
      as may be appropriate to the financial and administrative capacity of the
      barangay; and
   k. such other purposes as may be provided by law.




                                                                                  5
Any differentiation among barangays pursuant to this section shall be reasonably
related to their financial and administrative capacity and shall not diminish the
basic powers, functions, duties, and responsibilities vested in barangays by law.

SEC. 9. Differentiated Administrative Requirements. — Rules governing the
organization, personnel, budgeting, procurement, financial administration, and
other operations of barangays may be differentiated according to their respective
classifications.

       First and Second Class Barangays may, when their finances so allow,
maintain such additional offices and personnel and undertake such programs,
projects, services, activities, public utilities, and economic enterprises as may be
necessary for efficient and effective barangay government and the promotion of
the general welfare.

       Third Class Barangays shall maintain such organization and personnel as
may be necessary for the discharge of their functions and responsibilities.

       Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays shall, whenever practicable, be provided
simplified administrative procedures, technical assistance, common facilities,
shared services, and such other assistance as may enable them to discharge their
functions effectively.

       Nothing herein shall be construed as diminishing any power, duty,
function, or responsibility vested by law in a barangay.

SEC. 10. Barangay Equalization Fund. — There is hereby established a
Barangay Equalization Fund, hereinafter referred to as the Fund, for the purpose
of providing additional financial assistance to Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays
and, under the conditions provided in this Act, qualified Third Class Barangays,


                                                                                   6
in order to augment their resources for the adequate provision of basic services
and facilities.

       The Fund shall be separate and distinct from the regular share of barangays
in national taxes and from other shares, revenues, and receipts accruing to them
under law.

       No amount otherwise due a barangay shall be withheld, reduced, or
charged against its Equalization Share.

The Fund shall be administered in accordance with the principles and formula
provided under this Act.

SEC. 11. Sources of the Barangay Equalization Fund. — The Barangay
Equalization Fund shall consist of:

   a. such amount as may be annually appropriated for the purpose in the
       General Appropriations Act;
   b. such amounts as may be appropriated or otherwise provided for the
       purpose of promoting growth and equity among local government units,
       insofar as the same are intended for barangays;
   c. such additional amounts as Congress may provide from national
       government revenues in excess of programmed revenues;
   d. grants, contributions, donations, and other forms of assistance made
       available for the purposes of the Fund; and
   e. such other sources as may hereafter be provided by law.

   The amount annually provided for the Fund shall take into account the number
of qualified barangays, their respective financial capacity, the requirements for
basic services and facilities, and the amount reasonably necessary to reduce
disparities among barangays.
                                                                                 7
   Amounts accruing to the Fund shall be in addition to, and shall not be deducted
from, the share of barangays in national taxes or from any other share, allotment,
revenue, or fiscal entitlement accruing to them under law.

   No portion of the regular share of barangays in national taxes shall be
withheld, set aside, or otherwise used to constitute or augment the Fund.

SEC. 12. Principles Governing Fiscal Equalization. — The system of fiscal
equalization established under this Act shall be governed by the following
principles:

   a. Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays shall be regularly qualified to receive an
      Equalization Share;
   b. Third Class Barangays may receive an Equalization Share only when the
      conditions provided in this Act are present;
   c. Fifth Class Barangays shall, as a class, receive the greatest degree of
      equalization assistance;
   d. differences in financial capacity, population, poverty, geographic
      conditions, accessibility, and requirements for basic services and facilities
      shall be taken into account;
   e. barangays similarly situated shall, as far as practicable, have reasonably
      comparable capacity to provide basic services and facilities;
   f. the allocation of the Fund shall be determined according to a uniform and
      equitable formula and shall not depend upon discretionary selection or
      endorsement;
   g. the system shall encourage recipient barangays to generate and maximize
      the use of their resources and revenues; and
   h. improvements in locally generated revenues shall not result in an
      immediate and equivalent reduction in equalization assistance.

                                                                                  8
SEC. 13. Qualified Barangays. — Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays shall be
regularly qualified to receive an Equalization Share from the Fund.

      A Third Class Barangay may qualify for an Equalization Share only when
its financial resources are insufficient, in relation to its circumstances, to provide
basic services and facilities at reasonably adequate levels, as determined in
accordance with the succeeding section.

      First and Second Class Barangays shall not be entitled to an Equalization
Share from the Fund.

      The exclusion of a barangay from the Fund shall not affect its entitlement
to its regular share in national taxes or to any other share, grant, aid, subsidy,
program, project, or financial assistance otherwise authorized by law.

SEC. 14. Conditional Qualification of Third Class Barangays. — A Third Class
Barangay may qualify for an Equalization Share when, notwithstanding its
classification, its financial capacity is materially inadequate to meet the
requirements for basic services and facilities by reason of one or more of the
following circumstances:

   a. high poverty incidence;
   b. population or population density which imposes substantially greater
      requirements for basic services and facilities;
   c. geographic isolation, insularity, difficult accessibility, or substantial
      dispersion of population;
   d. severe or recurring exposure to natural calamities or other hazards;
   e. armed conflict or serious peace and order conditions;
   f. substantial daytime, transient, seasonal, displaced, or other population not
      adequately reflected in its regular revenues;

                                                                                    9
   g. serious deficiency in basic services and facilities which cannot reasonably
       be addressed from its available resources;
   h. extraordinary increases in the cost of providing basic services and facilities
       arising from circumstances beyond the reasonable control of the barangay;
       or
   i. such other analogous circumstances as may be prescribed.

   Provided, That the existence of any such circumstance shall not, by itself,
entitle a Third Class Barangay to an Equalization Share. The barangay must
further be shown, on the basis of official and verifiable data, to have regular
revenues and other resources insufficient to meet the corresponding requirements
for basic services and facilities.

   Qualification under this section shall be determined as part of the regular
computation of the Fund and shall not require political endorsement or
discretionary approval by any official.

SEC. 15. Allocation of the Barangay Equalization Fund. — The Fund shall be
allocated among qualified barangays in accordance with a uniform and equitable
formula which shall take into account:

   a. Classification, with preferential weight accorded to Fifth Class Barangays,
       followed by Fourth Class Barangays;
   b. Financial Capacity, based on regular income, locally generated revenues,
       and other resources reasonably available to the barangay;
   c. Population, based on the latest official census or population count;
   d. Poverty, based on the latest official data available;
   e. Land Area and Geographic Conditions, including isolation, insularity,
       accessibility, and dispersion of population; and


                                                                                  10
   f. Requirements for Basic Services and Facilities, taking into account
      conditions which result in substantially greater expenditures for the
      barangay concerned.

   The Fund shall first provide for the Equalization Shares of Fifth and Fourth
Class Barangays. Qualified Third Class Barangays may thereafter receive an
Equalization Share in accordance with the formula and subject to the amount
available for the Fund.

   The formula shall ensure that allocations to qualified Third Class Barangays
shall not prejudice the preferential assistance intended for Fourth and Fifth Class
Barangays.

   The formula, weights, and manner of computation shall be published and
made available to all barangays and to the public.

SEC. 16. Determination of Equalization Share. — The Equalization Share of a
qualified barangay shall be determined by taking into account the resources
reasonably available to it and the amount reasonably necessary for the proper
discharge of its functions and the provision of basic services and facilities.

      For this purpose, consideration shall be given to:

   a. its classification;
   b. its regular revenues and other resources reasonably available;
   c. its reasonable capacity to generate revenues from sources authorized by
      law;
   d. the amount necessary to maintain the minimum organization required for
      efficient and effective barangay government;
   e. the cost of providing basic services and facilities appropriate to its
      population and circumstances; and
                                                                                 11
   f. additional costs arising from poverty, geographic isolation, natural
       calamities, peace and order conditions, or other circumstances beyond the
       reasonable control of the barangay.

   The difference between the resources reasonably available to the barangay and
the amount reasonably necessary for the foregoing purposes shall constitute the
principal basis for determining its Equalization Share.

SEC. 17. Preferential Share of Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays. — Fifth
Class Barangays shall receive the highest priority in the allocation of the Fund,
followed by Fourth Class Barangays.

       Every Fifth Class Barangay shall be entitled to a minimum Equalization
Share sufficient to assist it in maintaining the basic organization, services, and
facilities necessary for the proper discharge of its functions.

       Fourth Class Barangays shall be entitled to an Equalization Share
according to their financial capacity and requirements for basic services and
facilities.

       A qualified Third Class Barangay shall receive only such supplementary
Equalization Share as may be necessary to address the circumstances which gave
rise to its qualification under this Act.

       Nothing in this section shall be construed to require equal allocations
among barangays belonging to the same class.

SEC. 18. Local Revenue Effort. — The equalization system shall encourage
qualified barangays to generate and maximize the use of their resources and
revenues.



                                                                                12
       A barangay shall not be entitled to a greater Equalization Share merely by
reason of its failure, without sufficient cause, to collect taxes, fees, charges, and
other revenues which it is authorized and reasonably able to collect.

       Neither shall a barangay which increases its locally generated revenues
suffer an immediate and equivalent reduction in its Equalization Share.

       In determining the qualification of a Third Class Barangay, particular
consideration shall be given to whether the barangay has reasonably exercised the
revenue-raising powers available to it.

       The implementing rules shall provide reasonable incentives for improved
revenue collection, sound fiscal administration, and economical and efficient use
of public funds.

SEC. 19. Effect of Reclassification on Equalization Share. — A Fifth Class
Barangay reclassified as Fourth Class shall remain qualified to receive an
Equalization Share, subject to the formula applicable to Fourth Class Barangays.

       A Fourth Class Barangay reclassified as Third Class shall thereafter qualify
for an Equalization Share only upon satisfaction of the conditions prescribed for
Third Class Barangays: Provided, That where it does not so qualify, its
Equalization Share shall be phased out over a period not exceeding two (2) fiscal
years when necessary to prevent substantial disruption in the delivery of basic
services and facilities.

       A Third Class Barangay reclassified as Second Class shall cease to be
qualified for an Equalization Share beginning on the fiscal year following the
effectivity of its reclassification.




                                                                                  13
       A barangay reclassified from First or Second Class to Third Class may
qualify for an Equalization Share only upon satisfaction of the conditions
prescribed under this Act.

       A barangay reclassified as Fourth or Fifth Class shall become regularly
qualified to receive an Equalization Share beginning on the fiscal year following
the effectivity of its reclassification.

SEC. 20. Duration of Qualification of Third Class Barangays. — The
qualification of a Third Class Barangay for an Equalization Share shall be
determined annually.

Such qualification shall continue only for so long as the circumstances giving rise
thereto exist and the barangay continues to satisfy the requirements of this Act.

The discontinuance of the Equalization Share of a Third Class Barangay shall not
affect any program, project, or contractual obligation lawfully funded from
amounts previously released to it.

Nothing herein shall prevent a Third Class Barangay from qualifying again in a
subsequent fiscal year when the conditions provided in this Act are present.

SEC. 21. Use of Equalization Funds. — Amounts received from the Fund shall
be used principally for:

   a. the provision, maintenance, and improvement of basic services and
       facilities;
   b. programs, projects, services, and activities contained in the approved
       barangay development plan;
   c. acquisition, operation, and maintenance of equipment necessary for
       barangay operations;

                                                                                 14
   d. construction, repair, and maintenance of barangay facilities and other
      public works;
   e. disaster preparedness and response, public safety, health, sanitation, social
      welfare, and environmental protection;
   f. strengthening the administrative and financial capability of the barangay;
      and
   g. such other developmental and general welfare purposes as may be
      authorized by law.

   Equalization funds shall accrue to the general fund of the barangay, subject to
such budgeting, accounting, auditing, and reporting requirements as may be
prescribed.

SEC. 22. Shared Services and Facilities. — Where the finances of a barangay
do not allow the maintenance of personnel, equipment, or facilities necessary for
the proper discharge of its functions, the city or municipality concerned may
provide common or shared services for two (2) or more barangays.

      Such services may include accounting, budgeting, planning, engineering
and technical services, procurement assistance, information systems, disaster
preparedness, social welfare, environmental management, and other services
which may be more economically and effectively provided on a common basis.

      Two (2) or more barangays may likewise, through appropriate agreements
and when their respective sanggunians so approve, group themselves, consolidate
or coordinate their efforts, services, and resources for purposes commonly
beneficial to them.

      The city or municipality concerned may provide financial, technical, or
administrative assistance for such arrangements.

                                                                                 15
SEC. 23. Assistance by Provinces, Cities, and Municipalities. — Provinces,
cities, and municipalities may extend financial, technical, or other forms of
assistance to barangays within their respective territorial jurisdictions when their
finances so allow.

       In extending such assistance, preference may be given to Fourth and Fifth
Class Barangays and to barangays which have inadequate basic services and
facilities or which, because of their circumstances, require additional assistance.

       Cities and municipalities are encouraged to provide technical assistance,
common facilities, and shared services to component barangays which lack the
financial or administrative capacity to maintain such services independently.

       Such assistance shall be in addition to any amount otherwise accruing to
the barangay under law.

SEC. 24. Organization and Personnel. — The organization of every barangay
shall be designed to ensure efficient, effective, and economical government and
shall be commensurate with its financial capacity, population, and requirements
for basic services and facilities.

       The appropriate national agencies may prescribe indicative organizational
structures and staffing patterns for the several classes of barangays.

       When the finances of the barangay allow, the sangguniang barangay may
provide for such additional personnel as may be necessary to carry out its
programs, projects, services, and activities, subject to applicable civil service,
compensation, and budgetary rules.

       Barangays which lack the financial capacity to maintain necessary
technical or professional personnel may avail themselves of shared services or

                                                                                  16
technical assistance from the city, municipality, province, or National
Government.

SEC. 25. Compensation, Honoraria, and Benefits. — The classification and
financial capacity of a barangay may be taken into account in determining the
rates of compensation, honoraria, allowances, and other benefits of barangay
officials and personnel.

      Such rates may be graduated according to classification: Provided, That the
finances of the barangay shall allow the payment thereof: Provided, further, That
no reclassification shall operate to diminish the compensation, honoraria,
allowances, or benefits lawfully received by an incumbent during his or her term
or tenure.

SEC. 26. Personal Services. — The limitations on appropriations for personal
services may be graduated according to the classification and financial capacity
of barangays.

      In prescribing such limitations, due regard shall be given to the need to
maintain adequate funds for development programs and basic services and
facilities and, at the same time, to enable barangays to maintain the personnel
reasonably necessary for efficient and effective governance.

      A barangay shall not be prevented from maintaining personnel necessary
for the discharge of functions required by law solely by reason of its limited
capacity to generate local revenues.

SEC. 27. Procurement and Financial Administration. — Rules governing
procurement, budgeting, accounting, disbursement, financial reporting, and other
fiscal matters   may prescribe different requirements according to the


                                                                               17
classification, financial capacity, administrative capability, and volume of
transactions of barangays.

      Such rules shall promote economy and efficiency, safeguard local funds
and property, and ensure accountability in their use and disposition.

      Barangays possessing sufficient financial and administrative capacity may
be accorded such appropriate authority and flexibility as may be warranted by
their classification, subject to adequate safeguards for transparency and
accountability.

      Simplified procedures, standard forms, common systems, and technical
assistance shall, whenever practicable, be provided to barangays with limited
financial and administrative capacity.

SEC. 28. Public Utilities and Economic Enterprises. — A barangay may, when
its finances and administrative capacity so allow and subject to such limitations
as may be provided by law, establish, maintain, lease, operate, or otherwise
provide for public utilities and economic enterprises intended primarily to serve
the needs of its inhabitants and promote the general welfare.

      The classification of the barangay may be considered in determining its
capacity to undertake such activities and the financial and administrative
requirements applicable thereto.

SEC. 29. Counterpart Requirements. — In programs and projects requiring
counterpart funds from barangays, the amount of counterpart required may be
graduated according to the classification and financial capacity of the barangay.




                                                                               18
      Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays may be required to provide a reduced
counterpart or may be exempted therefrom where their financial condition so
warrants.

      A qualified Third Class Barangay may likewise be granted a reduced
counterpart where the circumstances which qualified it for an Equalization Share
so warrant.

      The inability of a barangay to provide counterpart funds shall not, by itself,
disqualify it from a program or project intended primarily to address poverty,
deficiency in basic services and facilities, disaster recovery, public health, or
other urgent community needs.

SEC. 30. Other Financial Assistance. — Nothing in this Act shall prevent any
barangay, regardless of classification, from receiving grants, subsidies, disaster
assistance, financial assistance, or funding under national or local programs for
which it is otherwise qualified.

      Such assistance shall be separate and distinct from the Barangay
Equalization Fund and shall not be charged against amounts appropriated
therefor.

SEC. 31. No Diminution of Shares and Benefits. — Nothing in this Act shall be
construed to diminish the share of any barangay in national taxes, the proceeds
from the utilization and development of national wealth, or any other share,
benefit, aid, or allotment accruing to it under law.

      The Equalization Share granted to Fifth Class, Fourth Class, and qualified
Third Class Barangays shall be additional to, and shall not be in substitution for,
their regular shares, revenues, and receipts.


                                                                                  19
      The ineligibility of a barangay for an Equalization Share shall not
constitute a ground for withholding from it any grant, aid, subsidy, financial
assistance, program, project, or other benefit for which it is otherwise qualified.

      The classification of a barangay shall not, by itself, constitute sufficient
ground for withholding assistance intended for poverty reduction, health,
education, nutrition, social welfare, housing, disaster response and recovery,
peace and order, or other programs based upon the needs of its inhabitants.

SEC. 32. Barangay Classification and Equalization Records. — The
Department of Finance shall maintain complete and current records of the
income, revenues, classification, and other financial information of all barangays.

      The agencies concerned shall maintain and make available such official
information on population, land area, poverty, geographic conditions, basic
services and facilities, and other matters as may be necessary for the proper
implementation of this Act.

      The classification of every barangay, the principal basis therefor, the
formula for equalization, and the corresponding Equalization Share of each
qualified barangay shall be matters of public record.

SEC. 33. Annual Statement of Equalization. — The Department of Budget and
Management, in coordination with the Department of Finance and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, shall cause the annual
publication of a statement showing:

   a. the total amount available for the Fund;
   b. the formula and factors used in allocating the Fund;
   c. the Fourth and Fifth Class Barangays entitled to an Equalization Share;


                                                                                 20
   d. the Third Class Barangays qualified to receive an Equalization Share and
      the basis of their qualification;
   e. the Equalization Share corresponding to each qualified barangay; and
   f. such other information as may be necessary to ensure transparency and
      accountability in the administration of the Fund.

   The statement shall be transmitted to Congress and made available to all
barangays and to the public.

SEC. 34. Review of Classification and Equalization Share. — Any barangay
aggrieved by its classification, qualification, or the computation of its
Equalization Share may, within sixty (60) days from publication thereof, seek a
review by the Secretary of Finance.

      The review shall be limited to manifest errors or material omissions in the
official information used in determining the classification, qualification, or
allocation.

      The Secretary of Finance shall resolve the request within ninety (90) days
from receipt of complete records.

      Pending resolution of the request, the existing classification or
Equalization Share shall remain in force, without prejudice to such adjustment as
may thereafter be found proper.

SEC. 35. Periodic Review. — The classification and equalization system
established under this Act shall be periodically reviewed to determine its
effectiveness in strengthening barangay government, reducing disparities in
financial capacity, improving basic services and facilities, and promoting the
efficient and economical use of public funds.


                                                                               21
       Not later than five (5) years after the initial implementation of this Act, and
every six (6) years thereafter, the Department of Finance, Department of Budget
and Management, and Department of the Interior and Local Government shall
jointly submit to Congress a report on the implementation thereof, together with
such recommendations as may be necessary.

       The review shall include the adequacy of the income ranges, the amount
and distribution of the Fund, the equalization formula, the conditions governing
participation of Third Class Barangays, the effects of classification on barangay
administration, and the capacity of barangays to provide basic services and
facilities.

SEC. 36. Initial Classification and Equalization. — Within eighteen (18)
months from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance shall complete
the initial classification of all barangays.

       For this purpose, the Department of Finance, in coordination with the
Department of Budget and Management, Department of the Interior and Local
Government, Philippine Statistics Authority, and other concerned agencies, shall
undertake a nationwide assessment of the financial condition of barangays and
their requirements for basic services and facilities.

       The assessment shall determine:

   a. the distribution of barangay income and financial capacity;
   b. appropriate income ranges for the several classes;
   c. the reasonable cost of maintaining basic barangay operations;
   d. disparities in the capacity of barangays to provide basic services and
       facilities;
   e. the appropriate formula for the allocation of the Fund;

                                                                                   22
   f. the conditions and thresholds for determining the qualification of Third
      Class Barangays; and
   g. the amount reasonably necessary for the initial implementation of fiscal
      equalization.

   The initial classification and equalization formula shall take effect at the
beginning of the fiscal year immediately following their completion and
publication.

SEC. 37. Administration and Release of the Fund. — The Fund shall be
administered by the Department of Budget and Management in coordination with
the Department of Finance and the Department of the Interior and Local
Government.

      Amounts accruing to qualified barangays under the Fund shall be released
directly and in accordance with the formula prescribed under this Act.

      No additional approval, endorsement, or certification shall be required for
the release of an amount already determined to be due a barangay under the
equalization formula, except such documents as may be necessary to establish the
identity of the recipient barangay and the proper account to which the amount
shall be credited.

      No portion of the Fund shall be allocated through discretionary selection
of individual barangay beneficiaries.

SEC. 38. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the agencies
concerned.




                                                                               23
       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

       Appropriations for the Barangay Equalization Fund shall be separately
identified and shall be used exclusively for the purposes provided under this Act.

SEC. 39. Rules and Regulations. — Within six (6) months from the effectivity
of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, in consultation with the Secretary of Budget
and Management, the Secretary of the Interior and Local Government, the
Philippine Statistics Authority, the appropriate government agencies, and the
Liga ng mga Barangay, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
the effective implementation of this Act.

       Such rules and regulations shall provide, among others, for:

   a. the income ranges corresponding to the several classes of barangays;
   b. the manner of determining average annual regular income;
   c. the manner by which population, land area, locally generated revenues,
       poverty, geographic conditions, and requirements for basic services and
       facilities shall be considered in classification;
   d. the formula for the allocation of the Barangay Equalization Fund;
   e. the minimum Equalization Share for Fifth Class Barangays;
   f. the conditions and objective thresholds governing the qualification of
       Third Class Barangays;
   g. measures     to    encourage local      revenue effort and sound       fiscal
       administration;
   h. transitional arrangements upon reclassification;
   i. standards for differentiated        administrative   requirements; and (j)
       procedures for administrative review and correction.
                                                                                 24
   The rules and regulations shall be promulgated only after consultation with
barangays representing the different classes, regions, geographic conditions, and
levels of development.

SEC. 40. Transitory Provision. — Pending the initial classification of barangays
and the promulgation of the rules and regulations necessary to implement the
differentiated standards authorized under this Act, existing rules governing
barangay organization, personnel, compensation, budgeting, procurement,
accounting, and financial administration shall continue to apply.

      The implementation of any new requirement arising solely from the
classification of a barangay may be phased in over such reasonable period as may
be prescribed, but in no case exceeding two (2) fiscal years from the effectivity
of such requirement.

      No barangay shall be required to abolish an existing office, terminate a
permanent employee, discontinue an essential service, or diminish a lawful
benefit solely by reason of reclassification.

SEC. 41. Separability Clause. — If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 42. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with
any provision of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 43. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
