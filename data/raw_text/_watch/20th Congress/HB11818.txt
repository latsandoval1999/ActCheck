                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution upholds the right of workers to security of tenure as a
fundamental right and mandates the State to afford full protection to labor. At the same
time, it recognizes the right of enterprises to reasonable returns on investments and
to expansion and growth. These constitutional principles underscore the need to
balance the protection of workers' rights with the legitimate business interests of
employers.

        Notwithstanding, the status of employees whose work is dependent upon a
client or principal of their employer remains one of the recurring difficulties that is
inadequately addressed by our labor statutes and related executive issuances. For
instance, if an employer loses a service contract or experiences a reduction in the
client's requirements, the employees may be placed on floating status without wages
while awaiting reassignment, potentially leaving them without a reliable source of
income.

       The issue is particularly significant in industries where work assignments
depend on contracts with third-party clients, including the private security and business
process outsourcing industries. When a client contract expires, is terminated, or is
reduced, affected employees may be placed on floating status without wages while
awaiting reassignment. Although temporary suspension may be justified when work is
genuinely unavailable, the absence of clear statutory standards creates a risk that the
practice may be used to avoid the payment of wages and benefits or to circumvent
security of tenure.

        Recognizing that employees placed on floating status may be deprived of

employers to provide financial assistance throughout the perioO of su-ctr status. These

awaiting reassignmen_t or redeployment. The continuing fi'nancial obligation also

       To prevent floating status from becoming indefinite, the proposed measure

lawful ground and in accordance with applicable legal requiremenis. iogether,ihese
safeguards seek to protect emptoyees from undue financial hardship aid proionged

to protect labor.

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Protection Against
Abusive Floating Status Act."

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to afford full
protection to labor and promote the security of tenure of workers, while recognizing
the right of enterprises to reasonable returns on investments and to expansion and
growth. The State shall ensure that temporary suspension of work is permitted only
when justified by bona fide operational necessity and shall prevent the use of floating,
off-detail, work-pool, or reserved status to circumvent labor standards, evade the
payment of wages and benefits, or effect constructive dismissal.

SECTION 4, ProhibitedActs.
                             - lt shall be unlawful for any employer to:
   (a) place an employee on floating status without          a bona fide and
       demonstrable business necessity;

   (b) declare an employee on floating status when a suitable and reasonably
       available assignment exists for which the employee is qualified;

   (c) use floating status as a means to discriminate against, retaliate against,
       punish, harass, or compel the resignation of an employee;

   (d) falsely represent that no assignment, post, account, project, or position
       is available while retaining, hiring, or deploying another employee to
              perform substantially the same work, absent a legitimate and
              documented business reason;

           (e) deliberately rotate employees in and out of floating status for the
               purpose of avoiding the six-month maximum period;

          (f) terminate an employee at the expiration of floating status without
              complying with the substantive and procedural requirements for
              termination under this Code;

          (g) require an employee on floating status to waive, release, or surrender
              statutory rights as a condition for recallor reassignment;

          (h) retaliate against an employee for reporting or chailenging an unlawful
              placement on floating status; or

          (i) submit false, fraudulent, or materially misleading information to the
              Department of Labor and Employment concerning the basis for floating
              status.

      SECTION 5. Administrative Penalties.    Any employer found, after due notice
and hearing, to have violated any          -
                                    provision of this Act shall be subject to an
administrative fine of not less than ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS
(F100,000.00) but not more than TWO HUNDRED FIFTY THOUSAND PESOS
(P250,000.00) for each affected employee, to be imposed by the Secretary of Labor
and Employment or the duly authorized representative, in accordance with the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

         Where the violation is committed against ten (10) or more employees arising
from the same act or policy, the aggregate administrative fine may be increased by up
to fifty percent (50%) of the maximum aggregate fine othenrise imposable.

         ln determining the amount of the administrative fine, the Department of Labor
and Employment shall consider the gravity and duration of the violation, the number
of employees affected, the employer's history of violations, whether the violation was
willful, and whether the employer promptly corrected the violation.

       The administrative fine imposed under this Section shall be without prejudice
to the affected employee's right to reinstatement, backwages, separation pay in lieu
of reinstatement where legally proper, damages, attorney's fees, and other remedies
available under this Code or other applicable laws. The payment of an administrative
fine shall not extinguish or diminish any monetary or other substantive remedy to which
the affected employee may be entitled.

       The administrative penalties under this Section shall be imposed in addition to
any other penalty provided by law, subject to applicable rules against double recovery
and other applicable legal limitations.

       SECTION 8. Determination and Periodic Review of Financial Assisfan ce.

Employment shall determine and publish the initialdaily rate of financial assistance for

       ln determining and periodically reviewing the rate of financial assistance, the
