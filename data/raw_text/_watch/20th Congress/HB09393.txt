                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition that
affects communication, social interaction, behavior, sensory processing, and
adaptive functioning, with varying levels of support needs. Across the country,
many persons with ASD and their families continue to face challenges in
accessing early diagnosis, intervention services, inclusive education, healthcare,
employment opportunities, and social support systems. These difficulties are
further aggravated by the lack of comprehensive and coordinated government
programs specifically designed to address their unique needs.

      While existing laws such as Magna Carta for Disabled Persons and
Inclusive Education Act recognize the rights of persons with disabilities and
learners with disabilities, there remains a need for a more focused, integrated,
and nationwide approach that specifically addresses ASD across all stages of life.

      This proposed measure seeks to establish a National Comprehensive
Program on Autism Spectrum Disorder through a whole-of-government and
multi-sectoral framework that promotes early identification and intervention,
public awareness, inclusive education, healthcare access, skills development,
employment opportunities, community participation, and protection against
discrimination.

      This bill proposes the creation of an Inter-Agency ASD Coordinating
Committee (IACC) tasked with formulating and implementing policies and
programs relating to ASD. It likewise mandates epidemiological surveillance and
research initiatives to generate reliable data necessary for evidence-based
policymaking and service delivery.

      Recognizing the importance of accessibility and inclusion, the measure
also strengthens support mechanisms for families and caregivers, promotes
reasonable accommodation in schools and workplaces, expands training for
government personnel and service providers, and encourages the development
of community-based and telehealth intervention systems, particularly in
underserved areas.
      Further, this bill seeks to uphold the dignity, rights, and full participation
of persons with ASD in accordance with the principles embodied in the United
Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities and other relevant
laws and policies.

       By institutionalizing a comprehensive national program for persons with
ASD, this measure aims to foster a more inclusive, accessible, compassionate,
and responsive society where persons with ASD are empowered to reach their
fullest potential and meaningfully contribute to nation-building.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      INSTITUTING A NATIONAL COMPREHENSIVE PROGRAM ON
      AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Comprehensive
Autism Spectrum Disorder Intervention Act."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health of the people and to instill health consciousness
among them. The State likewise adopts an integrated and comprehensive
approach to health development and recognizes the important role of persons
with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in nation-building and community
development through their talents, skills, knowledge, and unique contributions.

      Towards this end, the State shall give priority to the promotion of the full
and active participation of persons with ASD in society and shall support their
personal development and holistic growth to enable them to attain their fullest
potential in an inclusive and accessible environment. The State shall likewise
establish and strengthen institutional and social mechanisms that provide
support and protection for children and adults with ASD.

       The State further recognizes its obligations under Magna Carta for
Disabled Persons, Inclusive Education Act, and the United Nations Convention
on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities and shall ensure that persons with
ASD enjoy equal rights, dignity, accessibility, inclusion, and full participation in
all aspects of society.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

      (a) Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) refers to a neurodevelopmental
          condition characterized by differences in communication, social
         interaction, behavior, sensory processing, and            adaptive
         functioning, with varying levels of support needs;

      (b) Diagnosis refers to necessary assessments, including but not
          limited to neuro-psychological evaluations, genetic testing, MRIs,
          hearing tests, psychometric tests to diagnose or discount autism
          and/or developmental disabilities in individuals;

      (c) Intervention refers to interceding action undertaken to help
          improve the autism prognosis. Intervention in autism may take
          the form of medical, pharmacological, therapeutic, educational,
          and non-traditional rehabilitative care;

      (d) Epidemiology refers to the study and analysis of the distribution
          and determinants of health conditions, like autism, in defined
          populations;

      (e) Reasonable Accommodation refers to necessary and appropriate
          modifications and adjustments that do not impose a
          disproportionate or undue burden, where needed in a particular
          case, to ensure that persons with ASD enjoy or exercise their
          rights and freedoms on an equal basis with others.

       SECTION 4. Epidemiological Surveillance Programs. - The National
Institute of Health (NIH) is hereby mandated to conduct an annual
epidemiological survey to determine the extent and total incidence and
prevalence of persons with ASD in the country in order for the government to
provide the necessary and appropriate interventions to address the needs faced
by persons with ASD.

      The NIH is mandated to coordinate with the Department of Health (DOH)
in the conduct of epidemiological research on the issues concerning ASD. The
NIH is mandated to strengthen its research focus on ASD; to renew its
investment into basic and clinical research; to expand its research into the link
between environmental factors and ASD; and to continue investigation into
causation, diagnosis, early detection, and treatment of ASD. Such factors that
will be studied shall include, among others, infection and immune function,
hormonal and reproductive factors, genetic factors, gastro-intestinal factors, and
socio-demographic factors.

       All collection, processing, storage, and sharing of data under this section
shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the ‘Data Privacy
Act of 2012.
      SECTION 5. Guidelines on Research. - The DOH shall issue the
standards, guidelines, and regulations in order to ensure that there is no conflict
of interest between the research agenda of the researchers and the drug
manufacturers or government agencies promoting the widespread use of a
particular medicine for persons with ASD.

      The DOH shall ensure the timely publication and dissemination of
information of these guidelines and regulations in order to ensure that the
various health providers and stakeholders all over the country are adequately
informed.

      All ASD-related research involving human participants shall undergo
ethical review by duly accredited ethics review boards and shall adhere to
nationally and internationally accepted ethical standards.

       SECTION 6. Creation of an Inter-Agency ASD Coordinating
Committee- There is hereby established an Inter-Agency ASD Coordinating
Committee (IACC) which shall serve as an inter-agency and multi-sectoral
mechanism tasked to promote the implementation of the National ASD Program
of the government.

      The IACC shall endeavor to:

      a. Promote public awareness, education, and information on issues
         concerning ASD;

      b. Establish sustainable networks of support groups for families of
         children and adults with ASD;

      c. Provide referral services to persons with ASD and identify the
         appropriate educational and medical expertise and facilities suited to
         the needs of the autistic person;

      d. Address the challenges associated with ASD through screening,
         education, early intervention, prompt referrals for treatment and
         services;

      e. Undertake research and/or collaborate with other entities on research
         activities regarding ASD in the Philippines;

      f. Coordinate with schools in formulating individualized programs for
         school children and students with ASD;

      g. Receive donations and grants from international donor institutions,
         foundations, and multilateral and bilateral foreign donor agencies
         involved in the prevention, rehabilitation, interventions, and treatment
         of persons with ASD;

      h. Promote the advocacy against bias and stereotyping against Persons
         with ASD thereby eliminating discrimination and/or misconceptions
         and stigma surrounding ASD with the end in view of opening the doors
         of productive economic opportunities to persons with ASD;

      i. Provide the avenue of exchange of information, exchange programs and
         sharing of best practices on issues relating to the early detection,
         prevention, interventions, treatment, and rehabilitation of persons
         diagnosed with ASD;

      j. Coordinate with local government units (LGUs) for the establishment
         of community-based autism programs, referral systems, and accessible
         intervention services in provinces, cities, and municipalities;

      k. Network with other organizations, public and private institutions and
         professionals working for the promotion of legislation and public
         programs for the persons diagnosed with ASD; and

      l. Assist in the creation of appropriate programs for individuals diagnosed
         with ASD.

      The IACC shall accomplish such objectives and fulfill its mandate towards
the end goal of setting up a nationwide network of centers for ASD in highly
urbanized cities and in each province in the country.

      SECTION 7. Composition of the Inter-Agency ASD Coordinating
Committee (IACC). - The IACC shall be headed by the Secretary of the DOH as
acting Chairperson. The IACC shall be composed of the following members:

   a. The Secretary of the Department of Social Welfare and Development
      (DSWD);
   b. The Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd);
   c. The Chairperson of the Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC);
   d. The Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
   e. The Executive Director of the Technical Education and Skills
      Development Authority (TESDA);
   f. The Executive Director of the National Council on Disability Affairs
      (NCDA);
   g. The Head of the Philippine Information Agency (PIA);
   h. One (1) representative from the pharmaceutical or healthcare sector
      in an advisory capacity only and without voting rights;
   i. One (1) representative from the duly accredited association of nurses
      in the Philippines;
   j. One (1) representative from the duly accredited association of
      medical doctors in the Philippines;
   k. One (1) representative from the non-governmental organizations
      (NGOs) involved in the advocacy for the prevention, rehabilitation,
      interventions, and treatment of persons with ASD;
   l. One (1) representative from a duly recognized association of parents
      or caregivers of persons with ASD;
   m. One (1) representative from duly accredited allied health
      professional organizations involved in ASD intervention, including
      occupational       therapists,    speech-language      pathologists,
      psychologists, or behavioral therapists; and
   n. Two (2) Persons with Autism who are the members of ASD civil
      society organization or non-governmental institutions.

       The non-ex officio members of the IACC shall be appointed by the President
of the Philippines from a list of three (3) nominees submitted by their respective
sectors, who shall serve for a term of three (3) years, and renewable for another
term: Provided, That the person appointed shall be of good moral character, of
unquestionable integrity, and with expertise and experience of at least six (6)
years in field of ASD, mental health and other related fields: Provided that, the
Persons with Autism who shall be appointed by the President of the Philippines,
shall serve for four (4) years and renewable for another four (4) years. Provided,
further, that in the event of a vacancy, the successor appointed to fill the same
shall serve only the unexpired portion of the term of the member he/she
succeeds to.

      The members of the IACC from the government agencies and institutions
may designate their permanent representatives who shall have a rank not lower
than an assistant secretary or its equivalent to attend meetings.

      All members of the IACC shall receive emoluments as may be determined
by the Committee, in accordance with existing budget and accounting rules and
regulations.

      Section 8. Meetings. - The IACC shall meet at least once a month upon
three-day notice signed by the Chairperson or as often as necessary upon the
written request of at least two-thirds (2/3) of its members.

     The presence of a majority of the members of the IACC shall constitute a
quorum in order for it to conduct its business.

      Section 9. Programs and Services of the Inter-Agency ASD
Coordinating Committee (IACC). - The programs and services that will be
provided by IACC shall include, among others, the following:
a. Orientation and Counseling Services for a family of newly
   diagnosed children with ASD;

b. Establishment of Family Support Networks and Sibling Programs
   for families of persons with ASD to seek psycho-social support
   for the various problems faced by families with persons with ASD;

c. Establishment of National and Regional Referral Services
   wherein the IACC will update a list of professionals and childcare
   institutions concerned with the care and development of persons
   with ASD;

d. Publication of reading materials, manuals and easily readable
   instruction materials for ASD early detection, prevention,
   rehabilitation and care;

e. The conduct of continuing education, information and training
   programs and lectures on the proper handling of persons with
   ASD;

f. The establishment of a databank and information and monitoring
   system (IMS) on demographic, socio-economic and incidence and
   prevalence of persons with ASD in the country;

g. The publication of a newsletter on current developments,
   resources, and research on ASD;

h. The conduct of continuing education/training of families,
   teachers, therapists, and caregivers of persons with ASD through
   seminars, conventions, and other forms of tri-media educational
   tools;

i. The conduct of training and education programs for families,
   caregivers, and persons with ASD on financial planning,
   including estate planning, investment planning, and trust
   instruments for long-term financial security;

j. The IACC shall maintain and update the roster of government
   agencies and private entities or corporations that employ persons
   with ASD;

k. The IACC shall monitor the list of persons diagnosed with ASD in
   the country in its central registry;

l. The establishment and maintenance of the central registry and
   information systems shall be subject to the Data Privacy Act of
         2012 and other applicable laws protecting confidential and
         sensitive personal information;
      m. Develop    telehealth   and     community-based  intervention
         mechanisms to ensure access to ASD services in geographically
         isolated and disadvantaged areas; and

      n. Coordinate with relevant government agencies to develop
         subsidy, financial assistance, or social protection programs for
         economically disadvantaged persons with ASD and their families.

       SECTION 10. Early Identification and Intervention for children with
ASD. – The IACC is hereby mandated to establish a program for early
identification, screening, and detection of ASD, and to provide the appropriate
services and referrals to programs of government, if any, to minors with
developmental delays associated with ASD.

     Screening and assessment tools shall be evidence-based, culturally
appropriate, and age-responsive.

      SECTION 11. ASD Early Detection and Screening Fees. - The Philippine
Health Insurance Corporation (PHIC) shall include the cost of early detection and
screening programs as one of the available benefits of its members.

      The early detection and screening test fees shall be applied to, among
others, testing costs, follow-up, and reasonable overhead expenses. These early
detection and screening tests are designed to identify children who should
receive more intensive diagnosis or assessment in order to improve child health
and well-being especially for children suspected within the autism spectrum
disorder.

      SECTION 12. Creation of a National Website for Persons with ASD. -
The IACC is hereby mandated to establish a national website on the National
Program for ASD in the Philippines. The website shall highlight the various
programs, interventions, services, and advocacy channels being provided by the
government on the issue concerning ASD so that parents and relatives of persons
with ASD can avail of the information and services being provided by the State.

      This website shall contain all the information on the variety of medical,
psychological, psycho-social and research interventions available in the
Philippines on the issues concerning ASD.

      The website shall also include information on the various DOH accredited
health service providers who can provide therapy, interventions, and treatment
to persons with ASD in the country.
      It will also be an avenue to clear misconceptions and incorrect information
on the nature and characteristics of ASD.

      SECTION 13. Inclusive Education. - As mandated in Republic Act No.
11650, otherwise known as the "Instituting a Policy of Inclusion and Services for
Learners with Disabilities in Support of Inclusive Education Act", the policy of
inclusion is hereby instituted in all early and basic education schools, both
public and private. All schools, whether public or private, shall ensure equitable
access to quality education to every learner with disability, including learners
with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), such that no learner shall be denied
admission on the basis of disability.

       SECTION 14. Continuing Education and Training of Health
Personnel and Service Providers on ASD. - All DOH, DSWD, CWC officers and
their staff in charge with providing various developmental and welfare services
to persons with ASD are mandated to undergo a mandatory continuing
education and training program to sensitize and empower service providers
including healthcare workers, guidance counselors, social workers, first
responders, and local government frontline personnel to become effective
frontliners in the delivery of health services to the persons with ASD, who are
the beneficiaries of this Act.

      SECTION 15. Protection against Discrimination in Schools. - The
Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), Department of
Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Council for the Welfare of Children
(CWC) and the National Institute for Health (NIH), shall formulate the necessary
implementing rules and regulations to ensure that all educational institutions
shall be free from policies and regulations that discriminate against school
children and students with autism.

      Towards this end, the DepEd shall conduct an annual education,
information, and training program for school administrators, faculty, and staff
to empower them to effectively handle school children and students with ASD.

     Educational institutions shall adopt reasonable accommodation measures
and anti-bullying policies to protect learners with ASD from discrimination,
harassment, exclusion, and abuse.

      SECTION 16. Non-Discrimination of Adults with ASD. - Within six (6)
months from the date of effectivity of this Act, the IACC shall formulate the
necessary guidelines, standards, and regulations in order to ensure that adults
with ASD are not discriminated against due to their condition.

      The IACC shall ensure the timely publication and dissemination of
information of these guidelines and regulations in order to ensure that the
various stakeholders all over the country in the early detection, prevention,
rehabilitation, and care of persons with ASD are adequately informed.

       SECTION 17. Trainings for Adults with ASD. - The TESDA shall make
available with the IACC the range of skills training programs that adults with
ASD may avail under the TESDA. The instructors of the TESDA on the aforesaid
skills programs may undergo the necessary trainings on autism similar to or
including those provided under items (e) and (h) of Section 9 of this Act.

      SECTION 18. Employment for Adults with ASD. - The DOLE shall work
with DepEd and CHED to develop a training program which will help learners
with ASD transition from school to work. The DOLE shall conduct employability
and enhancement seminars, provide pre-employment counseling and
orientation, and offer programs and activities on employment assistance
pursuant to Republic Act No. 8579, otherwise known as the Public Employment
Service Office Act of 1999, as amended.

      Employers are encouraged to adopt inclusive workplace practices and
provide reasonable accommodations for qualified persons with ASD.

       SECTION 19. Congressional Oversight and Annual Report. - The IACC
shall submit an annual report to Congress detailing the implementation status
of this Act, utilization of funds, statistical data, accomplishments, challenges
encountered, and recommendations for policy improvement.

      SECTION 20. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act shall be included and incorporated in the annual general
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD), National Institute of Health (NIH), and the
Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC).

      SECTION 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within six
(6) months from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the
DSWD, CWC, NIH, and PIA, shall promulgate necessary implementing rules and
regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

      SECTION 22. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof not consistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

     SECTION 23. Separability Clause. – If any provisions or part of this Act,
or the application, thereof to any person or circumstance, is held
unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions of this Act which are not affected
hereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
      SECTION 24. Effectivity Clause- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days from the date of its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least
two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

      Approved.
