                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise known as the "Ecological Solid Waste
  Management Act of 2000," established the country's comprehensive framework
  for the segregation, collection, transport, processing, recycling, recovery and
  disposal of solid waste and placed primary responsibility for solid waste
  management on local government units.

         Disasters, particularly typhoons and floods, can severely disrupt this
  waste disposal system. They can generate extraordinary volumes of household
  waste, spoiled food, fallen vegetation, damaged furniture and appliances, bulky
  materials, construction debris and other waste within a short period of time,
  while simultaneously limiting access to roads, collection facilities and disposal
  sites.

       When ordinary waste management capacity is overwhelmed,
  accumulated waste may obstruct roads and drainage systems, impede
  emergency response, aggravate flooding, create public health risks and
  contaminate waterways.

         Existing laws already provide mechanisms for disaster risk reduction,
  anticipatory action and emergency procurement. Republic Act No. 10121
  provides the disaster risk reduction and management framework and the Local
  Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund. Republic Act No. 12287
  provides for anticipatory action before an imminent disaster. Republic Act No.
  12009 provides appropriate procurement mechanisms for emergency
  situations.

         What is needed is to ensure that solid waste management is expressly
  integrated into these existing mechanisms. The measure likewise draws from
  international best practices in disaster waste management, urban drainage
  protection and flood resilience, adapting these approaches to the Philippine
  local government and solid waste management framework.

        This measure therefore strengthens Republic Act No. 9003 by requiring
  local government units to incorporate disaster-resilient solid waste
  management into their existing solid waste management plans. It requires
  LGUs to assess their waste surge capacity, identify temporary staging areas
  and alternative facilities, establish arrangements for additional collection and
hauling capacity, and provide for the continuity and rapid restoration of waste-
management services.

      The measure further clarifies that compatible, non-hazardous disaster-
generated waste may be consolidated with ordinary municipal solid waste,
thereby avoiding the unnecessary creation of a separate disaster-waste system.
Waste requiring special handling, including hazardous waste, shall continue to
be governed by applicable special laws.

      The bill further strengthens the enforcement of solid waste management
rules by establishing a national ₱5,000 penalty for prohibited disposal of solid
waste and a higher penalty for dumping into esteros, canals, waterways,
drainage systems and flood-control facilities, with the maximum penalty
applicable when the offense is committed during an officially declared disaster
period. The measure also allows recovery of reasonable and documented
government cleanup costs from the offender, without prejudice to liability
under other environmental laws.

       At the same time, the measure recognizes that effective solid waste
management should not rely solely on penalties. It therefore strengthens the
existing incentive framework under Republic Act No. 9003 and links disaster-
resilient solid waste management performance with the existing Seal of Good
Local Governance under Republic Act No. 11292. LGUs and communities that
demonstrate sustained compliance, effective waste diversion, protection of
waterways and preparedness for disaster-generated waste may thereby be
recognized and supported through existing government incentive mechanisms.

       The bill does not create a new national bureaucracy, a separate disaster
fund or a new procurement exemption. It instead strengthens and integrates
existing legal mechanisms.

      Through this measure, solid waste management becomes an integral
component of disaster preparedness, response and recovery, helping ensure
that a disaster does not result in a secondary crisis caused by unmanaged
waste.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                               “AN ACT
         STRENGTHENING    DISASTER-RESILIENT SOLID  WASTE
        MANAGEMENT, PROVIDING FOR THE CONTINUITY AND RAPID
        RESTORATION OF SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT SERVICES
        DURING AND AFTER DISASTERS, AMENDING FOR THE
        PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9003, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
        THE "ECOLOGICAL SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT ACT OF
        2000," AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
  Philippines in Congress assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Disaster-
  Resilient Solid Waste Management Act."

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to
  ensure the continuity, resilience and environmentally sound management of
  solid waste before, during and after disasters.

        For this purpose, the State shall:

        (a) Strengthen the capacity of local government units (LGUs) to maintain
            solid waste management services during disasters;

        (b) Enable LGUs to rapidly mobilize additional collection, hauling,
            processing, recovery and disposal capacity when ordinary capacity is
            exceeded;

        (c) Integrate disaster preparedness and anticipatory action into existing
            solid waste management plans;

        (d) Ensure the timely removal of accumulated waste that threatens
            public health, safety, mobility, flood control or emergency response;

        (e) Promote proper waste segregation, recovery, recycling and responsible
            disposal; and
      (f) Encourage and recognize LGUs, barangays, communities and other
          stakeholders that demonstrate effective and sustained solid waste
          management and disaster resilience.

      SECTION 3. Disaster-Generated Solid Waste. – Section 3 of Republic
Act No. 9003 is hereby amended by inserting the following definitions after the
last paragraph thereof:

      "() Disaster-Generated Solid Waste refers to solid waste generated,
      displaced, accumulated or requiring removal as a direct or indirect
      consequence of a disaster, including waste resulting from typhoons,
      floods, storm surges, earthquakes, landslides, fires and other
      hazards, including waste generated during emergency response,
      clearing and recovery operations;

      "( ) Waste Surge Capacity refers to the additional capacity for the
      collection, storage, transfer, transportation, processing, recovery
      and disposal of solid waste that may be mobilized by an LGU beyond
      its normal operating capacity in response to a disaster; and

      "( ) Temporary Disaster Waste Staging Area refers to a duly
      designated and managed temporary area for the receipt, segregation,
      consolidation, sorting or temporary storage of disaster-generated
      solid waste prior to its processing, recovery, transportation or final
      disposal."

       SECTION 4. Disaster Waste Management and Continuity
Component. – Section 17 of Republic Act No. 9003 is hereby amended by
inserting a new paragraph after paragraph (m), to read as follows:

      "(n) Disaster Waste Management and Continuity. – The plan
      shall provide for the continuity and rapid restoration of solid
      waste management services before, during and after disasters
      and shall include:

          “(1) An assessment of projected disaster-generated solid
          waste based on the hazards and vulnerabilities of the LGU;

          “(2) The determination of the LGU's normal solid waste
          management capacity and required waste surge capacity;

          "(3) Identification of temporary staging areas and alternative
          collection, transfer, processing, recovery and disposal
          facilities, taking into account flood risks and potential
          impacts on waterways, drainage infrastructure and flood-
          management facilities;

          "(4) Procedures for pre-disaster       waste   clearing   and
          emergency collection and hauling;
         "(5) Procedures for the management of disaster-generated
         waste, including its consolidation with ordinary municipal
         solid waste when compatible;

         "(6) Procedures for the separate handling of hazardous,
         infectious, chemical, bulky, construction and demolition
         and other waste requiring special management;

         "(7) An inventory of government and private-sector vehicles,
         equipment, facilities and personnel that may be mobilized
         during emergencies;

         "(8) Arrangements for mutual assistance with other LGUs
         and for obtaining additional capacity from qualified private
         entities and other lawful sources;

         “(9) A disaster waste treatment chain providing, to the
         extent practicable, for the collection, segregation,
         temporary storage, sorting, recycling or other resource
         recovery, treatment and final disposal of disaster-generated
         solid waste, including the identification of facilities and
         service providers for each stage;

         "(10) Arrangements for obtaining collection, transportation,
         processing, recovery, treatment or disposal capacity outside
         the affected local government unit when local capacity is
         insufficient;"

         "(11) Periodic training, drills and capacity-building activities
         for personnel, contractors, barangays and other
         stakeholders responsible for disaster-generated solid waste
         management; and

         “(12) Where technically and financially feasible, the
         integration of nature-based and blue-green measures that
         support stormwater management, flood resilience,
         protection of waterways and the prevention of solid waste
         accumulation or obstruction, including retention or
         detention areas, permeable surfaces, rain gardens,
         bioswales, green spaces and similar measures, consistent
         with existing land-use, environmental and infrastructure
         plans.”


      SECTION 5. Waste Surge Capacity. – Every city and municipality shall
determine and periodically assess its waste surge capacity based on
population, normal waste generation, hazard exposure, historical disaster
experience and available resources.
           The required surge capacity may be provided through:

           (a) Existing LGU resources;
           (b) Inter-LGU agreements;
           (c) Qualified private-sector service providers;
           (d) Facilities and equipment of national government agencies; or
           (e) Other lawful arrangements.

      The waste surge capacity assessment shall include the availability and
mobilization of personnel, collection vehicles, hauling and heavy equipment,
temporary collection points and other resources necessary for rapid post-disaster
waste clearance.

      An LGU shall not be required to maintain all surge capacity through
government-owned equipment.

           SECTION 6. Pre-Disaster Solid Waste Management. – When a State of
     Imminent Disaster is declared pursuant to Republic Act No. 12287, or when a
     disaster is reasonably anticipated based on official forecasts and advisories,
     the concerned LGU may, consistent with its approved plans and applicable
     laws:

           (a) Intensify collection of accumulated waste;
           (b) Clear and prepare materials recovery facilities, transfer stations and
               temporary staging areas;
           (c) Pre-position available collection vehicles and equipment;
           (d) Secure additional hauling, processing and disposal capacity;
           (e) Coordinate with neighboring LGUs and concerned national
               government agencies;
           (f) Remove or secure waste and materials that may be displaced by
               floodwaters, strong winds or other hazards;
           (g) Inspect and, where practicable, clear accumulated solid waste and
               other removable obstructions from esteros, canals, drainage systems,
               pumping stations, waterways and flood-control facilities identified as
               critical to flood management.

           SECTION 7. Continuity and Rapid Restoration of Services. – The
     concerned LGU shall, to the extent practicable, maintain the continuity of
     ordinary solid waste collection, transportation, processing, recovery and
     disposal services during and following a disaster.

           Where ordinary capacity is insufficient, the LGU shall activate the waste
     surge capacity identified in its Solid Waste Management Plan.

           Emergency operations shall prioritize areas where accumulated waste
     poses an immediate or substantial risk to:

           (a) Life and public health;
           (b) Emergency response and evacuation;
           (c) Hospitals, health facilities and evacuation centers;
      (d) Major roads, bridges and other critical transportation routes; and
      (e)   Drainage systems, canals, esteros, pumping stations, waterways
and flood-control facilities.

      For purposes of emergency waste clearance, the LGU shall, consistent
with public health and safety, prioritize the collection and appropriate handling
of:

      (1) Perishable, foul-smelling or rapidly decomposing waste;
      (2) Waste obstructing roads, drainage systems, waterways or emergency
          access;
      (3) Bulky disaster-generated waste, including damaged appliances,
          furniture and similar materials;
      (4) Hazardous, infectious or otherwise specially regulated waste, for
          immediate isolation, securing and referral to authorized handlers or
          facilities; and
      (5) Recyclable or recoverable materials, when their separate collection is
          practicable."

      SECTION 8. Emergency Waste Clearance. – Following a disaster, the
concerned LGU shall conduct a rapid assessment of disaster-generated solid
waste and shall commence emergency collection and clearing operations as
soon as reasonably practicable.

      For areas that are accessible and safe, such operations shall, as far as
practicable, commence within seventy-two (72) hours after the occurrence of
the disaster. The period provided herein shall not require personnel to enter
unsafe areas or interfere with life-saving emergency operations.

      SECTION 9. Consolidation of Compatible Disaster-Generated Solid
Waste. – Compatible, non-hazardous disaster-generated solid waste may be
consolidated, collected, transported, processed, recovered or disposed of
together with ordinary municipal solid waste, subject to applicable segregation,
environmental, health and safety requirements.

      The designation of waste as disaster-generated shall not, by itself, require
a separate collection or disposal system.

      Hazardous, infectious, chemical and other regulated waste shall be
separately managed in accordance with applicable laws, rules and regulations.
Construction and demolition debris, bulky waste and other materials shall be
segregated and separately managed where necessary for safety, recovery,
processing or disposal.

      SECTION 10. Temporary Disaster Waste Staging Areas. – An LGU may
designate temporary disaster waste staging areas identified in its Solid Waste
Management Plan or, when necessary, other suitable areas consistent with
applicable environmental and land-use requirements.
      Such areas shall:

      (a) Be located, to the extent practicable, outside areas exposed to flooding
          and other hazards;
      (b) Prevent the discharge of waste, leachate and contaminated runoff into
          waterways and drainage systems;
      (c) Provide appropriate measures for segregation, access control, fire
          prevention and public safety; and
      (d) Be cleared and restored after use.
      (e) Not obstruct or compromise floodways, waterways, drainage
          corridors, pumping facilities, flood-retention areas or other areas
          necessary for the conveyance, storage or management of floodwaters.

      A temporary staging area shall not be deemed or used as an open dump.

      SECTION 11. Inter-LGU Mutual Assistance. – Provinces, cities and
municipalities may enter into mutual assistance agreements for disaster solid
waste management, including the temporary sharing or use of collection
vehicles, heavy equipment, materials recovery facilities, transfer stations,
processing facilities and authorized disposal facilities. Such agreements may
form part of their respective Solid Waste Management Plans and Local Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management Plans.

       SECTION 12. Private Sector Participation. – LGUs may secure
additional disaster solid waste management capacity from qualified private
entities, subject to Republic Act No. 12009, otherwise known as the "New
Government Procurement Act," its implementing rules and regulations, and
other applicable laws.

     Nothing in this Act shall however be construed as creating a new
exemption from procurement requirements.

     SECTION 13. Disaster Solid Waste Management as a Disaster Risk
Reduction Activity. – Disaster solid waste management activities included in
an approved Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan and Solid
Waste Management Plan may be funded from the Local Disaster Risk Reduction
and Management Fund and other lawful sources, subject to Republic Act No.
10121, Republic Act No. 12287, the General Appropriations Act, and applicable
budgeting, accounting, procurement and auditing laws.

       Eligible activities may include pre-disaster waste clearing, emergency
collection and hauling, equipment mobilization or rental, temporary staging,
debris processing and recovery, emergency disposal, and protective equipment
for personnel.

      Nothing in this Section shall create a new mandatory fund or alter the
allocation requirements of Republic Act No. 10121.
       SECTION 14. Documentation and Accountability. – LGUs
undertaking emergency solid waste management operations using public funds
shall maintain reasonable records of the volume or weight of waste collected,
collection and hauling services, receiving or processing facilities, final disposal
and expenditures.

      The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), in
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG)
and the Office of the Civil Defense (OCD), shall establish simplified
documentation and reporting requirements appropriate for emergency
conditions.

      SECTION 15. Recovery and Resource Utilization. – Emergency solid
waste management operations shall, to the extent practicable and consistent
with public health and safety, observe the waste hierarchy and waste diversion
requirements under Republic Act No. 9003.

     Materials suitable for recycling, reuse, composting or other authorized
forms of resource recovery shall be recovered where technically and
economically feasible.

      SECTION 16. Priority Removal from Drainage and Flood-Control
Facilities. – Where disaster-generated solid waste obstructs drainage systems,
canals, esteros, pumping stations, waterways or flood-control facilities, the
concerned LGU shall prioritize its removal when necessary to restore or
maintain the proper flow of water and reduce risks to life and property. Such
operations shall be coordinated, when appropriate, with the Department of
Public Works and Highways, Metropolitan Manila Development Authority and
other concerned agencies.

       The concerned LGU shall, as part of routine solid waste management and
disaster preparedness, establish periodic inspection and clearing measures for
critical drainage and waterway facilities, particularly before periods of
forecasted heavy rainfall, typhoons or other hazards.

      SECTION 17. Incentives for Responsible Waste Management and
Disaster Resilience. – Section 45 of Republic Act No. 9003, as amended by
Republic Act No. 11898, is hereby further amended by adding the following
paragraphs:

      "(c) Disaster-Resilient Solid Waste Management Incentives. –
      Rewards, recognition and appropriate incentives may be provided
      to LGUs, barangays, communities, private organizations,
      enterprises, nongovernment organizations and other qualified
      entities that demonstrate outstanding and sustained
      performance in:

           "(1) Proper segregation, collection,       recycling,   reuse,
           composting and waste diversion;
          "(2) Prevention and reduction of illegal dumping and
          littering;

          "(3) Protection and maintenance of esteros, canals,
          waterways, drainage systems and flood-control facilities
          from solid waste;

          "(4) Effective implementation of disaster-resilient solid
          waste management measures;

          "(5) Maintenance of adequate waste surge capacity and
          continuity of solid waste management services;

          "(6) Rapid and environmentally sound management of
          disaster-generated solid waste; and

          "(7) Innovative, cost-effective and sustainable solid waste
          management practices.

          "(d) Incentives under this Section may include monetary
          awards, grants, technical assistance, equipment, capacity-
          building assistance, public recognition and other non-
          fiscal incentives, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
          auditing and procurement laws and regulations.

          "(e) The Department, in coordination with the DILG, shall
          establish objective and measurable criteria for the grant of
          incentives under this Section.

          "(f) No incentive under this subsection shall be granted for
          an activity or program directly related to a violation for
          which the recipient has been finally determined liable
          under applicable law."


       SECTION 18. Integration with the Seal of Good Local Governance. –
The DILG, through the Council of Good Local Governance established under
Republic Act No. 11292, in coordination with the DENR and other concerned
agencies, shall incorporate appropriate and measurable indicators on disaster-
resilient solid waste management into the applicable performance indicators
and assessment mechanisms of the Seal of Good Local Governance (SGLG)."

      The indicators may include:

      (a) Adoption and implementation of the Disaster Waste Management and
          Continuity Component of the local Solid Waste Management Plan;

      (b) Adequacy of waste surge capacity and arrangements for additional
          collection, hauling, processing and disposal capacity;
      (c) Implementation of appropriate pre-disaster waste management and
          anticipatory measures;

      (d) Timely restoration of solid waste management services following a
          disaster;

      (e) Compliance with segregation, waste diversion, recovery and recycling
          requirements;

      (f) Reduction and enforcement against illegal dumping and littering;

      (g) Protection and maintenance of esteros, canals, waterways, drainage
          systems and flood-control facilities from solid waste;

      (h) Proper management of disaster-generated solid waste; and

      (i) Community participation and sustained performance in ecological
          solid waste management.

      Compliance with these indicators may be considered in the assessment
for the SGLG and, for LGUs qualifying for the corresponding incentive under
Republic Act No. 11292, may be reflected in their Annual Investment Program
and Local Development Investment Program for eligible solid waste
management, disaster resilience, drainage and waterway protection projects,
subject to existing laws, rules and regulations.

      An LGU shall not be deemed non-compliant solely because disaster-
generated waste accumulated as a direct consequence of an event beyond its
reasonable control, provided that it demonstrates substantial compliance with
its approved plans, activates available surge capacity, undertakes timely
response measures and implements appropriate corrective actions.

      SECTION 19. Prohibition on Dumping in Esteros, Waterways and
Flood-Control Facilities. – Section 48, paragraph (1), of Republic Act No. 9003
is hereby amended to read as follows:

            **"(1) Littering, throwing, dumping or causing or permitting
            the dumping of solid waste in public places, including
            roads, sidewalks, canals, esteros, waterways, drainage
            systems, pumping stations, flood-control facilities, parks
            and other similar areas;"

      SEC. 20. Amendment of Penalties for Prohibited Disposal and
Dumping in Waterways and Flood-Control Facilities. – Section 49,
paragraph (a), of Republic Act No. 9003, as amended, is hereby amended and
a new paragraph (a-1) is inserted after paragraph (a), to read as follows::

             **" (a) Any person who violates Section 48, paragraph (1),
             of this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished with a fine
             of Five thousand pesos (P5,000.00), or community service
             of not less than one (1) day but not more than fifteen (15)
             days, or both, at the discretion of the court.

             (a-1) Any person who violates Section 48, paragraph (1),
             by dumping, throwing, depositing or causing or
             permitting the dumping of solid waste in an estero, canal,
             waterway, drainage system, pumping station, flood-
             control facility or other water conveyance facility shall,
             upon conviction, be punished with a fine of not less than
             Ten thousand pesos (P10,000.00) but not more than
             Twenty thousand pesos (P20,000.00), or imprisonment of
             not less than five (5) days but not more than thirty (30)
             days, or both, at the discretion of the court.

            If the violation is committed during a declared State of
            Imminent Disaster, state of calamity, or other officially
            declared disaster period, the maximum penalty shall be
            imposed.

            Upon conviction, the court may order the offender to
            reimburse the reasonable and documented cost incurred
            by the government or concerned local government unit in
            removing and properly disposing of the waste subject of
            the violation, without prejudice to other liabilities under
            applicable laws."



      SECTION 21. Hazardous Waste and Other Environmental Laws.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize or permit the disposal of
hazardous waste or hazardous substances in esteros, canals, waterways,
drainage systems, pumping stations, flood-control facilities or other
unauthorized locations.

      The handling, transport, storage and disposal of hazardous waste and
hazardous substances shall remain subject to Republic Act No. 6969,
otherwise known as the "Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear
Wastes Control Act of 1990," Republic Act No. 9275, otherwise known as the
"Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004," and other applicable laws, rules and
regulations.

      The enforcement of a violation under this Act shall be without prejudice
to liability under other applicable environmental laws, subject to the
Constitution and applicable rules on multiple offenses and double jeopardy.

      SECTION 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one
hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DENR, in
coordination with the DILG, OCD, NDRRMC, MMDA, DOH, representatives of
LGUs and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act.
      The IRR shall establish, among others, standards for disaster-generated
waste characterization, waste surge capacity, temporary staging areas,
emergency waste operations, documentation and the incentives provided under
this Act.

       SECTION 23. Compliance Period. – Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, every province, city and municipality shall review and,
where necessary, amend its existing Solid Waste Management Plan and Local
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan to comply with this Act.

      Existing facilities, contracts and arrangements shall remain valid,
subject to compliance with applicable laws.

      SECTION 24. Harmonization with Existing Laws. – Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to repeal, amend or diminish Republic Act No. 10121,
Republic Act No. 12287, Republic Act No. 11898, Republic Act No. 9275,
Republic Act No. 6969, Republic Act No. 7160, Republic Act No. 11292,
Republic Act No. 12009, the General Appropriations Act, or other applicable
laws.

      This Act shall be implemented in harmony with existing laws governing
disaster risk reduction and management, anticipatory action, procurement,
hazardous waste, water quality, extended producer responsibility, local
government powers and the Seal of Good Local Governance.

      SECTION 25. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 26. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 27. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
