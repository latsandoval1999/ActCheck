                              AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR HOSPITAL BENEFITS AND OTHER PRIVILEGES FOR TEACHERS
AND THEIR DEPENDENTS IN GOVERNMENT HEALTH FACILITIES,
STRENGTHENING THEIR ACCESS TO HEALTH SERVICES THROUGH PHILHEALTH,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution calls on the State to make health care affordable and accessible to
all, especially priority sectors. To ful ill this duty, the government created the National
Health Insurance Program so Filipinos can get medical care without facing heavy
 inancial burden. Teachers clearly belong to this priority group. They play a vital role
in shaping the nation’s future, yet many of them still struggle to get timely medical
consultations, tests, and hospital care, particularly those assigned in public schools
and remote or underserved areas.

Despite their service, teachers often face long queues, complicated procedures, and
out-of-pocket costs when seeking treatment in public hospitals. These challenges can
delay care, worsen illness, and add stress to educators who are already carrying heavy
workloads. When teachers’ health suffers, the quality and continuity of education also
suffer.

This bill responds with a practical solution. It creates a Teachers’ Health Access
Program that improves how teachers access services in government health facilities,
through simple measures such as help desks, referral and monitoring systems, and
coordinated preventive care, without taking away services from other patients.
Implemented through cooperation among the Department of Health, education

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Teachers’ Hospital Bene its
Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to adopt an
integrated and comprehensive approach to health development to make essential
goods, health, and other social services available to all people at affordable cost, with
due regard to priority sectors, including the underprivileged, the sick, the elderly, the
disabled, women, and children.

Toward this end, the State shall protect the welfare of teachers, who perform essential
public service, by improving their access—together with their dependents—to
preventive, promotive, diagnostic, curative, and rehabilitative health services in
government health facilities, and by strengthening coordination with the National
Health Insurance Program.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Teacher” refers to a person employed in a public or private educational
       institution, whether teaching or non-teaching, who performs any of the
       following functions:

      i.     Classroom teaching, including practical and vocational instruction, at
             the day-care, kindergarten, elementary, secondary, or post-secondary
             levels;

      ii.    Guidance and counseling services;

      iii.   Instructional supervision; or

      iv.    School health services as school physician, dentist, or nurse.

   (b) “Dependent” refers to:

      i.     The Legitimate spouse;

      ii.    Legitimate, illegitimate or legally adopted child who is: (i) below
             eighteen (18) years old, unmarried and not gainfully employed; or (ii)
             eighteen (18) years old or above but incapacitated and incapable of
             self-support due to a physical or mental disability; and

      iii.   Parents who are chie ly dependent upon the Teacher for support, as
             may be de ined and veri ied under the IRR.

   (c) “PhilHealth” refers to the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation created
       under the National Health Insurance Act of 1995.

SECTION 4. Teachers’ Health Access Program in Government Health Facilities.
— There is hereby established a Teachers’ Health Access Program to be
implemented by the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the
Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and PhilHealth, for
the purpose of improving access of Teachers and their Dependents to medical services
in Government Health Facilities.

SECTION 5. Minimum Bene its and Privileges. — Subject to the availability of
funds, staf ing, and facilities, and without prejudice to existing PhilHealth bene its and
other applicable laws, Government Health Facilities shall provide Teachers and their
Dependents the following:

   a.) Priority assistance / fast-lane system. Establish a priority lane or dedicated
       help desk for Teachers and their Dependents for registration, billing, and
       claims facilitation;

   b.) Medical services. Access to preventive, promotive, diagnostic, curative, and
       rehabilitative services consistent with DOH service capability standards;

   c.) Preferential scheduling. For non-emergency consultations and diagnostic
       procedures, preferential scheduling may be provided as determined by the
       head of facility, consistent with patient safety and clinical triage protocols;

   d.) Health monitoring and referral. A referral and monitoring system for
       Teachers and their Dependents, including periodic medical examination
       protocols to be de ined in coordination with DepEd, CHED, and TESDA.

For avoidance of doubt, nothing in this Act shall be construed as a basis to deny
emergency care or medically necessary services to non-bene iciaries.

SECTION 6. Bed Allocation and Discounts; Standards to be Set by DOH. —

   (A) Bed Allocation. Instead of a ixed statutory number of beds, the DOH shall,
       through the IRR, prescribe minimum service access standards (which may
       include indicative bed allotments or ward access arrangements) that
       Government Health Facilities shall implement based on level of hospital, bed
       capacity, and catchment population, to avoid impairing general patient
       access and hospital licensing standards.

   (B) Discounts. Any discount on consultations, diagnostics, medicines, or other
       hospital charges in Government Health Facilities shall be implemented only
       upon:

       1. An explicit DOH policy and pricing guideline; and

       2. A corresponding appropriation or identi ied funding source to prevent
          unfunded mandates and protect hospital operations.

SECTION 7. PhilHealth Coordination and Bene it Strengthening. — PhilHealth, in
coordination with the DOH, DepEd, CHED, and TESDA, shall study and implement,
as far as practicable and subject to actuarial viability and existing legal frameworks,
measures to improve bene it availment of Teachers and their Dependents, which may
include:

   (a) expanded provider accreditation for facilities commonly accessed by Teachers;

   (b) simpli ied claims assistance through hospital help desks; and

   (c) enhanced bene it designs for high-burden conditions affecting Teachers,
       consistent with PhilHealth’s fund stewardship and viability obligations.

       This section shall be implemented consistent with PhilHealth’s legal
       limitations on use of funds and bene it structuring and subject to pertinent
       rules and approvals.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with DepEd, CHED,
TESDA, PhilHealth, and other concerned stakeholders, shall promulgate the IRR for
its effective implementation.

SECTION 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and other
concerned agencies under the General Appropriations Act. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 10. Annual Report. — The Secretary of Health shall submit to the
Committees on Health of the Senate and the House of Representatives an annual
report on the implementation of this Act, including utilization, service delivery
indicators, and funding requirements.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.
