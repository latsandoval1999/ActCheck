                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive national framework for the
inclusive development, protection, and well-being of children with special needs.
This is employed with the end in view of strengthening early intervention services,
expanding access to inclusive education and healthcare, enhancing support systems
for caregivers, and promoting public awareness.

        Children with special needs continue to face significant barriers in accessing
timely diagnosis, therapy, quality education, and essential health services. Many
families shoulder overwhelming financial costs for rehabilitation, specialized
learning materials, assistive devices, and continuous medical care, often forcing
parents to reduce working hours or leave employment altogether to provide
full-time care. Despite existing laws protecting persons with disabilities, access to
intervention services remains uneven, particularly in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas where specialized professionals and facilities are scarce.1

       The urgency of strengthening support for children with special needs is
underscored by both global and local evidence. According to the United Nations
Children's Fund, nearly 240 million children worldwide are living with disabilities,
many of whom experience poorer educational, health, and social outcomes than
their peers.2 In the Philippines, the Department of Social Welfare and Development
emphasizes that children with disabilities remain among the country's most
vulnerable sectors, requiring strengthened early intervention, inclusive education,
rehabilitation services, and community-based support to achieve meaningful
inclusion.3

                           AN ACT
PROMOTING THE INCLUSIVE DEVELOPMENT AND WELL-BEING OF
CHILDREN WITH SPECIAL NEEDS, PROVIDING FOR COMPREHENSIVE
SUPPORT SYSTEMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Inclusive Development
and Well-Being of Children with Special Needs Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights of children with special needs, ensuring their full and
inclusive development. The State recognizes that children with special needs
possess inherent dignity and right to a quality of life that enables them to achieve
their fullest potential. To this end, the State shall:

           a. Prioritize and integrate the needs of children with special needs into all
       relevant legislative efforts, policies, programs, and services.​

          b. Ensure access to quality, comprehensive, and appropriate intervention,
       therapeutic and educational services.​

          c. Alleviate the financial and emotional burdens on families of children
       with special needs through various mechanisms.​

          d. Foster a society that embraces inclusivity, free from stigma and
       discrimination against children with special needs and their families.​

          e. Strengthen inter-agency collaboration and establish a comprehensive
       support system for children with special needs and their families.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:




                                            1
         a. Children with Special Needs (CSN) refers to individuals below
      eighteen (18) years of age who have developmental delays, disabilities, or
      conditions that require specialized interventions, including but not limited to,
      Autism Spectrum Disorder, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Down
      Syndrome, Cerebral Palsy, intellectual disability, specific learning disabilities,
      sensory impairments (visual or hearing), and speech and language disorders.
      This also includes those who have long-term physical, mental, intellectual or
      sensory impairments which in interaction with various barriers may hinder
      their full and effective participation in society on an equal basis.​

          b. Early Intervention Services refers to a range of services provided to
      infants and toddlers with developmental delays or disabilities and their
      families to promote the child’s development and minimize the effects of the
      disability.​

         c. Therapeutic Services refers to professional interventions such as, but
      not limited to, occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech and language
      therapy, behavioral therapy, and other related services designed to improve
      the functional abilities and well-being of CSN.​

          d. Special Education (SPED) refers to the educational program designed
      to meet the unique needs of CSN, including SPED centers, inclusive
      classrooms, and home-based programs.​

         e. Underserved Areas refers to geographical regions, localities, or
      communities where access to specialized services for CSN is limited or
      non-existent.​

         f. Assistive Tools and Medical Care refers to devices, equipment,
      technologies, medications, and healthcare services that assist CSN in their
      daily living, communication, mobility, and overall health management.​

      SEC. 4. Accessible Therapy and Education. – The State shall ensure that all CSN
have equitable access to quality therapy and education. To this end:​

         a. The Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the
      Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and the Department
      of Health (DOH), shall establish and expand SPED schools and centers in
      underserved areas, ensuring adequate facilities and qualified SPED teachers
      and therapists.​

         b. The DOH and DSWD, in coordination with the Philippine Health
      Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), shall develop and implement a subsidy
      program for therapeutic services for CSN, covering a significant portion of
      therapy and rehabilitation costs. The subsidy program shall prioritize
      indigent and low-income families.​




                                           2
         c.​ The DepEd shall strengthen its education program, providing
      necessary and inclusive training for teachers, learning resources, and
      education plans to facilitate the successful integration of CSN into
      mainstream classrooms wherever appropriate.
​
       SEC. 5. Financial Relief for Families. – To mitigate the financial burden on
families of CSN, the following measures shall be undertaken:

         a.​ The DSWD, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall establish a
      system of vouchers and grants for eligible families of CSN to support the
      costs of therapy, medical care, assistive tools, and specialized education.
      Priority shall be given to families categorized as indigent or vulnerable.​

          b.​ The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) shall explore and recommend to
      Congress appropriate tax incentives for parents or legal guardians of CSN,
      including deductions for medical expenses, therapy costs, and specialized
      educational fees.​

         c.​ PhilHealth shall expand its coverage to include a range of therapeutic
      services, diagnostic procedures, and assistive devices for CSN, with higher
      benefit limits and streamlined claims processes.

       SEC. 6. Public Awareness and Acceptance Campaigns. – The Department of
Education (DepEd), the Department of Social Welfare Development (DSWD), and
the Philippine Information Agency (PIA) shall develop and implement national
public awareness and acceptance campaigns on various special needs, which shall
promote understanding, empathy, and early identification. The DepEd shall
integrate modules on special needs awareness, inclusion, and disability rights into
relevant educational curricula at all levels.

       SEC. 7. Support Systems for Families and Caregivers. – The DSWD, in
coordination with the Local Government Units (LGUs), shall endeavor to establish
or support existing community centers in their jurisdiction. The support may
include any of the following: i) free or subsidized therapy services, ii) parental
training, and/or, iii) emotional support group for families of CSN.

       The DSWD and DOH shall, in coordination and cooperation with the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), develop and
implement comprehensive training programs for parents and caregivers of CSN.
These training programs shall focus on equipping them with the knowledge and
skills necessary to support their child’s development and manage challenging
behaviors.​

       SEC. 8. Stronger Policies for Inclusion. – The DepEd shall formulate and
implement guidelines for inclusive education, mandating reasonable
accommodations, and non-discriminatory practices for CSN in both public and
private schools. All government agencies and private establishments shall ensure




                                         3
that public spaces, facilities, and services are accessible to CSN, in compliance with
existing accessibility laws and regulations.

       SEC. 9. Inter-Agency Coordinating Council. – There is hereby created an
Inter-Agency Coordinating Council for Children with Special Needs (IACCSN),
hereinafter referred to as the “Council”, to ensure the effective implementation of
this Act. The Council shall be composed of representatives from the DSWD, DOH,
DepEd, National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), PhilHealth, and at least two
representatives from the private sector, which directly implement programs relevant
and related to the needs of the CSNs. The Council shall:

         a. Formulate and oversee the implementation of a comprehensive
      national plan for the welfare of CSN;​

         b. Coordinate the programs and activities of various government
      agencies and non-government organizations pertaining to CSN;​

         c. Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of policies and programs
      addressing the needs of CSN; and​

         d. Recommend legislative and policy reforms to further enhance the
      rights and well-being of CSN.

      SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the DOH, DepEd, NCDA,
PhilHealth, and in consultation with other relevant agencies and stakeholders, shall
formulate and promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.
      SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

     SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

    SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
