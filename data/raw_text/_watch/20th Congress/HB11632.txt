                              EXPLANATORY NOTE



The psychological well-being and mental health of our youth are foundational to
their overall development and academic success. In recent years, educational
institutions have witnessed an alarming surge in mental health issues among
students, including anxiety, depression, and self-harm. These challenges have
been exacerbated by the long-term impacts of the pandemic, cyberbullying, and
academic pressure.

While the State has enacted milestone legislations such as Republic Act No.
11036 (The Mental Health Act) and Republic Act No. 11206 (Secondary School
Career Guidance and Counseling Act), the effective delivery of mental health
services in schools remains severely crippled by a critical shortage of licensed
professionals. Currently, the public school system suffers from a disproportionate
counselor-to-student ratio, sometimes reaching one counselor for every tens of
thousands of students.

A primary driver of this shortage is the uncompetitive compensation framework.
Under the current system, highly qualified professionals with master’s degrees
and board licenses are often offered entry-level positions at Salary Grade 11.
Consequently, many licensed guidance counselors and psychologists opt for
private practice or corporate roles, leaving our schools understaffed and our
students vulnerable.

This proposed measure seeks to address this crisis by mandating the Department
of Education (DepEd) to hire and deploy school-based licensed guidance
counselors, psychologists, and allied mental health personnel (such as
psychometricians and clinical social workers) in all public basic education
institutions. Furthermore, to attract and retain these vital professionals, this bill
proposes the upgrading of their entry-level salary grades to match the rigorous
educational and licensure requirements of their professions.

By institutionalizing a standard student-to-mental-health-personnel ratio and
providing competitive remuneration, we can ensure that every school becomes a
safe, nurturing, and psychologically supportive environment for the Filipino
youth.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT MANDATING THE HIRING OF SCHOOL-BASED LICENSED
   GUIDANCE COUNSELORS, PSYCHOLOGISTS, AND OTHER MENTAL
 HEALTH PERSONNEL IN ALL PUBLIC BASIC EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS,
     UPGRADING THEIR SALARY GRADES, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
             THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "School Mental Health
Personnel and Compensation Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
The State recognizes the vital role of children and the youth in nation-building
and shall foster their holistic well-being. Towards this end, the State shall ensure
the provision of comprehensive, accessible, and responsive mental health services
in all basic education institutions by deploying an adequate number of qualified
and licensed mental health professionals and providing them with just and
competitive compensation.

SEC. 3. Mandated Hiring and Deployment. — The Department of Education
(DepEd), in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
is hereby mandated to create new plantilla positions and hire licensed guidance
counselors, licensed psychologists, and allied mental health personnel (including
registered psychometricians and mental health social workers) to be deployed in
all public elementary and secondary schools nationwide.
SEC. 4. Standard Personnel-to-Learner Ratio. — To ensure the effective delivery
of guidance, counseling, and mental health interventions, the DepEd shall
observe a standard ratio of one (1) licensed mental health professional for every
five hundred (500) learners.

In schools with a population of less than five hundred (500) learners, the DepEd
Schools Division Office shall cluster small schools to be supervised by a
designated roving licensed guidance counselor or psychologist, supported by
school-based psychometricians.

SEC. 5. Upgrading of Salary Grades. — To attract and retain qualified
professionals, the entry-level Salary Grades (SG) of mental health personnel in
the public basic education system shall be modified and upgraded as follows:

   1. Registered Psychometrician / Mental Health Associate: SG 15

   2. Registered Guidance Counselor I: SG 16

   3. Registered Psychologist I: SG 18

Personnel currently holding these positions shall have their salary grades
adjusted accordingly. The DBM and the Civil Service Commission (CSC) shall
modify the qualification standards and position classification to reflect these
upgrades.

SEC. 6. Role and Functions of School Mental Health Personnel. — The hired
personnel shall be responsible for:

   1. Implementing school-wide mental health promotion and literacy programs;
   2. Conducting psychological assessments, psychological first aid, and crisis
      interventions;
   3. Providing individual and group counseling for learners at risk;
   4. Identifying and referring severe psychiatric or psychological cases to
      external health facilities and specialized professionals; and
   5. Collaborating with school administrators, teachers, and parents to build a
      psychologically safe learning environment free from bullying and
      discrimination.

SEC. 7. Protection and Benefits. — All mental health personnel hired under this
Act shall be classified as public health workers and shall be entitled to the rights,
privileges, and benefits provided under Republic Act No. 7305, otherwise known
as the "Magna Carta of Public Health Workers," and Republic Act No. 11036,
otherwise known as the "Mental Health Act."

SEC. 8. Continuing Professional Development (CPD). — The DepEd shall allocate
a dedicated budget for the continuous capacity building, training, and
professional development of all school-based mental health personnel to ensure
their practices align with the latest scientific and ethical standards in psychology
and counseling.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the
DepEd. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the Annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Education, in consultation with
the DBM, CSC, Department of Health (DOH), Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC), and recognized national organizations of guidance counselors
and psychologists, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

SEC.11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved
