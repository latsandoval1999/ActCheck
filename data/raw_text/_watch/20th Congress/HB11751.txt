EXPLANATORY NOTE

Illegal drug syndicates that operate across borders are no longer mere criminal
enterprises. They finance violence, corrupt public institutions, and undermine national
security. This bill treats these transnational organizations for what they are: threats to the
security of the State.

Recent law enforcement operations involving massive cross-border drug shipments
originating from international hubs and destined for the Philippines demonstrate the evolving
sophistication of transnational narco-trafficking. Investigations into these operations
frequently reveal complex networks involving local logistics, air transport, and foreign
supply chains.

When illicit transactions are intercepted prior to reaching Philippine territory, legal
action within domestic jurisdiction is often limited to conspiracy charges. To effectively
combat these cartels and enable seamless international cooperation, there is a compelling
need to classify major transnational drug syndicates as terrorist organizations.

Under existing anti-terrorism statutes, designation and enforcement measures are
strictly tied to acts motivated by political, religious, or ideological objectives. Because
organized drug cartels operate primarily for financial gain and profit, the Anti-Terrorism
Council lacks the statutory authority under current law to designate them as terrorist entities.
Without an explicit legislative mandate, executive agencies cannot apply critical enforcement
tools, such as asset freezing, court-authorized surveillance, and extraterritorial jurisdiction,
against commercial narco-trafficking networks.

Existing drug laws also focus primarily on acts committed within domestic borders,
leaving financing, procurement, and logistics organized abroad difficult to prosecute prior to
the physical arrival of illicit cargo. Classifying transnational drug trafficking organizations as
terrorist entities bridges these legal gaps, grants the Council the necessary statutory authority
regardless of motive, and aligns domestic requests for intelligence sharing, asset freezing, and
extradition with international anti-terrorism standards.

===== [page 2] =====
This bill formally defines a transnational drug trafficking organization (TDTO),
authorizes the Anti-Terrorism Council to designate such entities upon a finding of probable
cause, and requires judicial proscription by the Court of Appeals. It establishes the offense of
narco-terrorism, penalizes material support, financing, and membership, and grants domestic
courts jurisdiction over acts committed abroad when illicit drugs are intended for local
distribution. Furthermore, public officers or law enforcement personnel found aiding or
abetting these organizations shall suffer maximum statutory penalties and perpetual
disqualification from public service. .

To ensure constitutional integrity, the bill incorporates essential safeguards.
Designation rests solely on an independent finding of probable cause by the Council, formal
delisting procedures are made available, and mandatory notification to human rights
authorities is required upon any arrest.

Transnational drug cartels operate without regard to national borders. Our legal
framework must be equipped with the necessary extraterritorial reach and statutory tools to

suppress them.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

MARK ANTHONY G. SA S

*House Bill No. L175 1

Introduced by HON. MARK ANTHONY G. SANTOS

AN ACT DECLARING TRANSNATIONAL DRUG TRAFFICKING
ORGANIZATIONS AS TERRORIST ORGANIZATIONS, DEFINING AND
PENALIZING NARCO-TERRORISM, STRENGTHENING INTERNATIONAL LAW
ENFORCEMENT COOPERATION AGAINST ILLEGAL DRUG SYNDICATES,
AND AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11479, OTHERWISE
KNOWN AS "THE ANTI-TERRORISM ACT OF 2020," AND REPUBLIC ACT NO.
9165, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS "THE COMPREHENSIVE DANGEROUS DRUGS
ACT OF 2002," AS AMENDED

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Narco-Terrorism
Act of 2026."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy — It is the policy of the State, as enshrined in the
1987 Constitution, to protect life, liberty, and property from the proliferation of dangerous

drugs and to safeguard national security against organized groups that traffic dangerous drugs
across national borders.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms —
The following terms are defined in this Act:

(a) Transnational Drug Trafficking Organization (TDTO) — refers to any group of two
(2) or more persons, organized for the purpose of committing, or which commits, any
act punishable under Article II of Republic Act No. 9165, as amended, where:

(i) the act is committed in more than one State; or
(ii) the act is committed in one State but a substantial part of its preparation,
planning, financing, direction, or control takes place in another State; or
(iii) | the dangerous drugs involved are destined for, transported through, or sourced
from a State other than where the act is committed;

(b) Narco-Terrorism — refers to the offense defined in Section 4 of this Act;

(c) Large-scale quantity — refers to dangerous drugs of not less than one (1) kilogram of

===== [page 4] =====
methamphetamine hydrochloride (shabu), cocaine, or heroin, or not less than ten (10)
kilograms of marijuana, or their equivalent for other dangerous drugs as determined
by the Dangerous Drugs Board;

(d) Material Support — shall have the same meaning as defined under Section 3(e) of
Republic Act No. 11479, and shall expressly include lodging, training, false documentation
or identification, communications equipment, facilities, aircraft, vessels, vehicles, hangars,
landing or docking rights, transportation, and personnel provided to a TDTO;

(e) Council — refers to the Anti-Terrorism Council (ATC) created under Republic
Act No. 11479; and

(f) All other terms shall have the meanings given them under Republic Act No. 9165,
as amended, and Republic Act No. 11479.

SECTION 4. Narco-Terrorism — Any person who, as a leader, member, financier, or
agent of a transnational drug trafficking organization (TDTO), commits, attempts, or
conspires to commit the importation, sale, trading, transportation, distribution, manufacture,
or delivery of a large-scale quantity of dangerous drugs into, within, or from the Philippines
shall be guilty of narco-terrorism and shall suffer the penalty of life imprisonment without the
benefit of parole and a fine of not less than Ten million pesos (P10,000,000.00) to Fifty
million pesos (P50,000,000.00).

The offense is consummated upon the commission of any overt act in furtherance of
the conspiracy, whether in the Philippines or abroad, even if the dangerous drugs are
intercepted before reaching the Philippines.

SECTION 5. — Designation of TDTOs as Terrorist Organizations. — Pursuant to
Section 25 of Republic Act No. 11479, the Council, motu proprio or upon recommendation of
the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA), the Philippine National Police (PNP), the
National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), or the National Intelligence Coordinating Agency
(NICA), may designate a Transnational Drug Trafficking Organization (TDTO), its leaders,
members, or financiers, as terrorist organizations, associations, or individuals upon its own
independent finding of probable cause that they are organized for or engaged in
narco-terrorism.

Information from foreign governments, supranational bodies, the United Nations
Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), INTERPOL, and the ASEAN Narcotics Cooperation
Center may be evaluated by the Council. In compliance with constitutional safeguards, no
designation shall issue solely upon the request or listing of a foreign jurisdiction; the Council
shall make an independent determination of probable cause.

The publication, posting, and administrative delisting procedures governing
designations under Section 25 of Republic Act No. 11479 and its implementing rules shall
apply to designations issued under this Section.

SECTION 6. Judicial Proscription — Any group of persons, organization, or
association proven to be organized for the purpose of engaging in narco-terrorism, or which

===== [page 5] =====
uses the acts penalized under Section 4 of this Act to commit narco-terrorism, may be
judicially proscribed as a terrorist organization by the Court of Appeals upon a verified
application filed by the Department of Justice, with authority from the Council, pursuant to
Section 26 of Republic Act No. 11479.

The application, due process requirements, hearings, temporary orders of proscription,
judgment, and delisting procedures set forth in Sections 26, 27, and 28 of Republic Act No.
11479 and the applicable procedural rules promulgated by the Supreme Court shall strictly
govern judicial proscription proceedings under this Act.

SECTION 7. Financing of Narco-Terrorism — Any person who knowingly provides,
collects, or makes available funds or property, directly or indirectly, intending or knowing
that they will be used in whole or in part by a TDTO or for the commission of
narco-terrorism, shall suffer the penalty of life imprisonment without the benefit of parole
and a fine equal to three (3) times the value of the funds or property involved, but not less
than Ten million pesos (P10,000,000.00).

SECTION 8. Material Support — Any person who knowingly provides material
support to a designated or proscribed TDTO, or to any person in the commission of
narco-terrorism, shall suffer the penalty of reclusion perpetua to life imprisonment without
the benefit of parole.

SECTION 9. Membership and Recruitment — Any person who knowingly joins,
recruits for, or acts on behalf of a designated or proscribed TDTO shall suffer the penalty of
imprisonment of twelve (12) years to twenty (20) years.

SECTION 10. Liability of Public Officers and Law Enforcers —- The maximum
penalty prescribed in this Act shall be imposed, together with perpetual absolute
disqualification from public office, on any public officer, employee, or member of the
uniformed services who:

(a) Commits any offense under this Act;

(b) Grants, arranges, or facilitates landing rights, hangar or docking access, customs or
immigration clearance, or protection for the benefit of a TDTO; or

(c) Knowingly fails to report, or tips off, any activity of a TDTO.

Administrative liability shall be imposed independently of criminal liability. Any
elected official convicted under this Act shall be perpetually disqualified from running for
any elective office.

SECTION 11. Extraterritorial Application — Without prejudice to Section 49 of
Republic Act No. 11479, Philippine courts shall have jurisdiction over offenses under this
Act committed outside the Philippines when:

(a) the offender is a Filipino citizen or a permanent resident of the Philippines;

(b) the dangerous drugs are destined for or intended to transit the Philippines

(c) the act is committed on board a Philippine-registered aircraft or vessel; or

(d) the act is directed against, or the proceeds are deposited in or transferred through, a
Philippine entity or financial institution.

This Section shall apply irrespective of whether the act is punishable in the place
where it was committed.

===== [page 6] =====
SEC. 12. Freezing and Forfeiture of Assets. — Upon designation under Section 5 of
this Act, the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) shall immediately freeze, ex parte,
the funds and property of the designated TDTO and individuals pursuant to Section 36 of
Republic Act No. 11479 and Republic Act No. 10168. Forfeited assets shall accrue to a
Special Account in the General Fund to be used exclusively for drug law enforcement,
prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation programs, including those of local government units.

SEC. 13. International Cooperation. - The Department of Justice (DOJ), as the
Central Authority, and the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) shall pursue mutual legal
assistance, extradition, joint investigation, intelligence sharing, and asset recovery with
foreign States in narco-terrorism cases on the basis of treaty, reciprocity, or comity. Offenses
under this Act shall be deemed extraditable offenses and shall not be considered political
offenses for purposes of extradition. The PDEA shall serve as the lead agency for operational
coordination with foreign counterpart agencies.

SEC. 14. Surveillance and Investigation. — The investigative and surveillance tools
available under Republic Act No. 11479, including court-authorized surveillance and
examination of bank deposits, shall be fully available in the investigation of offenses under
this Act, subject to the same judicial authorization and procedural safeguards set forth under
said law.

SEC. 15. Protection of Witnesses and Informants. — Witnesses and informants in
narco-terrorism cases shall be admitted to the Witness Protection, Security and Benefit
Program under Republic Act No. 6981. The PDEA may grant rewards from forfeited assets
under Section 12 for information leading to the conviction of offenders.

SEC. 16. Safeguards. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to:

(a) Penalize persons who use dangerous drugs or who are dependents and who are not
members of a TDTO; such persons shall be governed solely by Republic Act No.
9165, as amended;

(b) Authorize designation based solely on ethnicity, religion, political affiliation, or place
of origin;

(c) Dispense with the rights of the accused under the Constitution, including the
presumption of innocence.

Any designated person or organization may file a request for delisting with the
Council under Section 25 of Republic Act No. 11479 and its implementing rules. The
Commission on Human Rights shall be notified in writing of every arrest made under this Act
within twenty-four (24) hours.

SEC. 17. Prosecution Under Other Laws. — Prosecution under this Act shall not bar
prosecution under Republic Act No. 9165, Republic Act No. 9160, or other applicable laws,
provided that no person shall be twice put in jeopardy for the same offense.

SEC. 18. Amendment to Republic Act No. 11479. — Section 3 of Republic Act No.
11479 is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph to read:

"(o) Narco-Terrorism refers to the offense defined in the Anti-Narco-Terrorism Act of
2026, which for purposes of this Act shall be treated as a terrorist act regardless of its
motive.”

===== [page 7] =====
SEC. 19. Amendment to Republic Act No. 9165. — Article Il of Republic Act No.
9165, as amended, is hereby amended by inserting a new Section 26-A to read:

"SEC. 26-A. Transnational Drug Trafficking Organizations. — Offenses under this
Article committed by or on behalf of a Transnational Drug Trafficking Organization shall be
prosecuted and penalized under the Anti-Narco-Terrorism Act of 2026."

SEC. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Council, in coordination with the Dangerous Drugs Board, PDEA,
AMLC, DOJ, DFA, DILG, PNP, NBI, and Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines, shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

SEC. 21. Congressional Oversight. - The House Committee on Dangerous Drugs and
the Senate Committee on Public Order and Dangerous Drugs shall jointly oversee the
implementation of this Act. The Council shall submit an annual report on all designations,
proscriptions, delistings, frozen assets, and prosecutions under this Act.

SEC. 22. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the agencies concerned.
Thereafter, such sums as may be needed shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 23. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 24. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 25. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
