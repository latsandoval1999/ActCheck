                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Sharks, rays, and chimaeras are among the most ecologically important
and vulnerable groups of marine animals. Collectively known as
chondrichthyans, these species generally grow slowly, mature late, and produce
relatively few young. These biological characteristics make their populations
particularly vulnerable to overfishing and slow to recover once depleted.1
Globally, the conservation situation has become increasingly serious: a major
assessment found that oceanic shark and ray populations declined by
approximately 71 percent between 1970 and 2018, while 77 percent of assessed
oceanic species qualified as threatened with extinction.2

      The Philippines, as an archipelagic State with extensive marine resources,
has a particular interest in ensuring the long-term conservation and sustainable
use of these species. Existing Philippine legislation already recognizes the need
to conserve aquatic resources and maintain ecological balance. The Philippine
Fisheries Code of 1998 declares a policy of rational and sustainable management
and conservation of fisheries and aquatic resources,3 while the Wildlife

to conserve wildlife and their habitats and regulate the collection and trade of
wildlife.4 However, sharks and their relatives present distinctive management
challenges because many species are migratory, are caught incidentally in
fisheries, and are traded in multiple forms, including whole animals, meat, fins,
cartilage, oils, and other derivatives.

      The proposed measure responds to these concerns by establishing a
regulatory system covering the catching, sale, purchase, possession,
transportation, importation, and exportation of sharks, rays, chimaeras, and
their derivatives. The bill expressly provides for science-based non-detriment
findings, improved bycatch reporting and release, identification of threatened
species, establishment of critical habitats and shark sanctuaries, catch ceilings
and closed seasons, and traceability throughout the supply chain.

       Importantly, the proposed Act does not rely solely on prohibitions. It
recognizes the need to balance conservation with the legitimate interests of
fisherfolk by directing government agencies to provide appropriate technology,
research, credit, marketing assistance, and supplementary livelihood
opportunities. It also seeks to strengthen enforcement through traceability,
labeling, inter-agency cooperation, information and education campaigns, and
meaningful penalties for destructive practices such as finning, illegal trade,
habitat disturbance, and the unauthorized possession or movement of sharks
and their derivatives.

       The proposed legislation therefore provides a focused policy framework for
conserving sharks, rays, and chimaeras while promoting sustainable fisheries,
protecting marine ecosystems, supporting fishing communities, and ensuring
that the utilization and trade of these resources remain consistent with their
biological capacity to recover. Its enactment would strengthen the Philippines'
existing fisheries and wildlife conservation framework and provide a more
coordinated, science-based approach to the protection of these increasingly
threatened marine species.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
    REGULATING THE CATCHING, SALE, PURCHASE, POSSESSION,
TRANSPORTATION, IMPORTATION, AND EXPORTATION OF ALL SHARKS,
  RAYS, AND CHIMAERAS AND ANY PART THEREOF IN THE COUNTRY

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Shark Conservation
Act of the Philippines”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to conserve, protect, and sustainably manage sharks, rays, and chimaeras,
including their parts and derivatives, as vital components of Philippine marine
biodiversity and aquatic ecosystems, consistent with the constitutional duty to
protect the people’s right to a balanced and healthful ecology. The State shall
apply the precautionary principle and science-based, ecosystem-based
management; prevent illegal, unreported, and unregulated catching, trade, and
exportation; protect threatened, migratory, and ecologically significant species
and their habitats; strengthen research, monitoring, enforcement, and
traceability; fulfill relevant international obligations; and ensure the meaningful
participation of fisherfolk and coastal communities while safeguarding legitimate
livelihoods.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are hereby defined:
1. "By-catch" means the incidental take of undesirable size or age classes of
   the target species, or the incidental take of other non-target species or
   protected, endangered, or threatened species.

2. "By-product or derivatives" means any part taken or substance extracted
   from sharks, in raw or in processed form. This includes, but are not limited
   to, preserved sharks (curios), cartilage, gill rakers, fins, oils, and teeth;

3. "Corridor" means any artificial or natural venue where wide-ranging
   animals can travel, plants can propagate, genetic interchange can occur,
   populations can move in response to environmental changes and natural
   disasters, and threatened species can be replenished from other areas.
   Marine corridors encompass mangroves, coral reefs, and seagrass beds;

4. "Critical Habitat" means a place or environment that contains physical or
   biological features essential to its conservation;

5. "Dedicated tourism interaction site" means well-defined areas for marine
   wildlife tourism, either managed as a sanctuary/reserve/protected area or
   not, focused on a particular species or several species, with specified
   operating times;

6. "Discarded by-catch" means non-target species that are discarded at sea;

7. "Disturbance” means any intentional and unintentional acts that shall
   disrupt the natural cycle and activities of marine wildlife for the short or
   long term without necessarily causing any physical injury to the same,
   such as but not limited to acts producing noise, pollution, degradation of
   habitat, blockage of movement, displacement, and the like;

8. "Endangered Rare and/or Threatened Shark Species" means species in
   danger of extinction as provided for in existing Philippine laws and/or the
   International Union for the Conservation of Nature Red List of Species;

9. "Finning" means the practice of removing and retaining shark fins;

10.      "Full utilization" means use or consumption of the entire animal;

11.     "Habitat" means place or environment where species or subspecies
   naturally occurs or has naturally established its population;

12.       "Harassment" means an intentional or negligent act or omission
   which creates the likelihood of injury by annoying it to such an extent as
   to significantly disrupt normal behavioral patterns which include, but are
   not limited to, breeding, feeding, or sheltering;
   13.      "Non-dedicated Tourism Interaction Site" means opportunistic,
      accidental or unintentional interaction with marine wildlife tourism and
      non-tourism sites;

   14.      "Non-detriment Finding" (NDF) means a science-based risk
      assessment where the vulnerability of a species is considered in relation
      to how well it is managed;

   15.      "Secretary" means the Secretary of the Department of Agriculture;

   16.     "Shark" means cartilaginous fishes belonging to the class
      Chondrichthyes, which includes true sharks, skates, rays and chimaeras;

   17.     "Shark sanctuary" means a designated area where sharks cannot be
      caught;

   18.       "Traceability" means the ability to trace and follow food or a
      substance intended to be or expected to be incorporated into a food or
      product through all stages of fishing, production, processing, and
      distribution;

   19.      "Trade" means the act of engaging in the exchange, exportation,
      importation, re-exportation, purchase, or sale of sharks, their derivatives,
      or by-products, locally or internationally;

   20.     "Utilized by-catch" means non-target species that are traded or
      consumed, in part or whole.

SECTION 4. Scope of Application. – The provisions of this Act shall be
enforceable for all Philippine waters including other waters over which the
Philippines has sovereignty and jurisdiction, and the country's 200-nautical-
mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) and continental shelf, including protected
areas under Republic Act No. 7586, otherwise known as the National Integrated
Areas System (NIPAS) Act; critical habitats; and all Philippine-flagged fishing
vessels operating in areas governed by the Regional Fisheries Management
Organization, in high seas, or in waters of other coastal states.

SECTION 5. Jurisdiction of Department of Agriculture. – The Department of
Agriculture (DA) shall have jurisdiction over sharks, rays, and chimaeras, and
all declared aquatic habitats. In the Province of Palawan, jurisdiction herein
conferred is vested in the Palawan Council for Sustainable Development
pursuant to Republic Act No. 7611.

The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources of the Department of Agriculture
(DA-BFAR) shall be the lead agency in the implementation of this Act. The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Environment and
Natural Resources (DENR), the Philippine Coast Guard, the Philippine National
Police (PNP), other law enforcement agencies, and Local Government Units
(LGUs) shall likewise assist in the implementation of this Act.

The Department of Tourism (DOT) shall provide the necessary assistance to the
DA-BFAR and the LGUs in identifying shark ecotourism sites and declare the
same as protected.

                       CHAPTER II
CONSERVATION AND PROTECTION OF SHARKS, RAYS, AND CHIMAERAS

SECTION 5. Information Systems. – All activities, subsequently manifested
under this Chapter, shall be authorized by the Secretary upon proper evaluation
of best available information or scientific data showing that the activity is, or for
a purpose, not detrimental to the survival of the species or subspecies involved
and/or their habitat. For this purpose, the Secretary shall regularly update
wildlife information through research.

Section 6. Sharks in Captivity. No person or entity shall be allowed possession
of sharks unless such person or entity can prove financial capability and facility
to maintain said species: Provided, That the source was not obtained in violation
of this Act, and the source of stock is verifiable to monitor mortality.

For this purpose, DA-BFAR will develop husbandry and holding tank standards
as well as permitting systems for the keeping of sharks in captivity.

Section 7. Non-Detriment Findings (NDF). Within three (3) years after the
effectivity of this Act, DA-BFAR shall determine the NDF for targeted and
incidentally caught shark species.

Within the same period, DA-BFAR shall also provide technical and financial
assistance on research for gear restriction, and identify modified fishing gears to
reduce shark bycatch mortality, which may include bait restrictions, use of
different fishing hooks, varying hook depth, use of repellents, reducing soak
time, and reducing the number of hooks.

Section 8. Collection and/or Possession of By-Products and Derivatives. The
collection and possession of species shall only be allowed when the results of the
NDF show that despite a certain extent of collection, the population of such
species can still remain viable and capable of recovering its numbers

For this purpose, DA-BFAR shall establish a schedule and volume of allowable
harvests.
Section 9. Release and Reporting of Bycatch. Live sharks that have not been
assessed through the NDF shall be immediately released unharmed to the sea.
Dead sharks shall be handed over to the DA-BFAR for proper documentation
and disposal.

For this purpose, DA-BFAR shall develop a standard reporting system for sharks
that are bycatch. Any incident must be reported to the competent authority using
the standard reporting system.

Section 8. Guidelines on proper interaction. Within one (1) year after the
effectivity of this Act, the Department shall develop guidelines on:

   1. human-shark interaction guidelines, as regards sharks in captivity;
   2. human-shark interaction in the wild, in relation to tourism activities;
   3. research and researcher interaction guidelines for studies focusing on
      sharks.
   4. acceptable handling and release guidelines from fisheries of all types of
      protected and/or unwanted sharks based on the best available science
      and safe release experience.

SECTION 9. Determination of Threatened Species: Vulnerable,
Endangered, or Critically Endangered Species – Within two (2) years after
the effectivity of this Act, DA-BFAR shall determine whether shark species or
subspecies of a population or subpopulation is threatened (vulnerable,
endangered, or critically endangered) based on available scientific data and with
due regard to internationally accepted criteria, such as the International Union
for Conservation of Nature (IUCN). Criteria include but are not limited to the
following:

   1. Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of its
      habitat or range;
   2. Over-utilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational
      purposes;
   3. Inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms; and
   4. Other natural or man-made factors affecting the existence of sharks.

The DA-BFAR shall also prepare and publish a list of sharks which closely
resemble threatened wildlife, which shall be presumed to be threatened as well.

SECTION 10. Mandatory Review of Species List – Within two (2) years after
effectivity of this Act, DA-BFAR shall review, revise, and publish the list of
categorized threatened sharks. Thereafter, the list shall be updated regularly or
as the need arises. Provided, that a species listed as vulnerable threatened shall
not be removed therefrom within three (3) years following its initial listing.

Upon filing of a petition based on substantial scientific information of any person
seeking the addition or deletion of a species from the list, the DA-BFAR shall
evaluate in accordance with the relevant factors stated in the first paragraph of
this section, the status of the species concerned, and act on said petition within
a reasonable period.

SECTION 11. Identification of Critical Habitats, Reference Points, Limits,
and Seasons. Within two (2) years after the effectivity of this Act, DA-BFAR
shall:

   1. identify critical habitats for sharks and establish these as shark
      sanctuaries;
   2. determine Target Reference Points (TRP) and Limit Reference Points (LRP)
      based on the results of national stock assessment and the precautionary
      principle;

SECTION 12. Catch Ceiling Limitations. The DA-BFAR may prescribe
limitations or quotas on the total quantity of sharks captured, for a specified
period of time and specified area based on the best available evidence, harvest
strategies, and target limits.

Catch ceilings may be established upon the concurrence and approval or
recommendation of such special agency and the concerned LGU in consultation
with the Fisheries Aquatic and Resource Management Council (FARMC) for
conservation or ecological purposes.

SECTION 13. Establishment of Closed Season. The Secretary may declare,
through public notice in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation or in
public service announcements, whichever is applicable, at least five (5) days
before the declaration, a closed season in any or all Philippine waters outside the
boundary of municipal waters and in bays, for conservation and ecological
purposes.

Provided, however, that this shall be done only upon the concurrence and
approval or recommendation of such special agency and the concerned LGU and
FARMC. Provided, further, that in such municipal waters, fishery management
areas and other areas reserved for the use of the municipal fisherfolk, closed
season may be established by the concerned LGU in consultation with the
FARMC for conservation or ecological purposes.
The FARMCS may also recommend the establishment of closed seasons in
municipal waters, fisheries management areas, and other areas reserved for the
use of municipal fisherfolk.

SECTION 14. Support to Fisherfolk. - The Department and LGUS shall provide
support to commercial and municipal fisherfolk through appropriate technology
and research, credit, production, and marketing assistance and other services
such as, but not limited to, training for additional/supplementary livelihood.

SECTION 15. System, Labeling, and Identification. All business operators
identified in the supply chain of shark trading and utilization shall cooperate
with the DA-BFAR in carrying out official controls, and shall have responsibilities
including but not limited to the following:

   1. Implementation of traceability system. Business operators must operate
      traceability procedures for ingredients and raw materials at all stages of
      production, processing, transport, storage and distribution, and be able
      to:
          a. identify any person or business operator that has supplied them
             with the species;
          b. identify any person or business to which they have supplied their
             products;
          c. the products that were supplied, including quantity or volume, site
             of fishing grounds, species, and fishing gear used; and
          d. make this information available to DA-BFAR and their consumers;

   2. Labeling or identification. Business operators must ensure that their
      products are adequately labeled or otherwise identified through relevant
      documentation. They must also comply with the labeling requirements of
      the country destination.

                              CHAPTER II
                 ILLEGAL ACTS AND PENALTIES THEREFOR

SECTION 16. Illegal Acts. - Unless otherwise allowed in accordance with this
Act, it shall be unlawful for any person to willfully and knowingly exploit sharks
and their habitats, or undertake the following acts:

   1. Killing, taking, keeping captive, and trading vulnerable, threatened,
      endangered, or critically endangered species, except in the following
      instances:
         a. when it is done as part of the rituals of established tribal groups or
             indigenous cultural communities;
         b. when the shark is afflicted with an incurable communicable disease;
         c. when it is deemed necessary to put an end to the misery suffered by
             the shark;
         d. when the shark is killed or destroyed after it has been authorized for
             research or experiments.
  2. Inflicting injury which cripples and/or impairs the reproductive system of
     sharks;
  3. Harassing sharks and disturbing their habitats;
  4. Effecting any of the following acts in critical habitat(s):
         a. Dumping of waste products detrimental to sharks;
         b. Squatting or otherwise occupying any portion of the critical habitat;
             and
         c. Mineral exploration and/or extraction.
  5. Introducing, reintroducing, or restocking of shark species without permits;
  6. Finning;
  7. Collecting, hunting, possessing, importing, exporting, or re-exporting
     sharks, their by-products and derivatives without the result of the NDF
     and full traceability at all stages, from collection to production, processing,
     transport, storage, and distribution;
  8. Falsely advertising or mislabeling any product, which could mislead
     consumers;
  9. Obstructing corridors; and
  10.        Using shark wires.

SECTION 17. Penalties. The following fines and penalties shall be imposed for
violations of this Act:

  1. Killing, taking, keeping in captivity, inflicting injury, harassing, and trading
     vulnerable, threatened, or critically endangered species:

        a. Imprisonment of two (2) years and one (1) to four (4) years and/or a
           fine of Thirty Thousand Pesos (P 30,000.00) to Three Hundred
           Thousand Pesos (P300,000.00) per animal if inflicted or undertaken
           against vulnerable species;
        b. Imprisonment of one (1) year and one (1) day to two (2) years and/or
           a fine of Twenty Thousand Pesos (P20,000.00) to Two Hundred
           Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00) per animal if inflicted or undertaken
           against other threatened species;
      c. Imprisonment of four (4) years and one (1) day to six (6) years and/or
         a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) to Five hundred
         thousand pesos (P500,000.00) per animal if inflicted or undertaken
         against endangered species; and
      d. Imprisonment of a minimum of six (6) years and one (1) day to twelve
         (!2) years and/or a fine of One hundred thousand pesos
         (P100,000.00) to One million pesos (P 1,000,000.00) per animal if
         inflicted or undertaken against species listed as critically
         endangered.

2. Inflicting injury which cripples and/or impairs the reproductive system of
   sharks: Imprisonment of one (1) month to six (6) months and/or a fine of
   Five thousand pesos (P5,000.00) to Twenty thousand pesos (P20,000.00),
   if inflicted or undertaken against other wildlife species.

3. Harassing sharks and disturbing their habitats: Imprisonment of one (1)
   month to six (6) months and/or a fine of Five thousand pesos (P 5,000.00)
   to Twenty thousand pesos (P20,000.00), if inflicted or undertaken against
   other wildlife species.

4. Effecting any of the acts in critical habitat(s) mentioned in par. 4 of the
   immediately preceding section: Imprisonment of one (1) month to eight (8)
   years and/or a fine of Five thousand pesos (P5,000.00) to Five million
   pesos (P5,000,000.00);

5. Introducing, reintroducing, or restocking of shark species without permits:
   Imprisonment of one (1) month to eight (8) years and/or a fine of Five
   thousand pesos (P5,000.00) to Five million pesos (P5,000,000.00);

6. Finning: Upon a summary finding of administrative liability, the owner,
   operator, boat captain, and master fisherman of the vessel, or the chief
   executive officer in a corporation, or the managing partner in a partnership
   shall be punished with confiscation of the catch and fishing gear, and a
   fine three (3) times the value of the catch or the value indicated below,
   whichever is higher:

      a. Twenty Thousand Pesos (P20,000.00) for municipal fishing:
         Provided, That if the offender fails to pay the fine, he shall render
         community service;
      b. Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) for small-scale commercial
         fishing;
      c. One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) for medium-scale
         commercial fishing; and
      d. Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00) for large-scale
         commercial fishing. Upon conviction by a court of law, the offender
         shall be punished with imprisonment of two (2) years to six (6) years
         and a fine equivalent to twice the administrative fine.

7. Collecting, hunting, possessing, importing, exporting, or re-exporting sharks,
   their by-products and derivatives without the result of the NDF and full
   traceability at all stages, from collection to production, processing, transport,
   storage, and distribution: Failure on the part of the shipping or forwarding
   company from whose possession the fish or fishery species imported or
   exported are discovered or seized to fully cooperate in the investigation
   conducted by concerned government authorities shall create a
   presumption that there is connivance or conspiracy between the shipping
   company and the shipper to perpetrate the aforementioned offense.

   Upon a summary finding of administrative liability, the offender shall be
   punished with an administrative fine of five (5) times the value of the

   Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), whichever is higher, and forfeiture and/or
   destruction of the species.

   Upon conviction by a court of law, the offender shall be punished with
   eight (8) years of imprisonment and a fine of twice the administrative fine,

8. Falsely advertising or mislabeling any product, which could mislead

   Upon a summary finding of administrative liability, the offender shall be

      thousand pesos (P500,000.00), whichever is higher, and forfeiture and/or

      Upon conviction by a court of law, the offender shall be punished with

   10.        Using shark wires: Upon a summary finding of administrative

            and forfeiture of fishing gear and catch

SECTION 18. Escalation Clause. - The fines herein prescribed shall be
increased by at least ten percent (10%) every three (3) years to compensate for
inflation and to maintain the deterrent function of such fines.
                               CHAPTER III
                        MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

SECTION 19. Flagship Services. Local government units shall initiate
conservation measures for endemic and/or economically valuable species in
their areas. For this purpose, they may adopt flagship species such as the whale
shark (Rhincodon typus) or thresher shark (Alopias pelagicus), which shall serve
as emblems of conservation for the local government concerned.

SECTION 20. Information and Education. - The BFAR, DENR, DOT, DTI,
LGUs, and other academic institutions shall undertake a nationwide information
and education campaign to adequately inform the populace of the value of sharks
and rays in the marine ecosystem, and of the importance of preserving their
respective habitats and feeding grounds.

SECTION 21. Transition Period. Consistent with the provisions of this Act, a
transition period of three (3) years from its entry into force is hereby provided,
during which sharks, by-products, and derivatives may be sold and traded in
accordance with existing laws. Stocks that have been produced before the end of
the transitional period may continue to be sold or exported until exhausted.

SECTION 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within twelve (12)
months after the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Department of
Agriculture, in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
Department of Tourism, and the Committees on Environment and Ecology of the
Senate and the House of Representatives, respectively, shall promulgate rules
and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act. Whenever
appropriate, coordination in the preparation and implementation of rules and
regulations on joint and inseparable issues shall be done by the Departments.
The commitments of the State to international agreements and protocols shall
likewise be a consideration in the implementation of this Act.

SECTION 23. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part hereof is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain valid and subsisting.

SECTION 24. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed,
modified or amended accordingly.
SECTION 25. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in any two (2) newspapers of general
circulation in the Philippines.
