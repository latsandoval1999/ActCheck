                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “Carry each other's burdens, and in this way, you will fulfill the law of Christ.”
     — Galatians 6:2

      The Constitution mandates the State to promote social justice, protect the rights
and welfare of persons with disabilities (PWDs), and ensure their full participation in
society. As a State Party to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons
with Disabilities (UNCRPD), the Philippines has likewise committed to adopting
measures that guarantee equal access to healthcare, education, employment,
rehabilitation, assistive technologies, community support services, and independent
living for persons with disabilities. These commitments recognize that disability often
entails additional costs and barriers that require targeted government intervention
beyond general social welfare programs.

       Despite existing laws such as the Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities and
various programs providing discounts, privileges, and social assistance, many
Filipinos with disabilities continue to face significant disability-related expenses,
including costs for medicines, rehabilitation, mobility aids, assistive devices,
transportation, personal assistance, and specialized educational and employment
support. These additional financial burdens frequently result in deeper poverty,
reduced economic participation, and diminished opportunities for independent living,
particularly among indigent persons with disabilities.

      This bill seeks to establish a Disability Support Fund that will provide sustained
financial assistance and strengthen disability-specific services for indigent persons
with disabilities. The measure envisions a comprehensive support system that
complements existing national and local government programs by funding disability
support services, enhancing the capacity of Persons with Disability Affairs Offices
(PDAOs), supporting organizations of persons with disabilities, and providing a regular
monthly allowance to qualified beneficiaries to help address disability-related extra
costs. It likewise institutionalizes a disability and needs assessment system to ensure
that assistance is provided using a rights-based and functional approach consistent
with international standards.

                                             1
      The proposed measure further strengthens inter-agency coordination through
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), establishes an
integrated disability data management system linked with existing government
databases, and adopts a phased implementation strategy prioritizing children with
disabilities, persons facing the highest support needs, and those already enrolled in
existing social protection programs. By recognizing disability-related costs as a distinct
dimension of poverty, the bill seeks to promote greater independence, improve access
to essential services, facilitate employment and community participation, and reduce
long-standing barriers to inclusion.

      Through the establishment of a dedicated Disability Support Fund, the State
affirms its commitment to inclusive development by ensuring that persons with
disabilities receive not only legal recognition of their rights but also the practical
support necessary to exercise those rights fully and equally. Investing in disability
inclusion ultimately strengthens communities, expands economic participation, and
promotes a more equitable and compassionate society for all Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING THE DISABILITY SUPPORT FUND FOR THE EMPOWERMENT,
     FULL PARTICIPATION, AND INCLUSION OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES IN
     SOCIETY AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1       SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Disability Support
 2   Fund Act."
 3
 4         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The Philippines, as a State Party to the United
 5   Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), recognizes
 6   all the rights of persons with disabilities and shall take effective and appropriate
 7   measures to respect, protect, and fulfill the full enjoyment by persons with disabilities
 8   of these rights such as, but not limited to, access to habilitation and rehabilitation
 9   services, participation in socio-economic activities, access to a range of in-home,
10   residential, and other community support services, including personal assistance,
11   learning and assistive devices, and technology necessary to support living
12   independently and inclusion in the community, and to prevent isolation or segregation
13   from the community.
14
15         It is the declared policy of the State to adopt the generally accepted principles of
16   international law as part of the law of the land. It is likewise vital that the State advance
17   social justice in order to end poverty in all its forms, particularly the historical
18   deprivation of the support required by persons with disabilities for their effective
19   inclusion and full participation in society.
20
21         To attain a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and
22   independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies that
23   provide adequate social services, promote full employment, raises the standard of
24   living, and improve the quality of life, appropriate measures in developing social
25   protection programs and allowances that recognize the additional costs associated
26   with disability shall be undertaken.

13         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms are
14   defined, as follows:
15
16      (a) Disability is an umbrella term for impairments, activity limitations, and
17          participation restrictions, referring to the negative aspects of interaction
18          between an individual with health condition and contextual factors;
19      (b) Barriers are anything that hinders persons with disabilities from enjoying or
20          exercising all human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political,
21          economic, social, cultural, civil, or any other field, on an equal basis with others.
22          Barriers in all its forms encompass inaccessible physical environments and
23          transportation; the unavailability of assistive devices and technologies;
24          inaccessible information both in form and content; nonadaptive means of
25          communication; gaps in service delivery such as unavailability of personal
26          assistance and sign language interpretation service; discrimination including
27          denial of reasonable accommodation; attitude, prejudice, and stigma in
28          society; programs, policies, and laws infringing on the rights of persons with
29          disabilities as recognized under the Convention on the Rights of Persons with
30          Disabilities (CRPD);
31      (c) Indigent as used in this Act, is a person with disability who has no visible
32          means of income or whose income is insufficient for maintenance medications,
33          treatments, and other disability-related extra costs, as identified based on
34          specific criteria to be formulated by the Department of Social Welfare and
35          Development (DSWD), the National Council of Disability Affairs (NCDA), and
36          representatives from national organizations of persons with disabilities, in
37          accordance with the guiding principles set forth in Section 2;
38      (d) Impairment is the description of body structures that are diminished,
39          weakened, or damaged. Impairments can be divided into primary and
40          secondary:
41             (i)     Primary impairments are motor behaviors that are directly related to
42                     changes in any system. Examples may include muscle tone, postural
43                     stability, motor coordination. Primary impairments are any
44                     impairments that are present at birth such as heart defects, endocrine
45                     diagnoses, visual impairments;
46             (ii)    Secondary impairments develop over time and frequently as a result
47                     of the primary impairments. Examples may include decreased range
48                     of motion, force production and endurance;
49      (e) Persons with Disability (PWD) include those who have long-term physical,
50          mental, intellectual, or sensory impairments which in interaction with various

34   (l) Disability-Related Extra Costs are the expenses subjected to a person with
35       disability based on disability-specific needs and barriers resulting from
36       inaccessibility of facilities and services. Refers to the direct (e.g., buying
37       assistive device, paying for personal assistance or sign language
38       interpretation service, extra health care like medicines, more expensive
39       housing or transportation service, etc.) and indirect (e.g., loss productivity
40       because of longer travel time such as experienced by those who need to travel
41       by wheelchair because they cannot use the cheaper public transport, family
42       members who stop working to provide care and support to persons with
43       disabilities, etc.) costs as a result of their disability that drives persons with
44       disabilities in situation of poverty, exposure to vulnerability of exclusion, or
45       restriction to participation;
46   (m) Children with Disabilities include those individuals under the age of eighteen
47       who have long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments
48       which in interaction with various barriers may hinder their full and effective
49       participation in society on an equal basis;

17           SEC. 4. Disability Support Fund. - The Disability Support Fund aims to
18   address the gaps in the program implementation currently undertaken at the national
19   and at the local level through programs and projects of local government units (LGUs)
20   and civil society organizations (CSOs). LUs and accredited CSOS Will also be
21   assisted through funding to establish responsive services supporting independent
22   living of persons with disabilities.
23
24          The Disability Support Fund shall provide individuals with disabilities the
25   opportunity to avail of disability related services and have access to government
26   assistance and services for all. The Disability Support Fund shall have the following
27   components, such as, but not limited to the following:
28
29      (a) LGUs establishing a comprehensive Disability Support Services through
30          provincial, city and municipal persons with Disabilities Affairs Office;
31      (b) Development of affordable and accessible Disability Support Services
32          managed by organizations of persons with disabilities; and
33      (c) Individual Support Allowance for qualified persons with disabilities, identified
34          by the National Disability Assessment and Determination System which shall
35          be developed by a Technical Working Group led by the DSWD, Department
36          of Health (DOH), Department of Education (DepEd), and the Department of
37          Labor and Employment (DOLE) with diverse representations from
38          Organizations of Persons with Disabilities.
39
40        This support shall cover expenses related to disability such as, but not limited to,
41   augmentation to transportation services, food and nutrition, disability support services
42   provided by human interventions, maintenance medicines, rehabilitation services,
43   educational needs, such as but not limited to assistive devices, technology and
44   support equipment, pre-employment and during employment services, and access to
45   government assistance and services for all.
46
47         The Disability Support Fund shall aim to provide a periodic and regular stipend
48   to indigent persons with disabilities based on the eligibility criteria to be formulated by
49   the DSWD, the NDA, and representatives from national organizations of persons with
50   disabilities. The initial amount of Two Thousand Pesos (P2,000.00) a month per

 1   individual beneficiary shall be set for the Disability Support Fund, which will be indexed
 2   to inflation and must account for the expenses or costs of services and tools related
 3   to disability to preserve its positive impact on the standards of living and participation
 4   of people with disabilities. This would contribute, in combination with existing social

10       The Program shall be implemented in two (2) phases starting with the existing
11   master list of LUs who already have the ID:
12
13        (a) Phase 1 - The initial phase of the Disability Support Fund Program, to be
14            implemented in the first three (3) years, shall prioritize provision of stipends
15            to:
16                (i) Children with disabilities;
17                (ii) Adults with moderate and severe disability, who are facing the highest
18                      barriers and disability-related costs; and
19                (iii) Persons with disabilities who are recipients of social protection and
20                      economic empowerment programs such as but not limited to the
21                      Social Pension for Indigent Senior Citizens;
22        (b) Phase 2- The second phase of the Program, to be implemented in the next
23            three (3) years, shall include all persons with disabilities certified as indigent
24            by the DSWD, based on the eligibility criteria to be formulated under this Act.

29   may be given in addition to the abovementioned Program to indigent persons with
30   disabilities.

32         SEC. 5. Coverage. - All indigent persons with disabilities, as certified by the
33   DSWD, based on a human rights approach, shall be covered by the provisions of this
34   Act. Priority shall initially be given to children with disabilities and adults with significant
35   disabilities who are facing the highest barriers and disability-related costs.
36
37        However, the aim is to eventually cover all registered persons with disabilities
38   who have either no income or have low levels of income as well as persons with
39   disabilities who face disability-related extra costs that deprive them of an adequate
40   standard of living.
41
42        SEC. 6. Eligibility. - In determining eligibility to avail of the Disability Support
43   Fund, the Disability and Needs Assessment System shall be considered, with a focus
44   on functional limitations, support requirements, and barriers to participation.
45
46        In recognition of the multiple forms of poverty and the extent of disability-related
47   costs required for basic participation, eligibility of persons with disabilities to avail of
48   the Disability Support Fund will be compatible, insofar as practicable, with eligibility
49   and benefits from different social protection and economic empowerment programs

 7         SEC. 7. Role of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).
 8   - The DSWD shall be the administrator of the Disability Support Fund. All applications
 9   for availing of the Fund shall be lodged with the DSWD. In partnership with the NCDA,
10   organizations of persons with disabilities, all concerned government agencies, local
11   government units thru the PDAO, and non-government organizations shall implement
12   programs and services targeting persons with disabilities.
13
14        All policies concerning programs and services for persons with disabilities shall
15   be reviewed and enhanced for clarity of operationalization at the local level by the
16   DSWD.
17
18         SEC. 8. Disability and Needs Assessment System. - To effectively implement
19   social protection measures targeting persons with disabilities, a Disability Data
20   Management System shall be created and managed by the DSWD, in coordination
21   with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and shall
22   be linked to the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) which aid in the
23   development of poverty-reduction programs from local to the national level. Linkage
24   to the National ID System, and the DOH Persons with Disability Registry, which is
25   mirrored with the PhilHealth Database System, shall also be done to provide real-time
26   disability data and subsidized enrolment to PhilHealth insurance.
27
28         SEC. 9. Policy on Disability Identification Cards. - The issuance of Disability
29   Identification Cards pursuant to Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the
30   "Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities," as amended, along with other relevant
31   laws, administrative issuances, and local ordinances, shall incorporate the information
32   necessary for the granting of the periodic and regular Disability Support Fund
33   allocation to eligible persons with disabilities.

35        SEC. 10. Penal Provisions. - The penalties shall apply to the following:
36
37        (a) Any PWD, or his/her parent or his/her relative of any degree of consanguinity
38            or affinity, or any third person found to be deliberately committing any
39            fraudulent act of applying or enrolling a PWD to the benefits of this law when
40            a person is not eligible shall suffer the following:
41            (i) For the first violation, a fine of not less than Twenty-five Thousand Pesos
42                 (P25,000) but not exceeding Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000);
43            (ii) For any subsequent violations, a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand
44                 Pesos (P50,000.00) but not exceeding One Hundred Thousand Pesos
45                 (P100,000.00); and
46        (b) Any person, natural or juridical, violating any provision of this Act and its
47            Implementing Rules and Regulations, except violations pertaining to
48            Subsection (a) of this Section, shall be penalized with a fine of not less than
49            Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not exceeding One hundred
50            Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00). If the violator is a corporation, organization
