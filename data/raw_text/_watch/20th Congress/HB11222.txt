                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       A barangay drugstore that runs out of medicine or funding is failing to serve
its community.

      This bill institutionalizes the Department of Health’s (DOH) Botika ng
Barangay (BNB) program, directing the establishment of barangay drugstores in
every barangay within a three-year period, with priority given to economically
depressed and underserved communities.

       More than two decades after it was first established under DOH
Administrative Order No. 70, the BNB program remains hampered by the very
problems the bill seeks to resolve. A government review found that while the
number of BNBs expanded rapidly, not all of them received sufficient financial and
management support to remain sustainable. This prompted the DOH to institute a
moratorium on further BNB expansion until problems in restocking and financing
could be addressed.1 Field research in Cebu Province similarly found that the top
complaints among BNB clients were the availability of stocks, inconsistent operating
hours, and limited variety of medicines sold, even as households that did have
reliable access to a BNB saved significantly on health-related expenses compared to
commercial drugstore prices.2

       Part of the problem is that the program’s funding and supply mechanisms
have relied on a patchwork of administrative issuances rather than a permanent
statutory framework. DOH Administrative Order No. 70 provided the original
licensing framework in 2002, DOH Administrative Order No. 64 in 2003 tapped
Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office funds and Philippine International Trading
Corporation procurement support to sustain BNB operations, and DOH

measures have kept the program alive, but each remains an executive issuance that
can be revised or discontinued at any time, leaving BNBs without the guaranteed,
continuous funding a program of this scale requires.

       This bill mandates the DOH to establish a standardized inventory monitoring
and quarterly replenishment system to prevent the stockouts that have long
undermined community confidence in the BNB program. It creates a revolving fund
from BNB medicine sales, directs local government units to draw on their share of
national taxes under the Mandanas-Garcia ruling and the Special Health Fund under
the Universal Health Care Act, and allows continued access to Philippine Charity
Sweepstakes Office assistance. It further sets minimum operating-hour standards
and provides for a Botika ng Barangay Aide honorarium, and adds an explicit
appropriations clause so that funding for the program is guaranteed year after year
rather than left to chance.

       A Botika ng Barangay that families cannot rely on to be open, stocked, or
staffed defeats the very purpose for which it was built. It must be a dependable
source of affordable medicine for the Filipino families who need it most.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE ESTABLISHMENT OF BARANGAY DRUGSTORES, TO
BE CALLED AS “BOTIKA NG BARANGAY”, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREOF

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Botika ng Barangay
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them. Towards this end, the State shall provide adequate social services and
endeavor to make essential goods, health, and other social services available to
every barangay at affordable cost, and ensure that such services remain
continuously available through sustainable funding, efficient management, and a
reliable supply of medicines.

       SEC. 3. Botika ng Barangay. – The Department of Health (DOH) shall, within
sixty (60) days after the approval of this Act, undertake a three (3)-year program for
the establishment of barangay drugstores, to be called “Botika ng Barangay” (BNB)
in every barangay in the country.

       SEC. 4. Establishment Priority. – The DOH shall, in the establishment of BNB,
undertake prior consultations with the Municipal/City Development Council
concerned: Provided, That in the establishment of the BNB, highest priority shall be
given to economically depressed barangays. Existing BNBs shall be maintained and
shall be covered by relevant provisions of this Act. It shall give priority to barangays
on the basis of population, poverty incidence, extent of medical health care and
services, geographic conditions, financial and other socio-economic factors, and such
other considerations as may be deemed necessary in undertaking the program
within the context of national plans and policies on social services, health, and
medical care.

​      SEC 5. Drugs Available. – The BNB shall make available only those medicines
included in the “core list” of the Philippine National Drug Formulary, and the names
thereof listed on their respective generic and trade names made available to the
public.

​       SEC 6. Botika ng Barangay Aide. – Sale of medicines at the BNBs shall be
facilitated by an aide, known as the “Botika ng Barangay Aide”, who shall be chosen
by and trained under the direction of the Municipality of City Health Office, in close
coordination with the Punong Barangay and head of the Barangay Health Center.
The BNB shall undergo mandatory training on proper drug dispensing, inventory
management, and basic health education prior to assumption of duty, and shall
undergo further refresher training at least once a year.

      Each BNB shall operate for a minimum of six (6) days a week, for at least six
(6) hours each operating day, or as may be adjusted by the concerned local
government unit (LGU), based on community needs and subject to DOH guidelines.
The BNB Aide shall be entitled to a reasonable honorarium or incentive, to be
sourced from the revolving fund established under Section 8 of this Act or from
LGU funds, as determined by the concerned LGU.

​      SEC. 7. Inventory Management and Replenishment System. – The DOH, in
coordination with concerned LGUs and accredited Pharmaceutical Distribution
Networks (PDNs), shall establish a standardized inventory monitoring and
replenishment system for all BNBs to ensure the timely and adequate resupply of
medicines. Each BNB shall submit periodic stock and sales reports to the Municipal
or City Health Office, which shall consolidate and submit the same to the DOH
regional office concerned. The DOH shall prescribe minimum replenishment
schedules, which shall in no case be less frequent than once every quarter, to prevent
prolonged stockouts.

​      SEC. 8. Funding and Sustainability. – To ensure the continuous and sustainable
operation of BNBs, the following funding mechanisms shall be observed:

         a.​ Proceeds from the sale of medicines at each BNB shall be treated as a
      revolving fund, to be used exclusively for the replenishment of medicine
      stocks, minor repairs, and other operating expenses of the BNB concerned,
      subject to accounting and auditing rules and regulations;​

         b.​ Pursuant to the Supreme Court’s ruling in Mandanas v. Ochoa (G.R.
      Nos. 199802 and 208488) on the just share of local government units in
      national taxes, LGUs are enjoined to allocate a portion of their National Tax
      Allotment to support the establishment, funding, and sustainability of their
      respective BNBs;​

         c.​ LGUs may likewise draw from the Special Health Fund established
      under Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care
      Act”, to support BNB operations, consistent with its implementing rules and

         d.​ LGUs may utilize a portion of their mandated budget allocation for
      senior citizens and persons with disabilities to support BNB operations that
      directly address the medical needs of these sectors; and​

         e.​ The DOH may continue to source financial assistance from the
      Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) and other government and
      private entities, consistent with existing laws, rules, and regulations, to
      augment funding for the establishment and sustainability of BNBs.

​      SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of Health, in
cooperation with the Secretary of the Interior and Local Government and the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA), shall issue the necessary rules and regulations as
may be needed to fully implement the provisions of this Act. In addition, the DOH
and the Department of the Interior and Local Government shall also embark on a
sustained promotional campaign to increase the community’s awareness of the
BNBs through its personnel, barangay officials, and the media.

       SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
