26                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
27
28        This Act shall be known as the "New Human Rights Victims Reparation and
29   Recognition Act of 2026. ″
30
31          Section 11 of Article II of the 1987 Constitution declares that the State values
32   the dignity of every human, person and guarantees full respect for human rights. Section
33   12 of Article III of the Constitution prohibits the use of torture, force, violence, threat,
34   intimidation, or any other means which vitiate the free will and mandates the
35   compensation and rehabilitation of victims of torture or similar practices and their
36   families.
37
38          The State recognizes and adopts generally accepted principles of international
39   law as part of the law of the land through Section 2 of Article II of the Constitution.
40   These includes Universal Declaration of Human Rights, including the International
41   Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) and the Convention Against Torture
42   (CAT) and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment.
43
44        Republic Act No. 10368 entitled “An Act Providing for Reparation and
45   Recognition of Victims of Human Rights Violations During the Marcos Regime,
46   Documentation of Said Violations, Appropriating Funds Therefor and for Other


                                                                                                           1
 1   Purposes,” passed in 2013, provides for the State recognition of the heroism and
 2   sacrifices of all Filipinos who were victims of summary execution, torture, enforced or
 3   involuntary disappearance and other gross human rights violations committed during
 4   the regime of former President Ferdinand Marcos covering the period from September
 5   21, 1972 to February 25, 1986 and restore the victims’ honor and dignity.
 6
 7           Through the law, the State also acknowledges its moral and legal obligation to
 8   recognize and/or provide reparation to the 9,539 class suit plaintiffs and the 24 direct
 9   action plaintiffs who were adjudged as such victims by the US Federal Court System in
10   Honolulu, Hawaii in 1992. Section 17 provides for conclusive presumption for the
11   claimants in the class suit and direct-action plaintiffs while not depriving the Board of
12   “its original jurisdiction and its inherent power to determine the extent of the human
13   rights violations and the corresponding reparation and/or recognition that may be
14   granted.”
15
16          Under RA 10368, more than 75,000 applicants filed their claims, but only 11,103
17   were approved and duly recognized by the Human Rights Victims Claims Board
18   (HRVCB) until the board became functus officio on May 12, 2018. The HRVCB was
19   able to completely resolve the 6,737 appeals as of May 6, 2018.
20
21           However, seven (7) years after RA 10368 was passed, and 27 years after the
22   landmark decision in Hawaii, relatives and friends of the human rights violations are
23   still crying out for justice.
24
25          Many of those who filed their claim were ordinary people, peasants, workers, or
26   urban poor activists whose rights were violated by the martial law government’s arrests
27   and crackdowns against dissent, mass actions by workers and peasants and zoning of
28   urban poor communities. Most of the victims are now very old and continue to be
29   impoverished. They were certainly overwhelmed by the legal procedures and
30   requirements of the law as well as the difficulty in the submission of simple affidavits
31   from witnesses and human rights organizations who could confirm their status as
32   detainees or human rights violations victims.
33
34          Admittedly, RA 10368 should have considered the state of the victims and the
35   difficulty they will encounter if the law is not liberalized to provide them with the best
36   opportunity to process their claims without compromising the integrity of the process.
37
38           Many of those who were disqualified or denied were disqualified on the basis of
39   technicality such as failure to promptly present evidence or testimony or submit duly
40   notarized affidavits, failure to reply or understand notices, rules and regulations,
41   deadlines of submission and other problems encountered by ordinary people not
42   familiar with legal procedures. Many of them never had a due process chance to even
43   file an appeal from the decision denying their claim outright.
44
45         More and more relatives of Martial Law human rights violations victims have
46   brought up their case on what they call as “unjust” denial of their claims for reparation.


                                                                                             2
 1   Included among the claims denied by HRVCB were those from Hawaii class suit
 2   plaintiffs. Based on the reports of human rights organization Samahan ng mga Ex-
 3   Detainees Laban sa Detensyon at Aresto (SELDA), the claims for Hawaii case plaintiffs
 4   Maurico Anugot, Cesaria Anugot, Marilyn Anugot, Eufecio Anugot, Welito Anugot,
 5   Teofilo Anugot of Balamban Massacre, Raul Deri of Sorsogon, and Reynaldo Garcia
 6   of Manila were denied despite the law’s provision on conclusive presumption. Also
 7   included among those denied was delisted Hawaii claimant Petra Venus Villegas of
 8   Quezon.
 9
10          Adding further insult to injury, there are increasing signs that the Marcoses are
11   slowly being absolved of their crimes against the people—from Ferdinand Marcos’
12   burial in Libingan ng mga Bayani, non-arrest of Imelda Marcos despite the Court’s
13   guilty verdict, to the Marcoses’ continuing power and and dominance and their
14   continuing lies, deception and attempt to revise history. With these, it seemed, justice
15   has and will remain elusive for the tens of thousands of Martial Law human rights
16   violations victims.
17
18          With the HRVCB officially functios officio, there is a growing clamor among
19   many martial law human rights violations victims and relatives and human rights groups
20   for a new law that will continue the recognition and reparation as mandated by RA
21   10368.
22
23          This Bill seeks to continue the processing of claims for recognition and
24   reparation by human rights violations victims during martial law and the reconstitution
25   of the Board to process their claims. The amount of ten billion pesos sourced from ill-
26   gotten wealth of the Marcoses that have been remitted and will be remitted by the PCGG
27   to the Bureau of Treasury.
28
29           While no amount of monetary reparation will restore lost lives, properties,
30   broken relations and dreams of the human rights violations’ victims and their families,
31   the reparation is important for them, as it forms part of the overall recognition of the
32   reality of human rights violations and their immeasurable suffering of the people under
33   the Marcos regime.
34
35          It is thus a matter of justice that the recognition and reparation for martial law
36   human rights violations victims and their families continue through a new law providing
37   for reparation and recognition of victims of human rights violations during the Marcos
38   regime.

26                                        CHAPTER I
27                                  PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS
28
29         Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "New Human Rights
30   Victims Reparation and Recognition Act of 2026.″
31
32          Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — Section 11 of Article II of the 1987
33   Constitution of the Republic of the Philippines declares that the State values the dignity
34   of every human, person and guarantees full respect for human rights. Pursuant to this
35   declared policy, Section 12 of Article III of the Constitution prohibits the use of torture,
36   force, violence, threat, intimidation, or any other means which vitiate the free will and
37   mandates the compensation and rehabilitation of victims of torture or similar practices
38   and their families.
39
40          By virtue of Section 2 of Article II of the Constitution adopting generally
41   accepted principles of international law as part of the law of the land, the Philippines
42   adheres to international human rights laws and conventions, the Universal Declaration
43   of Human Rights, including the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights
44   (ICCPR) and the Convention Against Torture (CAT) and Other Cruel, Inhuman or
45   Degrading Treatment or Punishment which imposes on each State party the obligation
46   to enact domestic legislation to give effect to the rights recognized therein and to ensure


                                                                                                           1
 1   that any person whose rights or freedoms have been violated shall have an effective
 2   remedy, even if the violation is committed by persons acting in an official capacity. In
 3   fact, the right to a remedy is itself guaranteed under existing human rights treaties and/or
 4   customary international law, being peremptory in character (jus cogens) and as such has
 5   been recognized as non-derogable.
 6
 7          Consistent with the foregoing, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 8   recognize the heroism and sacrifices of all Filipinos who were victims of summary
 9   execution, torture, enforced or involuntary disappearance and other gross human rights
10   violations committed during the regime of former President Ferdinand E. Marcos
11   covering the period from September 21, 1972 to February 25, 1986 and restore the
12   victims’ honor and dignity. The State hereby acknowledges its moral and legal
13   obligation to recognize and/or provide reparation to said victims and/or their families
14   for the deaths, injuries, sufferings, deprivations and damages they suffered under the
15   Marcos regime.
16
17          Similarly, it is the obligation of the State to acknowledge the sufferings and
18   damages inflicted upon persons whose properties or businesses were forcibly taken
19   over, sequestered or used, or those whose professions were damaged and/or impaired,
20   or those whose freedom of movement was restricted, and/or such other victims of the
21   violations of the Bill of Rights.
22
23           Section 3. Definition of Terms. — The following terms as used in this Act shall
24   mean:
25
26      a) Detention refers to the act of taking a person into custody against his will by
27         persons acting in an official capacity and/or agents of the State.
28
29      b) Human rights violation refers to any act or omission committed during the
30         period from September 21, 1972 to February 25, 1986 by persons acting in an
31         official capacity and/or agents of the State, but shall not be limited to the
32         following:
33
34              1) Any search, arrest and/or detention without a valid search warrant or
35                 warrant of arrest issued by a civilian court of law, including any
36                 warrantless arrest or detention carried out pursuant to the declaration of
37                 Martial Law by former President Ferdinand E. Marcos as well as any
38                 arrest, detention or deprivation of liberty carried out during the covered
39                 period on the basis of an "Arrest, Search and Seizure Order (ASSO)", a
40                 "Presidential Commitment Order (PCO)" or a "Preventive Detention
41                 Action (PDA)" and such other similar executive issuances as defined by
42                 decrees of former President Ferdinand E. Marcos, or in any manner that
43                 the arrest, detention or deprivation, of liberty was effected;
44
45              2) The infliction by a person acting in an official capacity and/or an agent of
46                 the State of physical injury, torture, killing, or violation of other human


                                                                                               2
 1             rights, of any person exercising civil or political rights, including but not
 2             limited to the freedom of speech, assembly or organization; and/or the
 3             right to petition the government for redress of grievances, even if such
 4             violation took place during or in the course of what the authorities at the
 5             time deemed an illegal assembly or demonstration: Provided, That torture
 6             in any form or under any circumstance shall be considered a human rights
 7             violation;
 8
 9          3) Any enforced or involuntary disappearance caused upon a person who
10             was arrested, detained or abducted against one’s will or otherwise
11             deprived of one’s liberty, as defined in Republic Act No. 10353,
12             otherwise known as the "Anti-Enforced or Involuntary Disappearance Act
13             of 2012″;
14
15          4) Any force or intimidation causing the involuntary exile of a person from
16             the Philippines;
17
18          5) Any act of force, intimidation or deceit causing unjust or illegal takeover
19             of a business, confiscation of property, detention of owner/s and or their
20             families, deprivation of livelihood of a person by agents of the State,
21             including those caused by Ferdinand E. Marcos, his spouse Imelda R.
22             Marcos, their immediate relatives by consanguinity or affinity, as well as
23             those persons considered as among their close relatives, associates,
24             cronies and subordinates under Executive Order No. 1, issued on
25             February 28, 1986 by then President Corazon C. Aquino in the exercise
26             of her legislative powers under the Freedom Constitution;
27
28          6) Any act or series of acts causing, committing and/or conducting the
29             following:
30               i) Kidnapping or otherwise exploiting children of persons suspected
31                   of committing acts against the Marcos regime;
32              ii) Committing sexual offenses against human rights victims who are
33                   detained and/or in the course of conducting military and/or police
34                   operations; and
35             iii) Other violations and/or abuses similar or analogous to the above,
36                   including those recognized by international law.
37
38   c) Human Rights Violations Victim (HRVV) refers to a person whose human
39      rights were violated by persons acting in an official capacity and/or agents of the
40      State as defined herein. In order to qualify for reparation under this Act, the
41      human rights violation must have been committed during the period from
42      September 21, 1972 to February 25, 1986: Provided, however, That victims of
43      human rights violations that were committed one (1) month before September
44      21, 1972 and one (1) month after February 25, 1986 shall be entitled to
45      reparation, under this Act if they can establish that the violation was committed:



                                                                                          3
 1             1) By agents of the State and/or persons acting in an official capacity as
 2                defined hereunder;
 3             2) For the purpose of preserving, maintaining, supporting or promoting the
 4                said regime; or
 5             3) To conceal abuses during the Marcos regime and/or the effects of Martial
 6                Law.
 7
 8      d) Persons Acting in an Official Capacity and/or Agents of the State — The
 9         following persons shall be deemed persons acting in an official capacity and/or
10         agents of the State under this Act:
11
12             1) Any member of the former Philippine Constabulary (PC), the former
13                Integrated National Police (INP), the Armed Forces of the Philippines
14                (AFP) and the Civilian Home Defense Force (CHDF) from September 21,
15                1972 to February 25, 1986 as well as any civilian agent attached thereto;
16                and any member of a paramilitary group even if one is not organically
17                part of the PC, the INP, the AFP or the CHDF so long as it is shown that
18                the group was organized, funded, supplied with equipment, facilities
19                and/or resources, and/or indoctrinated, controlled and/or supervised by
20                any person acting in an official capacity and/or agent of the State as herein
21                defined;
22
23             2) Any member of the civil service, including persons who held elective or
24                appointive public office at any time from September 21, 1972 to February
25                25, 1986;
26
27             3) Persons referred to in Section 2(a) of Executive Order No. 1, creating the
28                Presidential Commission on Good Government (PCGG), issued on
29                February 28, 1986 and related laws by then President Corazon C. Aquino
30                in the exercise of her legislative powers under the Freedom Constitution,
31                including former President Ferdinand E. Marcos, spouse Imelda R.
32                Marcos, their immediate relatives by consanguinity or affinity, as well as
33                their close relatives, associates, cronies and subordinates; and
34
35             4) Any person or group/s of persons acting with the authorization, support
36                or acquiescence of the State during the Marcos regime.
37
38      e) Torture refers to any act by which severe pain or suffering, whether physical or
39         mental, is intentionally inflicted on any person under the custody of persons
40         acting in an official capacity and/or agents of the State, as defined by law,
41         jurisprudence, international conventions and Republic Act No. 9745, otherwise
42         known as the "Anti-Torture Act of 2009.″
43
44          Section 4. Entitlement to Monetary Reparation. — Any HRVV qualified
45   under this Act shall receive reparation from the State, free of tax, as herein prescribed:
46   Provided, That for a deceased or involuntary disappeared HRVV, the legal heirs as


                                                                                             4
 1   provided for in the Civil Code of the Philippines, or such other person named by the
 2   executor or administrator of the deceased or involuntary disappeared HRVV’s estate in
 3   that order, shall be entitled to receive such reparation: Provided, further, That no special
 4   power of attorney shall be recognized in the actual disbursement of the award, and only
 5   the victim or the aforestated successor(s)-in-interest shall be entitled to personally
 6   receive said reparation form the Board, unless the victim involved is shown to be
 7   incapacitated to the satisfaction of the Board or could not personally do so for reasons
 8   of severe illness, threat to life or security: Provided, further, That the reparation
 9   received under this Act shall be without prejudice to the receipt of any other sum by the
10   HRVV from any other person or entity in any case involving violations of human rights
11   as defined in this Act.
12
13          Section 5. Non-monetary Reparation. — The Department of Health (DOH),
14   the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the Department of
15   Education (DepED), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical
16   Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and such other government
17   agencies shall render the necessary services as nonmonetary reparation for HRVVs
18   and/or their families, as may be determined by the Board pursuant to the provisions of
19   this Act. The amount necessary for this purpose shall be sourced from the budget of the
20   agency concerned in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
21
22          Section 6. Amount of Reparation. — The amount of reparation under this Act
23   shall be in proportion to the gravity of the human rights violation committed on the
24   HRVV and in accordance with the number of points assigned to the individual under
25   Section 19 hereof.
26
27          Section 7. Source of Reparation. — The amount of Ten billion pesos
28   (P10,000,000,000.00) plus accrued interest which form part of the funds transferred to
29   the government of the Republic of the Philippines by virtue of the December 10, 1997
30   Order of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, adjudged by the Supreme Court of the
31   Philippines as final and executory in Republic vs. Sandiganbayan on July 15, 2003
32   (G.R. No. 152154) as Marcos ill-gotten wealth and forfeited in favor of the Republic of
33   the Philippines for the reparation of the HRVV under this Act shall be sourced from the
34   recovered ill-gotten wealth remitted by the Presidential Commission on Good
35   Government to the Bureau of Treasury.

38                           CHAPTER II
39       THE HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATION VICTIMS’ RECOGNITION AND
40                       REPARATION BOARD
41
42         Section 8. Creation and Composition of the Human Rights Violation
43   Victims’ Recognition and Reparation Board. — There is hereby created an
44   independent and quasi-judicial body to be known as the Human Rights Violation
45   Victims’ Recognition and Reparation Board, hereinafter referred to as the Board. It shall
46   be composed of nine (9) members, who shall possess the following qualifications:


                                                                                               5
 1
 2      a) Must be of known probity, competence and integrity;
 3      b) Must have a deep and thorough understanding and knowledge of human rights
 4         and involvement in efforts against human rights violations committed during the
 5         regime of former President Ferdinand E. Marcos;
 6      c) At least three (3) of them must be members of the Philippine Bar who have been
 7         engaged in the practice of law for at least ten (10) years; and
 8      d) Must have a clear and adequate understanding and commitment to human rights
 9         protection, promotion and advocacy;
10      e) Must not be included among the persons acting in an official capacity and/or
11         agents of the state as enumerated in Section 3 (d); and
12      f) Must not be a member or former member of the government’s uniformed and
13         military personnel including the Philippine National Police and Armed Forces of
14         the Philippines.
15
16          The Human Rights Victims’ Claims Board shall be attached to but shall not be
17   under the Commission on Human Rights (CHR).
18
19         The Board shall organize itself within thirty (30) days from the completion of
20   appointment of all nine (9) members and shall thereafter organize its Secretariat.
21
22          Section 9. Appointment to the Board. — The President shall appoint the
23   Chairperson and the other eight (8) members of the Board from among the list of
24   nominees provided by the Commission on Human Rights and Human Rights
25   Organizations: Provided, That human rights organizations such as, but not limited to,
26   the Task Force Detainees of the Philippines (TFDP), the Free Legal Assistance Group
27   (FLAG), the Movement of Attorneys for Brotherhood, Integrity and Nationalism
28   (MABINI), the Families of Victims of Involuntary Disappearance (FIND), Samahan ng
29   mga Ex-Detainees Laban sa Detensyon at Aresto (SELDA), and Kapatid shall submit
30   nominations to the President.
31
32          Section 10. Powers and Functions of the Board. — The Board shall have the
33   following powers and functions:
34
35      a) Receive, evaluate, process and investigate applications for claims under this Act;
36      b) Issue subpoena/s ad testificandum and subpoena/s duces tecum;
37      c) Conduct independent administrative proceedings and resolve disputes over
38         claims;
39      d) Approve with finality all eligible claims under this Act;
40      e) Deputize appropriate government agencies to assist it in order to effectively
41         perform its functions;
42      f) Promulgate such rules as may be necessary to carry out the purposes of this Act,
43         including rules of procedure in the conduct of its proceedings, with the Revised
44         Rules of Court of the Philippines having suppletory application;
45      g) Exercise administrative control and supervision over its Secretariat;



                                                                                           6
 1      h) The Board, at its discretion, may consult the human rights organizations
 2         mentioned in Section 9 herein to vouch or support a claim; and
 3      i) Perform such other duties, functions and responsibilities as may be necessary to
 4         effectively attain the objectives of this Act.
 5
 6          Provided that, the Board shall exercise liberality and utmost consideration in
 7   evaluation and approval of claims in favor of the human rights violations’ victims,
 8   without prejudice to its power and jurisdiction to fully evaluate and deny false claims.
 9
10          Section 11. Resolution of Claims. — The Board shall be composed of three (3)
11   divisions which shall function simultaneously and independently of each other in the
12   resolution of claims for reparation. Each division shall be composed of one (1)
13   Chairperson, who shall be a member of the Philippine Bar and two (2) members to be
14   appointed by the Board en banc.
15
16          Section 12. Emoluments. — The Chairperson and members of the Board shall
17   have the rank, salary, emoluments and allowances equivalent to s Presiding Justice and
18   Associate Justice of the Court of Appeals, respectively.
19
20          Section 13. Secretariat of the Board. — The Board shall be assisted by a
21   Secretariat which may come from the existing personnel of the CHR, without prejudice
22   to the hiring of additional personnel as determined by the Board to accommodate the
23   volume of required work. The following shall be the functions of the Secretariat:
24
25      a) Receive, evaluate, process and investigate applications for claims under this Act;
26      b) Recommend to the Board the approval of applications for claims;
27      c) Assist the Board in technical functions; and
28      d) Perform other duties that may be assigned by the Board.
29
30          The Chairperson of the Board shall appoint a Board Secretary who shall head the
31   Secretariat for the duration of the existence of the Board. There shall be a Technical
32   Staff Head assisted by five (5) Legal Officers and three (3) Paralegal Officers; and an
33   Administrative Staff Head assisted by three (3) Administrative Support Staff.
34
35          When necessary, the Board may hire additional contractual employees or
36   contract a service provider to provide services of counselors, psychologists, social
37   workers and public education specialists, among others, to augment the services of the
38   Secretariat: Provided, That the maximum contract amount per year shall not exceed
39   more than fifteen percent (15%) of the total annual operating budget of the Board.
40
41         Section 14. Operating Budget of the Board. — The operating budget of the
42   Board shall be included in the budget of the Commission on Human Rights in the annual
43   General Appropriations Act: Provided, That it shall not exceed Seventy million pesos
44   (P70,000,000.00) a year.
45

 3   properly disbursed in accordance with the policies stated by Congress and relevant

 7                               CHAPTER III
 8                 CLAIMANTS, REPARATION AND RECOGNITION
 9
10         Section 16. Claimants. — Any person who is an HRVV may file a claim with
11   the Board for reparation and/or recognition in accordance with the provisions of this
12   Act.
13
14           Section 17. Conclusive Presumption for Hawaii Plaintiffs and Bantayog ng
15   mga Bayani HRVV. — The claimants in the class suit and direct action plaintiffs in
16   the Human Rights Litigation Against the Estate of Ferdinand E. Marcos (MDL No. 840,
17   CA No. 88-0390) in the US Federal District Court of Honolulu, Hawaii wherein a
18   favorable judgment has been rendered, shall be extended the conclusive presumption
19   that they are HRVVs: Provided, That the HRVVs recognized by the Bantayog Ng Mga
20   Bayani Foundation shall also be accorded the same conclusive presumption: Provided,
21   further, that such conclusive presumption shall mean that the claims of such HRVVs
22   shall not be denied: Provided, further, That nothing herein shall be construed to deprive
23   the Board of its original jurisdiction and its inherent power to determine the extent of
24   the human rights violations and the corresponding reparation and/or recognition that
25   may be granted.
26
27          Section 18. Motu Proprio Recognition. — The Board may take judicial notice
28   motu proprio of individual persons who suffered human rights violations as defined
29   herein and grant such persons recognition as HRVVs and included in the Roll of
30   Victims as provided for in Section 26 hereof.
31
32          Section 19. Determination of Award. —
33
34      a) The Board shall follow the point system in the determination of the award. The
35         range shall be one (1) to ten (10) points, as follows:
36            1) Victims who died or who disappeared and are still missing shall be given
37                ten (10) points;
38            2) Victims who were tortured and/or raped or sexually abused shall he given
39                six (6) to nine (9) points;
40            3) Victims who were detained shall be given three (3) to five (5) points; and
41            4) Victims whose rights were violated under Section 3, paragraph (b), nos.
42                (4), (5) and (6) under this Act shall be given one (1) to two (2) points.
43
44          The Board shall exercise its powers with due discretion in the determination of
45   points for each victim, which shall be based on the type of violation committed against
46   the HRVV, frequently and duration of the violation. In each category, HRVVs who had


                                                                                            8
 1   suffered more would receive more points. In instances where a victim is classified in
 2   more than one category, one shall be awarded the points in the higher category:
 3   Provided, That in cases where there are several eligible claims filed for reparation by
 4   or on behalf of a particular HRVV, the Board shall award only one (1) valid claim which
 5   corresponds to the category obtaining the highest number of points for each eligible
 6   claimant.
 7
 8      b) The Board shall proceed to determine the award for each claimant classified
 9         under Sections 16, 17 and 18 of this Act.
10
11      c) The Board shall then compute the final monetary value of one’s award that is
12         equivalent to the numerical value of one point multiplied by the number of points
13         that a claimant is entitled to, as previously determined by the Board.
14
15      d) Within thirty (30) days after the Board has approved with finality each eligible
16         claim pending before it and after due publication of such legitimate claim, the
17         award of monetary compensation shall take effect: Provided, That any pending

19         resolved by it within one hundred twenty (120) days from the filing of the appeal.

25          Section 20. Transfer of Funds. — Pursuant to the judgment mentioned in
26   Section 7 hereof, the amount of Ten Billion Pesos (P10,000,000,000.00) plus the

30          Section 21. Documentation of Human Rights Violations Committed by the
31   Marcos Regime. — In the implementation of this Act and without prejudice to any
32   other documentary or other evidence that may be required for the award of any
33   reparation, any HRVV seeking reparation shall execute a detailed sworn statement
34   narrating the circumstances of the pertinent human rights violation/s committed.
35
36          Section 22. Publication. — Consistent with Section 23 herein, the Board, after
37   having been duly convened, shall set the period for the commencement and termination
38   of applications by HRVVs and cause the publication of the same: Provided, That such
39   period shall only become operative fifteen (15) days after its last publication, which
40   shall be once a week for three (3) consecutive weeks in at least two (2) national
41   newspapers of general circulation.
42
43          Section 23. Period for Filing of Claims; Waiver. — An HRVV shall file an
44   application for reparation with the Board within six (6) months from the effectivity of
45   the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of this Act: Provided, That failure to file
46   an application within said period is deemed a waiver of the right to file the same:

 1   Provided, further, That for HRVVs who are deceased, incapacitated, or missing due to
 2   enforced disappearance, their legal heir/s or representatives, shall be entitled to file an
 3   application for reparation on their behalf: Provided, further, that previous claimants
 4   whose applications have been denied shall be allowed to refile claims with the Board
 5   within the aforementioned six-month period: Provided, further, that claims which have
 6   been granted and fulfilled, and those found to be fraudulent, are not qualified for refiling

16          Section 24 Appeal. — Any aggrieved claimant or oppositor may file an appeal
17   within ten (10) calendar days from the receipt of the Resolution of the Division, to the
18   Board en banc, whose decision shall then become final and executory.
19
20          Section 25. Penalties; Applicability of the Revised Penal Code. — Any
21   claimant who is found by the Board, after due hearing, to have filed a fraudulent claim,
22   shall be referred to the appropriate office for prosecution. If convicted, he shall suffer
23   the imprisonment of eight (8) to ten (10) years, shall be disqualified from public office
24   and employment and shall be deprived of the right to vote and be voted for in any
25   national or local election, even after the service of sentence unless granted absolute
26   pardon. Such claimants shall not be eligible to refile a claim for reparation as prescribed
27   in Section 23 of this Act.
28
29          Any member of the Board and its Secretariat, public officer, employee of an
30   agency or any private individual mandated to implement this Act, who shall misuse,
31   embezzle or misappropriate the funds for the reparation of HRVVs or who shall commit
32   fraud in the processing of documents and claims of HRVVs, or shall conspire with any
33   individual to commit the same, shall also be prosecuted.

39          Section 28. Guidelines for the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).
40   — In implementing this Act and in formulating the corresponding rules and regulations,
41   and to ensure that all applications are properly screened for fraudulent claims, the Board
42   must provide for:
43
44      a) Transparency in the processing of the claims;




                                                                                            11
 1      b) A procedure that allows any concerned party to oppose an application or claim
 2         on the ground that it is fraudulent, fictitious or spurious and gives that party the
 3         opportunity to question the same and to present evidence in support thereof; and
 4      c) A procedure that is speedy and expeditious without sacrificing any of the parties’
 5         fundamental rights.
 6
 7         Within fifteen (15) days from the date of its organization, the Board shall
 8   promulgate the necessary IRR and procedures for the effective implementation of this
 9   Act. The IRR shall be effective fifteen (15) days after its publication in two (2) national
10   newspapers of general circulation.
11
12           Section 29. Work Period; Sunset Clause. — The Board shall complete its work
13   within four (4) years from the effectivity of the IRR promulgated by it. After such a
14   period, it shall become functus officio: Provided that, all documents collected by the
15   Board shall be turned over to the Human Rights Violations Victims’ Memorial
16   Commission created under Section 27: Provided further, that the Commission on
17   Human Rights shall take over all the remaining functions of the Board until such time
18   that all qualified claims for reparation have been fully satisfied.

21   this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
