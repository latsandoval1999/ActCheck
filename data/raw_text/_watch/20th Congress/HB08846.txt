                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Aklan piña weaving is one of the most refined and culturally significant textile
traditions in the Philippines. Woven from fibers of the Red Spanish pineapple and shaped
by centuries of indigenous knowledge and craftsmanship, piña cloth has long served as
a symbol of Filipino identity and ceremonial dress. In 2023, Aklan piña weaving was
inscribed in the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of
Humanity, affirming its global cultural importance and urgent need for safeguarding
measures.1

        Despite its international recognition, the piña weaving tradition faces serious and
well-documented threats. A study on piña weaving in Kalibo, Aklan noted over 50% of
weavers surveyed were over age 50, showing a significant lack of younger entrants to the
craft.2 The same study documented challenges such as low wages, competition from other
fibers, and limited production scale, which undermine long-term viability for weavers
and contribute to erosion of skill transmission. 3

       Cultural heritage experts emphasize that intangible traditions survive only when
supported by living communities and viable economic systems. UNESCO’s 2003
Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage defines intangible
cultural heritage as practices, knowledge and skills recognized by communities as part

       This bill establishes the Aklan Piña Conservation and Innovation Center in the
Municipality of Kalibo, Aklan, the historic heart of piña weaving, as a specialized
institution dedicated to safeguarding, promoting, researching, and modernizing this
heritage industry. The Center operationalizes the State policy to conserve and promote
cultural heritage under Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution, which mandates the
protection of Filipino artistic and historic traditions.

       The proposed Center adopts a holistic framework that links cultural preservation
with agricultural sustainability, science and technology, education, and trade
development. It institutionalizes cultural mapping and archiving consistent with national
safeguarding programs, supports sustainable pineapple cultivation in partnership with
the Department of Agriculture (DA), promotes textile innovation aligned with research
directions of the Philippine Textile Research Institute (PTRI), and strengthens the global
competitiveness of piña products in line with creative economy strategies advocated by
the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI). The Center also facilitates intellectual
property protection for traditional designs, consistent with international best practices on
safeguarding community knowledge under the World Intellectual Property
Organization.

       By anchoring conservation in innovation and economic empowerment, this
measure recognizes that cultural heritage thrives when culture-bearers are supported
and given sustainable livelihoods. The creation of the Aklan Piña Conservation and
Innovation Center affirms the State’s commitment to protecting Filipino artistic heritage
while transforming it into a dynamic driver of regional development, cultural pride, and
global cultural presence.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT CREATING THE AKLAN PIÑA CONSERVATION AND INNOVATION
    CENTER IN THE MUNICIPALITY OF KALIBO, AKLAN, DEFINING ITS
    PURPOSES,  AND    PROVIDING    FOR   ITS  ORGANIZATION,
    MANAGEMENT, PROGRAMS, AND FUNDING

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Aklan Piña Conservation
and Innovation Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
conserve, promote, and popularize the nation’s historical and cultural heritage,
resources, and artistic creations, as well as to pursue measures that ensure the
achievement of the stated policy.

       SEC. 3. Creation of the Aklan Piña Conservation and Innovation Center. – There
is hereby created the Aklan Piña Conservation and Innovation Center (the “Center”), a
specialized institution for the safeguarding, promotion, research, innovation, and
transmission of Aklan piña weaving. The Center shall be based in the Municipality of
Kalibo, Province of Aklan, and shall operate under the oversight of the National
Commission for Culture and the Arts (“NCCA”), in close coordination with relevant
stakeholders, including the Provincial Government of Aklan, the Municipal Government
of Kalibo, academic institutions, and weaving communities. For its establishment, the
Center shall utilize and strengthen existing facilities, resources, and networks provided
by local governments, regional line agencies, academic institutions, or weaving
communities.

       SEC. 4. Staffing. – The Center shall be headed by a Chief who shall be responsible
for its overall management and coordination with local stakeholders and national
agencies. The staffing pattern of the Center shall be prepared by the NCCA, subject to the
approval of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and in accordance with
existing laws, rules, and regulations.

      SEC. 5. Functions. – The Center shall:

          (a) Undertake cultural mapping, oral history collection, and digital archiving
              of Aklan piña weaving traditions in partnership with the NCCA and local
              governments;

          (b) Support farmers in cultivating the Red Spanish pineapple, in collaboration
              with the Department of Agriculture (DA), including the distribution of
              planting materials, tools, and training in sustainable agriculture;

          (c) Develop programs to ensure the transmission of weaving knowledge,
              including Schools of Living Traditions and mentorship initiatives, and
              coordinate with the Department of Education and the Commission on
              Higher Education to integrate piña weaving and textile heritage into
              appropriate educational and youth programs, modules, and research
              initiatives;

          (d) Collaborate with the Department of Science and Technology, universities,
              and design institutions to advance research on textile fibers, promote
              sustainable technologies, support product standardization, and expand
              piña product applications;

          (e) Work with the Department of Trade and Industry to strengthen the market
              positioning, branding, competitiveness, and value chain integration of piña
              textiles, including support for product development and participation in
              local and international trade platforms;

          (f) Partner with the Department of Tourism and Local Government Units
              (LGUs) to integrate piña weaving into cultural tourism programs, festivals,
              and heritage routes;
          (g) Coordinate with the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines to assist
              weavers and cooperatives in securing appropriate intellectual property
              protection, including designs, trademarks, and potential geographical
              indications, with due regard to the rights of communities and culture-
              bearers; and

          (h) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the intent
              and purposes of this Act.

     SEC. 6. Governance and Administration. – The Center shall be governed by a
Management Council(the “Council”) composed of representatives from the following:

          (a) National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), as Chairperson;
          (b) Provincial Government of Aklan, as Co-Vice Chairperson;
          (c) National Museum of the Philippines;
          (d) Municipal Government of Kalibo;
          (e) Department of Trade and Industry;
          (f) Department of Agriculture;
          (g) Department of Science and Technology – Philippine Textile Research
              Institute;
          (h) Department of Tourism;
          (i) Department of Education;
          (j) Commission on Higher Education;
          (k) Technical Education and Skills Development Authority;
          (l) Academe and research institutions, as may be designated; and
          (m)        Recognized piña weavers’ associations or cooperatives, which shall
              constitute at least two (2) seats in the Council, one of whom shall be
              appointed as Co-Vice Chairperson.

      The Council shall formulate the policies, programs, and priorities of the Center,
ensuring inter-agency coordination, community participation, and cultural sensitivity.

       SEC. 7. Funding. – The initial funding necessary to implement the provisions of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the NCCA. Thereafter,
such amounts as may be necessary for the continuous implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the NCCA, in coordination with the concerned government
agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

      SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
