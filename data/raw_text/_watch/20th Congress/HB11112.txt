    CREATING THE PHILIPPINE CENTER FOR DISEASE CONTROL AND

                   AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) has taken most of the world by
surprise, infecting more than five million persons and taking the lives of at least
three hundred thousand persons worldwide. It has become a pandemic that
threatens countries without strong public health care systems and facilities for
control and prevention of communicable and infectious diseases.

      Our lack of institutional capacity to implement comprehensive public
health management programs, especially in public health protection, shows the
harsh reality that our country needs to modernize and upgrade our public health
systems to decisively address the risks of communicable diseases.

       From sudden cases of polio-related diseases, to human immunodeficiency
virus (HIV)-related infections, to tuberculosis and dengue outbreaks, there is a
need for an institution to focus and create scientifically accurate and research-
backed solutions to combat infectious and emerging diseases.

      While the Department of Health has been effective to some degree in the
delivery of health services amid the pandemic, we must strengthen its absorptive
capacity and framework in dealing with difficult communicable diseases.
     This bill seeks to establish a Philippine National Center for Disease Control
and Prevention, which shall serve as the principal health protection agency of
the Philippines aimed at preventing, protecting, and managing the spread of
communicable and infectious diseases and other health emergencies in the
country.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
   CREATING THE PHILIPPINE CENTER FOR DISEASE CONTROL AND
       PREVENTION, DEFINING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS
                  AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Center for
Disease Control and Prevention Act”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State:

   (a) To protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
       consciousness among them;
   (b) To adopt, support, establish, and maintain an effective health mechanism
       responsive to the country’s health needs, and emergencies;
   (c) To ensure the health and well-being of Filipinos and protect them from
       communicable diseases;
   (d) To prevent the emergence and transmission of serious diseases;
   (e) To provide a framework for communicable disease control and prevention
       in the country; and
   (f) To establish a health protection agency to save lives and protect people
       from health threats.

SECTION 3. Creation of the Philippine Center for Disease Control and
Prevention. – There is hereby created an independent body to be known as the
Philippine Center for Disease Control and Prevention, hereinafter referred to as
“CDC”.

      The CDC shall be the principal agency tasked to identify, assess, and
communicate current and emerging threats to human health posed by
communicable diseases. It shall carry out activities and programs for the
prevention and control of communicable diseases for the promotion of public
health.

      The CDC shall be attached to the Department of Health for policy and
program coordination.

SECTION 4. Powers and Functions. – The CDC shall exercise the following
powers and functions:

   (a) Establish procedures for systematically searching for, collecting, collating
       and analyzing information and data to identify emerging health threats in
       the country;
   (b) Undertake research activities in the diagnosis, control, and prevention of
       communicable diseases that are major causes of mortality and morbidity
       in the country;
   (c) Investigate disease outbreaks and other threats to public health;
   (d) Conduct clinical trials aimed at better understanding and control of
       communicable diseases in the country;
   (e) Foster the development of sufficient capacity for diagnosis, detection,
       identification, and characterization of infectious agents which may
       threaten public health;
   (f) Coordinate data collection, validation, analysis, and provide health
       information to protect against expensive and dangerous health threats;
   (g) Ensure that the public and any interested party are rapidly given objective,
       reliable, and easily accessible information with regard to the results of its
       work;
   (h) Provide information, expertise, advice, and risk assessment to local
       government units in the control and prevention of communicable diseases;
   (i) Assist local government units in the control of a broad range of
       communicable diseases;
   (j) Formulate an early warning and response system to make available
       information for the general public;
   (k) Confront global disease threats through advanced computing and
       laboratory analysis of data to quickly find solutions to the same;
   (l) Maintain the database for epidemiological surveillance;
  (m) Provide independent scientific opinions, expert advice, data and
       information;
   (n) Promote and initiate scientific studies necessary for the prevention and
       control of communicable diseases;
   (o) Submit to Congress an annual evaluation of the current and emerging
       threats to health in the country.

SECTION 5. Early Warning and Response System. – The CDC shall operate
an Early Warning and Response System which will ensure that the national
government and local government units are able to respond in a timely and
coordinated manner to emerging health threats in the country.

      The CDC shall take action to ensure that the early warning and response
system is efficiently and effectively linked with other forms of community alert
systems.

SECTION 6. Obligation of Local Government Units. – Local government units
must provide the CDC in a timely manner with available health and technical
data relevant to the prevention and control of communicable diseases.

SECTION 7. Transfer of Agencies and Personnel. – The following offices and
agencies are hereby abolished and their powers and functions, applicable funds
and appropriations, records, equipment, property, and personnel transferred to
the CDC:

   (a) Infectious Diseases for Elimination Division (under the Disease Prevention
       and Control Bureau of the Department of Health);
   (b) Infectious Diseases for Prevention and Control Division (under the Disease
       Prevention and Control Bureau of the Department of Health);
   (c) The Epidemiological Bureau of the Department of Health; and
   (d) The Research Institute of Tropical Medicine.

SECTION 8. Executive Director. – The CDC shall be headed by an Executive
Director, with the rank of Undersecretary, who shall be appointed by the
President on the basis of technical expertise, academic background, and
appropriate work experience.

      The Executive Director shall perform the following functions and powers:

   (a) Manage and direct the activities of the Philippine Center for Disease
       Control and Prevention;
   (b) Coordinate CDC response to health emergencies;
   (c) Certify the existence of an epidemic, which shall be treated as a public
       health emergency;
   (d) Recommend to the President the exercise of special powers in case of
       confirmation as to the existence of an epidemic;
   (e) Coordinate local and international health activities relating to disease
       prevention and control;
   (f) Liaise with other government agencies, international organizations,
       including the World Health Organization, learning institutions, and other
       relevant outside groups; and
   (g) Appoint employees of the CDC in accordance with the Civil Service Law,
       and other relevant rules and regulations.

SECTION 9. Organizational Structure. – The Secretary of the Department of
Health, in consultation with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
shall determine the organizational structure, qualification standards, staffing
pattern, and compensation of the newly created CDC, in accordance with
existing laws, rules, and regulations.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be taken from the current fiscal year’s
appropriation of the offices and agencies herein absorbed by the CDC.
Thereafter, the amount needed for the operation and maintenance of the CDC
shall be included under the Department of Health in the General Appropriations
Act.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, orders, rules and regulations, other
issuances, or part thereof, inconsistent with any provision of this Act, are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
