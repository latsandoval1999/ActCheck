                              AN ACT
IMPOSING EXCISE TAXES ON HIGH-SODIUM AND HIGH-SUGAR FOOD AND
SWEETENED BEVERAGES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 150-B AND
288-A, CREATING A NEW SECTION 150-C, OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL
REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

        A healthy population is indispensable to national productivity and inclusive growth. The
excessive intake of sodium and added sugars are dietary risk factors that drive the country’s most
burdensome noncommunicable diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular disease,
all of which further contribute to chronic kidney disease (“CKD”). Diabetes and hypertension has
been recognized as the two leading drivers of CKD, which was reported to contribute 50.62% and
23.26% to CKD worldwide, respectively.1

       The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has found that approximately one-
third of adults with diabetes have CKD, while approximately one-fifth of adults with
hypertension have CKD.2 Diabetes and hypertension are major risk factors for cardiovascular
disease. Since the cardiovascular system and kidneys are deeply interconnected, when one of
these systems develops problems, it can lead to problems in the other. A UK Biobank study
showed that about 5.6% of people with cardiovascular disease developed CKD within 10 years.3

       Fiscal measures can reduce consumption of products that contain excessive levels of
nutrients of concern while giving manufacturers a direct incentive to reformulate. A well-
designed excise tax should therefore be objective, measurable, administratively workable, and
structured so that a product can avoid or reduce the tax when its sodium or added-sugar content
is lowered.

       This bill adopts that approach.

        For sweetened beverages already covered by Section 150-B of the National Internal
Revenue Code, the bill retains the existing excise-tax framework but shifts calorically sweetened
beverages toward sugar-concentration tiers. This aligns the tax more closely with the public-
health objective by imposing a higher tax on beverages with more added sugar while preserving
the existing treatment of beverages that use non-caloric sweeteners. Products already taxed under
Section 150-B are not again taxed for sugar under the new Section 150-C.

        For prepackaged processed foods, it uses uniform nutrient concentrations per one
hundred (100) grams and applies tiered specific excise taxes per kilogram of product. The use of
a uniform one-hundred-gram basis avoids manipulation through artificially small serving sizes
and creates a clear reformulation target. The tax is based on the highest applicable nutrient tier
so that a single product is not subjected to cumulative sodium and sugar excises under the same
section.

        Fresh and unprocessed basic foods, food prepared and sold for immediate consumption
on-site, infant formula, and food for special medical purposes are excluded. The bill does not
exempt particular high-sodium processed products merely because they are inexpensive. Instead,
every manufacturer is given the same path to lower tax liability: reduce the sodium or added-
sugar content below the statutory threshold.

       The measure also provides for inflation indexation of specific tax rates, objective rules for
determining nutrient content, manufacturer and importer declarations subject to verification, a
reasonable transition period for reformulation and compliance, and periodic review by Congress
based on revenue, health, consumer, and industry effects.

        The revenues from the new food excise are dedicated to Universal Health Care, medical
assistance and health facilities, and nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions. In this
manner, the tax both discourages excessive consumption and helps finance the public health
programs needed to address the diseases associated with unhealthy diets.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “High Sodium and High
Sugar Food and Beverage Tax Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the
right to health; discourage excessive consumption of sodium and added sugars;
encourage the reformulation of processed food and beverages toward healthier nutrient
profiles; impose excise taxes that are objective, measurable, and administratively
workable; and use the resulting revenues to strengthen Universal Health Care and
nutrition programs.

SEC. 6. Preservation of Existing Sweetened-Beverage Provisions. — Except for the
amendments expressly made by this Act, all provisions governing sweetened beverages
under Section 150-B of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended,
including applicable exclusions, filing and payment rules, administrative requirements,
and penalties, shall remain in force. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to impose two
sugar excise taxes on the same beverage.

SEC. 7. Baseline and Impact Evaluation. — Within nine (9) months from the effectivity
of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, in coordination with the Secretary of Health, the
Secretary of Trade and Industry, the Secretary of Agriculture, the Commissioner of
Internal Revenue, the Food and Drug Administration, the National Nutrition Council,
and the National Tax Research Center, shall establish baseline data on covered products,
nutrient concentrations, prices, household expenditure, expected revenues, and relevant
industry and health indicators.




                                           11
       Within twelve (12) months after commencement of collection, and every three (3)
years thereafter, the same agencies shall submit to Congress and make publicly available
an evaluation of the tax’s revenue, consumer-price, consumption, reformulation, health,
enforcement, and industry effects, including effects on low-income households and small
businesses, subject to laws protecting confidential taxpayer and proprietary information.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue and in coordination with the Secretary of Health, the
Food and Drug Administration, the Bureau of Customs, and other concerned agencies,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of
this Act. The rules shall include uniform procedures for registration, declarations,
product classification, laboratory testing, sampling, rounding, audit, treatment of
product reformulations, import verification, and payment of tax.

SEC. 9. Transition Period. — Collection of the taxes under Sections 150-B and 150-C as
amended or created by this Act shall commence one (1) year from the effectivity of this
Act. During the transition period, manufacturers and importers shall register covered
products, submit required nutrient declarations, and may reformulate products without
incurring the new or adjusted tax until the commencement date.

SEC. 10. Periodic Legislative Review. — Congress shall review the rates, nutrient
thresholds, exclusions, revenue allocation, and administrative performance of this Act
not later than three (3) years after commencement of collection and every three (3) years
thereafter, taking into account the evaluation required under Section 7.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
