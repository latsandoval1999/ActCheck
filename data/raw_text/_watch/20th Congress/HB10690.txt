                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Rising prices continue to strain Filipino households. Headline inflation
rose to 6.2 percent in July 2026, down only slightly from 6.4 percent in the
preceding month.1 Such inflation rates are carried over to costs related to
housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels. Electricity is a large part of this
burden by design: as of 2026, Philippine residential electricity rates were already
among the highest in Southeast Asia, surpassing even Singapore.2 For
households already stretched thin, a meaningful share of that bill has nothing
to do with the electricity they consumed.

       That share is the System Loss Charge. Under Republic Act No. 7832 (1994)
and Republic Act No. 9136, the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001,
distribution utilities and electric cooperatives are allowed to pass on to
consumers the cost of electricity lost within their own networks, whether through
outdated equipment, poor maintenance, or theft. Private utilities may currently
recover as much as 8.5 percent of total kilowatt-hours purchased and generated,
while electric cooperatives, which serve the overwhelming majority of rural and
provincial consumers, may recover up to 13 percent, nearly half again as much.

       In practice, this means the very consumers who already contend with less
reliable service, older infrastructure, and fewer alternatives than their
counterparts in Metro Manila are also the ones most exposed to paying for
electricity that never reached their homes. System loss typically makes up not
less than five percent of a household's monthly bill, and, as currently treated, is
subject to the 12 percent value-added tax on top of that.

        This is not a new concern, nor is it one this representation raises for the
first time. Versions of this measure have been filed in before, and the elimination
of system loss charges has since been raised as a policy priority by the current
administration, with President Ferdinand Romualdez Marcos Jr. declaring it as
a top priority legislation, going as far as explicitly demanding Congress amend
EPIRA to stop both the system loss charge and its VAT from being passed to
consumers. At its core, this measure asks a simple question: should consumers
bear the cost of electricity that never reaches them, or should that remain a
business risk of the entities that own and operate the distribution system?
Consumers do not design distribution networks, choose feeder conditions,
replace aging transformers, or prevent electricity theft.

        These are matters entirely within the control of distribution utilities and
electric cooperatives, and Article XII of the 1987 Constitution subjects public
utilities precisely to this kind of State regulation in the public interest. Congress
may determine which costs may lawfully form part of a regulated electricity rate,
and which must instead be absorbed as an ordinary cost of doing business by
the entity that controls the system. This bill takes the more direct path. Rather
than merely narrowing the tax base, as prior proposals have done by exempting
the System Loss Charge from value-added tax while leaving the underlying
charge intact, this measure prohibits the recovery of distribution system loss
from consumers altogether, whether technical or non-technical in origin.

       Once that recovery is prohibited, no taxable consumer charge exists for
value-added tax to attach to in the first place; the VAT problem is resolved as a
direct consequence of removing the charge itself, not through a separate carve-
out in the tax code. This bill accordingly amends Republic Act No. 7832 and
Republic Act No. 9136 to bar distribution utilities and electric cooperatives from
billing, collecting, or otherwise recovering system loss costs, in any form and
under any name, from electricity end-users, while preserving their ability to
recover the legitimate, audited cost of modernizing and maintaining their
networks, with particular support extended to electric cooperatives serving
geographically     isolated,   disaster-prone,    and   underserved     provincial
communities.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT PROHIBITING THE DIRECT OR INDIRECT RECOVERY OF
DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES, INCLUDING ANY VALUE-ADDED
   TAX ATTRIBUTABLE THERETO, FROM ELECTRICITY CONSUMERS,
GIVING PRIORITY TO THE MODERNIZATION OF DISTRIBUTION SYSTEMS
   IN RURAL AND PROVINCIAL AREAS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
 REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832, AS AMENDED, AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136,
  OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM
             ACT OF 2001," AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “System Loss
Elimination Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect
the rights of every electricity consumer without compromising the viability of
distribution utilities' and electric cooperatives' operations. Electricity is an
indispensable commodity for economic growth and social welfare, and reliable
service at a just and reasonable cost is a matter of basic equity for every Filipino
household, particularly those in rural and provincial areas that already contend
with less reliable service and fewer alternatives than urban centers.

       To this end, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect electricity
consumers from unreasonable and avoidable charges; to promote transparent,
just, and reasonable electricity rates; to require distribution utilities and electric
cooperatives to operate efficiently and accountably; to encourage continuing
investment in safe, modern, and reliable distribution systems, with priority given
to underserved rural and provincial networks; and to place responsibility for
distribution system loss upon the entity that owns, operates, manages, or
controls the distribution network, consistent with the principle that financial
responsibility should follow operational control.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Distribution system loss refers to the difference between the total
          electrical energy received by a distribution utility at its points of
          delivery and the total electrical energy delivered and properly metered
          to end-users and other authorized recipients, including technical and
          non-technical losses;
      (b) Technical system loss refers to electricity lost because of the physical
          characteristics, design, condition, operation, or maintenance of
          distribution lines, transformers, substations, meters, and other
          distribution facilities;
      (c) Non-technical system loss refers to electricity lost because of
          pilferage, illegal connections, meter tampering, inaccurate metering,
          billing or administrative errors, unregistered consumption, fraud, or
          other causes not arising solely from the physical delivery of electricity;
      (d) Distribution utility refers to any private corporation, government-
          owned utility, local government unit, or other entity, other than an
          electric cooperative, authorized to distribute electricity to rers;
      (e) Electric cooperative refers to a distribution utility organized under
          Presidential Decree No. 269, as amended, or other applicable law, that
          primarily serves rural, provincial, and franchise areas outside Metro
          Manila;
      (f) End-users means any person or entity requiring the supply and
          delivery of electricity for its own use;
      (g) Direct or indirect recovery refers to the billing, collection, allocation,
          embedding, capitalization, deferral, reimbursement, reclassification,
          imputation, or recovery of any amount from an end-user, regardless of
          the name, rate component, account, formula, methodology, or
          mechanism used; and
      (h) Prohibition Date means the date established under Section 15 of this
          Act upon which the complete prohibition against the recovery of
          distribution system loss shall commence.

      SECTION 4. Allocation of Distribution-System Risk. — Technical and non-
technical distribution system losses shall be considered inherent operational and
business risks of the distribution utility or electric cooperative that owns,
operates, manages, or controls the distribution system in which the losses
occurred.

      The physical unavoidability of a portion of technical system loss shall not
constitute legal or regulatory authority to recover its cost from an end-user.
       Distribution system loss shall be for the sole account of the distribution
utility or electric cooperative concerned and shall not constitute a prudent,
reasonable, eligible, or recoverable cost for purposes of determining any retail
rate, distribution wheeling charge, revenue requirement, rate base, regulatory
asset, deferred account, true-up adjustment, surcharge, rider, or other amount
payable by an end-user.

       SECTION 5. Prohibition Against Direct or Indirect Recovery. — Beginning
on the Prohibition Date, no distribution utility, electric cooperative, retail
electricity supplier, local retail electricity supplier, supplier of last resort, billing
agent, or other person or entity billing electricity end-users shall directly or
indirectly charge, bill, allocate, collect, or otherwise recover any distribution
system loss from an end-user.

       The prohibition shall apply to technical and non-technical system losses
regardless of the name, description, account, tariff, adjustment mechanism,
regulatory methodology, or billing arrangement used, and shall apply uniformly
to private distribution utilities and electric cooperatives alike.

       SECTION 6. Prohibition Against Circumvention and Offset. — No regulated
entity shall recover or offset distribution system loss by:

      (a) Changing the name or description of the charge;
      (b) Incorporating or embedding the cost in another rate or billing
          component;
      (c) Creating or modifying a rider, surcharge, adjustment mechanism,
          regulatory asset, deferred account, true-up mechanism, or similar
          device;
      (d) Increasing another regulated charge for the purpose of recovering,
          replacing, or offsetting prohibited system-loss revenue;
      (e) Transferring the cost through an affiliate, subsidiary, contractor,
          supplier, or related entity; or
      (f) Adopting any other arrangement that has the purpose or effect of
          transferring distribution system loss to end-users.

      In reviewing any rate application filed after the effectivity of this Act, the
Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) shall determine whether the requested
rate or revenue requirement directly or indirectly restores, replaces, or offsets
any prohibited system-loss recovery. Any amount determined to have such
purpose or effect shall be disallowed.

      SECTION 7. Consequential Treatment of Value-Added Tax. — No value-
added tax attributable to any distribution system-loss amount prohibited under
this Act shall be billed, collected, allocated, or passed on to an electricity end-
user.
      This section shall not be construed as granting a tax exemption for an
otherwise lawful charge. It recognizes that no taxable consumer charge exists
with respect to a distribution system-loss amount whose recovery is prohibited
by law. The Bureau of Internal Revenue shall issue the necessary conforming
rules on invoicing, accounting, tax adjustment, credit, and refund treatment
consistent with this Act.

      SECTION 8. Amendment of Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832. — Section
10 of Republic Act No. 7832, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Sec. 10. Distribution System Losses. — No private electric utility, electric
cooperative, government-owned utility, local government unit, or other
distribution utility shall directly or indirectly recover any technical or non-
technical distribution system loss from an electricity end-user.

       All distribution system losses shall be for the sole account of the
distribution utility or electric cooperative concerned and shall not constitute a
recoverable operating expense, pass-through cost, regulatory asset, or
component of any rate or charge imposed upon an end-user.

       The Energy Regulatory Commission shall establish, monitor, and enforce
progressively improving system-loss performance standards for both private
distribution utilities and electric cooperatives, considering differences in
customer density, geography, and network conditions between urban and rural
or provincial service areas. Such standards shall be used exclusively for
benchmarking, regulatory supervision, corrective action, incentives, and
sanctions and shall not constitute authority to recover any portion of distribution
system loss from consumers.

       Nothing in this section shall prevent a distribution utility or electric
cooperative from recovering directly from a person responsible for electricity
pilferage, illegal connection, meter tampering, fraud, or other unlawful acts in
accordance with this Act and other applicable laws."

      SECTION 9. Amendment of Section 11 of Republic Act No. 7832. — Section
11 of Republic Act No. 7832, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Sec. 11. Area of Coverage. — The prohibition established under Section
10 shall apply to all existing and subsequently added franchise, service, or
distribution areas of every private electric utility, electric cooperative,
government-owned utility, local government unit, and other distribution utility,
without distinction between urban and rural or provincial service areas."

      SECTION 10. Amendment of Section 25 of Republic Act No. 9136. Section
25 of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby amended to read as follows:
       "Sec. 25. Retail Rate. — The retail rates charged by distribution utilities
and electric cooperatives for the supply of electricity in their captive markets
shall be subject to regulation by the ERC based on the recovery of prudent and
reasonable economic costs actually incurred in providing electricity and
distribution services, or such other principles that promote efficiency,
affordability, reliability, and consumer protection.

       Distribution system loss shall not be considered a prudent, reasonable,
eligible, or recoverable economic cost and shall not be directly or indirectly
included in any retail rate, distribution charge, revenue requirement, rate base,
regulatory asset, adjustment mechanism, or other amount imposed upon an
end-user.

      Every distribution utility and electric cooperative shall identify and
segregate in its bills to end-users all components of the retail rate. No charge
shall be imposed or collected unless expressly authorized by law and approved
by the ERC."

      SECTION 11. Amendment of Section 43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136. — The
paragraph of Section 43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136 beginning with the words "To
achieve this objective and to ensure the complete removal of cross subsidies" is
hereby amended to read as follows:

      "To achieve this objective and ensure the efficiency of regulated entities,
the ERC shall establish, monitor, and enforce system-loss performance
standards based on load density, sales mix, cost of service, delivery voltage,
network configuration, geographic conditions, and other relevant technical
considerations, including standards appropriately calibrated for electric
cooperatives serving low-density, rural, or geographically isolated areas.

      Such standards shall be used solely for benchmarking, regulatory
supervision, corrective action, incentives, and sanctions. Under no circumstance
shall such standards authorize the recovery or pass-through of distribution
system loss to an end-user."

      Item (iii) of the same subsection is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "(iii) In determining eligible costs of services that may be passed on to end-
users, the ERC shall establish minimum efficiency performance standards for
transmission entities, distribution utilities, and electric cooperatives, including
system-loss performance, interruption-frequency rates, and collection efficiency:
Provided, That distribution system loss shall be excluded from all eligible or
recoverable costs and shall not be included in any rate, rate base, revenue
requirement, regulatory asset, or adjustment mechanism."
       SECTION 12. Authorized Distribution-System Modernization Investments.
— Nothing in this Act shall prevent a distribution utility or electric cooperative
from applying for the recovery of prudent and reasonable capital expenditures
actually incurred for the rehabilitation, modernization, safety, reliability,
efficiency, automation, digitalization, or expansion of its distribution system.

      No expenditure shall be recoverable unless the ERC determines, after
notice and hearing, that it:

      (a) Is necessary and directly related to the provision of distribution service;
      (b) Is technically and economically reasonable;
      (c) Has been procured through a transparent and competitive process,
          except when another mode is permitted and justified under applicable
          law;
      (d) Is used and useful in providing service to consumers;
      (e) Does not arise from negligence, mismanagement, regulatory
          noncompliance, or unjustified delay; and
      (f) Has not been recovered through another rate, subsidy, grant,
          insurance payment, or other source.

      The cost of electricity already lost within the distribution system shall not
be treated as a capital investment or modernization expenditure.

       SECTION 13. Distribution Efficiency Plans and Independent Audits. —
Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, every
distribution utility and electric cooperative shall submit to the ERC a five-year
Distribution Efficiency and System Loss Reduction Plan containing:

      (a) Its historical technical and non-technical system losses;
      (b) The identified causes of such losses;
      (c) An assessment of the condition of its distribution facilities;
      (d) Annual reduction targets and performance indicators;
      (e) Proposed rehabilitation, modernization, metering, and anti-pilferage
          measures; and
      (f) The corresponding implementation schedule.

      Each distribution utility and electric cooperative shall undergo an
independent energy and system-loss audit at least once every two (2) fiscal years.
The audit shall verify energy input, metered sales, technical and non-technical
losses, billing and accounting practices, metering accuracy, and compliance with
this Act.

      SECTION 14. Electric-Cooperative Modernization and Transition
Assistance. — The Department of Energy and the National Electrification
Administration shall establish a program providing qualified electric cooperatives
with appropriate technical, institutional, and financial assistance for:
      (a) Rehabilitation and modernization of distribution facilities;
      (b) Replacement of obsolete conductors, transformers, meters, and other
          equipment;
      (c) Installation of advanced metering, automation, and loss-detection
          systems;
      (d) System planning, network reconfiguration, and load management;
      (e) Anti-pilferage and meter-integrity programs;
      (f) Personnel training and institutional-capacity development; and
      (g) Preparation and implementation of Distribution Efficiency and System
          Loss Reduction Plans.

       Given that electric cooperatives serve the overwhelming majority of rural
and provincial electricity consumers and, prior to the effectivity of this Act, were
permitted to pass through system loss at nearly half again the rate allowed
private distribution utilities, priority and proportionally greater funding under
this program shall be given to electric cooperatives serving geographically
isolated,   disaster-prone,     low-density,    underserved,    or     economically
disadvantaged provincial areas.

       Assistance under this section shall not authorize the recovery of
distribution system loss from consumers.

      SECTION 15. Transition and Existing Rate Orders. — The complete
prohibition against billing or collecting distribution system loss and the value-
added tax attributable thereto shall begin with the first billing cycle commencing
ninety (90) days after the effectivity of this Act.

      Within the same period, the ERC shall:

      (a) Identify and remove all system-loss factors from approved retail rates,
          distribution wheeling charges, revenue requirements, rate bases,
          adjustment mechanisms, and regulatory accounts;
      (b) Require the revision of billing, accounting, and information systems;
      (c) Prescribe uniform accounting rules separating distribution system loss
          from recoverable costs; and
      (d) Issue the provisional or final rate adjustments necessary to implement
          this Act.

      During the transition period, no existing system-loss cap may be
increased, and no new or expanded system-loss recovery mechanism may be
approved. All existing tariffs, rate orders, methodologies, and regulatory
issuances shall be deemed modified on the Prohibition Date to the extent
necessary to conform with this Act.
       SECTION. 16. Billing Transparency, Refunds, and Bill Credits. — Every
electricity bill shall contain a certification, in a form prescribed by the ERC, that
no distribution system-loss charge or value-added tax attributable thereto has
been billed, embedded, allocated, or collected from the consumer.

       Any amount collected in violation of this Act after the Prohibition Date
shall be refunded to the affected consumer or credited against succeeding
electricity bills.

      The refund or bill credit shall include:

      (a) the unlawfully collected principal amount;
      (b) Any value-added tax or other amount collected thereon; and
      (c) Interest at the legal rate from the date of collection until full refund or
          credit.

        No refund, bill credit, administrative fine, litigation expense, or other
liability arising from a violation of this Act shall be included in any regulated rate
or passed on to consumers.

       SECTION. 17. Public Distribution-Utility and Electric-Cooperative
Performance Scorecards. — The ERC shall prepare and publish annually, in an
accessible and machine-readable format, a performance scorecard for every
distribution utility and electric cooperative.

      Each scorecard shall contain:

      (a) the utility's or cooperative's technical, non-technical, and total
          distribution system-loss percentages;
      (b) The national average and the average for utilities or cooperatives with
          comparable operating conditions, disaggregated between private
          distribution utilities and electric cooperatives and, where practicable,
          between urban and provincial service areas;
      (c) The applicable statutory prohibition and regulatory performance
          standard;
      (d) The reduction or increase in system loss from the preceding year;
      (e) The amount of refunds or bill credits ordered and implemented;
      (f) Interruption-frequency, interruption-duration, service-quality, and
          other relevant reliability indicators;
      (g) The status of approved modernization and loss-reduction projects,
          including assistance extended under Section 14 of this Act;
      (h) Enforcement actions and penalties imposed; and
      (i) The utility's or cooperative's comparative ranking among similarly
          situated distribution utilities or electric cooperatives.
      The ERC shall ensure that differences in customer density, geography,
voltage level, sales mix, network configuration, and other material operating
conditions are clearly disclosed when making comparative rankings.

      SECTION 18. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — It shall be unlawful for any
person or entity to:

      (a) Bill, collect, allocate, embed, or recover distribution system loss from
          an end-user;
      (b) Conceal, rename, or misclassify distribution system loss as another
          rate or billing component;
      (c) Increase another charge for the purpose of replacing or offsetting
          prohibited system-loss recovery;
      (d) Bill or collect value-added tax attributable to a prohibited system-loss
          amount;
      (e) Submit false, incomplete, or misleading system-loss, metering, energy-
          input, sales, or billing information;
      (f) Obstruct an audit, inspection, investigation, or lawful order of the ERC;
          or
      (g) Knowingly authorize, assist, tolerate, or conceal any violation of this
          Act.

      After notice and hearing, the ERC may order refunds, bill credits,
disgorgement, cost disallowance, corrective action, suspension or revocation
proceedings, cease-and-desist measures, and the fines or penalties authorized
under Section 46 of Republic Act No. 9136 and other applicable laws.

      Each billing cycle containing a prohibited charge may be treated as a
separate violation.

      SECTION 19. Annual Report to Congress. — The ERC, in coordination with
the Department of Energy and the National Electrification Administration, shall
submit an annual report to the appropriate committees of the Senate and the
House of Representatives containing:

      (a) Nationwide and utility-specific system-loss performance, disaggregated
          between private distribution utilities and electric cooperatives;
      (b) The amounts removed from approved electricity rates pursuant to this
          Act;
      (c) Refunds and bill credits ordered and implemented;
      (d) Enforcement actions and penalties imposed;
      (e) Modernization investments approved by the ERC;
      (f) Assistance extended to electric cooperatives under Section 14 of this
          Act, disaggregated by province; and
      (g) Recommendations for further legislative or administrative action.
      SECTION 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the
Department of Energy, National Electrification Administration, Bureau of
Internal Revenue, distribution utilities, electric cooperatives, consumer
organizations, and other concerned stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation.

      The implementing rules shall prescribe the procedures and standards for
detecting indirect recovery or offset through future rate applications. Failure to
issue the implementing rules within the prescribed period shall not delay the
Prohibition Date or suspend the enforceability of this Act.

      SECTION 21. Separability. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the other
provisions hereof.

      SECTION 22. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, and issuances
or portions thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 23. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
