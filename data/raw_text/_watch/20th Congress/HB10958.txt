                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
        The privilege of owning or possessing a firearm carries a corresponding duty
to keep it secure and beyond the reach or control of unauthorized persons. A firearm
that is carelessly stored, left unattended, or otherwise inadequately safeguarded may
be lost, taken, accessed, or used by another person, creating a grave and preventable
risk to public safety.

        Republic Act No. 10591, otherwise known as the “Comprehensive Firearms
and Ammunition Regulation Act,” regulates the licensing, possession, transfer, and
reporting of firearms. The law, however, does not establish a clear and direct criminal
offense that focuses specifically on negligent failure to safeguard a firearm before any
unlawful transfer or failure to report occurs.

        This measure therefore creates two separate criminal acts. First, a licensed
firearm holder who, through negligence, fails to safely secure or safeguard a firearm
shall be punished by imprisonment of two (2) years. Second, when the firearm
negligently left unsecured or inadequately safeguarded is subsequently used in the
commission of a crime, the firearm holder shall be punished by imprisonment of five
(5) years.

        The bill further recognizes the heightened duty borne by public officers and
employees who are entrusted with government-issued firearms by reason of their
office, position, or employment. Because such firearms are public property placed in
their custody for an official purpose, the penalties are increased when the negligent
firearm holder is a public officer or employee and the firearm involved is
government-issued. Under this measure, the corresponding penalties are four (4) years
for negligent failure to safeguard a government-issued firearm and ten (10) years
when that firearm is subsequently used in the commission of a crime.

       The proposed offenses are directed at negligent custody and safekeeping. They
do not diminish the criminal liability of the person who unlawfully takes, possesses,
or uses the firearm, and they do not prevent prosecution under other applicable laws
where the facts independently establish another offense.
         The measure likewise protects lawful defensive access to and use of a firearm
during an emergency. The mere fact that a spouse, relative, household member, guest,
or other person gains access to a firearm in order to defend the firearm holder, another
person, or the dwelling against an actual or imminent unlawful attack shall not, by
itself, establish negligent safekeeping. Nor shall a firearm used under a justifying
circumstance recognized in Article 11 of the Revised Penal Code be deemed to have
been used in the commission of a crime for purposes of the enhanced offense under
this Act. This protection is intended to preserve the right of individuals to act lawfully
in defense of life and safety while maintaining accountability for genuinely negligent
firearm custody.

        By establishing clear duties and proportionate penalties, this measure seeks to
promote responsible firearm ownership, strengthen accountability among public
officers entrusted with government firearms, and reduce the risk that negligently
secured firearms will be used to endanger life, property, and public order.

       Approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
ESTABLISHING CRIMINAL LIABILITY FOR THE NEGLIGENT FAILURE
TO SAFEGUARD FIREARMS, PROVIDING ENHANCED PENALTIES
WHEN SUCH FIREARMS ARE SUBSEQUENTLY USED IN THE
COMMISSION OF A CRIME OR WHEN THE FIREARM INVOLVED IS
GOVERNMENT-ISSUED, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC
ACT NO. 10591, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “COMPREHENSIVE
FIREARMS AND AMMUNITION REGULATION ACT”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Responsible Firearm
Ownership Accountability Act.”

       Section 2. Insertion of New Sections 40-A and 40-B in Republic Act No.
10591. New Sections 40-A and 40-B are hereby inserted after Section 40 of Republic
Act No. 10591, to read as follows:

        “Section 40-A. Negligent Failure to Safeguard a Firearm. - Any licensed
firearm holder who, through negligence, fails to safely keep, secure, store, retain
custody of, or otherwise safeguard a firearm under his or her possession or control,
thereby allowing the firearm to be lost, taken, accessed, possessed, or controlled by an
unauthorized person, shall be guilty of negligent failure to safeguard a firearm and
shall be punished by imprisonment of two (2) years.

       For purposes of this section, negligence exists when the firearm holder fails to
exercise the degree of care that a reasonably prudent firearm holder would exercise
under the same or similar circumstances, taking into account the nature of the firearm,
the place where it is kept, the persons who may reasonably gain access to it, and
applicable firearm safekeeping requirements.

        If the offender is a public officer or employee and the firearm involved is
owned by the Government and was issued or entrusted to him or her by reason of
office, position, employment, assignment, or official duty, the penalty shall be
imprisonment of four (4) years.
        For purposes of this section, the access, possession, control, or use of a firearm
by a person other than the licensed firearm holder shall not, by itself, constitute
evidence of negligent failure to safeguard the firearm when such access, possession,
control, or use occurs under circumstances justified under Article 11 of the Revised
Penal Code, including lawful self-defense, defense of a relative, defense of a stranger,
avoidance of a greater evil or injury, or any other applicable justifying circumstance
recognized by law.

        No liability under this section shall arise solely from the fact that a spouse,
relative, household member, guest, or other person accessed or used the firearm when
such access or use was reasonably necessary in response to an actual or imminent
unlawful aggression or other emergency recognized by law.

       The absence of the licensed firearm holder’s prior knowledge, express consent,
or authorization shall not, by itself, render such access, possession, control, or use
unauthorized where the same is otherwise lawful or legally justified.”

       “Section 40-B. Negligent Failure to Safeguard a Firearm Subsequently Used
in the Commission of a Crime. - Any licensed firearm holder who commits the
negligent act defined in Section 40-A, and, as a direct and reasonably foreseeable
consequence of such negligent failure to safeguard, the firearm thereby lost, taken,
accessed, possessed, or controlled by an unauthorized person is subsequently used in
the commission of a felony under the Revised Penal Code or an offense punishable
under a special law, other than an offense consisting solely of the unlawful possession,
custody, control, carrying, or acquisition of the firearm itself, shall be guilty of
negligent failure to safeguard a firearm subsequently used in the commission of a
crime and shall be punished by imprisonment of five (5) years.

        If the offender is a public officer or employee and the firearm involved is
owned by the Government and was issued or entrusted to him or her by reason of
office, position, employment, assignment, or official duty, the penalty shall be
imprisonment of ten (10) years.

         For purposes of this section, a firearm shall not be considered to have been
used in the commission of a crime when the use thereof is justified under Article 11 of
the Revised Penal Code, including lawful self-defense, defense of a relative, defense
of a stranger, avoidance of a greater evil or injury, or any other applicable justifying
circumstance recognized by law. The fact that a person other than the licensed firearm
holder accessed or used the firearm during such justified emergency shall not, by
itself, establish liability under this section.

        For purposes of this section, the subsequent criminal act must be an offense
separate and distinct from the mere unlawful possession, custody, control, carrying, or
acquisition of the firearm. The fact that an unauthorized person unlawfully possesses
or carries the firearm, without more, shall not satisfy the requirement that the firearm
was subsequently used in the commission of a crime. Furthermore, when a firearm
registered to or under the lawful custody or control of a licensed firearm holder is
proven to have been used by an unauthorized person in the commission of a felony
under the Revised Penal Code or an offense punishable under a special law, such fact
shall give rise to a disputable presumption that the firearm holder negligently failed to
safely keep, secure, store, retain custody of, or otherwise safeguard the firearm. The
firearm holder may rebut such presumption by presenting competent evidence that he
or she exercised the degree of care required of a reasonably prudent firearm holder
under the same or similar circumstances, or that the loss, taking, access, possession, or
use of the firearm resulted from robbery, theft involving force or violence, force
majeure, lawful emergency access, a justifying circumstance under Article 11 of the
Revised Penal Code, or another circumstance beyond his or her reasonable control
despite the exercise of reasonable and lawful safeguards. Nothing in this provision
shall dispense with the constitutional requirement that the prosecution establish the
accused’s guilt beyond reasonable doubt.

        Liability under this section shall be in lieu of, and not in addition to, liability
under Section 40-A for the same act of negligent safekeeping. Nothing in this section
shall require that the firearm holder participated in, intended, or agreed to the
subsequent crime; it shall be sufficient that the prosecution proves beyond reasonable
doubt the negligent failure to safeguard the firearm and its qualifying subsequent use
in the commission of a separate crime as provided herein.

        Moreover, any member of The Armed Forces of the Philippines, The
Philippine National Police and any law enforcement agencies who is a licensed holder
of a firearm owned by the national government, who, through fault or negligence
appropriates, takes, misappropriates, or consents to the taking by another person of his
or her issued firearm, shall, in addition to the penalties prescribed under this Act, be
criminally charged with the crime of malversation of public property under Article
217 of the Revised Penal Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 1060.”

       Section 3. Circumstances Relevant to Negligent Safekeeping. In determining
whether a firearm holder negligently failed to safeguard a firearm, the court may
consider, among others, whether the firearm was:

       (a) left loaded and unsecured in a place reasonably accessible to a minor or to
       a person not legally authorized to possess a firearm;

       (b) left unattended in a motor vehicle, residence, workplace, public place, or
       other location without reasonable security measures;

       (c) stored or carried in violation of safekeeping or storage requirements
       prescribed by law, regulation, or lawful official policy;

       (d) left accessible to a person whom the firearm holder knew or reasonably
       should have known was intoxicated, under the influence of dangerous drugs,
       mentally incapable of safely handling a firearm, or otherwise legally
       disqualified from possessing one; or

       (e) otherwise kept in a manner that created an unreasonable risk of
       unauthorized access, possession, loss, or use.
        The circumstances listed above are evidentiary considerations only. The fact
that another person was able to obtain access to the firearm during an emergency for a
lawful defensive purpose shall not, standing alone, constitute proof of negligence.

         Section 4. Emergency Defensive Access, Justified Use, and Other
Exceptions. - No criminal liability under Sections 40-A or 40-B shall arise solely
from the fact that a spouse, relative, household member, guest, or other person
obtained access to or possession of the firearm when such access or possession was
reasonably necessary to defend the firearm holder, another person, or the dwelling
against an actual or imminent unlawful attack, or to respond to another emergency
involving an immediate threat of death or serious physical injury. In determining
liability, the court shall consider the totality of the circumstances, including whether
the firearm holder intentionally, recklessly, or negligently made the firearm available
for an unlawful purpose and whether reasonable safeguards were maintained before
the emergency arose.

        The mere emergency access to or justified defensive use of the firearm by a
person other than the licensed firearm holder shall not create a presumption of
negligent safekeeping. Nothing in this provision shall be construed to exempt any
person from criminal liability for a use of the firearm that is not justified by law.
        For purposes of Section 40-B, a use of the firearm falling within any justifying
circumstance under Article 11 of the Revised Penal Code shall not constitute use of
the firearm “in the commission of a crime” under this Act.

        No criminal liability under Sections 40-A or 40-B shall likewise attach where
the loss, taking, or access resulted from robbery, theft involving force or violence,
force majeure, or another circumstance beyond the firearm holder’s reasonable
control, provided that reasonable and lawful measures had been taken to safeguard the
firearm before the occurrence.

       Section 5. Separate Liability of the Principal Offender. The liability imposed
under this Act shall be separate and distinct from the criminal liability of any person
who unlawfully takes, possesses, carries, transfers, or uses the firearm. The
prosecution of the firearm holder under this Act shall not preclude the prosecution of
any other person for an offense arising from the same incident.

        Section 6. Other Criminal, Civil, and Administrative Liability. Nothing in
this Act shall preclude prosecution or liability under the Revised Penal Code,
Republic Act No. 10591, anti-graft laws, civil service laws, military or police
regulations, or other applicable laws and rules when the facts independently establish
the elements of another offense or ground for civil or administrative liability. No
person shall, however, be punished twice for the same offense in violation of the
Constitution.

       Section 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Philippine National Police, in coordination with
the Department of Justice, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

       Section 8. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this Act shall not be affected thereby.
        Section 9. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations
or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

      Section 10. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
