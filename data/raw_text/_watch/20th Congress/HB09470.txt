                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

In recent years, more and more Filipinos have been gaining international recognition
for their accomplishments in various sports events. Their wins are no longer isolated
victories but a testament to Filipinos’ hope, dedication, and perseverance. Notably,
Carlos Yulo, Aira Villegas, and Nesthy Petecio took home Olympic medals in the 2024
Summer Olympics in Paris. In the 2025 SEA Games, the Philippines won a total of
277 medals,1 with medalists including Alex Eala, EJ Obiena, and Kayla Sanchez.

Watching these athletes compete to the best of their abilities and gain recognition for
their country has brought Filipinos together. Ordinary Filipinos and even various
private corporations have rallied behind these athletes who continue to bring pride to
the country.2

In recognition of their contribution to national unity, Republic Act No. 10699 or the
National Athletes and Coaches Benefits and Incentives Act was enacted. This law
grants benefits and privileges to registered national athletes and coaches, such as
discounts for transportation services, hotels, and restaurants, grants free medical and

athletes who win gold, silver, and bronze medals, and to their coaches. Meanwhile,
the National Internal Revenue Code exempts from income tax “all prizes and awards
granted to athletes in local and international sports competitions and tournaments
whether held in the Philippines or abroad and sanctioned by their national sports
associations.”4 These laws show the State’s intent to enable athletes to enjoy the
benefits of their hard work, and to incentivize sports in the country, consistent with the
constitutional mandate to give priority to sports to foster patriotism and nationalism.5

However, there remain gaps in our laws. The income tax exemption does not cover
prizes and awards received by coaches of the athletes. Donations granted to said
coaches are also subject to donor’s tax. Additionally, the tax exemptions for athletes
only apply to prizes and awards and are thus contingent upon winning. This overlooks
the effort and sacrifice of athletes who compete but do not secure a podium finish, and
fails to encourage support for athletes during their training stages.

This bill therefore seeks to expand the exemptions provided by current law. It seeks
to exempt from national or local taxes any and all incentives, rewards, bonuses,
donations, gifts, endowments, and contributions received by either competing or
winning athletes and their coaches, whether from public or private persons. This
measure also incentivizes contributing to the athlete’s training, as it provides that
donations made prior to the competition and exclusively used to fund training and
competition-related expenses will be considered as allowable deductions from the
gross income of the donor. While we rightfully reward results, we also seek to nurture
the training and development of our athletes.

Our athletes and their coaches are the embodiment of hope. In every competition or
tournament, they carry with them their own dreams and their nation’s hope and pride.
In turn, we provide them with the unwavering support they need as we encourage the
next generation of Filipino athletes and coaches to continue on.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. A new section, designated as Section 4-A, is hereby inserted in Republic
Act No. 10699, otherwise known as the “National Athletes and Coaches Benefits and
Incentives Act”, to read as follows:

      SEC. 4-A. EXEMPTION FROM TAXES OF INCENTIVES, REWARDS,
      BONUSES AND OTHER FORMS OF EMOLUMENTS RECEIVED BY
      COMPETING OR WINNING NATIONAL ATHLETES AND COACHES. –
      ALL INCENTIVES, REWARDS, BONUSES AND OTHER FORMS OF
      EMOLUMENTS, AS WELL AS DONATIONS, GIFTS, ENDOWMENTS
      AND CONTRIBUTIONS, WHETHER FROM PUBLIC OR PRIVATE
      PERSONS OR ENTITIES, RECEIVED BY NATIONAL ATHLETES WHO
      COMPETE OR WIN IN INTERNATIONAL SPORTS COMPETITIONS AS
      DEFINED IN THIS ACT, INCLUDING THOSE RECEIVED BY THEIR
      RESPECTIVE COACHES, SHALL BE EXEMPT FROM ANY AND ALL
      NATIONAL OR LOCAL TAXES, FEES AND CHARGES, SUBJECT TO
      RULES AND REGULATIONS TO BE ISSUED BY THE SECRETARY OF
      FINANCE, AS RECOMMENDED BY THE COMMISSIONER OF
      INTERNAL REVENUE: PROVIDED, HOWEVER, THAT DONATIONS,
      GIFTS, ENDOWMENTS AND CONTRIBUTIONS MADE PRIOR TO THE

       COMPETITION ITSELF AND DIRECTLY AND EXCLUSIVELY USED TO
       FUND TRAINING AND COMPETITION-RELATED EXPENSES, AS WELL
       AS INCENTIVES, REWARDS, BONUSES AND OTHER FORMS OF
       EMOLUMENTS RECEIVED BY COMPETING OR WINNING NATIONAL
       ATHLETES AND COACHES, SHALL BE CONSIDERED AS ALLOWABLE
       DEDUCTIONS FROM GROSS INCOME FOR PURPOSES OF
       COMPUTING THE TAXABLE INCOME OF THE DONOR IN
       ACCORDANCE WITH THE PROVISIONS OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL
       REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED.

       THE EXEMPTION FROM DONOR’S TAX AND DEDUCTIBILITY FROM
       GROSS INCOME PROVIDED HEREIN MAY BE AVAILED OF WITHIN
       ONE (1) YEAR BEFORE THE COMMENCEMENT OF THE
       INTERNATIONAL SPORTS COMPETITION WHERE THE NATIONAL
       ATHLETE WILL COMPETE IN, AS CERTIFIED BY THE RESPECTIVE
       BODIES THAT ACCREDITED THEM AND THEIR COACHES, WHICH
       MAY BE THE PHILIPPINE SPORTS COMMISSION (PSC), PHILIPPINE
       OLYMPIC COMMITTEE (POC), OR THE PHILIPPINE PARALYMPIC
       COMMITTEE (PPC), AND WITHIN THREE (3) MONTHS AFTER THE
       INTERNATIONAL SPORTS COMPETITION: PROVIDED, THAT THE
       REQUIRED PERIOD TO AVAIL THE TAX EXEMPTION UNDER THIS
       PARAGRAPH SHALL NOT APPLY TO ALL DONATIONS, GIFTS,
       ENDOWMENTS AND CONTRIBUTIONS FOR FUND TRAINING AND
       COMPETITION-RELATED EXPENSES OF NATIONAL ATHLETES
       COURSED THROUGH THE PSC, POC, AND PPC.

       THE EXEMPTIONS AND PRIVILEGES PROVIDED HEREIN SHALL TAKE
       EFFECT ON JANUARY 1, 2027 AND SHALL NOT BE AVAILED IN LIEU
       OF ANY TAX EXEMPTION OR PRIVILEGE GRANTED UNDER EXISTING
       LAWS.”

SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within thirty (30) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance shall, upon recommendation by the
Bureau of Internal Revenue, promulgate rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act: Provided, That failure to promulgate said rules and
regulations shall not prevent the implementation of the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 3. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with any provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly

SEC. 4. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
