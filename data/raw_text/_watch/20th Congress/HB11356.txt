                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

           Access to clean, safe, accessible, and well-maintained toilet and
      sanitation facilities is a basic public-health and public-dignity
      concern. Yet in many public places, including markets, parks,
      transport areas, tourist destinations, and government centers,
      adequate sanitation facilities remain insufficient, inaccessible, poorly
      maintained, or unavailable to the public.

            The absence of reliable public sanitation facilities can contribute
      to poor hygiene, environmental contamination, open defecation, and
      preventable health and safety risks. It disproportionately affects
      children, senior citizens, persons with disabilities, pregnant person,
      persons with mobility limitations, workers, commuters, and other
      vulnerable members of the public.

           While existing laws provide sanitation and accessibility
      requirements for particular establishments and facilities, there
      remains a need for a coordinated framework that specifically addresses
      public access to sanitation facilities across high-use public places.

            This measure therefore mandates the provision, maintenance, or
      designation of compliant public toilets and sanitation facilities;
      establishes minimum standards for cleanliness, water, handwashing,
      safety, privacy, accessibility, and gender responsiveness; and requires
      local government units to prepare Public Sanitation Access Plans
      identifying existing facilities, access gaps, priority areas, maintenance
      responsibilities, and financing options.

             The measure also promotes the use and upgrading of existing
      facilities before new construction, provides for phased implementation,
      and establishes coordination among the Department of Health, local
      government units, and other concerned agencies. It further
      harmonizes the measure with existing sanitation and accessibility
laws, including Republic Act No. 11311, to avoid duplication and
double penalties.

       The enactment of this measure will strengthen the country's
sanitation system by ensuring that the public can reasonably access
facilities that are not only available, but also safe, clean, accessible,
functional, and worthy of human dignity.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                               “AN ACT
            MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF ACCESSIBLE,
            SAFE, CLEAN, GENDER-RESPONSIVE, AND WELL-
            MAINTAINED PUBLIC TOILETS AND SANITATION
            FACILITIES  IN   PUBLIC   PLACES,  TRANSPORT
            TERMINALS, TOURIST AREAS, MARKETS, PARKS, AND
            GOVERNMENT CENTERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

             Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
      Philippines in Congress assembled:

             SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Public
      Toilet and Sanitation Access Act".

            SECTION 2. Declaration of State Policy. - It is the policy of the
      State to protect and promote the right to health and ensure dignified,
      equitable, safe, clean, accessible, gender-responsive, and inclusive
      access to public toilets and sanitation facilities in public places and
      other areas where people regularly congregate, work, travel, transact,
      or obtain public services.

            The State recognizes adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities
      as essential to public health, environmental protection, human dignity,
      accessibility, public safety, and local development, with particular
      consideration for children, senior citizens, persons with disabilities,
      pregnant person, persons with mobility limitations, and other
      vulnerable groups.

            SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

            a) “Public Toilet” refers to a toilet, restroom, comfort room, or
               similar facility intended or designated for public use, whether
        operated by a government entity or a private person or entity
        pursuant to law, regulation, agreement, or designation;

     b) “Sanitation Facility” refers to a facility or combination of
        facilities intended to support hygienic sanitation and public
        toilet use, including public toilets, handwashing facilities,
        water supply, sanitary waste receptacles, menstrual hygiene
        disposal facilities, and diaper-changing areas, where
        applicable;

     c) “Covered Public Place” includes public markets; transport
        terminals, stations, stops, and similar passenger facilities;
        parks and plazas; tourist areas; government centers and
        public service facilities; public hospitals; schools and other
        educational institutions when their facilities are made
        available for public events or public use, subject to applicable
        education and facility standards; and other high-use public
        areas identified pursuant to this Act and its implementing
        rules and regulations;

     d) “Accessible Facility” refers to a sanitation facility designed,
        constructed, equipped, operated, and maintained for safe and
        reasonably independent use by persons with disabilities,
        senior citizens, pregnant person, persons with mobility
        limitations, and other persons requiring accessible facilities,
        consistent with Batas Pambansa Blg. 344, Republic Act No.
        7277, as amended, and applicable standards; and

     e) “Operator” refers to any person or entity responsible, by
        ownership, law, regulation, contract, lease, franchise,
        agreement, or other lawful arrangement, for the
        administration, operation, management, or maintenance of a
        covered public place or sanitation facility.

      SECTION 4. Mandatory Public Toilet Access Program and
Minimum Sanitation Standards. - The following minimum standards
and requirements are hereby established:

     a) Local government units and operators of covered public places
        shall, within their respective powers and available resources,
        ensure reasonable public access to safe, clean, functional,
        accessible, and hygienic public toilets by providing,
        maintaining, or designating existing compliant sanitation
        facilities, taking into account population, pedestrian or user
        volume, operating hours, the nature of the area, geographic
        conditions, and existing facilities;
b) Without prejudice to applicable standards under Presidential
   Decree No. 856, public toilets and sanitation facilities shall,
   as applicable, provide adequate and safe water; functional
   toilets and handwashing facilities with soap or appropriate
   cleansing agents; adequate lighting and ventilation; privacy
   and safety features; sanitary waste receptacles; menstrual
   hygiene disposal facilities; appropriate diaper-changing
   facilities where necessary; accessibility features consistent
   with applicable laws; appropriate signage; and regular
   cleaning, disinfection, repair, and maintenance;

c) Covered public places shall provide gender-responsive and
   inclusive sanitation facilities that respect privacy, dignity, and
   safety. Where appropriate, separate facilities for women and
   men and accessible facilities shall be provided;

d) Public toilets shall be reasonably available during the
   operating hours of the covered public place and shall not be
   unnecessarily locked or rendered unavailable. Temporary
   closure for cleaning, repair, emergency, or public safety
   purposes shall be allowed, provided alternative access is made
   available whenever practicable;

e) No fee shall be charged for ordinary use of a basic public toilet
   established, maintained, or designated by a government
   entity. Where a privately operated facility is designated as a
   required public sanitation facility, basic public access shall
   likewise be provided without charge, subject to applicable
   laws and regulations, including Republic Act No. 11311,
   otherwise known as “An Act to Improve Land Transportation
   Terminals, Stations, Stops, Rest Areas and Roll-On/Roll-Off
   Terminals, Appropriating Funds therefor, and for Other
   Purposes”;

f) Each covered local government unit shall, in accordance with
   the standards and timelines prescribed in the implementing
   rules and regulations, prepare and periodically update a
   “Public Sanitation Access Plan” identifying existing facilities,
   sanitation and accessibility gaps, priority and high-use areas,
   maintenance responsibilities, financing options, and
   measures for achieving reasonable public sanitation access;
   and

g) Existing compliant facilities shall, where feasible, be utilized,
   upgraded, rehabilitated, or designated before new facilities are
   constructed.
       SECTION 5. Implementing Agency. - The Department of Health
(DOH) shall be the lead implementing agency, without prejudice to the
specific regulatory and enforcement authority vested by existing laws
in other government agencies, and shall coordinate with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Department
of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), Department of Tourism (DOT),
Department of Transportation (DOTr), Department of Environment and
Natural Resources (DENR), National Commission on Disability Affairs
(NCDA), local government units, and other concerned agencies and
stakeholders.

      The DOH shall establish national minimum sanitation and
public-health standards and oversee implementation and monitoring,
while concerned agencies shall perform functions within their
respective mandates. The DILG shall assist in coordinating and
monitoring local government compliance with Public Sanitation Access
Plans.

       SECTION 6. Penalties and Remedies. - Any owner, operator,
administrator, or other person responsible for a covered public place or
sanitation facility who willfully or repeatedly fails to comply with this
Act, its implementing rules and regulations, or a lawful order issued
pursuant thereto shall, after due notice and opportunity to correct the
violation, be subject to administrative sanctions, including corrective
orders and other appropriate measures, as prescribed by the
implementing rules and regulations.

      Where a violation is separately punishable under the Code on
Sanitation of the Philippines, Batas Pambansa Blg. 344, Republic Act
No. 7277, as amended, Republic Act No. 11311, the National Building
Code, environmental laws, local ordinances, or other applicable laws,
the penalties and remedies provided therein shall likewise apply.

      For facilities covered by Republic Act No. 11311, the penalties
prescribed therein shall govern violations of requirements specifically
imposed by that Act. No person shall be subjected to duplicative
penalties under this Act and another law for the same act or omission
where the penalties arise from the same regulatory requirement.

      Any person may file a complaint concerning non-compliance
before the appropriate local health office or other office designated in
the implementing rules and regulations.

        SECTION 7. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the
initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current
appropriations of the concerned agencies and local government units,
insofar as authorized under existing budgeting, accounting, and
auditing laws.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act and appropriate local
government budgets, subject to existing laws, rules, regulations, and
fiscal capacity.

        Implementation shall be phased, giving priority to high-use,
underserved, sanitation-deficient, and vulnerable areas. Existing
facilities, rehabilitation, grants, donations, public-private partnerships,
and other lawful financing arrangements may be utilized where
appropriate.

     Nothing in this Act shall require an expenditure in excess of an
appropriation duly authorized by law.

      SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within
ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination
with concerned agencies, local government units, and relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
its implementation.

       The implementing rules and regulations shall establish the
national minimum standards and criteria for reasonable public
sanitation access; accessibility and gender-responsive requirements;
cleaning, maintenance, inspection, and operating standards; water,
wastewater, sewerage and septage requirements, consistent with
existing laws, standards, and regulatory requirements; procedures for
Public Sanitation Access Plans; designation of existing facilities;
complaints and enforcement; phased implementation; and inter-agency
coordination and monitoring.

       SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 10. Harmonization and Non-Repeal Clause. -
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, amend, diminish, or
impair the provisions of Presidential Decree No. 856, otherwise known
as the "Code on Sanitation of the Philippines"; Republic Act No. 7160,
otherwise known as the "Local Government Code of 1991"; Batas
Pambansa Blg. 344, otherwise known as the "Accessibility Law";
Republic Act No. 7277, as amended; Republic Act No. 11311, otherwise
known as “An Act to Improve Land Transportation Terminals, Stations,
Stops, Rest Areas and Roll-On/Roll-Off Terminals, Appropriating
Funds therefor, and for Other Purposes;” the National Building Code;
or other applicable laws and regulations.

       This Act shall be construed as supplemental to existing laws.
Facilities covered by Republic Act No. 11311 shall continue to comply
with the specific requirements and enforcement mechanisms provided
therein, while this Act shall apply to matters not specifically governed
by such law, insofar as consistent therewith.

       No provision of this Act shall be construed to authorize
duplicative penalties for the same act or omission where the penalties
arise from the same regulatory requirement.

      SECTION 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.

      Approved.
