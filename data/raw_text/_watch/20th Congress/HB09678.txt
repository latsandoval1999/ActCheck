                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

Known for its vast rice fields and various contribution to the field of agriculture, The
Province of Nueva Ecija has long since earned its title as the “Rice Granary of the
Philippines”. Now, it positions itself as a major contributor towards rice production
and food security nationwide.

However, its history is far from peaceful being deeply rooted in the courage, industry,
and the enduring spitir of its people. Historically, the province was established by
virtue of a superior decree issued under Governor-General Rafael María de Aguilar on
April 25, 1801. It was originally a corregimiento or a military district named after the
Spanish City of Écija being composed of Upper Pampanga and certain settlements of
Tayabas.

Various records has also shown its active participation in the fight for the country’s
independence and freedom from the Spanish reign. During 1896, thousands of Novo
Ecijanos took up arms and joined the revolution for the country’s freedom and
independence. This was later known as the “The First Cry of Nueva Ecija” or “Ang
Unang Sigaw ng Nueva Ecija”. This act of defiance against our oppressors became
the Province’ legacy. Being regarded as one of the first eight provinces to rise against
the Spanish reign, the Province is immortalized as one of the eight rays of the sun in
the Philippine National Flag.

Despite its valuable contributions then and now as well as its rich culture and history,
the province has yet to formally commemorate its true founding date. Realizing the
need to establish the true birth of the Province with both certainty and finality, a
historical research initiative was conducted from 2013 under the leadership of
Governor Aurelio “Oyie” Matias Umali.

After three years of arduous investigation, examination, and verification, from various
historical records, it has been confirmed that Nueva Ecija was indeed founded on
April 25, 1801. Subsequently, this finding was then confirmed by the National
Historical Commission on October 24, 2025 through its Chairperson, Regalado Trota
Jose, Jr. Later on, the Office of the President, by virtue of Proclamation No. 1211, s.

   AN ACT DECLARING APRIL 25 OF EVERY YEAR AS NUEVA ECIJA
       FOUNDING DAY, A SPECIAL NON-WORKING HOLIDAY IN THE
        PROVINCE OF NUEVA ECIJA, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


Be it enacted by the Congress of the Philippines:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Nueva Ecija Founding
Day”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Nueva Ecija Founding Day. — April 25 of every year
is hereby declared as Nueva Ecija Founding Day to commemorate the founding
anniversary of the Province of Nueva Ecija.


SECTION 3. Declaration of Special Non-Working Holiday. — April 25 of every
year is hereby declared a special non-working holiday in the Province of Nueva Ecija,
including all cities and municipalities therein allowing the people of the province to
fittingly celebrate and participate in commemorative, civic, historical, cultural,
educational, and tourism-related activities.


SECTION 4. Historical Significance. — The annual commemoration of Nueva
Ecija Founding Day shall honor the province’s history, cultural heritage, and the
sacrifices and contributions of its forebears, leaders, and people in preserving

SECTION 5. Lead Implementing Agency. — The Provincial Government of Nueva
Ecija, in coordination with the National Historical Commission of the Philippines, the
Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education, local government
units within the province, and other concerned national government agencies, may
lead, organize, and support appropriate programs and activities for the annual
celebration of Nueva Ecija Founding Day.


Such activities may include historical lectures, cultural presentations, civic
ceremonies, tourism promotions, educational programs, exhibits, and other activities
that promote awareness of Nueva Ecija’s founding, history, heritage, and
contributions to the nation.


SECTION 6. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
issuances, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.


SECTION 8. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
