                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Founded in 1907, Central Luzon State University (CLSU) is a premiere
comprehensive university with banner programs in agricultural science and technology,
and allied fields. The University’s sustained commitment to service and excellence is
reflected in the quality of instruction it offers and in the viable culture of research for
development it continuously nurtures.

       The Commission of Higher Education (CHED) identifies CLSU as a university
demonstrating excellent performance in areas of instruction, research and publication,
extension and linkages, and institutional qualifications hence declaring it as a Center of
Excellence (COE) in agriculture, agricultural engineering, biology, fisheries, teacher
education and veterinary medicine.

        CLSU’s membership to several prestigious international organizations of higher
education institutions like the ASEAN University Network (AUN), ASEAN
International Mobility for Students (AIMS), Association of Universities in Asia and the
Pacific (AUAP), University Mobility for Asia and the Pacific (UMAP), and Southeast
Asian University Consortium for Graduate Education in Agriculture and Natural
Resources (UC) as well as its inclusion in the Quacquarelli Symonds (QS) World
University Rankings 2021: Asia earn the University a global foothold which comes
with a challenge to constantly upgrade its processes to meet international standards and
to maintain its responsiveness amidst the evolving landscape of higher education in a
global perspective.

        Over the years, CLSU continues to implement research programs of high impact
and significance to the agriculture sector which is dubbed as the country’s backbone.
The CLSU - Tuklas Lunas Development Center (TLDC) is named by the DOST-
Philippine Council for Health Research and Development (PCHRD) as a leading center
in developing medicinal solutions through drug discovery using mushrooms. The
CLSU Nanotechnology R and D Program and Facility is the first nanotechnology
program with the state-of-the-art facility in the country that focuses on agri-fishery. The
Indoor Vertical Farming System for Year-Round Production of High Value Crops in
the Lowland Tropics is a cutting-edge technology that uses a controlled environment to
revolutionize vertical agriculture. This year alone, CLSU received funding support

                    House of Representatives
                           Quezon City, Metro Manila

from the Department of Agriculture to establish the Precision and Digital Agriculture
Center (PreDiCt) and the Center for Transboundary Animal Diseases (CenTrAD), the
country’s first and only center to handle transboundary animal diseases such as African
swine fever and avian flu, among others by developing technologies and tools for
accurate disease diagnosis and prevention, hence protecting the animal industry and the
health of the local economy.

        For the last few years, the collective efforts of CLSU researchers and the
University’s strong collaborations with its partner institutions yielded a conservative
estimate of ₱971.4 million worth of funding support for the implementation of cutting-
edge research and development projects. The unwavering trust of the partner agencies
helps translate the institution’s vision and mission into reality, thus propelling CLSU to
subscribe to an even greater organizational commitment for the people it serves. From
2023-2025, the target of CLSU to generate and disseminate knowledge and technology
was translated into fruitful outcomes: 244 research completed, 205 knowledge products
generated, 291 IPR applied, 32 technologies patented, 241 techno-demo sites
established, 27,032 individuals trained, 16,933 clients provided with technical advisory
and consultancy services, and 327 linkages established.

        The commitment of CLSU to contribute to the betterment of society remains
steadfast. A well-defined and sustainable allocation of resources for research and
development projects will put CLSU to be in a better position to fulfill its noble
mandates. A sustainable and stable financial funding ensures the sustainability of each
of the centers proposed herewith.

       In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  (CLSU) AS RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT REGIONAL ZONE AND
               APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Title. This Act shall be known as the “CLSU as Research and
Development Regional Zone Act”.

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to give
priority to research and development among others as provided for under Article XIV,
Section 10 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution:

“Science and technology are essential for national development and progress. The State
shall give priority to research and development, invention, innovation and their
utilization; and to science and technology education, training, and services. It shall
support indigenous, appropriate, and self - reliant scientific and technological
capabilities, and their application to the country’s productive systems and national life.

CLSU are hereby declared as Regional Centers:

3. REGIONAL CENTER FOR NANOTECHNOLOGY IN AGRICULTURE
AND FISHERIES

         The Center for Nanotechnology in Agriculture and Fisheries (proposed to be
named as Regional Center for Nanotechnology in Agriculture and Fisheries) plays a
vital and strategic role in advancing nanotechnology research, development, and
innovation (RDI) in the Philippines, particularly in support of sustainable agriculture,
fisheries, food systems, public health, and environmental protection. Initially
established as a Nanotechnology Research and Development Facility and subsequently
institutionalized as a Center, CeNAF is firmly anchored on the research, innovation,
and extension mandate of Central Luzon State University (CLSU), a premier state
university in Region III with long-standing strengths in agriculture, fisheries,
environmental science, and technology-based innovation.

       Nanotechnology is widely recognized as an enabling and cross-cutting
technology with transformative impacts on food production systems, natural resource
management, environmental protection, and human health. In response to both national
development priorities and regional needs in Central Luzon, CeNAF actively harnesses
nanotechnology applications across agriculture, fisheries, food processing, and
packaging to enhance productivity, sustainability, resilience to climate variability, and
food safety.

Core Research and Technology Development Areas

CeNAF’s research and development initiatives focus on the application of
nanotechnology in the following priority areas:

       a. Agriculture and Fisheries Productivity and Sustainability

               The Center develops nano-enabled tools such as nanosensors for real-
       time monitoring of soil, water, and crop conditions; rapid detection systems for
       plant and animal pathogens; and water quality monitoring technologies for
       inland fisheries and aquaculture systems. CeNAF also designs nanocapsules and
       nano-carriers for the efficient delivery of biofertilizers, biopesticides, vaccines,
       micronutrients, and feed additives for both terrestrial and aquatic organisms.

       b. Environmental Protection and Resource Management

              CeNAF conducts advanced research on nanomaterials for
       environmental remediation, including the application of quantum dots and
       photocatalytic nanomaterials for the degradation of persistent organic pollutants
                            Republic of the Philippines
                    House of Representatives
                           Quezon City, Metro Manila

       such as dyes commonly found in industrial wastewater. These studies
       demonstrate the potential of nanotechnology in addressing water pollution and
       supporting sustainable environmental management in agricultural and industrial
       landscapes.

       c. Food Processing, Packaging, and Food Safety

              The Center advances nano-enabled innovations in food systems,
       including nanoemulsions and nanoparticles to improve nutrient dispersion and
       bioavailability; nano-encapsulated nutraceuticals and natural flavor enhancers
       derived from agro-industrial by-products; and biodegradable nanosensors for
       monitoring temperature, moisture, and shelf life. CeNAF also supports the
       development of nanosmart packaging materials using agricultural wastes,
       nanoclays, and nanofilms to reduce spoilage, improve barrier properties, and
       enhance antimicrobial and antifungal performance.

Research Outputs and Commercialization Potential

        CeNAF has generated a diverse portfolio of nanotechnology-based products
with strong potential for technology transfer, business incubation, and
commercialization. These include, among others: silica, zinc oxide, copper oxide,
calcium oxide, gold, and cellulose-based nanoparticles; nano-zeolite composites; nano-
encapsulated anthocyanins and vitamins; nano-enhanced sanitizers and alcohol
formulations; nano-fiber filtration materials; nano-structured composites; green-
synthesized nanomaterials for pest management, herbicides, seed nano-priming, and
smart delivery of bioactive natural products; and nanofibers derived from agricultural
wastes for metal recovery and environmental applications. These outputs demonstrate
the Center’s capability to translate laboratory-scale research into scalable, application-
ready technologies responsive to regional and national needs.

Nano-enabled Technologies for Public Health and Emergency Response

        In response to urgent public health challenges, particularly during the COVID-
19 pandemic, CeNAF developed nano-enabled products such as nano-structured
alcohol, nano-enhanced sanitizers, and reusable nanofiber-based face masks with
improved filtration efficiency. These innovations underscore the Center’s capacity to
rapidly mobilize scientific expertise and infrastructure in support of national emergency
response and public health protection.

High-Impact Technologies for Agriculture and Fisheries

environmental monitoring, and nano-enhanced technologies for climate-resilient

4. REGIONAL CENTER FOR CLIMATE CHANGE AND ENVIRONMENTAL
MANAGEMENT

         The Center for Climate Change and Environmental Management (CCCEM),
being proposed as the Regional Center for Climate Change and Environmental
Management, leads the delivery of research, innovation, extension, and instruction
initiatives that address the pressing challenges of climate change and environmental
degradation.
                            Republic of the Philippines

        In Central Luzon, the Center for Climate Change and Environmental
Management is the only center that has a facility which helps in building resistance to
climate change threats using knowledge, tools, technologies and resources that are
environmentally friendly and ethical, ecologically sustainable, practical and affordable.
This conducts research activities about climate change impacts, vulnerability and risk
assessment of various municipalities and cities on the consequences of climate change.
It provides environmental education and training regarding climate change, the science
behind it, its impacts, adaptation and mitigation strategies. This center also trains the
local government units on the preparation of the Local Climate Change Adaptation Plan
(LCCAP) to enhance the resiliency of the local communities. Moreover, it is also
engaged with disaster risk reduction and management activities to develop and enhance
the knowledge and skills of the faculty, staff, students and all sectors of the society in
the region and even beyond.

        The center develops and enhances the local communities, local government
units, government agencies, private organizations, non-government organizations, and
people’s organizations in Region III into resilient, safe, healthy, thriving and
sustainable units and communities that are empowered, educated and aware. The
services mentioned do not only help the target beneficiaries but also their families and
the community where they belong. When these undertakings are sustained, more people
will become knowledgeable about climate change, climate change adaptation, green
technology, biodiversity conservation, and environmental education. It is also able to
provide assistance to national agencies through partnerships and collaborations.
Specifically, the achievement of a shared responsibility and collective action for
environmental management, biodiversity conservation, adaptation to climate change
impacts through partnerships among local communities, academe, and government will
make Central Luzon prepare, equip and sustainable.

        With more sustainable projects, the institute would be able to monitor possible
threats and problems in biodiversity and immediately develop solutions, formulate
more technologies aside from phytoremediation to address pollution, regularly monitor
changes that would be incorporated in various remote sensing studies and LiDAR
applications such as production of high value crops, irrigation assessment, aquaculture
production, forest protection and discovery of renewable energy sources, continue
conducting climate change studies in different parts of Central Luzon, and conduct
follow-up activities, seminars, workshops, and training regarding environmental
education.
