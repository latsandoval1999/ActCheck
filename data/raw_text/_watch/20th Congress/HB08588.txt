                              AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11053, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ANTI-
HAZING ACT OF 2018,” BY DECLARING HAZING RESULTING IN DEATH, RAPE,
SODOMY, OR MUTILATION AS A HEINOUS CRIME FOR PURPOSES OF LAWS
IMPOSING SPECIAL CONSEQUENCES ON HEINOUS OFFENDERS

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Hazing continues to cause serious harm to many young Filipinos. In some cases,
initiation rites have led to severe injuries, sexual abuse, and even death. No student
should have to suffer violence or humiliation just to belong to an organization. The
government has already taken a strong stand against this practice through the Anti-
Hazing Act of 2018 (Republic Act No. 11053), which prohibits hazing and imposes
heavy penalties on those who plan, carry out, or participate in such acts.

Under the current law, those responsible for hazing that results in death, rape,
sodomy, or mutilation can already face reclusion perpetua and a large ine. The
Fuertes v. Senate of the Philippines decision of the Supreme Court of the
Philippines also recognized that Congress may impose strict penalties to stop hazing
and protect the public. However, there is still a need to clearly classify these extreme
forms of hazing as heinous crimes, so that the law fully re lects the seriousness of
these acts and ensures that offenders face the strict consequences applied to the most
serious crimes.

This bill therefore seeks to amend the Anti-Hazing Act of 2018 by declaring hazing
a heinous crime when it results in death, rape, sodomy, or mutilation. This will not
change the existing penalties but will strengthen the law by ensuring that offenders

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Heinous Hazing
Consequences Act.”

SECTION 2. Amendment to Republic Act No. 11053. — Republic Act No. 11053 is
hereby amended by inserting a new section after Section 14 to read as follows:

     “SEC. 14-A. Heinous Crime Classi ication; Consequences. — Hazing, as
     de ined under this Act, shall be classi ied as a heinous crime when, as a
     consequence of the hazing, death, rape, sodomy, or mutilation results
     therefrom.

     For purposes of applying laws that impose special treatment, restrictions,
     or consequences on persons convicted of heinous crimes, hazing resulting
     in death, rape, sodomy, or mutilation shall be deemed a heinous crime.

     Accordingly, a person convicted under Section 14(a) of this Act shall be
     considered a high-level offender and a person deprived of liberty (PDL)

     convicted of a heinous crime, and shall be subject to the applicable
     provisions on custody, security classi ication, and con inement in separate
     facilities for persons convicted of heinous crimes.”

SECTION 3. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not otherwise affected shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 4. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 5. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
