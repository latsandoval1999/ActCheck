                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Nuestra Señora del Rosario de Manaoag, more widely known as “Our Lady of the
Rosary of Manaoag” and fondly called “Apo Baket” by the people of Pangasinan,
canonically crowned under Pope Pius XI, stands among the most cherished Marian
devotions in the Philippines. The sacred image, a seventeenth-century ivory statue
depicting the Blessed Virgin Mary holding the Child Jesus, was brought to the country
from Spain during the early Spanish colonial period through the Manila-Acapulco galleon
trade by Dominican missionaries. It is now enshrined at the high altar of the Minor Basilica
of Our Lady of the Rosary of Manaoag in Manaoag, Pangasinan, a shrine administered
by the Order of Preachers under the Archdiocese of Lingayen-Dagupan and recognized
today as one of the nation’s foremost pilgrimage destinations.

       Over the centuries, devotion to Our Lady of the Rosary of Manaoag has become
deeply woven into the religious and cultural fabric of Filipino life. For countless faithful,
she has served as a source of comfort, hope, and spiritual renewal. The enduring
reverence accorded to Apo Baket reflects the central role of Marian devotion in shaping
the values, traditions, and collective identity of many communities, particularly in Northern
Luzon.

       Traditionally, the “Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary of Manaoag” is observed every
third Wednesday of Easter. The celebration is marked by solemn religious rites and
communal gatherings that foster not only spiritual growth but also moral formation and
social solidarity among devotees.

      Declaring every third Wednesday of Easter as the “Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary
of Manaoag” accords due recognition to this long-standing religious and cultural
observance. Such recognition affirms the State’s respect for faith traditions as part of our
national heritage, while remaining consistent with the constitutional guarantee of religious
freedom.

       Beyond its spiritual significance, the formal acknowledgement of this feast may
likewise contribute to the promotion of cultural tourism, strengthening the Philippines’
standing as a destination for religious pilgrimage in Southeast Asia and supporting local
economies anchored on heritage and faith-based tourism.

      Ultimately, this proposed measure seeks to honor a sacred tradition while
recognizing its meaningful contribution to the spiritual and cultural life of the Filipino
people.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is respectfully urged.

           Introduced by Representative MARLYN L. PRIMICIAS-AGABAS

 AN ACT DECLARING EVERY THIRD WEDNESDAY OF EASTER AS THE “FEAST
             OF OUR LADY OF THE ROSARY OF MANAOAG”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. The third Wednesday of Easter is hereby declared a special working
holiday in the entire country in celebration of the Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary of
Manaoag. The day shall be known as the “Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary of Manaoag
Day.”

      SEC. 2. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
