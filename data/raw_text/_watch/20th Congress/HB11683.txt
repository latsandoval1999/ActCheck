                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Corals, in their healthiest forms, look vibrant, drab, and all spectrum of
wonderful colors. But in death, they turn completely white, a stark color that
stands out when blanketed by the dark sea. Many may see this as beautiful, but
stark white corals are already dead. The phenomena of corals turning completely
white is known as coral bleaching, and such change is otherwise terminal except
for the most rigorous of responses to save the coral in question.

      As an archipelagic country in Southeast Asia, the Philippines rests at the
center of global marine activity, a place known as the Coral Triangle. Together
with Indonesia, Malaysia, Timor-Leste, Papua New Guinea, and the Solomon
Islands, the country sits atop what is the busiest, liveliest, and most biodiverse
part of the ocean. We are quite literally at the heart of global marine life.

      But things are changing for the worst. And much too fast. As seas grow
warmer, and as the effects of climate change wreak havoc across all ecosystems,
our oceans and the life under our seas are struggling to stay alive. Corals are
load-bearing foundations for marine life. They help provide shelter, food, and
symbiotic relationships that have kept ocean biodiversity thriving and alive for
much of human history. Now they are under threat. More and more, we need
measures such as this to keep awareness high, to raise public consciousness,
and to ignite action for our struggling planet. The best time to act was yesterday.
The second-best time to act is now.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is urgently sought.

                          AN ACT
    DECLARING THE MONTH OF SEPTEMBER AS CORAL BLEACHING
                     AWARENESS MONTH

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Coral Bleaching
Awareness Month”.

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord
with the rhythm and harmony of nature. This likewise extends to the
safeguarding and protection of the country’s rich maritime resources and the
biodiversity that exists within such ecosystems.
      SECTION 3. Coral Bleaching Awareness Month. – September of every year
is hereby declared as “Coral Bleaching Awareness Month.” All schools and
relevant government offices shall ensure suitable celebration for the month-long
observation.
      This annual observance aims to:
      (a) Enhance public awareness and understanding of corals and the crucial
          role they play in ocean health and ecology;
      (b) Promote marine conservation, preservation, and protection among
          public and institutional consciousness;
      (c) Encourage coral health as a point of pride for the country’s maritime
          and archipelagic character, thereby strengthening opportunities for
          sustainable tourism, ecosystem restoration, and national pride.
      SECTION 4. Lead Agencies. – The Department of Agriculture – Bureau of
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DA-BFAR) shall, in collaboration with the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), Department of
Education (DepEd), and other relevant government agencies and non-
government organizations, lead the implementation of programs and activities in
observance of the “Coral Bleaching Awareness Month.”
       SECTION 5. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any provision or part
of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 7. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
