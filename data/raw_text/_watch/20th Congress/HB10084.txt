                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to protect consumers from ticket scalping and fraudulent
transactions. It prohibits the buying and selling of admission tickets through
unauthorized sales channels.

        Admission tickets are intended to provide the public with fair and equal access
to entertainment events. However, the growth of online marketplaces and digital
payment platforms has enabled unauthorized sellers to profit from ticket resale at the
expense of legitimate consumers. Tickets acquired through unauthorized channels are
often sold at steep prices, duplicated, or sometimes rendered invalid. It leaves buyers
vulnerable to fraud and denies genuine patrons the opportunity to purchase tickets at
their intended price.

       Ticket scalpers exploit high demand through bulk purchases which distorts the
market and creates a picture of fake scarcity. Instead of rewarding genuine patrons,
these practices inflate prices and divert sales away from authorized ticket distributors.

       Aside from prohibiting the unauthorized sale of admission tickets above their
face value, this proposed measure also restricts the use of automated software and other
schemes to unfairly acquire tickets for resale, and penalizes violations. It further
requires event organizers and ticket sellers to adopt stronger safeguards, provide
transparent pricing information, and implement measures that discourage ticket
scalping to protect the public from fraudulent transactions.

       Entertainment events should be enjoyed by the public, not exploited for
excessive profit. This measure ensures that admission tickets remain accessible through
legitimate channels, giving fans fair opportunities to attend events they support while
reinforcing public confidence in the ticketing system.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE SELLING AND BUYING OF ADMISSION TICKETS
THROUGH UNAUTHORIZED SALES CHANNELS, PROVIDING PENALTIES
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Ban on Ticket Scalping
(BTS) Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect the interests of consumers, to promote their general welfare, and to establish
standards of conduct for business and industry. Towards this end, the State shall
implement measures to protect consumers against deceptive, exploitative, and
unconscionable and unfair sales acts and practices.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Admission ticket refers to evidence of a right to entry to an entertainment
       event, whether printed or digital;

           b. Entertainment event refers to any occasion, occurrence, or activity
       organized by persons that provides amusement or recreation, is open to the
       public, and involves the distribution of admission tickets, such as, but not limited
       to, sporting events, concerts, stage performances, beauty pageants, festivals, road
       shows, fan meets, or any similar undertakings;

          c. Face value price refers to the original cost of the admission ticket set by
       the event producer and/or organizer; and

           d. Ticket scalping refers to the unauthorized acquisition, resale, or
       distribution of admission tickets at a price higher than their face value price.




                                              1
       SEC. 4. Prohibition of Ticket Scalping. – The following acts shall constitute ticket
scalping, whether committed online or in person:

           a.​ Offering, hoarding, selling, distributing, dealing in, disposing of, or
       otherwise obtaining admission tickets for entertainment events, without the
       written permission from the authorized event producer, organizer, or distributor,
       for the purpose of reselling at a price higher than the face value price: Provided,
       That reasonable service or transaction fees authorized by the organizers or
       authorized distributor shall not be deemed a violation of this Act;

          b.​ Financing, managing, or operating ticket scalping activities, including the
       usage of bot software to purchase admission tickets for the purpose of resale.

       SEC. 5. Other Offenses. – The following shall also constitute as offenses under this
Act:

         a.​ Offering or selling admission tickets for entertainment events without
       providing the face value price on each ticket;

          b.​ Selling admission tickets through unauthorized platforms for the purpose
       of resale in violation of this Act;

         c.​ Using bot software to purchase admission tickets in bulk;

         d.​ Willfully aiding or abetting in the commission of any of the offenses
       enumerated under Section 4 of this Act; and

         e.​ Willfully attempting to commit any of the offenses enumerated under
       Section 4.

      SEC. 6. Duties and Responsibilities of Entities Engaged in the Business of Event
Production and/or Event Ticketing. –

          a.​ Event producers and/or organizers shall ensure that events are run with
       due care and skill, and as described;

          b.​ Event ticket sellers shall ensure that the admission tickets match the
       description of the entertainment event, advertise the full face value price upfront,
       and provide a receipt for the ticket purchase;

           c.​ Covered entities shall post, publish, or display signs or notices on their
       websites and on conspicuous places within their premises warnings against
       ticket scalping; and

           d.​ Covered entities shall adopt, implement, and enforce internal policy
       guidelines, including prevention and reporting mechanisms, on ticket scalping,
       in accordance with this Act.




                                            2
       SEC. 7. Institution of Complaints. – Any aggrieved person may institute a
complaint for any violation of this Act with the Department of Trade and Industry
(DTI), through its relevant bureaus or attached agencies, the Philippine National Police
(PNP), the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), or other authorized law enforcement
agencies. The DTI, the PNP, the NBI, and other authorized law enforcement agencies
may investigate violations of this Act and institute a complaint before the appropriate
court.

      SEC. 8. Penalties. – The following penalties shall be imposed upon any person
found guilty of any act prohibited under:

          a.​ Sections 4 and 5 hereof:

                i.​ For the first offense, a fine of One hundred thousand pesos (Php
              100,000.00) or imprisonment of six (6) months, or both, at the discretion of
              the court;

                ii.​ For the second offense, a fine of Two hundred fifty thousand pesos
              (Php 250,000.00) or imprisonment of one (1) year, or both, at the discretion
              of the court; and

               iii.​ For the third and subsequent offenses, a fine of Five hundred
              thousand pesos (Php 500,000.00) or imprisonment of three (3) years, or
              both, at the discretion of the court.

          b.​ Section 6 hereof:

                i.​ For the first offense, a written warning and order to comply within
              ten (10) days from receipt thereof;

               ii.​ For the second offense, a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (Php
              50,000.00); and

               iii.​ For the third and subsequent offenses, a fine of One hundred
              thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) for each subsequent violation.

       If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, or other juridical entity,
the responsible directors, trustees, stockholders, members, officers, employees, or other
persons who authorized, directed, knowingly permitted, or participated in the
commission of the offense shall be held liable.

       SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DTI, in coordination with the
PNP, the NBI, and other relevant government agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) to carry out the provisions of
this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

      SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, other parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

      SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
