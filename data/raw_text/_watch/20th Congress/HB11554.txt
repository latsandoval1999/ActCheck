                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Every day, informal ambulant vendors earn a living by selling food,
goods, and other products, or by offering services, in streets, parks, terminals,
and other public spaces. They are part of the daily life of our communities and
contribute to our local economies. Yet, despite their important role, many of
them remain outside the reach of government programs and social protection
because they are not formally registered or recognized by the local
government units where they operate.
     Being part of the informal economy should not mean being invisible to
government.
       Informal ambulant vendors face unique challenges. They often have no
permanent place of business, limited access to social protection, and little
security in their livelihood. At the same time, their operations must also be
balanced with the legitimate concerns of consumers, communities, and local
governments, particularly on public safety, sanitation, traffic, and the proper
use of public spaces.
      This bill seeks to address these concerns by recognizing informal
ambulant vendors as legitimate participants in our local economy and by
providing a clear and simplified framework for their registration and
protection.




                                                                                  1
      The proposed Magna Carta of Informal Ambulant Vendors seeks to
protect and promote their rights to just and humane working conditions,
social protection, access to justice, and workplace security. It likewise
provides for a simplified registration process at the local level so that informal
ambulant vendors can be properly identified and connected to government
programs and services without being burdened by complicated procedures
and unnecessary fees.
      By formalizing the informal ambulant vending sector, this measure
seeks to ensure that these workers are no longer overlooked, but are instead
recognized as part of our communities and included in the social protection
and economic opportunities that government provides.
     In light of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                       AN ACT
                             PROVIDING FOR THE RIGHTS OF
                            INFORMAL AMBULANT VENDORS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta
 2      of Informal Ambulant Vendors.”

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
 4      State to:
 5             (a) Recognize the significant contribution of informal ambulant vendors to
 6                  the economy, to protect and promote the rights of every worker
 7                  including the right to self-organization, just and humane conditions of
 8                  work, access to social protection programs and services, access to
 9                  justice, and the right to security in the workplace;
10             (b) Promote a just and dynamic social order that shall ensure the prosperity
11                  and independence of the nation and free the people from poverty
12                  through policies that provide adequate social services, promote decent

 1             and environmentally sound and full employment, a rising standard of
 2             living, and an improved quality of life for all; and
 3          (c) Improve the quality of life of every Filipino as mandated by the
 4             Constitution.
 5          To this end, the State shall protect the informal ambulant vendors from
 6   being deprived of their livelihood as well as the rights of consumers and the
 7   general public from any negative effect that the vending industry may have on the
 8   environment and on societal welfare and well-being.
 9          SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover informal ambulant vendors who
10   are registered pursuant to this Act and not registered under any other law.
11          Informal ambulant vendors shall refer to individuals who sell goods or offer
12   non-professional services without a fixed business location, moving from place to
13   place, typically using portable means like carts and stands, and operate in public
14   spaces, such as streets and parks: Provided, That an individual who lends money
15   or extends any means of financial services to any person, whether occasionally or
16   habitually, as a source of income or livelihood shall not be deemed an ambulant
17   vendor.
18          SEC. 4. Registration of Informal Ambulant Vendors. – All informal
19   ambulant vendors (“IAVs”) shall register with the city, municipality, or barangay
20   where they are vending or intend to vend within the territorial jurisdiction of such
21   city, municipality, or barangay.
22          The registration of IAVs shall be simplified and shall not follow the regular
23   procedure in registering businesses and other commercial activities in the LGU.
24          In the registration of IAVs, the following documents shall be required:
25          (a) Valid government-issued identification card;
26          (b) Certification from the Punong Barangay attesting that the IAV is a
27             resident of the barangay, if applicable;




                                                                                       2
 1          (c) A duly accomplished Registration Form indicating the vending site
 2             where such IAV is vending or intends to vend; and
 3          (d) Certificate of Indigency.
 4          Within seven (7) days after submission of documents, the certificate shall
 5   be issued by the concerned LGU to the applicant IAV without charging any
 6   licensing, processing, or similar fee. The certificate shall be displayed by the IAV
 7   in a conspicuous position at their vending site.
 8          SEC. 5. Rights of Informal Ambulant Vendors. – IAVs shall have the
 9   following rights:
10             a) Right to renewal of registration as IAVs for such designated vending
11                 place, subject to said person’s continuing qualification as an IAV;
12             b) Right to security of tenure in the designated vending place as
13                 assigned by the concerned LGU;
14             c) Right to be recognized in other LGUs upon securing a prior
15                 certificate from the other LGU; and
16             d) Right to social protection and safety nets, labor market programs,
17                 and social welfare interventions such as social security, health care,
18                 and insurance.
19          SEC. 6. Local Inter-Agency Council on the Security and Protection of
20   Registered Informal Ambulant Vendors. – The City or Municipal Mayor shall
21   establish a Local Inter-Agency Council through an appropriate local ordinance
22   within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act. The council shall be
23   responsible for coordinating efforts to ensure the security and protection of
24   registered IAVs.
25          The Local Inter-Agency Council shall be headed by the City or Municipal
26   Administrator and shall be composed of the following:
27          a) Chairperson of the Sanggunian Committee on Market, Business, or
28             Trade;

 1         b) City or Municipal Planning and Development Officer;
 2         c) City or Municipal Market Administrator, or a person holding an
 3             equivalent position;
 4         d) Chief, City or Municipal Police;
 5         e) President, Liga ng mga Barangay;
 6         f) At least two (2) representatives from the City or Municipal Vendors
 7             Association; and
 8         g) In the case of Metropolitan Manila, a representative from the
 9             Metropolitan Manila Development Authority.
10         SEC. 7. Powers and Functions. – The Local Inter-Agency Council shall
11   perform the following functions:
12         a) Formulate plans, programs, projects or activities that shall ensure the
13             security of registered informal ambulant vendors in the workplace for
14             the approval of the Sanggunian;
15         b) Recommend appropriate measures to the City or Municipal Mayor and
16             the Sanggunian to enhance the participation of registered informal
17             ambulant vendors in local socio-economic development efforts;
18         c) Assist in conducting surveys on vacant areas for vending sites; and
19         d) Conduct regular inspections and monitor the compliance of registered
20             informal ambulant vendors to health and safety standards.
21         SEC. 8. Authority to Designate a Vending Site. – Pursuant to the
22   recommendation of the Local Inter-Agency Council, the concerned Sanggunian
23   shall designate a vending site through an ordinance. The use of a public park, city
24   or municipal street, or sidewalk for vending purposes shall be subject to the
25   provisions of Sections 16 to 21 of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the
26   “Local Government Code of 1991”, and Republic Act No. 4136, otherwise known
27   as the “Land Transportation and Traffic Code.”

 1          SEC. 9. Prior Consultation Required. – No vending site shall be
 2   designated unless prior consultation with IAVs, the local police, and the affected
 3   inhabitants is undertaken.
 4          SEC. 10. Policy on Relocation of Vending Sites. – Registered IAVs shall
 5   not be arbitrarily deprived of their vending sites, nor shall their stalls be
 6   demolished without due process of law.
 7          Pending the designation of a viable vending site, vendors occupying a
 8   public place not previously designed as a vending site shall be provided with a
 9   viable temporary vending site by the city or municipal government.
10          SEC. 11. Health and Safety Standards. – All registered IAVs shall comply
11   with basic health and safety standards to be established by the concerned LGU in
12   coordination with the Local Inter-Agency Council in accordance with RA 11058,
13   entitled “An Act Strengthening Compliance with Occupational Safety and Health
14   Standards and Providing Penalties for Violations Thereof.”
15          SEC. 12. Revocation of Certificate. – Certificates issued to IAVs shall be
16   revoked by the appropriate LGU in case of material misdeclaration or
17   misrepresentation in their documentary requirements: Provided, That the
18   concerned IAV shall be accorded due process.
19          SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
20   from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Department of the Interior and
21   Local Government shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and
22   regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
23          SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is held
24   invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
25   remain in full force and effect.
