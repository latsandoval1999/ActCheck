                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise known as the Paternity Leave Act
of 1996, was a landmark social legislation that institutionalized paid
paternity leave for married male employees in both the private and public
sectors.1 Enacted to promote shared parental responsibility and support
maternal recovery, the law grants a qualified employee seven (7) days of




1 Republic Act No. 8187 (1996).
paid leave for the first four (4) deliveries of his legitimate spouse with whom
he is cohabiting.2

        Although progressive at the time of its enactment, R.A. No. 8187 has
not kept pace with evolving labor standards, changing family structures,
and contemporary understandings of gender equality in childcare.3 While
existing laws such as the 105-Day Expanded Maternity Leave Law have
strengthened protections for mothers, women continue to bear the
disproportionate burden of child-rearing and unpaid domestic work.
Evidence shows that childcare in the Philippines remains deeply gendered.

        According to data cited by the Philippine Commission on Women and
national studies, Filipino women spend nearly 11 hours daily on unpaid care
and domestic work, significantly limiting their access to education,
employment, and economic opportunities.4 At the same time, research
supported by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies shows that
married women are three to four times more likely than men to cite
housework as the primary reason for not participating in the labor force,
highlighting how entrenched care responsibilities constrain women’s
economic participation.5

        This imbalance fails to reflect the realities of modern parenting and
undermines the State’s policies on family solidarity, child development, and
gender equality. Nearly three decades since its enactment, R.A. No. 8187
has not kept pace with evolving labor standards, changing family

       Article III, Section 1 of the Constitution guarantees that “no person
shall be denied the equal protection of the laws,” underscoring the need to
eliminate unjustified distinctions in parental benefits. Article II, Section 14
mandates that the State “shall ensure the fundamental equality before the
law of women and men,” which extends to the duty to care for children.

       Article XV, Section 1 recognizes the Filipino family as “the foundation
of the nation” and directs the State to strengthen its solidarity and promote
its total development, including responsible parenthood. Meanwhile, Article
XV, Section 3 (2) mandates the State to defend “right of children to
assistance, including proper care and nutrition, and special protection from
all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation and other conditions
prejudicial to their development.”

       Taken together, these constitutional principles compel the State to
update existing laws to reflect gender equality, support family solidarity,
and advance child development and the best interests of children through
more inclusive and equitable parental leave policies.

       Against this constitutional backdrop, several limitations in the
current law become evident. First, the duration of paid leave, which is
limited to seven (7) days, is no longer sufficient to meaningfully support
postpartum recovery, newborn care, and paternal bonding during the
critical early stages of child development.7 Second, the eligibility criteria,

regarding inclusivity and discrimination, especially in light of diverse
family arrangements and constitutional principles on equal protection.8
Notably, data from the Philippine Statistics Authority show that a majority
of births in the country now occur outside of marriage—more than half
(57%) of registered live births in 2020 were born to unmarried mothers,
reflecting a significant shift in family structures.9 This trend has persisted
in recent years, with over 57% of births likewise recorded as non-marital in
2021.10 Lastly, workers in the informal economy are not covered under the
existing law.

        Hence, this bill which seeks to: (1) increase the paid paternity leave
to ninety (90) continuous days for live childbirth and sixty (60) continuous
days in cases of miscarriage or emergency termination of pregnancy; (2)
introduce an additional fifteen (15) days of paid parental leave, which may
be availed within one (1) year from childbirth, on a continuous or non-
continuous basis; (3) expand eligibility beyond married fathers to include
non-marital fathers and alternative caregivers; (4) extend coverage to
workers in the informal economy and voluntary Social Security System
(SSS) members, subject to contribution requirements; (5) provide that, in
the private sector, SSS shall reimburse employers one hundred percent
(100%) of paternity leave benefits advanced; (6) guarantee security of tenure
by prohibiting dismissal, demotion, or discrimination when a worker avails
of the leave benefits; (7) impose penalties on non-compliant employers and
persons, including fines, imprisonment, and revocation of business permits;

equality, improves child and family wellbeing, and strengthens labor force
participation. It affirms that childcare is not solely the responsibility of
women, but a shared parental duty that must be actively supported by the
State.

    Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
    Philippines in Congress assembled:

1         Sec. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Paternity and
2   Parental Leave Act.”

3         Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of
4   the State to recognize, promote, and support the evolving roles of working
5   parents in family life and child development. It is also the policy of the State
6   to:
7      a. Promote     shared   parental    responsibility   in    child-rearing    and
8         household management and ensure the active involvement of fathers
9         and partners from the earliest stages of a child’s life, including the
10        prenatal, birth, and postnatal periods;
11     b. Establish an inclusive and equitable system of paternity and parental
12        leave responsive to diverse family structures and work arrangements
13        and provide sufficient, flexible, and accessible leave benefits that
14        support the wellbeing of children and families;
15     c. Recognize    caregiving    and    child-rearing    as     valuable      social
16        contributions and promote gender equality in the workplace and at
17        home by eliminating stereotypes that assign childcare primarily to
18        women; and
19     d. Protect workers from discrimination, job insecurity, and income loss
20        arising from the exercise of their duties as parents.

21        Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the
22   following terms are hereby defined:

23     a. Delivery. — Refers to childbirth, miscarriage, or emergency
24        termination of pregnancy.

25     b. Alternative Caregiver. — Refers to an individual, regardless of sex,
26        who is designated by the mother to care for her child in case of
27        absence, death, or incapacity of the child’s father. The alternative
28        caregiver may be:

29          i.   A relative of either parent within the fourth civil degree of
30               consanguinity or affinity; or
31         ii.   The current partner of the mother who is sharing the same
32               household with the mother and her child.
33         Sec. 4. Grant of Paternity Leave. – Every male worker in
34   government and the private sector, including the informal economy,
35   regardless of his civil status or the legitimacy of his child, shall be entitled
36   to paternity leave with full pay for each delivery of his child: Provided, That
37   the worker is cohabiting with the child’s mother.

38         In cases of live childbirth, the paternity leave shall be for a period not
39   exceeding ninety (90) days. In cases of miscarriage or emergency
40   termination of pregnancy, the paternity leave shall be for a period not
41   exceeding sixty (60) days. The paternity leave shall be availed of after
42   delivery, or both before and after delivery, in a continuous and
43   uninterrupted manner.

44         Sec. 5. Paternity Leave for Male Employees in the Public
45   Sector. – Any male employee in the government service, regardless of
46   employment status, whether in national government agencies, local
47   government units, government-owned or controlled corporations, or state
48   universities and colleges, must notify the head of the agency in writing prior
49   to availing the paternity leave, or within a reasonable period thereafter in
50   cases of medical emergency, in accordance with applicable civil service rules.

51         Sec. 6. Paternity Leave for Male Employees in the Private
52   Sector – A male employee in the private sector who has paid at least three
53   (3) monthly Social Security System (SSS) contributions within the twelve
54   (12)-month period immediately preceding the semester of the delivery shall
55   be entitled to paternity leave benefits which shall be computed based on his
56   average monthly salary credit.

57         The employer shall advance the full payment of paternity benefits to
58   the employee within thirty (30) days from the filing of the paternity leave
59   application, subject to the following conditions:
60      a. The male employee must have notified his employer of the pregnancy
61         and the expected or probable date of delivery, which shall be
62         transmitted to the SSS in accordance with its rules;
63      b. The SSS shall reimburse the employer one hundred percent (100%) of
64         the amount of paternity benefits advanced, upon submission of
65         complete and satisfactory proof of payment;
66      c. The availment of paternity benefits shall preclude recovery of sickness
67         benefits under Republic Act No. 1161 for the same period of paternity
68         leave; and
69      d. Failure of the employer to remit the required SSS contributions or to
70         comply with notification requirements shall render the employer
71         liable to the SSS for the amount of benefits due to the male employee,
72         without prejudice to other penalties provided by law.

73         Sec. 7. Paternity Leave Benefits for Male Employees or
74   Workers in the Informal Economy and Voluntary SSS Members. –
75   Paternity leave benefits under this Act shall extend to qualified male
76   workers in the informal economy and voluntary male members of the Social
77   Security System (SSS). A male worker in the informal economy or a
78   voluntary male SSS member shall be entitled to paternity benefits if he has
79   paid at least three (3) monthly contributions within the twelve (12)-month
80   period immediately preceding the semester of the delivery.

81         Sec. 8. Parental Leave. –           A working father or designated
82   alternative caregiver with actual custody of the child shall also be entitled
83   to fifteen (15) days of paid parental leave.

84         Parental leave may be availed of within one (1) year from the date of
85   birth of the child, in a continuous or non-continuous manner. Parental leave
86   may be used for illnesses or emergencies involving the child, in which case
87   the application therefor may be filed after the worker’s absence.
88          The employer, whether in government or the private sector, shall be
89    responsible for the payment of parental leave benefits.

90          The parental leave is not convertible to its monetary equivalent,
91    unless otherwise allowed in a contract, collective bargaining or negotiation
92    agreement, or company policy or practice.

93          Sec. 9. Security of Tenure. – No worker in government or the
94    private sector shall be dismissed, demoted, or otherwise prejudiced in
95    working conditions by reason of availing the benefits under this Act.

96          Reassignment or parallel transfer is allowed but it must not result in
97    the diminution of the worker’s rank, status, salary, or benefits nor
98    constitute constructive dismissal.

99          Sec. 10. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act shall
100   be construed as to diminish existing benefits more favorable to the worker,
101   whether granted under existing laws, collective bargaining or negotiation
102   agreements, individual contracts, or company policies or practices. Any
103   alternative work arrangement agreed upon during the period of paternity
104   or parental leave is valid: Provided, That such arrangement is voluntarily
105   agreed to in writing by the worker without prejudice to the purpose of the
106   leave and the benefits of this Act.

107         Sec. 11. Penalties. – Any person who fails or refuses to comply with
108   the provisions of this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not
109   less than twenty thousand pesos (₱20,000.00) nor more than two hundred
110   thousand pesos (₱200,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than six (6) years
111   and one (1) day but not more than twelve (12) years, or both, at the
112   discretion of the court.
113         If the offense is committed by a corporation, partnership, or any
114   juridical entity, the penalty will be imposed upon the responsible officers,
115   including directors, partners, or managing heads.

116         Non-compliance with this Act constitutes a ground for the denial,
117   revocation, or non-renewal of local business permits, subject to the
118   discretion of the issuing authority.

119         Sec. 12. Accountability and Proper Use of Paternity Leave. –
120   The paternity and parental leaves under this Act are granted to ensure that
121   an eligible father will provide essential care to his newborn and necessary
122   support to the child’s mother during her recovery. Any father who, while on
123   paternity or parental leave, fails to provide such care and support is deemed
124   to have committed an act of violence under Section 5 (i) of Republic Act No.
125   9262, otherwise known as the Anti-Violence Against Women and Their
126   Children Act of 2004.

127         Sec. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60)
128   days from the effectivity of this Act, the Civil Service Commission (CSC),
129   Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), and SSS must promulgate
130   the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation. Failure
131   to promulgate the IRR will not affect the effectivity of this Act.

132         Sec. 14. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or
133   provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, other parts or
134   provisions not affected thereby will continue to be in full force and effect.

135         Sec. 15. Repealing Clause. – Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise
136   known as the Paternity Leave Act of 1996, is hereby repealed. All other
137   laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules and regulations, and
138   other issuances or part or parts thereof contrary to or inconsistent with this
139   Act are likewise hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
140         Sec. 16. Effectivity. – This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
141   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
