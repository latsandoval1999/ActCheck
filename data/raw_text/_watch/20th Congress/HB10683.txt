                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines, with over 36,289 kilometers of coastline 1 , is among the countries most vulnerable
to the impacts of climate change, sea level rise, storm surges, coastal erosion, and other coastal
hazards. According to the Climate Change Commission (CCC), mangroves and beach forests serve
as natural barriers that reduce wave energy, stabilize shorelines, protect coastal communities from
storm surges, enhance biodiversity, and support fisheries and carbon sequestration2. Despite
existing conservation efforts, the degradation of these ecosystems continues to expose millions of
Filipinos and their livelihoods to increasing climate and disaster risks.

State to "protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord
