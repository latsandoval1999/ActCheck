                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) is an integral part of Filipino
society and a significant contributor to our nation’s development. Yet, after
years of working abroad and being separated from their families, many OFWs
face another challenge upon returning home: rebuilding their livelihoods and
reintegrating into their communities.


       This measure seeks to establish a comprehensive, integrated, and
sustainable reintegration program for OFWs covering the entire migration
cycle—from pre-departure, to on-site support, and upon return. It adopts a
whole-of-government approach to provide returning OFWs with access to
employment, entrepreneurship, education and skills development, financial
literacy, psychosocial support, and other social protection services.


       The measure likewise seeks to strengthen coordination among
government agencies and private sector partners, improve job matching and
skills recognition, and ensure that undocumented OFWs are not excluded
from reintegration assistance. It recognizes that effective reintegration
requires not only financial assistance, but sustained support that enables
OFWs to achieve economic self-sufficiency, social stability, and psychosocial
well-being upon their return.

return home, they are provided meaningful opportunities to rebuild their
lives and contribute once again to the country’s development.


      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                   SUSTAINABLE REINTEGRATION PROGRAM

 1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bagong
 2      Balikbayan Act”.

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the invaluable
 4      contributions of Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) to national development and
 5      is committed to upholding their fundamental rights, including the right to equal
 6      opportunity, decent living, dignity, and participation in decision-making processes
 7      affecting their lives and well-being. Further, it is the declared policy of the State
 8      to:
 9             (a) Implement a comprehensive, integrated, and sustainable reintegration
10                program     for    returning   OFWs,     addressing   economic,     social,
11                psychosocial, gender-responsive, and cultural dimensions, regardless of
12                their documentation status;




                                                                                           1
 1          (b) Ensure such reintegration programs are customized to the needs and
 2             skills of OFWs and mainstreamed throughout the migration cycle and
 3             promote:
 4             1) Access to local employment, entrepreneurship, agriculture, fisheries,
 5                 aquaculture, and other livelihood opportunities;
 6             2) Development of financial literacy programs; and
 7             3) Attainment of economic self-sufficiency, social stability, and
 8                 psychosocial well-being for returning OFWs and their families.
 9          (c) Foster conditions where migration is a matter of choice rather than
10             necessity; and
11          (d) Collaborate with relevant public and private stakeholders in the design,
12             implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of reintegration programs
13             and assistance.
14          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms
15   are defined as follows:
16          (a) Full-cycle Reintegration Program refers to interventions starting with
17             the pre-departure stage, on-site support during employment, and
18             assistance upon return;
19          (b) Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a Filipino who is to be
20             engaged, is engaged, or has been engaged in remunerated activity in a
21             country of which they are not an immigrant, citizen, or permanent
22             resident or is not awaiting naturalization, recognition, or admission,
23             whether land-based or sea-based regardless of status; excluding a
24             Filipino engaged under a government-recognized exchange visitor
25             program for cultural and educational purposes;
26          (c) Reintegration refers to a multi-dimensional process enabling OFWs to
27             re-establish economic, social, and psychosocial well-being upon return;
28             and

22         SEC. 5. Initiation of Reintegration Process. – The reintegration process
23   for concerned OFWs shall be initiated by the relevant Migrant Workers Offices
24   (MWOs) six (6) months before the intended return of the OFW to the Philippines.
25   The process shall commence by providing access to four (4) paths for
26   reintegration, namely:
27         (a) Education, upskilling / reskilling/ retooling / micro-credentialing
28             (Kaalaman);

 2         (c) Entrepreneurship (Negosyo); and
 3         (d) Employment (Hanapbuhay).
 4         SEC. 6. Returnee-OFWs Management Information System (ROMIS) and
 5   Reintegration Management System (RMS). –
 6         (a) Establishment of ROMIS. The DMW shall establish and maintain the
 7            ROMIS, which shall serve as the primary information network and
 8            database containing information on the educational attainment, skills
 9            levels, and experience of all returning OFWs. The ROMIS shall function
10            as a referral system to the private sector and government institutions to
11            facilitate job matching, possible hiring, and/or skills upgrading.
12         (b) Development and Function of RMS. Within the ROMIS framework, the
13            DMW shall finalize the development and operationalization of the RMS.
14            The RMS shall be an online system and platform designed to streamline
15            the reintegration process for returnee-OFWs. It shall provide access to a
16            range of services, including financial literacy programs, livelihood
17            training, and access to funding opportunities.
18         (c) System Objectives. Both the ROMIS and RMS shall facilitate effective
19            connections with government and private sector partners to ensure a
20            smooth transition for returnee-OFWs back into local employment or
21            entrepreneurship. The RMS shall further aim to provide efficient support
22            for the holistic needs of returnee-OFWs and their families, thereby
23            seeking to empower them to reintegrate successfully and continue
24            contributing to national development.

12         SEC. 10. Components of Reintegration Services. – Reintegration services

14   migration cycle:
15         (a) Pre-Departure:
16                 1. Mandatory pre-departure orientation seminars covering available
17                      reintegration services and support for undocumented OFWs;
18                 2. Financial literacy training focused on management of earnings
19                      and future planning; and
20                 3. Family counseling on coping mechanisms for separation and
21                      eventual reunification.
22         (b) On-Site Support:
23                 1. Provision of accessible support services through MWOs for
24                      OFWs in distress, including undocumented workers;
25                 2. Opportunities for skills development and training designed to
26                      enhance employability upon return; and
27                 3. Establishment of mechanisms for addressing exploitation and
28                      abuse, ensuring access to legal and psychosocial support.

 4                2. Delivery    of   economic    reintegration   assistance,   including

15         SEC. 11. Protection of Undocumented OFWs. – No undocumented OFW
16   shall be denied reintegration services due to their status. Mechanisms shall be
17   established for the regularization of undocumented OFW’s status upon return to
18   facilitate access to services and benefits. Information dissemination campaign shall
19   inform undocumented OFWs of their rights and available reintegration support. A
20   dedicated hotline and online platform shall be established for confidential
21   assistance to undocumented OFWs.
22         SEC. 12. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The MDW shall develop a
23   monitoring and evaluation framework to assess the reintegration program’s
24   effectiveness and conduct regular impact assessments to ensure program
25   responsiveness to OFWs’ evolving needs.

19         SEC. 14. Program for Qualified OFWs to Teach in the Academe. – The
20   DMW shall, in coordination with the CHED and TESDA, provide a program to

22   recognized by the CHED and/or in training centers accredited by the TESDA.
23         SEC. 15. Social Assistance Measures. – The DMW shall, together with the
24   OWWA, the Department of Social Welfare and Development, and the DA, shall
25   include returnee-OFWs as beneficiaries in their respective social protection and
26   other relevant programs.
27         The DMW and the OWWA, through their regional offices, shall encourage
28   community engagements for returnee-OFWs and their families.
