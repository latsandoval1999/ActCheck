29                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
30
31           The New Manila International Airport Project or the “Aerotropolis” was an
32   unsolicited proposal of the San Miguel Aerocity, Inc presented by San Miguel
33   Corporation (SMC) President Ramon Ang to the Duterte administration in February
34   2017. It was never a public policy but a profit-oriented brainchild of the private sector,
35   particularly from one of the richest oligarchs in the country. The SMC is neither a
36   scientific institution, nor a champion of democratic governance in the country. In fact,
37   its history is tainted with its immoral involvement with the multi-billion-peso coconut
38   levy fund scam during the Marcos dictatorship.
39
40          The issue of additional airport for Greater Metro Manila was raised in May 2011
41   by the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) to the Aquino administration. It
42   claimed that there is an urgent need for a new gateway airport as the Ninoy Aquino
43   International Airport (NAIA) was already almost saturated. In October 2013, it
44   identified Sangley Point in Cavite and Laguna Bay as potential sites based on cost and




                                                                                                            1
 1   viability. It excluded the Manila Bay area of Bulacan province.
 2
 3          In June 2014, the JICA recommended Sangley Point to be the project site and
 4   began its feasibility study. In August 2015, the then-Department of Transportation and
 5   Communication (DOTC) received the New Manila Sangley Airport feasibility study
 6   report from JICA.
 7
 8          According to the report, the Obando site along Manila Bay has several
 9   disadvantages such as risk of flooding, limited possibility of urban development and
10   expected high cost for stabilization of sub-surface soils. Furthermore, the development
11   of NMIA in this site could cause significant obstruction against discharge from the river
12   in case of heavy rain, resulting in a high risk of flooding. The JICA noted that there is
13   no significant advantage offsetting these disadvantages.
14
15          The SMC’s Aerotropolis Project is just around 4 kilometers away from the JICA-
16   studied Obando site, and the marine environment and the geological characteristics are
17   comparable. As early as 2015, there were overwhelming literature and scientific studies
18   that demolished the feasibility of constructing an airport at the Manila Bay area of
19   Bulacan province
20
21          In August 2019, the Office of the Vice Chancellor for Research and Development
22   of the University of the Philippines Diliman campus (OVCRD UP Diliman) and
23   National Institute of Geological Science (NIGS) published its study entitled
24   “Articulating geomorphology and geohazards in the coastal development issues of
25   communities along the northern coasts of Manila Bay, Philippines.” The study focused
26   on the impact on Barangay Taliptip, the ground zero of the Aerotropolis Project.
27
28   It concluded that:
29
30          “Most sitios of Brgy. Taliptip are built upon shallow tidal flats of the tidal-
31          dominated Pampanga delta system. Four major geohazards were identified:
32          earthquake, tidal and fluvial flooding, storm surge, and subsidence. These
33          geohazards are commonly present and consistent with the geology,
34          geomorphology, and hydrometeorological setting of the area. The first three
35          hazards corroborated with anecdotes of residents while subsidence, though
36          unreported, remains a threat based on earlier studies.”
37
38   It also cited as potential impact of the aerotropolis project:
39
40          “Construction of the Bulacan Aerotropolis will bring both permanent and
41          temporary changes to the immediate vicinity and nearby areas. Thus, it is
42          important to consider potential effects in all stages of development (i.e.,
43          construction and operational stage) to the social, physical, and environmental
44          resources of the area.
45
46          One of the immediate and obvious effects would be the alteration of the physical


                                                                                             2
 1         environment. Loss and reclamation of land, changes in river and drainage
 2         pathways, and removal of natural flora and fauna, consequently, influence the
 3         sediment and water transport pathways. If the risk is unmitigated, these could
 4         lead to flooding and siltation in nearby areas.”
 5
 6   It added,
 7
 8         “Pollution is another problem that should be taken into consideration. Air and
 9         noise pollution are unavoidable during construction and operation. Air pollution
10         may come from the emissions of aircraft, ground service vehicles, and
11         transportation in and out of the airport. Noise pollution, on the other hand, may
12         be caused by construction, airborne activities (e.g., sonic booms, landing and
13         take-off noise, engine testing), ground operations, and road traffic noise. Water
14         and soil pollution near airports is also possible if waste is not properly managed.
15         Inadequate treatment of waste and leakage from waste storage may infiltrate the
16         soil and also flow into bodies of water, thus polluting them. Excessive pumping
17         of groundwater may also cause saltwater contamination in aquifers, and
18         subsidence from compaction.”
19
20         Despite the impact of the project to the environment and the communities, the
21   Department of Environment and Natural Resources Environmental Management
22   Bureau–Region 3 (DENR-EMB Region 3) advanced the interest of the SMC.
23
24          In June 2019, it issued an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC) for a
25   “land development” project of the Silvertides Holdings, Corp., a contracted firm of San
26   Miguel Aerocity, Inc. Worse, it refuses to disclose the submitted Environmental Impact
27   Statement (EIS) that is supposedly the basis of approving Silvertides Holdings Corp.’s
28   ECC. The EIS remains concealed from the public, affected fisherfolk sector of
29   Barangay Taliptip and advocates of genuine Manila Bay rehabilitation. This immoral
30   cover-up by the DENR-EMB Region 3, an agency under the Duterte administration, is
31   outright admission of the baselessness of the Aerotropolis Project.
32
33         Moreover, Silvertides Holdings, Corp. is claiming ownership of the more than
34   2,000-hectare fishpond areas, which are supposedly administered by the Fishpond
35   Lease Agreement (FLA) provision of the Republic Act No. 8550 or Fisheries Code of
36   1998. This “land development” project involves fishpond and marine areas of Manila
37   Bay that are part of the Bulacan Fishing Reservation as declared by the Republic Act
38   No. 4701.
39
40   Based on the Fisheries Code:
41
42         “Section 49. Reversion of All Abandoned, Undeveloped or Underutilized
43         Fishponds. - The DENR, in coordination with the Department, LGUs, other
44         concerned agencies and FARMCs shall determine which abandoned,
45         underdeveloped or underutilized fishponds covered by FLAs can be reverted to
46         their original mangrove state and after having made such determination shall


                                                                                             3
 1         take all steps necessary to restore such areas in their original mangrove state.”
 2
 3          Where was the process of determination that was supposed to facilitate the
 4   reversion of the fishponds as mangrove forest? Worse, their classification was deemed
 5   transformed into being alienable and disposable, with the claim of ownership of the
 6   Silvertides Holdings Corp.
 7
 8   Briefly, the DENR-EMB Region 3:
 9
10      ● usurped the powers of the legislature to reclassify lands,
11      ● recognized the questionable ownership of the Silvertides Holdings Corp.; and
12      ● issued an ECC based on an EIS that remains hidden in a “vault,” refusing the
13        scrutiny of the affected sectors and the public.
14
15          Thus, the SMC’s Aerotropolis Project should be declared void, as its early stage
16   is plagued with irregularities and apparent violations of existing laws, on top of its
17   baselessness and contradiction to the scientific rationale of preserving the Manila Bay
18   area of Bulacan province from any reclamation project. By transitiveness, the Republic
19   Act No. 11506, otherwise known as “Granting San Miguel Aerocity Inc. A Franchise
20   To Construct, Develop, Establish, Operate, And Maintain A Domestic And
21   International Airport In The Municipality Of Bulakan, Province Of Bulacan, And To
22   Construct, Develop, Establish, Operate, And Maintain An Adjacent Airport City” is
23   unscientific.
24
25          The displacement of the small fisherfolk community of Brgy. Taliptip has been
26   well-documented that even the University of the Philippines academic community
27   carried out its own study, and members of its faculty have visited the site numerous
28   times for participatory research activities on the plight of the affected sector.
29
30          On the joint position paper of Assistant Professor Devralin T. Lagos of the
31   College of Social Work and Community Development (UP CSWCD) and Pamalakaya
32   resident fisheries expert and Chairperson of AGHAM UP Diliman Jerwin Baure
33   supporting the House Bill No. 7524 or the Special Protection to Coastal Fishing
34   Communities, she cited that “[t]he displacement of communities from large-scale
35   reclamation of near-shore and coastal areas is a kind of Development-Induced
36   Displacement” and the impact concerns on small fisherfolk residents displaced by the
37   aerotropolis project.
38

44         dagat), ang mga anak ko, busog, 3 beses sa isang araw kumain, may ulam. Yun
45         ang pinagkaiba ngayon.”

18         The Pambansang Lakas ng Kilusang Mamamalakaya ng Pilipinas or

20   the country, chaired by former Anakpawis Party-list Representative Fernando “Ka

22   Aerotropolis project”
23
24         “Fisherfolk and residents of Brgy. Taliptip in Bulakan town, earliest heard of a
25         project in 2008, around the same time Silvertides Holdings went on a buying
26         spree of fishponds,” and “In the past years, SMC never showed its face to the
27         affected population, but local officials told them that would be soon relocated.
28         Without any prior meetings or consultations or venues to present their opinions,
29         they are deemed to be displaced from their homes and sources of livelihood.”
30
