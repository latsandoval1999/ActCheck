                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


With almost everything within reach of our fingertips because of the internet, several unsuspecting
debtors became vulnerable to debt-shaming and harassment by debt collectors or debt collection
agencies. As of August 2023, the Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”) has revoked the
primary registration of 2,084 lending companies and ordered the closure of 81 online lending
applications for their non-compliance with Republic Act 9474 or the Lending Company Regulation
Act for using threats, obscene language, false representation and maliciously using personal
information in their debt collection practices.1


        The number of online lenders involved in this kind of debt collection practice is quite
alarming, especially considering that there is a lack of a law that penalizes said practice. As of today,
the National Privacy Commission (“NPC”) has issued a Circular concerning the processing of
personal data in loan transactions,2 while the SEC issued a Memorandum Circular prohibiting
unfair debt collection practices.3 These circulars aim to protect debtors from having their personal
information used in illegal and unethical collection methods to compel them to pay their debts.


        With the passage of this Bill, borrowers will be able to defend themselves against abusive
practices from debt collectors and agencies, and at the same time promote and safeguard their

                              AN ACT
    INSTITUTIONALIZING FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES BY DEBT
          COLLECTORS AND PENALIZING VIOLATIONS THEREOF

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:


        SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fair Debt Collection
Practices Act”.


        SECTION 2. Declaration of State Policy — The State values the right of debtors to be
protected from abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt-collection practices employed by debt collectors
and debt collection agencies as means to collect debts. This law also aims to safeguard and promote
the lawful processing of the personal data of debtors that are used by debt collectors and debt
collection agencies in the performance of their responsibilities.


        SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean
as follows:


    a. Creditor — any person who offers or extends credit creating a debt or to whom a debt is
        owed, but such term does not include any person to the extent that he receives an
        assignment or transfer of a debt in default solely to facilitate collection of such debt for
        another.




                                                                                                            3
b. Contact — means communications made by telephone, mobile phone, facsimile, e-mail,
   automated call system, text messaging, or any other modes, or in person, including
   messages left for a debtor with another person or by voicemail or on an answering machine,
   or any other form of communication not specifically excluded, but does not include
   communications that the debtor has expressly consented to or solicits in advance.


c. Debt — any monetary obligation owed by the debtor to the creditor, including, but not
   limited to a purchase on credit, accounts receivable, a loan of money, or the provision of
   goods or services.


d. Debtor — any person who has an obligation for a debt owed to the creditor, including but
   not limited to the owner of a sole proprietorship, a member of a partnership, or an
   individual who has provided a personal guarantee.


e. Debt Collector — any person authorized by the creditor, or by the debt collection agency,
   whose principal purpose is the collection of any debts, or who regularly collects or attempts
   to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be wowed or due another.
   It includes any creditor, who in the process of collecting his debts, uses any name other
   than his own, indicating that a debt collection agency or a third-party service provider is
   collecting or attempting to collect such debts.


   This definition does not apply to the following:

       1. An officer or employee of the creditor while, in the name of the creditor, collects
          the debt for the creditor.

       2. Any person while acting as a debt collector for another person, both of whom are
           related by common ownership or affiliated by corporate control, if the person
           acting as a debt collector does so only for persons to whom it is so related or
           affiliated and if the principal business of such person is not the collection of debts.


       3. Any officer or employee of the government, whether national or local, to the extent
           that collecting or attempting to collect any debt is in the performance of his official
           duties.




                                                                                                4
           4. Any person while serving or attempting to serve legal process on any other person
               in connection with the judicial enforcement of any debt.


   f. Debt Collection Agency — an agency that carries on the activities of collecting or attempting
       to collect a debt or debts from a debtor under any name that differs from that of the
       creditor to whom the debt is or was originally owed, regardless of to whom or where the
       payment is made, on behalf of another person. The definition does not include:


           1. A person who is attempting to collect or who is collecting a debt of which the
               person is the original creditor or owner.
           2. A business that purchases a debt or debts through acquiring or merging with a
               business in a transaction that includes the transfer of accounts receivable.


           3. A business that acquires a debt or debts through the seizure of accounts receivable
               under a security agreement.


           4. A person to whom the contract that gave rise to the debt was assigned to finance
               the transaction.


           5. Any of the enumerated persons in the previous section.


           a. Express Consent — freely given, specific, informed indication of will, given by the
               debtor about the processing of personal data and evidenced by written, electronic,
               or recorded means.


           b. Representative — a person, other than the debtor or creditor, who is authorized by
               the debtor or creditor to contact or be contacted and communicate with and about
               the debt.


       SECTION 4. Acquisition of Location Information — Any debt collector communicating
with any person other than the borrower to acquire location information about the borrower shall:


       a. Identify himself, state that he is confirming or correcting location information
           concerning the consumer, and, only if expressly requested, identify his employer;
       b. Not state that such borrower owes any debt;
                                                                                                 5
        c. Not communicate with any such person more than once, unless requested to do so by
            such person or unless the debt collector reasonably believes that the earlier response
            of such person is erroneous or incomplete and that such person now has correct or
            complete location information;
        d. Not communicate by postcard;
        e. Not use any language or symbol on any envelope or in the contents of any
            communication effected by the mails or telegram that indicates that the debt collector
            is in the debt collection business or that the communication relates to the collection of
            a debt; and
        f. After the debt collector knows the consumer is represented by an attorney about the
            subject debt and has knowledge of, or can readily ascertain, such attorney's name and
            address, not communicate with any person other than that attorney, unless the attorney
            fails to respond within a reasonable period for communication from the debt collector.


        SECTION 5. Allowable Debt Collection Practices. – Debt collectors and debt collection
agencies, whether or not hired by financing companies, or lending companies as third-party service
providers, shall use reasonable and legally permissible means to collect amounts due under the loan
agreement, provided that in the exercise of their rights and performance of their duties, they must
observe good faith and reasonable conduct, and refrain from engaging in unscrupulous and
untoward acts as enumerated in the succeeding section.


        SECTION 6. Unfair Collection Practices. — The following acts shall constitute unfair
collection practices, which shall be subject to the penalties proscribed herein:


        a. The use or threat of use of violence or other criminal means to harm the physical
           person, reputation, or property of any person;

        b. The use or threats to take any action that cannot legally be taken;

        c. The use of obscenities, insults, or profane language the natural consequence of which
           is to abuse or shame the debtor or his or her family and/or which amounts to a criminal
           act or offense under applicable laws;

        d. Disclosure or publication of the names and other personal information of borrowers
           who allegedly refuse to pay debts, except as may be allowed under Section _____
           hereof;



                                                                                                   6
e. Communicating or threatening to communicate to any person loan information, which
   is known or which should be known to be false, including the failure to communicate
   that the debt is being disputed, except as may be allowed under Section _____ hereof;

f. The use of any false representation or deceptive means to collect or attempt to collect
   any debt or to obtain information concerning a borrower; and

g. Making contact at unreasonable or inconvenient times or hours, which shall be defined
   as contact before 6:00 A.M. or after 10:00 P.M. unless, the account is past due for more
   than fifteen (15) days, or the borrower has given express consent that said times are the
   only reasonable or convenient opportunities for contact.

h. The collection of any amount (including any interest, fee, charge, or expense incidental
   to the principal obligation) unless such amount is expressly authorized by the
   agreement creating the debt or permitted by law.

i.   The acceptance by a debt collector from any person of a check or other payment
     instrument postdated by more than five days unless such person is notified in writing
     of the debt collector's intent to deposit such check or instrument not more than ten
     nor less than three business days prior to such deposit.

j.   The solicitation by a debt collector of any postdated check or other postdated payment
     instrument for the purpose of threatening or instituting criminal prosecution.

k. Depositing or threatening to deposit any postdated check or other postdated payment
   instrument prior to the date on such check or instrument.

l.   Causing charges to be made to any person for communications by concealment of the
     true purpose of the communication. Such charges include, but are not limited to, collect
     telephone calls and telegram fees.

m. Taking or threatening to take any nonjudicial action to effect dispossession or
   disablement of property if –
   1. there is no present right to possession of the property claimed as collateral through
       an enforceable security interest;
   2. there is no present intention to take possession of the property; or
   3. the property is exempt by law from such dispossession or disablement.

n. Communicating with a consumer regarding a debt by post card.

o. Using any language or symbol, other than the debt collector's address, on any envelope
   when communicating with a consumer by use of the mails or by telegram, except that
   a debt collector may use his business name if such name does not indicate that he is in
   the debt collection business.


                                                                                           7
       SECTION 7. Other Prohibited Acts and Practices. — No debt collector or debt
collection agency shall commit any of the following acts and practices:


       a. Collect or attempt to collect money for a creditor except on the belief in good faith
          that the money is due and owing by the person to the creditor;

       b. Charge any fee to a person for whom the collection agency or collector acts in addition
          to those fees provided for in the agreement with that person;

       c. Carry on the activities of a collection agency in a name other than the name in which it
          is licensed unless allowed by law;

       d. When using an automated call system, fail to provide a contact number for the debtor
          to call when leaving a message;

       e. When the collector fails to provide the collector’s name as shown on the license in all
          contacts and correspondence, including the loan agreement, and

       f. Make any arrangement with a debtor to accept a sum of money that is less than the
          amount of the balance due and owing to a creditor as final settlement without the prior
          express consent of the creditor;

       g. Fail to provide any person for whom the collection agency or collector acts with written
          report on the status of that person’s account;

       h. Make any personal call or telephone call for the purpose of collecting or attempting to
          collect a debt on any day except between 7 A.M. and 9 P.M;

       i.   Directly or indirectly threaten or state an intention to proceed with any action for which
            the collection agency or the collector does not have the prior express consent of the
            creditor or for which there is no lawful authority;

       j.   Contact or attempt to contact the debtor, any member of the debtor’s household, any
            relative of the debtor, the debtor’s employer, or any neighbor, friend, or acquaintance
            of the debtor by any means in such a manner as to constitute harassment, including
            without being limited to:

                   i.   the use of threatening, profane, intimidating, or coercive language,
                  ii.   the use of undue, excessive, or unreasonable pressure, or
                 iii.   the use of telephone or e-mail to call or send messages excessively;

       k. Give any person, directly or indirectly, by implication or otherwise, any false or
          misleading information, including, but not limited to, references to the police, a law
          firm, prison, credit history, court proceedings, or a lien or garnishment;

                                                                                                    8
l.   Continue to collect or attempt to collect money from, or continue to communicate
     with, the person, where the person has informed the collection agency or the collector
     that the person is not the debtor unless the collection agency or collector first takes all
     reasonable precautions to ensure that the person is the debtor, or the debtor, where
     the debtor has informed the collection agency or the collector by any verifiable means,
     including, but not limited to, personal service, certified mail, courier, facsimile, or
     e-mail, or by any other method, that the debt is in dispute and that the debtor wishes
     the creditor to take the matter to court;

m. Contact a debtor’s spouse or adult interdependent partner, relative, neighbor, friend,
   or acquaintance unless the contact is limited to the purpose of obtaining the debtor’s
   residential address, personal telephone number or employment telephone number;

n. Contact the debtor’s employer for any purpose other than to confirm the debtor’s
   employment status, business title, and the address of the business, in preparation for
   legal proceedings;

o. Contact the debtor when the debtor has notified the collection agency in writing to
   communicate only with the debtor’s representative and has provided a current address
   and telephone number for the representative, and the representative:

        i. Make reasonable arrangements to discuss the debt with the collection agency or
           collector, and
       ii. Discusses the debt with the collection agency or collector following the
           arrangements;

p. Contact a debtor at the debtor’s place of employment if the debtor (i) requests the
   collection agency or collector not to contact the debtor there, (ii) makes reasonable
   arrangements to discuss the debt with the collection agency or collector, and (iii)
   discusses the debt with the collection agency or collector in accordance with the
   arrangements;

q. Communicate information about the debt or the existence of the debt with any person
   except the debtor, a guarantor of the debt, the debtor’s representative, or the creditor
   of the debt unless the debtor has expressly consented to the communication;

r. Indicate to the debtor or any other person contacted to collect the debtor’s debt that
   the collection agency or the collector is part of a law firm or the legal department of a
   business, including a legal department of the collection agency itself or of the creditor
   of the debt;
s. If a collector indicates to a debtor that the collector is a legal collector, litigation
   specialist or the like;

        t. Charge any fee to a debtor beyond the debt that is due and owing from the debtor to
           the creditor, excluding a reasonable fee for a dishonored cheque, if the fee was disclosed
           to the debtor in writing before the submission of the cheque;

        u. Refuse to provide sufficient information on request to the debtor to ensure that the
           debtor is aware of the identity of the original and current creditor of the debt and the
           details of the debt;

        v. Enter into or arrange wage assignments with a debtor or the employer of a debtor;

        w. Exceed three (3) unsolicited contacts on behalf of the same creditor with a debtor in

           materially unless the debtor has misrepresented the debtor’s financial circumstances;

        SECTION 8. Other Prohibited Arrangements. — Other than the agreements previously
mentioned, any agreement entered into by the debtor and the debt collector and/or debt collection
agency shall be deemed void if that agreement:


            a. Misrepresents the rights and powers of a person collecting or attempting to collect
               a debt;

            b. Misrepresents the obligations or legal liabilities of a debtor; or

            c. Is misleading as to its true nature and purpose.

        SECTION 9. Processing of personal data for debt collection. — All debt collectors and
debt collection agencies shall process the personal and sensitive information of debtors under any
of the criteria set out for lawful processing as provided for under Sections 12 and 13 of the Data
Privacy Act (DPA) and shall implement measures for the protection of personal data and uphold
the rights of debtors, as data subjects.


       SECTION 10. Confidentiality of Information. — Creditors shall keep strictly
confidential the personal data of the borrower, except under the following circumstances:

        a. Disclosure of information with the express written consent of the borrower;

        b. Release, submission, or exchange of customer information with other financial
           institutions, credit information bureaus, lenders, their agents and/or representatives;
                                                                                                 10
        c. Upon orders of a court or tribunal of competent jurisdiction or any government office
           or agency authorized by law;

        d. Disclosure to debt collectors or debt collection agencies to enforce their rights against
           the borrower;

        e. Disclosure to third parties such as insurance companies, solely for the purpose of
           insuring the creditor from borrower default or other credit loss, and the borrower from
           fraud or unauthorized charges.

        SECTION 11. Issuance of Receipts. — Every debt collector and debt collection agency
must acknowledge the receipt of all cash transactions, payments made in person, or payments made
at the debtor’s request that the debt collector or the debt collection agency collects or receives from
a debtor for distribution to the debtor’s creditors by means of receipts that must contain the
following:


        a. The date the amount is collected or received,
        b. The name of the debtor,
        c. The name of the creditor,
        d. The amount due,
        e. The name of the debt collector or the person for whom the collection agency acts, and
        f. The amount received from the debtor.


        SECTION 12. Settlement Agreement. — If a debt collector or debt collection agency
reaches a settlement agreement, not prohibited herein, with a debtor, the collection agency or
collector must, when the amount set out in the settlement agreement, has been paid in full, provide
to the debtor a receipt containing:


        a. The amount paid,
        b. The name of the creditor or creditors, and
        c. A statement that the amount received is in the final settlement of the debt or debts
             owed.


        SECTION 13. Accounting. — A collection agency must provide the debtor on request
with a complete accounting of all the details of the debt and, if such an accounting is not in the
possession of the collection agency, the agency must request that the creditor provide it.

                                                                                                    11
        If, within 30 days after receiving a request for an accounting from a collection agency, the
creditor has not provided a complete accounting of the debt, the collection agency must:


        a. Inform the debtor in writing that it cannot provide the accounting and the reasons for
            it, and
        b. Cease all collection activity for that account and not resume collection activity until the
            accounting is provided.


        A debtor may request a complete accounting only once every six (6) months unless the
collection agency fails to provide the complete accounting as requested.


        SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — A technical working group shall
be formed to formulate the rules and regulations governing the implementation of this Act. It shall
be presided over by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and co-chaired by the Bangko
Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP). Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, the technical
working group shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.


        SECTION 15. Penalties — Violation of any section of this law shall be subjected to the
following penalties:


    a. Amount of Damages. – Any debt collector who violates any provisions of this Act shall be
        liable to pay:
           i.   Any actual damage sustained by the person as a result of the violation.
          ii.   In the case of any action by an individual, such additional damages as the court may
                allow, but not exceeding Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000.00); or
         iii.   In the case of a class action, such amount for each named plaintiff as could be
                recovered under paragraph (a), and such amount as the Court may allow for all
                other class members, without regard to a minimum individual recover, not to
                exceed One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP 100,000.00) or one (1) percent of the
                net worth of the debt collector, whichever is lesser.


    b. Administrative Sanctions. – Any debt collection agency who violates any provisions of this
        Act shall be liable to pay:
                                                                                                   12
           i.   In case of a first offense, the agency shall pay Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000.00)
          ii.   In case of a second offense, the agency shall pay One Hundred Thousand Pesos
                (Php 100,000.00).
         iii.   In case of a third and succeeding offense, the agency shall pay, at the discretion of
                the SEC, a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 100,000.00)
                but not more than One Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00), and/or closure of the
                agency.


   c. Factors to be Considered by the Court. – In determining the amount of liability and appropriate
       sanctions, the court shall consider, among other relevant factors:
           i.   In any individual action under paragraph (a) (ii) of this section, the frequency and
                persistence of non-compliance by the debt collector, the nature of such non-
                compliance, and the extent to which such noncompliance was intentional; or
          ii.   In any class action under paragraph (a) (iii) of this section, the frequency and
                persistence of non-compliance by the debt collector, the nature of such non-
                compliance, the resources of the debt collector, the number of persons adversely
                affected, and the extent to which the debt collector’s non-compliance was
                intentional.


   d. Intent.– A debt collector or debt collection agency may not be held liable in any action
       brought under this section if the debt collector shows by a preponderance of evidence that
       the violation was not intentional and resulted from a bona fide error in good faith.


   e. Consent. – Apart from the lack of intent, the debt collection agency may not be held liable
       for any action brought under this section if it has proven that it had neither given its consent
       nor ratified the action of its debt collector in support of the latter’s action that violates any
       provision of this law.


   f. Jurisdiction. – To enforce any liability created by this Act, an action may be brought in any
       appropriate court having jurisdiction on the amount of debt involved.


       SECTION 16. Separability Clause.— If, for any reason, any section of this Act shall be
deemed unconstitutional and invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof shall not be affected
and shall remain in force and effect.

       SECTION 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
