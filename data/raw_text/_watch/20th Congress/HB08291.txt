                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       A country’s public transportation system is one of the lifebloods of its economy
and among the necessary components that enables its citizens to attain their
fundamental rights. With a decent public transportation system, it enables workers to
reach their work places, students to attend their schools, medical professionals to
respond to emergencies on time, and other relevant sectors that need mobility in order
to drive the nation towards social and economic development.

       However, the Philippine public transportation system may have been failing
to accommodate economic demands and population growth of our country. This is
shown by persistent overcrowding in public vehicles, service unreliability, supply
shortages, and chronic congestion.1 Consequently, it impedes Filipinos in effectively
carrying out their daily roles, resulting in hours of time stolen from productive labor,
educational opportunities, critical medical care, essential rest, and generally affects
the development of our country.

      Data shows that the country’s urban population grew from 29% in 1955 to 45%
in 2015, and is projected to reach 60% by 2050. Despite this surge, transportation

      In 2020, service contracting program for public transportation was
implemented by our government due to the conditions brought by the COVID
pandemic. With its centralized model, its ability to manage the intricate, daily
transport needs of every locality is limited.3 Eventually, the said program was
continued after the pandemic, but it has not been widely implemented, and was
unable to significantly accommodate localized demand, particularly for intra-
municipal and intra-city routes. Despite minimal performance, it demonstrated its
potential in addressing supply gaps and providing a glimpse of a more dignified
transport system.

       Additionally, localized models have demonstrated that LGUs are equally, if
not, better equipped to tailor transportation solutions to the specific needs of the
community. To further illustrate, one example is the Q City Bus Program of the
Quezon City Local Government.4 This program also started in 2020 and was
continued by the said local government even after the pandemic. In the 2022 - 2023
Annual Report of the Quezon City Government, it highlights the said program to have
served over 15.4 million passengers as of June 2023. According to the report, these
millions of passengers were saved from transit costs, and were provided with special
services to assist them in going to public events or those who were stranded due to
transport strikes. Later on, the Quezon City Council institutionalized the Q City bus
Program by legislating Ordinance No. SP-3184, S-2023, also ensuring the provision
and improvement of the program, as well as the needed services and facilities.

      The Q City Bus program demonstrates the benefits of a legislated public
transportation program, and the provision of a fare or tariff mechanism in order to
promote the sustainability of such a program is seen as an opportunity to further
develop such program. This is opposite to the general view of the public
transportation sector that has become market-driven, resulting in significant issues,
such as unsafe competition among drivers, service disruptions during low-demand
periods, and the widespread use of polluting vehicles.5

       Given the narrative stated above, the need for a localized mass public
transportation service is evident, thus, this Representation forwards this proposed
measure seeking to amend the Local Government Code of 1991 and recognize local
mass public transportation as one of the fundamental services that LGUs should
provide to its citizens. This proposed measure likewise aims to provide the mandate
for LGUs to conceptualize, establish, operate, and maintain mass public transport
systems and facilities, and enable them to access and utilize various financing
instruments such as loans, grants, investments, partnerships, service contracts, or joint
ventures. This proposed measure is also perceived to promote interconnectivity of
mass public transportation with national and inter-LGU networks guided by
corresponding government and non-government agencies and institutions.

        Similarly, it expands the franchise and regulatory provisions for municipalities,
cities, and provinces by allowing them to regulate and grant franchises for LGU-
initiated and contracted public transportation services within their jurisdictions. At
present, the franchising of most public land transport services is exercised through the
Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB), which primarily
issues Certificates of Public Convenience (CPCs) and sets operating conditions, while
LGUs retain authority to manage local traffic and implement local transport policies
subject to national standards.

       Our LGUs possess the local knowledge of their communities yet existing law
does not clearly empower them to serve as architects of their public transportation
system. It is therefore timely to recognize and strengthen their authority to endeavor
to plan, finance, implement, operate, and regulate LGU-initiated or contracted mass
public transportation systems

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT EXPANDING THE POWERS OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS TO
    PROVIDE LOCAL MASS PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION SYSTEMS,
 AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE
  KNOWN AS THE “LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991”, AND FOR
                     OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “LGU
Transportation Act.”

      SEC. 2. Section 17 of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local
Government Code of 1991, is hereby amended, inserting new subsection (k), as
follows:

       “SEC. 17. Basic Services and Facilities. —
             (a) xxx

              (K) ALL LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS SHALL ENDEAVOR TO
              PROVIDE     SAFE,  AFFORDABLE,  ACCESSIBLE  AND
              SUSTAINABLE MASS PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION SYSTEMS,

        SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, in
consultation with the Department of Budget and Management, the Department of
Transportation, the Department of Public Works and Highways, the Department of
Finance, the Department of Economy Planning, and Development, the League of
Provinces, the League of Cities, the League of Municipalities, and other government
agencies concerned, shall jointly promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 7. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

      SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to or
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the
Philippines.
