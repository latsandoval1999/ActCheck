                            AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE DETENTION OF MOTOR VEHICLE DRIVERS INVOLVED IN
TRAFFIC ACCIDENTS WHO HAVE EXERCISED REASONABLE DEFENSIVE
DRIVING AND HAVE NO PRIMA FACIE CRIMINAL NEGLIGENCE, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Defensive Drivers
Protection Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the constitutional
rights of motorists and ensure that no person shall be deprived of liberty without due
process of law.

The State recognizes that not all traffic accidents arise from recklessness or criminal
negligence, and that drivers who exercise reasonable defensive driving should not be
subjected to automatic detention solely on the basis of involvement in a vehicular
accident.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:

     (a) Defensive Driving refers to the exercise of reasonable care, caution,
         alertness, and compliance with traffic laws and regulations in order to prevent
         accidents despite the actions or negligence of other road users.

     (b) Prima Facie Criminal Negligence refers to evident reckless imprudence,
         gross negligence, intoxication, willful violation of traffic laws, hit-and-run
         conduct, or other circumstances clearly indicating probable criminal liability.

     (c) Traffic Accident refers to any vehicular collision, crash, or road incident
         involving one or more motor vehicles resulting in property damage, physical
         injury, or death.

SEC. 4. Prohibition Against Unnecessary Detention. – No motor vehicle driver
involved in a traffic accident shall be subjected to custodial detention solely by reason
of such involvement where the initial investigation conducted by the proper law
enforcement officer shows that:
     (a) the driver exercised reasonable defensive driving;
     (b) the driver did not flee the scene of the accident;
     (c) the driver was not under the influence of alcohol, dangerous drugs, or similar
         substances;
     (d) there is no prima facie evidence of reckless imprudence, gross negligence,
         or willful violation of traffic laws; and
     (e) the driver cooperates with lawful investigation procedures.

Provided, That the mere occurrence of death, physical injury, or property damage shall
not by itself justify detention absent probable cause of criminal negligence.

SEC. 5. Grounds for Lawful Detention. – Nothing in this Act shall prohibit the lawful
detention of a driver where:

     (a) probable cause exists for reckless imprudence resulting in homicide,
         physical injuries, or damage to property under the Revised Penal Code;
     (b) the driver is under the influence of alcohol or dangerous drugs;
     (c) the driver attempts to flee or evade authorities;
     (d) the driver refuses lawful identification or investigation procedures;
     (e) the driver has an outstanding warrant of arrest; or
     (f) detention is otherwise authorized under existing laws and the Rules of
         Criminal Procedure.

SEC. 6. Standard Investigation Protocol. – The Philippine National Police (PNP),
Land Transportation Office (LTO), and other concerned agencies shall formulate
uniform guidelines for the objective determination of defensive driving, probable
criminal negligence, and the proper handling of traffic accident investigations
consistent with this Act.

SEC. 7. Administrative Liability. – Any law enforcement officer who knowingly orders
or causes the unlawful detention of a driver in violation of this Act shall be subject to
appropriate administrative sanctions, without prejudice to civil or criminal liability under
existing laws.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
