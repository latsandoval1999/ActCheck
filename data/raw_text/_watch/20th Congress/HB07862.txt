                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

State universities and colleges play a vital role in operationalizing the Constitutional
mandate to make quality education accessible to all. However, SUCs continue to face
persistent challenges, chief among them the adequacy of school infrastructure.

SUC administrators relay that while the funding they receive can cover regular
operations, it is not enough to pursue their goals of becoming globally competitive,
such as through infrastructure development.1 SUCs also struggle with having limited
budgets for capital outlay, thus delaying the implementation of some priority
infrastructure projects.2 Some SUCs have expressed concern over inadequate
funding for facilities and equipment upgrades, resulting in the prioritization of essential
expenses like utilities instead of facility rehabilitation for a more conducive learning
environment. The need for additional infrastructure is heightened by the increasing
number of student enrollment.3

In the 2026 General Appropriations Act, the total allocation for SUCs is P137.9 billion,
including P5.64 billion in capital outlay to support the construction, rehabilitation, and

This bill aims to build on the progress made by the 2026 education-focused budget by
requiring the Commission on Higher Education to identify priority educational
infrastructure for SUCs and establish a priority five-year plan. The establishment of
this plan will ensure that the government's limited resources are strategically directed
to SUCs with the most urgent needs. Further, the plan will be an important tool in
determining which SUCs require expanded facilities to accommodate a growing
student population, or rehabilitated structures to improve the learning spaces. While it
may not be feasible to construct additional buildings or repair facilities at the same
time, we can identify SUCs with the most urgent infrastructure needs, and ensure that
all SUCs are gradually able to address their facility requirements over the next few
years.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Priority Infrastructure for
SUCs Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels. To this end, the
State shall ensure the development of adequate, safe, resilient, and inclusive physical
learning environments in State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), recognizing that
appropriate educational infrastructure is essential to the intellectual, social, and
holistic development of learners.

SECTION 3. Priority Educational Infrastructure. – The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall identify priority educational infrastructure for SUCs and shall
establish a priority five-year plan, taking into consideration the needs of each SUC.

SECTION 4. Inter-Agency Committee. - There is hereby established an Inter-
Agency Committee (lAC) for the monitoring of the implementation of this Act. The lAC
shall be headed by the Chairperson of the CHED, with the President of the Philippine
Association of State Universities and Colleges as Vice-Chairperson, and the following
as members:

   (a) Department of Budget and Management;

   (c) Department of Economy, Planning, and Development.

The lAC shall issue implementing guidelines, monitor project implementation, and
submit an annual progress report to Congress.

SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall automatically be included in the annual appropriation of CHED for the next
five years for the implementation of this Act.

SECTION 6. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, proclamations, or administrative orders, rules or regulations that
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
