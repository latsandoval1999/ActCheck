                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       With the instability and uncertainties surrounding the electricity generation
in the country, Filipinos are increasingly turning to renewable energy sources such
as solar power. The Institute for Climate and Sustainable Cities (ICSC) reported a
surge on rooftop solar adoption in the Philippines, with roughly 600 megawatts
(MW) added in the last 12 months leading to April 2026, amid rising power costs
and the high cost of living.

       Behind-the-meter (BTM) energy storage, such as rooftop solar panels and
storage systems, offers significant benefits to consumers and the power grid alike. By
generating clean, fuel-free energy directly at the point of use, these systems
immediately protect households from volatile retail power rates, provide critical
back-up electricity during recurring grid blackouts, and reduce overall peak demand
on distribution grid. However, everyday Filipino consumers face severe hurdles in
adopting these solutions. Case in point: the installation of BTM systems continues
being hindered by technology costs, administrative red tape, excessive local
government unit (LGU) permit and clearance requirements, among other obstacles.

       As such, this measure seeks to simplify the regulatory requirements for the
installation of behind-the-meter electricity generation and battery storage systems,
intended solely for personal use. Through the removal of the existing administrative
restrictions, this bill aims to promote public access to these technologies for
electricity generation, and to making clean power simple, easy and affordable for
Filipinos Households.

       Through this, the mainstreaming of renewable energy across the country will
be accelerated. This measure will expand Filipinos access to alternative renewable
energy and directly alleviate the concerns over the rising electricity expenses.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

assembled:

        Section 5. Protection Against Unreasonable Restrictions. – No homeowners’
association, subdivision developer, property manager, or similar entity shall prohibit the
installation of a behind-the-meter electricity generation or energy storage system on property
owned or exclusively controlled by an end-user. Reasonable requirements may be imposed
only for structural safety, public safety, or the protection of common property.

       For this purpose, the Department of Energy shall develop and publish model rules that
such entities may adopt.

        Section. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) calendar
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy, in consultation with the
Energy Regulatory Commission and other relevant government agencies and stakeholders,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its implementation.

        Section 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full
force and effect.

        Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, decrees, rules and
regulations, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provision of this Act are deemed
repealed or modified accordingly.

        Section 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
