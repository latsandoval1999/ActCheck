                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), farmers, fisherfolk, artisans,
cooperatives, and community-based producers remain among the most important
drivers of the Philippine economy. They generate employment, sustain local
industries, strengthen food security, and contribute significantly to rural
development and national economic growth. Despite their economic contribution,
many local producers continue to face limited access to wider markets, insufficient
digital infrastructure, logistical barriers, financial exclusion, and unequal
participation in the rapidly growing digital economy.

       The rise of e-commerce and digital marketplaces has transformed how goods
and services are bought, sold, and distributed both locally and globally. However, a
significant number of Filipino producers—particularly those in rural, geographically
isolated, and underserved communities—remain unable to fully benefit from these

                                                                                     1
 opportunities due to inadequate digital literacy, high logistics costs, limited online
 visibility, fragmented support systems, and barriers to onboarding in existing
 commercial platforms.

       Thus, this bill seeks to establish a National Digital Marketplace and E-
 Commerce Support Program that will strengthen market access, digital inclusion,
 financial literacy, logistics support, and technological capability for Filipino local
 producers. The measure adopts a comprehensive approach that combines
 government support, public-private partnerships, digital infrastructure development,
 consumer protection mechanisms, and capacity-building initiatives.

        The bill likewise promotes interoperability among digital payment systems,
 logistics providers, and accredited digital commerce platforms while ensuring
 compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and applicable cybersecurity
 standards. It also provides mechanisms for consumer protection, dispute resolution,
 anti-fraud safeguards, and transparency in digital transactions. Furthermore, it seeks
 to empower underserved sectors—including women-led enterprises, indigenous
 peoples, youth entrepreneurs, cooperatives, and rural producers—through targeted
 training, onboarding assistance, and market linkage opportunities.

        Digital commerce is no longer merely an option but an essential component
 of inclusive economic participation in the modern economy. By providing local
 producers with the tools, infrastructure, and institutional support needed to compete
 in the digital marketplace, the State can help create a more resilient, innovative, and
 inclusive economy for all Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Digital Marketplace
and E-Commerce Support for Local Producers Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State,
consistent with the Constitution, to promote entrepreneurship, rural development,


                                                                                1
innovation, digital inclusion, and equitable access to economic opportunities for
local producers.


The State recognizes the important role of digital commerce and technology-enabled
market systems in strengthening supply chain resilience, increasing income
opportunities, reducing barriers to market access, and improving the competitiveness
of Filipino products in local and global markets.


Towards this end, the State shall establish a comprehensive and inclusive digital
marketplace and e-commerce support ecosystem that empowers farmers, fisherfolk,
artisans, cooperatives, and micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) through
accessible digital platforms, logistics support, financial inclusion, digital literacy,
and consumer protection mechanisms.


The State shall likewise ensure that underserved and geographically isolated
communities are provided meaningful access to digital commerce opportunities.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:


   a. Digital Marketplace refers to an online platform, portal, application, or
      electronic system where local producers may market, advertise, sell, or
      distribute products and services directly to consumers or institutional buyers;
   b. Local Producers refer to individual farmers, fisherfolk, artisans, cooperatives,
      associations, social enterprises, and registered or community-recognized




                                                                                      2
      MSMEs engaged in the production, processing, manufacture, or crafting of
      goods and services;
   c. MSME refers to micro, small, and medium enterprises as defined under
      Republic Act No. 9501, otherwise known as the Magna Carta for MSMEs;
   d. E-Commerce refers to the buying, selling, marketing, distribution, or
      exchange of goods and services through electronic systems and digital
      platforms;
   e. Platform refers to a government-supported or government-accredited digital
      commerce system, marketplace, or online platform operating under this Act;
   f. Payment Gateway refers to a secure Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)-
      regulated or accredited electronic payment system used for digital financial
      transactions;
   g. Logistics Partner refers to a public or private courier, transport, warehousing,
      fulfillment, or delivery service provider accredited under this Act;
   h. Digital Literacy refers to the ability to access, understand, use, and participate
      effectively in digital technologies, online platforms, and electronic
      transactions; and
   i. Institutional Buyers refer to government agencies, local government units
      (LGUs), schools, hospitals, cooperatives, and private organizations engaged
      in bulk procurement of goods and services.


SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Digital Marketplace and E-Commerce
Support Program. - The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination
with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT),
Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Science and Technology (DOST),
Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), and other relevant agencies, shall


                                                                                       3
establish a National Digital Marketplace and E-Commerce Support Program for
Local Producers.


The Program may include the establishment, accreditation, support, or integration of
one or more digital commerce platforms accessible to local producers nationwide.
The Program shall:


   a. Enable verified local producers to market and sell products online;
   b. Ensure interoperability with accredited e-commerce systems, payment
      gateways, logistics providers, and digital government services;
   c. Provide multilingual, mobile-friendly, and accessible user interfaces;
   d. Support real-time inventory management, product categorization, ratings and
      reviews, and customer service systems;
   e. Facilitate partnerships with public and private e-commerce platforms,
      cooperatives, and technology providers;
   f. Comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy
      Act of 2012, applicable cybersecurity standards, and consumer protection
      laws; and
   g. Promote fair competition and avoid undue market distortion against existing
      private-sector digital commerce platforms.


The DTI may establish a national brand identity or marketplace name for
implementation purposes, subject to the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).


SEC. 5. Eligibility and Onboarding of Sellers. - Eligible participants under this
Act shall include local producers registered with the DTI, DA, CDA, Securities and


                                                                                  4
Exchange Commission (SEC), local government units, or recognized producer
associations and cooperatives.


Informal, community-based, or emerging producers may be provisionally onboarded
subject to simplified registration assistance and compliance support.


Participating sellers shall undergo orientation or training programs on digital
commerce, consumer protection, financial literacy, and platform operations through
the DTI, DA, TESDA, SUCs, Negosyo Centers, Agricultural Extension Offices, or
accredited training partners.


The onboarding process shall be simplified, affordable, digitally accessible, and
designed to minimize bureaucratic barriers for small producers.


Verified sellers shall be issued appropriate accreditation or Seller Identification
credentials under the Program.


SEC. 6. Logistics and Fulfillment Support. - The DTI and DICT, in coordination
with the Department of Transportation (DOTr), Philippine Postal Corporation
(PHLPost), LGUs, cooperatives, and accredited logistics providers, shall develop
logistics and fulfillment support systems for participating producers.
Such support systems shall include:


   a. Affordable and time-bound delivery services;
   b. Order tracking, return management, and proof-of-delivery systems;
   c. Barangay-based drop-off and pick-up points in remote and underserved areas;


                                                                                  5
   d. Cold-chain and climate-resilient logistics support for agricultural, fishery, and
      perishable products; and
   e. Shared warehousing, consolidation, and fulfillment facilities where feasible.


The State shall encourage public-private partnerships to strengthen nationwide
logistics access and delivery efficiency.


SEC. 7. Digital Payment Integration and Financial Inclusion. - The Bangko
Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), in coordination with the DICT, Department of Finance
(DOF), and DTI, shall promote secure, interoperable, and inclusive digital payment
systems for all transactions conducted under this Act. The Program shall:
   a. Integrate BSP-regulated digital payment systems and electronic fund transfer
      facilities;
   b. Enable cashless, mobile wallet, bank transfer, and cash-on-delivery payment
      options where appropriate;
   c. Promote financial literacy and digital banking access among participating
      producers;
   d. Provide onboarding assistance for digital wallets and financial accounts; and
   e. Implement fraud prevention, dispute-resolution, and transaction security
      measures.


SEC. 8. Capacity-Building and Digital Skills Development. - To ensure the long-
term sustainability and competitiveness of participating producers, the State shall
provide continuous training and technical assistance on:


          a. Digital literacy and e-commerce operations;


                                                                                      6
         b. Product branding, packaging, and quality standards;
         c. Digital marketing, product photography, analytics, and online customer
            engagement;
         d. Entrepreneurship, bookkeeping, and financial management;
         e. Intellectual property protection and anti-counterfeiting measures;
         f. Export readiness and participation in international digital commerce
            opportunities; and
         g. Emerging digital technologies and innovation systems.


TESDA, SUCs, DTI, DOST, DICT, and accredited civil society organizations shall
jointly implement the capacity-building programs under this Act.


Dedicated support initiatives shall be established for women-led enterprises, youth
entrepreneurs, indigenous peoples, cooperatives, and geographically isolated
communities.


SEC. 9. Consumer Protection and Dispute Resolution. - The DTI, in coordination
with the DICT and relevant agencies, shall establish consumer protection and
dispute-resolution mechanisms for transactions conducted under this Act. Such
mechanisms shall include:


         a. Complaint handling and mediation systems;
         b. Refund, replacement, and return policies;
         c. Seller accountability standards;
         d. Anti-fraud and anti-counterfeit safeguards;
         e. Product authenticity and quality verification measures; and


                                                                                  7
          f. Transparent platform policies and user protections.


Consumers and sellers shall have access to fair, timely, and accessible remedies for
disputes arising from digital marketplace transactions.


SEC. 10. Data Privacy and Cybersecurity. - All systems, databases, platforms,
and operations established or accredited under this Act shall comply with the Data
Privacy Act of 2012, the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012, and other applicable
laws, rules, and regulations relating to cybersecurity and digital transactions.


The DICT shall establish minimum cybersecurity standards and protocols for
accredited platforms and service providers under this Act.


Appropriate safeguards shall be implemented to protect personal information,
financial data, transaction records, and user accounts from unauthorized access,
misuse, or cyber threats.


SEC. 11. Procurement and Market Access Support. - Government agencies,
government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities and
colleges (SUCs), public hospitals, and local government units may prioritize
procurement from accredited local producers and cooperatives participating under
this Act, subject to existing procurement laws and regulations.


The State shall likewise promote the inclusion of participating producers in local and
international trade fairs, tourism initiatives, institutional purchasing arrangements,
and digital export promotion programs.


                                                                                     8
SEC. 12. Responsibilities of Local Government Units. - LGUs shall:


         a. Integrate digital commerce and local producer support programs into
            their local economic development plans and investment programs;
         b. Establish local support mechanisms for onboarding, training, logistics
            coordination, and market linkage;
         c. Assist local producers in securing registration, permits, and digital
            access;
         d. Support the establishment of community-based logistics and fulfillment
            systems; and
         e. Submit annual reports on implementation and participation within their
            jurisdiction.


SEC. 13. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Transparency. - The DTI, in coordination
with the DICT, DA, DOST, and participating agencies, shall conduct periodic
monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of this Act. The annual report to
Congress shall include:


         a. The number of registered sellers and active users;
         b. Transaction volume and sales generated;
         c. Geographic reach and participation rates;
         d. Producer income growth and digital adoption indicators;
         e. Logistics efficiency and fulfillment performance;
         f. Complaints received and resolved; and
         g. Recommendations for policy and operational improvements.


                                                                                 9
The DTI shall maintain a publicly accessible digital transparency dashboard
containing non-confidential implementation statistics and program updates.


SEC. 14. Funding. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DTI, DICT, DA, and
participating agencies. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


The DTI may enter into public-private partnerships and may receive grants,
donations, technical assistance, and other lawful forms of support from domestic or
international sources, subject to existing accounting and auditing laws.


Priority implementation shall be given to underserved, rural, and geographically
isolated communities.


SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DTI, in coordination with the DICT, DA, BSP, DOST,
CDA, DOF, TESDA, and other concerned agencies, and in consultation with
producer organizations, MSMEs, cooperatives, digital commerce stakeholders, and
civil society organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
the effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

SEC. 18. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
