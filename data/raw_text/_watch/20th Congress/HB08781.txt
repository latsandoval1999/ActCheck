                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Energy prices play a critical role in achieving these constitutional goals.
Petroleum products power transportation, support agriculture, sustain
manufacturing, and influence the price of nearly all essential goods and services.
When fuel prices increase sharply, the effects cascade throughout the economy,
directly affecting food prices, electricity costs, public transportation, and the overall
cost of living.

       In 2026, the world is experiencing one of the most serious energy disruptions
in recent history due to the escalating conflict between Israel and Iran. The war has
severely disrupted oil production and shipping routes in the Middle East,
particularly in the Strait of Hormuz, a vital passage through which roughly one-fifth
of the world’s oil supply normally passes.1

        As a result of the conflict and the threat of disruptions in global energy
supply, oil prices have surged dramatically, with some benchmarks briefly
approaching $120 per barrel and analysts warning that prices could rise even further
if the crisis persists. The International Energy Agency has warned that the war has
caused one of the largest disruptions to oil supply in modern history, prompting the
release of hundreds of millions of barrels from emergency global reserves in an
attempt to stabilize markets.2

        The Philippines relies heavily on imported petroleum products, such
volatility poses serious risks. Sharp and sudden fuel price increases have immediate
consequences for Filipino consumers, including higher transportation fares,
increased food prices, rising electricity costs, and greater inflationary pressure on the
national economy.

      While international market forces influence fuel prices, it remains the
responsibility of the State to ensure that price adjustments within the domestic
market remain fair, transparent, and consistent with legitimate increases in
production and operational costs. Unreasonable or unjustified price increases during
periods of global instability can aggravate the economic burden on consumers and
undermine public welfare.

       This proposed measure seeks to establish a mechanism to address such
situations by imposing civil penalties on unreasonable price increases for crude oil,
residual fuel oil, and refined petroleum products. The measure directs the
Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), to issue regulations that prohibit unreasonable price increases and
provide for appropriate civil penalties for violations.

       In light of the current global energy instability and its profound implications
for the Philippine economy, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
IMPOSING CIVIL PENALTIES ON UNREASONABLE PRICE INCREASES FOR
CRUDE OIL, FUEL OIL, AND REFINED PETROLEUM PRODUCTS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Fuel Pricing Fairness
Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote the general
welfare and protect consumers from trade malpractices.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. -

        (A) 'Energy-producing company' means a person engaged in:
                (1) Ownership or control of mineral fuel resources or non‑mineral
               energy resources;
                (2) Exploration for, or development of, mineral fuel resources;
                (3) Extraction of mineral fuel or non‑mineral energy resources;
                (4) Refining, milling, or otherwise processing mineral fuel or
               non‑mineral energy resources;
                (5) Storage of mineral fuel or non‑mineral energy resources by any
               means whatever; or
                (6) Wholesale or retail distribution of mineral fuels, non‑mineral energy
               resources or electrical energy.

      (B) 'Unreasonable price increase' means any price increase that exceeds any
concurrent increase in the production or operation cost of the energy‑producing
company that are directly related to the products being sold.

        (C) 'Energy industry' means all energy‑producing companies.
      (D) 'Crude oil' means a naturally‑occurring substance found trapped in certain
rocks below the earth's crust. It is a dark, sticky liquid which is classed as a
hydrocarbon and is highly flammable.

       (E) ‘Fuel oil' means a liquid or semi‑liquid, high‑boiling fraction of residue from
the distillation of petroleum which is used as a fuel.

       (F) 'Refined petroleum products' means products derived from crude oils
through processes such as catalytic cracking and fractional distillation. These include
gasoline, kerosene, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), No.2 Fuel Oil, No.4 Fuel Oil, No.5
Fuel Oil (Bunker B), and No.6 Fuel Oil (Bunker C).

       (G) 'LPG' means liquefied petroleum gas.

       (H) 'DOE' means Department of Energy.

       (I) 'DTI' means Department of Trade and Industry.

       SEC. 4. Civil Penalty for Unreasonable Price Increases -

        (A) In General. Not later than three (3) weeks after the date of enactment of this
Act, the Secretary of Energy, in coordination with the Secretary of Trade and Industry,
shall issue regulations that:

        (1) Apply to all crude oil, residual fuel oil, and refined petroleum products that
are sold and used as energy fuel in the Philippines;

       (2) Prohibit any unreasonable price increase for such products by an
energy‑producing company; and

      (3) Impose a civil penalty of not less than Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00) but
not more than Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00) for each unreasonable price increase.

       (B) Determination of Unreasonable Price Increase. The DOE, in coordination
with the DTI, shall determine at least bi-annually whether any energy‑producing
company has implemented an unreasonable price increase in violation of regulations
issued under paragraph (A) of this Section.

       SEC. 5. Weekly Monitoring of Fuel Prices. – The Department of Energy (DOE),
in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall establish and
implement a weekly monitoring system for the prices of crude oil, fuel oil, and refined
petroleum products sold and distributed within the Philippines.

       The monitoring system shall:
       (A) Track and record weekly movements in domestic pump prices and
wholesale prices of petroleum products;
       (B) Compare domestic price adjustments with movements in international
crude oil benchmarks, importation costs, and other relevant production and
operational cost indicators;
       (C) Require energy-producing companies and fuel distributors to submit
relevant pricing, cost, and supply information necessary for effective monitoring; and
       (D) Serve as the primary basis for the determination of unreasonable price
increases under Section 5(B) of this Act, including the conduct of investigations and
enforcement of civil penalties provided under this Act.

       The DOE shall maintain records of such monitoring and shall publish periodic
reports to promote transparency and inform the public of fuel price movements.

        SEC. 6. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision not otherwise affected
shall remain valid and subsisting.

       SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provision of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

      SEC. 8. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
