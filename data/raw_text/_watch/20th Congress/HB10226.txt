      Globally, governments are shifting toward preventive frameworks.
Australia has enacted a minimum age ban on social media access, while
European jurisdictions, such as France, have adopted parental consent models.
Legislative proposals in the United States also aim to restrict algorithmic
targeting of minors and impose heightened duties of care on platforms. These
developments signal a growing international consensus that protecting minors
in the digital environment requires proactive, design-based regulation and
shared responsibility among the State, parents, and digital service providers.

       The "Responsible Social Media Access for Minors Act" proposes a hybrid
regulatory approach, balancing the State's duty to protect minors, the
constitutional rights to expression and access to information, and the primary
role of parents and guardians. It recognizes evolving capacities of minors while
ensuring that digital environments are designed with their safety and well-being
in mind.

      Instead of a total prohibition, the bill introduces a tiered system of access
combined with strict platform accountability, ensuring both protection and
proportionality. By embedding safeguards directly into the platform design and
access mechanisms, the measure seeks to mitigate risks before harm occurs,
thereby promoting a safer and more developmentally appropriate digital
environment for Filipino youth.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
 sought.

                            AN ACT
  ESTABLISHING A TIERED REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR MINORS’
  ACCESS TO SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Responsible Social Media
Access for Minors Act.”
SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to safeguard minors from harmful digital environments while upholding their
rights to information and expression, and recognizing the primary role of parents
and guardians in child development. The State shall ensure that social media
platforms are designed and operated in a manner consistent with the best
interests of the child.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

a. Minor – Any person below eighteen (18) years of age
b. Adolescent Minor – Fifteen (15) to below (18) years of age
c. Social Media Platform – Any online service that primarily enables users to
create, share, and disseminate user-generated content to a broad audience, and
to interact with other users through public or semi-public networks.
d. Age Assurance System – Privacy-preserving mechanisms to determine or
estimate a user’s age
e. High-Risk Features - Platform features that are reasonably likely to encourage
compulsive or excessive use, including but not limited to algorithmically curated
feeds, autoplay, push notifications designed to engage users, and infinite scroll
 mechanisms.
SECTION 4. Tiered Access Framework. A tiered regulatory system for social
media access is hereby established:
       a. Below Fifteen (15) Years Old. Prohibited from creating or maintaining
       social media accounts. Platforms shall block access except for education­
       specific or closed-network platforms
        b. Fifteen (15) to below eighteen (18) years Old. Access to social media
        platforms shall require verified parental consent for initial account
        creation, after which users may independently assess, provided that:
        i.)     Default accounts to high privacy settings;
        ii.)    Disable or limit high-risk feature;
        iii.)   Prohibit targeted advertising and behavioral profiling; and
         iv.)   Provide accessible safety tools, including time-use dashboards,
                content-filters, and reporting mechanisms.

  SECTION 5. Obligations of Social Media Platforms. All covered platforms
  operating in the Philippines shall: (a) implement robust and reliable age
  assurance systems prior to account creation; (b) apply graduated protections
  based on user age; (c) Disable or restrict high-risk features for users below
  eighteen (18); (d) provide clear, accessible parental control tools; (e) Ensure
   privacy-by-design and safety-by-design architecture; (f) Establish fast-track
   reporting and takedown mechanisms for harms involving minors; and (g) submit
   annual transparency reports to regulatory authority in such form and containing
   such information as may be required under this Act and its implementing rules
   and regulations.
    SECTION 6. Prohibited Acts. It shall be unlawful for any platform to allow
    access in violation of the tiered fra.IUework; to engage in behavioral profiling of,
    or targeted advertising directed at minors; to employ manipulative or deceptive
    design practices that materially encourage excessive use among minors; and to
    fail without justifiable cause, to act with reasonable promptness on verified
     reports involving harm to minors.
     SECTION 7. Parental Rights, Responsibilities, and Accountability. Parents
     or legal guardians shall have the right to provide or withdraw consent for a
     minor's access to a covered platform, to access and utilize platform-provided
     supervision and control tools, and to request the deletion, deactivation, or
restriction of a minor's account, in accordance with this Act and its implementing
rules and regulations.
In the exercise of these rights, parents or guardians shall have the corresponding
responsibility to exercise reasonable supervision over a minor's access to social
media platforms and to take appropriate measures to ensure compliance with
the provisions of this Act.
A parent or legal guardian shall be presumed to have acted in good faith in the
supervision of a minor's access to social media platforms, unless there is clear
and convincing evidence to the contracy.
Any parent or legal guardian who knowingly and willfully permits or facilitates a
minor's access to a social media platform in violation of this Act shall, after due
notice and hearing, be subject to the following:
    a. First offense- Mandatory attendance in a digital parenting or online safety
       seminar to be administered by the Department of Social Welfare and
       Development (DSWD or other accredited institutions;

    b. Second Offense- A fine of not less than Ten Thousand Pesos (Php
       10,000.00) but not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 50,000.00),
       taking into account the circumstances of the violation;

    c. Subsequent Offenses- A fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php
       50,000.00) but not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php
       100,000.00), and mandatory participation in enhanced parental
       responsibility programs.
 Penalties under this section shall be imposed only upon a finding of willful
 conduct or gross negligence, with full observance of due process.
 No penalty shall be imposed where the parent or guardian demonstrates good
 faith efforts to supervise or restrict the minor's access, including the use of
 available parental controls or other reasonable safeguards.
