                           AN ACT
GRANTING FULL TAX BENEFITS TO GOVERNMENT PURCHASES DURING
       A STATE OF CALAMITY, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippines remains among the countries most exposed and
vulnerable to natural and climate-related hazards. Its location along the Pacific
Ring of Fire, archipelagic geography, extensive coastline, and exposure to tropical
cyclones, flooding, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, droughts, and other
extreme-weather events make the country particularly susceptible to recurrent
disasters. In the 2025 World Risk Index, the Philippines ranked first among 193
countries, with a “very high” disaster-risk score of 46.56, underscoring the
continuing urgency of strengthening the country’s disaster preparedness,
response, and recovery systems.

       The country’s experience with the COVID-19 pandemic further
demonstrated that large-scale public emergencies may arise not only from
natural hazards but also from public-health threats. The pandemic disrupted
livelihoods, overwhelmed health systems, and required the immediate
mobilization of public resources for medicines, vaccines, personal protective
equipment, testing supplies, food assistance, emergency shelters, and other
essential goods and services.

     To address the perennial challenge of the country, the State has enacted
Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Act of 2010,” which institutionalized a
comprehensive, all-hazards, and multi-sectoral approach to disaster risk
reduction and management. Republic Act No. 10821, or the “Children’s
Emergency Relief and Protection Act,” likewise requires special protection and
emergency relief for children affected by disasters and other emergency
situations.

        More recently, Republic Act No. 12009, or the “New Government
Procurement Act,” authorizes negotiated procurement in emergency cases,
including when there is imminent danger to life or property during a state of
calamity, when time is of the essence due to natural or human-induced
calamities, or when immediate action is necessary to prevent loss of life or
property and restore essential public services. While this mechanism expedites
government procurement, government agencies may still incur value-added tax,
excise tax, customs duties, and other applicable taxes in the purchase or
importation of urgently needed goods and services, thereby reducing the quantity
of life-saving assistance that limited emergency funds can procure.

       This bill seeks to grant full tax benefits to qualified government purchases
made during a duly declared state of calamity. By exempting such purchases
from applicable taxes, duties, fees, and charges, the measure will enable the
government to obtain more relief goods, medicines, medical equipment,
emergency shelter materials, food, water, fuel, rescue equipment, and other
critical supplies using the same appropriated funds.

      The proposed measure complements existing disaster-risk-reduction and
emergency-procurement laws by strengthening the government’s fiscal capacity
to respond immediately and effectively to calamities. It ensures that public
resources are directed principally toward the urgent needs of disaster-affected
individuals, families, and communities, while promoting accessible, equitable,
and timely delivery of emergency assistance and essential public services.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT

       A STATE OF CALAMITY, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Applicability. – This Act shall apply to any national government
agency or local government unit which purchases goods for distribution during
a state of calamity.

SECTION 2. State of Calamity. - A state of calamity shall be understood as a
condition involving mass casualties and/or major damages to property,
disruption of means of livelihood, roads, and normal way of life of people in the
affected areas as a result of the occurrence of a natural or human-induced
hazard. Provided, that there must first be a declaration from the President that
there is a state of calamity in a local government unit or in the country.

SECTION 3. Full Tax Benefits. - For purposes of this Act, full tax benefits shall
pertain to the following:

      (a) Value Added Tax Exemption. – The purchases of any national
          government agency or local government unit of food, medicines,
          medical supplies, clothing, and hygiene kits for distribution during a
          state of calamity shall be exempted from value-added tax; and

      (b) Customs Duties Exemption. – The purchases of any national government
          agency or local government unit of food, medicines, medical supplies,
         clothing, and hygiene kits for distribution during a state of calamity
         shall be exempted from customs duties.

SECTION 4. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected
thereby shall remain in force and effect.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of existing laws, orders, rules,
and regulations or parts thereof which are in conflict or inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days
following the completion of its publication either in the Official Gazette or in a
newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines.
