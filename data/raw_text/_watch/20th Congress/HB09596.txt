                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that the State shall protect and
advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with the
rhythm and harmony of nature. In pursuit of this constitutional mandate, Congress
enacted various environmental laws, including Republic Act No. 9003 or the
“Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000,” Republic Act No. 8749 or the
“Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999,” Republic Act No. 9275 or the “Philippine Clean
Water Act of 2004,” and Republic Act No. 9729 or the “Climate Change Act of 2009.”

          In the landmark case of Oposa v. Factoran, the Supreme Court eloquently
affirmed the doctrine of intergenerational responsibility, recognizing the duty of the
present generation to protect the environment for “generations yet unborn.” The
Court declared that the right to a balanced and healthful ecology is so basic that it
“need not even be written in the Constitution for they are assumed to exist from the
inception of humankind.” This principle underscores the urgent need to
institutionalize effective and accountable environmental governance mechanisms at
the local level to ensure that natural resources are preserved, protected, and
sustainably managed for present and future Filipinos.

          Despite the existence of these landmark environmental laws, implementation
at the local government level remains weak and inconsistent. Many provinces, cities,
and municipalities still lack permanent and properly staffed environment offices
capable of effectively enforcing environmental laws, managing solid waste, protecting
watersheds and coastal resources, regulating extractive activities, and implementing
climate adaptation and disaster resilience programs.




                                                                                     1
         In many local government units (LGUs), environmental functions are merely
assigned as additional duties to existing offices or personnel without specialized
expertise, technical support, or institutional continuity. As a result, environmental
governance suffers from fragmented implementation, poor enforcement, inadequate
planning, and limited accountability.

        The increasing threats posed by climate change, improper waste disposal,
flooding, deforestation, water pollution, biodiversity loss, and environmental
degradation underscore the urgent need to institutionalize permanent Local
Environment and Natural Resources Offices in all provinces, cities, and municipalities
nationwide.

         This measure seeks to establish and institutionalize permanent Provincial
Environment and Natural Resources Offices (PENROs) and Municipal/City
Environment and Natural Resources Offices (MENROs/CENROs) in the entire
country, provide for plantilla positions with minimum qualification standards, create
dedicated environmental enforcement units, mandate annual environmental
performance audits, and establish dedicated local environmental funds to ensure
sustained implementation of environmental programs and policies.

         By strengthening local environmental governance and institutional capacity,
this proposed measure aims to empower LGUs to become more proactive,
accountable, and effective stewards of the environment and natural resources for
present and future generations.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING A LOCAL ENVIRONMENT AND NATURAL
      RESOURCES OFFICE IN ALL PROVINCES, CITIES, AND MUNICIPALITIES,
             PROVIDING FOR PERMANENT PLANTILLA POSITIONS,
          ENVIRONMENTAL ENFORCEMENT UNITS, ENVIRONMENTAL
       PERFORMANCE AUDITS, AND DEDICATED LOCAL ENVIRONMENTAL
                    FUNDS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:


 1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Local Environment
 2   and Natural Resources Office Institutionalization Act.”

 3          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 4   protect and promote the constitutional right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology
 5   and to strengthen environmental governance through institutionalized, professionalized, and
 6   adequately funded local environmental offices in all local government units.

 7          The State recognizes the vital role of local government units in environmental
 8   protection, ecological solid waste management, climate change adaptation and
 9   mitigation, biodiversity conservation, pollution control, watershed management, and
10   sustainable development.

11          SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all provinces, cities, and municipalities
12   nationwide.




                                                                                                 3
 1          SEC. 4. Creation and Institutionalization of Local Environment and Natural
 2   Resources Offices- The creation and institutionalization shall be as follows:

 3      (a) Every province shall establish and maintain a permanent Provincial
 4          Environment and Natural Resources Office (PENRO).
 5      (b) Every city and municipality shall establish and maintain a permanent City or
 6          Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office (CENRO/MENRO).
 7      (c) The PENRO, CENRO, or MENRO shall be a regular department or office under
 8          the organizational structure of the local government unit concerned.

 9          SEC. 5. Powers and Functions.- The PENRO, CENRO, or MENRO shall have the
10   following powers and functions:

11      (a) Formulate and implement local environmental management plans, policies,
12          and programs;

13      (b) Enforce environmental laws, ordinances, rules, and regulations;

14      (c) Implement ecological solid waste management programs pursuant to Republic
15          Act No. 9003;

16      (d) Conduct environmental monitoring, inspection, and compliance activities;

17      (e) Manage reforestation, watershed protection, and biodiversity conservation
18          programs;

19      (f) Coordinate climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction initiatives
20          related to environmental protection;

21      (g) Regulate and monitor local extractive and environmentally critical activities
22          consistent with existing laws;

23      (h) Conduct environmental education and community awareness campaigns;

24      (i) Maintain environmental databases, mapping systems, and records;

25      (j) Coordinate with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
26          (DENR), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Climate
27          Change Commission (CCC), and other relevant agencies; and

                                                                                       4
 1      (k) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the provisions
 2          of this Act.

 3          SEC. 7. Staffing Pattern and Qualification Standards- The staff qualifications are as
 4   follows:

 5      (a) A PENRO, CENRO, or MENRO shall be headed by a permanent Environment and
 6          Natural Resources Officer appointed by the local chief executive.
 7      (b) The Environment and Natural Resources Officer shall preferably possess:

 8                1) A bachelor’s degree in environmental science, forestry, biology,
 9                   environmental engineering, agriculture, fisheries, natural resource
10                   management, or related fields;

11                2) Relevant training and experience in environmental management; and

12                3) Eligibility as required under civil service laws, rules, and regulations.

13          (c) The LGUs shall create the necessary plantilla positions, technical personnel,
14   and support staff necessary for the effective implementation of this Act, subject to
15   existing budgeting, civil service, and compensation laws.

16          SEC. 8. Environmental Enforcement Units- Every province, city, and municipality
17   shall establish an Environmental Enforcement Unit under the PENRO, CENRO, or MENRO
18   tasked to:

19      (a) Conduct environmental inspections and monitoring;

20      (b) Apprehend violators of environmental laws and ordinances;

21      (c) Issue notices of violation and environmental citations as may be authorized by
22          law or ordinance;

23      (d) Assist in anti-illegal dumping, anti-pollution, anti-illegal logging, anti-illegal
24          quarrying, and related environmental operations; and

25      (e) Coordinate with law enforcement agencies and national government agencies
26          in environmental protection activities.




                                                                                                 5
 1          Personnel assigned to the Environmental Enforcement Unit shall undergo
 2   mandatory environmental law enforcement training.

 3          SEC. 9. Annual Environmental Performance Audit – The performance of the
 4   department shall be evaluated through these indicators:

 5          (a) All provinces, cities, and municipalities shall conduct an annual environmental
 6   performance audit to assess compliance with environmental laws, policies, and programs.

 7          (b) The audit shall include, among others:

 8              1) Solid waste management compliance;

 9              2) Functionality of Materials Recovery Facilities (MRFs);

10              3) Waste diversion rates;

11              4) Water and air quality management initiatives;

12              5) Reforestation and conservation activities;

13              6) Climate adaptation and mitigation programs;

14              7) Environmental law enforcement accomplishments; and

15              8) Utilization of environmental funds.

16          (c) The DILG, in coordination with the DENR and CCC, shall develop a
17   standardized environmental audit and performance evaluation system.

18          SEC. 10. Local Environmental Fund - Every province, city, and municipality shall
19   establish a Local Environmental Fund exclusively for environmental protection, conservation,
20   enforcement, climate adaptation, and ecological programs.

21          The Local Environment Fund may be sourced from any of the following:

22          (a) Annual appropriations of the LGU;

23          (b) Environmental fees and charges;

24          (c) Grants, donations, and endowments;

25          (d) Fines and penalties collected from environmental violations; and


                                                                                               6
 1          (e) Such other lawful sources as may be determined by the LGU.

 2          The Local Environmental Fund shall be used exclusively for:

 3          (a) Environmental programs and projects;

 4          (b) Purchase of equipment and facilities;

 5          (c) Capacity-building and training;

 6          (d) Environmental enforcement activities;

 7          (e) Disaster resilience and climate adaptation projects; and

 8          (f) Community-based environmental initiatives.

 9          SEC. 10. Coordination with National Government Agencies- The DENR, DILG,
10   CCC, and other relevant agencies shall provide technical assistance, training, and
11   capacity-building support to LGUs in the implementation of this Act.

12          SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from
13   the effectivity of this Act, the DENR and DILG, in coordination with the CCC,
14   Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and Civil Service Commission (CSC),
15   shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
16   of this Act.

17          SEC 12. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation
18   of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned
19   agencies and LGUs. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be included
20   in the annual General Appropriations Act and local budgets.

21          SEC. 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
22   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
23   continue to be in full force and effect.

24          SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
25   regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
26   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
