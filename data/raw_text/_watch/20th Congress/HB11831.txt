WOMEN”

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 9710, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Women,”
established the Gender and Development (GAD) budget as a critical instrument for
mainstreaming gender equality in government planning, programming, and resource
allocation. It requires government agencies and instrumentalities to allocate at least
five percent (5%) of their total budget appropriations for programs, activities, and
projects (PAPs) that address gender issues and advance gender equality.

The five percent (5%) requirement is an important statutory commitment.
However, compliance with a financial threshold, by itself, cannot be the measure of
whether government is effectively advancing gender equality. Public resources devoted
to GAD must be directed toward identified gender issues, properly attributed, actually
utilized, reach their intended beneficiaries, and produce measurable and verifiable
results.

Government audit findings demonstrate why this distinction is necessary. The
Commission on Audit (COA), through its GAD Audit Performance Summary Reports,
has documented recurring deficiencies in the implementation of the GAD budget.
Among the common audit observations identified are the absence of GAD Plans and
Budgets, GAD allocations below the required five percent (5%), underutilization of GAD
funds, and partial implementation of approved GAD Plans and Budgets. COA has noted
that some of these deficiencies have remained prevalent even in more recent audit
periods. The COA’s consolidated reporting for Calendar Years 2019 to 2023 further
underscores the continuing need to strengthen the implementation, monitoring, and
accountability mechanisms governing GAD resources.

These findings are significant because they demonstrate that the challenge is not
limited to whether the required amount has been allocated. The more fundamental
question is whether resources reported as GAD expenditures are genuinely responsive
to identified gender issues and whether they are producing the results for which they
were appropriated.

===== [page 2] =====
COA’s existing audit framework itself recognizes the need to examine more than
financial compliance. Its audit procedures include determining whether GAD programs,
activities, and projects are correctly attributed to GAD and whether they have achieved
their stated goals, objectives, targets, and performance indicators. Where
implementation falls short, auditors are expected to determine the causes and
recommend appropriate corrective measures. The audit framework likewise recognizes
the need to address deficiencies through audit observations and, where warranted,
notices and other appropriate audit actions.

Despite these existing mechanisms, gaps remain in translating the five percent
(5%) requirement into a consistent, measurable, and results-oriented system of public
accountability. Paper compliance, unsupported or inappropriate attribution of
expenditures, routine expenditures charged to GAD, underutilization of available funds,
incomplete implementation, weak outcome measurement, and recurring audit
observations can diminish the effectiveness of the GAD budget and weaken public
confidence in the use of resources intended to advance gender equality.

This measure therefore seeks to strengthen the statutory five percent (5%) GAD
budget requirement under Republic Act No. 9710. It establishes a clearer connection
between GAD allocations, identified gender issues, program objectives, measurable
targets, performance indicators, actual beneficiaries, and verifiable outcomes.

The bill introduces an outcome-based framework under which every GAD
program, activity, and project must demonstrate a clear relationship between the
gender issue being addressed, the resources allocated, the intervention undertaken,
and the results achieved. It seeks to ensure that the amount attributed to GAD
corresponds to the actual gender-responsive component of the program or activity and
is supported by appropriate documentation and a defensible basis for attribution.

The measure further strengthens the preparation, review, certification,
monitoring, and reporting of GAD Plans and Budgets and GAD Accomplishment
Reports. It establishes more consistent standards for budget attribution and requires
agencies to maintain adequate documentation and outcome information that can be
subjected to audit and verification.

The bill also strengthens coordination between the Philippine Commission on
Women (PCW), the Commission on Audit (COA), the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), and other concerned oversight and implementing agencies. The
framework is intended to harmonize planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring,
and audit so that the five percent (5%) requirement is not treated as an isolated
budgeting exercise but as part of a continuing cycle of gender analysis, resource
allocation, implementation, measurement, audit, and corrective action.

Public transparency is likewise strengthened by requiring the systematic
reporting of GAD allocations, utilization, attribution, implementation status, audit
observations, corrective actions, and measurable results. Annual reporting to Congress
will provide a basis for legislative oversight and allow policymakers to determine
whether public resources devoted to GAD are addressing persistent gender gaps and
producing meaningful improvements.

Accountability mechanisms are likewise provided for improper attribution,
unsupported GAD expenditures, misuse or diversion of GAD resources, failure to

===== [page 3] =====
implement approved GAD programs without sufficient justification, and persistent
noncompliance with statutory requirements. These mechanisms are intended not
merely to penalize deficiencies, but to promote correction, institutional learning, and
continuous improvement in the implementation of gender-responsive programs.

The measure recognizes that not every gender-responsive intervention can be
reduced to an immediate or single quantitative outcome. Accordingly, the proposed
framework allows the use of appropriate qualitative and quantitative indicators,
baseline information, output and outcome measures, and other verifiable evidence
suited to the nature of the intervention. What is essential is that every expenditure
attributed to GAD must have a demonstrable and documented connection to an
identified gender issue and a reasonable basis for determining whether the intervention
achieved its intended purpose.

The objective, therefore, is not to require government to spend more merely for
the sake of meeting a percentage. It is to ensure that the resources already required
by law are used more deliberately, transparently, and effectively.

Every peso counted toward the five percent (5%) GAD requirement should be
capable of answering four fundamental questions: What gender issue is being
addressed? Why is the expenditure properly attributable to GAD? What was
accomplished with the resources provided? And what measurable or verifiable change
resulted?

By strengthening attribution, outcome measurement, monitoring, audit,
transparency, and accountability while retaining the existing statutory commitment to
a minimum five percent (5%) GAD budget, this measure seeks to transform GAD
budgeting from a primarily compliance-oriented exercise into a more credible
instrument for achieving gender equality and women's empowerment.

In the end, the measure advances a simple but essential principle: GAD
budgeting must be measured not merely by the amount tagged, but by the gender gap
addressed, the public resources properly used, the people reached, and the results
achieved.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “GAD Budget
Accountability and Outcomes Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote substantive
equality between women and men and to ensure that public resources allocated for
Gender and Development (GAD) are used effectively, efficiently, transparently, and
accountably to address identified gender issues and advance gender equality and
women's empowerment.

The State recognizes that the minimum five percent (5%) GAD budget under
Republic Act No. 9710 is a fiscal instrument for gender mainstreaming and shall not be
treated as a mere expenditure quota, accounting classification, or compliance
requirement.

Accordingly, the GAD budget shall be anchored on identified gender issues,
evidence-based interventions, measurable targets, and _ verifiable outcomes.
Compliance with the minimum allocation shall be assessed together with the quality,
relevance, utilization, and results of GAD programs, activities, and projects.

SEC. 3. Amendatory Provision. — Section 36 of Republic Act No. 9710, otherwise
known as the “Magna Carta of Women,” is hereby amended by adding new paragraphs
after paragraph (a) thereof, to read as follows:

“Section 36. Gender Mainstreaming as a Strategy for Implementing the Magna
Carta of Women. - Within a period prescribed in the implementing rules and
regulations, the National Commission on the Role of Filipino Women (NCRFW) shall
assess its gender mainstreaming program for consistency with the standards under this
Act. It shall modify the program accordingly to ensure that it will be an effective
strategy for implementing this Act and attaining its objectives.

Ail departments, including their attached agencies, offices, bureaus, state
universities and colleges, government-owned and -controlled corporations, local
government units, and other government instrumentalities shall adopt gender
mainstreaming as a strategy to promote women's human rights and eliminate gender
discrimination in their systems, structures, policies, programs, processes, and
procedures which shall include, but not limited to, the following:

(a) Planning, budgeting, monitoring and evaluation for GAD. GAD programs
addressing gender issues and concerns shall be designed and implemented based on
the mandate of government agencies and local government units, Republic Act No.
7192, gender equality agenda of the government and other GAD-related legislation,
policies, and commitments. The development of GAD programs shall proceed from the

generation and review of sex-disaggregated data; and consultation with
gender/women's rights advocates and agency/women clientele. The cost of
implementing GAD programs shall be the agency's or the local government unit's GAD
budget which shall be at least five percent (5%) of the agency's or the local

Pursuant to Republic Act No. 7192, otherwise known as the Women in Development

The utilization and outcome of the GAD budget shall be annually monitored and

gender equality, and GAD.

Local government units are also encouraged to develop and pass a GAD Code based
on the gender issues and concerns in their respective localities based on consultation
with their women constituents and the women's empowerment and gender equality
agenda of the government. The GAD Code shall also serve as basis for identifying
programs, activities, and projects on GAD.

Where needed, temporary gender equity measures shall be provided for in the plans
of all departments, including their attached agencies, offices, bureaus, state
universities and colleges, government-owned and -controlled corporations, local
government units, and other government instrumentalities.

(1) Macro socioeconomic plans such as the Medium-Term Philippine Development

(2) Annual plans of all departments, including their attached agencies, offices,

AND SHALL NOT BE CONSIDERED SATISFIED SOLELY BY THE IDENTIFICATION,
ATTRIBUTION, RESERVATION, OBLIGATION, OR EXPENDITURE OF AN AMOUNT
EQUIVALENT TO AT LEAST FIVE PERCENT (5%) OF TOTAL BUDGET APPROPRIATIONS.

EVERY GAD PROGRAM, ACTIVITY, OR PROJECT CHARGED AGAINST OR
ATTRIBUTED TO THE GAD BUDGET SHALL:

(1) ADDRESS AN IDENTIFIED GENDER ISSUE OR GAP WITHIN THE MANDATE OF
THE IMPLEMENTING AGENCY OR LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT;

(2) IDENTIFY ITS INTENDED BENEFICIARIES OR TARGET POPULATION;
(3) ESTABLISH MEASURABLE OUTPUTS AND OUTCOMES;

(4) IDENTIFY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS AND TARGETS;

(S) SPECIFY THE AMOUNT AND SOURCE OF THE GAD ATTRIBUTION;

(6) PROVIDE A BASIS FOR DETERMINING THE PROPORTION OF THE COST
ATTRIBUTABLE TO GAD WHERE THE ACTIVITY FORMS PART OF A BROADER REGULAR
PROGRAM OR PROJECT; AND

(7) PROVIDE FOR MONITORING AND EVALUATION OF RESULTS.

GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS SHALL NOT ATTRIBUTE
TO THEIR GAD BUDGETS EXPENDITURES THAT ARE UNRELATED TO AN IDENTIFIED
GENDER ISSUE OR THAT MERELY CONSTITUTE ROUTINE OR GENERAL
ADMINISTRATIVE EXPENDITURES WITHOUT A DEMONSTRATED AND DOCUMENTED
GENDER-RESPONSIVE PURPOSE.

WHERE A REGULAR PROGRAM OR PROJECT HAS GENDER-RESPONSIVE
COMPONENTS, ONLY THE PORTION OF ITS COST THAT IS REASONABLY
ATTRIBUTABLE TO THE IDENTIFIED GENDER-RESPONSIVE INTERVENTION MAY BE
CHARGED OR ATIRIBUTED TO THE GAD BUDGET, SUBJECT TO STANDARDS
PRESCRIBED BY THE PHILIPPINE COMMISSION ON WOMEN, IN COORDINATION WITH
THE DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT AND THE COMMISSION ON AUDIT.

(C) MULTI-YEAR GAD PROGRAMMING. AGENCIES AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS

[(c)] (G) Generation and Maintenance of GAD Database. All departments, including

SEC. 4. New Section 36-A. — Republic Act No. 9710 is hereby amended by inserting
a new Section 36-A after Section 36, to read as follows:

“SEC. 36-A. GAD Budget Eligibility and Attribution. — The PCW, in coordination with
the DBM and COA, shall establish a standardized GAD Budget Eligibility and Attribution
Framework.

The Framework shall establish objective criteria for determining:

(a) Whether a program, activity, or project is responsive to an identified gender
issue;

(b) whether a proposed expenditure may properly be charged against the GAD
budget;

(c) the proportion of the cost of a mainstreamed program or project that may be
attributed to GAD;

(d) the minimum documentation required to support an attribution;

(e) the outcome and performance indicators applicable to different classes of GAD
interventions; and

(f) the circumstances under which an expenditure shall be considered ineligible for
GAD attribution.

The Framework shall be periodically reviewed to reflect changes in national gender
equality priorities, government programs, statistical evidence, and audit findings.

The following shall not, by themselves, constitute sufficient basis for GAD
attribution:

(1) Routine office operations or genera! administrative overhead;

(2) general office supplies, utilities, maintenance, or equipment without a
demonstrated and documented gender-responsive purpose;

(3) personnel compensation that does not correspond to identifiable GAD functions
or interventions;

(4) generic meetings, conferences, seminars, trainings, information campaigns, or
commemorative activities that do not address an identified gender issue or have
measurable gender-responsive objectives;

(5) infrastructure projects without a demonstrable gender-responsive component;

(6) expenditures mandated by law that are not specifically designed or implemented
to address an identified gender issue; and

(7) other expenditures identified by the PCW, DBM, or COA as ineligible under the
GAD Budget Eligibility and Attribution Framework.

Nothing in this Section shall prevent the attribution of a legitimate gender-
responsive component of a regular program, project, or activity, provided that the

===== [page 9] =====
amount attributed is supported by a reasonable methodology and adequate
documentation.”

SEC. 19. GAD Budget as a Fiscal Accountability Instrument. — The GAD budget
shall be considered a component of the government's gender mainstreaming strategy
and a public accountability mechanism.

Compliance shall therefore be assessed through three separate but related
dimensions:

(a)Allocation compliance — whether the required minimum budget has been
provided;

(b) Attribution and utilization compliance — whether the amounts charged or
attributed to GAD are eligible, supported, properly utilized, and consistent with the
approved GPB; and

(c) Results compliance — whether the interventions produced the intended outputs
and contributed to measurable gender equality outcomes.

===== [page 17] =====
Failure in one dimension shall not be concealed or offset by compliance in another
dimension.

SEC. 20. No Paper Compliance. — No government agency or local government unit
shall be considered fully compliant with the GAD budget requirement solely because it
has identified or reported an amount equivalent to at least five percent (5%) of its
total budget appropriations.

The PCW shall distinguish in its annual compliance reports between:

(a) allocation compliance;

(b) planning compliance;

(c) attribution compliance;

(d) utilization compliance;

(e) reporting compliance; and

(f) outcome performance.

SEC. 21. Risk-Based Audit. — The COA and PCW shall adopt a risk-based approach
in selecting agencies and local government units for enhanced audit and evaluation.

Risk factors may include:

(a) Size of GAD budget;

(b) materiality of attributed expenditures;

(c) history of audit findings;

(d) repeated deficiencies in GAD planning or reporting;

(e) substantial variances between allocations and actual utilization;

(f) unusually high attribution to regular programs;

(g) complaints or verified information concerning misuse;

(h) weak outcome performance; and

(i) other indicators of fiscal or programmatic risk.

SEC. 23. Protection of GAD Resources. — No GAD allocation shall be cancelled,
reduced, transferred, or realigned solely because of administrative convenience, failure
of an agency to plan adequately, or the availability of the funds for unrelated purposes.

Where a GAD program becomes unnecessary because its objective has been
achieved or circumstances have materially changed, any modification shall be

supported by appropriate documentation and shall remain consistent with the
objectives of this Act and applicable budgeting laws.

SEC. 24. Capacity-Building. — The PCW, in coordination with the DBM, COA, DILG,
Civil Service Commission, and other concerned agencies, shall establish continuing
capacity-building programs for agency and LGU personnel responsible for:

(a) Gender analysis;

(b) GAD planning and budgeting;

(c) budget attribution;

(d) performance measurement;

(e) monitoring and evaluation;

(f) financial management; and

(g) audit compliance.

Capacity-building expenditures shall themselves be subject to the standards for
GAD attribution under this Act.

SEC. 25. Harmonization with Existing Government Systems. — The mechanisms
established under this Act shall, to the greatest extent practicable, be integrated with
existing government planning, budgeting, accounting, procurement, monitoring, audit,
and reporting systems.

No separate reporting system shall be established where existing systems can
reasonably accommodate the requirements of this Act.

SEC. 26. Local Government Units. — The provisions of this Act concerning GAD
planning, budgeting, utilization, monitoring, and accountability shall apply to local
government units consistent with the Constitution, Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise
known as the “Local Government Code of 1991,” and other applicable laws.

The DILG, DBM, PCW, and other concerned agencies shall issue harmonized
guidelines to ensure that GAD budget accountability is implemented without
unnecessarily increasing administrative burdens on local governments.

SEC. 27. Role of the Philippine Commission on Women. — The PCW shall:

(a) Develop national standards for GAD planning and budgeting;

(b) review and monitor GPBs;

(c) maintain national GAD budget compliance data;

(d) develop the GAD Budget Eligibility and Attribution Framework;

(e) coordinate the Joint GAD Audit Framework with COA;

(f) monitor outcome performance;

(g) provide technical assistance;

(h) publish consolidated reports;

(i) maintain the GAD Budget Transparency Portal; and

(j) recommend policy, administrative, legislative, and budgetary reforms based on
monitoring and evaluation findings.

SEC. 28. Role of the Department of Budget and Management. — The DBM shall:

(a) Integrate the requirements of this Act into the national budget preparation and
execution process;

(b) coordinate with PCW in establishing budget attribution standards;

(c) incorporate appropriate GAD performance information into budget review
processes;

(d) assist in developing mechanisms for monitoring GAD budget releases,
obligations, and expenditures; and

(e) perform such other functions as may be necessary to implement this Act.

SEC. 30. Role of Local Chief Executives and Agency Heads. — The head of each
covered government agency and the local chief executive of each covered local
government unit shall be responsible for ensuring that:

(a) GAD plans and budgets are properly prepared;

(b) expenditures are properly attributed;

(c) GAD resources are used for authorized purposes;

(d) required reports are submitted;

(e) corrective measures are undertaken; and

(f) appropriate internal controls are established.

Such responsibility shall be exercised in accordance with the principle that
accountability attaches to officials according to their actual participation, authority, and
responsibility under applicable law.

SEC. 31. Public Participation and Complaints. — The PCW shall establish accessible
mechanisms through which citizens, women's organizations, civil society organizations,
beneficiaries, and other stakeholders may submit information or complaints
concerning:

(a) Improper GAD budget attribution;

(b) misuse or diversion of GAD funds;

(c) nonimplementation of approved GAD programs;

(d) false or misleading GAD accomplishment reports; or

(e) other violations of this Act.

===== [page 21] =====
Complaints shall be referred to the appropriate government institution when they
fall within the jurisdiction of COA, the Ombudsman, the Civil Service Commission, the
DILG, or another competent authority.

No person shall be subjected to retaliation for making a good-faith report under this
Section, without prejudice to liability for knowingly false or malicious complaints.

SEC. 32. Whistleblower Protection. — A public officer, employee, beneficiary, or
private individual who, in good faith, reports a material violation of this Act or provides

relevant evidence to a competent government institution shall be protected from
retaliation in accordance with existing laws.

The PCW shall establish referral and confidentiality protocols for reports requiring
protection.

SEC. 33. Administrative Sanctions. — Administrative sanctions under existing civil
service, local government, auditing, and other applicable laws shall be imposed upon
responsible officials for violations of this Act.

Where a violation constitutes grave misconduct, serious dishonesty, gross neglect of
duty, conduct prejudicial to the best interest of the service, or another administrative

offense under applicable law, the appropriate administrative authority shall proceed in
accordance with law.

SEC. 34. Offenses Under Other Laws. — If conduct punishable under this Act also
constitutes an offense under the Revised Penal Code, Republic Act No. 3019, Republic
Act No. 6713, Republic Act No. 7160, or any other special law, prosecution under the
applicable law shall not be precluded.

This Act shall not be construed to limit the authority of the Ombudsman, COA, Civil
Service Commission, DILG, Department of Justice, or other competent institutions.

(f) outcomes achieved;

SEC. 36. Periodic Review of the Five Percent (5%) Requirement. — Five (5) years
after the effectivity of this Act, and every five (5) years thereafter, Congress shall
review the implementation and effectiveness of the minimum five percent (5%) GAD
budget requirement.

The review shall consider:

(a) Whether the minimum allocation remains appropriate;

(b) the extent to which GAD budgets address identified gender gaps;

(c) the quality of budget attribution;

(d) utilization and absorption rates;

(e) measurable gender equality outcomes;

(f) recurring audit findings;

(g) administrative costs of compliance;

(h) the effect of GAD expenditures on gender mainstreaming across the remaining
ninety-five percent (95%) of agency or LGU budgets; and

(i) whether further legislative reforms are necessary.

SEC. 37. Implementing Rules and Regulations. -—— Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the PCW, in coordination with the DBM, COA,
DILG, Civil Service Commission, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this
Act.

SEC. 38. Transitory Provision. — Existing GAD Plans and Budgets, guidelines,
reporting mechanisms, and systems shall remain in effect insofar as they are not
inconsistent with this Act.

The PCW, DBM, and COA shall review and harmonize existing GAD budget rules
within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act.

The full outcome-based attribution and audit requirements under this Act shall be
incorporated into the first full budget cycle following the issuance of the implementing
rules and regulations.

SEC. 39. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the PCW, DBM, COA,
DILG, and other concerned agencies, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and
auditing laws.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 40. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shal! remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 41. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 42. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
