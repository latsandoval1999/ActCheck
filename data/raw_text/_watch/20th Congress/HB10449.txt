                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE
      MSMEs or Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises are the backbone of the
Philippine economy. Despite being often unrecognized and under-supported,
they remain key drivers of sustainable growth, generating employment and
contributing greatly in the country’s export earnings.
      Latest data from a Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) report from
2024 showed that MSMEs made up over 99.6% of all the enterprises in the
country1. As each new business evolves and progresses, they serve as “the
engine of net employment growth” while also enhancing the overall quality of
jobs that Filipinos can be employed in. Despite most of them (90.6%) being
micro enterprises or those having less than ten (10) employees or assets less
than P3 million, they still employ more than half of the Philippine workforce.
       By 2024, MSMEs employed 66.6% of the total employment in the
Philippines or around six and a quarter million people. MSMEs also account for
up to 45% of the country’s GDP and contribute to 25% of our total exports
revenue.2 These astounding numbers stem from the nature of their role in the
economy and society. In a study by Dano-Luna, Francisco, and Canare,3 they
said that “[o]n the one hand, [MSMEs] contribute in maintaining the
social-political stability in a country enabling the attainment of inclusive and
sustainable national development. On the other hand, [they] have the potential

       Meanwhile, a study by Miranda and Miranda4, supported the view of

distribution.”
      With all these contributions and more, MSMEs deserve more support
and protection from the government. Their decline would be a significant blow
in the employment of our fellow Filipinos and in our economy as a whole, while
their continued empowerment and growth will strengthen our economic
dynamics. As we have seen in the past few years, such as during the COVID-19
Pandemic, the United States tariffs under the Trump Administration, and the
more recent oil crisis brought on by the Iran-American Conflict in West Asia,
MSMEs are particularly vulnerable to developments brought about by crises,
economic shocks, and geopolitics.
      While there is an increase in pushing for a more robust MSME sector, it
is prudent to institutionalize the aid and support for MSMEs so that we can
ensure their survival and sustainability.
       It is for these reasons that the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING GOVERNMENT AID AND SUPPORT FOR
         MICRO, SMALL AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES (MSMEs)
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “MSME
Empowerment Act.”
      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the
indispensable role of the private sector, specifically the micro, small, and
medium enterprises that make up most of all enterprises in the country. It is
likewise the policy of the State to encourage private enterprise, address
employment, and to provide incentives for needed investments.
      SECTION 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:
      a) Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) pertain to those
business activities or enterprises engaged in industry, agri-business, services,
and technology with an asset size, excluding land, of up to one hundred million
pesos (PHP 100,000,000.00) and an employment size of less than or equal to
two hundred (200) employees. Included in this definition are startups; and
      b) Startups refer to persons or entities registered in the Philippines
which aim to develop innovative products, processes, or business models as
defined in R.A. 11337.
      SECTION 4. Interest-free loans and Interest-free Periods for Loans
Secured by MSMEs. – The Landbank of the Philippines and the Development
Bank of the Philippines shall introduce an interest-free loan program
exclusively for MSMEs, provided that the maximum loanable amount shall not
exceed the MSMEs’ operational costs, including direct labor costs, for a period
of three months, provided further that the MSME is a registered business with
all the necessary permits and licenses, and qualified based on the
requirements set by the bank.
       All government-funded loan programs, the Landbank of the Philippines
and the Development Bank of the Philippines, and other government banking
and lending institutions, shall introduce loan programs with certain
interest-free periods. For a three to five-year loan term, there shall be an
interest-free period of three (3) months, which shall begin simultaneously with
the first monthly installment payment schedule for the loan, for a five to
ten-year loan term, there shall be an interest-free period of six (6) months, and
for loan terms exceeding ten years, there shall be an interest-free period of one
(1) year.
      Notwithstanding the foregoing provisions, the President or the President’s
authorized representative shall have the discretion to provide loan moratorium
for MSMEs, emergency loan programs available only during a crisis or a
national emergency or ease other regulations affecting the ability of MSMEs to
access and secure loans from the government.
      SECTION 5. Technical Assistance to MSMEs. – The DTI, in coordination
with the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), shall provide technical
assistance as well as offer programs for the training and education of MSMEs
on business digitization, artificial intelligence, research and development,
upskilling and reskilling of workers, innovation, and other relevant fields either
through a grant, programmatic campaigns, or for a minimal charge.
       SECTION 6. Stimulus Fund for MSMEs during National Emergencies and
Crisis. – A stimulus fund in the amount of ten billion pesos (P
10,000,000,000.00) shall be made available for distribution to qualified MSME
beneficiaries during national emergencies, a nationwide crisis, or a significant
external trade disruption, or after the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
in coordination with the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev) and the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), certifies the existence of a
significant external trade disruption, provided further that MSMEs who are
critically-impacted, or MSMEs belonging to a certain sector most critically hit
by the national emergency, crisis, or trade disruption shall be given priority in
the distribution.
      For purposes of this Act, a "significant external trade disruption" refers
to adverse changes in the tariff, trade, or market access conditions affecting
Philippine exports, imposed by a foreign government or resulting from an
international trade agreement, policy shift, or conflict, which the DTI
determines has caused or is reasonably expected to cause substantial harm to
the operations, revenues, or workforce of Philippine MSMEs, particularly those
engaged in export-oriented industries.
      The DTI shall be the agency assigned to conduct the application
assessment and distribution of the stimulus fund to MSMEs. It shall ensure
that the documentary requirements and other proof required from MSMEs to
be qualified for the stimulus fund shall not be unnecessarily burdensome or
unreasonable considering the circumstances of the times.
      The Stimulus Fund shall not be lower than one hundred percent (100%)
of their direct labor costs for a month but shall not exceed P100,000.00 for
every qualified MSME.
       SECTION 7. Accountability and Transparency Mechanism. – To ensure
the proper, transparent, and accountable use of the Stimulus Fund, the
following safeguards shall apply:
     a) The DTI shall submit a semi-annual report to the President and the
Commission on Audit (COA), disclosing the total amount released, the number
and names of MSME beneficiaries, the amount received by each beneficiary,
and the sector and region of each beneficiary, for any quarter in which the
Stimulus Fund has been activated; and
      b) The COA shall conduct a mandatory post-disbursement audit of the
Stimulus Fund no later than after one (1) fiscal year after the lifting of the
national emergency, nationwide crisis, or trade disruption that triggered its
release, and shall submit its findings.
       SECTION 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.
      SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SECTION 9. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
