                          AN ACT
EXCLUDING SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES FROM THE VALUE-ADDED TAX BASE
ON ELECTRICITY, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 106 OF THE
NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Under existing policy, the Value-Added Tax (VAT) is imposed not only on electricity actually
delivered to households and businesses but also on system loss charges passed on by
distribution utilities. System loss refers to electricity generated but not ultimately delivered to
end-users due to line dissipation, technical inefficiencies in transmission and distribution
systems, and, in some instances, pilferage or unauthorized connections.

In its current application, consumers are effectively made to pay VAT on electricity they never
consumed. This contradicts the fundamental principle of VAT as a tax on consumption. By
design, VAT is intended to apply only to the value of goods and services actually supplied,
delivered, and consumed. However, system loss charges do not represent actual consumption
by end-users; rather, they reflect recoverable costs associated with electricity that is lost before
it reaches the consumer.

Under Republic Act No. 9136 (EPIRA), system losses are recognized as part of the operational
costs of distribution utilities, subject to caps and regulatory review by the Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC). These charges are allowed to be passed on to consumers strictly as a cost-
recovery mechanism, not as payment for actual electricity usage. ¹ ² As such, they are
fundamentally different in nature from charges for electricity that is metered and consumed.

In practice, system loss levels in the Philippines are typically allowed at up to approximately
8.5% for private distribution utilities and 13% for electric cooperatives, subject to regulatory
caps.² This means that a portion of every electricity bill represents electricity that was never
delivered to the consumer. When VAT is applied to this portion, households effectively pay a
12% tax on 8–13% of non-consumed electricity, translating to an estimated additional burden
of around 1% to 1.5% of the total electricity bill. While seemingly modest on a per-bill basis,
this accumulates significantly over time, particularly for low- and middle-income households
already burdened by high energy costs.
            The continued inclusion of system loss charges in the VAT base thus expands the scope of
            VAT beyond consumption and into cost recovery, thereby distorting the tax base. This results
            in consumers bearing an additional tax burden on inefficiencies and losses that are inherent in
            the system and beyond their control. International VAT principles, as articulated in guidelines
            by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, emphasize that VAT should
            be levied on final consumption and should not apply to amounts that do not correspond to an
            actual supply consumed by the taxpayer.³

            At a time when Filipino households are already facing rising electricity rates, inflationary
            pressures, and broader economic challenges, the imposition of VAT on system loss charges
            further aggravates financial strain. A fair and equitable tax system requires that such distortions
            be corrected.

            This measure, therefore, proposes to expressly limit the VAT base on electricity to actual
            metered consumption, and to exclude system loss charges from VAT computation. By
            clarifying the tax base in this manner, the bill restores consistency with the fundamental nature
            of VAT, ensures that consumers are taxed only on what they actually consume, and prevents
            the undue taxation of inefficiencies embedded in the power distribution system.

            By doing so, the bill promotes fairness, enhances consumer protection, and contributes to
            easing the cost of living for millions of Filipinos.

            In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Electricity VAT Fairness Act.”

SEC. 2. Amendment. Section 106(A)(1) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended,
is hereby further amended to read as follows:

         “SEC. 106. Value-Added Tax on Sale of Goods or Properties.

         (A) Rate and Base of Tax. — There shall be levied, assessed, and collected on every sale,
         barter, or exchange of goods or properties, a value-added tax equivalent to twelve percent
         (12%) of the gross sales of the goods or properties sold, bartered, or exchanged, such tax to be
         paid by the seller or transferor.

         (1) ‘Goods or Properties.’ — The term ‘goods’ or ‘properties’ shall mean all tangible and
         intangible objects which are capable of pecuniary estimation and shall include:

                  (a) Real properties held primarily for sale to customers or held for lease in the
                      ordinary course of trade or business;
                  (b) The right or privilege to use patent, copyright, design or model, plan, secret
                      formula or process, goodwill, trademark, trade brand, or other like property or
                      right;
                  (c) The right or privilege to use in the Philippines any industrial, commercial, or
                      scientific equipment;
                  (d) The right or privilege to use motion picture films, tapes, and discs; and
                  (e) Radio, television, satellite transmission, and cable television time.

         Provided, That in the sale of electricity by generation companies, transmission entities, and
         distribution utilities, the value-added tax shall apply only to actual metered electricity
         delivered to and consumed by the end-user, and shall exclude system loss charges, as defined
         under Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of
         2001.”
SEC. 3. Definition of Term. For purposes of this Act, “System Loss Charges” shall refer to recoverable
technical and non-technical losses, as authorized and regulated by the Energy Regulatory Commission
pursuant to Republic Act No. 9136.

SEC. 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
the Bureau of Internal Revenue, in coordination with the Energy Regulatory Commission, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

SEC. 5. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations, or parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 6. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
