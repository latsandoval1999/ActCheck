                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

     At every stage of the electric system—from generation power plants to
     substations of distribution utilities (DUs) up to household outlets—there are
     bound to be energy losses. System losses are inherent when electricity is
     transported from one place to another due to several reasons such as heat,
     noise, pilferage, and technical losses. The only way to zero-out system loss in
     the power system is to turn the power off.

     In a Senate Committee on Energy hearing conducted last 2017, a number of
     DUs presented simulations if reduction of technical losses to 5% will be
     required from them. Using 2017 financial parameters, Angeles Electric Corp.
     will need to spend P892 million, Cagayan Electric Power and Light Company
     will be needing P1.84 billion, and Meralco would have to spend P65.53 billion.
     Repeated simulations showed that the costs that the utilities will incur in
     investing on infrastructure will significantly outweigh any possible decrease
     in the system loss charge. Capital Expenditure (CAPEX) is a pass on charge
     to the consumers, hence it will eventually lead to electricity rate increases to
     customers.

     Since it is scientifically and technically impossible to eliminate system losses
     completely, governments and electric regulators around the world allow
     electric DUs to recover minimal system loss rates as part of their regulated
     charges. In the Philippines, the Congress enacted the Anti-Electricity Pilferage
     Act of 1994, which set an initial cap on losses that was gradually reduced
    over time. In 2017, the ERC issued a resolution further reducing the cap to
    its present level of 5.50 percent for private DUs and 12 to 8.25 percent for
    electric cooperatives.

    Recognizing the urgent need to mitigate the rapid increase of electricity rates
    while also taking into account the viability of distribution operations, this bill
    seeks to regulate and reduce system losses by institutionalizing a set of rules
    on the allowable systems loss cap of electric distribution utilities. As such,
    this measure is ultimately aimed at enhancing the efficiency and
    competitiveness of DUs in their delivery of electric service to the public. More
    importantly, this bill is envisioned to cushion the impact of electricity bill
    spikes to Filipino consumers as well as inflation and impending surges in fuel
    costs and other commodities.

    In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly and sincerely
    sought.

                                 AN ACT
     INSTITUTIONALIZING THE RULES ON SYSTEM LOSS CAP OF ELECTRIC
           DISTRIBUTION UTILITIES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
            REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832 AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Republic
     of the Philippines in Congress assembled:


11        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Distribution
12   System Loss Act of 2026.”

13         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It shall be the policy of the State to
14   protect the rights of every electric consumer without compromising the State’s
15   obligation to ensure the viability of electric distribution utilities. Towards this
16   end, the State shall ensure that the imposition of electricity distribution
17   charges to electricity end-users is justifiable and equitable, and that no
18   unnecessary cost is recovered by distribution utilities.

19         SEC. 3. Transmission Losses. – Transmission losses are hereby
20   disaggregated from the computation of allowable system loss charges. These
21   losses shall not form part of the passed-on costs to electricity end-users.

22   For the purposes of this Act, transmission loss shall mean the difference
23   between kilowatt-hour (kWh) delivered by the generation company to a
24   distribution utility as indicated in the powerplant billing meter installed at the
 1   plant gate and the kWh eventually received by the substation of an individual
 2   distribution utility as indicated in the billing meter installed in the delivery
 3   point.

 4         SEC. 4. Distribution System Loss (DSL) Caps for Private
 5   Distribution Utilities. – Private distribution utilities are hereby allowed to
 6   pass on system losses to their consumers only to the extent that these system
 7   losses result from design fault and technical loss as defined by the Energy
 8   Regulatory Commission (ERC), which in no case shall be more than five
 9   percent (5%).

10         SEC. 5. Distribution System Loss (DSL) Caps for Electric
11   Cooperatives. – Distribution utilities are hereby allowed to pass on system
12   losses to their consumers only to the extent that these system losses result
13   from design fault and technical loss as defined by the Energy Regulatory
14   Commission (ERC), which in no case shall be more than the following
15   percentages:

16         A. Cluster 1 – 12%

17         B. Cluster 2 – 10.25%

18         C. Cluster 3 – 8.25%

19          A system loss charge shall be determined annually based on the
20   previous year’s costs and revenues of the distribution utilities which shall be
21   verified by the ERC to ensure that only legitimate costs are borne by
22   consumers. For the purpose of this Act, system loss shall mean the difference
23   between kWh purchased and/or generated and kWh sold by a distribution
24   utility.

 4        SEC. 7.     Powers and Functions of the Energy Regulatory
 5   Commission. – In addition to its functions under Republic Act No. 9136, the
 6   ERC shall:

 7      a. Determine, as often as is necessary, whether the caps shall be reduced
 8         further on the basis of load density, sales mix, cost of service, delivery
 9         voltage, and other technical considerations, as well as international
10         benchmarks, taking into account the viability of DUs and ECs and the
11         interest of the consumers;
12
13      b. Exercise the emergency power, when deemed necessary, to increase the
14         system loss rates caps as prescribed by this act;
15
 1      c. Review and verify the system loss charges reported by distribution
 2         utilities to ensure that only legitimate costs are borne by the
 3         consumers.
 4
 5      d. Technical system losses shall remain an eligible cost of service
 6         recoverable from end-users only when such losses are directly
 7         attributable to the physical operation of transmission or distribution
 8         facilities properly measured and documented within the maximum
 9         allowable technical system-loss rate approved by the ERC.
10
11      e. The ERC shall determine the treatment, resolution, and disposition of
12         non-technical system losses, including whether, to what extent, and
13         under what conditions such losses may be recovered, disallowed,
14         absorbed, or otherwise addressed, taking into account the protection of
15         end-users, the financial and operational viability of distribution utilities
16         and electric cooperatives, the reliability and continuity of electric
17         service, and the accountability of the distribution utility or electric
18         cooperative concerned for such losses.

19      Subsequently and every year thereafter, the ERC shall review the system
20   loss rates prescribed by this Act based on parameters such as load density,
21   sales mix, cost of service, delivery voltage and any other technical
22   considerations, as provided in the EPIRA, necessary for establishing different
23   caps for distribution utilities.

24           SEC. 8. System Loss Reduction Program and Incentives. – The ERC
25   shall develop an incentive mechanism to reward distribution utilities whose
26   actual system losses are below the prevailing approved caps. The incentive
27   mechanism so adopted may be incorporated by the ERC in the performance
28   incentive scheme under the performance-based regulation for private
29   distribution utilities and in the benchmarking methodology for electric
30   cooperatives. Towards this end, expenses associated with any system loss
31   reduction program duly approved by the ERC shall be considered part of the
32   capital, expenditure plans or programs, and operating costs of distribution
33   utilities.

34          SEC. 9. Emergency Clause. – The application of allowable system loss
35   rates provided in this Act shall be suspended upon the occurrence of the
36   following: acts of God, riots, war, terrorist act, epidemic, pandemic, civil
37   commotion, natural catastrophes, and other emergency circumstances
38   wherein the distribution utility is unable to accurately determine the energy
39   sold because of damage or loss of consumers billing meters and other facilities
40   used in the determination of the consumers energy consumption, as
41   determined by the ERC.
 1          SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The ERC, in

 5   effectivity of this Act.

 6         SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
 7   orders, letters of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts
 8   thereof which is are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
 9   are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

10          SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any of the sections or provisions of
11   this Act is held invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
12   valid.

13         SEC. 13. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen
14   (15) days following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two
15   (2) newspapers of general circulation whichever is earlier.
