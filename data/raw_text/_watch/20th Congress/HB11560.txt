EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article XIII, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to undertake, in
cooperation with the public sector, a continuing program of urban land reform and housing
that will make available affordable and decent housing and basic services to underprivileged
and homeless citizens. Yet in many highly urbanized cities, one of the greatest obstacles to
providing affordable housing is not merely the cost of construction, but the extreme scarcity
and high market cost of available land.

At the same time, local government units periodically acquire real properties as a
consequence of unpaid real property taxes. Under Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known
as the Local Government Code of 1991, real properties may, after compliance with the
required notice, levy, and public auction procedures, be acquired by the local government
when there is no qualified bidder or when the highest bid is insufficient to satisfy the taxes
and related charges due.

The law likewise preserves the property owner's statutory right of redemption before
ownership finally vests in the local government unit. Once ownership has lawfully and finally
vested in the local government, however, existing law generally allows the property to be
subsequently disposed of through public auction.

This measure seeks to introduce a more socially productive alternative. Before an
LGU-owned property acquired through tax delinquency proceedings is resold, the property
should first be evaluated to determine whether it can be utilized for socialized or public
housing. Properties that are safe, accessible, appropriately located, and economically feasible
for housing development may be placed in a Local Housing Land Bank rather than
immediately returned to the private real estate market.

This policy complements Republic Act No. 7279, otherwise known as the Urban
Development and Housing Act of 1992, which recognizes land banking as a mode of
acquiring land for socialized housing and accords priority to government-owned, abandoned,
and idle lands. The proposed Local Housing Land Bank will provide LGUs with an

The measure expressly protects property owners. Mere tax delinquency shall never be

may be included until all procedures required by law have been completed and the owner's
statutory right of redemption has fully expired.

The measure further promotes in-city and on-site development whenever practicable so that
families receiving government housing are not unnecessarily displaced far from their
employment, schools, health facilities, transportation, and established communities. Housing
should not merely provide a roof over a family's head. It should provide security of tenure
while keeping families connected to their livelihoods and communities.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE LOCAL HOUSING LAND BANK SYSTEM,
MANDATING THE SUITABILITY ASSESSMENT AND PRIORITY RESERVATION
OF TAX-DELINQUENT REAL PROPERTIES FOR SOCIALIZED HOUSING,
AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 264 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991," AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Local Housing Land
Bank Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy — The 1987 Constitution provides that the State
shall, by law, and for the common good, undertake, in cooperation with the public sector, a
continuing program of urban land reform and housing which will make available at
affordable cost decent housing and basic services to underprivileged and homeless citizens in
urban centers and resettlements areas. It shall also promote adequate employment
opportunities to such citizens. In the implementation of such program the State shall respect
the rights of small property owners.

The fundamental law likewise recognizes that each local government unit shall have
the power to create its own sources of revenues and to levy taxes, fees, and charges subject to
such guidelines and limitations as the Congress may provide, consistent with the basic policy
of local autonomy.

Accordingly, the State hereby adopts the following policies:

(a) Promote the efficient and socially responsive utilization of government-owned
land and strengthen the capacity of local government units to provide
adequate, safe, affordable, resilient, and accessible housing for
underprivileged and homeless citizens;

===== [page 4] =====
(b) Encourage the conversion and priority utilization of suitable real properties
lawfully and finally acquired by local government units through real property
tax delinquency proceedings into land for socialized and public housing,
subject to existing constitutional and statutory safeguards; and

(c) Promote in-city, near-city, and on-site housing development, whenever
practicable, in order to preserve access of beneficiaries to employment,
education, health services, transportation, and established social and
community networks.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms —

a) “Acquired Tax-Delinquent Property” refers to real property located within the
territorial jurisdiction of a local government unit that has been acquired by or
forfeited in favor of said local government unit pursuant to the applicable
provisions of Republic Act No. 7160, as amended, after all legal requirements
have been complied with and the period for redemption has fully expired
without the property having been redeemed;

b) “Housing-Suitable Property” refers to land determined by the local
government unit, in accordance with standards prescribed by the Department
of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), to be appropriate
for socialized or public housing considering its location, physical condition,
environmental safety, accessibility, availability of utilities and basic services,
development cost, land use classification, and other relevant factors;

c) "Land Banking" refers to the acquisition of land at values based on existing
use in advance of actual need to promote planned development and socialized
housing programs;

d) “Local Housing Land Bank” refers to the inventory and pool of real properties
owned or acquired by a local government unit and reserved or designated for
present or future socialized housing, public housing, urban renewal,
community development, or other housing-related purposes;

e) “In-City Housing” refers to a housing or resettlement project located within
the same city or municipality where the qualified beneficiaries reside,
whenever feasible;

f) “Socialized Housing” shall have the same meaning provided under Republic
Act No. 7279, as amended; and

g) “Qualified Beneficiaries” refers to persons or families qualified to avail
themselves of government socialized or public housing programs under
Republic Act No. 7279, as amended, and other applicable housing laws, rules,
and regulations.

SECTION 4. — Territorial Jurisdiction and Vesting of Tax-Delinquent Properties. —

All real properties acquired or forfeited through real property tax delinquency
proceedings under Republic Act No. 7160, as amended, in view of Section 39 of Republic
Act No. 7279, shall exclusively vest in and accrue to the ownership of the local government
unit, whether a city, highly urbanized city, or municipality, that exercises territorial
jurisdiction over the area where the real property is physically located.

Cities and municipalities shall establish and maintain a Local Housing Land Bank
consisting of an inventory of government-owned properties situated within their respective

===== [page 5] =====
territorial jurisdictions that may be utilized for housing and urban development.

Such inventory shall include acquired tax-delinquent properties whose ownership has
finally vested in the local government unit having territorial jurisdiction over said property
after the expiration of the redemption period prescribed by law.

Provinces may likewise establish provincial housing land banks for properties
acquired within their administrative purview and may make such properties available to
component cities and municipalities through inter-local agreements authorized by the
sanggunian concerned, pursuant to Section 17 of Republic Act No. 7160.

SECTION 5. — Mandatory Housing Suitability Assessment. — Before any acquired
tax-delinquent property is resold, transferred, leased, or otherwise disposed of by a local
government unit, the property shall first undergo a housing suitability assessment.

The assessment shall be conducted by the appropriate local planning and development
office, local housing office, or equivalent office, in coordination with the local assessor, local
treasurer, local disaster risk reduction and management office, local environment and natural
resources office, and other appropriate offices.

The assessment shall consider, among others:

(a) Consistency with the Comprehensive Land Use Plan, zoning ordinance, Local
Shelter Plan, and other local development plans;

(b) Freedom from unacceptable geologic, flooding, environmental, health, or
other hazards;

(c) Proximity and accessibility to employment and livelihood opportunities;

(d) Access to public transportation;

(e) Access to schools, hospitals, health centers, markets, and other basic services;

(f) Availability or feasibility of water, sanitation, drainage, electricity, and other
utilities;

(g) Size, configuration, and development potential of the property;

(h) Feasibility of medium-rise, high-density, mixed-use, or other appropriate
housing designs;

(i) Existing occupants and communities within the property;

(j) Existing liens, easements, legal restrictions, or other conditions affecting
development; and

(k) Overall financial and social feasibility of utilizing the property for housing.

SECTION 6. Priority Reservation for Housing. — An acquired tax-delinquent
property determined to be suitable for socialized or public housing shall, upon approval by
the sanggunian of the local government unit having territorial jurisdiction, be reserved and
placed in its Local Housing Land Bank.

As a general rule, housing-suitable properties shall not be resold or otherwise
disposed of for purely private purposes when there is an identified local housing need and the
property can reasonably be developed for such purpose.

A housing-suitable property may be released from the Local Housing Land Bank only
upon:

===== [page 6] =====
(a) A finding that its continued reservation is no longer financially, environmentally,
technically, or socially practicable;

(b) A determination that another public purpose is more urgent and beneficial to the
community; and

(c) Approval by ordinance of the sanggunian concerned after a public hearing.

Nothing in this section shall prohibit mixed-use development when commercial,
institutional, or other compatible uses are necessary to support the financial sustainability and
livability of the housing project, provided that the principal public purpose of the property is
preserved.

SECTION 7. Development and Utilization of Properties. — Properties included in
the Local Housing Land Bank may be developed through any mode authorized by law,
including:

(a) Direct development by the local government unit;

(b) Development in partnership with the National Housing Authority;

(c) Programs undertaken with the Social Housing Finance Corporation;

(d) Joint ventures and public-private partnerships;

(ec) Community mortgage and community-driven housing programs;

(f) Cooperative housing;

(g) Land assembly or consolidation;

(h) Medium-rise or high-density residential development;

(i) Rental housing, rent-to-own housing, or other affordable tenure arrangements;

(j) Mixed-income or mixed-use development with a substantial socialized
housing component; and

(k) Such other innovative housing arrangements as may be approved by the
DHSUD.

The local government unit may contribute land within its Local Housing Land Bank
as its equity or counterpart contribution to housing projects implemented jointly with national
government agencies, government-owned or -controlled corporations, private entities,
cooperatives, civil society organizations, or qualified people's organizations, subject to
existing laws.

SECTION 8. Preference for In-City and On-Site Housing. — In the utilization of
properties under this Act, preference shall be given to in-city and on-site development
whenever technically, environmentally, and financially feasible.

No qualified beneficiary shall be relocated outside the city or municipality solely on
the ground that off-city land is cheaper when suitable government-owned land within the
locality is reasonably available for housing development.

In evaluating housing projects, the social and economic costs of displacement,
including additional transportation costs, loss of livelihood, disruption of education, and
reduced access to health and other essential services, shall be considered together with
construction and land costs.

SECTION 9. Beneficiaries. — Housing projects established pursuant to this Act shall
primarily benefit qualified underprivileged and homeless citizens in accordance with
Republic Act No. 7279, as amended, and other applicable laws.

===== [page 7] =====
Subject to national housing policies and local shelter requirements, priority may be
extended to:

a) Informal settler families affected by national or local government infrastructure
projects;

b) Families occupying danger areas or environmentally critical areas where continued
habitation poses a serious threat to life or health;

c) Families affected by government clearing, rehabilitation, or urban renewal programs;

d) Families subject to lawful court-ordered eviction or demolition;

e) Qualified low-income families who have continuously resided or worked within the
city or municipality;

f) Qualified senior citizens, solo parents, and persons with disabilities requiring
accessible housing arrangements; and

g) Other sectors identified under existing socialized housing laws and regulations.

Actual qualified occupants of properties covered by this Act shall be accorded the
rights and preferences provided under Republic Act No. 7279, as amended.

SECTION 10. Protection of Property Owners and Interested Parties. — Nothing in
this Act shall be interpreted as authorizing the taking, transfer, or use of privately owned
property merely because real property taxes have become delinquent.

A property shall not be included in a Local Housing Land Bank unless:

(a) All notice, levy, publication, auction, forfeiture, and registration requirements
under Republic Act No. 7160, as amended, have been strictly complied with;

(b) The period for redemption prescribed by law has fully expired;

(c) The property has not been validly redeemed;

(d) Ownership has lawfully and finally vested in the local government unit having
territorial jurisdiction; and

(e) All other applicable requirements of law have been observed.

Nothing in this Act shall diminish or impair the right of a delinquent taxpayer or any
person having a legal interest in the property to redeem the same within the period and under
the conditions provided by law.

SECTION 11. Notice to Local Housing and Planning Offices. — Upon the final
vesting of ownership of an acquired tax-delinquent property in favor of a local government
unit having territorial jurisdiction, the local treasurer shall, within thirty (30) days, transmit to
the local housing office or its equivalent and the local planning and development office a
certified description of the property for purposes of the assessment required under Section 5
of this Act.

of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991.” is
hereby amended to read as follows:

“SEC. 264. Resale or Utilization of Real Estate Taken for Taxes, Fees or Charges. —
BEFORE THE SALE OR DISPOSITION OF REAL PROPERTY ACQUIRED UNDER THE

SECTION 13. Local Housing Land Bank Registry. — Every participating city and
municipality shall maintain a Local Housing Land Bank Registry containing, at a minimum:

(a) The location, area, and technical description of each property;
(b) The mode and date of acquisition;

(c) The status of title and registration;

(d) Existing occupants, if any;

(e) Current land use and zoning classification;

(f) Housing suitability assessment;

(g) Proposed development or utilization;

(h) Status of project implementation; and

(i) Such other information as may be required by the DHSUD.

The DHSUD shall establish a national digital reporting system that allows the consolidation
of local land-bank information for housing planning and policy purposes.

SECTION 14. Technical and Financial Assistance. — The DHSUD, NHA, Social
Housing Finance Corporation, and other key shelter agencies shall provide technical
assistance to local government units for:

(a) Land suitability studies;

(b) Master planning and site development;

(c) Preparation of feasibility studies;

(d) Community consultation and social preparation;
(e) Housing design and engineering;

(f) Financing and cost-recovery arrangements;

(g) Beneficiary selection and validation;

(h) Development of people's plans;

===== [page 9] =====
(i) Public-private partnership arrangements; and
(j) Estate and property management.

SECTION 15. Local Housing Land Bank and Development Fund. — Local
government units may establish, by ordinance, a Local Housing Land Bank and Development
Fund to finance the acquisition, planning, development, improvement, and administration of
properties utilized under this Act.

The Fund may consist of:

(a) Appropriations of the local government unit;

(b) Proceeds from the socialized housing tax authorized under Republic Act No. 7279, as
amended;

(c) National government appropriations and housing grants;

(d) Grants, donations, and contributions from public or private sources;

(e) Financing or assistance from government financial institutions and key shelter
agencies;

(f) Income derived from housing projects, subject to applicable laws and accounting
rules;

(g) Local government bonds or other financing instruments authorized by law; and

(h) Such other lawful sources as may be authorized under existing laws.

SECTION 16. Safeguards Against Speculation and Abuse. — Housing units,
homelots, and interests granted under projects developed pursuant to this Act shall be subject
to occupancy, transfer, resale, and other restrictions prescribed under existing socialized
housing laws and regulations to prevent speculation, profiteering, and the circumvention of
beneficiary qualifications.

No public official or employee directly involved in the identification, acquisition,
valuation, disposition, development, or award of property under this Act, or their relatives
within the degree prohibited by applicable law, shall directly or indirectly acquire any
financial or beneficial interest therein, except when lawfully qualified as a beneficiary under
a program of general application and after full disclosure and compliance with applicable
ethics and conflict-of-interest laws.

SECTION 17. Community Participation and Transparency. — Local government
units shall ensure meaningful consultation with affected communities and intended
beneficiaries in the planning and development of housing projects under this Act.

The inventory of properties included in the Local Housing Land Bank, their status,
and their intended use shall be made publicly accessible, subject to the Data Privacy Act and
other applicable laws.

Disposition or release of land from the Local Housing Land Bank shall be undertaken

transparently and only after the requirements provided under this Act have been satisfied.

SECTION 18. Jmplementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DHSUD, in coordination with the Department of the Interior

SECTION 19. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions or parts not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 20. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 21. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
