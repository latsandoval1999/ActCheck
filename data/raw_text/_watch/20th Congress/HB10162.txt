                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The World Health Organization has observed that the major causes of
death in low-income countries can be treated effectively with simple essential
medicines. However, the medicines are often not available, not accessible or
not affordable.

      Recent evidence indicates that limited access to affordable and essential
medicines remains a persistent challenge in the Philippine health system. A
Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) assessment found that
only 2 in 10 hospitals have a complete set of essential medicines, forcing many
patients to procure medicines outside health facilities at their own expense.1

       The Department of Health (DOH) has an ongoing free medicine program
for indigents called Medical Assistance Program (MAP), a program intended to
provide medical assistance to patients seeking consultation, rehabilitation,
examination, or otherwise confined in government hospitals.

      On the other hand, the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD) also provide medical assistance to indigent patients in its Assistance
to Indiviuals in Crisis Situations (AICS).2

PhilHealth’s outpatient medicine benefits (e.g., the YAKAP Program),
availability remains uneven, and facilities are required to maintain sufficient
stocks of covered medicines to ensure continuity of care.

       This bill seeks to improve Filipinos’ access to essential medicines by
institutionalizing a free medicine assistance program for the poor and
marginalized, and by establishing accessible distribution sites in all cities and
municipalities nationwide. Through these measures, it aims to enhance
survival rates among underserved populations suffering from treatable
conditions, thereby contributing to improved overall public health outcomes
in the country.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Free Medicine for
the Poor Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health and well-being of every
Filipino and to adopt a system of implementing the delivery of free basic
medicines to those who have less in life in all health facilities in the country,
giving priority to the underprivileged, ailing, malnourished, elderly, women,
children, disabled, and the poorest of the poor.

SECTION 3. Objectives. – This Act pursues the following objectives:

      a) To promote optimal health and well-being of every Filipino by
         improved access to medicines; and
      b) To address the health needs of the marginalized sectors such as the
         poor, malnourished, elderly, women, children, and the disabled
         through the establishment of a Free Basic Medicine Assistance
         Program in all government health facilities nationwide.

SECTION 4. Establishment of Free Medicine Assistance Program. – There
shall be established a Free Medicine Assistance Program, hereinafter referred
to as the “Libreng Gamot Program,” to be administered by the Department of
Health (DOH) through government district hospitals, local health units, and
barangay health centers in all cities and municipalities, in coordination with
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).

SECTION 5. Eligible Beneficiaries. – The Libreng Gamot Program shall be
restricted to qualified poor recipients as certified by the barangay and the
DSWD officials.

SECTION 6. Availment of Free Medicines. – Free medicines shall be made
available in all government district hospitals, local health units, and barangay
health centers. These health facilities shall account for all the medicines
released to qualified beneficiaries through the submission of quarterly reports
to the DOH.

SECTION 7. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the appropriations of the
DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOH, in
coordination with the DSWD and in consultation with the Philippine Medical
Association (PMA), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to
effectively implement the provisions of this Act within six (6) months of the
effectivity hereof.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act shall
be held unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
