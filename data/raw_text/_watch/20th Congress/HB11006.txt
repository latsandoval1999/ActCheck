                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to include a Personal Financial Literacy Course (PFLC) as a
mandatory component of the Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET)
curriculum in all public and private Technical-Vocational Institutions (TVIs) and
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) training centers. It
recognizes that preparing Filipinos for employment and entrepreneurship requires
not only technical competence but also the ability to manage income and make
sound financial decisions.

       The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas recognizes financial capability as essential to
meaningful financial inclusion, since access to financial services do not by itself
enable individuals to make informed financial decisions1. While TVET equips
learners with industry-relevant skills for employment and livelihood, it does not
uniformly provide structured instruction on personal financial management.
Republic Act (RA) No. 10922, otherwise known as the “Economic and Financial
Literacy Act”, and RA No. 10679, otherwise known as the “Youth Entrepreneurship
Act”, promote financial education but do not specifically require a dedicated
financial literacy course across TVET programs.

       The proposed measure requires the PFLC as a stand-alone course for all TVET
learners, regardless of their chosen program or qualification. The course will cover
essential personal finance concepts, which includes but are not limited to budgeting,
saving, debt management, investment, insurance, retirement, planning, credit,
consumer protection, and digital financial services. It shall also introduce learners to
the Philippine financial system and cybersecurity risks in digital transactions. The
curriculum shall be periodically reviewed to remain responsive to developments in
financial products, technology, and national financial inclusion priorities.

       Financial literacy empowers individuals long before wealth is accumulated.
For many technical-vocational graduates, their first paycheck is the beginning of a
family’s opportunity to move forward. Technical skills may open the door to

                          AN ACT
MANDATING THE INCLUSION OF A PERSONAL FINANCIAL LITERACY
COURSE IN THE TECHNICAL-VOCATIONAL EDUCATION AND TRAINING
CURRICULUM OF TECHNICAL-VOCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS AND
TECHNICAL EDUCATION AND SKILLS DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY
TRAINING CENTERS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Personal Financial
Literacy in Technical-Vocational Education and Training Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote financial literacy as an essential life skill for all citizens, and to strengthen
the technical-vocational education and training system as a vehicle for developing
not only practical occupational skills but also the financial knowledge and discipline
necessary for economic security and well-being.

       Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize financial education within
the TVET curriculum, ensuring that technical-vocational students are equipped to
make sound and well-informed financial decisions upon entering the workforce, to
responsibly manage personal finances, and to participate meaningfully in the formal
financial system.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act shall have the following objectives:

          a. Ensure that all technical-vocational institutions (TVIs) and Technical
       Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) training centers shall
       provide a structured and standards-based financial education program as
       part of the Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET)
       curriculum;

         b. Equip technical-vocational learners with knowledge and competencies
       on personal financial management, including budgeting, saving, debt

      management, investment, insurance, and retirement planning, to enable them
      to make sound financial decisions upon entering the workforce;

          c.​ To promote awareness of consumer rights and protections, particularly
      in relation to digital financial services, and to develop responsible financial
      behavior among learners; and

          d.​ To align financial education within TVET with national financial
      inclusion objectives and standards established by the relevant oversight
      agencies.

       SEC. 4. Mandatory Inclusion of Personal Financial Literacy Course in the TVET
Curriculum. – All public and private TVIs and TESDA training centers shall include
the Personal Financial Literacy Course (PFLC) as a mandatory component of the
TVET curriculum. The PFLC shall be offered to all enrolled technical-vocational
students and trainees, regardless of the specific program or qualification they are
pursuing. A passing grade in the PFLC shall be a prerequisite for completion of any
TVET program.

       The PFLC shall focus exclusively on personal finance and financial literacy. It
shall be offered as a stand-alone course, separate from other technical-vocational
competency units, with its own curriculum, instructional materials, and assessment
standards developed in accordance with Section 5 of this Act.

      SEC. 5. Minimum Content Standards of the Personal Financial Literacy Course. –
The PFLC shall, at minimum, provide instruction on the following topics:

        a.​ Fundamentals of personal finance, including budgeting, income
      management, and financial goal-setting;

          b.​ ﻿Time value of money and its practical applications in everyday
      financial decision-making;

          c.​ Consumer behavior in the Philippine economic and cultural context;

          d.​ Debt management, including responsible borrowing, loan repayment
      strategies, and debt rehabilitation;

          e.​ Savings strategies, emergency fund planning, and financial resilience;

          f.​ ﻿Basic investment concepts and financial planning;

         g.​ Retirement planning, including relevant government-mandated social
      protection programs such as those administered by the Social Security
      System (SSS), the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), and the
      Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG);

          i.​ Credit scoring, credit reports, and the responsible use of credit;

         j.​ Overview of the Philippine financial system and the roles of key

          k.​ Responsible use of digital financial services, including mobile banking,
      electronic payments, and digital wallets; and

         l.​ Cyber-hygiene, cybersecurity awareness, and consumer protection in

       The inter-agency committee established under Section 6 hereof may update,
revise, or supplement the minimum content standards prescribed in this Section in
accordance with emerging financial products, technologies, and national financial
inclusion priorities, subject to the requirements of the implementing rules and
regulations.

      SEC. 6. Inter-Agency Development and Oversight. – TESDA shall lead the
development, review, and updating of the PFLC curriculum, instructional materials,
and assessment standards. In discharging this mandate, TESDA shall collaborate
with the following agencies, which shall serve as co-developing and advisory
bodies:

         a.​ ﻿The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), for matters relating to the
      monetary system, financial inclusion, digital payments, and consumer
      protection in the financial sector;

          b.​ ﻿The Department of Finance (DOF), for matters relating to public
      finance, fiscal policy, and government financial programs;

          c.​ ﻿The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), for matters relating
      to capital markets, investment, and corporate governance; and

         d.​ ﻿The Insurance Commission (IC), for matters relating to insurance
      products, risk management, and insurance regulation.

       The aforementioned agencies shall constitute an Inter-Agency Committee
(IAC) that shall jointly formulate, review, and update the minimum content
standards and instructional guidelines for the PFLC. The IAC shall meet regularly,
and at least once every three (3) years, to assess and revise the PFLC content
standards in response to developments in financial products, services, and national
financial literacy priorities.

       TESDA shall serve as the lead convenor of the IAC and shall provide the
secretariat support. The IAC shall adopt its own rules of procedure within sixty (60)
days from its constitution.

       SEC. 7. Qualifications and Development of PFLC Instructors. – TESDA, in
consultation with the IAC, shall establish minimum qualifications and competency
standards for instructors assigned to deliver the PFLC. TVIs shall ensure that PFLC
instructors meet these standards prior to being assigned to teach the course.

      TESDA shall develop and make available continuing professional
development programs for PFLC instructors to ensure the currency of their
knowledge and pedagogical competencies, consistent with updates to the PFLC
content standards.

​       SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the TESDA shall, in consultation with the BSP, DOF, SEC, IC,
and other relevant stakeholders, promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to
effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
