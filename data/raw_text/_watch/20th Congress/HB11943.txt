        This measure does not grant one company control over all essential medicines.
 Different medicines may be produced by different manufacturers, while more than
 one qualified manufacturer may be selected for the same medicine to maintain
 competition and reduce the risk of supply interruption. The remaining government
 requirement shall continue to be open to other qualified local and foreign suppliers,
 and imported medicines may still be procured when local production is unavailable,
 insufficient, delayed, too costly, or unable to meet quality requirements. Through
 this balanced approach, the government will provide manufacturers with a
 predictable market and coordinated assistance, while participating companies will
 be required to undertake actual local production, meet regulatory and localization
 commitments, maintain reliable supply, and reflect the benefits of government
 support in lower or more stable prices. Ultimately, this bill seeks to build a stronger
 and more self-reliant pharmaceutical industry and ensure that safe, quality, and
 affordable medicines are continuously available to every Filipino.

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. ± The State shall ensure the continuous
availability, safety, quality, and affordability of essential medicines by:

      (a) increasing the production of medicines and pharmaceutical inputs in the
      Philippines;
(b) providing qualified manufacturers with a predictable government market;

(c) supporting investment, research, voluntary technology transfer, product
development, and workforce training;

(d) providing coordinated and time-bound government assistance to
companies establishing or expanding local pharmaceutical production;

(e) maintaining competition among local and foreign suppliers;

(f) preventing medicine shortages, overstocking, and expiration; and

(g) ensuring that government support results in reliable supply and lower or
stable medicine prices.

Section 3. Definition of Terms. ± For purposes of this Act:

(a) Actual Local Production refers to formulation, compounding,
manufacture, compression, encapsulation, filling, finishing, quality-control
testing, batch release, or production of active pharmaceutical ingredients,
excipients, primary packaging, or other pharmaceutical inputs in the
Philippines.

Importation, trading, relabeling, repacking, and secondary packaging alone
shall not constitute Actual Local Production.

(b) Government Purchase Share refers to the percentage of the annual
government requirement for a specific Priority PNF Medicine that shall be
purchased under a Local Production and Technology Partnership Agreement.
(c) Guaranteed Government Purchase Commitment refers to the
obligation of the Department of Health and participating government
procuring entities to purchase the Government Purchase Share and minimum
annual quantity stated in an awarded Partnership Agreement.

(d) Import-Dependent Medicine refers to a medicine for which more than
fifty percent of the national supply of the same generic name, dosage form,
and strength during the preceding twelve months was imported, or for which
no FDA-licensed manufacturer produced the same medicine in the Philippines
during that period.

Import dependence shall be measured by standard units of medicine supplied
and not by peso value.

(e) Localization Milestone refers to a production, technology, testing,
regulatory, investment, or workforce requirement that must be completed in
the Philippines under a Partnership Agreement.

(f) Localization Period refers to the period during which the manufacturer
shall complete the Localization Milestones and the government shall
implement the Guaranteed Government Purchase Commitment.

(g) Local Production and Technology Partnership or Partnership refers
to an agreement for the establishment or expansion of Actual Local
Production of a specific Priority PNF Medicine.

(h) Philippine National Formulary or PNF refers to the current Philippine
National Formulary and its subsequent updates approved by the Department
      of Health, which contains the list of essential medicines that can be procured
      by the government.

      (i) Priority PNF Medicines refer to medicines included in the PNF and
      selected under this Act for local production, supply monitoring, government
      purchasing, Strategic Importation, price review, or reserve-supply measures.

      (j) Strategic Importation refers to government procurement of an imported
      Priority PNF Medicine to address inadequate supply, failed delivery, lack of
      competition, a Supply Alert, or a declared medicine shortage.

      (k) Supply Alert refers to a finding by the Department of Health that the
      available and incoming supply of a Priority PNF Medicine may be insufficient
      to meet expected consumption.

      Section 4. Priority PNF Medicines List. ± The PNF shall be the primary basis
for implementing this Act.

The Department of Health shall publish an interim Priority PNF Medicines List
within twelve months from the effectivity of this Act.

Priority shall be given to:

      (a) insulin and other medicines for diabetes;

      (b) medicines required for continuous treatment of chronic diseases;

      (c) medicines with high government procurement or PhilHealth use;

      (d) medicines with recurring supply interruptions;
(e) medicines supplied by a limited number of manufacturers or importers;

(f) Import-Dependent Medicines;

(g) medicines for which local production will reduce a supply vulnerability;
and

(h) medicines whose prices materially affect government or household health
expenditure.

The Department shall consider:

(a) public health need and disease burden;

(b) actual government and private consumption;

(c) number and capacity of suppliers;

(d) source of the finished medicine and active pharmaceutical ingredient;

(e) availability of generic, biosimilar, or therapeutic alternatives;

(f) patent and licensing status;

(g) technical feasibility and cost of local production;

(h) government, PhilHealth, wholesale, and retail prices;

(i) shelf life and storage requirements;

(j) prior shortages and delivery failures; and

(k) risk of overstocking and expiration.
The list shall state the generic name, dosage form, strength, and the measure
applicable to each medicine.

The Department shall revise the interim list after completion of the first national
supply and consumption baseline under Section 5 and shall update the list annually
thereafter.

Removal from the Priority PNF Medicines List shall not remove a medicine from
the PNF.

      Section 5. Supply and Price Reporting. ± Manufacturers, importers, and
exclusive distributors of Priority PNF Medicines shall submit quarterly reports to
the Department of Health stating:

      (a) production or importation volume;

      (b) usable inventory and remaining shelf life;

      (c) average monthly sales or distribution;

      (d) source and manufacturing site of the finished medicine;

      (e) source of the active pharmaceutical ingredient;

      (f) expected supply interruption;

      (g) manufacturer or importer price;

      (h) wholesale price; and

      (i) suggested retail price.
Government health facilities shall submit consumption, inventory, and expiration
information through the system designated by the Department of Health.

The Department shall integrate the reports with available information from the FDA,
Bureau of Customs, PhilHealth, government procuring entities, and government
health facilities.

A person or entity shall not be required to submit information already filed in the
required format with another government agency. The agency holding the
information shall transmit it to the Department of Health.

The Department shall establish or upgrade the reporting system within eighteen
months from the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations.

The first twelve consecutive months of reporting shall serve as the baseline for
consumption, import dependence, inventory requirements, supply risks, and price
movements.

The Department shall protect trade secrets and confidential commercial information
and shall publish aggregate supply, shortage, and price information.

       Section 6. Local Production and Technology Partnership Program. ± A
Local Production and Technology Partnership Program is hereby established under
the Department of Health and the Department of Trade and Industry.

The Program shall:

       (a) select Priority PNF Medicines for local production;

       (b) competitively select qualified manufacturers;
      (c) establish Localization Milestones;

      (d) provide a Guaranteed Government Purchase Commitment;

      (e) provide regulatory, financial, research, technical, customs, and workforce
      assistance; and

      (f) measure changes in local production, import dependence, supply, and
      medicine prices.

The first call for Partnership proposals shall be issued within six months from
publication of the interim Priority PNF Medicines List and shall cover not more than
five medicines.

Additional medicines may be included after evaluation of the first call and subject
to government demand and available appropriations.

      Section 7. Separate and Multiple Partnerships. ± A separate competitive
selection shall be conducted for each medicine, dosage form, and strength selected
under the Program.

One or more Partnerships may be awarded for each medicine.

No manufacturer shall be granted the exclusive right to produce or supply all Priority
PNF Medicines.

Each Partnership Agreement shall cover only the medicine, dosage form, and
strength stated in the agreement.
Different medicines may be awarded to different manufacturers. A manufacturer
may receive a Partnership for more than one medicine only after separately
qualifying and being competitively selected for each medicine.

The bidding documents shall permit an award to more than one manufacturer.

When at least two qualified and responsive proponents meet the stated price,
production, and delivery requirements, the Department of Health shall award the
Government Purchase Share to at least two proponents, unless market scoping shows
that a split award will prevent commercially viable local production or increase the
total contract price beyond the approved budget.

A single Partnership may be awarded only when:

      (a) only one qualified and responsive proposal remains after competitive
      selection;

      (b) the proposal satisfies Republic Act No. 12009;

      (c) the offered price is within the approved price ceiling; and

      (d) the Department of Health maintains another source that may supply the
      medicine when the selected manufacturer fails to deliver.

The award of a Partnership shall not prevent another company from manufacturing,
importing, distributing, or selling the same medicine.

      Section 8. Selection of Partnerships. ± A Partnership shall be selected
through competitive bidding, competitive dialogue, or another applicable
competitive mode under Republic Act No. 12009.
Competitive dialogue may be used when the government cannot fully determine
before bidding the required production process, technology arrangement,
Localization Milestones, price structure, or allocation of technical and financial
risks.

The selection shall consider:

         (a) technical capacity;

         (b) FDA compliance;

         (c) proposed investment;

         (d) production and delivery capacity;

         (e) technology acquisition, transfer, or development plan;

         (f) Localization Milestones and timetable;

         (g) proposed government price;

         (h) price-reduction commitments;

         (i) supply-continuity plan;

         (j) Filipino employment and training; and

         (k) government assistance requested.

The contract shall be awarded in accordance with the award criteria and post-
qualification requirements of Republic Act No. 12009.
      Section 9. Partnership Agreement and Localization. ± The selected
manufacturer shall enter into a Local Production and Technology Partnership
Agreement with the Department of Health.

The Agreement shall state:

      (a) the medicine, dosage form, and strength;

      (b) the production activities to be performed in the Philippines;

      (c) the technology or technical knowledge to be licensed, acquired,
      transferred, or developed;

      (d) each Localization Milestone and deadline;

      (e) the Localization Period;

      (f) the Government Purchase Share;

      (g) the minimum and maximum annual purchase quantities;

      (h) the contract price and price-adjustment method;

      (i) the price-reduction schedule;

      (j) production and delivery requirements;

      (k) remaining shelf-life requirements;

      (l) government incentives and assistance; and

      (m) the grounds for reducing, suspending, or terminating the purchase
      commitment.
The Localization Period shall be at least five years but shall not exceed ten years.

The minimum production and technology requirements for each medicine or class
of medicines shall be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.

The requirements shall be based on the nature of the medicine, available Philippine
capacity, FDA requirements, required investment, production process, technology,
testing, and expected demand.

      Section 10. Guaranteed Government Purchase Commitment. ± The
Department of Health and participating government procuring entities shall purchase
the Government Purchase Share stated in the Partnership Agreement during the
Localization Period.

Before final bids are submitted, the bidding documents shall state:

      (a) the Government Purchase Share;

      (b) the minimum annual purchase quantity;

      (c) the contract period;

      (d) the price ceiling or price-setting method;

      (e) the price-volume discount schedule; and

      (f) the conditions for increasing, reducing, or suspending the purchase share.

The Government Purchase Share shall be determined separately for each medicine
based on:

      (a) average annual government consumption;
      (b) projected government demand;

      (c) usable government inventory;

      (d) quantities covered by existing contracts;

      (e) verified local production and delivery capacity;

      (f) investment and technology required;

      (g) number and capacity of other suppliers;

      (h) effect on medicine prices and competition;

      (i) shortage risk;

      (j) shelf life and storage requirements; and

      (k) available appropriations and multiyear budget authority.

The method for applying these factors shall be prescribed in the implementing rules
and regulations.

The Guaranteed Government Purchase Commitment shall take effect only after:

      (a) competitive selection under Republic Act No. 12009;

      (b) issuance of the applicable Multi-Year Contractual Authority or equivalent
      budget authorization;

      (c) availability of the required appropriation;

      (d) completion of the required initial Localization Milestone;
      (e) commencement of Actual Local Production;

      (f) issuance of the required FDA authorizations; and

      (g) submission of the required performance security.

      Once effective, the government shall purchase the minimum annual quantity
      when the manufacturer:

      (a) maintains the required FDA authorizations;

      (b) supplies medicine produced according to the verified Localization
      Milestone;

      (c) delivers the required quantity on time;

      (d) meets the approved product specifications and remaining shelf life; and

      (e) charges no more than the contract price.

The Government Purchase Share shall not be reduced solely because a nonpartner
supplier later offers a lower price.

The share may be reduced or suspended only when the manufacturer:

      (a) fails to complete a Localization Milestone within the agreed period;

      (b) cannot supply the committed quantity;

      (c) fails FDA quality testing;

      (d) loses the required FDA authorization;
      (e) fails to deliver on time;

      (f) charges more than the contract price; or

      (g) fails to replace rejected or nonconforming medicines.

The remaining government requirement and any quantity not delivered by the
Partnership shall be procured from qualified local or foreign suppliers.

The Partnership shall not prevent Strategic Importation or procurement from another
supplier when required to prevent a shortage or protect public health.

      Section 11. Market Certainty and Budget Programming. ± The Guaranteed
Government Purchase Commitment shall cover the Localization Period stated in the
Partnership Agreement.

Before procurement begins, the Department of Health shall obtain the applicable
Multi-Year Contractual Authority or equivalent authorization covering the total
contract cost.

The Department of Health shall:

      (a) include the Partnership in its multiyear expenditure plan;

      (b) include the annual purchase requirement in its procurement plans;

      (c) include the annual funding requirement in its budget proposal; and

      (d) undertake early procurement activities when authorized by law.

The Department of Budget and Management shall evaluate the annual funding
requirement for inclusion in the National Expenditure Program.
The amounts required for existing Partnership Agreements shall be included in the
proposed annual appropriations of the Department of Health, subject to
congressional deliberation and approval.

Deliveries shall be staggered or made through call-off orders, supplier-held
inventory, or another arrangement that prevents unnecessary government storage
and expiration.

      Section 12. Price Reduction and Public Savings. ± The Partnership price
shall not exceed the price ceiling or price-setting method stated in the bidding
documents.

The Agreement shall require a price reduction when:

      (a) the government purchase volume increases;

      (b) a Localization Milestone reduces importation, production, licensing,
      testing, or logistics costs;

      (c) a tax incentive, grant, subsidized loan, shared facility, or other government
      support reduces project cost; or

      (d) the cost of acquiring or transferring the technology has been recovered.

The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the method for determining
the initial price, applying price-volume discounts, measuring cost reductions,
adjusting prices, and comparing the Partnership price with other local and
international prices.
The method shall require savings from government support, higher volume, and
lower production costs to be reflected in the government price.

      Section 16. Government Demand Forecast and Pooled Procurement. ±
Government hospitals and local government units participating in pooled

      (b) be based on the PNF, subject to exemptions authorized by law;

      Section 17. Strategic Importation. ± The Department of Health or another
authorized government procuring entity may undertake Strategic Importation when:

      (a) no FDA-authorized local manufacturer is available;

      (b) local manufacturers cannot supply the required quantity;

      (c) a Partnership manufacturer cannot supply the committed quantity;

      (d) a Supply Alert or medicine shortage has been issued;

      (e) successive procurements fail;

      (f) a contracted supplier fails to deliver;

      (g) the local product fails FDA or procurement quality requirements;

      (h) the local bid exceeds the approved procurement budget after application
      of domestic preference; or

      (i) another independent source is required to protect supply continuity.
Strategic Importation shall comply with Republic Act No. 12009, Republic Act No.
9502, the Intellectual Property Code, FDA requirements, customs laws, and
applicable international obligations.

Only medicines registered, authorized, or otherwise permitted by the FDA may be
imported.

      Section 18. Supply Alerts and Targeted Reserve Supply. ± Manufacturers,
importers, and exclusive distributors of Priority PNF Medicines shall notify the
Department of Health of a planned discontinuation, material reduction, or
unexpected supply interruption.

The notice periods, reduction thresholds, Supply Alert levels, shortage levels, and
supporting documents shall be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations
based on:

      (a) actual consumption;

      (b) usable inventory;

      (c) production and importation lead time;

      (d) pending production and shipments;

      (e) number and capacity of suppliers;

      (f) available therapeutic alternatives;

      (g) shelf life and storage requirements; and

      (h) effects of interrupted treatment.
      Upon verification of a Supply Alert or shortage, the Department shall:

      (a) publish the affected medicine, dosage form, strength, and area;

      (b) redistribute available government stocks;

      (c) activate other suppliers under existing contracts;

      (d) publish available FDA-authorized alternatives;

      (e) undertake emergency procurement or Strategic Importation when
      authorized by law; and

      (f) publish supply updates until the shortage ends.

The Department may maintain or contract for a reserve supply only for a medicine
that is lifesaving, required for uninterrupted treatment, vulnerable to recurring
shortage, subject to a long delivery lead time, or without an available therapeutic
alternative.

The reserve quantity and arrangement shall be determined through the implementing
rules and regulations based on actual consumption, lead time, shelf life, storage
requirements, shortage history, supplier capacity, available appropriations, and risk
of expiration.

The Department shall first compare supplier-held reserves, consignment, call-off
orders, vendor-managed inventory, stock rotation, and government-owned stock.

Government-owned reserve stock shall not be purchased when expected
consumption, remaining shelf life, storage capacity, or available alternatives create
an unreasonable risk of expiration or wastage.
      Section 19. Price Review. ± The Department of Health shall review the price
of a Priority PNF Medicine when supply, procurement, reimbursement, and market
information indicates an unusual price increase, limited competition, a supply
shortage, unexplained government expenditure, or a substantial difference from
comparable international prices.

The review thresholds and comparison methods shall be prescribed in the
implementing rules and regulations.

The review shall consider production or landed costs, exchange rates, licensing
costs, storage and distribution costs, government procurement prices, PhilHealth
prices, comparable international prices, available alternatives, number of suppliers,
and the effect of price measures on supply and competition.

After notice and hearing, the Secretary of Health may:

      (a) negotiate lower prices or price-volume agreements;

      (b) establish reference or negotiated prices for government procurement and
      PhilHealth;

      (c) require price information authorized under Republic Act No. 9502;

      (d) apply cost-containment measures authorized by law; and

      (e) recommend to the President the imposition of a maximum retail or
      wholesale price under Republic Act No. 9502.

      Section 20. PhilHealth GAMOT Coverage. ± PhilHealth shall review Priority
PNF Medicines for inclusion or expanded coverage under PhilHealth GAMOT.
Priority shall be given to medicines for diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular
disease, asthma, chronic respiratory disease, and other chronic conditions with high
disease burden and outpatient use.

PhilHealth shall publish the medicines reviewed, medicines approved, applicable
prices, dispensing limits, participating facilities, and the basis for excluding a
reviewed medicine.

Coverage shall remain subject to applicable health technology assessment
requirements, actuarial evaluation, program rules, and available funds.

      Section 21. Appropriations and Implementing Agencies. ± The amounts
necessary to implement this Act shall be determined during the preparation and
deliberation of the annual General Appropriations Act and included in the
appropriations of the concerned agencies.

The Department of Health shall administer the Priority PNF Medicines List,
Partnership Program, government purchase commitments, supply monitoring,
Strategic Importation, reserve supplies, price review, and annual reporting.

The Department of Trade and Industry and Board of Investments shall evaluate
projects, operate the One-Stop Assistance Facility, and verify investment and
localization commitments.

The Food and Drug Administration shall regulate pharmaceutical products and
establishments, operate the FDA Green Lane, assign regulatory case officers,
evaluate applications, inspect facilities, test products, and verify regulatory
Localization Milestones.
The Department of Science and Technology shall administer grants, research
assistance, testing support, and technology-development programs.

The Department of Budget and Management shall evaluate multiyear budget
requirements.

The Government Procurement Policy Board shall issue procurement guidelines for
Partnerships, competitive dialogue, multiple awards, Government Purchase Shares,
and multiyear purchase commitments.

PhilHealth, the Bureau of Customs, government financial institutions, Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority, and other concerned agencies shall
perform the functions assigned to them under this Act.

The Department of Health shall submit to Congress and publish an annual report on
the medicines selected, Partnerships awarded, purchase shares, quantities purchased,
milestones completed, assistance provided, local production, import dependence,
prices, shortages, Strategic Importations, reserve supplies, and PhilHealth coverage.

      Section 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. ± Within one hundred
eighty days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health and
Department of Trade and Industry, in coordination with the concerned agencies,
shall issue the implementing rules and regulations.

The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe matters that require
medicine-specific, scientific, manufacturing, financial, procurement, or market
determination, including:

      (a) measurement of import dependence;
      (b) selection and ranking of Priority PNF Medicines;

      (c) standard units for supply and consumption;

      (d) reporting forms and systems;

      (e) proposal evaluation and scoring;

      (f) determination and division of the Government Purchase Share;

      (g) minimum and maximum purchase quantities;

      (h) Localization Periods and Milestones;

      (i) production and local-value requirements;

      (j) technology documentation;

      (k) price-setting, adjustment, and reduction methods;

      (l) grant amounts and counterpart requirements;

      (m) supply-alert and shortage thresholds;

      (n) reserve quantities and arrangements;

      (o) shelf-life requirements; and

      (p) verification and audit procedures.

The implementing rules and regulations shall not remove or reduce the Guaranteed
Government Purchase Commitment, competitive-selection requirement, right to
multiple awards, market-certainty provisions, FDA safeguards, or government
assistance established by this Act.

      Section 23. Separability Clause. ± If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected by the declaration
shall remain in full force and effect.

      Section 24. Repealing Clause. ± All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      Section 25. Effectivity. ± This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
