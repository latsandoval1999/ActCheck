EXPLANATORY NOTE

Public transportation is essential to the daily lives of millions of Filipinos. Every day,
workers, students, and commuters rely on public utility vehicles (PUVs) to reach their workplaces,
schools, and essential services. At the heart of this system are PUV drivers and conductors, whose
livelihoods often depend on the completion of daily trips, passenger collections, and other
operational requirements of public transportation services.

For many transport workers, particularly those engaged under boundary, trip-based,
commission-based, or similar arrangements, interruptions in transport operations may have
immediate financial consequences. Such interruptions may arise from government-imposed
transport safety restrictions, serious vehicle defects, or other objectively verifiable conditions that
prevent the safe continuation of an assigned trip.

Existing labor and occupational safety and health laws recognize the nghts of workers to
safe working conditions, appropriate compensation, and protection against retaliation. These
safeguards remain applicable to transport workers covered by such laws. Nevertheless, public
transportation presents distinct operational circumstances. Drivers and conductors may be required
to account for passenger collections, remit boundary amounts, or comply with trip-related financial
arrangements even when assigned trips cannot be completed because of safety-related
interruptions.

The absence of a clear, transport-specific procedure for documenting these interruptions
and accounting for their financial consequences may expose affected workers to disputed charges,
unexplained collection adjustments, and disagreements over obligations arising from trips that
could not lawfully or safely proceed.

This measure seeks to address these concerns by establishing safeguards for public utility
vehicle drivers and conductors during safety-related interruptions of transport operations.
Under the proposed measure, transport operators shall recognize duly established safety-related
interruptions, provide an accounting of affected trip-related financial obligations upon request, and
refrain from imposing additional financial penalties solely because drivers or conductors complied
with applicable safety restrictions.

The measure likewise prohibits the recovery of unrealized trip collections from covered
transport workers when such collections could not be generated because of a qualifying safety-
related interruption. The proposed safeguards shall apply without prejudice to existing wage
protections, occupational safety and health standards, and lawful contractual obligations.

The bill does not establish a new mandatory paid leave entitlement, automatically cancel
lawful boundary obligations, or require compensation for all periods of interrupted operations.
Rather, it provides a practical mechanism for ensuring that the financial consequences of safety-
related interruptions are documented, transparent, and not unfairly transferred to transport workers.

By promoting accountability in the treatment of interrupted trips, the measure seeks to
protect the livelihood of public transport workers while reinforcing compliance with lawful safety
requirements and safeguarding the commuting public.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
ESTABLISHING SAFEGUARDS FOR PUBLIC UTILITY VEHICLE DRIVERS AND
CONDUCTORS DURING SAFETY-RELATED INTERRUPTIONS OF TRANSPORT
OPERATIONS, PROVIDING FOR THE FAIR ACCOUNTING OF TRIP-RELATED
FINANCIAL OBLIGATIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Public Transport Workers'
Safety-Related Work Interruption Protection Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. —It is hereby declared the policy of the State to afford
full protection to labor, promote safe and fair working conditions, and protect the welfare of the
commuting public.

The State recognizes that public utility vehicle drivers and conductors may be subject to
compensation and operating arrangements under which their earnings or financial obligations are
affected by the completion of assigned trips.

Toward this end, the State shall promote fair and transparent treatment of trip-related
financial obligations arising from safety-related interruptions of public transportation operations,
without diminishing existing labor rights or unduly impairing lawful contractual obligations.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “Public Utility Vehicle (PUV)” refers to a motor vehicle authorized to provide public land
transportation services under applicable laws and regulations;

(b) “Transport Operator” refers to any individual, partnership, cooperative, corporation, or
other entity authorized to operate public utility vehicles under applicable laws and
regulations;

(c) “PUV Driver” refers to a person engaged by a transport operator to operate or drive a public
utility vehicle in the provision of authorized public land transportation services, regardless
of the manner of compensation;

(d) “PUV Conductor” refers to a person engaged by a transport operator to assist in public
utility vehicle operations, including fare collection, passenger assistance, and other duties
incidental to the provision of public transportation services, regardless of the manner of
compensation;

(e) “Covered Transport Worker” refers collectively to a PUV driver or PUV conductor as
defined under this Act;

(f) “Boundary Arrangement” refers to an arrangement under which a PUV driver is required
to remit a fixed or otherwise agreed amount to a transport operator from earnings derived
from the operation of a public utility vehicle;

(g) “Safety-Related Work Interruption” refers to the temporary suspension, discontinuance, or

non-completion of an assigned public transportation trip arising from any of the following:

(i) A lawful order or directive issued by the appropriate government agency or local

government unit suspending or restricting public transportation operations on
grounds of public safety;

(ii) A serious vehicle defect or mechanical condition that renders continued operation
unsafe, as established through an inspection, official report, or other competent
verification under applicable transportation safety regulations; or

(iii) | A work stoppage or suspension of operations arising from an imminent danger
situation recognized or determined in accordance with applicable occupational
safety and health laws and regulations;

(h) “Trip-Related Financial Obligation” refers to a boundary remittance, trip collection target,
commission adjustment, or other financial obligation directly connected with the
performance or completion of an assigned public transportation trip; and

(i) “Unrealized Trip Collection” refers to an amount representing anticipated fares or other
trip revenues that were not actually collected because an assigned trip was not undertaken
or completed due to a safety-related work interruption.

SECTION 4. Scope and Coverage. — This Act shall apply to public utility vehicle
operators and covered transport workers engaged in authorized public land transportation services,
including those operating under boundary, commission-based, trip-based, or similar compensation
arrangements.

The protections under this Act shall apply regardless of the manner by which covered
transport workers are compensated, without prejudice to the determination of employment status
and corresponding rights under existing labor laws.

SECTION 5. Recognition of Safety-Related Work Interruptions. — A transport operator
shall recognize a safety-related work interruption established in accordance with Section 3(g) of
this Act.

A covered transport worker who experiences such interruption shall, as soon as reasonably
practicable, notify the transport operator or its authorized representative of the circumstances
preventing the continuation or completion of the assigned trip.

The existence of such interruption may be established through official government
directives, inspection findings, dispatch records, or other reliable documentation. A report made
in good faith shall not, by itself, constitute conclusive proof of a qualifying interruption, without
prejudice to the worker's rights under existing occupational safety and health laws.

SECTION 6. Fair Accounting of Trip-Related Financial Obligations. — Where a safety-
related work interruption affects an assigned trip, the transport operator shall, upon request of the
covered transport worker, provide an accounting of the trip-related financial obligations affected
by such interruption.

Such accounting shall indicate the amounts actually collected or remitted, any remaining
trip-related financial obligation claimed by the transport operator, and the basis of any adjustment
or additional charge arising from the interruption.

The accounting may be provided in written or electronic form using existing operational records.

SECTION 7. Protection Against Unfair Financial Charges. — No transport operator shall
require a covered transport worker to remit, replace, or personally shoulder unrealized trip
collections solely because an assigned trip could not be undertaken or completed due to a duly
established safety-related work interruption.

No transport operator shall impose any additional fine, surcharge, or punitive financial
charge upon a covered transport worker solely on account of such interruption.

Unrealized trip collections shall not be converted into additional financial obligations
chargeable to a covered transport worker, whether through subsequent boundary adjustments,
collection targets, or similar arrangements.

Nothing in this section shall be construed as automatically cancelling, reducing, or
suspending lawful fixed boundary obligations or other financial obligations independently
incurred under applicable agreements, subject to existing laws and regulations.

SECTION 8. Protection Against Retaliation. — No transport operator shall dismiss,
suspend, deny future trip assignments to, or otherwise subject a covered transport worker to
adverse treatment solely for requesting an accounting under Section 6, contesting a financial
charge prohibited under Section 7, or filing a complaint in good faith concerning a violation of this
Act.

Nothing in this section shall diminish the protections against retaliation provided under
existing labor and occupational safety and health laws, nor prevent the imposition of lawful
disciplinary measures for legitimate grounds in accordance with applicable laws.

SECTION 9. Complaints and Enforcement. — The Department of Transportation (DOTr),
through the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB), shall monitor
compliance with this Act by public utility vehicle operators under its regulatory jurisdiction.

Complaints arising from alleged violations of this Act shall be referred to the appropriate
government agency or tribunal in accordance with existing laws and regulations.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed as expanding or diminishing the jurisdiction of the
LTFRB, the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), or other competent authorities under
existing laws.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOTr, through the LTFRB, in coordination with the DOLE and the
Land Transportation Office (LTO), and after consultation with representatives of transport
operators, drivers, and conductors, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
implementation.

SECTION 11. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
