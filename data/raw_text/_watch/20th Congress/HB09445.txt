                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right
to health and ensure access to essential health services for all. Article II, Section 15 of
the 1987 Constitution expressly provides that “the State shall protect and promote the
right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them,” while Article
XIII, Section 11 requires the State to adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach
to health development and make health services available to all at affordable cost,
prioritizing the underprivileged. Despite these mandates, many Filipinos from Unserved
and Underserved areas (UUAs), Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas
(GIDAs) and Indigenous Peoples communities, continue to face systemic barriers to
healthcare access.

       According to the Department of Health (DOH)1, as of 2024, there are over 7,193
areas all over the country that remain partially or severely underserved, with large
clusters of UUAs found in Mindanao, the Cordillera, Eastern Visayas, and remote island
provinces. Families in these areas often travel several hours, or in some cases days, by
boat, habal-habal, or on foot to reach the nearest primary care facility. These barriers
contribute to delayed diagnoses, preventable maternal deaths, untreated chronic
illnesses, and widening inequities in health outcomes—challenges consistently
acknowledged in national health assessments.

      The COVID-19 pandemic revealed the immense potential of telehealth and digital
medicine to bridge these gaps. DOH documented a surge of 100,000 patients a month

        Telehealth has since proven essential for primary care, specialist referrals,
mental health counseling, follow-up care, and health education. It was confirmed by the
National Institute of Health of the University of the Philippines-Manila that there was at
least a 50% increase in the use of telemedicine services in 2020 from pre-pandemic
levels in urban areas3. However, not so much has been recorded from rural areas due
to the lack of access.

        This bill seeks to strengthen the Universal Health Care Act by institutionalizing
telehealth services, establishing Rural Telehealth Centers (RTCs), and integrating them
into local Service Delivery Networks. It mandates the DOH and LGUs to ensure referral
linkages, operational standards, and digital infrastructure support. Crucially, the bill also
establishes a Community Telehealth Development Program in partnership with the
National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), TESDA, and the Indigenous
Peoples Health Program. This program will train Katutubo Health Workers (KHWs)4,
Barangay Health Workers, midwives, and community telehealth aides in telemedicine
operations, culturally appropriate primary care, emergency teletriage, and digital health
literacy, ensuring that IP communities and UUAs receive safe, respectful, and effective
healthcare within their cultural context.

      Ultimately, this bill reinforces UHC by ensuring that digital health solutions
become permanent, standardized, and accessible components of the national health
system.

      In view of the foregoing, and with the urgent need to ensure that no Filipino,
regardless of geography, ethnicity, or economic class, is denied timely and quality
medical care, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
  STRENGTHENING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11223, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
   “UNIVERSAL HEALTH CARE ACT,” BY INSTITUTIONALIZING TELEHEALTH
 SERVICES, ESTABLISHING RURAL TELEHEALTH CENTERS, AND PROVIDING
  COMMUNITY-BASED TRAINING PROGRAMS FOR INDIGENOUS AND RURAL
                         HEALTH WORKERS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

    Section 1. Amendments to Section 4 of Republic Act No. 11223 (Definition of
Terms). Sections 4 (v), (w), and (x) of RA 1123 are hereby inserted to read as follows:

            “Section 4. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

       (a) Abuse of authority – xxx

       (b) Amenities – xxx

       …

      (u) Unethical act – xxx

      (v) Telehealth or Telemedicine refers to the delivery of health care services
      using information and communications technologies, including
      teleconsultations,     remote     diagnostics,      digital    prescriptions,
      telecounseling, and remote patient monitoring.

      (w) Rural Telehealth Centers (RTCs) are community-based telehealth
      service sites established by the DOH and LGUs and integrated into the
      Service Delivery Network (SDN), prioritizing Geographically Isolated and
      Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs).

      (x) Katutubo Health Workers (KHWs) refer to trained community health
      workers belonging to Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous
      Peoples (ICCs/IPs), developed in coordination with the Indigenous Peoples
      Health Program (IPHP).”


     Section 2. Amendments to Section 17 of Republic Act No. 11223
(Population-based Health Services). Section 17 (d) of RA 1123 is hereby inserted to
read as follows:

             “Section 17. Population-based Health Services. - The DOH shall
      endeavor to contract province-wide and city-wide health systems for the delivery
      of population-based health services. Province-wide and city-wide health systems
      shall have the following minimum components:

      (a)​Primary care provider network with patient records accessible throughout the
          health system;

      (b)​Accurate, sensitive, and timely epidemiologic surveillance systems; and

      (c)​Proactive and effective health promotion programs or campaigns.

      (d)​A Service Delivery Network (SDN) that integrates Telehealth Services
          and Rural Telehealth Centers (RTCs) as essential components of local
          health systems. Local Government Units (LGUs) shall ensure the
          establishment, operationalization, and referral linkage of RTCs into
          primary care facilities, specialty centers, and higher-level hospitals
          within the SDN, prioritizing access for Unserved and Underserved
          Areas (UAAs), formerly known as Geographically Isolated and
          Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs). ”

      Section 3. Amendments to Section 23 of Republic Act No. 11223 (National
Health Human Resource Master Plan). Section 23 (a) of RA 1123 is hereby inserted
to read as follows:

           ​ “Section 23. National Health Human Resource Master Plan. - The DOH,
     together with stakeholders, shall ensure the formulation and implementation of a
     National Health Human Resource Master Plan that will provide policies and
     strategies for the appropriate generation, recruitment, retraining, regulation,
     retention and reassessment of health workforce based on population health
     needs.

          To ensure continuity in the provision of the health programs and services, all
     health professionals and health care workers shall be guaranteed permanent
     employment and competitive salaries.

         (a)​Community Telehealth Development. The DOH, in partnership with
             the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), Technical
             Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and the
             Indigenous Peoples Health Program (IPHP), shall implement a
             Community Telehealth Development Program to train:
                (1)​Katutubo Health Workers (KHWs);
                (2)​Barangay Health Workers (BHWs);
                (3)​Midwives and community health navigators; and
                (4)​Telehealth aides supporting digital consultations.

             Training shall include telemedicine operations, culturally appropriate
             primary care, maternal and child health, emergency teletriage,
             mental health first aid, digital health literacy, among others.”

       Section 4. Repealing clause. All existing laws, orders, decrees, rules and
regulations or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       Section 5. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
