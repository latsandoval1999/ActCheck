                               AN ACT
STREAMLINING ALL PLANS, PROGRAMS, ACTIVITIES, AND PROJECTS TO
REHABILITATE, PROTECT, SUSTAINABLY MANAGE AND DEVELOP THE MANILA
BAY REGION, THEREBY CREATING THE MANILA BAY AUTHORITY, AND PROVIDING
FUNDS THEREFOR


                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

The rehabilitation, protection, and sustainable development of Manila Bay has long been
a national priority. As the country’s premier gateway for commerce, industry, tourism,
fisheries, and maritime activities, Manila Bay is both an economic engine and an ecological
treasure. Its degradation over the years, manifested in declining water quality, recurring
flooding, habitat loss, and unmanaged coastal development, underscores the urgent need
for a unified, science-based, and institutionally coherent governance framework.


In 2008, the Supreme Court issued a Writ of Continuing Mandamus directing concerned
agencies to clean up, rehabilitate, and preserve Manila Bay. Since then, numerous
programs and initiatives have been undertaken by various national government agencies,
local government units, and stakeholders. However, the fragmentation of mandates and
overlapping jurisdictions have hindered the efficient and sustained implementation of
rehabilitation efforts.


This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by creating the Manila Bay
Authority, a Government-Owned or Controlled Corporation that shall serve as the
primary agency to integrate, harmonize, and oversee all plans, programs, activities, and
projects (PAPs) concerning the Manila Bay region and its watershed.


                                                                                              1
Significantly, this bill is firmly anchored on, and in full alignment with, the Manila Bay
Sustainable Development Master Plan (MBSDMP), a comprehensive twenty-year
development blueprint crafted over a considerable period through extensive collaboration
among national agencies, local governments, civil society organizations, the private sector,
and development partners. The MBSDMP was spearheaded by the Department of
Economy, Planning, and Development (formerly the National Economic and Development
Authority) in partnership with the Government of the Netherlands.


The MBSDMP envisions an integrated “ridge-to-reef” approach that promotes inclusive
growth, climate resilience, ecosystem restoration, disaster risk reduction, water quality
improvement, and the upgrading of informal settlements. Central to the Master Plan is the
proposal to establish a single, dedicated entity to manage and coordinate the sustainable
development of Manila Bay. The creation of the Manila Bay Authority is the legislative
realization of that long-standing institutional reform recommendation.


By subsuming existing coordinating bodies and consolidating functions presently
dispersed among multiple agencies, the proposed Authority will:
   1. Ensure policy coherence and streamlined decision-making;
   2. Fast-track compliance with environmental laws and the Supreme Court’s
      continuing mandamus;
   3. Institutionalize the user-pays and polluter-pays principles;
   4. Strengthen enforcement mechanisms and water quality management;
   5. Promote sustainable investments and balanced socio-economic development; and
   6. Establish a Manila Bay Coastal Greenbelt to enhance climate adaptation and
      ecological protection.


Moreover, this measure safeguards local autonomy by preserving the powers of local
government units over purely local matters, while providing a unified regional framework
for watershed-based governance. It likewise harmonizes its functions with the Laguna
Lake Development Authority to ensure coordinated management of interconnected water
systems.


The proposed capitalization and dedicated funding structure will allow the Authority to
implement long-term, strategic interventions consistent with the MBSDMP and other
environmental statutes. By institutionalizing a permanent governance mechanism, the

                                                                                          2
State affirms its commitment to transform Manila Bay into a resilient, vibrant, and
sustainably managed ecosystem that benefits present and future generations.


The creation of the Manila Bay Authority is not merely an administrative reform. It is the
culmination of years of multi-stakeholder planning and international cooperation, and a
decisive step toward restoring Manila Bay as a symbol of national pride and sustainable
progress.


In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

11   Sec. 3. Definition of Terms:
12
13            a. Coastal Greenbelt shall refer to a strip of natural or planted coastal
14                vegetation, typically composed of mangroves and beach forest species,
15                designed to prevent erosion and mitigate the impacts of natural disasters like
16                storm surges, tsunamis, and climate change. These zones generally stretch at
17                least 100 meters from the sea towards land and serve as crucial, nature-based
18                buffers for coastal communities.
19
20            b. Manila Bay Sustainable Development Master Plan (MBSDMP) refers to the
21                20-year comprehensive and sustainable master plan for Manila Bay and the
22                immediate coastal zone with the aim for inclusive growth, ecosystem
23                protection, climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction, water
24                quality improvement, and upgrading informal settlements;
25
26            c. Operational Plan for the Manila Bay Coastal Strategy (OPMBCS) refers
27                to a framework of strategies identified to provide stakeholders what needs to
28                be executed to achieve the shared vision of the future of the Manila Bay region,
29                and defines long-term actions, as well as the necessary support and
30                participation of all stakeholders;
31
32            d. Polluter-pays Principle shall refer to the principle that those causing
33                pollution should meet the costs to which it gives rise. The Authority shall
34                endeavor to promote the internalization of environmental costs and the use of
35                economic instruments, taking into account the approach that the polluter

                                                                                                  5
1                     should, in principle, bear the cost of pollution, with due regard to the public
2                     interest and without distorting international trade and investment;
3
4                e. Ridge to Reef Approach shall refer to a mechanism that aims to provide a
5                     holistic intervention for protecting the coastal area by targeting environmental
6                     degradation in the uplands (ridge) that impact the coastal ecosystems through
7                     sedimentation, by restoring the shoreline and protecting marine ecosystems
8                     (reef), thereby mitigating storm surges; and
9
10               f.      User-pays Principle shall refer to the variation of the polluter-pays
11                    principle that calls upon the user of a natural resource to bear the cost of
12                    running down natural capital.

21   a close coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR),
22   for policy and program harmonization.

4    Sec. 39. Immunity from Suit. – The board of directors, the administrator, officers, and
5    employees of the Authority, including the PCG and PNP-MG personnel assigned to exercise
6    police and enforcement powers of the Authority, shall not be subject to any action, claim,
7    or demand, in connection with any act done or omitted in the performance of duties and
8    exercise of powers, except for actions and intentional omissions done in evident bad faith
9    or gross negligence.

11   Sec. 40. Indemnity.– Unless the actions of the board of directors, the administrator, or
12   any of its officers and employees of the Authority, including the PCG and PNP-MG
13   personnel assigned to exercise police and enforcement powers of the Authority, shall be
14   found to be in willful violation of this act, performed with evident bad faith or gross
15   negligence, the board of directors, the administrator, officers,     and employees of the
16   Authority, including the PCG and PNP-MG personnel, shall be held free and harmless to
17   the fullest extent permitted by law, from any liability, and shall be indemnified for all
18   liabilities, losses, claims, demands, damages, deficiencies, costs, and expenses of
19   whatsoever kind and nature that may arise, in connection with the exercise of their powers
20   and in the performance of their duties and functions.
21
22   The Authority shall underwrite or advance litigation costs and expenses, including legal
23   fees and other expenses of external counsel, or provide legal assistance to its board of
24   directors, the administrator, officers,   and employees of the Authority, including the

26   administrative or any other action or proceeding, to which they are made a party by reason
27   of, or in connection with, the exercise of their authority or the performance of their duties

15   Sec. 41. Prohibited Acts, Fines and Penalties, Criminal and Civil Liabilities.
16   Therewithal the prohibited acts, fines and penalties, criminal and civil liabilities enclosed
17   in PD 1586, RA 8749, RA 9003, RA 6969, and other laws related to the protection and
18   preservation of the Manila Bay region, the Authority shall have the power to impose other
19   prohibited acts, fines and penalties, file separate criminal charges and pursue separate
20   civil actions for damages resulting from violations of the provisions of this Act, and rules
21   and regulations issued pursuant thereto.
22
23   Upon the effectivity of this Act, the Authority shall also have the capacity to outline a
24   graduated fines and penalties register that shall applicably correspond with the prohibited
25   acts and violations on the aforesaid environmental laws: Provided, That the Board,
26   through the recommendation of the Enforcement Service, shall periodically update the
27   said register through a Board Resolution.
