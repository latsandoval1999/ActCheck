 1                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
 2   The proposed Gender Responsive and Inclusive Pandemic, Emergency or Disaster
 3   Management Act seeks to institutionalize gender-responsive and inclusive systems,
 4   protocols, and programs in all stages of public health emergencies, disasters, and similar
 5   crises.

 6   The COVID-19 pandemic and recent disasters have revealed how women, particularly
 7   those in marginalized sectors, bear disproportionate burdens during emergencies. They
 8   face heightened risks of gender-based violence, loss of livelihood, limited access to
 9   essential health services—especially sexual and reproductive health—and increased
10   unpaid care work. These gender-differentiated impacts are often overlooked in existing
11   emergency and disaster management policies, resulting in responses that fail to address
12   the specific needs of women and other vulnerable groups.

13   While current laws such as the Magna Carta of Women and the Philippine Disaster Risk
14   Reduction and Management Act affirm gender equality and women’s rights, they do
15   not provide clear, enforceable mechanisms to ensure that gender considerations are
16   systematically integrated in emergency preparedness, response, and recovery. This bill
17   fills that gap by requiring government agencies and local government units to adopt
18   gender-responsive approaches, guarantee continuity of essential services, and ensure

                                                 1
1   meaningful participation of women in decision-making and leadership roles during
2   crises.

3   By enacting this measure, the State will not only uphold its constitutional duty to protect
4   and promote the rights of women but also strengthen the overall effectiveness, equity,
5   and sustainability of public health and disaster responses. The proposed Act will help
6   build a more resilient and inclusive society where no one is left behind, even in times
7   of crisis.

8   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 5          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State hereby affirms its role as the primary

 6   duty-bearer in ensuring the enjoyment and fulfillment of all human rights during

 7   pandemics, emergencies or disasters, especially the right to life, which permits no

 8   derogation even in times of public emergency, and the right to health, so that every

 9   individual shall enjoy the highest attainable level of health. It affirms its Constitutional

10   duties to guarantee the equality of men and women to the enjoyment of their human

11   rights, with focus on the differentiated needs of, and impact of emergencies on, women,
                                                  3
1    prohibit discrimination, and create an enabling environment under the “new normal”

2    that guarantees the human rights of women. It acknowledges its corresponding duties

3    relating to women’s rights to participation and representation, protection from gender-

4    based violence during emergency situations, sexual and reproductive health rights

5    (SRHR), and economic empowerment of women. It adopts a zero-tolerance policy

6    towards gender-based violence, abuse and exploitation, which is rooted in gender

7    inequality and discrimination. The implementation of approaches and strategies

8    relevant to disaster risk reduction, emergency and pandemic management must

9    integrate human rights and gender lenses. It is fundamental that policies, plans,

10   programs, actions, conduct and results are non-discriminatory and that no one is left

11   behind.

13   frameworks on gender-responsive programming during the pandemic, emergency or

14   disaster and shall likewise establish mechanisms and processes to ensure the adoption,

15   reprogramming and/or continuity of programs that serve and address the gender-

16   differentiated needs of women.

20              intersectional analysis in pandemic policy and decision making, planning

22          (c) To develop and craft guidelines and instructions on practices, protocols,

20          SEC. 6. Leadership, Participation and Empowerment of Women. – During a

21   pandemic, emergency or disaster, women shall be empowered to be included in the

22   national and local government’s response systems to address the gender-differentiated

23   needs of the different groups of women especially the marginalized. It is thus mandated

24   that response and recovery systems must ensure that women are placed strategically

25   and participate meaningfully in leadership, decision-making, and policy-making

26   positions at all levels. In accordance with this, the Philippine Commission on Women


                                                 16
1    (PCW) shall be made a permanent member in the IATF-EID or similar bodies that will

2    be created in response to a pandemic, emergency or disaster to help facilitate the

3    continuous integration of a gender-responsive plan to be developed therein in national

4    governance response systems. Further, forty percent (40%) of membership of all

5    development councils from the regional, provincial, city, municipal and barangay levels

6    shall be composed of women.

7           SEC. 7. Humanitarian Principles Guiding Programming and Management to

8    Address Gender-Differentiated Needs of Women During the Pandemic, Emergency or

9    Disaster. – Following the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Guidelines for

10   Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, and the

11   United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) Minimum Standards for Prevention and

12   Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies, the following principles shall

13   ground, inform and guide the formulation and implementation of policies, plans,

14   programs, and other appropriate measures, including affirmative actions, in managing

15   the pandemic, emergency or disaster:

16          (a) A survivor-centered approach which creates a supportive environment in

17              which survivors’ rights and wishes are respected, their safety is ensured, and

18              they are treated with dignity and respect;

19          (b) A human rights-based approach that seeks to analyze and address the root

20              causes of discrimination and inequality to ensure that everyone has the right

21              to live with freedom and dignity, safe from violence, exploitation and abuse,

22              in accordance with principles of human rights law;

23          (c) A community-based approach that ensures that affected populations are

24              engaged actively as partners in developing strategies related to their

25              protection and the provision of humanitarian assistance. This approach


                                               17
1       involves direct involvement of women, girls and other at-risk groups at all

2       stages in the humanitarian response, to identify protection risks and

3       solutions, and build on existing community-based protection mechanisms;

4    (d) The humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and

5       neutrality which should underpin the implementation of the Minimum

6       Standards and are essential to maintaining access to affected populations

7       and ensuring an effective humanitarian response;

8    (e) The “do no harm” approach which takes into account all measures

9       necessary to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of the actions

10      of humanitarian actors;

11   (f) The principles of partnership which comprise a framework for all actors in

12      the humanitarian space to follow principles of equality, transparency, a

13      results-oriented approach, responsibility and complementarity;

14   (g) Taking into account the best interests of the child wherein child and

15      adolescent girl and boy survivors of sexual abuse have the right to have their

16      best interests assessed and determined and taken as a primary consideration

17      in all decisions that affect them;

18   (h) Focus should be given to the intersectionality of the circumstances of

19      women, taking into account the specific needs and concerns of marginalized

20      and vulnerable women groups including women and girls deprived of

21      liberty, women working in the informal sector, women migrant workers, and

22      other similarly situated at-risk individuals and groups; and

23   (i) Rights-based policing wherein human rights principles and practices are

24      taught and applied at all levels, in policy, doctrinal, operational and

25      administrative functions of the police organization.

10          SEC. 11. Gendered Approach to Vaccination. – The procurement, allocation,

11   distribution, delivery, facilitation and administration of vaccines must be transparent

12   and must take into account gender-based differences in immunological responses, care

13   burden or work of women both paid and unpaid, security of women against gender-

14   based violence and other forms of attacks and other factors that increase women’s

15   vulnerability. Safe, efficacious and free vaccines or medications, including newly-

16   approved ones for the current pandemic or health emergency, must be available and

17   accessible to women, taking into account intersectional considerations that may impede

18   such access and vulnerabilities of women that affect their right to life, health and

19   security: Provided, That such access shall be in accordance with the priority and health

20   protocols of the Philippines or relevant code of practice, and is rights-based, non-

21   discriminatory, voluntary and based on informed consent. Information on vaccination

22   and the scientific evidence behind it must be clear and effectively communicated. These

23   gendered approach must be integrated in legal, policy and program implementation of

24   vaccinations to address the pandemics, emergencies or disasters.

8           SEC. 19. National Preparedness and Response Program to Address the

9    Gender-Differentiated Needs of Women During the Pandemic, Emergency or Disaster.

10   – To ensure access to essential services including access to justice and remedial

11   measures and to mitigate the impact of any health event of public concern, emergency

12   or disaster on the economy, a coherent, appropriate, efficient, developmental, evidence-

13   based and survivor-centered national program against GBV and addressing gender-

14   differentiated needs of women especially from marginalized sectors must be developed

15   to inform, direct and coordinate its implementation at all levels and to ensure

16   accessibility, availability and quality of services at the community level. For this

17   purpose, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC)

18   and PCW, in coordination with the IATF-EID, shall come up with said national plan,

19   to be cascaded to the local government units, and must be automatically integrated into

20   the national strategy to manage the pandemic, emergency or disaster. This plan shall be

21   subjected to review every two (2) years. It shall address GBV and gender-differentiated

22   needs at all stages of the pandemic, emergency or disaster management, and

23   anticipating related events and factors that might impede the functioning of legal,

24   medical, social, remedial and other protection measures and mechanisms, to include,

25   but not limited to:

1           SEC. 23. Penalties. - Any public officer, employee of an agency or any private

2    individual mandated to implement this Act, who shall fail to perform in accordance

3    with the mandates, duties, tasks and other acts imposed by this law shall be

4    administratively liable for neglect in the performance of duty during the pandemics,

5    emergencies or disasters. Should damage or injury be inflicted on any person by reason

6    of such neglect, the aggrieved party can have recourse against the erring public officer,

7    employee or private individual for appropriate legal remedies as the law may provide

8    or afford, including criminal cases. In case of gender-based violence amounting to a

9    crime or offense is committed by a public officer or employee while in the performance

10   of any acts covered herein, penalties next higher in degree shall be imposed, including

11   sexual harassment, especially in quarantine facilities, and against persons belonging to

12   at risk individuals and those adversely affected by the pandemic, emergency or disaster.
