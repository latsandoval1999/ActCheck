                            EXPLANATORY NOTE
       The Programme for International Student Assessment recorded the
Philippines as having the highest incidence of bullying among all participating
countries in 2018, with 65% of Filipino learners reporting bullying at least a
few times a month; in 2022, roughly 43% of girls and 53% of boys still reported
repeated monthly incidents, against OECD averages of about 20% and 21%.1
DepEd itself has declared bullying a national concern rather than a
school-level problem.2 Meanwhile, the National Center for Mental Health
registered 7,189 suicide-related hotline calls from January to September 2025
mostly from individuals aged 18 to 30.3 These are the ordinary, daily conditions
that a functioning guidance service exists to meet.
       Yet Filipino learners are in the middle of a mental health crisis that our
schools are not staffed to answer. In the hearing of the Committee on
Appropriations on 3 September 2025, the Department of Education (DepEd)
confirmed that the country had only 4,069 licensed guidance counselors as
against the more than 50,000 required to meet the recommended global ratio
of one counselor for every 250 students.4 Roughly 79% of DepEd guidance
counselor positions (4,460 positions) remain unfilled, with several regions
posting vacancy rates above 80% and with single counselors covering multiple
campuses.5 Higher education fares no better: data of the Philippine Guidance
and Counseling Association (PGCA) show an 80% shortfall in higher education
institutions (HEIs), where only 1,029 counselors serve against an ideal
requirement of 5,315.6

       The deficiency is not one of demand but of supply. Since 2008, only a
little over 3,000 individuals have passed the licensure examination for
guidance counselors.7 Against approximately 4,286 counselors needed and
only about 339 graduates of master's programs in Guidance and Counseling
produced each year, there are roughly seventeen (17) vacant posts for every
graduate, and the system would require twelve (12) to fourteen (14) years to
close the existing gap even on the heroic assumptions of zero attrition and zero
enrollment growth.8 The bottleneck is the graduate degree itself: Republic Act
No. 9258 requires both a master's degree and licensure,9 yet the cost of that
degree is borne almost entirely by the individual, while entry-level
compensation begins at Salary Grade 11 (PhP 31,705.00) and rises only to PhP
36,125.00 at Salary Grade 13 for the same qualification.10
      This bill institutionalizes that investment as a permanent national
program. A scholarship alone will not solve a problem this large, and this bill
does not pretend otherwise. It does, however, address the single constraint that
no amount of plantilla creation, campus security hardware, or emergency
deployment can overcome: there are simply not enough licensed professionals
to hire. Metal detectors screen for weapons; only a counselor can screen for
despair.
     In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL SCHOLARSHIP PROGRAM FOR GRADUATE
  STUDIES IN GUIDANCE AND COUNSELING, STRENGTHENING THE
 GUIDANCE AND COUNSELING WORKFORCE OF THE COUNTRY, AND
               APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
     SEC. 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “National
Guidance and Counseling Scholarship Act.”
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right of all citizens to quality education and to the highest
attainable standard of mental health, and to maintain a system of
scholarship grants available to deserving students. Toward this end, the State
shall build a sufficient, competent, and equitably distributed corps of
Registered Guidance Counselors by financing graduate education in
Guidance and Counseling, directing that investment to underserved regions,
and securing a period of public service in return.
      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
      (a) Program refers to the Scholarship Program established under
           Section 4 of this Act;
      (b) Registered Guidance Counselor (RGC) refers to a person duly
           registered and licensed under Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise
           known as the “Guidance and Counseling Act of 2004”;
      (c) School Counselor Associate (SCA) refers to the position created
           under Republic Act No. 12080;​
      (d) Qualifying Program refers to a master's degree program in Guidance
           and Counseling, or an allied graduate program certified under
           Section 12 of this Act, offered by a higher education institution
           (HEI) recognized by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED);​
      (e) Priority Region refers to a region whose guidance counselor vacancy
            rate exceeds eighty percent (80%) based on the latest data of the
            Department of Education (DepEd), CHED, and the Philippine
            Guidance and Counseling Association (PGCA); and​
      (f) Scholar refers to a person admitted to and receiving benefits under
            the Program.
​
​     SEC. 4. Establishment of the Program. — There is hereby established
a permanent national scholarship program for graduate studies in Guidance
and Counseling, to be administered by CHED in coordination with DepEd,
the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Civil Service Commission
(CSC), and the PGCA. The Program shall finance the completion of Qualifying
Programs leading to licensure as an RGC and shall support the core guidance
services of testing, individual inventory, orientation, career guidance,
counseling, and follow-up.​
​
​      SEC. 5. Scope and Coverage. — The Program shall cover master's
degree programs in Guidance and Counseling and allied graduate programs
certified under Section 12. It shall be open to: (a) personnel of DepEd
endorsed by their Schools Division Offices (SDOs); (b) personnel of public and
private HEIs, state universities and colleges, and local government units; and
(c) other qualified Filipino citizens. Not less than sixty percent (60%) of
annual slots shall be reserved for DepEd personnel and not less than twenty
percent (20%) for open-category applicants.​
​
​     SEC. 6. Qualifications. — An applicant shall:
      (a) be a Filipino citizen and a resident of the Philippines;​
      (b) hold a bachelor's degree in Guidance and Counseling or in an allied
           discipline acceptable under Republic Act No. 9258 and the
           standards of the PRC Board of Guidance and Counseling;​
      (c) have obtained an undergraduate general weighted average of at least
           eighty-five percent (85%) or its equivalent, subject to such
           adjustment as the implementing rules may allow for applicants
           from Priority Regions and geographically isolated and
           disadvantaged areas (GIDAs);​
      (d) have been admitted to or be enrolled in a Qualifying Program; and​
      (e) be of good moral character, with no pending criminal,
          administrative, or disciplinary case.​
       SEC. 7. Scholarship Benefits. — A Scholar shall receive:
(a) tuition and other school fees, paid directly to the delivering HEI;
(b) a monthly living stipend; and
(c) book, thesis, licensure review, and connectivity allowances as may be
provided in the implementing rules.
       The amounts shall be set by CHED in consultation with the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and shall be reviewed at least
once every three (3) years to account for the normative cost of education and
inflation. Support shall cover two (2) academic years, extendible for one (1)
academic year for thesis completion, and shall include coverage of the fees for
one (1) taking of the licensure examination.​
​
​     SEC. 8. Allocation of Slots. — CHED shall determine the number of
slots annually. Each CHED Regional Office shall receive a minimum
allocation, and Priority Regions shall receive at least fifteen percent (15%)
more than the base allocation. Unfilled slots in a region may be reallocated
only after all qualified applicants in that region have been exhausted, and
such reallocation shall not reduce the allocation of a Priority Region. The final
allocation shall be published at least ninety (90) days before the start of the
relevant academic year.​
​
​     SEC. 9. Selection. — Applications shall be filed with the CHED
Regional Office having jurisdiction over the applicant's residence or HEI and
evaluated by a Selection Committee composed of representatives of CHED,
DepEd, and the PGCA, on the basis of academic performance, financial need,
demonstrated commitment to the profession, and geographic priority. In case
of a tie, preference shall be given, in order, to applicants from GIDAs,
applicants from regions with the highest vacancy rates, and public school
personnel with the longest service.​
​
​     SEC. 10. Return Service. — A Scholar shall render return service as
an RGC, or in a guidance and counseling capacity pending licensure, in a
public school, state university or college, or government agency for a period
equivalent to the duration of the scholarship received, but in no case less
than two (2) years. Deployment shall prioritize Priority Regions and GIDAs.
Service rendered in a GIDA shall be credited at one and one-half (1.5) years
for every year of actual service.​
​
​       SEC. 11. Bridging Pathway for School Counselor Associates. —
Incumbent School Counselor Associates shall be accorded preferential
consideration under the Program. CHED, DepEd, the CSC, and the PRC shall
jointly establish a bridging pathway allowing an SCA to pursue a Qualifying
Program without forfeiting the position, through study leave, flexible or
modular delivery, or a similar arrangement, and shall determine the extent to
which supervised practice as an SCA may be credited toward practicum
requirements.​
​       SEC. 12. Certification of Allied Graduate Programs. — Within one (1)
year from the effectivity of this Act, the PRC Board of Guidance and
Counseling, in coordination with CHED, shall issue and thereafter maintain a
published list of graduate programs whose completion qualifies a graduate to
sit for the licensure examination for guidance counselors.
      ​
​     SEC. 13. Refund. — A Scholar shall refund the benefits received, in
whole or in part as the implementing rules may provide, in case of voluntary
withdrawal without justifiable cause, failure to complete the Qualifying
Program, failure to take the licensure examination within two (2) years from
graduation, failure to render return service, or misrepresentation.​
​
​     SEC. 14. Roles of Agencies. — For purposes of this Act:​
      (a) CHED shall administer the Program, issue guidelines, maintain the
           national registry of Scholars, and evaluate program performance;
      (b) DepEd shall endorse employee-applicants, furnish annual vacancy
           and deployment data by SDO, designate deployment areas, and
           integrate Scholars into its staffing plan;
      (c) the PRC, through the Board of Guidance and Counseling, shall
           certify qualifying academic credentials and furnish licensure and
           pass-rate data;
      (d) the CSC shall advise on eligibility and appointment requirements
           and facilitate recognition of Republic Act No. 1080 eligibility for
           appointment purposes; and
      (e) the PGCA shall provide distribution data, designate mentors for
           Scholars, and sit in the Selection Committee.
      SEC. 15. Review of Compensation and Career Progression. —
Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the DBM, in coordination
with the CSC, DepEd, and CHED, shall review and submit to Congress its
recommendations on the salary structure and career progression of guidance
counselors in the public sector, including the differentials among Salary
Grades 11, 12, and 13, the creation of higher promotional ranks, and the
grant of hardship or assignment premiums for counselors deployed in Priority
Regions and GIDAs.​
​     SEC. 16. Monitoring and Report to Congress. — CHED, in
coordination with DepEd and the PGCA, shall conduct an annual evaluation
of the Program covering enrollment and graduation, licensure pass rates,
deployment and return service compliance, regional distribution of
beneficiaries, and the resulting movement in the counselor-to-student ratio.
The report shall be submitted to the President and to both Houses of
Congress not later than the last working day of March of the succeeding year.​
​
​      SEC. 17. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current
appropriations of CHED, including the appropriation for specialized
post-graduate scholarships under the General Appropriations Act. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
      ​
​     SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, CHED and DepEd, in consultation with
the PRC, the CSC, the DBM, and the PGCA, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary to implement this Act.​
​
​     SEC. 19. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.​
​
​     SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.​
​
​     SEC. 21. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.​
