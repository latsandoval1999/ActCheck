                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Republic of the Philippines and the Federal Republic of Germany share a long-standing
relationship built on trade, intellectual exchange, diplomacy, development cooperation, and
strong people-to-people ties. Formal diplomatic relations were established in 1954, and over the
decades the partnership has grown into a stable and productive collaboration that continues to
benefit both countries.

Germany has been an important partner of the Philippines in the areas of science, research, and
education. Many Filipino scholars and professionals have pursued advanced studies in German
universities through scholarships from the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD).
Development cooperation programs implemented through the Deutsche Gesellschaft für
Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ) have also supported Philippine initiatives in
environmental protection, renewable energy, biodiversity conservation, and disaster resilience.

Economic relations between the Philippines and Germany remain strong. Germany is among the
Philippines’ key trading partners within the European Union. Philippine exports to Germany
include electronics, semiconductors, coconut products, and garments, while the Philippines
imports industrial machinery, medical equipment, automotive components, and other high-
quality technologies that support the modernization of local industries. German companies such
as Siemens, Bosch, and BASF have established operations and partnerships in the country,
contributing to employment, technology transfer, and industrial development.

Education has likewise played a vital role in strengthening Philippine–German ties. The German
European School Manila serves as an important institution that promotes intercultural learning
and academic exchange. By offering internationally recognized programs such as the German
International Abitur and the International Baccalaureate, the school provides students with
access to high-quality education that prepares them for higher learning and careers in an
increasingly globalized world.

Recognizing the German European School Manila as an educational institution of international
character affirms the Philippines’ commitment to strengthening global educational partnerships
and supporting institutions that foster academic excellence, cultural understanding, and
international cooperation.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
      RECOGNIZING THE GERMAN EUROPEAN SCHOOL MANILA, INCORPORATED
      AS AN EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTION OF INTERNATIONAL CHARACTER, AND
     GRANTING CERTAIN PREROGATIVES CONDUCIVE TO ITS DEVELOPMENT AND
                                 GROWTH

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1   SECTION 1. The German European School Manila, Inc., herein referred to as the “German
 2   School”, is hereby recognized as a basic educational institution of international character
 3   authorized to operate recognized by the Federal Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF) of
 4   Germany that primarily and principally adhere to universally accepted and recognized
 5   educational policies.

 6   SEC. 2. In support of the duty of the State to give priority to education, the German School shall
 7   provide basic education services and curricular programs that are socially responsible, forward
 8   thinking, and democratically disseminated, drawing inspiration from three (3) main guiding
 9   principles, namely:

10      (a) Brain and Intelligence Research;
11      (b) Change and Social Policy Development; and
12      (c) Language and Culture Studies.

13   The resultant unique international curriculum of the German School thus integrates the
14   following:
15   (1) The latest scientific findings on brain and intelligence research as well as critical pedagogy
16   pertaining to diversity and inclusive education;

17   (2) Language study at the age when a child learns multiple languages naturally and intuitively;
18   (3) Exposure to and proper use of technology; and
19   (4) Culturally responsive teaching through the use of multicultural and interactive learning
20   materials.

21   SEC. 3. The members of the German School shall be comprised of the parents or guardians of
22   the children, whether of Philippine or foreign nationality, who have enrolled and attended the
23   German School for at least one (1) school year: Provided, That no group of foreign nationality
24   shall comprise more than one-third (1/3) of the German School’s enrollment; Provided, further,
25   that the school year shall be determined in accordance with the German School’s own calendar
26   of duties and work schedule: Provided, finally, That the German School shall be governed by a
27   Board of Trustees, herein referred to as the Board, elected at large from among the corporation’s
28   German and/or Filipino members of good standing.
 1   SEC. 4. To enable the German School to continue carrying out its educational program, improve
 2   its standard of instruction, and meet the special needs of the foreign temporary resident
 3   community for quality education, it shall:

 4        (a) Accept applicant for admission, regardless of nationality, in accordance with its own
 5        eligibility standards and rules for admission, and grade placement: Provided, That no single
 6        alien nationality shall consist more than one-third of the entire student population in a given
 7        school year, as stated in the records of the German School, except in case of dependents of
 8        foreign diplomatic personnel or other foreign temporary residents, as may be provided by
 9        law;

10        (b) Whenever necessary to carry out its objectives, have the right to obtain leases on real
11        properties, acquire personal properties, tangible or intangible, or any interests in such
12        properties other than real, by purchase, lease, barter, donation, or gift, and dispose of,
13        mortgage, or encumber them for the benefit of the German School concerned, subject to the
14        limitations provided by law;

15        (c) Be managed and administered by a Head of German School who is a Filipino citizen, and
16        who possesses the qualifications prescribed by the Board;

17        (d) Upon consultation with the Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd),
18        determine its own curriculum, teach whatever language or languages it may deem proper,
19        and determine the amount of fees and assessments which may be reasonably imposed upon
20        its learners and students, to maintain or conform to the German School's standards of
21        education;

22        (e) Maintain the international standards of education compatible with those obtained in
23        similar schools of generally recognized standing;

24        (f) Employ its own teaching and management personnel selected by the Board either locally
25        or abroad, from the Philippines or other countries or nationalities, such foreign personnel
26        being exempt from otherwise applicable laws and regulations attending their employment,
27        except laws that have been or will be enacted for the protection of employees: Provided, That
28        such exemption shall not extend to their liability for income taxes, unless otherwise provided
29        by a bilateral agreement: Provided, further, That the German School shall give priority to
30        hiring locally qualified teaching and management personnel;

31        (g) Promote international friendship, goodwill, and understanding among members of the
32        foreign community and the citizens of the Philippines; and

33        (h) Not to teach anything derogatory to the prestige and well-being of the Philippines and
34        its people, nor those which will subvert its institution and government.

35   SEC 5. Notwithstanding the recognition and prerogatives under this Act, the German School shall
36   be under the supervision and regulation of the DepEd and shall adhere to the basic laws and the
37   rules and regulations of the DepEd governing international or foreign school.

38   SEC 6. Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the DepEd shall, in
39   consultation with the members of the Board, issue the rules and regulations implementing the
40   provisions of this Act.

41   SEC 7. If any part or provision of this Act is subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional,
42   the remaining provisions not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

43   SEC 8. Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the rules and regulations under Section 6 herein,
44   this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a
45   newspaper of general circulation.
