                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to adequate, safe, and affordable housing is a fundamental human right
enshrined in the Philippine Constitution. Yet, despite steady economic growth, a
significant segment of the population—particularly poor and low-income households—
continues to grapple with skyrocketing housing costs and a severe shortage of decent
shelter. Rapid urbanization has exacerbated this crisis, forcing millions into informal
settlements or substandard living conditions devoid of basic infrastructure and
services.

While government programs have historically prioritized homeownership, buying a
home remains an unattainable dream for marginalized families whose daily struggles
focus on basic survival. Recognizing rental housing as an essential component of a
holistic national housing strategy is long overdue. For millions of urban poor and low-
income citizens, a well-regulated, affordable, and decent rental housing market
provides a realistic, flexible, and sustainable path to secure shelter.

To bridge this monumental housing gap, the State must actively partner with the
private sector. Private enterprise possesses the capital, efficiency, and scalability
required to rapidly develop and manage residential housing units on a mass scale.
However, developers are frequently deterred by high construction costs, stringent
regulatory burdens, and narrow profit margins when targeting the low-income
segment.

This measure, the proposed "Affordable and Decent Rental Housing Incentives
Act", seeks to unlock private sector investment by providing a robust, targeted
package of fiscal and non-fiscal incentives. Specifically, the bill offers:

   •    Value-Added Tax (VAT) exemptions on the lease of residential units dedicated
        to qualified tenants.
   •    Applicable tax incentives under Republic Act No. 11534, otherwise known as
        the Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act.
   •    A 25% tax credit on gross rentals derived from qualified rental housing for a
        period of five years.
   •   Preferential interest rates, flexible repayment arrangements, and risk-sharing
       mechanisms from government financial institutions.
   •   Credits toward compliance with the Balanced Housing Development Program
       under Republic Act No. 7279.

Furthermore, to ensure comprehensive social protection and long-term program
integrity, this expanded measure incorporates critical safeguards:

   •   Rent Control and Price Escalation Caps: Mandates joint DHSUD and DTI
       guidelines to cap annual rent increases, preventing sudden, unaffordable price
       spikes for vulnerable households.
   •   Tenant Security of Tenure and Grievance Mechanisms: Protects qualified
       tenants from arbitrary eviction and establishes dedicated channels for resolving
       tenant-management disputes.
   •   Green Building and Climate Resilience Standards: Requires compliance with
       Republic Act No. 10866 (Philippine Green Building Code) and disaster-resilient
       building standards to guarantee structural safety against extreme weather
       events.
   •   LGU Streamlining and Support: Directs local government units to fast-track
       permits within a non-extendable period of thirty working days and encourages
       local tax relief for registered projects.
   •   Congressional Oversight: Institutes biennial impact evaluation reports
       submitted by the DHSUD to Congress to measure unit output, fiscal
       sustainability, and beneficiary outcomes.

By lowering the barriers to entry for developers and operators while embedding clear-
cut tenant safeguards and green standards, this Act ensures that the private sector is
strongly incentivized to build and maintain safe, dignified, and reasonably priced rental
communities. In turn, strict monitoring by the Department of Human Settlements and
Urban Development (DHSUD) guarantees that these housing options remain strictly
dedicated to underprivileged families throughout the mandated affordability period.

Passing this legislation is a crucial step toward dismantling urban poverty and
upholding the dignity of every Filipino worker and family through secure, resilient, and
accessible housing.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
PROMOTING ACCESS TO AFFORDABLE AND DECENT RENTAL HOUSING FOR
POOR AND LOW-INCOME HOUSEHOLDS THROUGH THE PROVISION OF
INCENTIVES FOR THE DEVELOPMENT, CONSTRUCTION, REHABILITATION,
AND OPERATION OF QUALIFIED RENTAL HOUSING, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Affordable and Decent
Rental Housing Incentives Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall, by law and for the common good,
undertake, in cooperation with the private sector, a continuing program of urban land
reform and housing which will make available at affordable cost decent housing and
basic services to underprivileged and homeless citizens in urban centers and
resettlement areas. The State shall likewise recognize the indispensable role of the
private sector, encourage private enterprise, and provide incentives to needed
investments.

To this end, the State shall recognize rental housing as an essential component of the
national housing strategy and foster an environment through appropriate and targeted
incentives for the private sector to develop, construct, rehabilitate, and operate
affordable and decent rental housing for poor and low-income households.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For the purposes of this Act:

   a) ‘Affordability Period’ refers to the time determined by the Department of Human
      Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) during which the developer,
      operator, or administrator of qualified rental housing shall comply with the
      conditions and requirements set forth in this Act or under agreements entered
      into pursuant thereto: Provided, That the affordability period shall commence
      upon the occupancy by the first qualified tenant after the registration and
      certification of the qualified rental housing by the DHSUD and shall in no case
      be shorter than ten (10) years.
   b) ‘Low-Income Household’ refers to one with an aggregate income higher than
        one hundred percent (100%) but not exceeding two hundred percent (200%) of
        the official poverty threshold as defined by the Department of Economy,
        Planning, and Development (DEPDev).
   c)   ‘Poor Household’ refers to one with an aggregate income not exceeding the
        official poverty threshold as defined by the DEPDev.
   d)   ‘Qualified Rental Housing’ refers to a residential project or a portion thereof
        offered for rent to qualified tenants in accordance with Section 4 of this Act.
   e)   ‘Qualified Tenant’ refers to a lessee who belongs to a poor or low-income
        household.
   f)   ‘Residential Unit’ refers to an apartment, house, or land on which another's
        dwelling is located and used for residential purposes, and shall include
        buildings, houses, dormitories, rooms, and bedspaces offered for rent by their
        owners, as well as those used for home industries, retail stores, or other similar
        business purposes if the owner thereof and his or her family actually live therein
        and use it principally for dwelling purposes. The term does not include motels,
        motel rooms, hotels, and hotel rooms.

SEC. 4. Affordable and Decent Rental Housing Incentives. - Natural and juridical
persons engaged or intending to engage in the development, construction,
rehabilitation, or operation of rental housing for qualified tenants, whether
independently or through a partnership with government agencies and
instrumentalities, may avail themselves of the incentives provided in this Act: Provided,
That such rental housing is registered with and certified by the DHSUD and complies
with the following requirements:

   1. Offer qualified rental housing to qualified tenants at rental rates and under such
      reasonable conditions as may be prescribed by the DHSUD, taking into
      consideration the income of the qualified tenant, the prevailing rental rates in
      the locality, the unit size and type, and other relevant factors.
   2. Comply with applicable laws, rules, and regulations governing residential
      leases and tenant ejectment.
   3. Maintain and submit complete and accurate records on rental rates, occupancy,
      and other relevant information as may be required by the DHSUD.
   4. Comply with such other conditions or requirements as may be prescribed under
      this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

SEC. 5. Incentives for Qualified Rental Housing. - Subject to compliance with the
conditions and requirements under this Act and other applicable laws and regulations,
the developers, operators, or administrators of qualified rental housing shall be entitled
to:

   a) Tax Incentives: Tax incentives for qualified rental housing units that are actually
        leased to and occupied by qualified tenants, as certified by the DHSUD, which
        shall include:
           o Exemption from Value-Added Tax on the lease of residential units;
           o Applicable tax incentives provided under Republic Act No. 11534 or the
                "Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises Act"; and
           o Tax credit equivalent to twenty-five percent (25%) of gross rentals
                derived from the lease of residential units for a period of five (5) years
                from the registration and certification of the qualified rental housing by
                the DHSUD: Provided, That such tax credit shall be creditable against
                the income tax liability of the qualified taxpayer, subject to the conditions
                and limitations provided under Republic Act No. 8424 or the "National
                Internal Revenue Code of 1997", as amended.
   b)   Preferential Financing: Preferential interest rates, extended repayment periods,
        reduced documentary and processing requirements, flexible repayment
        arrangements, and such other preferential lending terms from the Home
        Development Mutual Fund, Land Bank of the Philippines, Development Bank
        of the Philippines, and such other government financial institutions authorized
        under their respective charters to extend financing for the construction, repair,
        or rehabilitation of residential units to be used as qualified rental housing:
        Provided, That such financing programs shall take into consideration the
        lending policies and financial sustainability of the government financial
        institutions concerned.
   c)   Risk-Sharing Mechanisms: Risk-sharing mechanisms under existing
        government programs, including credit guarantees, interest subsidies, and
        viability gap funding.
   d)   Compliance Credits: Credits toward compliance with the requirements of the
        Balanced Housing Development Program under Section 18 of Republic Act No.
        7279 or the "Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992, as amended".
   e)   Program Participation: Participation in housing programs implemented by
        national government agencies and local government units (LGUs).
   f)   Institutional Support: Preferential access to qualified tenant referrals, technical
        assistance, and such other forms of support from the DHSUD, LGUs, and other
        government agencies concerned.
   g)   Additional Assistance: Such other non-fiscal incentives and assistance as may
        be identified by the DHSUD in coordination with other government agencies
        concerned: Provided, That a residential unit used as qualified rental housing
        shall not be entitled to the same or substantially similar fiscal incentives under
        other laws unless expressly allowed under such laws.

SEC. 6. Rental Housing Agreements. - Private entities may avail themselves of the
incentives provided under this Act through usufruct, lease, concession, public-private
partnership, or such other lawful contractual arrangements entered into with
government agencies and instrumentalities for the development, rehabilitation,
construction, or operation of qualified rental housing, subject to applicable laws, rules,
and regulations: Provided, That such agreements entered into by the private sector
entities and government agencies shall incorporate the requirements under Section 4
of this Act.

SEC. 7. Continued Compliance During the Affordability Period . - Compliance with
the requirements imposed under Section 4 of this Act shall be maintained throughout
the affordability period. Failure to comply with such requirements shall constitute
grounds for the suspension, cancellation, or revocation of incentives, the recovery of
improperly availed benefits, and the imposition of other sanctions under applicable
laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 8. Sale, Transfer, Assignment, Donation, or Conveyance of Qualified
Rental Housing. - The sale, transfer, assignment, donation, or conveyance of
qualified rental housing during the affordability period shall not relieve the owner
thereof of the obligations imposed under this Act: Provided, That the buyer, transferee,
assignee, donee, or recipient of the subject qualified rental housing shall assume the
obligations imposed under this Act as a condition of the sale, transfer, assignment,
donation, or conveyance: Provided, further, That where the buyer, transferee,
assignee, donee, or recipient does not assume such obligations, the incentives
granted in respect of the qualified rental housing shall be withdrawn and the incentives
improperly availed of may be recovered in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

SEC. 9. Monitoring and Enforcement. - The DHSUD, in coordination with national
government agencies and LGUs concerned, shall monitor and enforce compliance
with this Act: Provided, That the DHSUD may conduct inspections of qualified rental
housing with proper notice to ensure adherence to standards and occupancy
requirements.

SEC. 10. Rent Control and Price Escalation Caps. — The initial rental rates for
qualified rental housing shall be determined and approved by the Department of
Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), taking into account the
income profile of qualified tenants, prevailing local market rates, and unit
classifications. Throughout the affordability period, any annual adjustment or increase
in rental rates shall be strictly regulated and capped in accordance with guidelines
jointly promulgated by the DHSUD and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI):
Provided, That in no case shall rental increases exceed the maximum allowable
percentage prescribed under existing rent control laws or rules, preventing arbitrary
rate adjustments that compromise affordability for poor and low-income households.

SEC. 11. Tenant Security of Tenure and Grievance Mechanisms. — Qualified
tenants residing in qualified rental housing shall enjoy security of tenure and shall not
be dispossessed or evicted from the leased premises except for cause and upon final
order of a court of competent jurisdiction or authorized administrative body pursuant
to existing laws and rules on residential leases.

Every developer, operator, or administrator of qualified rental housing shall establish
an accessible operational grievance redress mechanism to receive, evaluate, and
resolve tenant complaints regarding unit maintenance, safety standards, and lease
enforcement. The DHSUD shall designate an operational unit tasked with monitoring
tenant welfare and mediating disputes arising from rental housing operations under
this Act.

SEC. 12. Green Building and Climate Resilience Standards. — All new
construction, development, or structural rehabilitation projects undertaken pursuant to
this Act shall comply with the mandatory provisions of Republic Act No. 10866,
otherwise known as the "Philippine Green Building Code," and such disaster-resilient
building standards as may be established by the DHSUD in consultation with the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Department of
Public Works and Highways (DPWH).

Compliance with structural integrity requirements against seismic hazards and
extreme weather events, alongside energy and water efficiency measures, shall serve
as a mandatory prerequisite for the registration and certification of qualified rental
housing by the DHSUD.

SEC. 13. Local Government Unit (LGU) Streamlining and Support. — Local
government units (LGUs) shall process, review, and act upon all applications for local
permits, licenses, clearances, and zoning certifications required for the development,
construction, rehabilitation, or operation of qualified rental housing within a non-
extendable period of thirty (30) working days from the date of submission of complete
requirements.

LGUs are hereby authorized and encouraged to grant local tax relief, including
exemptions from or reductions of Real Property Tax (RPT), and the waiver of local
regulatory fees for registered qualified rental housing projects located within their
territorial jurisdictions for the duration of the affordability period.

SEC. 14. Periodic Impact Evaluation and Congressional Oversight. — The
DHSUD, in coordination with the Department of Finance (DOF) and the Department
of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), shall submit a comprehensive
progress and impact evaluation report to both Houses of Congress every two (2) years
following the effectivity of this Act.

The report shall include an assessment of the aggregate number of qualified rental
units constructed and occupied, total fiscal and non-fiscal incentives granted,
beneficiary demographic profiles, and recommendations for statutory amendments
necessary to accomplish the objectives of this Act.

SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act, in the respective annual
appropriations ordinances of the LGUs concerned, and in the respective annual
corporate operating budgets of the government-owned or -controlled corporations
concerned.
SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. –Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DHSUD, in consultation with the Department of Finance,
Fiscal Incentives Review Board, Board of Investments, and other public and private
stakeholders concerned, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to
implement the provisions of this Act.
SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder hereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.
SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, presidential decrees, executive orders,
issuances, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts thereof,
inconsistent herewith are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
