                          EXPLANATORY NOTE



      The Philippine Normal University (PNU) occupies a unique and singular
position in the Philippine higher education system. It is the only institution
designated by law as the National Center for Teacher Education, and the only
public higher education institution in the country whose academic mandate is
exclusively devoted to teaching and allied education professions. Unlike
comprehensive universities with multiple disciplinary mandates, PNU’s sole and
enduring mission is the preparation, development, and advancement of teachers,
teacher educators, and education leaders across all levels and contexts of
Philippine education. As a multi-campus national university system, PNU carries
out this mandate through its flagship campus in Manila and its constituent
campuses in Isabela, Quezon, Negros Occidental, and Agusan del Sur, enabling
the University to respond to the diverse educational, cultural, linguistic,
environmental, and socio-economic realities of Philippine education across the
archipelago.

      Through this coordinated network of regional and thematic hubs, PNU
strengthens the country’s capacity for context-responsive teacher education,
research, innovation, extension, and policy support. This system-wide structure
places the University in a unique position to respond to the systemic challenges
facing Philippine education. Since the quality of education is fundamentally tied
to the strength and coherence of teacher education, persistent issues in teacher
preparation point to the need for coordinated, long-term, and nationally anchored
responses.

      The quality of Philippine education depends on the strength, coherence,
and sustainability of its teacher education system. National assessments and
policy reviews over time have consistently identified persistent challenges in
Philippine education, particularly in teacher preparation. These include uneven
quality of teacher education across institutions and regions, weak integration of
research into teaching practice, misalignment between pre-service education and
classroom realities, and limited institutional capacity to sustain innovation and
reform in teacher education over time. These challenges are systemic in nature
and require coordinated, long-term responses.

      Recent international large-scale assessments further highlight the urgency
of these reforms. The Philippines has consistently ranked among the lowest-
performing countries in major global and regional assessments, including the
Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), Trends in International
Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS), and Southeast Asia Primary Learning
Metrics (SEA-PLM). In the 2022 PISA, the Philippines ranked 77th out of 81
countries. In the 2019 TIMSS, it placed last among 58 participating countries in

                                                                                2
both mathematics and science. While modest gains were seen in the 2024 SEA-
PLM, the country still remained near the bottom among participating ASEAN
nations. These results point to continuing gaps in foundational learning and
highlight the important role of teacher quality in improving student performance
and overall education outcomes.

      In response, the State has implemented major reforms aimed at
strengthening the teaching profession and improving governance in teacher
education. Republic Act No. 9647 designated the Philippine Normal University
(PNU) as the National Center for Teacher Education (NCTE), while Republic Act
No. 11713 institutionalized the Teacher Education Council (TEC) to promote
policy coordination and coherence across education agencies. These reforms have
been reinforced by the Enhanced Basic Education Act, the adoption of
professional standards for teachers and school leaders, licensure and regulatory
systems administered by the Professional Regulation Commission, and quality
assurance mechanisms under the Commission on Higher Education. Together,
these measures have strengthened the regulatory, standards-setting, and
accountability frameworks that govern teacher education and professional
development in the country.

      While these reforms have established a more coherent policy and
regulatory environment, they have also revealed a critical institutional gap.
Existing laws primarily address coordination, regulation, standards, and
oversight, but they do not provide a dedicated national academic institution with
the mandate, authority, and continuity to support sustained system-wide
improvement in teacher education. In particular, the NCTE framework under
Republic Act No. 9647 positions PNU primarily as a coordinating and advisory
body and does not sufficiently equip it to undertake long-term research
engagement, reform piloting, professional learning, and institutional capacity

                                                                                3
development at scale in partnership with Teacher Education Institutions
nationwide.

      To address these gaps, this measure seeks to revise the Charter of the
Philippine Normal University and formally designate it as the National University
for Teacher Education (NUTE). The proposed charter revision preserves the
University’s institutional continuity while strengthening its legal, academic, and
organizational foundations to carry out an expanded national mandate in teacher
education and education sciences. More importantly, the designation of PNU as
a national university represents a substantive institutional reform. It aims to
strengthen the State’s long-term capacity to lead, coordinate, and sustain teacher
education reforms across administrations, regions, and diverse educational
contexts. Through this measure, PNU is positioned not only as a center of
excellence, but also as a national institution with the mandate, authority, and
capacity to advance innovation, research, policy development, and standards-
setting for the continuous improvement of teacher education in the Philippines.

      As the country’s only institution exclusively mandated for teacher
education, the Philippine Normal University (PNU) operates within a field that is
broad, complex, and multidisciplinary. Teacher education supports the country’s
basic education system by preparing teachers across different subject areas,
learner needs, and socio-cultural contexts. National assessments, including
findings from the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II),
have identified continuing shortages and uneven teacher preparation in key areas
such as mathematics, science, technology, technical-vocational and livelihood
education, values education, special education, inclusive education, and other
emerging fields.




                                                                                  4
      Alongside strengthening academic and professional preparation, teacher
education must continue to uphold values formation as a central component of
the profession. Teachers are expected not only to demonstrate competence in
instruction and content knowledge, but also to embody ethical leadership, social
responsibility, and commitment to nation-building, consistent with nationally
recognized professional standards for teachers and education leaders.

      Given the Philippine context as an archipelagic and disaster-prone nation,
teacher education must prepare educators not only in pedagogy and content
knowledge, but also in resilience, emergency preparedness, and responsible
technology use. Teachers must be equipped with practical competencies such as
basic life support, first aid, water safety, survival skills, disaster response, and
financial literacy to help safeguard learners and respond effectively during
emergencies and community disruptions. At the same time, while artificial
intelligence and digital technologies offer important opportunities for innovation
in teaching and learning, their integration must remain balanced and purposeful.
Teacher education must continue to strengthen foundational literacy, reading
comprehension, critical thinking, and writing skills, ensuring that technology
supports rather than replaces human judgment, creativity, and intellectual
discipline.

      Addressing persistent gaps in basic education requires a steady and
sustained supply of teachers with strong disciplinary preparation and professional
competence across a wide range of specializations. This demand calls for a
teacher education system anchored by a national institution with the capacity,
disciplinary breadth, and long-term mandate to respond to both existing shortages
and emerging educational priorities. As a core responsibility of the State, teacher
education requires sustained institutional focus, strategic investment, and
coordinated national leadership. Unlike comprehensive universities that

                                                                                   5
distribute resources and attention across multiple academic fields, a dedicated
National University for Teacher Education ensures that teacher preparation,
teacher development, and education sciences remain central institutional
priorities.

       The Philippine Normal University is uniquely positioned to fulfill this role.
PNU operates through a system of specialized campuses that collectively provide
one of the broadest ranges of teacher education expertise in the country. Each
campus functions as a regional and thematic hub responsive to local development
needs and national education priorities, forming an integrated network of
complementary strengths. Declaring PNU as the National University for Teacher
Education will strengthen its capacity to lead national reforms, expand
specialization areas, support and partner with other Teacher Education
Institutions, and contribute to a more adequate, balanced, and future-ready supply
of competent teachers across disciplines and regions.

       Institutionally, PNU’s quality and maturity are well established. It is a SUC
Level III university, ISO 9001:2015 certified, and a Philippine Quality Award
(PQA) recipient. It maintains 110 Level IV accredited programs and is recognized
by CHED and the Teacher Education Council as a Center of Excellence in
Teacher Education. Since 2009, PNU has produced 149 topnotchers in the
Licensure Examination for Teachers, demonstrating sustained academic
excellence.

       Internationally, PNU continues to build a strong reputation for innovation
and social responsibility, including recognition in global rankings and leadership
in regional teacher education networks.




                                                                                   6
      Declaring the Philippine Normal University as the National University for
Teacher Education (NUTE) is not merely an institutional recognition but a
strategic national reform. As NUTE, PNU will:

   a. lead national research and innovation in teacher education and lifelong
      learning;
   b. strengthen academic capacity and quality assurance across Teacher
      Education Institutions;
   c. provide policy advice and technical expertise to national education bodies;
   d. expand equitable access to high-quality teacher education, particularly for
      underserved learners; and
   e. enhance the resilience, inclusivity, and global competitiveness of the
      teaching profession.

   To support these objectives, the proposed measure introduces key structural
and policy reforms, including a mandatory collaboration framework with
education agencies, the establishment of laboratory schools for clinical practice,
strengthened faculty development mechanisms, integration of values formation
and life skills in teacher education, and a balanced framework for the use of
artificial intelligence in teaching and learning. The proposed measure likewise
recognizes the importance of participatory and transparent governance. The
proposed measure likewise promotes participatory, transparent, and sector-
responsive governance mechanisms within the University, consistent with its
national mandate and public character.

   This measure aligns with the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028,
AmBisyon Natin 2040, and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4
on Quality Education. It affirms the country’s commitment to ensuring that every



                                                                                 7
Filipino learner is taught by a competent, ethical, resilient, and future-ready
teacher.

   As it marks its 125th year of service and 15th year as the National Center for
Teacher Education, the Philippine Normal University stands ready to fulfill this
expanded mandate as the National University for Teacher Education (NUTE),
leading systemic transformation in teacher education and advancing excellence,
inclusion, and innovation in Philippine education.



      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                                   ARTICLE I
                        PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Revised Philippine
Normal University Charter Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote inclusive access to, and equity in, high-quality teacher education and
professional development. The State shall ensure holistic, transformative,
innovative, collaborative, and research-based teacher education that enhances the
quality of basic and higher education, thereby developing knowledgeable, skilled,
and well-rounded learners and citizen-leaders equipped with critical thinking, strong
literacy, and values formation grounded in moral, civic, ethical, and cultural
foundations, as well as resilience and responsibility in diverse and changing
contexts, and values grounded in national identity and global citizenship.

      The State shall institutionalize innovations and alternative systems in
teacher education covering pedagogy, curricula, instruction, assessment, training,
research, and the development and transfer of educational innovations and related
technologies. These initiatives shall be aligned with international standards and
best practices, supportive of national development goals, and responsive to the
current and emerging needs of education stakeholders. Such innovations shall
include strengthened integration of science, technology, and mathematics
education; values education; inclusive education; health and well-being
education; school community well-being; and life skills such as financial literacy,
health and safety, disaster preparedness, basic life support, first aid, water safety,
survival skills, and emergency response.

      Furthermore, the State shall advance teacher education consistent with
national and global standards and practices, particularly those that:

   1. address inequalities in learning outcomes;

   2. strengthen professional environments;

   3. promote lifelong learning; and

   4. adapt to technological advancements including artificial intelligence, while
      ensuring resilience amid socio-economic, political, and ecological
      challenges and maintaining strong foundations in reading, writing,
      comprehension, and critical thinking through a balanced integration of
      digital technologies and traditional pedagogies that safeguard independent

                                                                                     2
       thinking, creativity, and intellectual discipline.

       The State likewise recognizes the Philippine context as an archipelagic and
disaster-prone nation, and affirms that teacher education must prepare educators
and learners with the necessary life skills, resilience, and practical competencies
to respond to emergencies,           environmental challenges,    and economic
vulnerabilities, thereby promoting safety, well-being, and long-term societal
resilience.

       To this end, the Philippine Normal University (PNU) is hereby declared as
the National University for Teacher Education (NUTE). As NUTE, PNU shall
serve as the nation’s academic, research, and leadership institution for teacher
education, guiding the continuous improvement of the country’s teacher
education system through innovation, scholarship, and capacity development in
partnership with Teacher Education Institutions and relevant stakeholders
nationwide. Consistent with its statutory role under Republic Act No. 11713,
PNU shall continue to advise the Teacher Education Council (TEC) by providing
timely, evidence-based policy guidance, research, and academic expertise to
strengthen decision-making and reforms in teacher education and to support
coordinated, system-wide improvements in teacher quality and educational
outcomes.

SEC. 3. Seat of Administration and Seal of the University. — The seat of
administration of the Philippine Normal University System shall be located in its
main campus in the City of Manila.

There shall be one (1) common Seal of the Philippine Normal University, to be
known as the PNU Seal, composed of the following symbols and their corresponding
meaning:


                                                                                  3
   a. The Torch, symbolizing enlightenment and the guiding light of education that
      nurtures generations of learners;

   b. The Open Book, representing the accessibility of knowledge and the
      continuous pursuit of wisdom; and

   c. The Laurel Leaves, signifying victory, honor, and achievement in the noble
      mission of teaching.

These symbols are interwoven with the University’s enduring values of Truth,
Excellence, and Service, as it continues to uphold its national mandate to produce
and nurture future-ready teachers and education leaders.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms – The following terms are adopted from Republic Act
No. 11713, unless otherwise defined in this Act:

   a. “Artificial intelligence literacy” refers to the ability to understand, use,
      evaluate, and critically engage with artificial intelligence technologies,
      including awareness of their limitations, ethical implications, data privacy
      risks, and potential effects on cognition and foundational skills, distinct
      from mere technical proficiency;

   b. “Clinical practice” or “laboratory school” refers to a structured teaching
      practicum wherein pre-service teachers deliver instruction under the direct
      supervision of experienced master teachers within a school setting that is
      operationally connected to a teacher education institution, with real-time
      feedback, research integration, and opportunities for pedagogical
      experimentation;

   c. “Emergency preparedness” refers to the knowledge, skills, and systems
      required to anticipate, respond to, and recover from natural disasters and
      emergencies, including typhoons, floods, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions,
                                                                                 4
   fires, and other calamities common in the Philippine setting;

d. “Financial literacy” refers to the ability to understand and apply financial
   concepts such as budgeting, saving, investing, borrowing, and risk
   management, and to make informed, effective, and responsible decisions
   to achieve personal financial well-being, including the capacity to
   recognize and avoid fraudulent schemes;

e. “Foundational literacy” refers to the essential competencies in reading
   comprehension, analytical writing, critical thinking, and numeracy that
   serve as the basis for all further learning, distinct from digital or
   technological proficiency;

f. “Life skills” refer to practical competencies necessary for daily living,
   safety, and well-being, including but not limited to water safety and
   survival swimming, basic life support, first aid, disaster response,
   emergency evacuation, and health and safety practices;

g. “National intake mechanism” refers to the system established by the
   Philippine Normal University to ensure equitable access to its
   undergraduate and graduate programs for students from all regions and
   provinces of the country, prioritizing academic preparedness, aptitude for
   teaching, commitment to public service, and regional representation, and
   including provisions for financial assistance and service obligations;

h. “National university for teacher education” refers to the Philippine Normal
   University in its capacity as the State's designated academic, research, and
   leadership institution for teacher education, with the mandate to guide
   national   improvement of teacher        education    through innovation,
   scholarship, policy advice, and capacity development;

i. “Pre-service teacher education programs” refer to undergraduate degrees
   and certificates of teaching covering mandatory curriculum and practicum
   specifications designed to ensure strong foundations in pedagogy, subject
   matter expertise, assessment, supervised clinical practice, and alignment
   with national and international standards;

j. “Pre-service teachers” refer to students enrolled in a teacher education
   degree program offered by Teacher Education Institutions (TEIs) who are
   undergoing academic and clinical preparation aligned with professional
   standards for beginning teachers;

k. “Resilience” refers to the ability of individuals, communities, or systems

l. “State scholar” refers to a student granted scholarship support funded by

m. “Teacher” refers to a person qualified to practice teaching under the law

   competence in content knowledge, pedagogy, assessment, use of

   technology, and commitment to lifelong professional development;

n. “Teacher Education” refers to formal pre-service and in-service education
   and training, including graduate education, in various areas of
   specialization undertaken by pre-service teachers and teachers which
   integrates research-based pedagogy, inclusive education, values formation,
   life skills, resilience, and the responsible and balanced use of emerging
   technologies;

o. “Teacher Education Institutions (TEIs)” refer to higher education

   adaptability to diverse and evolving learning environments;

q. “Traditional pedagogy” refers to established teaching methodologies that
   have demonstrated effectiveness in developing foundational literacy,
   critical thinking, and writing skills, including but not limited to explicit
   instruction, close reading, handwritten composition, classroom discussion

                                  ARTICLE II
        MANDATE, ROLE, AND FUNCTIONS OF THE NATIONAL
                UNIVERSITY FOR TEACHER EDUCATION

SEC. 5. Role of PNU. — As NUTE, PNU shall serve as the State’s academic,
research, and leadership institution for teacher education, guiding the continuous
improvement of the teacher education system through innovation, scholarship, and
capacity development.

Nature and Scope of the Mandate. — The exercise of PNU’s mandate as the
NUTE shall be academic, research-based, developmental, and model-setting in
nature. In carrying out this mandate, PNU shall develop academic standards,
research-based benchmarks, and model frameworks; lead the formulation and
piloting of innovations and reforms; and provide policy advice and technical
expertise, all without prejudice to and subject to the statutory regulatory,
accreditation, and licensure powers of the CHED, TEC, the Professional
Regulation Commission (PRC), the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), and other relevant government agencies under existing laws.

           i.   Provide evidence-based policy advice, research-informed
                recommendations, and technical support to the Department of
                Education (DepEd), CHED, TEC, PRC, TESDA, and other
                relevant agencies for teacher education policy, innovation, and

                                                                              8
       system improvement; and serve as the principal academic
       adviser to the TEC in the formulation and implementation of
       teacher education policies and programs.
 ii.   Lead in the formulation, piloting, and scaling of national reforms
       in teacher education, training, and curriculum innovation.
       Integrate artificial intelligence (AI), digital tools, and adaptive
       technologies      in     teacher     preparation    and       professional
       development to ensure inclusion of underserved and remote
       regions. Collaborate with national agencies and TEIs in co-
       designing research-based, inclusive, and future-ready curricula.
iii.   Serve as a research-intensive university advancing teacher
       education and education sciences, emphasizing teacher quality,
       pre-service and graduate education, lifelong learning, and
       continuing professional development for teachers and education
       leaders.
iv.    Set standards and benchmarks for transformative, inclusive,
       innovative, and sustainable teacher education and education
       sciences across undergraduate, post-baccalaureate, and graduate
       levels.
 v.    Serve as the international hub for collaborative teacher
       education,      global    mobility,     and    multilateral     academic
       partnerships,     promoting        international   benchmarking       and
       alignment with global and regional standards, particularly within
       ASEAN and the Asia-Pacific, in accordance with the relevant
       rules and guidelines on international linkages.
vi.    Lead the creation, utilization, and dissemination of research with
       policy implications to teacher education and education sciences
       through its national research units, the Research Institute for

                                                                                9
                 Teacher Quality (RITQ), the Institute for Educational Policy
                 Research and Development (IEPRD), the Research Institute for
                 Lifelong Learning (RILL), and the Research Institute for
                 Teacher Welfare and Well-Being (RITWW), and other similar
                 units as may be approved by the Board of Regents (BOR).
          vii.   Lead the development and implementation of research-based
                 and alternative pedagogies and assessment models in teacher
                 education and lifelong learning. The University may establish,
                 upon BOR approval, specialized centers to advance lifelong
                 learning and continuing education.
         viii.   Upon request of Congress or its committees, provide technical
                 assistance and evidence-based inputs in the formulation, review,
                 or evaluation of legislative measures concerning teacher
                 education,     teacher      training,    continuing    professional
                 development, and lifelong learning.
           ix.   Serve as the lead academic partner of the Department of
                 Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev), Commission
                 on Higher Education (CHED), Department of Education
                 (DepEd),     Professional     Regulation     Commission     (PRC),
                 Technical Education         and Skills     Development Authority
                 (TESDA), Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and
                 other national agencies.

Balanced Integration of Artificial Intelligence and Traditional Pedagogy. — In
pursuing the integration of artificial intelligence (AI), digital tools, and adaptive
technologies in teacher education, the University shall adopt a balanced
framework that safeguards foundational literacy, critical thinking, and intellectual
independence. The University shall ensure that the use of AI enhances, and does
not replace, human cognition, creativity, and professional judgment.
                                                                                  10
In furtherance of this framework, the University shall:

   a. Ensure that pre-service teachers demonstrate proficiency in foundational
      skills, including reading comprehension, analytical writing, critical
      thinking, and problem-solving without reliance on AI tools, prior to and
      alongside the integration of such technologies;

   b. Develop and implement pedagogical guidelines that clearly distinguish AI
      as a tool for learning support and augmentation, and not as a substitute for
      cognitive effort, emphasizing that AI shall complement but not replace
      human reasoning, reflection, and authorship;

   c. Require coursework on AI literacy for all pre-service teachers, including
      ethical and responsible use, data privacy, misinformation and bias,
      verification of AI-generated outputs, and the cognitive implications of AI
      dependency, consistent with national frameworks on responsible AI use;

   d. Mandate that teacher education programs include explicit instruction and
      practice in traditional and evidence-based pedagogical approaches,
      including close reading, writing without AI assistance, classroom
      discourse, and other methods that strengthen comprehension, memory, and
      higher-order thinking skills;

   e. Establish and support research initiatives within the Research Institute for
      Lifelong Learning (RILL) and other relevant research units to study the
      impact of AI and digital technologies on teacher cognition, student learning
      outcomes, and foundational skills, and to generate evidence-based
      recommendations for policy and practice. Such research shall include the
      study of cognitive, pedagogical, and developmental impacts of artificial
      intelligence on learners and teachers, and shall inform national policy and
      curriculum standards. And;
                                                                               11
      f. Collaborate with the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission
        on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
        Development Authority (TESDA), and the Teacher Education Council
        (TEC) in the development of national guidelines on the balanced and
        responsible integration of AI in teacher education and learning
        environments.

Mandatory Collaboration Framework for Addressing Education Sector
Challenges. — To ensure that the University’s mandate translates into
measurable improvements in the basic education, higher education, and technical-
vocational education and training sectors, the University shall establish and
maintain a structured collaboration framework with the Department of Education,
the Commission on Higher Education, the Teacher Education Council, the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, and other relevant
agencies. Under this framework, the following mechanisms shall be
implemented:

 i.     Annual Education Sector Work Plan. — The University, in consultation
        with the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education,
        and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, shall
        prepare and submit to the Teacher Education Council an annual education
        sector work plan identifying priority challenges in teacher education and
        basic education, including but not limited to learning outcomes in
        international assessments, teacher shortages in critical subject areas,
        technical-vocational education, and disaster-related education disruptions.
        The work plan shall outline the University’s proposed research, policy
        recommendations,      technical    assistance,   and     capacity-building
        interventions to address these concerns and shall be made publicly
        available.

                                                                                12
 ii.   Regular Policy Advisory with Formal Response Requirement. — The
       University shall issue a formal policy advisory to the Teacher Education
       Council at least once every two years, drawing from its research institutes.
       The University may issue advisories more frequently as it deems necessary
       or upon request of the Teacher Education Council. The Teacher Education
       Council shall respond in writing within sixty days of receipt of an advisory,
       indicating which recommendations have been adopted, which are under
       consideration, and the reasons for any non-adoption. Advisories pertaining
       to technical-vocational education shall be shared with the Technical
       Education and Skills Development Authority for its information and
       appropriate action.
iii.   Education Sector Crisis Response Protocol. — The University, in
       coordination with the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher
       Education, and the Technical Education and Skills Development
       Authority, shall develop a crisis response protocol that enables the rapid
       mobilization of the University’s research, training, and technical expertise
       in response to urgent education challenges, including but not limited to
       learning recovery following natural disasters, public health emergencies,
       or identified declines in national or international learning assessments.
iv.    Longitudinal Impact Assessment. — The University shall establish a
       comprehensive Longitudinal Teacher Impact and Tracer System to
       systematically track the performance of its graduates in the classroom
       including, where feasible, the learning outcomes of the students taught by
       PNU-trained teachers and, for technical-vocational graduates, the
       performance outcomes as may be relevant to TESDA competency
       standards. Findings shall be disaggregated by campus, program, and
       region, and shall be used to inform the continuous improvement of the
       University’s teacher education programs. A report on these findings shall

                                                                                   13
         be submitted to the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher
         Education, the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority,
         and Congress at least once every three years. This system shall function as
         the University’s official tracer study mechanism and feedback loop for
         continuous program improvement and national policy development.
 v.      Collaboration Report to Congress. — The University shall submit to
         Congress a report detailing its collaborative activities under this section,
         including the status of the Annual Education Sector Work Plan, policy
         advisories issued and corresponding responses, crisis response activations,
         and findings of the longitudinal impact assessment. The report shall
         include a specific section on collaboration with TESDA regarding
         technical-vocational   teacher education and identify any resource
         constraints or legislative barriers affecting effective collaboration.
vi.      Non-Duplication with Existing Mechanisms. — The mechanisms
         established under this section shall complement, and not duplicate, existing
         coordination mechanisms under Republic Act No. 11713, Republic Act
         No. 7796, and other relevant laws. The University shall coordinate with the
         Teacher Education Council, TESDA, and other agencies to ensure

Mandatory Pedagogical Priorities. — In line with its mandate to lead national

SEC. 30. The students. — All officially admitted undergraduate and graduate
students of the Philippine Normal University (PNU), as the National University for
Teacher Education (NUTE), shall be recognized as part of a national community of
teacher scholars committed to academic excellence, professional competence, and
service to the nation.

      For purposes of this Act, a “State Scholar” refers to a student who is granted
scholarship support funded by the State, covering tuition and other authorized
educational expenses, subject to qualifications, standards of merit, and financial
need as may be prescribed by the University and relevant government policies.

      Students of the University may be granted State Scholar status or other forms
of financial assistance based on merit, need, and academic performance, in
accordance with rules approved by the Board of Regents and consistent with national
policies on public scholarships.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as automatically conferring State
Scholar status upon all admitted students without compliance with prescribed
qualifications and requirements.

National Intake. — To ensure equitable access to high-quality teacher education and
to strengthen the national supply of teachers, the Philippine Normal University, as
the National University for Teacher Education, shall establish a National Intake
Mechanism for its undergraduate and graduate programs.

      The mechanism shall initially prioritize the admission of National Teacher
Education Scholars from different regions and provinces of the country, with the
                                                                               49
long-term goal of expanding to cover municipalities and cities as resources permit,
based on academic preparedness, aptitude for teaching, commitment to public
service, regional representation, and other criteria as may be determined by the
University through policies approved by the Board of Regents. Local governments
and other relevant agencies may recommend and sponsor high-performing students
for admission under this program, subject to policies established by the University.
The National Intake Mechanism shall promote equitable access across regions while
upholding rigorous academic and professional standards consistent with the
University’s mandate as the National University for Teacher Education.

In the implementation of the National Intake Mechanism, PNU shall:

   a. Set clear entry qualifications and a strict retention policy to ensure the quality
      and readiness of scholars;

   b. Require an agreement between the University, the sponsoring local
      government or agency, and the student upon enrollment, providing that the
      scholar shall render service in the education system, preferably in the region
      of the sponsoring local government, consistent with national teacher demand;

   c. Develop and administer rigorous assessments to select students who will be
      admitted as pre-service teachers under the program;

   d. Provide financial assistance and support programs to National Teacher
      Education Scholars, including tuition exemption, stipends, and allowances,
      with the aim of ensuring a high graduation and completion rate across all
      campuses of the University.

   National Teacher Education Scholars shall be subject to a service obligation,
requiring them to serve in the Philippine education system for a period to be
determined by the Governing Board, consistent with existing laws and policies. The

                                                                                  50
service obligation shall be designed to address national and regional teacher
shortages, particularly in underserved and high-need areas, subject to existing
deployment and civil service policies. Scholars may enroll in any campus of the
Philippine Normal University System depending on their chosen program of study,
specialization, or regional preference. The service obligation under this Section shall
be implemented in accordance with existing laws, civil service rules, and
deployment policies of the Department of Education.             The National Intake
Mechanism shall be implemented progressively, subject to the absorptive capacity
of the University and the availability of resources, as may be determined by the
Board of Regents.

Teacher Education Financial Assistance and Privileges for Students. — To
strengthen the National Intake Mechanism, local government units (LGUs),
government agencies, and partner institutions may provide financial sponsorships or
contributions for National Teacher Education Scholars. Such sponsorships may be
sourced from their general funds or other authorized sources, and shall be remitted
to the PNU National Teacher Education Endowment Fund or directly to the
University in accordance with guidelines issued by the Governing Board. The
implementation of service obligations and deployment preferences shall be
coordinated with the Department of Education and other relevant agencies,
consistent with existing deployment and hiring policies.

Sponsored scholars shall be subject to a tripartite agreement among the University,
the sponsoring LGU or agency, and the student. The agreement shall specify the
terms of financial assistance and the scholar’s service obligation, which shall include
preferential deployment in the sponsoring LGU or agency, consistent with national
teacher demand and existing laws and policies.

          FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT, TAX EXEMPTIONS, AND
                                SUSTAINABILITY

SEC. 35. Financial Management and Sustainability. —                There shall be an
independent trust committee to be composed of the President of the University, as
Chairperson and with representatives from the Bankers Association of the
Philippines (BAP), Investment Houses Association of the Philippines (IHAP), Trust
Officers Association of the Philippines (TOAP), Financial Executive Institute of the
Philippines (FINEX) as representatives, and who shall be entitled to a reasonable per
diem, as the Board may specify.

      The independent trust committee shall recommend to the board five (5)
universal banks selected on a provident basis. Trust departments shall manage the
corporate and other funds through trust agreements of the Philippine Normal
University on a non-directed basis: Provided, that any such agreement shall be for a
period of not more than two (2) years.

      The independent trust committee shall provide the Board with direction on the
appropriate investments with the view of preserving the value of funds while
allowing the PNU to earn a reasonable return thereon.

      To support NUTE's expanded functions, PNU shall adopt a workload
differentiation scheme, institutionalize research incentives, and decentralize
governance by expanding campus and stakeholder participation. Investments in
infrastructure and digital platforms shall be prioritized in the IRR.

SEC. 36. Tax Exemptions. — The provisions of any general or special law to the
contrary notwithstanding:



                                                                               59
   a) All revenues and assets of the Philippine Normal University for educational
      purposes or in support thereof shall be exempt from all taxes and duties;

   b) Gifts and donations of real and personal properties of all kinds shall be exempt
      from the donor's tax and the same shall be considered as allowable deductions
      from the gross income of the donor, in accordance with the provisions of the
      National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended: Provided, That the
      allowable deductions shall be equivalent to 150 percent of the value of such
      donation. Valuation of assistance other than money shall be based on the
      acquisition cost of the property. Such valuation shall take into consideration
      the depreciated value of property in case said property has been used;

   c) Importation of economic, technical, vocational, scientific, philosophical,
      historical and cultural books, supplies and materials duly certified by the
      Board, including scientific and educational computers and equipment, shall
      be exempt from customs duties;

   d) The University shall only pay 0% value-added tax for all transactions subject
      to this tax; and

   e) All institutional awards or incentives shall be exempt from all taxes.

SEC. 37. Appropriations. — In addition to the regular appropriations for the
Philippine Normal University as a state university under the General Appropriations
Act, there shall be a distinct and separate line-item appropriation for its mandates as
the National University for Teacher Education.

      The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act as NUTE shall
be specifically appropriated to improve and expand capital infrastructure, including
the construction of new buildings and acquisition of land; establish and operate
national and regional research and development centers on teacher education; create

                                                                                 60
additional plantilla positions required to implement its expanded mandates;
operationalize comprehensive student support systems; and provide full scholarships
with appropriate financial, academic, and welfare support to all qualified
undergraduate and graduate students of the University as the State’s scholars for
teacher education, ensuring that no student shall be hindered by economic or social
barriers in completing their programs.

      For the initial year of the implementation of this Act, the sum of One billion
pesos (₱1,000,000,000.00) shall be appropriated as a lump sum for the NUTE
functions of PNU, in addition to its regular appropriations. Thereafter, an annual
appropriation shall be included in the General Appropriations Act under a separate
line item for NUTE, at no less than One Hundred Million pesos (₱100,000,000.00),
subject to automatic adjustments consistent with the annual government-approved
increases in higher education appropriations. Such appropriations shall support the
system-wide functions of the Philippine Normal University across its constituent
campuses and regional and thematic hubs, including infrastructure modernization,
laboratory schools, research and innovation systems, digital and flexible learning
infrastructure, faculty and research development, student support mechanisms, and
inter-campus coordination necessary to fulfill its mandate as the National University
for Teacher Education.

SEC. 38. PNU–NUTE Endowment Fund. — There is hereby established a
Philippine Normal University – National University for Teacher Education
Endowment Fund, hereinafter referred to as the PNU - NUTE Endowment Fund, to
strengthen the financial base of the University. For the purpose of constituting the
Fund, the amount of One billion pesos

      (₱1,000,000,000.00) hereby appropriated and shall be released to the
University upon the effectiveness of this Act.

      The Fund shall thereafter be augmented by proceeds from the development,
lease, or use of real properties and land grants of the University, consistent with
existing laws; gifts, donations, grants, and benefactions; and income derived from
other authorized sources.

      The principal of the Endowment Fund shall not be diminished and shall be
invested by the Board of Regents through a trust mechanism. Only the income,
interest, or profits thereof shall be used, unless otherwise authorized by the Board

      The income from the Endowment Fund shall be used exclusively to support

Teacher Education, including the strengthening of its constituent campuses and

comprehensive support for undergraduate and graduate students as the State’s
scholars.

SEC. 39. Authority to Retain and Utilize Income. — In addition to its regular
appropriations under the General Appropriations Act, the Philippine Normal
University, through its College for Flexible Learning and Multiple Pathways and
other income-generating units, is hereby authorized to retain and utilize all fees,
charges, and income derived from tuition, international student admissions, training
programs, certification services, research partnerships, technology transfer, and
other entrepreneurial activities consistent with its mandate as the National University
for Teacher Education.

      Such income shall be deposited in an authorized government depository bank
and shall constitute a Special Trust Fund, to be administered by the University under
the accountability of the Board of Regents. The Special Trust Fund shall be used
                                                                                 62
exclusively to support the academic, research, faculty development, technological
infrastructure, and student support programs of the University, with priority given
to the sustainability of the College for Flexible Learning and Multiple Pathways.

      The University is further authorized to use this Special Trust Fund to support
work arrangements and competitive compensation schemes for contractual, adjunct,
and visiting faculty, including international hires, subject to guidelines approved by
the Board of Regents and existing auditing and accounting rules and regulations.

      A portion of the revenues of the College for Flexible Learning and Multiple
Pathways, as determined by the Board of Regents, shall be earmarked for the
University’s Internationalization Program, which shall include but not be limited to:

   a. Scholarships and financial assistance for qualified international students
      enrolled in the University;

   b. Support for PNU faculty participation in international training, exchanges,
      and graduate or postgraduate studies abroad;

   c. Joint research projects and academic collaborations with international partner
      institutions; and

   d. Other programs that enhance the global visibility, competitiveness, and
      engagement of the University.

      The University shall submit an annual report to Congress, the Commission on
Higher Education, and the Commission on Audit on the collection, allocation, and
utilization of said income to ensure transparency and accountability.

                                   ARTICLE X
       IMPLEMENTING, TRANSITORY, AND FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days after
the effectiveness of this Act, the Board, in consultation with the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and
other relevant oversight agencies, shall formulate the rules and regulations to fully
implement the provisions of this Act. The implementing rules and regulations shall
include the following:

   a) Provision for the Philippine Normal University (PNU) modernization
      program, including its corresponding program budget to CHED and DBM;

   b) Provision for additional plantilla positions, including teaching faculty,
      research faculty, technical, support-to-technical, and key administrative
      positions, for the existing constituent campuses — PNU Manila, PNU South
      Luzon, PNU North Luzon, PNU Visayas, and PNU Mindanao — together
      with their respective extension campuses, and the creation of dedicated
      research faculty items necessary for the effective operation of the University’s
      research institutes and research hubs, with corresponding augmentation of
      personnel services allocation from DBM, in order to enhance the efficiency
      and effectiveness of the administration, research, and management of the
      University, towards the attainment of its mandate as the national teacher
      education university;

   c) Provision that the funding requirements for the establishment, operation, and
      sustainability of the research institutes, research hubs, and related research
      coordination mechanisms created under this Act shall be explicitly reflected
      in the annual budget of the Philippine Normal University, subject to existing
      budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules and regulations; and

                                                                                64
   d) The implementing rules and regulations shall outline the engagement of
       stakeholders such as CHED, the Department of Education (DepEd), the
       Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Technical Education and
       Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and DBM, as well as regional
       councils, through Technical Working Groups, with PNU advocating for
       shared governance, grounded legal frameworks, and legislative alignment
       with existing education laws.

   e) The establishment of a clear, transparent, and consultative mechanism for the
       selection of private sector representatives to the Board of Regents, with
       participation from relevant stakeholders, including students and sectoral
       representatives.

SEC. 41. Transitory Provision. — The incumbent President shall serve as the first
President under this Act during the transition period with a term of four years, which
is the same term proposed for the PNU President.

SEC. 42. Separability Clause. — Should any provision herein be declared
unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity of the other provisions of this
Act.

SEC. 43. Repealing Clause. — All laws, including RA 7168 and RA 9647, decrees,
orders, resolutions, and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are inconsistent
with this Act are hereby deemed repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 44. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

SEC. 45. Independent External Evaluation. — The Philippine Normal University,
as the National University for Teacher Education (NUTE), shall undergo an
independent external evaluation at least once every five (5) years.
                                                                                  65
      The evaluation shall assess the University’s performance in fulfilling its
national mandate, including teacher education quality, research impact, policy
influence, collaboration effectiveness, and contribution to national learning
outcomes.

      The evaluation shall be conducted by an independent panel composed of
recognized experts in teacher education, educational policy, and institutional
governance, which may include international experts to ensure global
benchmarking.

      The results of the evaluation shall be submitted to Congress, the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and the Teacher Education Council
(TEC), and shall be made publicly accessible.

      The University shall utilize the findings to inform institutional reforms,
strategic planning, and continuous improvement of teacher education programs.
