                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

In medical emergencies, having fast access to an ambulance or emergency transport
can save lives. Patients who are seriously ill or injured often need to be brought
immediately from their homes, accident sites, or far-flung communities to the nearest
hospital. Any delay can lead to death or permanent injury.

However, in reality, ambulance and emergency transport services are often
expensive. Many Filipino families are asked to pay large amounts of money before or
during an emergency. Because of this, some patients are delayed or unable to get
proper medical care when they need it most.

The 1987 Constitution clearly states that the government must make health services
accessible and affordable to all Filipinos, especially the poor, the sick, senior citizens,
women, children, and persons with disabilities. To fulfill this duty, the government
created PhilHealth through Republic Act No. 7875 to provide health insurance
coverage to all citizens.

While PhilHealth coverage has expanded over the years, there is still a serious gap
when it comes to ambulance and emergency medical transport. Many emergency
cases are not clearly covered, or families are unsure if PhilHealth will pay for
transport services. As a result, patients suffer delays and families face heavy financial
burdens.

Republic Act No. 10932, also known as the Anti-Hospital Deposit Law, recognizes that
emergency transport is part of emergency care. It allows the free use of emergency
vehicles when medically necessary and provides PhilHealth reimbursement for poor
and indigent patients. However, this law does not yet provide a clear, standard, and

comprehensive ambulance benefit that applies to all PhilHealth members and their
dependents.

This bill seeks to fix that problem. It aims to clearly include medically necessary
ambulance and emergency medical transport services as part of PhilHealth’s benefit
packages. It ensures that all members and their dependents are covered, with
guaranteed support especially for poor and indigent patients. The bill also allows
PhilHealth, together with the Department of Health, to set clear rules on coverage,
payment rates, and accreditation to ensure quality service while keeping the system
sustainable.

By making ambulance and emergency transport a guaranteed PhilHealth benefit, this
measure will help patients get faster emergency care, reduce out-of-pocket expenses
for families, improve coordination with local governments, and uphold the
constitutional promise of affordable and accessible health services for all.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
MANDATING THE INCLUSION OF AMBULANCE AND EMERGENCY MEDICAL
TRANSPORT SERVICES IN THE BENEFIT PACKAGES OF THE NATIONAL HEALTH
INSURANCE PROGRAM, AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7875, AS AMENDED, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Ambulance and Emergency
Medical Transport Services Coverage Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State,
consistent with the National Health Insurance Act of 2013 and the constitutional
mandate to make health services available at affordable cost, to ensure timely access
to life-saving emergency medical care by providing financial risk protection for
medically necessary ambulance and emergency medical transport services through
the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP).

Towards this end, the State shall guarantee that ambulance and emergency medical
transport services form an integral component of the basic minimum package and
appropriate supplementary packages of health insurance benefits under the NHIP,
with particular priority to indigent and other vulnerable members.

SECTION 3. Amendment of Republic Act No. 7875, as Amended. —


                                                                              3 | Page
A new section, to be numbered appropriately under Article III of Republic Act No.
7875, as amended by Republic Acts No. 9241 and 10606, is hereby inserted to read
as follows:

     “SEC. 11. Ambulance and Emergency Medical Transport Benefit. –
     (a) The National Health Insurance Program shall cover medically necessary
         ambulance and emergency medical transport services for all members
         and their dependents, whether for:

         (1) initial transport from the site of emergency, the scene of an incident, or
             the patient’s residence to the nearest appropriate health care facility;
             and

         (2) medically necessary inter-facility transfer between health care
             providers when the services required for the proper management of the
             patient are not available in the referring facility.

     (b) For purposes of this Act, “ambulance and emergency medical transport
         services” shall refer to the use of a land, water, or air vehicle, duly equipped
         and staffed in accordance with standards set by the Department of Health
         (DOH), for the transport of a patient who, in the professional judgment of a
         licensed physician or appropriately trained emergency medical personnel,
         requires prompt medical attention or monitoring during transport to
         prevent death, disability, serious impairment of health, or injury to or loss
         of the unborn child.

     (c) As a minimum, coverage under this section shall include the basic
         emergency care and transportation services rendered to poor and
         indigent patients as contemplated under Republic Act No. 10932, without
         prejudice to the coverage of other categories of members.

     (d) The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) shall design and
         implement standard benefit packages and case rates for ambulance and
         emergency medical transport services, taking into account, among others,
         the level of care required, distance, mode of transport, and the classification
         and accreditation status of providers. Such benefit packages shall, to the
         extent practicable, be harmonized with existing and future benefit designs
         for emergency care, Z-benefits, and other specialized packages.



                                                                               4 | Page
     (e) Only ambulance and emergency medical transport providers that meet
         the clinical, technical, and operational standards set by the DOH and
         are accredited by PhilHealth shall be eligible for reimbursement under
         the NHIP, without prejudice to the obligation of hospitals and local
         government units (LGUs) under existing laws to provide emergency
         transport in appropriate cases.

     (f) Reimbursement for ambulance and emergency medical transport services
         shall be made to the accredited health care provider or ambulance service
         provider, including LGUs operating accredited emergency medical vehicles,
         in accordance with PhilHealth rules and regulations.

     (g) PhilHealth may adopt differentiated co-payment, co-insurance, or no-
         balance-billing arrangements for ambulance and emergency medical
         transport services, giving priority and preferential protection to indigent,
         sponsored, senior citizen, and other vulnerable members in accordance
         with law.”

SECTION 4. Integration with the Anti-Hospital Deposit Law. — To ensure effective
implementation of Republic Act No. 10932 and related laws, PhilHealth shall align the
ambulance and emergency medical transport benefit packages created under this Act
with its obligation to reimburse the cost of basic emergency care and
transportation services incurred by hospitals and medical clinics for
emergency medical services given to poor and indigent patients, and with the
responsibilities of LGUs to allow the free use of their emergency medical vehicles in
appropriate cases.

SECTION 5. Funding. — The cost of implementing the ambulance and emergency
medical transport benefit shall be charged against the National Health Insurance
Fund, subject to the usual budgeting, actuarial, and auditing rules, and supplemented
by appropriations under the annual General Appropriations Act as necessary.

The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall ensure that sufficient funds
are allocated to PhilHealth to support the progressive realization of full ambulance
and emergency medical transport coverage, with priority to indigent, sponsored, and
senior citizen members consistent with existing laws.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth, the Department
of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), LGUs, professional societies, emergency

medical services providers, and other concerned stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act,
including:

   (a) Clinical, technical, and operational standards for ambulance and emergency
       medical transport services;

   (b) Accreditation requirements and quality assurance mechanisms for
       ambulance providers;

   (c) Detailed benefit designs, case rates, and payment mechanisms, including
       integration with existing emergency and Z-benefit packages; and

   (d) Mechanisms for monitoring, evaluation, and reporting of utilization,
       outcomes, and financial impact.

Such rules and regulations shall be consistent with the prohibitions under the
National Health Insurance Act that PhilHealth shall be limited to paying for the
utilization of health services and purchasing health services on behalf of
beneficiaries, and shall not itself provide health care directly.

SECTION 7. Monitoring and Review. — Within three (3) years from the effectivity
of this Act, and every three (3) years thereafter, PhilHealth shall submit to Congress
and the Office of the President a comprehensive report on the implementation of
the ambulance and emergency medical transport benefit, including data on:

   (a) Utilization by membership category and geographic area;

   (b) Impact on emergency care access, health outcomes, and financial risk
       protection; and

   (c) Recommendations for adjustments to benefit design, provider payment, and
       funding.

       Congress shall review the report and may enact further legislation to
       strengthen and expand ambulance and emergency medical transport coverage
       under the NHIP.
