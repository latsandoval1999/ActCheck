                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE
        The illegal trade in tobacco and nicotine products poses a serious challenge to
legitimate business operations, fair market competition, consumer welfare, and the proper
conduct of domestic and international trade. The widespread circulation of smuggled,
counterfeit, unregistered, and other noncompliant products places lawful businesses at a
disadvantage, particularly those that invest in production, importation, distribution,
employment generation, regulatory compliance, and consumer protection. These illicit traders
have an unfair market advantage and disrupt legitimate economic activities.

        Illicit trade flourishes when weaknesses in enforcement and supply chain monitoring
are exploited to distribute products without complying with the requirements of the law on
registration, labeling, product quality and safety standards, intellectual property rights,
licensing, and other trade regulations.

       The consequences of illicit trade are felt throughout the broader business sector.
Legitimate manufacturers, importers, distributors, wholesalers, retailers, and online commerce
operators that adhere to Philippine laws and regulations face increased competitive pressures
from entities that operate outside the legal framework. Such circumstances discourage
innovation, brand development, and long-term investment, as compliant businesses must
absorb the full costs of meeting regulatory requirements while competing with operators that
avoid these obligations. Consequently, legitimate enterprises may find it more difficult to
expand their operations, create employment opportunities, and contribute meaningfully to the
country's economic progress.

        The expansion of digital marketplaces, cross-border commercial activities, and
increasingly complex distribution systems has further facilitated the circulation and sale of illicit
products. These developments present additional enforcement challenges for government
authorities and increase the risk that consumers will purchase products that have not
undergone the necessary regulatory assessment and verification required by Philippine law.

        Although existing legislation provides measures to combat smuggling, counterfeiting,
trademark violations, tax evasion, and other illegal trade activities, the implementation of these
measures remains distributed among multiple laws and government agencies. This situation
highlights the need for a more integrated and comprehensive legal framework that strengthens
responsibility and accountability throughout the supply chain, enhances inter-agency
cooperation, responds to evolving methods of illicit trading, and ensures fairer competitive
conditions for businesses operating within the law.

         In response to these concerns, this proposed measure aims to reinforce the protection
of legitimate commerce by enhancing the capacity of the government to identify, investigate,
prevent, and suppress illicit trade involving covered products. It seeks to establish more
effective safeguards against unfair commercial practices, strengthen the enforcement of
intellectual property rights, promote accountability at every stage of the distribution process,
encourage compliance with applicable regulations, and address the increasing use of digital
platforms to facilitate unlawful trade.

         This proposed legislation recognizes that a transparent, competitive, and rules-based
trading environment is fundamental to economic development and investor confidence. By
reinforcing the legal and institutional mechanisms against illicit trade, the State seeks to uphold
the rights and interests of legitimate businesses, protect consumers from unlawful commercial
activities, and maintain the integrity of Philippine commerce and trade.

       In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

                              AN ACT
STRENGTHENING    LAW   ENFORCEMENT   AGAINST ILLICIT TRADE IN
TOBACCO PRODUCTS AND RELATED PRODUCTS AS A MATTER OF PUBLIC
ORDER, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to uphold public order and the
rule of law by preventing and suppressing the illicit importation, manufacture, sale, distribution,
and possession of tobacco and related products through effective law enforcement and a
whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach.
         The State recognizes that illicit trade in tobacco and related products fuels organized
criminal activity, weakens State authority, fosters corruption, and undermines regulatory and
law enforcement efforts, thereby endangering community safety, public health, and the
integrity of lawful commerce.
         The State further declares its commitment to strengthen inter-agency coordination and
develop scientific, technical, and institutional capacity to detect, investigate, interdict, and
prosecute all forms of illicit trade in tobacco and related products, including those facilitated
through digital platforms, while safeguarding government revenues and protecting the public
interest.

SEC. 2. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
  a) Illicit tobacco trade refers to any manufacture, distribution, shipment, transport,
      storage, sale, purchase, possession, or import/export of tobacco products, tobacco-
      manufacturing equipment, or non-tobacco materials that is undertaken in
      contravention of applicable laws, regulations, licensing and regulatory requirements,
      or fiscal obligations."
  b) Covered Products refer to locally manufactured and/or imported tobacco products,
      cigars, cigarettes, heated tobacco products, vapor products, and novel tobacco
      products, including hand-rolled cigarettes, smokeless tobacco, nicotine pouches,
      gums, and lozenges;
  c) Digital platforms refer to information and communications technology-enabled
      systems, applications, websites, or other online interfaces that facilitate, enable, or
      allow the listing, advertising, marketing, offering for sale, sale, purchase, payment,
      delivery, or distribution of goods or services through electronic means, including e-
      commerce platforms, mobile applications, online delivery platforms, and social media
      platforms;
  d) E-marketplace refers to digital platforms whose business is to connect online
      consumers with online merchants, facilitate the shipment of goods or provide logistics
      services and post-purchase support within such platforms, and otherwise retain
      oversight over the consummation of the transaction;
  e) E-retailer refers to a person or entity that sells goods or services directly to consumers
      through its own online platform (such as a website, webpage, or application);
   f) Retailer any person or entity that habitually sells goods directly to the general public
      for consumption.
   g) Noncompliant or illegal covered products refer to covered products that are
      manufactured, imported, possessed, transported, stored, distributed, offered for sale,
      or sold in violation of applicable laws, rules, or regulations, including, as may be
      applicable, those: (i) without the required registrations, permits, or licenses; (ii) bearing
      fake, falsified, unauthorized, or misleading marks, labels, or product identifiers; (iii) not
      included in the list of registered brands of the BIR or the Department of Trade and
      Industry (DTI), when required; or (iv) otherwise declared by the appropriate regulatory
      agency as prohibited, counterfeit, smuggled, or illicit.

SEC. 3. Acts Constituting Illicit Trade of Covered Products. – The crimes and statutory
offenses under Republic Act No. 8424, otherwise known as the “National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended;” Republic Act No. 10863, otherwise known as the “Customs
Modernization and Tariff Act (CMTA);” Republic Act No. 8293, otherwise known as the
“Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines;” Republic Act No. 11900, otherwise known as
the “Vaporized Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act;” and Republic Act No.
10643, otherwise known as “The Graphic Health Warnings Law,” insofar as they pertain to
covered products, shall be considered as illicit trade.
         In addition, illicit trade involving covered products, as defined under Section 2 of this
Act, shall include the following acts:
    a) Manufacturing and/or importing noncompliant or illegal covered products into or within
         the country without the requisite registration or import permits from the appropriate
         regulatory agencies: Provided, That the same does not fall under the thresholds
         provided under Republic Act No. 12022, otherwise known as the “Anti-Agricultural
         Economic Sabotage Act”;
    b) Manufacturing covered products for export that are not compliant with the laws,
         regulations, including fiscal and regulatory marking requirements of the country where
         the tobacco products will be ultimately sold;
    c) Using import permits issued to persons, whether natural or juridical, other than those
         specifically named in the permit;
    d) Using fake, fictitious, falsified, or fraudulent import permits or shipping documents;
    e) Selling, lending, leasing, assigning, or otherwise consenting to or allowing the use of
         import permits of corporations, non-governmental organizations, associations,
         cooperatives, or sole proprietorships by other persons;
    f) Using dummy corporations, one-person corporations, non-governmental
         organizations, associations, cooperatives, or sole proprietorships for the purpose of
         obtaining import permits;
    g) Knowingly allowing the use of private ports, government ports, fish ports, fish landing
         sites, resorts, marinas, airports, warehouses, aircraft, and/or vessels to facilitate or
         perpetrate smuggling;
    h) Producing or manufacturing any covered product, or any part or component thereof,
         using equipment, machinery, or components that are concealed, undeclared, or
         otherwise unregistered with the BIR;
    i) Declaring covered products as for export when, in fact, they are diverted, sold, or
         intended for sale in the domestic market in violation of applicable laws, rules, or
         regulations;
    j) Declaring covered products for transshipment when, in fact, they are diverted, sold, or
         intended for sale in the domestic market in violation of applicable laws, rules, or
         regulations;
    k) Selling or distributing genuine covered products intended for other countries that are
         diverted from the legitimate supply chain and unlawfully sold or distributed in the
         domestic market;
   l) Distributing covered products through wholesale or other business-to-business
      transactions where such products are not included in the list of brands registered with
      the BIR or the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);
   m) Knowingly selling or distributing covered products bearing marks used without the
      authorization of the trademark owner, or marks that cannot be distinguished in their
      essential aspects from the original mark.
   n) Knowingly causing the transport of, or storing, noncompliant or illegal covered
      products, or covered products involved in any act constituting illicit trade under this
      Section;

      Transport or storage of noncompliant or illegal covered products shall not constitute illicit
trade when undertaken for any of the following purposes, subject to the limitations provided in
the immediately succeeding paragraph:
      i) When the transport or storage is for purposes of judicial, quasi-judicial, or
           administrative proceedings;
      ii) When the transport or storage is for exhibition or educational purposes with the
           consent of the trademark owner;
      iii) When the transport or storage is for exhibition or educational purposes, with prior
           notice given to the Inter-Agency Council on Anti-Illicit Trade; or
      iv) When undertaken in connection with confiscation, destruction, or other lawful actions
           in the enforcement of applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

       For purposes of subparagraphs (ii) and (iii), such transport or storage is limited to non-
commercial quantities, as may be determined in the implementing rules and regulations:
Provided, further, That such limitation shall not apply to transport, storage, custody, or
handling undertaken by, or upon lawful authority of, the Philippine National Police, the Bureau
of Internal Revenue, the Bureau of Customs, the Department of Trade and Industry, or other
duly authorized law enforcement or regulatory agencies in connection with seizure,
confiscation, evidence preservation, case build-up, prosecution, or destruction, in accordance
with existing laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 4. Penalties for Violation of Section 3. –
a) Any person found guilty of any of the punishable acts enumerated in Section 3 shall be
punished with imprisonment for not less than twenty (20) years and one (1) day but not more
than forty (40) years, and a fine equivalent to twice the fair market value of the covered
products and the aggregate amount of the taxes, duties, and other charges avoided or sought
to be avoided through such illicit trade, as may be applicable;

b) Any person found guilty of aiding or abetting in any of the punishable acts enumerated in
Section 3 shall be punished with imprisonment for not less than twelve (12) years and one (1)
day but not more than twenty (20) years, and a fine equivalent to the fair market value of the
covered products involved and the aggregate amount of the taxes, duties, and other charges
avoided or sought to be avoided, as may be applicable;

c) Any person found guilty of any of the punishable acts enumerated in Section 3 who provides
information or testifies on insider knowledge material to the establishment of the case against
any other involved party shall, as a mitigating circumstance, be subject to a reduced penalty
of imprisonment for not less than six (6) years and one (1) day but not more than twelve (12)
years;

        In all cases, the illicit covered products, manufacturing equipment, instruments, boats
or vessels, and any other means of transportation used to distribute or transport the same,
without distinction, shall be confiscated and forfeited in favor of the Government. The same
shall be destroyed or disposed of in government-owned or government-supervised materials
recovery, composting, and extrusion facilities to be established, maintained, and secured by
the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), the Bureau of Customs (BOC), or the Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI) in major strategic areas of the country.
        When the offender is a juridical person, criminal liability shall attach to the members of
its Board of Directors, its president, chief operating officer, manager, or any other corporate
officer thereof, owner in the case of a sole proprietorship, or partners in the case of a
partnership who consents to, knowingly tolerates, or ignores the commission of the offense.
        Any person, whether natural or juridical, found guilty under Section 3 of this Act shall
likewise suffer the penalty of perpetual absolute disqualification to engage in any business
involving importation.
        If the offender is a public official or employee, the penalty shall be imprisonment for
not less than forty (40) years and a fine equivalent to twice the fair market value and the
aggregate amount of the taxes, duties, and other charges avoided through the illicit trade in
finished covered products. In addition, the public official or employee, whether elected or
appointed, shall be deemed absolutely and perpetually disqualified from holding any public
office.
        If the offender is an alien, the alien shall, in addition to the penalties herein prescribed,
be deported without further proceedings after serving the penalties herein imposed.
        Prosecution under this Act shall be without prejudice to any liability under the Revised
Penal Code, as amended, and other applicable special laws.

SEC. 5. Liability of Digital Platforms Selling Illicit Covered Products. – Digital platforms shall
be responsible for ensuring that illicit covered products are not posted or sold on their
platforms by:
    1) Requiring all merchants, as defined under Section 2 hereof, selling covered products
        to display, on their shop pages, proof of registration with the Department of Trade and
        Industry (DTI) or the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), and the Bureau of
        Internal Revenue (BIR);
    2) Requiring all such merchants to provide the permits, licenses, and other authorization
        information required for the lawful sale of covered products; and
    3) Contractually requiring such merchants to comply with sale procedures and limitations,
        and all other relevant conditions for the sale of covered products.

         The DTI and the BIR shall regularly publish, on their respective official websites, a list
of legitimate products that may be sold online.
         Digital platforms shall be solidarily liable when they knowingly or negligently allow the
posting, offering for sale, sale, or distribution of noncompliant or illegal covered products, or
when they fail to remove or disable listings that facilitate any act constituting illicit trade under
Section 3 hereof, upon notice or verification. Upon verification by the Council, the Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), and/or the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), as may be
appropriate, the DTI may order the blocking of specific webpages, accounts, or listings of
merchants that are in violation of this Section and/or Section 3 hereof, or, in case of repeated
or willful noncompliance, the entire website or application of a digital platform or online
marketplace. Such blocking shall be implemented by the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), through the National Telecommunications Commission,
in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations.
         A digital platform shall likewise incur criminal liability where it is shown that it willfully
participates in, cooperates in, or benefits from the sale or distribution of illicit covered products.
         Any violation of this Section shall be punished by:
     1. Imprisonment of not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years, and
     2. A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) but not more than
         Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00),
at the discretion of the court, without prejudice to the imposition of higher penalties under
existing laws.
SEC 6. Liabilities of Retailers Selling Illicit Covered Products – Any person who sells illicit
products in violation of this Act and its implementing rules and regulations, shall, upon
apprehension, be subject first to the immediate seizure, confiscation, and forfeiture in favor of
the Government of all illicit goods, including the instruments and proceeds of the offense. Such
confiscation shall precede and be without prejudice to the imposition, upon conviction, of the
following penalties:
(a) First Offense – After the immediate seizure, confiscation, and forfeiture of the illicit goods,
instruments, and proceeds of the offense, the offender shall suffer a fine of Ten Thousand
Pesos (₱10,000.00), without prejudice to administrative sanctions.
(b) Second Offense – After the immediate seizure, confiscation, and forfeiture of the illicit
goods, instruments, and proceeds of the offense, the offender shall suffer a fine of One
Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00). In default of payment of the fine, the same shall be
subject to subsidiary liability in accordance with existing laws.
(c) Third and Subsequent Offenses – After the immediate seizure, confiscation, and forfeiture
of the illicit goods, instruments, and proceeds of the offense, the offender shall suffer the
penalty of imprisonment of not less than five (5) years but not more than ten (10) years, and
a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00).
For purposes of this Section, each act of sale or distribution involving illicit goods shall
constitute a separate offense.

SEC. 7. Inter-Agency Council on Anti-Illicit Trade. – There is hereby established an inter-
agency council on anti-illicit trade, hereinafter referred to as the “Council,” which shall oversee
the implementation of this Act. The Council shall be chaired by the Department of Finance and
shall be composed of one (1) representative from each of the following agencies:
        1) Department of Finance;
        2) Department of Justice;
        3) Department of Agriculture/National Tobacco Administration;
        4) Department of the Interior and Local Government;
        5) Department of Transportation;
        6) Department of Trade and Industry;
        7) Department of Information and Communications Technology; and
        8) Representative from the legitimate tobacco industry.

The Council shall convene at least once a month, or upon the initiative of the Chairperson.

The Department of Finance shall serve as the Secretariat of the Council, using its existing
organizational structure. The Council may invite resource persons, as it may deem necessary,
to discuss matters relevant to the performance of its functions.

SEC. 8. Powers and Functions of the Council. – The Council shall have the following powers
and functions:
   a) Formulate and implement appropriate measures to prevent and suppress illicit trade
       in covered products;
   b) Issue letters of authority directing the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), the Bureau of
       Customs (BOC), and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) to enforce measures
       to combat illicit tobacco trade, including by effecting searches, seizures, and arrests,
       and by conducting inspections and securing documents: Provided, That for this
       purpose, the BIR, the BOC, and the DTI may coordinate with law enforcement
       agencies in the conduct of their operations;
   c) Establish mechanisms for the sharing of data and information on illicit trade in covered
       products within and among member agencies, including the identification and analysis
       of patterns of illicit trade in localities, possible routes, networks involved, and potential
       hotspot areas;
   d) Ensure that information obtained or disclosed is properly recorded and maintained,
       and that its handling complies with existing laws, rules, and regulations;
   e) Develop effective pathways, procedures, and guidelines for the implementation of
      measures against illicit trade in covered products;
   f) Disseminate the policies and guidelines adopted by the Council and ensure their
      implementation at the national and local levels;
   g) Coordinate and network with similar bodies internationally and share lessons and
      experiences to strengthen the implementation of measures against illicit trade in
      covered products;
   h) Submit an annual report to the Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on Illicit
      Trade on Excisable Products on its accomplishments and the status of its operations;
   i) Act on citizens’ complaints: Provided, That any person may file a complaint with, or
      report violations to, any member of the Council;
   j) Ensure the prompt investigation and prosecution of persons accused or detained for
      offenses under this Act, and monitor the progress of their cases;
   k) Coordinate with government agencies, including government-owned or -controlled
      corporations and local government units (LGUs), as well as the business sector and
      non-governmental organizations, in implementing this Act; and
   l) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the purposes of this
      Act, in accordance with the rules adopted by the Council.

SEC. 9. Law Enforcement Implementation and Coordination. – The Philippine National Police
(PNP), and other law enforcement agencies attached to or operating under the Departments
represented in the Council including but not limited to the Bureau of Internal Revenue, Bureau
of Customs, National Bureau of Investigation, Philippine Coast Guard and the enforcement
offices of the Department of Agriculture, Department for Trade and Industry and the National
Tobacco Administration, and such other agencies as may be authorized by law or deputized
by the Council, shall enforce and implement the provisions of this Act, consistent with their
respective mandates. For this purpose, the PNP and other law enforcement agencies shall
coordinate with, and may be deputized by, the Inter-Agency Council on Anti-Illicit Trade
created under Section 6 hereof, including in the conduct of intelligence gathering,
investigation, interdiction operations, search and seizure, arrest, and case build-up and
prosecution support, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 10. Custody and Disposition of Confiscated, Seized, and/or Surrendered Covered
Products. – The apprehending officer or team from the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), the
Bureau of Customs (BOC), or the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall take charge
and have custody of all covered products, as well as any manufacturing equipment, parts,
packaging materials, and printing equipment or paraphernalia confiscated, seized, and/or
surrendered, for proper disposition as follows:
    a) The apprehending officer or team having initial custody and control of the covered
       products shall, immediately after seizure and confiscation, conduct a physical
       inventory and photograph the seized or confiscated covered products in the presence
       of the person from whom such items were confiscated and/or seized, or such person’s
       representative or counsel. The inventory shall be duly signed by the apprehending
       officer or team leader and witnessed and signed by at least two (2) barangay officials,
       whether elected or appointed, and a copy thereof shall be furnished to the person
       concerned: Provided, That the physical inventory and photograph shall be conducted
       at the place where the search warrant is served;
    b) The complete list of the names of the apprehending officer or team, together with the
       inventory duly signed and witnessed under paragraph (a) hereof, shall be submitted to
       the Council;
    c) The apprehending officer or team shall be responsible for the safekeeping of the seized
       or confiscated covered products until their final destruction;
    d) Any apprehending officer, team member, or other government personnel having
       custody of seized, confiscated, or surrendered covered products who, through malice,
       gross negligence, or fault, fails to account for, loses, misappropriates, unlawfully
      disposes of, permits the unauthorized removal of, or otherwise causes the loss,
      substitution, deterioration, or disappearance of such items, in whole or in part, shall,
      without prejudice to any criminal, civil, or administrative liability under existing laws, be
      liable under the penalties prescribed under Section 4(a) hereof;
   e) All seized or confiscated covered products shall be destroyed, except for the retention
      of representative samples for use in judicial, quasi-judicial, or administrative
      proceedings; and
   f) In every case, the BOC, BIR, or DTI shall cause the destruction of the seized or
      confiscated illicit items in the presence of at least one (1) representative from the
      tobacco or vapor product manufacturing sector and at least one (1) official
      representative from: (i) the Department of Agriculture–National Tobacco
      Administration, for tobacco products and materials; or (ii) the DTI–Fair Trade
      Enforcement Bureau, for vapor products and materials. Such destruction shall be
      conducted not earlier than five (5) calendar days from receipt of the notice, as duly
      acknowledged by the industry and government witnesses.

SEC. 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Department of Finance, through the BIR, shall, in consultation with the
Department of Trade and Industry, through the Fair Trade Enforcement Bureau, the
Department of Agriculture, through the National Tobacco Administration, the Department of
Information and Communications Technology, through the National Telecommunications
Commission, the Department of Justice, the Department of the Interior and Local Government,
and the Department of Transportation, through the Philippine Coast Guard, promulgate the
necessary guidelines for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, other parts or provision hereof not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
