                              AN ACT
STRENGTHENING MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES AND SUPPORT SYSTEMS IN
STATE UNIVERSITIES AND COLLEGES, ESTABLISHING CAMPUS MENTAL
HEALTH OFFICES THEREIN, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Young Filipinos today experience rising levels of psychological distress, depression, and
suicide-related behaviors, yet access to mental health care remains limited and unevenly distributed.
Recent reports indicate that an estimated 7.5% of Filipino youth have ever attempted suicide, reflecting
increasing psychological distress among adolescents and young adults.¹ Nearly 2,000 suicide cases were
recorded by the Philippine National Police in the first half of 2025 alone, underscoring the urgency of
strengthening mental health services nationwide.² Alongside suicide attempts, Filipino youth also
exhibit high rates of depressive and anxiety symptoms—conditions commonly associated with
academic stress, social pressures, and insufficient access to mental health support in school
environments.³

        At the same time, mental health resources remain limited. Access to professional support
continues to be constrained by the insufficient number and uneven distribution of trained mental health
professionals relative to the country’s population needs, while stigma remains a major barrier to help-
seeking behavior.⁴ Within tertiary educational institutions, these challenges become more pronounced:
students face intense academic pressures and personal transitions, yet many campuses lack sufficient
personnel, services, and awareness programs to address emotional, developmental, and psychosocial
concerns that directly affect learning outcomes, safety, and overall well-being.

        A measure substantially similar to this proposal was approved on Third and Final Reading in
the House of Representatives during the 19th Congress, reflecting broad legislative recognition of the
urgent need to institutionalize mental health services within State Universities and Colleges.

        This representation believes that strengthening mental health services in State Universities and
Colleges through the recruitment and training of additional mental health personnel, alongside sustained
awareness and support programs, will reduce barriers to care, help students manage emotional and
psychological challenges, and promote holistic development. Strengthening campus mental health
systems likewise safeguards student welfare while removing barriers to learning and helping form
productive and resilient citizens.

           In this light, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
STRENGTHENING MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES AND SUPPORT SYSTEMS IN
STATE UNIVERSITIES AND COLLEGES, ESTABLISHING CAMPUS MENTAL
HEALTH OFFICES THEREIN, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “State Universities and
Colleges (SUCs) Mental Health Service Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold
the basic right of every Filipino to sound mental health and ensure access to appropriate
interventions, therapy, and treatment as needed. Pursuant to this mandate, the State shall
institute policies to promote the mental health and wellness of students, faculty, teaching and
non-teaching staff, and other personnel in the campuses of SUCs through, among others, the
establishment of a Mental Health Office in all SUCs, and the hiring, deployment, and training
of additional SUCs-based mental health service personnel.

         All SUCs shall ensure that proper and adequate mental health services are provided to
their students, faculty, non-teaching staff and other personnel. In this regard, all SUCs, through
their Mental Health Offices, shall design programs and provide services that shall assist
students in resolving emotional, behavioral, and psychosocial issues that are barriers to
learning and enhance the preparedness of the faculty, teaching, non-teaching staff, and other
personnel to foster an enabling environment for students to acquire knowledge and to develop
intellectually.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
        a) Guidance counselor refers to an individual who possesses a license or certification
granted by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) and holds at the minimum a
master’s degree in Guidance and Counseling, Guidance, or Counseling, without prejudice to
those registered under the grandfather’s clause of Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise known as
the “Guidance and Counseling Act of 2004”, and is competent to perform guidance and
counseling, career guidance and implement mental health programs;
        b) Mental health refers to a state of well-being in which the individual realizes one’s
own abilities and potentials, copes adequately with the normal stresses of life, displays
resilience in the face of extreme life events, works productively and fruitfully, and is able to
make a positive contribution to the community;
        c) Mental health professional refers to a medical doctor, nurse, guidance counselor,
psychologist, or any other appropriately-trained and qualified person with specific skills
relevant to, and necessary for, the provision of mental health services;
        d) Mental health services refer to the psychosocial, psychiatric, or neurologic activities
and programs along the whole range of the mental health support services including promotion,
prevention, treatment, and aftercare, which are provided by mental health facilities and mental
health professionals;
        e) Psychologist refers to a natural person who has professional training and clinical
skills in psychotherapeutic intervention and assessment for mental health problems and
possesses a license or certification granted by the PRC and holds at the minimum a master’s
degree in Psychology without prejudice to those granted licenses under the grandfather’s clause
of Republic Act No. 10029, otherwise known as the “Philippine Psychology Act of 2009”;
        f) SUCs-based mental health service personnel refers to any mental health professional
who is hired or deployed by, or has a contract with an SUC to render mental health services in
its campus.

        SEC. 4. Establishment of Mental Health Offices in All SUCs Campuses. – The
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall, subject to the rules and regulations of the
Civil Service Commission (CSC) and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and
the policies, standards, and guidelines of the CHED, require all SUCs to establish a Mental
Health Office in all their campuses.

       The Mental Health Offices shall set up campus hotlines with dedicated and trained
guidance counselors to provide assistance to the whole SUC Community, especially the
students. Special attention shall be given to those identified to have mental health problems or
conditions, especially those at risk of committing suicide.

        The CHED and the SUCs, through their Mental Health Offices, shall initiate and sustain
a heightened campaign to raise the collective consciousness about mental health. The
awareness campaign shall give special emphasis to the issues of suicide prevention, stress
handling, mental health and nutrition, and guidance and counseling. The campaign shall ensure
that the whole SUC community, especially the students, is aware of in-campus mental health
services.

       The SUCs, through the Mental Health Offices, shall implement mental health programs
and projects designed to promote the mental health and well-being of faculty, students, and
personnel alike.

        Each Mental Health Office shall be manned by trained mental health professionals who
shall serve as the mental health service personnel of the SUCs.
        The SUCs-based mental health service personnel of the Mental Health Offices,
including those who shall be hired upon the effectivity of this Act, shall create a mechanism
for timely crisis intervention and suicide prevention and the appropriate response strategies for
the whole SUCs community, especially the students.

        The CHED shall, in coordination with the SUCs, establish mechanisms to maximize
existing services offered by other executive departments such as the attached agencies to the
Department of Health (DOH) dealing with mental health and the Department of Social Welfare
and Development (DSWD).

        SEC. 5. Hiring, Training and Deployment of Mental Health Professionals. –
Subject to the guidelines set by the CHED and the SUCs, CSC and DBM, the SUCs shall hire,
train and deploy mental health professionals, including a psychologist, to the Mental Health
Office to ensure that each SUC shall have mental health professionals or mental health service
providers within five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act.

        The SUCs shall, in coordination with the CHED, prepare the appropriate plantilla
positions to be submitted to the DBM for evaluation and approval for the additional SUC-based
mental health service personnel to be hired under this Act, subject to the existing
organizational, staffing, position classification and compensation policies, rules, and
regulations.

        Notwithstanding the provisions of Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise known as the
“Guidance and Counseling Act of 2004”, SUCs may hire, as may be necessary, college
graduates who do not yet qualify as mental health professionals but who possess a background
in psychology or similar fields to provide mental health services: Provided, That the CHED
shall provide for the appropriate training and instruction of such college graduates in order for
them to gain and develop the necessary skills to render counselling services to faculty, students
and school personnel, and assist them in managing mental health issues: Provided, further, That
such services shall be rendered under the supervision of qualified mental health professionals
within the schools division: Provided, finally, That a college graduate hired to work in a Mental
Health Office shall, within three (3) years from the date of employment in the SUC, secure the
appropriate license to practice as a mental health professional and meet the relevant
qualification requirements of their position.

         The CHED and the SUCs, through the Mental Health Offices, shall formulate a scheme
for the continuous training of mental health service personnel, taking into consideration the
latest scientific studies, psycho-social approaches and know-how on mental health and mental
health services, in accordance with the provisions of Republic Act No. 10912, otherwise known
as the “Continuing Professional Development Act of 2016”.

      SEC. 6. Professional and Career Development of Mental Health Professionals. –
The CHED shall, in coordination with the DBM, CSC, DOH and the PRC:
       a) Prepare a uniform career and personnel development plan that shall include
          provisions on personal wellness, merit promotion, performance evaluation, in-
          service training, graduate scholarships, research grants, and incentive award system;
       b) Match the salary grades of mental health professionals with related plantilla items
          under the provisions of Republic Act No. 6758, otherwise known as the
          “Compensation and Position Classification Act of 1989,” as amended;
       c) Align the qualifications and standards for the qualification outcomes of Mental
          Health Professionals to the provisions of Republic Act No. 10968, otherwise known
          as the “Philippine Qualifications Framework Act’’;
       d) Revisit the organizational structure of the SUCs to provide competent and
          professional personnel who shall provide supervision and technical assistance to the
          guidance counselors;
       e) Review, revise and develop the duties and responsibilities of mental health
          professionals in accordance with laws governing their professions;
       f) Prepare a performance evaluation plan that considers the improvement of the
          efficiency and organizational effectiveness of each counselor: Provided, That each
          employee shall be informed regularly by their immediate supervisor of the
          evaluation of their performance; and
       g) Provide periodic training, aimed at retooling and upgrading the skills of mental
          health professionals.

        SEC. 7. Salary Scale. – The career and salary scales of mental health professionals in
SUCs shall provide for a gradual progression from a minimum to a maximum salary by means
of regular increments, to be given regularly based on merit and competencies.

        The entry level of mental health professionals shall not be lower than Salary Grade 16.
Mental health professionals hired by SUCs as Psychometricians shall be hired at Salary Grade
11 at the entry level.

        SEC. 8. Contractual Hiring of Mental Health Personnel. – In case of a scarcity of
mental health professionals who are available to work on a full-time basis in a Mental Health
Office, the concerned SUC may hire qualified professionals on a contractual or part-time basis,
subject to the evaluation and approval of the DBM.

       SEC. 9. Benefits. – All mental health professionals employed in the various Mental
Health Offices shall enjoy the rights, privileges, and other benefits provided in Republic Act
No. 7305, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Public Health Workers”, Republic Act No.
11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act”, and other pertinent laws.

       SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
Act shall be included in the Annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the CHED shall, in coordination with the DOH, the Philippine
Association of State Universities and Colleges, and other concerned stakeholders, issue rules
and regulations to govern the hiring, training and deployment of guidance counselors,
psychologists and other mental health service personnel in SUCs, and for the implementation
of this Act.

       SEC. 12. Suppletory Application. – The applicable provisions of Republic Act No.
11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act”, insofar as they are not inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act, shall apply suppletorily.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected, shall remain in full
force and effect.

        SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act is repealed, modified or amended accordingly.

        SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
