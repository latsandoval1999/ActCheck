                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The proposed Comprehensive Education-to-Employment Alignment Act
establishes a coherent national framework that deliberately aligns education and
workforce development as a shared responsibility central to national progress,
social mobility, and human dignity. While the country has made significant
investments in higher education and technical-vocational training, persistent
skills mismatches, underemployment, and uneven regional workforce outcomes
indicate the need for a more integrated and evidence-based system. This measure
institutionalizes an Education-to-Employment Framework that ensures learning
standards, curricula, quality assurance, accreditation, and program delivery are
intentionally oriented toward productive, decent, and sustainable employment,
without reducing education to short-term labor market matching. It affirms that
education quality must be measured not only by academic rigor, but also by its
capacity to prepare individuals for meaningful work, adaptability, citizenship,
innovation, and inclusive growth—while fully respecting academic freedom,
institutional autonomy, and diverse educational missions.

      The Act mandates structured and continuing coordination among the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) to ensure policy coherence and operational alignment
across the education-to-employment continuum. Through harmonized quality
assurance reforms, differentiated accreditation pathways, curriculum-to-
occupation mapping, tracer studies, employer feedback integration, and regular
National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessments, the measure embeds
workforce relevance into program design, review, and evaluation. At the same
time, it safeguards due process, learner protection, and developmental—not
punitive—approaches to program rationalization. Labor market intelligence,
industry participation, and interoperable data systems will serve as strategic
planning tools to anticipate skills mismatches, respond to technological and
sectoral change, and strengthen school-to-work transition mechanisms
nationwide.

       Ultimately, the bill strengthens accountability and transparency by
integrating education-to-employment outcome indicators into quality assurance
systems and requiring the submission of an Annual Education-to-Employment
Alignment Report to Congress. It provides for capacity-building, technical
assistance, and institutional support to enable higher education institutions and
training providers to undertake meaningful reform. By modernizing
accreditation, promoting regional labor market alignment, enabling rapid
curriculum adjustment in high-mismatch sectors, and prioritizing inclusive and
equitable workforce participation, this Act advances a responsive, future-ready,
and socially accountable education and training system. The passage of this
measure is therefore earnestly sought to ensure that the nation’s education
investments translate into dignified employment, sustained productivity, and
long-term national development.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION. 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive
Education-to Employment Alignment Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is the declared policy of the State to uphold
education and workforce development as a shared national responsibility and a
central pillar of national development, social mobility, and human dignity. To this
end, higher education, technical-vocational education and training, and
workforce development shall function as an integrated and mutually reinforcing
system, deliberately oriented toward enabling every learner to attain productive,
decent, and sustainable employment, meaningful livelihood, and lifelong
economic participation.

The State shall ensure the strategic alignment of quality assurance, curriculum
development, program delivery, learning pathways, and credential recognition
across the education-to-employment continuum. Such alignment shall be
undertaken within the respective constitutional and statutory mandates of the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), and shall be guided by labor market realities, national and
regional development priorities, technological and industrial transformation, and
the evolving needs of society, in furtherance of the common good.

Education quality shall be understood and measured not solely by academic rigor
or institutional inputs, but by its capacity to prepare individuals for work,
adaptability, and citizenship, and by its contribution to economic productivity,
social equity, innovation, and sustainable development. In pursuing this policy,
the State shall respect and protect academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and
the diversity of educational missions, while affirming the State’s duty to ensure
that public and private education and training systems remain relevant,
accountable, and responsive to the needs of the people and the nation.

SEC. 3. Institutionalization of the Education-to-Employment Framework —
An Education-to-Employment Framework is hereby institutionalized as a
national policy mechanism and shall apply to all public and private higher
education institutions, technical-vocational institutions, training providers, and
other post-secondary learning programs, whether degree-granting or non-degree-
granting, including alternative learning pathways and industry-recognized
training arrangements, as may be provided by law.
                                                                                2
The Framework shall serve as the principal policy architecture for ensuring that
education and training operate in a coherent, integrated, and outcomes-oriented
manner, from learning entry to workforce participation. Accordingly, all covered
institutions and programs shall align their policies, systems, and practices with
the objectives of this Framework, without prejudice to academic freedom,
institutional autonomy, and diversity of educational missions.

Pursuant thereto, the Framework shall ensure coherence and alignment among
the following components:

      (a) Learning standards and competency frameworks, including national,
      sectoral, and industry-validated standards, which shall define the
      knowledge, skills, values, and competencies required for effective
      participation in the labor market, further education, and lifelong learning,
      and which shall be periodically reviewed to reflect technological change,
      occupational evolution, and national development priorities;

      (b) Curriculum design and delivery, which shall be outcomes-based,
      competency aligned, and responsive to labor market needs, and which may
      incorporate   work-integrated    learning,   apprenticeships,   internships,
      simulation, digital and blended modalities, and other innovative
      pedagogies, provided that such curricula remain consistent with minimum
      academic standards and professional requirements, where applicable;

      (c) Quality assurance, accreditation, and program approval mechanisms,
      which shall be harmonized and calibrated to assess not only academic rigor
      and institutional capacity, but also workforce relevance, program
      effectiveness, learner progression, and graduate outcomes, and which shall
      support continuous improvement, differentiated pathways, and innovation
      rather than mere regulatory compliance; and

                                                                                3
      (d) Employment, productivity, and lifelong learning outcomes, including
      employability, job quality, skills adaptability, career mobility, and
      opportunities for upskilling and reskilling, which shall be recognized as
      essential indicators of program effectiveness and societal value, without
      reducing education to short-term labor market matching.

Provided, that the implementation of the Education-to-Employment Framework
shall be guided by labor market intelligence, employer and industry engagement,
and evidence-based policy, and shall take into account regional and sectoral
conditions, priority industries, and inclusive development goals.

Provided further, that the appropriate government agencies shall, within their
respective mandates, adopt measures to operationalize this Framework through
guidelines, standards, and support mechanisms, consistent with the intent and
purposes of this Act.

SEC. 4. Inter-Agency Coordination — The Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
and the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall jointly implement
this Act through a structured and continuing inter-agency coordination
mechanism, which shall ensure policy coherence, operational alignment, and
effective execution, consistent with their respective statutory mandates and areas
of authority.

Such inter-agency coordination shall be designed to facilitate regular
consultation,   information-sharing,    joint   planning,    and    synchronized
implementation of policies, programs, and standards relating to education quality,
skills development, and workforce outcomes. The mechanism shall operate
without prejudice to the existing powers, functions, and responsibilities of each



                                                                                4
agency, and shall avoid unnecessary duplication of functions or regulatory
processes.

In the implementation of this Act:

      (a) The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall exercise primary
      responsibility over matters relating to higher education policy, the setting
      and enforcement of academic program standards, outcomes-based
      education frameworks, and higher education quality assurance, including
      program approval, monitoring, and evaluation, in accordance with existing
      laws and regulations;

      (b) The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
      (TESDA) shall exercise primary responsibility over technical-vocational
      education and training, including the development and updating of
      competency-based standards, training regulations, assessment and
      certification systems, and the quality assurance of non-degree and skills-
      oriented programs, consistent with national qualifications frameworks and
      industry standards; and

      (c) The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall serve as the
      principal source of labor market intelligence, employment and workforce
      data, and demand-side signals, including information on priority sectors,
      emerging occupations, skills shortages, and employment outcomes, which
      shall be utilized to inform education and training planning, curriculum
      development, and program review.

Provided, that DOLE’s labor market information and analysis shall be
communicated to CHED and TESDA in a timely, systematic, and usable manner,
and shall be integrated into policy formulation and program evaluation processes,
as appropriate.
                                                                                5
Provided further, that the inter-agency coordination mechanism shall promote
evidence-based decision-making, anticipate skills mismatches, and support
proactive alignment of education and training with workforce needs, while
ensuring flexibility to respond to regional and sectoral variations.

Provided finally, that nothing in this section shall be construed to impair or
diminish institutional autonomy, academic freedom, or the diversity of
educational missions, nor shall it authorize undue interference in academic
decision-making, but shall be implemented in a manner that respects the
constitutional and statutory protections accorded to educational institutions.

SEC. 5. Quality Assurance and Accreditation as Core Pillars of Education-to
Employment — Quality assurance and accreditation are hereby declared core
pillars of the Education-to-Employment Framework and shall be recalibrated,
modernized, and continuously applied to ensure a direct, intentional, and
enduring link between education quality, workforce outcomes, and societal
relevance. For this purpose, quality assurance and accreditation shall function not
merely as regulatory instruments, but as strategic mechanisms for continuous
improvement, workforce alignment, and public accountability across the
education and training system.

The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall undertake accreditation reform
within their respective jurisdictions to ensure that accreditation standards,
processes, and outcomes explicitly incorporate workforce responsiveness,
employability, program relevance, and graduate outcomes, while continuing to
uphold academic rigor, integrity, and institutional accountability.

Accordingly, accreditation systems shall:



                                                                                 6
      (a) Give substantive and demonstrable weight to outcomes such as
           employability, workforce readiness, skills adaptability, and career
           progression, in addition to academic and institutional inputs;
      (b) Incentive and operationalize the establishment of ladderized pathways
           across TVET and higher education, including mechanisms to
           recognize prior learning, in order to encourage further upskilling and
           progression of learners, consistent with Republic Acts 10647 and
           12124.
      (c) Recognize and reward programs with strong industry engagement,
           work integrated learning, enterprise partnerships, or professional
           alignment, where applicable;
      (d) Support innovation and continuous improvement by emphasizing
           developmental feedback and outcomes assessment rather than
           prescriptive or purely compliance-based requirements; and
      (e) Reduce unnecessary regulatory burden by streamlining processes,
           harmonizing requirements, and avoiding duplicative documentation,
           consistent with existing laws.

SEC. 6. Differentiated Quality Assurance and Accreditation Pathways —
CHED and TESDA shall further establish differentiated quality assurance and
accreditation pathways, particularly for emerging, priority, and rapidly evolving
sectors, including but not limited to digital technologies, health and care services,
advanced manufacturing, agriculture and agri-technology, green and climate-
resilient industries, and creative and knowledge-based sectors.

Such differentiated pathways may include:

      (a) Accelerated or adaptive accreditation cycles for programs in fast-
      changing sectors;


                                                                                   7
       (b) Modular, competency-based, or stackable accreditation approaches
       aligned with industry standards and occupational frameworks;

       (c) Provisional, pilot, or sandbox accreditation mechanisms to allow
       innovation while ensuring minimum quality thresholds; and

       (d) Enhanced collaboration with industry, professional bodies, and
       employers in the validation of standards and outcomes.

Provided, that in sectors with nascent accreditation bodies and programs, CHED
and TESDA shall provide support for their establishment;

Provided further, that differentiated pathways shall be applied in a manner that
ensures quality, protects learners, and maintains public trust, while allowing
flexibility and innovation.

Provided further, that in implementing this section, CHED and TESDA shall
calibrate quality assurance and accreditation requirements according to
institutional typology, program nature, and stage of development.

Provided finally, that the appropriate agencies shall issue the necessary guidelines
as well as grants and supports in order to operationalize accreditation reform and
differentiated quality assurance pathways consistent with the objectives of this
Act.

SEC. 7. Expansion of Quality Assurance Criteria — The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), within their respective jurisdictions and consistent with
existing laws, shall expand, refine, and operationalize quality assurance criteria
to explicitly and systematically incorporate workforce relevance indicators,
alongside established academic and institutional standards.


                                                                                  8
For this purpose, quality assurance criteria shall include, but not be limited to, the
following:

      (a) Graduate employment, underemployment, and job-matching
      outcomes, including the timeliness, quality, and sustainability of
      employment, which shall be assessed using labor market information,
      tracer studies, and employment data provided, generated, or validated by
      the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), subject to applicable
      data privacy and confidentiality laws;

      (b) Alignment of curricula with industry-validated competencies and
      occupational standards, including consistency with national qualifications
      frameworks, sectoral skills frameworks, and professional or licensure
      requirements, where applicable, and the regular updating of learning
      outcomes to reflect evolving industry practices and technologies;

      (c) Integration of work-based learning, apprenticeships, internships,
      enterprise-based training, and other forms of experiential learning, as
      appropriate to the program and sector, including the quality, duration,
      supervision, and relevance of such learning experiences to intended
      workforce outcomes;

      (d) Employer and industry participation in curriculum development,
      program design, delivery, and review, including representation in
      advisory bodies, consultation processes, validation of learning outcomes,
      and feedback on graduate performance and skills readiness;

      (e) Responsiveness to regional and sectoral labor market priorities,
      including alignment with national, regional, and local development plans,
      priority industries, and inclusive growth objectives, taking into account
      geographic context, institutional mission, and community needs; and
                                                                                    9
      (f) Contribution to innovation, productivity, entrepreneurship, and
      community development, including the promotion of applied research,
      technology transfer, enterprise creation, and socially responsive programs
      that generate economic and social value.

Provided, that workforce relevance indicators shall be contextualized and
differentiated according to institutional typology, program nature, learner profile,
and stage of development, and shall not be applied in a uniform or mechanistic
manner.

Provided further, that the application of such criteria shall support continuous
improvement and developmental quality assurance, and shall not result in the
arbitrary withdrawal of program recognition or accreditation without due process
and reasonable transition mechanisms.

Provided finally, that CHED and TESDA shall issue the appropriate guidelines
to operationalize the expanded quality assurance criteria under this section, in
coordination with DOLE and relevant stakeholders, consistent with the objectives
of this Act.

SEC. 8. National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessment — CHED,
TESDA, and DOLE shall regularly conduct, at least every three years, a regular
and systematic National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessment for the
purpose of identifying, analyzing, and addressing mismatches between
educational and training offerings, acquired competencies, and actual labor
market demand.

Such assessment shall serve as a core evidence-based planning tool to support
policy formulation, program development, quality assurance, and workforce
alignment across the education and employment ecosystem. The assessment shall
be conducted at intervals to be determined by the implementing agencies, taking
                                                                                 10
into account labor market volatility, technological change, and national and
regional development priorities.

The National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessment shall include, but not
be limited to, the following components:

      (a) Skills shortages and surpluses by sector, occupation, and region,
      including quantitative and qualitative analysis of current and projected
      workforce demand, supply pipelines, and replacement needs, as informed
      by labor market intelligence, employer surveys, and administrative data;

      (b) Identification of programs producing graduates with persistent
      underemployment, unemployment, or job mismatch, including analysis of
      duration, job quality, and relevance of employment relative to training
      received, while recognizing differences in institutional mission and learner
      pathways;

      (c) Analysis of occupations with high vacancy rates despite the presence
      of available graduates, including barriers to employment such as skills
      gaps, geographic mismatch, credential misalignment, wage conditions, or
      regulatory constraints; and

      (d) Assessment of emerging and evolving occupations insufficiently
      addressed by existing curricula or training regulations, particularly in
      priority and fast-changing sectors, including digital, green, health, creative,
      and advanced industrial fields.

The results of the National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessment shall be
formally documented, disseminated, and utilized by CHED, TESDA, and DOLE
to inform curriculum review and enhancement, program approval and



                                                                                  11
rationalization, quality assurance and accreditation decisions, funding and
scholarship priorities, and targeted workforce development interventions.

Provided, that the use of assessment results shall support developmental and
corrective action rather than punitive measures, and shall be applied with due
regard to institutional autonomy, academic freedom, and regional and sectoral
context.

Provided further, that the appropriate agencies shall issue guidelines governing
the methodology, frequency, data sources, and reporting of the National
Education-to Employment Gap Assessment, consistent with the objectives of this
Act and applicable data privacy and security laws.

SEC. 9. Mandatory Tracer Studies and Employer Feedback Integration — All
public and private higher education institutions and technical-vocational
education and training providers shall be required to conduct regular and
systematic graduate tracer studies and to solicit structured employer feedback as
integral components of their respective quality assurance and continuous
improvement processes.

Graduate tracer studies shall be designed to generate reliable and actionable data
on employment status, job relevance, skills utilization, job quality, and career
progression of graduates within a defined period after program completion. Such
studies shall likewise consider employment stability, sectoral alignment,
geographic placement, and pathways to further education, training, or
entrepreneurship, subject to applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws.

Structured employer feedback mechanisms shall be established to assess graduate
preparedness, workplace adaptability, technical and transversal competencies,
work ethics, and capacity for continuous learning, as observed in actual
employment settings. Employer feedback may be gathered through surveys,
                                                                                 12
interviews, industry consultations, or other appropriate instruments, and shall be
conducted in a manner that is representative, systematic, and responsive to
sectoral and regional contexts.

The findings of tracer studies and employer feedback shall be formally
documented and submitted to the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) or
the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), as
applicable, in accordance with guidelines to be issued by the respective agencies.
Such findings shall be meaningfully integrated into curriculum review, program
redesign, teaching and learning strategies, faculty development, and other
program improvement initiatives, rather than treated as mere compliance
submissions.

Provided, that tracer study and employer feedback requirements shall be
calibrated according to institutional typology, program nature, and scale of
operations, and shall not impose disproportionate administrative burden,
particularly on small or developing institutions.

Provided further, that CHED and TESDA shall utilize aggregated tracer study
and employer feedback data to inform quality assurance standards, accreditation
processes, and policy development, consistent with the objectives of this Act.

SEC. 10. Curriculum-to-Occupation Mapping — The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA) shall require covered institutions to undertake and maintain
curriculum-to occupation mapping as part of program development, approval,
review, and quality assurance processes. Such mapping shall demonstrate a clear,
coherent, and evidence-based alignment between program learning outcomes and
identified occupations, job roles, or competency standards relevant to the labor
market.

                                                                                 13
Curriculum-to-occupation mapping shall identify the specific competencies,
skills, and learning outcomes developed by each program and their
correspondence to recognized occupational standards, industry-validated
competencies, or qualifications frameworks, as may be applicable. Mapping may
also reflect multiple occupational pathways, career progression options, and
opportunities for further education or skills upgrading, consistent with the nature
and objectives of the program.

Programs that are unable to demonstrate reasonable occupational alignment, as
determined in accordance with guidelines to be issued by CHED or TESDA, shall
be subject to review, redesign, consolidation, or phase-out, as appropriate. Such
review shall be undertaken in a developmental manner, with the primary
objective of improving relevance and outcomes rather than imposing punitive
measures.

Provided, that any decision to redesign, consolidate, or phase out a program shall
be implemented with due regard for learner protection, including reasonable
transition arrangements, teach-out plans, credit transfer or bridging options, and
clear communication to affected students and stakeholders.

Provided finally, that CHED and TESDA shall issue the necessary guidelines and
standards to operationalize curriculum to-occupation mapping, consistent with
the objectives of this Act and applicable quality assurance frameworks.

SEC. 11. Continuous Program Review and Curriculum Enhancement —
CHED and TESDA shall require all covered academic and training programs to
undergo regular, systematic, and outcomes-oriented program review at least
every three years for the purpose of ensuring continued relevance, quality, and
alignment with labor market needs and national development priorities.



                                                                                14
Such program review shall be an integral component of quality assurance and
accreditation processes and shall be undertaken in a developmental manner,
aimed at strengthening program effectiveness and learner outcomes. Reviews
shall be conducted in accordance with guidelines to be issued by CHED and
TESDA, taking into account institutional typology, program nature, and sectoral
context.

At a minimum, program review shall include the following components:

      (a) Assessment of labor market demand, informed by labor market
      intelligence, employment and workforce data, and demand signals
      provided or validated by the Department of Labor and Employment
      (DOLE), including sectoral, regional, and occupational trends;

      (b) Updating and enhancement of curricula to reflect technological
      change, evolving occupational standards, industry practices, and emerging
      competencies, including digital, green, and transversal skills, where
      applicable;

      (c) Strengthening of applied, experiential, and work-integrated learning,
      including     apprenticeships,   internships,   enterprise-based   training,
      simulation, or other authentic learning modalities appropriate to the
      program and sector; and

      (d) Evaluation of graduate outcomes and employer feedback, including
      findings from tracer studies, employer surveys, and other evidence of
      program effectiveness and workforce relevance.

Programs operating in rapidly evolving or high-growth sectors, including but not
limited to digital technologies, health and care services, advanced manufacturing,



                                                                               15
agriculture and agri-technology, and green industries, shall be subject to shorter
and more adaptive review cycles, as may be determined by CHED or TESDA.

Provided, that program review and curriculum enhancement processes shall be
implemented with due regard for academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and
learner protection, and shall prioritize continuous improvement over punitive
action.

Provided further, that CHED and TESDA shall issue the necessary implementing
guidelines to ensure consistency, transparency, and effectiveness in the conduct
of program reviews under this section.

SEC. 12. Strengthening School-to-Work Transition Mechanisms — The
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in coordination with the Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), shall strengthen, expand, and institutionalize
school-to-work transition mechanisms to ensure smoother, faster, and more
inclusive movement of learners from education and training into productive
employment, entrepreneurship, or further skills development.

Such mechanisms shall include, but shall not be limited to, work-based learning
arrangements, apprenticeships, internships, enterprise-based training, career
guidance and counseling, job placement and referral services, and the recognition
of prior learning, experience, and competencies, as may be appropriate to
program nature and sectoral requirements. These mechanisms shall be designed
to enhance employability, workplace readiness, and adaptability, and to reduce
friction and delay in the transition from school to work.

CHED and TESDA shall ensure that school-to-work transition mechanisms are
systematically embedded in program design and delivery and are aligned with


                                                                               16
labor market demand and employer participation, with support from DOLE’s
employment facilitation and labor market services.

Priority in the implementation of such mechanisms shall be accorded to first-time
jobseekers, displaced and transitioning workers, and learners from disadvantaged
or marginalized backgrounds, including those from low-income households,
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, and other vulnerable sectors, in
order to promote equitable access to employment opportunities and inclusive
workforce participation.

Provided, that the strengthening of school-to-work transition mechanisms shall
be undertaken with due regard for learner protection, quality assurance, and fair
labor standards.

Provided further, that CHED, TESDA, and DOLE shall issue the necessary
guidelines and coordination arrangements to operationalize this section,
consistent with the objectives of this Act.

SEC. 13. Rapid Curriculum Adjustment for High-Mismatch Sectors — CHED
and TESDA shall establish and operationalize accelerated and adaptive
mechanisms for curriculum adjustment in sectors exhibiting persistent or
emerging education-to-employment mismatches, skills shortages, or rapid
occupational change.

Such mechanisms shall be designed to enable timely and evidence-based
responses to labor market signals and may include, as appropriate, expedited
curriculum and program approvals, pilot or provisional program offerings,
modularized, stackable, or competency-based course structures, and flexible or
blended modes of delivery, consistent with the nature of the program and the
needs of learners and employers.


                                                                              17
Accelerated curriculum adjustment mechanisms shall be informed by labor
market intelligence, skills gap analyses, employer feedback, and the findings of
the National Education-to-Employment Gap Assessment and shall prioritize
sectors critical to national and regional development, including those
experiencing sustained vacancies, technological disruption, or emerging
workforce demand.

Provided, that the implementation of such accelerated mechanisms shall not
compromise academic standards, quality assurance requirements, or learner
protection, and shall be subject to clearly defined minimum standards, monitoring
arrangements, and sunset or review provisions.

Provided further, that pilot or provisional offerings undertaken pursuant to this
section shall be subject to evaluation and shall be scaled, modified, or
discontinued based on demonstrated effectiveness, workforce outcomes, and
quality benchmarks.

SEC. 14. Rationalization of Low-Outcome Programs — CHED and TESDA
shall systematically identify and monitor academic and training programs that
exhibit consistently poor employment outcomes, persistent underemployment, or
significant job mismatch, as evidenced by tracer studies, labor market data,
employer feedback, and other relevant quality assurance indicators.

Upon such identification, CHED and TESDA may, in accordance with guidelines
to be issued, recommend or require appropriate corrective or developmental
measures, which may include, as warranted by the circumstances, program
redesign or enhancement, adjustment or reduction of student intake, consolidation
with related or complementary programs, transformation into alternative learning
or skills pathways, or the gradual phase-out of the program.



                                                                              18
Any such action shall be undertaken in a developmental and evidence-based
manner, with the primary objective of improving relevance, quality, and
outcomes rather than imposing punitive sanctions. Institutions shall be afforded
due process, reasonable timelines for compliance, and access to technical
assistance or capacity-building support, where appropriate.

Provided, that in all cases, affected learners shall be adequately protected,
including through approved teach-out arrangements, credit transfer or bridging
options, articulation to related programs, or other reasonable transition
mechanisms, to ensure that learners are able to complete their studies or transfer
without undue disadvantage.

SEC. 15. Rationalization of Low-Outcome Programs — All covered higher
education institutions shall demonstrate clear and measurable alignment with
local and regional labor market needs as an integral component of program
design, delivery, and quality assurance. Such alignment shall be reflected in the
development of regionally responsive programs, sustained employer and industry
partnerships, and learning approaches that are embedded in the economic, social,
and developmental context of the communities they serve.

Higher education institutions shall engage with local employers, industry groups,
professional associations, local government units, and other relevant stakeholders
to ensure that educational and training offerings respond to regional workforce
demand, priority industries, and development plans, including those identified in
regional development councils, local investment and employment plans, and
sectoral roadmaps.

Local and regional labor market alignment may be demonstrated through, but
shall not be limited to, employer-supported curricula, localized occupational
pathways, community-embedded and work-integrated learning, applied research

                                                                               19
addressing regional challenges, and placement or employment outcomes within
the region, where appropriate.

The measures herein shall be implemented to address geographic skills mismatch,
mitigate regional concentrations of underemployment, and promote balanced and
inclusive regional development, including the strengthening of provincial and
rural economies, the generation of quality employment opportunities beyond
major urban centers, and the retention and effective utilization of skilled workers
within their regions of origin or training.

Provided, that the application of regional alignment requirements shall be
contextualized and proportionate, with due regard for institutional mission,
academic freedom, and learner mobility, and shall not be construed to restrict or
limit the right of graduates to seek employment or pursue further opportunities
beyond their region.

SEC. 16. Accreditation Reform and Harmonization — CHED shall reform and
modernize higher education accreditation policies, and TESDA shall refine and
enhance training regulations and program registration systems, as well as support
the establishment of external accreditation mechanisms, in order to support an
outcomes-based, workforce-responsive, and differentiated quality assurance
regime across the education and training continuum.

Such reforms shall ensure that quality assurance frameworks are responsive to
labor market demand, adaptive to technological and occupational change, and
capable of accommodating diverse institutional missions, program modalities,
and learner pathways. Accreditation and program registration processes shall be
calibrated to promote continuous improvement, innovation, and relevance, rather
than mere regulatory compliance.



                                                                                20
In implementing this section, employability outcomes, graduate workforce
readiness, and meaningful industry engagement shall carry substantial and
demonstrable weight in accreditation and program evaluation, alongside
established academic and institutional standards. At the same time, such
weighting shall be applied in a manner that preserves academic rigor, integrity,
and public trust, and safeguards the quality, credibility, and social value of higher
education and technical-vocational training.

SEC. 17. Industry and Labor Participation — CHED and TESDA shall
institutionalize formal and sustained mechanisms for the participation of industry,
employers, professional associations, and labor sector representatives in
curriculum review, program development, and quality assurance processes, in an
advisory capacity and in a manner consistent with established principles of
academic governance.

Such participation shall be designed to provide timely and relevant input on
workforce needs, occupational standards, emerging skills, and workplace
realities, and may include representation in advisory councils, consultative
bodies, technical working groups, or other appropriate platforms, as determined
by CHED or TESDA.

Participation by industry and labor representatives shall be governed by clear
conflict of-interest safeguards, including, but not limited to, the following:

      (a) Disclosure of any direct or indirect financial, proprietary, or
      organizational interests that may reasonably be perceived to influence
      recommendations or advice;

      (b) Prohibition against the use of advisory roles to secure preferential
      treatment, exclusive arrangements, or undue commercial advantage for any
      individual entity or group;
                                                                                  21
      (c) Recusal from deliberations or recommendations where a material
      conflict of interest exists; and

      (d) Balanced representation to prevent dominance by any single sector,
      firm, or interest group.

Provided, that industry and labor participation shall complement, and not
supplant, the authority of educational institutions over academic decisions, and
shall not undermine academic freedom, institutional autonomy, or the integrity of
curricular and quality assurance processes.

Provided further, that CHED and TESDA shall issue the necessary guidelines to
operationalize this section, including standards for disclosure, recusal,
composition, and transparency, ensuring that advisory participation remains
credible, impartial, and aligned with the objectives of this Act.

SEC. 18. Labor Market Intelligence and Data Integration — CHED, TESDA,
and DOLE shall strengthen, institutionalize, and integrate labor market
intelligence and data systems to support evidence-based education and workforce
planning, quality assurance, and policy development under this Act.

For this purpose, DOLE shall serve as the primary source and coordinating
agency for labor market intelligence, including employment statistics,
occupational demand and supply data, skills shortages and surpluses, vacancy
trends, wage information, and projections of emerging and declining occupations.
Such data shall be generated, validated, and regularly updated using reliable
methodologies, employer inputs, and administrative records, consistent with
applicable data privacy and security laws.

CHED and TESDA shall systematically utilize labor market intelligence provided
or validated by DOLE to inform curriculum design and review, program approval

                                                                              22
and rationalization, quality assurance and accreditation processes, funding and
scholarship prioritization, and the identification of priority sectors and regions for
intervention. Labor market data shall likewise guide decisions relating to program
expansion, consolidation, transformation, or phase-out, as appropriate.

The agencies shall establish interoperable data-sharing mechanisms to enable
timely, accurate, and actionable exchange of information among education,
training, and employment systems. Such mechanisms may include shared
databases, dashboards, standardized indicators, and reporting protocols, subject
to safeguards on data privacy, confidentiality, and responsible use.

Labor market intelligence shall be disaggregated, where feasible, by sector,
occupation, region, gender, age group, and other relevant variables, in order to
support targeted, inclusive, and regionally responsive policy interventions, and to
address skills mismatch, underemployment, and inequities in workforce
participation.

Provided, that labor market intelligence shall be used as a strategic planning and
improvement tool, and not as the sole or mechanical basis for academic decision
making and shall be interpreted with due regard to institutional mission, learner
pathways, and long-term human development goals.

Provided further, that CHED, TESDA, and DOLE shall issue joint or harmonized
guidelines governing data standards, sharing arrangements, frequency of
reporting, and the use of labor market intelligence for education and training
alignment.

Provided finally, that the integration of labor market intelligence under this
section shall strengthen transparency, accountability, and responsiveness across
the education-to employment continuum, consistent with the objectives of this
Act.
                                                                                   23
SEC. 19. Accountability for Education-to-Employment Outcomes — CHED
and TESDA shall integrate education-to-employment outcome indicators into
institutional and program-level quality assurance systems as an essential
component of continuous improvement, strategic planning, and evidence-based
policy guidance.

Such outcome indicators may include, as appropriate, graduate employment and
underemployment rates, job relevance and skills utilization, career progression,
employer feedback, workforce readiness, and alignment with labor market
demand. The selection, interpretation, and application of such indicators shall be
guided by methodological rigor, data reliability, and contextual relevance.

Education-to-employment outcome indicators shall be utilized primarily for
developmental and improvement-oriented purposes, including the identification
of strengths and gaps, curriculum enhancement, program redesign, capacity-
building interventions, and informed decision-making at the institutional and
system levels. They shall not be applied in a mechanistic or punitive manner, nor
used as the sole basis for sanctions, funding withdrawal, or adverse regulatory
action.

SEC. 20. Annual Education-to-Employment Alignment Report — The
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) shall jointly prepare and submit to Congress an Annual
Education-to Employment Alignment Report, which shall serve as a principal
accountability and oversight instrument under this Act.

The Report shall summarize, among others, identified education-to-employment
gaps, priority skills mismatches, and regional or sectoral disparities, as well as
the policies, programs, and corrective actions undertaken by the concerned

                                                                               24
agencies in response thereto. It shall likewise present trends in education and
workforce outcomes, including employability, underemployment, skills
utilization, and labor market demand, based on available data and analysis.

The Annual Education-to-Employment Alignment Report shall further include
recommended legislative, regulatory, or budgetary measures necessary to
strengthen alignment between education and workforce development, address
emerging challenges, and support priority sectors and regions. The Report shall
be submitted in such form and within such period as may be prescribed by
Congress, and shall be made accessible to the public, subject to applicable laws
on data privacy and confidentiality.

SEC. 21. Capacity Building and Institutional Support — The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), in coordination with the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), shall provide and institutionalize capacity-building
programs, technical assistance, and appropriate incentives to support the effective
implementation of workforce-responsive quality assurance systems and
curriculum reform initiatives under this Act.

Such support shall include, but not be limited to, training and professional
development for faculty, trainers, administrators, and quality assurance
personnel, technical guidance on curriculum redesign and outcomes-based
education, assistance in the use of labor market intelligence and data analytics,
and support for the development of industry partnerships and work-integrated
learning arrangements.

Incentives may be provided, subject to existing laws and available resources, to
encourage innovation, exemplary practice, and sustained improvement in
