                                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        More than a decade after the enactment of the Batas Kasambahay, many domestic
workers are still outside the social protection systems that the law promised them. A 2019
government survey1 revealed that 83% of the 1.4 million registered domestic workers
were not covered by any social security benefit, while 2% shouldered the full cost of their
entire social benefit contributions without employer’s share.2 The survey further showed
that 79.2% were not enrolled in the SSS, 80.7% had no PhilHealth benefits, and 87.6% were
not covered by Pag-IBIG benefits.3

       This is not new. As early as 2014, the Department of Labor and Employment
reported that out of an estimated two million Filipino domestic workers, only around
120,000 were registered with the SSS, 60,000 with PhilHealth, and 25,000 with Pag-IBIG.4

      Compliance with the statutory duty of household employers to register, report,
and remit contributions for SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG coverage remains inadequate
and uneven due to various reasons, including lack of awareness, confusion over
procedures, financial difficulty, or the persistent informality of household employment.

       The proposed Kasambahay CARE (Contribution Amnesty and Relief for
Employers) Act seeks to address this compliance gap by establishing a one-time penalty
condonation program for household employers with unpaid, deficient, or late
contributions to the SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG Fund arising from the employment of
kasambahays.

        By waiving penalties that may otherwise remain uncollected, this measure
facilitates the collection of principal contributions, the updating of membership records,

         DEFICIENT, OR LATE SOCIAL SECURITY SYSTEM, PHILIPPINE HEALTH
       INSURANCE CORPORATION, AND HOME DEVELOPMENT MUTUAL FUND
      CONTRIBUTIONS OF HOUSEHOLD EMPLOYERS UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO.
     10361, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “DOMESTIC WORKERS ACT” OR “BATAS
                    KASAMBAHAY,” AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

            Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Kasambahay CARE
 2   (Contribution Amnesty and Relief for Employers) Act.”

 5

16
17          (d) Kasambahay refers to a domestic worker as defined under RA No. 10361 and its
18              implementing rules and regulations (IRR); and
19
20          (e) Penalties refer to all penalties, interests, surcharges, fines, and similar monetary
21              charges imposed by the concerned agencies by reason of the late, deficient, or

 4           Section 3. Social Benefits Penalty Condonation Program. – There is hereby
 5   established a one-time penalty condonation program for household employers of
 6   kasambahays who have failed to register, report, or remit, in whole or in part,
 7   contributions due to the concerned agencies, pursuant to RA No. 10361.
 8
 9           A covered household employer may, within six (6) months from the effectivity of
10   the implementing rules and regulations of this Act provided under Section 14 hereof, pay
11   the full amount of unpaid principal contributions or submit a proposal to pay the same in
12   installments within a period not exceeding twenty-four (24) months from approval of the
13   proposal.
14
15          Upon full payment of the principal contributions or faithful compliance with an
16   approved installment plan, the applicable penalties arising from the late, deficient, or non-
17   remittance of such contributions shall be condoned.
18
19         The condonation shall not apply to the principal amount of unpaid contributions,
20   which shall remain due and demandable.
21
22          Section 4. Coverage. – This Act shall cover household employers who, at any time
23   from 4 June 2013 until the effectivity of this Act, failed to register, report, or remit, in whole
24   or in part, contributions due by reason of the employment of a kasambahay under
25   Republic Act No. 10361 and its IRR.
26
27          A household employer may avail of the condonation program only once. Such
28   availment may cover all unpaid, deficient, or late contributions, and the corresponding
29   penalties, interests, surcharges, fines, or similar monetary charges, arising from the
30   employment of one or more kasambahays by the same household employer during the
31   covered period.
32
33          The following household employers may avail of the condonation program:
34
35          (a) Those not yet registered with the appropriate registration system of the
36              concerned agencies, provided that they register within the availment period
37              prescribed under this Act;
38
39          (b) Those with pending civil or administrative collection cases involving unpaid
40              contributions, penalties, interests, surcharges, fines, or similar monetary
41              charges; and
42
43          (c) Those who, before the effectivity of this Act, have paid the principal
44              contributions due but have not paid the corresponding penalties, interests,

 3          This Act shall apply only to delinquencies arising from the employment of a
 4   kasambahay. It shall not apply to delinquencies arising from commercial, business,
 5   professional, or non-household employment.
 6
 7           Section 5. Exclusions. – No condonation shall be granted in cases involving fraud,
 8   falsification, misrepresentation, misappropriation, or willful evasion, without prejudice
 9   to the authority of the concerned agencies, prosecutors, or courts to determine liability
10   under applicable laws.
11
12          No condonation shall be granted with respect to amounts deducted or withheld
13   from the compensation of the kasambahay but not remitted to the concerned agency. Such
14   amounts shall be immediately remitted and shall not be subject to installment payment,
15   without prejudice to the imposition of civil, administrative, or criminal liability under
16   applicable laws.
17
18          Section 6. Application. – To avail of the condonation program, the household
19   employer shall submit an application to the concerned agency through the Kasambahay
20   Unified Registration System (KURS) and comply with the requirements of this Act and its
21   IRR.
22
23           Unregistered household employers shall likewise register with the appropriate
24   registration system of the concerned agencies within the availment period prescribed
25   under this Act.
26
27         The concerned agencies shall adopt simplified, coordinated, and accessible
28   procedures for registration, assessment, payment, and monitoring of compliance by
29   household employers.
30
31          Section 7. Installment Payment. – A household employer who is unable to pay the
32   full amount of unpaid principal contributions within the availment period may submit a
33   proposal to pay the same in installments within a period not exceeding twenty-four (24)
34   months from approval of the proposal.
35
36          The concerned agencies shall approve or disapprove the installment proposal in
37   accordance with the standards and procedures prescribed in the implementing rules and
38   regulations.
39
40         The approved installment plan shall specify the total principal contributions due,
41   the amount and schedule of amortizations, the conditions for continued availment of the
42   condonation, and the consequences of default.
43
44          Section 8. Effect of Default. – Failure to pay the principal contributions within the

 1   availment period, or default in the payment of any installment under an approved
 2   proposal, shall result in the cancellation of the condonation.

 4          In such case, the household employer shall be liable for the full amount of unpaid

 8
 9          The implementing rules and regulations may prescribe a reasonable cure period

11   with the approved installment plan.
12
13          Section 9. Pending Cases. – Availment of the condonation program may be
14   considered a ground for the suspension, compromise, or termination of civil or
15   administrative collection proceedings, subject to full payment of the principal
16   contributions or faithful compliance with an approved installment plan.
17
18          Nothing in this Act shall be construed to extinguish criminal liability arising from
19   fraud, falsification, misrepresentation, misappropriation, willful evasion, or other
20   criminal acts, unless otherwise allowed by applicable law and approved by the competent
21   authority.
22
23           Section 10. Protection of Kasambahays. – The failure, refusal, or default of the
24   household employer to register, report, or remit contributions shall not prejudice any
25   right, remedy, benefit, or entitlement available to the kasambahay under RA No. 10361,
26   RA No. 11199, RA No. 9679, RA No. 7875, as amended, RA No. 11223, and their respective
27   implementing rules and regulations.
28
29         The concerned agencies shall adopt mechanisms to ensure that a kasambahay is
30   not deprived of benefits or entitlements solely by reason of the household employer’s
31   noncompliance, subject to the conditions and procedures prescribed by law.
32
33         Section 11. No Refund. – Penalties, interests, surcharges, fines, or similar monetary
34   charges already paid before the effectivity of this Act shall not be refunded.
35
36          Section 12. Continuing Compliance. – A household employer whose application
37   has been approved shall thereafter comply with all registration, reporting, and remittance
38   requirements under applicable laws, rules, and regulations. Any subsequent delinquency
39   shall not be eligible for condonation under this Act.
40
41         Section 13. Report to Congress. – Within six (6) months from the expiration of the
42   availment period, the concerned agencies shall submit to Congress a joint report on the
43   implementation of this Act.
