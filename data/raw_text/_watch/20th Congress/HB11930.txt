                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1, Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that the
goals of the national economy are a more equitable distribution of opportunities,
income, and wealth, a sustained increase in the amount of goods and services
produced by the nation for the benefit of the people, and an expanding
productivity as the key to raising the quality of life for all, especially the
underprivileged. Complementing this constitutional mandate, Section 5, Article
XIII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution recognizes the rights of farmers,
farmworkers, cooperatives, and independent farmers' organizations to participate
in the planning, organization, and management of agricultural development and
directs the State to provide them with adequate financial, production, marketing,
technology, research, and other support services. In fulfillment of these
constitutional mandates, this measure seeks to establish the Agricultural Value-
Chain Cluster Development Program, which institutionalizes Agricultural Value-
Chain Clusters (AVCCs) as geographically integrated agricultural ecosystems
that integrate production, processing, storage, logistics, and market distribution
under a unified governance framework. The proposed measure recognizes that
agricultural modernization extends beyond increasing farm productivity; it
likewise requires efficient institutional coordination, integrated infrastructure,
and coordinated value-chain governance that enable agricultural producers to
participate competitively in domestic and international markets.

       Agriculture remains fundamental to the Philippine economy, contributing
significantly to food security, rural employment, and economic development.
However, despite sustained government interventions, the sector continues to
face persistent structural constraints arising from fragmented production systems,
inadequate post-harvest facilities, weak logistics infrastructure, limited cold-
chain capacity, and poor market integration. These inefficiencies contribute to
substantial post-harvest losses, increased transportation and transaction costs,
unstable farmgate prices, and reduced income opportunities for farmers and
fisherfolk. The Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 identifies agricultural
transformation through a whole-of-value-chain approach as a key strategy for
improving productivity, strengthening food security, increasing farmers' incomes,
and promoting inclusive economic growth by investing in logistics systems, post-
harvest infrastructure, market connectivity, and institutional coordination.1
       Recent local research further demonstrates the need for a more integrated
governance framework for agricultural value chains. A study by the Philippine
Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) found that although the Agriculture and
Fisheries Modernization Act (AFMA) has significantly advanced agricultural
modernization, implementation gaps remain in logistics systems, infrastructure
development, institutional coordination, and value-chain integration, limiting the
competitiveness of Philippine agriculture.2 Likewise, another PIDS study
concluded that Philippine agriculture has yet to fully ascend the value-added
ladder due to weak forward and backward linkages among production,
processing, storage, logistics, and marketing systems, recommending stronger
value-chain governance and integrated cluster-based development to improve
farmers' productivity and market participation.3
      International evidence likewise supports the establishment of integrated
agricultural value-chain clusters. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation
and Development (OECD) emphasizes that improving agricultural
competitiveness requires coordinated investments in infrastructure, logistics,

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Agricultural
Value-Chain Cluster Development Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State, consistent with Article XII and Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution, to
promote agricultural modernization, strengthen food security, improve farmers'
productivity and competitiveness, and enhance rural economic development
through integrated agricultural value chains.

      Towards this end, the State shall establish Agricultural Value-Chain
Clusters that integrate agricultural production, processing, storage, logistics, and
market distribution within strategically identified agricultural areas. The State
shall encourage inter-local government cooperation, public-private partnerships,
and farmer participation to improve supply chain efficiency, reduce post-harvest
losses, increase value addition, and ensure resilient agricultural systems.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

      (a) Agricultural Value-Chain Cluster (AVCC) refers to a designated
      contiguous geographic area where agricultural production, processing,
      storage, logistics, and marketing infrastructure are planned, developed, and
      managed as an integrated agricultural ecosystem.

      (b) Cluster Management Body (CMB) refers to the inter-agency governing
      body organized under this Act to oversee the planning, coordination,
      implementation, and administration of an Agricultural Value-Chain
      Cluster.

      (c) Cluster Development Plan refers to the comprehensive development
      framework prepared for every designated Agricultural Value-Chain
      Cluster.

      (d) Inter-LGU Coordination refers to the institutional collaboration among
      two (2) or more local government units for the planning, financing,
      operation, and management of Agricultural Value-Chain Clusters.

      (e) Shared Service Facilities refer to common agricultural infrastructure
      such as processing centers, cold storage facilities, warehouses, packaging
      facilities, laboratories, and logistics hubs available for shared use by
      participating stakeholders.

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the Agricultural Value-Chain Cluster
Development Program. - There is hereby established the Agricultural Value-
Chain Cluster Development Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program,

                                                                                2
which shall serve as the national framework for the planning, establishment,
development, management, and monitoring of Agricultural Value-Chain Clusters
throughout the country.

      The Program shall promote integrated agricultural development through
coordinated investments in production, processing, storage, logistics, and market
connectivity.

      SEC. 5. Designation of Agricultural Value-Chain Clusters. — The
Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of Trade
and Industry (DTI), the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev),
and concerned Local Government Units, shall identify and designate Agricultural
Value-Chain Clusters. The designation shall consider:

      (a) concentration of agricultural production;

      (b) existing or potential agro-industrial activities;

      (c) availability of transport and logistics infrastructure;

      (d) opportunities for inter-LGU cooperation;

      (e) climate resilience and disaster risk considerations; and

      (f) consistency with national and regional development plans.

      Where appropriate, Agricultural Value-Chain Clusters may be established
within or across Strategic Agriculture and Fisheries Development Zones
(SAFDZs) established under Republic Act No. 8435, otherwise known as the
“Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act of 1997” (AFMA).

      SEC. 6. Coverage of the Program. — The Program shall support the
establishment and development of:
                                                                               3
      (a) production zones;

      (b) shared processing facilities;

      (c) cold-chain and warehouse facilities;

      (d) logistics and transport systems;

      (e) distribution and trading centers;

      (f) digital supply chain management systems;

      (g) agricultural innovation and technology centers; and

      (h) other infrastructure necessary for integrated agricultural value-chain
      development.

      SEC. 7. Creation of Cluster Management Bodies. — A Cluster
Management Body (CMB) shall be organized for every designated Agricultural
Value-Chain Cluster to oversee the implementation of this Act. The CMB shall
serve as the principal coordinating body responsible for the planning, operation,
monitoring, and administration of the Cluster.

      SEC. 8. Composition of the Cluster Management Body. — The Cluster
Management Body shall be composed of:

      (a) A Cluster Administrator designated by the Secretary of Agriculture,
      who shall serve as Chairperson;

      (b) The Provincial Governor or Governors concerned;

      (c) The City and Municipal Mayors of participating LGUs;

      (d) One (1) representative each from the DTI, DPWH, DILG, and
      DEPDev;

      (e) Two (2) representatives from accredited agricultural cooperatives
      operating within the Cluster;

      (f) One (1) representative from the private logistics or agribusiness sector;
      and

      (g) One (1) representative from a state university or college with
      recognized agricultural expertise.

      SEC. 9. Powers and Functions of the Cluster Management Body. — The
Cluster Management Body shall:

      (a) prepare and implement the Cluster Development Plan;

      (b) coordinate national government agencies and participating LGUs;

      (c) supervise the operation of shared agricultural infrastructure;

      (d) establish logistics coordination mechanisms;

      (e) facilitate investments and public-private partnerships;

      (f) recommend infrastructure priorities;

      (g) monitor cluster performance and supply chain efficiency;

      (h) ensure compliance with quality and food safety standards;

      (i) maintain stakeholder coordination; and

      (j) submit annual reports to the Department of Agriculture.

      SEC. 10. Inter-Local Government Cooperation. — Participating Local
Government Units are authorized to enter into inter-local cooperation agreements
consistent with Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local

Government Code of 1991.” for the planning, financing, construction, operation,
maintenance, and management of Agricultural Value-Chain Clusters.

      Such    agreements      may     include   cost-sharing   arrangements,   joint
investments, shared infrastructure management, and other cooperative
mechanisms authorized by law.

      SEC. 11. Cooperative and Private Sector Participation. — Accredited
farmer cooperatives, associations, agribusiness enterprises, and private logistics
providers may participate in the Program.

      Participating entities shall be given priority access to shared service
facilities, government technical assistance, institutional market linkage programs,
and other support services provided under existing laws and regulations.

      SEC. 12. Agricultural Value-Chain Cluster Information System. — The
Department of Agriculture shall establish and maintain an Agricultural Value-
Chain Cluster Information System containing information on:

      (a) designated clusters;

      (b) production areas;

      (c) processing facilities;

      (d) logistics infrastructure;

      (e) storage capacities;

      (f) participating cooperatives;

      (g) investment opportunities; and

      (h) performance indicators necessary for planning and policy formulation.

      SEC. 13. Monitoring and Evaluation. — The Department of Agriculture
shall establish a monitoring and evaluation system to assess the effectiveness of
each Agricultural Value-Chain Cluster. Performance indicators shall include:

      (a) reduction in post-harvest losses;

      (b) improvement in farmers' income;

      (c) logistics efficiency;

      (d) market accessibility;

      (e) investment generation;

      (f) infrastructure utilization; and

      (g) supply chain resilience.

      The Department of Agriculture shall submit to Congress an annual report
on the implementation of this Act, including the performance of each Agricultural
Value-Chain Cluster, within sixty (60) days after the end of each calendar year.

      SEC. 14. Implementing Agencies. — The Department of Agriculture shall
be the lead implementing agency. It shall coordinate with the Department of
Trade and Industry, Department of the Interior and Local Government,
Department of Public Works and Highways, National Economic and
Development Authority, Cooperative Development Authority, Department of
Science and Technology, state universities and colleges, and other concerned
government agencies.

      SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture, in
consultation with the DTI, DILG, DEPDev, concerned government agencies,
local government units, farmer organizations, cooperatives, and private

stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 16. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Agriculture.

      Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 17. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 19. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
