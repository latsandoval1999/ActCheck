                                                EXPLANATORY NOTE


It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the welfare of all terrestrial, aquatic and
marine animals in the Philippines by supervising and regulating the establishment and
operations of all facilities utilized for breeding, maintaining, keeping, treating or training of
all animals either as objects of trade or as household pets. 1

Thirteen years have passed since the amendment to the Republic Act No. 8485 has been
enacted, and still, nefarious animal abuse, cruelty, maltreatment, neglect, and abandonment
continue to sprawl on the streets in broad daylight and even in the privacy of some dwellings.
This can be gleaned from the records of the Philippine Animal Welfare Society (PAWS) and
the Compassion and Responsibility for Animals (CARA) Welfare Philippines where over
7,000 cases of animal cruelty between 2010 and 2017 and 3,000 in the year 2020 were
reported.2

On April 8, 2026, A video of a group of high school students allegedly abusing stray cats in
Concepcion, Tarlac has gone viral and has raised alarm among netizens, local officials, and
animal welfare groups. In the video, two boys can be seen putting the cats inside a sack and
then appearing to shake them. A girl can also be seen repeatedly kicking the sack, while one
of the boys was hitting the cat with a slipper.3

On May 3, 2026, authorities are investigating the death of thirteen dogs in San Miguel, Iloilo
whose caretakers believe were poisoned. The family who took care of the dogs found a blue
substance mixed into the animals’ food when they found the dogs dead on April 29, 2026.
The investigator said it is possible that the culprit may just be a resident in the area because

    AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION AND WELFARE OF
 ANIMALS, REPEALING FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8485, AS
 AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10631, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “THE
                ANIMAL WELFARE ACT OF 1998”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Expanded Animal
Welfare and Protection Act of 2025” or the “Animal Life Advocacy and Guardianship
Act (ALAGA)”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It shall be the policy of the State to provide
special protection to animals from all forms of cruelty, neglect, abuse, exploitation, and
conditions detrimental to their welfare, taking into full consideration their species,
ecological role, physiological needs, and sentence.
       In pursuit of this policy, the State shall promote the humane and ethical treatment
of animals based on the internationally recognized Five Domains of Animal Welfare,
namely:
       (a) Adequate nutrition;
       (b) Appropriate physical environment;
       (c) Optimal health and veterinary care;
       (d) Opportunities to express natural behaviors; and
       (e) The promotion of a positive mental state free from fear, distress, and suffering.
       For this purpose, the State shall:
       (a) Fully implement the protections guaranteed under the Universal Declaration
on Animal Welfare, the World Organization for Animal Health (WOAH) animal welfare
standards, and other relevant international instruments and treaties promoting animal
welfare, environment sustainability, and biodiversity conservation;
        (b) Consider international commitments such as the Convention on Biological
Diversity, the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna
and Flora (CITES), and other global best practices on the ethical treatment, conservation,
and sustainable management of animals, in the implementation of its treaty obligations
and of this Act.
       (c) Recognize animals as sentient beings capable of feeling pain, distress, comfort,
and pleasure, and as such entitled to humane treatment and protection from degradation,
maltreatment, exploitation, and neglect;
        (d) Consider as paramount the welfare of animals in all settings where their
survival, safety, or well-being is at risk, and adopt policies that prevent unnecessary
suffering and promote their physical and psychological well-being in accordance with the
Five Domains;
       (e) Take all feasible measures to prevent and penalize acts of cruelty, neglect, or
inhumane treatment of animals, and ensure the effective implementation, monitoring, and
enforcement of this Act and related laws;
        (f) Address the root causes of animal cruelty and welfare violations, including, but
not limited to, poverty, lack of education, irresponsible ownership, habitat destruction,
illegal trade, and exploitative industries that threaten the Five Domains of Animal
Welfare;
        (g) Recognize its primary role in providing effective protection, rescue, and
rehabilitation to animals in situations of abuse, disaster, environmental degradation,
captivity, abandonment, or other threats to their welfare;
        (h) Fulfill its responsibility to pursue accountability and end impunity for
violations of animal welfare laws, especially in cases involving systemic or repeated
harm to animals;
       (i) Ensure the participation of key stakeholders, including civil society
organizations, animal welfare groups, veterinarians, Local Government Units (LGUs),
indigenous communities, and the general public, in the design, implementation, and
evaluation of policies, programs, and decisions concerning the care, protection, and
humane treatment of animals.
       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
        (a) Animal Control Facility refers to a government-operated or accredited
establishment responsible for the capture, impoundment, quarantine, and disposition of
stray, abandoned, or nuisance animals, in accordance with humane standards prescribed
by the National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW);
        (b) Animal Facility refers to any premises, establishment, or institution where
animals are housed, bred, treated, trained, or used for commercial, research, or
educational purposes, including veterinary clinics, pet shops, zoos, laboratories, and
similar establishments;
        (c) Animal Shelter refers to a registered or accredited establishment, operated by a
government entity or private organization, that provides temporary refuge, care, and
rehabilitation for stray, abandoned, neglected, or surrendered animals, with the goal of
rehoming or responsible adoption;
        (d) Animal Welfare Partner (AWP) refers to a registered non-government
organization, academic institution, or private entity accredited by the National One
Health and Animal Welfare Council or local government units (LGUs) to assist in rescue,
rehabilitation, education, enforcement, and community outreach in support of the
objectives of this Act; and
       (e) Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) Program refers to humane population
control method for free-roaming dogs and cats involving their capture, sterilization,
vaccination against rabies and other diseases, and return to their original location, as part
of a public health and animal welfare management strategy.
       SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Commission on Animal Welfare. – The
National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW) is hereby created as a quasi-judicial
agency attached to the Department of Agriculture. It shall serve as the central authority
with original and exclusive jurisdiction over all matters pertaining to animal welfare –
including enforcement, licensing, issuance of welfare declarations, regulation of facilities,
and adjudication of infractions.
       The Commission shall possess fiscal and administrative autonomy, have its own
budget line item under the Department of Agriculture and shall not be subsumed under
any existing bureau or office.
      The functions of the Committee on Animal Welfare (CAW) with respect to policy
making, as provided for in Republic Act No. 8485, as amended, are hereby transferred to
the Commission.
       SEC. 5. Composition of the National Commission on Animal Welfare. – The
Secretary of Agriculture shall serve as Chairperson of the Commission in an ex-officio
capacity. The President shall appoint the other six (6) members of the Commission,
which shall include:
        (a) Two (2) scientists specializing in the welfare, humane treatment, and
protection of wildlife, farmed animals, companion animals, or working animals;
       (b) One (1) lawyer engaged in the enforcement of animal welfare and protection
of laws; and
       (c) Three (3) animal welfare advocates, or representatives of Non-Government
Organizations involved in the welfare, humane treatment, and protection of animals.
       SEC. 6. Secretariat. – The Commission shall be supported by a Secretariat
headed by an Executive Director with the rank of Undersecretary, assisted by two Deputy
Directors: one for Operations & Services, and one for Administration & Finance. The
Secretariat shall provide technical and administrative support to the Commission. Within
three (3) months from the effectivity of this Act, the Office of the Secretary of
Agriculture shall finalize the organizational plan for the Commission.
        SEC. 7. Reconstruction of the Existing Committee on Animal Welfare into the
National Animal Care Advisory Council. – To ensure institutional continuity, technical
consistency, and the preservation of under the Animal Welfare Act of 1998, as amended,
shall be reconstituted to serve in an advisory capacity as the National Animal Care
Advisory Council (NACAC), which shall function as a consultative body to the
Commission in the formulation of standards, regulations, and national policy directions.
The advisory role of the NACAC shall be consultative in nature, and shall not diminish
the powers vested in the Commission under this Act. For this purpose, the Commission
shall: convene regular consultations with the NACAC; ensure stakeholder representation
from animal welfare organizations, veterinary professional groups, academe, local
government leagues, and industry sectors; and adopt internal rules governing the
composition and functions of the NACAC.
        SEC. 8. Fiscal Autonomy and Funding. – The Commission shall be provided with
an annual budget sufficient for its operations, enforcement activities, staffing, training,
public education, and program implementation. Its initial funding shall be taken from the
appropriations of the Department of Agriculture, and subsequently included as a separate
line item in the General Appropriations Act (GAA).
       The Commission shall also manage the Animal Welfare Fund, composed of:
       (a) Fines and damages collected under this Act;
       (b) Fees from registration, inspection, and permits;
       (c) Grants, donations, and endowments from local and international sources; and
       (d) Legislative appropriations.
       All such funds shall be used exclusively for implementing and strengthening
animal welfare initiatives under this Act.
        In order to provide the necessary funds to cover the expenses of the Commission
in the performance of its mandate under this Act, aside from the budget allocation, ten
percent (10%) of the registration fees of all animal facilities shall hereby be collected as a
special fund to be used by the Commission to construct holding facilities for large animal
confiscations.
      SEC. 9. Power and Functions. – The Commission shall exercise the following
powers and functions:
       I. General Powers
        (a) Enter into contracts, joint venture agreements or understanding, public-private
partnerships, and memoranda of agreement or understanding, either domestic or foreign,
under such terms and conditions as the Commission may deem proper and reasonable
subject to existing laws;
       (b) Receive, take, and hold by bequest, device, gift, purchase or lease, either
absolutely or in trust for any of its purposes from foreign and domestic sources, any asset,
grant or property, real or personal, subject to such limitations provided under existing
laws and regulations;
        (c) Discharge all responsibilities of government that may arise from treaties,
agreements and other commitments on animal welfare to be extended through bilateral or
multilateral loans and/or assistance programs;
        (d) Determine, fix, and collect reasonable amounts to be charged as fees and
charges necessary for the effective implementation of all laws, rules and regulations
enforced by the Commission and impose reasonable fines and penalties for violation
thereof: Provided, however, that all income generated from fees, fines, charges, and other
collections shall be deposited with the National Treasury as income of the general fund;
       (e) Perform such other related functions as may be mandated by law.
       II. Rule-Making Power
       (a) Enforce all laws relating to animal welfare that are not expressly or impliedly
repealed or modified by this Act, including all existing rules and regulations, which shall
continue to be in full force and effect, until otherwise modified or amended by the
Commission;
       (b) Prescribe rules and regulations not otherwise inconsistent with this Act,
including standards, policies, and guidelines related to animal treatment, transport,
research, and facility operations;
      (c) Prohibit the use of devices, food, drugs, equipment or other means found to be
harmful to animal health or welfare;
        (d) Review the rules, policies and practices of government agencies, divisions,
and instrumentalities to ensure compliance with this Act, applicable rules and regulations,
and other laws related to animal welfare, as well as recommend amendments and
revisions to said rules, policies, and practices;
       III. Licensing & Registration
        (a) Register and regulate all animal facilities and handlers in accordance with
existing animal welfare laws and the latest scientific studies, as well as regulate the
design and construction of animal facilities;
        (b) Issue, suspend or revoke permits and licenses, and to impose or collect fees for
the issuance of such licenses and permits, to persons required to obtain the same;
       (c) Regulate and supervise the use of animals in sporting events, television and
movie productions, and all other activities involving the display or exhibition of animals,
to ensure the promotion of their health and welfare;
        (d) Impose fines, penalties and forfeitures for violations of the rules and
regulations promulgated or issued by the Commission; and
      (e) Adjudicate welfare violations committed by animal facilities and handlers,
impose sanctions, and order closures or confiscations.
       IV. Animal Welfare Enforcement & Education
       (a) Prosecute cases of animal abuse, maltreatment, and cruelty, as defined in this
Act;
       (b) Provide guidance to individuals, organizations and LGUs who wish to file
criminal cases involving animal abuse, maltreatment, and cruelty;
       (c) Create or fund the creation of holding facilities for live animals confiscated in
animal cruelty cases until the offenders are convicted or acquitted;
       (d) Provide technical assistance and training to LGUs, non-government
organizations, and law enforcement agencies;
       (e) Conduct public education campaigns on animal welfare and humane care;
        (f) Administer incentives, recognitions, and support programs for animal-friendly
practices, including certifications for humane animal husbandry; and
       (g) Collect data, monitor cases, and report to stakeholders.
       V. Quasi-Judicial Functions
        (a) Motu Proprio or upon petition of any interested party, after notice and hearing,
modify, suspend or revoke any permit or license for failure to comply with and/or for
violation of any provision of law or any order, rules or regulations issued thereunder:
Provided, That the Commission may for good cause and without notice and hearing,
order the temporary suspension of such permit or license whenever such step, in the
judgment of the Commission, shall be necessary and for the best interest of animal
welfare: Provided, further, that except in cases where summary actions are taken, in the
settlement of dispute and other problems connected with the registry of facilities or any
administrative aspect, the Commission shall act as a body, and all hearings and
investigations before it shall be governed by the rules of procedures it shall promulgate;
       (b) For the purposes of carrying out the provisions of this Act, the Commission
shall have the power and authority to summon parties, issue subpoena or require the
production of such books, papers, contracts, records, or statement of accounts as may be
material to a just determination of any matter under investigation; and
        (c) Designate any of its members or officers to conduct hearings and
investigations on any matter pending before it.
       SEC. 10. Regulation of Animal Ownership & Animal Facilities. – The
Commission shall establish regulations on responsible animal ownership, including the
maximum number of animals that an individual, household, or organization may keep,
based on their capacity to provide adequate care, shelter, and veterinary attention.
        Any person, group, or organization maintaining a number of animals beyond the
prescribed limit, or operating an animal shelter, sanctuary, or rescue facility, must secure
the appropriate permits and accreditation from the Commission.
        Entities or individuals soliciting or accepting donations for the care of animals
shall be required to present proof of valid shelter or rescue operation permits, subject to
inspection and compliance with national animal welfare standards. Non-compliance shall
be grounds for the imposition of penalties, suspension of operations, or revocation of
permits.
         SEC. 11. Regulation of City Pounds and Public Access to Data. – City pounds
shall be classified as animal control facilities, distinct from animal shelters. The
following items shall be included in the regular budget of every pound or animal control
facility maintained by an LGU:
       (a) Adequate food to feed each impounded animal at least once daily;
      (b) Reasonable and necessary expenses for the care, maintenance, and welfare of
impounded animals;
       (c) Immediate treatment for animals with open wounds or injuries;
        (d) Humane euthanasia for animals that are severely injured, terminally ill, or
diagnosed by a licensed veterinarian with a highly communicable disease: Provided, that,
animals recommended for euthanasia shall be reported to the public at least eight (8)
hours prior to the procedure through social media or any other means readily-accessible
by the public, accompanied by a video showing the animal’s condition as assessed by a
veterinarian.
        All impounded animals shall be spayed or neutered prior to adoption or release to
their owners. If the animal is deemed unfit for surgery, the owner or adopter shall sign a
written undertaking to have the animal spayed or neutered within three (3) months from
the date of release and shall submit proof of compliance from a licensed veterinarian. If
the animal remains unfit for surgery after said period, the owner must secure a
certification from a licensed veterinarian indicating when the procedure may be safely
performed. Failure of the owner to comply shall subject the owner to reasonable fines for
every month of delay. Administrators and staff of pounds who release impounded
animals without spay-neuter compliance shall also be deemed in violation of this Act.
        Failure to maintain and produce records of animal impoundment, adoption, or
euthanasia shall likewise constitute a violation of the Animal Welfare Act and shall
subject the responsible staff or administrator to administrative sanctions.
        For the purposes of this Act, failure to spay or neuter previously impounded
animals prior to release, or failure to maintain or submit spay-neuter records certified by
a licensed veterinarian, shall be considered an animal welfare violation.
       Likewise, failure to rescue or release caged animals during a calamity, when such
can be done without harm to oneself, shall constitute neglect on the part of the pound or
animal control facility.
       City pounds and other animal control facilities shall be required to maintain
publicly accessible records relates to impounded and admitted animals, animals for
adoption, adopted animals, animals due for euthanasia, deaths, and disease outbreaks, in
accordance with the Freedom of Information Act, and other legislation related to
government transparency.
      Pounds and other animal control facilities shall be open to the public on Saturdays,
Sundays, and holidays, and until at least 7:00 PM on weekdays.
         SEC. 12. Regulation of Animal Experimentation. – Elementary schools shall be
strictly prohibited from conducting any form of animal experimentation or dissention,
whether for instructional, demonstrative, or research purposes. All secondary and tertiary
educational institutions that conduct research involving animals shall comply with
internationally accepted humane standards and practices for the care and use of
laboratory animals. All such experiments shall be reviewed, approved, and monitored by
and Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC), to be established by every
educational institution conducting animal research, to ensure, among others, adherence to
the 3Rs Principle: Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement.
        The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the Department of Education
(DepEd), in coordination with the National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW),
shall jointly issue guidelines and monitoring mechanisms for compliance with this
provision.
        Any educational institution found to be conducting animal experimentation in
violation of this Section shall be subject to administrative sanctions, including the
suspension or revocation of its research permit or institutional accreditation, without
prejudice to the filing of appropriate penalties under this Act.
        SEC. 13. Localization of Animal Welfare Efforts. – Local Government Units shall
play a primary role in the implementation and enforcement of animal welfare laws,
regulations, and standard within their jurisdiction, consistent with provisions of this Act,
and within their powers under the Local Government Code, and the principles of local
autonomy, including but not limited to:
       (a) Implementation of Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) programs;
       (b) Deployment and supervision Animal Welfare Officers (AWOs) trained and
accredited by the National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW);
        (c) Integration of animal welfare into barangay-level disaster preparedness and
public health programs;
    (d) Establishment of a Municipal or City Animal Welfare Office (MAWO or
CAWO) under the office of the local chief executive; and
      (e) Operate or maintain animal impounding facilities or shelters consistent with
humane standards.
        National policies and standards shall serve as the minimum threshold, as
embodied in this Act and other relevant legislation. LGUs may adopt ordinances or
policies providing greater protection or more specific implementation measures in
accordance with their local needs and capacities. Provinces, cities, and municipalities
may enter into cooperation agreements to jointly fund, operate, or maintain shared animal
welfare services such as shelters, mobile veterinary units, or rescue teams.
        SEC. 14. Partnership with Civil Society and the Private Sector. – The State shall
actively promote collaboration among government agencies, veterinary associations,
academic institutions, civil society organizations, and private entities for the joint
implementation of research, programs, and projects related to animal welfare. The
Commission shall maintain and regularly update a registry of accredited Animal Welfare
Partners (AWPs) which may be:
         (a) Deputized to assist in animal rescue, rehabilitation, and law enforcement
activities;
       (b) Authorized to operate animal shelters or sanctuaries, subject to compliance
with national standards and periodic inspection;
       (c) Authorized to engage in public education, training, and community outreach
on animal welfare; and
       (d) Accredited to participate in the monitoring, documentation, and reporting of
animal welfare violations.
       All partnerships between government agencies or LGUs and civil society or
private entities shall be governed by a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) or other
formal instrument defining the roles, operational scope, accountability measures, and
resource-sharing arrangements of the parties involved.
       Accredited AWPs shall be invited to participate in policy consultations, local
animal welfare council meetings, and program evaluation activities to strengthen
transparency, collaboration, and policy coherence in animal welfare governance.
       The National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW) shall prescribe the
standards and accreditation procedures for Animal Welfare Partners (AWPs), and shall
monitor compliance in coordination with relevant agencies and LGUs.
        SEC. 15. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any person to torture any
animal, to neglect to provide adequate care, subject any dog or horse to dogfights or
horse fights, kill or cause or procure to be tortured or deprived of adequate care,
sustenance or shelter, or maltreat or use the same in research or experiments not
expressly authorized by the National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW). The
killing of any animal other than cattle, pigs, goats, sheep, poultry, rabbits, carabaos and
horses is likewise hereby declared unlawful except in the following instances:
       (a) When it is done as part of the religious rituals of an established religion or sect
or a ritual required by tribal or ethnic custom of indigenous cultural communities;
however, leaders shall keep records in cooperation with the Committee on Animal
Welfare;
       (b) When the pet animal is afflicted with an incurable communicable disease as
determined and certified by a duly licensed veterinarian;
       (c) When the killing is deemed necessary to put an end to the misery suffered by
the animal as determined and certified by a duly licensed veterinarian;
         (d) When it is done to prevent an imminent danger to the life or limb of a human
being;
         (e) When done for the purpose of animal population control;
       (f) When the animal is killed after it has been used in authorized research of
experiments; and
        (g) Any other ground analogous to the foregoing as determined and certified
licensed veterinarian. In all the above-mentioned cases, including those of cattle, pigs,
goats, sheep, poultry, rabbits, carabaos and horses, the killing of the animals shall be
done through humane procedures at all times. For this purpose, humane procedures shall
mean the use of the most scientific methods available as may be determined and approve
by the committee. Only those procedures approved by the Committee shall be used in the
killing of animals.
        SEC. 16. Prohibition of Abandonment. – It shall be unlawful for any person who
has custody to an animal to abandon the animal. If any person being the owner or having
charge or control of any animal shall without reasonable cause or excuse abandon it,
whether permanently or not, without providing for the care of that animal, such act shall
constitute maltreatment under Section 18. If the animal is left in circumstances likely to
cause the animal any unnecessary suffering, or if this abandonment results in the death of
the animal, the person liable shall suffer the maximum penalty. Abandonment means the
relinquishment of all right, title, claim, or possession of the animal with the intention of
not reclaiming its ownership or possession.
        SEC. 17. Prohibition of Animal Fighting. – All forms of animal fighting,
including but not limited to dog fighting, horse fighting, bullfighting, cockfighting, and
similar practices, are hereby prohibited. Such acts are deemed inherently cruel and
exploitative and are often associated with illegal gambling, violence, and other social
evils. Violators shall be prosecuted under this Act and other applicable laws.
        SEC. 18. Prohibition of Animal Poisoning. – It shall be unlawful to administer or
use poison or any toxic substance for the purpose of killing animals in public spaces.
Such acts are considered environmentally hazardous and pose serious risks to non-target
wildlife and public health. No poison or chemical agent intended to kill animals shall be
used, distributed, or applied in any commercial or open public area without a special
permit issued by the Commission, in coordination with the Department of Environment
and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Department of Health (DOH).
        SEC. 19. Habitat Protection. – It shall be the duty of every person to protect the
natural habitat of the wildlife. The destruction of said habitat shall be considered as a
form of cruelty to animals and its preservation is a way of protecting the animals.
       SEC. 20. Penalties. – Any person who subjects any animal to cruelty,
maltreatment or neglect shall, upon conviction by final judgment, be punished by
imprisonment and/or fine, as indicated in the following graduated scale:
        (a) Causing Death of an Animal. Minimum imprisonment of ten (10) years and
six (6) months and one (1) day, and a fine of five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00),
to a maximum of up to fifteen (15) years, and fine not exceeding one million pesos
(P1,000,000.00), if the animal subjected to cruelty, maltreatment, or neglect dies;
       (b) Causing Grave Injury to an Animal. Minimum imprisonment of six (6) years
and six (6) months and one (1) day, and a fine of five hundred thousand pesos
(P500,000.00), to a maximum of ten (10) years and six (6) months, and a fine not
exceeding one million pesos (P1,000,000.00), if the animal subjected to cruelty,
maltreatment or neglect survives but is severely injured with loss of its natural faculty to
survive on its own and needing human intervention to sustain its life; and
        (c) Causing Injury not Classified as Grave. Imprisonment of three (3) years to
five (5) years and a fine not exceeding two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00), for
subjecting any animal to cruelty, maltreatment or neglect but without causing its death or
incapacitating it to survive on its own.
        (d) Inaction in relation to Reports of Animal Cruelty, Maltreatment, or Neglect.
Minimum imprisonment of one (1) year and six (6) months and one (1) day, and a fine of
one hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00), to a maximum of up to two (2) years, and
fine not exceeding two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00);
       In any of the foregoing situations, the offender shall also pay for the veterinary
treatment of the injured or killed animal, including shelter treatment costs incurred by the
owner, animal welfare group, or the impounding agency. The offender shall suffer
subsidiary imprisonment in case of insolvency and the inability to pay the fine.
        If the violation is committed by a juridical person, the officer responsible thereof
shall suffer the maximum fine and imprisonment. If the violation is committed by an
alien, he or she shall be immediately be deported after the service of sentence without any
further proceeding.
       The foregoing penalties shall also apply for any other violation of this Act,
depending upon the effect or result of the act or omission as defined in the immediately
preceding sections.
        Any person found guilty of engaging in the dog meat trade, producing or
distributing content depicting animal cruelty such as crush videos, or committing cruelty
for commercial gain, shall be penalized with a fine of not less than three million pesos
(P3,000,000.00), and imprisonment of not less than ten (10) years, in addition to the
penalties provided under this Section.
        The maximum penalty applicable shall automatically be imposed if committed by:
(a) a syndicate; (b) an offender who makes business, or derives commercial gain, out of
cruelty to an animal; (c) a public officer or employee; or (d) where at least two (2)
animals are involved.
        The penalty of disqualification, suspension, or removal from office, at the
discretion of the court, shall also be imposed in case the offender is a public officer or
employee.
        SEC. 21. Presumption and Reporting of Pet Abuse, Maltreatment, and Neglect. –
Pet neglect shall be presumed committed by the legal owner or occupant of the property
where an animal is found in a state of neglect. Barangay officials, landlords, neighbors,
and relatives who have knowledge of such neglect shall have the duty to report the

lawful intervention of a person acting to protect the welfare of an animal in distress.
       SEC. 22. Animal Welfare Enforcement Officers. – The National Commission on
Animal Welfare (NCAW) shall have the authority to deputize animal welfare
enforcement officers from government agencies, local government units (LGUs), non-
government organizations, civil society groups, community organizations, and qualified
volunteers who have undergone the necessary training and accreditation as prescribed by
the Commission.
       The Philippine National Police (PNP), the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI),
and other law enforcement agencies shall designate and assign animal welfare
enforcement officers in coordination with the Commission to ensure the effective
enforcement of this Act.
        Deputized animal welfare enforcement officers shall have the authority, subject to
existing laws, rules, and regulations on arrest and detention, to:
       (a) inspect premises and investigate reports of animal cruelty, neglect, or
maltreatment;
       (b) Seize and rescue animals subjected to cruelty, abuse, or illegal trade; and
       (c) Apprehend violators of this Act and assist in the filing of appropriate cases.
       The Commission shall:
       (a) Prescribe the qualifications, training requirements, and accreditation process
for animal welfare enforcement officers;
       (b) Establish a system for supervision, monitoring, and accountability of
deputized officers; and
       (c) Maintain a registry of accredited animal welfare enforcement officers
nationwide.
        SEC. 23. Construction. – This Act shall be liberally construed in favor of the
protection and welfare of animals.
       SEC. 24. Information Dissemination. – The Philippine Information Agency, in
coordination with the National Commission on Animal Welfare, DepEd, DILG, LGUs,
and other relevant government agencies shall ensure proper and adequate information
dissemination of the contents of, and the rights and duties contained in, this Act to
pertinent entities in all provinces, cities, municipalities and barangays.
        SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within twelve (12) months from
the effectivity of this Act, the National Commission on Animal Welfare (NCAW), in
coordination with relevant government agencies and accredited animal welfare
organizations, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.
        SEC. 26. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the remaining provisions
not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
       SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, department or
memorandum orders and other administrative issuances or parts thereof which are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby modified, superseded, or repealed
accordingly.
        SEC 28. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
