 “AN ACT REGULATING DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES TO PROTECT DEBTORS
          FROM ABUSES, HARASSMENT, UNFAIR TREATMENT, AND
                        MISREPRESENTATION”

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

The increasing availability of consumer credit, online lending platforms, and digital financial services
has improved access to financing for many Filipinos. However, alongside these developments has
emerged a growing number of complaints involving abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection
practices. Many debtors have experienced harassment, intimidation, threats, public shaming,
unauthorized disclosure of personal information, repeated and excessive communications, and other
collection methods that violate their dignity, privacy, and fundamental rights.

While creditors have a legitimate right to collect lawful debts, such right must be exercised in a
manner consistent with the principles of fairness, transparency, accountability, and respect for human
rights. Unscrupulous collection practices not only harm debtors and their families but also undermine
public trust in the financial system and the legitimate lending industry.

Despite existing regulations issued by government agencies, there remains a need for a
comprehensive statutory framework that clearly defines the rights and obligations of creditors, debt
collectors, and debtors, establishes standards for lawful collection activities, prohibits abusive and
misleading practices, protects personal information, and provides effective mechanisms for redress
and enforcement.

This measure seeks to establish a Fair Debt Collection Practices Act that sets clear rules governing
debt collection activities in the Philippines. It prohibits harassment, intimidation, misrepresentation,
and unfair collection methods; safeguards debtor privacy and confidential information; promotes
transparency through accounting requirements, issuance of receipts, and loan cost disclosures;
strengthens consumer protection in online lending platforms; and provides administrative, civil, and
regulatory remedies against violators.

Ultimately, the bill seeks to strike a fair balance between the right of creditors to recover legitimate
debts and the right of debtors to be treated with dignity, respect, and fairness under the law. By

contributes to a more accountable, transparent, and trustworthy financial services sector.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Fair Debt Collection Practices Act."

22   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall prohibit and eliminate the use of abusive, deceptive,
23   and unfair debt collection practices to protect debtors against abuse and harassment from unscrupulous lenders
24   and debt collectors. Consistent with Republic Act No. 11765 8 or the "Financial Products and Services
25   Consumer Protection Act," the State shall uphold the principles of fair treatment, transparency, and
26   accountability in debt collection.

27   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

28            (a) Debt Collector refers to any person (1) who uses any instrumentality or business, the principal
29   purpose of which is the collection of debts; or (2) who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or
30   indirectly, debts owed or asserted to be owed to another. It includes creditors who have in-house collectors
31   or employees/officers who, by reason of their employment/office, engage in debt collection for the former, or
32   any creditor who, in the process of collecting debts, uses any name other than one's own to give the impression
33   that a third person is collecting or attempting to collect such debt. It also includes a debt collection agency or
34   a third-party service provider engaged by a creditor to collect debts owed by another.

35           This definition shall not include:

36                    (i) A business that acquires a debt or debts through the seizure of accounts receivable under
37                    a security agreement;

38                    (ii) A corporation that acquires or merges with a business and thereby acquires the latter's
39                    accounts receivable;

40                    (iii) A person to whom the contract that gave rise to the debt was assigned for the purpose of
41                    financing the transaction;



                                                                                                                     3
 1                   (iv) Any officer or employee of the national or local government to the extent that collecting
 2                   or attempting to collect any debt is in the performance of their official duties;

 3                   (v) Any person serving or attempting to serve legal process on any other person in connection
 4                   with the judicial enforcement of any debt; and

 5                   (vi) Any attorney-at-law collecting a debt on behalf of a client.

 6            (b) Contact refers to any communication, whether in person, by telephone, mobile phone, facsimile,
 7   e-mail, automated call system, text messaging, voicemail, answering machine, or any other electronic/digital
 8   mode, including messages left for a debtor with another person, by voicemail, on an answering machine, or
 9   any other form of communication not specifically excluded. This definition shall not include communications
10   that the debtor expressly consented to or solicited in advance.

11           (c) Creditor refers to a person who has extended credit to a debtor, including credit in the form of a
12   sale on credit, a loan of money, or the provision of goods and services on credit. It includes a financing
13   company and a lending company.

14           (d) Debt refers to a legally enforceable monetary obligation owed by a debtor, including a purchase
15   on credit, accounts receivable, a loan of money, or the provision of goods or services.

16           (e) Debtor refers to an individual who owes a debt, personally or as the owner of a sole proprietorship,
17   a partner in a partnership, or an individual who has provided a personal guarantee.

18           (f) Express Consent refers to consent verifiable through writing, whether in electronic or printed form,
19   or audio recordings.

20           (g) Financing company refers to a corporation primarily organized to extend credit facilities to
21   consumers and to industrial, commercial, or agricultural enterprises, whether by direct lending; by discounting
22   or factoring commercial papers or accounts receivable; by buying and selling contracts, leases, chattel
23   mortgages, or other evidence of indebtedness; or by financial leasing of movable as well as immovable
24   property. It does not include banks, investment houses, savings and loan associations, insurance companies,
25   cooperatives, and other financial institutions organized and operating under other special laws.

26           (h) Lending company refers to a corporation engaged in granting loans from its own capital funds or
27   from funds sourced from not more than nineteen (19) persons. It does not include banking institutions,
28   investment houses, savings and loan associations, financing companies, pawnshops, insurance companies,
29   cooperatives, and other credit institutions already regulated by law. The term shall be synonymous with
30   lending investors.

31           (i) Online Lending Platform (OLP) refers to mobile lending applications, websites, and other financial
32   technology (FinTech)-enabled programs or systems where the services and products of financing companies
33   and lending companies are available.

34           (j) Representative means a person duly authorized by the debtor or creditor, as applicable, to contact
35   or be contacted in relation to the debt.

36   SECTION 4. Acquisition of Location Information. – Any creditor or debt collector communicating with any
37   person other than the debtor for the purpose of locating the latter shall ensure, prior to collection of personal
38   data, that the processing is specific for the declared purpose and is authorized by the debtor, or is otherwise
39   not protected under Republic Act No. 10173 or the "Data Privacy Act of 2012," or other applicable laws, and
40   that the acquisition is not otherwise prohibited by law;

41          They shall identify themselves, state that they are collecting, confirming, or correcting location
42   information concerning the debtor, and if requested, identify their employer.

                                                                                                                    4
 1           They shall not:

 2           (a) Access, use, or process the debtor's phone contact list and shall only communicate with the
 3   representative of the debtor as defined in this Act;

 4           (b) State any detail about the debt or loan contract, except the fact of its existence;

 5            (c) Communicate with any such person more than once unless the person requests it or unless such
 6   creditor or debt collector reasonably believes that the earlier response was erroneous or incomplete and that
 7   the person now has correct or complete location information;

 8           (d) Communicate by post card; and

 9            (e) Use any language or symbol on any envelope or in the contents of any communication indicating
10   that the debt collector is in the debt collection business or that the communication relates to the collection of
11   debt.

12            The foregoing notwithstanding, should a creditor or debt collector discover that, with regard to the
13   subject debt, the debtor is represented by an attorney whose name and address can be readily ascertained, such
14   creditor or debt collector shall be prohibited from communicating with any other person under this section,
15   unless the attorney fails to respond within a reasonable period of time to the communication from the debt
16   collector.

17             SECTION 5. Allowable Debt Collection Practices. – Creditors and debt collectors may use all
18   reasonable and legally permissible means to collect amounts due them under the loan agreement such as
19   undergoing mediation, arbitration, or other forms of alternative dispute resolution, or filing the appropriate
20   complaint before the proper court, among other reconciliatory or judicial means: Provided, That in the exercise
21   of their rights and performance of their duties, they observe good faith and reasonable conduct, and not engage
22   in unduly intrusive, abusive, unfair, and untoward conduct prohibited under this Act.

23            SECTION 6. Prohibition Against Harassment or Abuse. – Financing companies, lending companies,
24   and debt collectors shall not harass or engage in oppressive or abusive conduct in connection with the
25   collection of a debt. They shall not:

26           (a) Use or threaten violence or other criminal means on the person, reputation, or property of the
27   debtor or their family;

28          (b) Use insults, obscene or profane language that abuses or shames the debtor or their family, or which
29   amounts to a criminal act or offense under applicable laws;

30            (c) Disclose, publish, or post the names and other personal information of the debtor who allegedly
31   refuses to pay debts, except as may be allowed under Section 11 of this Act;

32           (d) Advertise for sale any debt to coerce payment;

33           (e) Call, repeatedly and incessantly, the debtor, their family, or any person other than the debtor or
34   engage any such person in telephone or mobile phone conversation, repeatedly or continuously, with intent to
35   annoy, abuse, or harass;

36          (f) Make telephone or mobile phone calls without meaningful disclosure of the caller's identity, except
37   where such disclosure is specifically prohibited under this Act;

38            (g) Send text blasts or indiscriminate messaging to a debtor's phone contact list for the purpose of
39   collecting an outstanding loan; and

40           (h) Other analogous circumstances.

 1            SECTION 7. False or Misleading Representations. – Creditors and debt collectors shall not use any
 2   false, deceptive, or misleading representation or means in connection with the collection of any debt. The
 3   following acts shall be considered violations of this section:

 4           (a) The unauthorized use of any badge, uniform, or a facsimile thereof;

 5            (b) The false representation on the following: (1) the character, amount, or legal status of any debt; or
 6   (2) any service rendered or compensation which may be lawfully received by any debt collector for the
 7   collection of the debt;

 8         (c) The false representation or implication that an individual is: (1) an attorney or that any
 9   communication is from an attorney; or (2) an employee or personnel of the government, or that any
10   communication is from such government agency or office;

11           (d) The representation or implication that non-payment of any debt will result in the arrest or
12   imprisonment of any person, or in the seizure, garnishment, attachment, or sale of any property or wages,
13   unless such non-payment is unlawful and the debt collector or creditor intends to take legal action;

14           (e) Threatening to take any action that cannot be legally taken;

15           (f) The false representation or implication that a sale, referral, or other transfer of interest in a debt
16   shall cause the debtor to:

17                    (1) Lose any claim or defense in relation to the payment of the debt; or

18                    (2) Become subject to any practice prohibited by this Act;

19          (g) The false representation or implication that the debtor committed any crime or other similar
20   conduct contrary to the provisions of this Act;

21            (h) Communicating or threatening to communicate to any person false credit information, including
22   the failure to communicate the fact that a debt is disputed;

23           (i) The use or distribution of any written communication which simulates, or is falsely represented to
24   be, a document authorized, issued, or approved by any court, official, or agency of the national or local
25   government, or which creates a false impression as to its source, authorization, or approval;

26           (j) The use of any false representation or deceptive means to collect or attempt to collect any debt, or
27   to obtain information concerning a debtor;

28            (k) The failure to clearly disclose in all communications that any information obtained will be used
29   for that purpose, except in instances where disclosure of such information is specifically prohibited in this
30   Act;

31           (l) The false representation or implication that accounts have been turned over to innocent purchasers
32   for value;

33           (m) The use of any business, company, or organization name other than the true name of the creditor
34   or debt collector's business, company, or organization;

35            (n) Giving any person, directly or indirectly, by implication or otherwise, any false or misleading
36   information in relation to the collection of debt, including references to the police, a law firm, prison, credit
37   history, court proceedings, or a lien or garnishment; and

38           (o) Any analogous circumstances.

 1           SECTION 8. Unfair Collection Practices. – Creditors and debt collectors shall not use unfair or
 2   unconscionable means to collect or attempt to collect any debt. The following acts shall be considered
 3   violations of this section:

 4           (a) The collection of any amount other than the principal obligation, including any interest, fee, charge,
 5   or incidental expense, unless such amount is expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or
 6   permitted by law;

 7           (b) The acceptance by a debt collector from any person of a check or other payment instrument
 8   postdated by more than five (5) days, unless such person is notified in writing of the debt collector's intent to
 9   deposit such check or instrument not less than three (3) working days prior to such deposit;

10          (c) Threatening to deposit any postdated check or instrument prior to the date on such check or
11   instrument;

12          (d) Causing communication fees or charges to be imposed on any person by concealing the true
13   purpose of the communication, including through collect telephone calls, telegrams, or similar means;

14            (e) Taking or threatening to take any non-judicial action to effect dispossession of property or to render
15   it inoperable if:

16                   (1) There is no present right to possession of the property claimed as collateral through an
17           enforceable security interest;

18                    (2) There is no present right to take possession of the property; or

19                    (3) The property is exempt by law from such dispossession or from being rendered inoperable;

20         (f) Using any language or symbol, other than the debt collector's address on any envelope when
21   communicating with a debtor by mail or by telegram; and

22          (g) Processing or disclosure of debtor's personal data without lawful basis or in a manner that is
23   unnecessary, inadequate, irrelevant, unsuitable, or excessive, in relation to the specified and declared purpose.

24           SECTION 9. Other Prohibited Acts and Practices. – No debt collector shall:

25           (a) Collect or attempt to collect money for a creditor except on the belief in good faith that the money
26   is due and owed by the debtor to the creditor;

27            (b) Fail to provide a contact number for the debtor to call when the debt collector uses an automated
28   call system to contact the debtor;

29           (c) Collect or attempt to collect a debt without providing the real name of the agent or representative
30   of the debt collector, the contact number, the registered name, and address of the debt collector;

31           (d) Make any arrangement with a debtor to accept, as final settlement, a sum of money, that is less
32   than the amount of the balance due and owing to a creditor without the prior express consent of the latter;

33           (e) Fail to provide a written report on the account status of any person with whom the debt collector
34   transacts;

35           (f) Make a call to the debtor or any person other than the debtor for the purpose of collecting or
36   attempting to collect a debt on any day outside the window of 6AM to 10PM unless the debtor has given
37   express consent or unless said hours are the only reasonable or convenient opportunities for contact;

38           (g) Directly or indirectly threaten or state an intention to proceed with any action for which the debt
39   collector does not have the prior express consent of the creditor or for which there is no lawful authority;

                                                                                                                      7
 1           (h) Continue to collect or attempt to collect money from, or to communicate with:

 2                   (1) A person who has informed the debt collector that he or she is not the debtor, unless the
 3           debt collector has taken all reasonable precautions to ensure that the person is in fact the debtor; or

 4                   (2) The debtor, after he or she has informed the debt collector, through verifiable means, that
 5           the debt is in dispute and that either the creditor or the debtor has taken the matter to court;

 6            (i) Contact the debtor's spouse, relative, neighbor, friend, acquaintance, or reference person indicated
 7   in the contract unless the contact is limited to the purpose of confirming or correcting the debtor's residential
 8   address or contact details;

 9           (j) Contact the reference person indicated in the contract after the loan has been approved. A reference
10   person is used solely for the purpose of loan approval;

11            (k) Contact the debtor's employer for any purpose other than to confirm the debtor's employment
12   status, business title, and the address of the business, in preparation for legal proceedings;

13           (l) Contact the debtor directly after the latter has notified the debt collector in writing to communicate
14   only with the debtor's representative, providing therein the current address and telephone number of such
15   representative for the purpose of:

16                    (1) Making reasonable arrangements to discuss the debt with the debt collector; and

17                    (2) Discussing the debt with the debt collector in accordance with such arrangements;

18           (m) Contact a debtor at his or her place of employment after the debtor has requested the debt collector
19   not to do so: Provided, That the debtor makes reasonable arrangements to discuss the debt with the debt
20   collector and actually discusses the debt in accordance with such arrangements;

21           (n) Communicate information about the debt or the existence of the debt with a person other than the
22   debtor, a guarantor of the debt, the debtor's representative, or the creditor, unless the debtor has expressly
23   consented to communicating such information to persons other than those enumerated herein;

24           (o) Refuse to provide sufficient information on the identity of the original and current creditor, as well
25   as the details of the debt, upon request of the debtor;

26           (p) Enter into or arrange automatic wage or salary assignments with a debtor or the employer of a
27   debtor without the debtor's consent;

28           (q) Cancel or alter a payment agreement with a debtor after the latter has complied with the terms of
29   the agreement and the debtor's financial circumstances have not changed materially, unless the debtor has
30   misrepresented his or her financial circumstances;

31           (r) Process debtor's contact list, in whatever form, in a manner that is unconstrained, excessive, and
32   disproportional to the specified and declared purpose; or

33           (s) Treat the debtor's representative as defined in this Act as a guarantor or a surety.

34           SECTION 10. Prohibited Agreements. – Any agreement entered into by the debtor and the debt
35   collector shall be deemed void if that agreement:

36           (a) Misrepresents the rights and powers of a person collecting or attempting to collect a debt;

37           (b) Misrepresents the obligations or legal liabilities of a debtor;

38           (c) Is misleading as to its true nature and purpose; or


                                                                                                                     8
 1           (d) Requires or results in the debtor’s waiver of any of his or her rights under this Act.

 2           SECTION 11. Confidentiality of Information. – Creditors and debt collectors shall keep the data on
 3   the debtor strictly confidential, except under the following circumstances:

 4          (a) Disclosure of information with the written consent of the debtor specific to the declared purpose,
 5   or when authorized under existing laws;

 6          (b) Release, submission, or exchange of customer information with other financial institutions, credit
 7   information bureaus, lenders, their subsidiaries and affiliates in accordance with relevant provisions of
 8   Republic Act No. 10173;

 9           (c) Upon order of a court of competent jurisdiction or any government office or agency authorized by
10   law;

11           (d) Disclosure by creditors to debt collectors, counsels, and other agents to enforce their rights against
12   the debtor; and

13           (e) Disclosure to third parties, such as insurance companies, solely to insure the creditor from default
14   of the debtor or other credit loss, or insuring the debtor from fraud of unauthorized charges.

15           In any case, creditors shall retain ownership and accountability for debtors' data handled by their debt
16   collectors, collection agents, or third-party service providers.

17            SECTION 12. Issuance of Receipts. – Every debt collector shall issue an acknowledgment receipt for
18   all cash transactions, payments made in person, or payments made by the debtor. Such acknowledgment
19   receipt shall contain the following information:

20           (a) The date the amount is collected or received;

21           (b) The name of the debtor;

22           (c) The name of the person on whose behalf the debt collector acts;

23           (d) The amount received from the debtor; and

24           (e) The signature of the debt collector.

25           SECTION 13. Settlement Agreement. – Once a debt collector reaches a settlement agreement with a
26   debtor and the amount in such agreement has been paid in full, the debt collector shall issue to the debtor an
27   invoice containing the following information:

28           (a) The amount paid;

29           (b) The name of the creditor or creditors;

30           (c) A statement that the amount received is in final settlement of the debt/s owed; and

31           (d) The signature of the debt collector.

32            SECTION 14. Request for Accounting. – Upon the request of the debtor, the debt collector shall
33   provide a complete accounting of all the details of the debt: Provided, That if such accounting is not in the
34   possession of the debt collector, the latter must request the creditor to provide the same: Provided, further,
35   That if the creditor fails to provide a complete accounting of the debt within thirty (30) days after receiving a
36   request therefor, the debt collector must:

37           (a) Inform the debtor in writing that it cannot provide the accounting and the reasons for the same;
38   and

                                                                                                                     9
 1           (b) Cease all collection activity for that account and not resume collection activity until the accounting
 2   is provided.

 3            A debtor may request a complete accounting only once every six (6) months, unless the debt collector
 4   fails to provide the complete accounting as requested: Provided, That in the case of short-term loans offered
 5   by creditors, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) may prescribe the appropriate periods for the
 6   provision of the accounting and the frequency of such requests by the debtor.

 7           SECTION 15. Loan Cost Transparency and Calculator. – All creditors utilizing OLP’s that are
 8   operating in the Philippines shall integrate within their application or digital platform a Loan Cost Calculator
 9   that:

10            (a) Allows prospective and existing debtors to compute, in real time, the total amount payable for any
11   debt or loan product offered or outstanding, including, but not limited to:

12                    (1) The principal amount;

13                    (2) The interest charges, expressed both as a rate and monetized amount;

14                    (3) All fees, penalties, charges, and other costs associated with the debt or loan contract; and

15                    (4) The schedule of amortization or payment breakdown over time.

16          (b) Must be accessible, clearly visible before the prospective debtor executes or signs any loan
17   agreement, and available at any time during the existence of the loan;

18            (c) Must dynamically update calculations when any changes in the debt or loan terms occur, including,
19   but not limited to, restructuring, refinancing, and imposition of additional fees; and

20           (d) Must present results in a user-friendly format, with clear, itemized breakdowns and plain language
21   explanations.

22           SECTION 16. Administrative Penalties. – The SEC shall issue rules and regulations for the
23   implementation of the provisions of this Act and for the imposition of reasonable administrative penalties for
24   violations thereof.

25            For this purpose, the SEC may prescribe and impose penalties such as monetary fines not exceeding
26   One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00); suspension of financing and lending activities; and/or revocation of the
27   erring creditor's Certificate of Authority to Operate as a Financing or Lending Company.

28           SECTION 17. Civil Liability. –

29           (a) Amount of Damages. - Any creditor or debt collector who violates any of the provisions of this
30   Act with respect to any person shall be liable to such person in an amount equal to the sum of:

31                    (1) The damages sustained by such person as a result of the violation;

32                   (2) In the case of any action by an individual, such additional damages as the court may allow,
33           but not exceeding Thirty thousand pesos (P30,000.00); or

34                  (3) In the case of a class action, such amount for each named plaintiff as could be recovered
35           under paragraphs (1) and (2), as well as such additional amount as the court may allow for all other
36           class members: Provided, That such additional damages shall not exceed Sixty thousand pesos
37           (P60,000.00) or one percent (1%) of the net worth of the creditor or debt collector, whichever is higher.

38            (b) Factors to be Considered by the Courts. – In determining the amount of liability under paragraph
39   (a) of this section, the courts shall consider, along with other relevant factors, the following:

                                                                                                                   10
 1                   (1) In any individual action under paragraph (a)(2) of this section, the frequency and
 2           persistence of non-compliance by the creditor or debt collector, the nature of such non-compliance,
 3           and the extent to which such non-compliance was intentional; and

 4                    (2) In any class action under paragraph (a)(3) of this section, the frequency and persistence of
 5           non-compliance by the creditor or debt collector, the nature of such non-compliance, the resources of
 6           the creditor or debt collector, the number of persons adversely affected, and the extent to which the
 7           creditor or debt collector's non-compliance was intentional.

 8            (c) Intent. – A creditor or debt collector may not be held liable in any action brought under this section
 9   if the creditor or debt collector shows by a preponderance of evidence that the violation was not intentional
10   and resulted from an error in good faith.

11           (d) Jurisdiction. – An action to enforce any liability under this Act may be brought in any court of
12   appropriate jurisdiction.

13            SECTION 18. Solidary Liability. – A creditor shall be responsible for ensuring that its debt collectors,
14   authorized representatives, or third-party service providers engaged in debt collection comply with this Act
15   and all applicable laws, rules, and regulations. The creditor shall be solidarily liable with such agents or service
16   providers for any violation of this Act committed in the course of debt collection.

17            SECTION 19. Debtor Complaints and Redress Mechanism. – Financing and lending companies shall
18   establish and maintain a customer service department, with actual human agents and not merely machines or
19   bots, who shall be responsible for promptly addressing questions and concerns of debtors and serve as an
20   internal complaint handling and redress mechanism that is accessible, transparent, and free of charge. The
21   SEC shall oversee compliance with this requirement and provide an external redress mechanism for escalation
22   of unresolved complaints, including an accessible online platform for reporting violations of this Act.

23           The SEC shall have a dedicated complaints desk to address violations of this Act and shall act on
24   complaints received through such desk within five (5) working days from receipt. Failure of the responsible
25   public official or employee to act on any complaint within the prescribed period shall constitute simple neglect
26   of duty, subject to the filing of the appropriate administrative action based on prevailing Civil Service
27   Commission rules and regulations: Provided, That in cases where the delay is due to force majeure or other
28   analogous circumstances, the prescribed period shall be suspended, and appropriate adjustments shall be
29   made: Provided, further, That in such cases of justifiable delay, the responsible public official or employee
30   shall notify the complainant of the reason for the delay and the adjusted period to act on the complaint.

31            SECTION 20. Information and Education Campaign. – The SEC shall conduct a continuing
32   information and education campaign to inform the general public of their rights under this Act. It shall inform
33   financial consumers on how to identify abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices and on how to
34   avail of their rights including requests for accounting or issuance of receipts, use of the loan cost calculator,
35   and other available redress mechanisms.

36           SECTION 21. Temporary Restraining Order. – In cases where the creditor or debt collector resorts
37   to the use of threat, intimidation, or commission of other crimes as a means of effecting debt collection, the
38   debtor may apply for a temporary restraining order (TRO) or injunction against such creditor or debt collector
39   before the appropriate courts. The application shall be supported by affidavits and other evidence to establish
40   the use of threat, intimidation, or commission of other crimes.

41          SECTION 22. Good Faith Requirement. – The protections under this Act shall only apply to debtors
42   who invoke them in good faith and shall not be available to those who invoke such protections to evade
43   payment of their obligation.

 1           SECTION 23. Interpretation. – In case of doubt, the provisions of this Act, including its
 2   implementing rules and regulations and any subsequent issuances of the implementing agencies, shall be
 3   construed in a manner that accords the highest respect for human dignity, human rights, and individual privacy.

 4           SECTION 24. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
 5   unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

 6           SECTION 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, presidential
 7   proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts thereof contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby
 8   repealed or modified accordingly.

 9           SECTION 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its publication
10   in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
