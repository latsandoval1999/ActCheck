                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Free and fair elections are the foundation of democracy. Our laws on campaign
spending were created to give candidates a fair chance to reach voters and prevent
elections from being dominated by money and political in luence. However, the
campaign spending limits set by law in 1991 have remained unchanged for more than
three decades, despite the rising costs of transportation, communication, printing,
media, and other lawful campaign activities. What may have been reasonable in 1991
is no longer realistic today.

Because these limits no longer re lect present economic conditions, candidates may
struggle to conduct legitimate campaigns within the outdated ceilings. This can
encourage underreporting, weaken transparency, and push campaign activities into
informal or unregulated channels. Instead of strengthening accountability, unrealistic
limits may unintentionally undermine public trust in the electoral process.

This bill seeks to update the allowable campaign spending limits and provide a fair
system for adjusting them over time based on in lation and of icial economic data. The
measure does not remove spending controls; rather, it ensures that the law remains

opportunity among candidates and preventing excessive in luence by wealth and
political machinery.

The measure also continues to encourage transparency by maintaining existing rules
that exempt properly reported campaign contributions from donor’s tax. Ultimately,
this bill aims to strengthen clean, honest, and accountable elections by ensuring that
campaign inance laws remain fair, realistic, and effective for both candidates and the
Filipino people.

Limits Indexation Act.”

SECTION 3. In lation Indexation; Publication of Adjusted Amounts. — Beginning
the next regular national and local elections after the effectivity of this Act, and every
three (3) years thereafter, the Commission on Elections (COMELEC) shall cause the
adjustment of the peso amounts stated in Section 13 of Republic Act No. 7166, as
amended, in accordance with the following rules:

   (a) The adjusted amounts shall be computed by multiplying each peso amount by
       the ratio of the latest available annual average Consumer Price Index (CPI), All
       Items, Philippines, as published by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), to
       the CPI baseline year corresponding to the year of the last adjustment;

   (b) The adjusted amounts shall be rounded off to the nearest centavo;

   (c) COMELEC’s function under this Section shall be ministerial. It shall, not later
       than one hundred eighty (180) days before the start of the of icial campaign
       period, publish the adjusted amounts in the Of icial Gazette and in at least two
       (2) newspapers of general circulation, and post the same in its of icial website.

       For this purpose, COMELEC may consult the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP),
       the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), and the PSA for
       technical inputs.

SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the Commission on Elections shall promulgate rules and regulations to
implement this Act.

SECTION 5. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, regulations, issuances, and parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied
accordingly.

SECTION 7. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
