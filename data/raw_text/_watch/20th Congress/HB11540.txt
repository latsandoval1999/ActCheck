                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Rice is a staple food of Filipino households and remains central to the
country’s food security, agricultural economy, and household welfare. Republic
Act No. 8178, as amended by Republic Act No. 11203, liberalized rice importation
and shifted the National Food Authority (NFA) away from its previous regulatory
and commercial functions toward maintaining an emergency rice buffer stock.1

      While rice tariffication was intended to improve market efficiency and
provide consumers access to competitively priced rice, subsequent experience
has underscored the continuing need for effective government capacity to
monitor the rice market, protect consumers from unreasonable price
movements, and safeguard the interests of domestic rice producers. The
proposed measure therefore seeks to strengthen the regulatory powers of the

ability to respond to supply disruptions and market abuses.2

       The measure recognizes that food security requires more than simply
ensuring the availability of imports. The proposed amendments strengthen the
government’s ability to register and inspect grain warehouses and other facilities,
collect and analyze market data, and respond to cornering, hoarding,
profiteering, artificial scarcity, and unwarranted increases in rice prices.3 These
powers are intended to give the government the tools necessary to identify and
address conduct that can undermine the normal operation of the rice market
and adversely affect consumers. The bill also provides for greater coordination
among the DA, NFA, Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Bureau of Customs
(BOC), local government units, and law-enforcement agencies, recognizing that
effective rice-market regulation requires timely information and coordinated
enforcement.

       At the same time, the bill seeks to strengthen the role of the government
in maintaining a reliable rice buffer stock and supporting domestic farmers. It
requires the NFA to source its buffer stock primarily from local farmers, farmer
cooperatives, and farmers’ associations, while allowing procurement in milled-
rice form when necessary and providing an order of preference that gives priority
to local agricultural institutions and facilities. The measure also establishes
more flexible mechanisms for the disposition of buffer stocks, including sales
through accredited retailers, government entities, KADIWA outlets, farmers and
farmer cooperatives, and public auction, subject to requirements of
transparency, competitiveness, accountability, and public monitoring. These
provisions are designed both to improve stock management and to ensure that
government procurement can provide a dependable market for local farmers
while preventing the unnecessary aging and wastage of public rice stocks.

       Finally, the measure seeks to provide farmers with greater protection
against excessively low palay prices while balancing this objective with consumer
welfare. It authorizes the DA to establish reasonable and flexible floor prices for
palay at the national, regional, and provincial levels, taking into account
production costs, prevailing market prices, reasonable farmer margins, and the
welfare of both farmers and consumers. This approach recognizes that a resilient
rice industry must protect both ends of the food-supply chain: consumers must

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Rice Industry and
Consumer Empowerment (RICE) Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to ensure food security and protect the welfare of both Filipino consumers and
rice farmers by maintaining an adequate and stable supply of rice, promoting
reasonable and stable prices, strengthening government capacity to regulate the
rice industry, preventing hoarding, profiteering, and other market abuses, and
ensuring that public interventions in the rice market are timely, transparent,
accountable, and responsive to the needs of the Filipino people, while providing
farmers with fair and sustainable returns for their produce.
