                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Article XIV of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides the right of all citizens
to quality education at all levels and to ensure that such education is accessible to all.
Public libraries and study hubs support the education of our learners through free,
equitable access to information and technology, and safe learning environments,
regardless of their socioeconomic status.

       Despite the passage of Republic Act No. 7743, or “An Act Providing for the
Establishment of Congressional, City and Municipal Libraries and Barangay Reading
Centers Throughout the Philippines, Appropriating the Necessary Funds Therefor and
for Other Purposes,” that mandates the establishment of congressional, city, and
municipal libraries, as well as barangay reading centers throughout the Philippines,
implementation remains significantly behind schedule.

        Data from the National Library of the Philippines (NLP) reveals a severe
implementation gap: a 2023 study reported that public libraries constitute only 3% of the
ideally prescribed statutory target1. Subsequent reporting highlights that out of 44,000
local government units, only 1,721 maintained NLP affiliation in 20242, a number that
rose only slightly to 1,754 by February 20263. These figures fall considerably short of
what would be expected given the country's population and geographic distribution.
Furthermore, many of these existing facilities operate during limited hours, with some
failing to offer extended evening or 24/7 access for learners who rely on library resources
after working or attending school during the day.

        The lack of 24/7 public libraries or learning hubs forces students and young
professionals to rely on commercial venues like coffee shops and fast-food chains,
incurring unnecessary costs to access safe and conducive environments for study and
work.

       A 2018 study on extended library hours in the Philippines4 reveals that the main
reason library users study during extended library hours is the lack of availability during
the day making nighttime the most practical period for their study and research work.
Furthermore, respondents recommended extending library operating hours to 24 hours a
day, seven days a week.

        This measure seeks to address the challenges faced by many Filipino learners and
young professionals: the lack of access to free, well-equipped, and safe learning hubs at
any time of day. The proposed establishment of 24/7 public libraries or learning
hubs constitutes a strategic initiative designed to significantly enhance educational
accessibility and efficacy, alleviating the financial constraints and scheduling conflicts
learners face.

        Furthermore, this bill envisions 24/7 public libraries or learning hubs as safe and
secure, creating the perfect environment for focused study or research. By providing this
reliable infrastructure, we can make education fairer for everyone, use resources more
effectively, and help learners achieve better results through consistent, barrier-free access
to quality learning spaces.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
       INSTITUTIONALIZING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF 24/7 PUBLIC
   LIBRARIES OR LEARNING HUBS IN ALL CITIES AND CLUSTERED
 MUNICIPALITIES IN THE COUNTRY TO PROVIDE SAFE, SUSTAINABLE,
 AND ACCESSIBLE PUBLIC STUDY SPACES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
       Section 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “24/7 Public Libraries or
Learning Hubs Act”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy.- It is the declared policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of citizens to quality education and safe, inclusive learning
environments; to elevate the literacy level of every Filipino through support for learner
productivity; and to recognize the vital role of knowledge and information in nation
building.

        Section 3. Mandatory establishment of 24/7 Public Libraries or Learning
Hubs.- All cities and cluster municipalities in the country shall establish and operate at
least one (1) public library or learning hub that remains open and operational twenty-four
(24) hours a day, seven (7) days a week. Priority shall be given to underserved, low-
income communities, and geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

        These facilities must be safe, well-lit, and equipped with essential amenities such
as broadband internet, charging stations, study desks, proper ventilation, and clean
restroom facilities. They shall be designed and retrofitted in accordance with recognized
green building standards, incorporating energy-efficient lighting, solar panels, rainwater
harvesting systems, and other sustainability features to reduce their environmental
footprint and operational costs.

        Section 4. Accessible Location and Active Transport Integration.- To promote
inclusive mobility, 24/7 public libraries or learning hubs shall be located in accessible
areas and supported by active transport infrastructure, including but not limited to bicycle



                                             3
racks, pedestrian walkways, and proximity to public transportation stops to ensure ease of
access for users at all hours.

        Section 5. Green and Climate- Resilient Infrastructure and Procurement- To
encourage sustainability and resilience in public infrastructure, local government units
(LGU) are strongly encouraged to design, retrofit, and operate the hubs in accordance
with green building principles and climate-adaptive features. This may include energy-
efficient lighting, passive cooling systems, solar energy, water-saving technologies, and
disaster-resilient design, particularly in flood- or earthquake-prone areas.

        LGUs are further encouraged to adopt environmentally and culturally responsive
procurement practices. This includes prioritizing locally sourced, renewable, or recycled-
- materials such as sustainably harvested bamboo, rattan, abaca, and other indigenous and
heritage-based materials- for furniture, furnishings, and finishes used within the hubs.

        In support of these efforts, national government agencies may provide technical
assistance, knowledge-sharing platforms, or grant incentives to LGUs that adopt
innovative or exemplary green and cultural design practices in their implementation of
this Act.

        The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), in coordination
with the Climate Change Commission (CCC), the National Commission for Culture and
the Arts (NCAA), National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), and the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall develop supplemental guidelines or
incentive programs to support local governments in adopting sustainable and culturally
respectful standards under this section.

        Section 6. Social Inclusions and Accessibility.- No person shall be denied access
to 24/7 public libraries or learning hubs on the basis of age, gender, disability, religion,
ethnicity, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, and sexual characteristics
(SOGIESC), or socioeconomic status.

       All facilities shall adopt gender-responsive and disability-inclusive designs
including but not limited to:
         Separate and secure restrooms for all genders;
         Ramps, tactile surfaces, and assistive technology for persons with
             disabilities; and
         Well-lit and safe premises with trained security personnel and surveillance
             systems.

       Section 7. 24/7 Public Libraries or Learning Hubs as Zones of Peace- To
ensure the protection and continuity of education of learners, all 24/7 public libraries or
learning hubs are hereby declared as zones of peace consistent with the provisions of
Republic Act No. 7610, Special Protection of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation, and
Discrimination Act; Republic Act No. 10821, the Children’s Emergency Relief and




                                             4
Protection Act; and Republic Act No. 11188,the Special Protection of Children in
Situations of Armed Conflict Act.

        Section 8. Child Protection Policy. - All 24/7 public libraries or learning hubs
shall adopt a child protection policy and guidelines for the prevention of violence against
children. Staff shall be trained to detect and respond to any form of child abuse, and
relevant information on reporting cases of child abuse shall be posted in a visible area
inside the premises.

        Section 9. Safe Spaces and Anti-Harassment Protocols. -The 24/7 public
libraries or learning hubs shall implement safety measures in accordance with Republic
Act No. 11313, or the Safe Spaces Act. Library staff and security personnel shall undergo
gender sensitivity and anti-sexual harassment training. Each facility shall post clear safe
spaces guidelines, and provide accessible mechanisms for reporting and addressing
violations.

        Section 10. Community Participation and Feedback Mechanisms.- LGUs shall
ensure meaningful community engagement in the planning and operation of 24/7 public
libraries or learning hubs. Consultation with different stakeholders, including children
and youth, women’s organizations, organizations of persons with disabilities, civil
society organizations, and the academe shall be conducted. Each facility shall maintain an
anonymous feedback system, both on-site and online, to continuously monitor safety,
accessibility, and service delivery.

         Section 11. Support from National Agencies.- The Department of Interior and
Local Government (DILG), in coordination with the Department of Education (DepED),
the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), the National Commission
on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), and the National Library of the Philippines (NLP), shall
assist the LGUs in the planning, technical implementation, staffing, and sustainability of
these facilities.

        Section 12. Implementation Plan and Reporting. - Within six (6) months from
the effectivity of this Act, covered LGUs shall submit to the DILG a library development
and operations plan that includes site identification, facility design, sustainability
components, budget allocation, and staffing arrangements for overnight operations.

        The DILG shall consolidate and submit an annual progress report to Congress on
the status of implementation, challenges encountered, and best practices, for a period of
five (5) years.

        Section 13. Implementing Rules and regulations.- Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in coordination with DepEd, DICT, DENR, CCC,
NLP, PCW, NCDA, NCIP, CHR, in consultation with relevant stakeholders including
civil society organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

        Section 14. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned local
government units. Thereafter, such amounts necessary for the continued implementation
shall be included in the annual budgets for the concerned LGUs.

        Section 15. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in
full force and effect.

        Section 16.. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees
or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary to
or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

       Section 17. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
