                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has moved from the margins of Philippine economic
and civic life to its center. Independent industry research cited in recent House
deliberations found that more than nine in ten surveyed Philippine organizations
have already adopted AI in some form, with more than half using generative AI
tools for over a year. AI already assists in hiring and firing decisions, credit and
loan approvals, medical triage, classroom instruction, government frontline
services, and the content millions of Filipinos see on their screens every day.
This proliferation of AI across the economy and daily life can no longer be treated
as a future concern to be legislated once the technology matures. It is a present
reality, and a national regulatory framework must be set in place now, before
gaps in oversight translate into irreversible harm to workers, consumers, and
vulnerable groups.

Despite this reality, the Philippines has no single law that defines or regulates
AI as such. Existing statutes like the Data Privacy Act of 2012, the Cybercrime
Prevention Act of 2012, and the recently enacted E-Governance Act each cover
only a slice of the problem, while the executive branch's National AI Strategy for
the Philippines (NAIS-PH), approved in May 2025, and the Department of Science
and Technology's National Artificial Intelligence Center for Research and
Innovation (NAICRI), activated in February 2026, provide policy direction and
research infrastructure but carry no binding regulatory force on their own.

Congress has not been silent on this gap. Both chambers of the 19th and 20th
Congresses have seen a steady stream of measures responding to different facets
of AI governance.

The Department of Information and Communications Technology has
consistently urged that AI regulation be "principle-based and flexible" and
calibrated to actual risk, cautioning that heavy-handed rules could stifle
innovation and discourage the investment the country needs to remain
competitive; industry and startup groups have echoed concerns that blanket
registration or documentation requirements could be disproportionately
burdensome for micro, small, and medium enterprises. At the same time, labor
advocates pointing to the Department of Labor and Employment's own
acknowledgment that Filipino workers are already losing jobs to automation
have pressed for enforceable protections: advance notice before AI-driven
displacement, access to retraining, and an assurance that no worker will be
dismissed by an algorithm alone.
Civil society and academic voices behind the AI Bill of Rights language found in
earlier bills have emphasized transparency, protection from discriminatory
profiling, a right to human review of consequential decisions, and safeguards for
children and other vulnerable groups. Data privacy regulators and legal
commentators, for their part, have stressed that any new AI framework must
remain harmonized with, rather than duplicate or contradict, the Data Privacy
Act of 2012. Community concerns have also emerged around the energy and
water footprint of the AI data centers now concentrating around Metro Manila
and Cebu.

This bill seeks to reconcile these positions in a single, risk-based, and workable
framework. It does not impose uniform, one-size-fits-all obligations on every AI
system regardless of its actual risk profile. Instead it: (i) affirms a Bill of Rights
in Artificial Intelligence covering notice, explanation, human review, data
control, and protection from discrimination; (ii) creates the National Artificial
Intelligence Regulatory Authority (NAIRA) under the Department of Information
and Communications Technology, supported by a National AI Multi-Stakeholder
Advisory Council representing government, labor, industry, academe, and civil
society; (iii) classifies AI systems by risk prohibiting unacceptable uses outright,
subjecting only high-risk systems to registration and pre-deployment review,
requiring basic transparency for limited-risk systems, and leaving minimal-risk
innovation largely unencumbered; (iv) guarantees that no Filipino worker may
be dismissed, demoted, or have compensation reduced on the sole basis of an
automated decision, and requires at least sixty (60) days' advance notice and
access to retraining before AI-driven displacement; (v) establishes a regulatory
sandbox to keep the door open for startups, researchers, and MSMEs; and (vi)
provides proportionate administrative, civil, and criminal remedies for violations.

Innovation and regulation are not opposing goals. A clear, predictable, and
appropriately calibrated legal framework is the foundation on which sustained
public trust and, with it, sustained AI adoption and investment will be built. In
view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly requested.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR
      THE DEVELOPMENT, DEPLOYMENT, AND USE OF ARTIFICIAL
   INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS IN THE PHILIPPINES, CREATING FOR THE
   PURPOSE THE NATIONAL ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE REGULATORY
  AUTHORITY AND THE NATIONAL AI MULTI-STAKEHOLDER ADVISORY
     COUNCIL, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF,
    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

                                  CHAPTER I
                               General Provisions

       SEC. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Artificial
Intelligence Regulatory Framework Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote
the responsible, ethical, and inclusive development and use of Artificial
Intelligence (AI) as a tool for national development, economic competitiveness,
and improved delivery of public services, while protecting the constitutional
rights of every Filipino, including the rights to privacy, due process, equal
protection, and gainful employment. The State recognizes that innovation and
regulation are not opposing goals: a clear, predictable, and risk-based legal
framework is necessary to build the public trust on which continued AI adoption
and investment depend. Towards this end, the State shall adopt a framework
that is proportionate to the actual risk posed by an AI system, that avoids
imposing undue compliance burdens on low-risk innovation, startups, and
micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), and that establishes clear,
enforceable safeguards where an AI system poses a significant risk to safety,
livelihood, or fundamental rights.

       SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to the research,
development, deployment, importation, sale, and use of AI systems by public and
private entities operating within the Philippines, and to AI systems that, although
hosted, trained, or developed abroad, produce effects on persons, entities, or
data within Philippine territory. This Act shall apply suppletorily to sector-
specific rules issued by financial, health, education, telecommunications,
electoral, and other regulators, which shall retain primary jurisdiction over
matters within their respective competence, in coordination with the Authority
created under this Act.
SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

(a) “Artificial Intelligence (AI)” refers to a machine-based system that, for a
    given set of human-defined objectives, generates outputs such as
    predictions, recommendations, content, or decisions that influence real
    or virtual environments, with varying degrees of autonomy.

(b) “AI System” means any software, hardware, or combination thereof that
    implements one or more AI techniques, including machine learning, to
    process data and produce an output for a given set of objectives.

(c) “Automated Decision” means a decision made, in whole or in
   substantial part, by an AI system without meaningful human judgment
   applied to the specific case, that produces legal effects on a person or
   similarly and significantly affects that person.

(d) “Deployer” means any natural or juridical person that puts an AI
   system into use, whether by developing it in-house or by procuring it
   from a developer.

(e) “Developer” means any natural or juridical person that designs, builds,
    trains, or substantially modifies an AI system.

(f) “Foundation Model” means a large-scale AI model trained on broad data
     and adaptable to a wide range of downstream tasks.

(g) “Generative AI” means an AI system capable of generating text, images,
    audio, video, code, or other content that resembles human-generated
    output.

(h) “High-Risk AI System” means an AI system classified as such under
    Section 18 of this Act.

(i) “Human Oversight” means the presence of effective mechanisms
    enabling a natural person to understand, monitor, intervene in, or
    override the operation or output of an AI system.

(j) “Regulatory Sandbox” means a controlled, time-bound testing
    environment authorized by the Authority under Section 32 that allows
    for conditional exemptions from specified requirements of this Act.

(k) “Synthetic Content” means image, audio, video, or text content that has
    been generated or manipulated by an AI system such that it would
    falsely appear to a reasonable person to be authentic or to accurately
    depict a real person, place, or event.

(l) “Authority” refers to the National Artificial Intelligence Regulatory
    Authority (NAIRA) created under Section 6 of this Act.

(m) “Council” refers to the National AI Multi-Stakeholder Advisory Council
   created under Section 13 of this Act.

                            CHAPTER II
              Bill of Rights in Artificial Intelligence
       SEC. 5. AI Bill of Rights. – In recognition of the impact of AI on Filipino
life, work, and society, and in order to preserve human dignity and democratic
accountability, the State affirms the following rights of every person in relation
to the design, deployment, and use of AI systems:

      (a) Right to Notice. – Every person has the right to know, in clear and
          accessible terms, when they are interacting with, or are subject to a
          decision made or materially assisted by, an AI system, unless such use
          is clearly evident from the context.

      (b) Right to Explanation. – Every person subject to an automated decision
          affecting their rights, benefits, or opportunities has the right to a
          meaningful explanation of the logic, main factors, and data involved.

      (c) Right to Human Review. – No automated decision that materially affects
          a person's employment, credit, insurance, government benefit, or
          access to education shall be made solely by an AI system, and every
          such person has the right to request review and, where warranted,
          correction or reversal by a competent human officer.

      (d) Right to Data Control. – Consistent with Republic Act No. 10173, every
          person has the right to access, correct, and object to the processing of
          their personal data by an AI system, including a right to request the
          removal of their personal data from training or operational datasets,
          subject to legitimate retention requirements under law.

      (e) Right against Algorithmic Discrimination. – Every person has the right
          to be free from unlawful discrimination, profiling, or surveillance by an
          AI system on the basis of any characteristic protected under existing
          law.

      (f) Right to Safety. – Every person has the right to be protected from a high-
          risk AI system that has not undergone the risk assessment,
          registration, and human-oversight requirements of this Act.

      (g) Right to Redress. – Every person adversely affected by a violation of this
          Act has the right to file a complaint with the Authority and to pursue
          civil, administrative, or criminal remedies as provided herein and under
          other applicable law.

      (h) Rights of Workers. – No worker shall be dismissed, demoted, or have
          their wages or benefits diminished on the sole basis of an automated
          decision, and every worker is entitled to advance notice, consultation,
          and transition support in connection with AI-driven changes to their
          work, as further provided in Section 24 of this Act.

                               CHAPTER III
         The National Artificial Intelligence Regulatory Authority

     SEC. 6. Creation of the National Artificial Intelligence Regulatory
Authority (NAIRA). – There is hereby created the National Artificial Intelligence
Regulatory Authority (NAIRA), attached to the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) for purposes of policy and program
coordination, which shall exercise regulatory, quasi-judicial, and administrative
functions over AI systems in the Philippines as provided in this Act. NAIRA shall
have the authority to promulgate rules, conduct investigations, impose
administrative sanctions, and issue orders necessary to carry out the purposes
of this Act.

      SEC. 7. Powers and Functions. – NAIRA shall have the following powers
and functions:

      (a) Formulate, issue, and enforce rules, standards, and technical
         requirements for the classification, registration, and oversight of AI
         systems;

      (b) Classify AI systems by level of risk pursuant to Chapter V of this Act
          and maintain an indicative, periodically updated list of high-risk AI use
          cases;

      (c) Establish and maintain the National AI Registry required under Section
          10;

      (d) Conduct pre-deployment reviews, audits, and post-deployment
         monitoring of high-risk AI systems;

      (e) Investigate complaints, issue compliance orders, cease-and-desist
          orders, and impose administrative fines under Section 35;

      (f) Refer suspected criminal violations of this Act to the Department of
          Justice and appropriate law enforcement agencies;

      (g) Issue binding and advisory opinions on the application of this Act;

      (h) Coordinate with sectoral regulators, including the Bangko Sentral ng
          Pilipinas, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the Insurance
          Commission, the National Privacy Commission, the Department of
          Health, the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher
          Education, the Commission on Elections, and the National
          Telecommunications Commission, on matters of overlapping
          jurisdiction;

      (i) Administer the regulatory sandbox program under Section 32;

      (j) Promote the development of domestic AI talent, research, and industry
          in coordination with the Department of Science and Technology and the
          Department of Trade and Industry; and

      (k) Submit an annual report to Congress on the state of AI regulation,
          registrations, enforcement actions, and legislative recommendations.

     SEC. 8. Organizational Structure. – NAIRA shall be headed by a Director-
General, appointed by the President of the Philippines, and shall be assisted by
Deputy Director-Generals for: (a) Standards and Risk Classification; (b)
Compliance and Enforcement; (c) Innovation and Regulatory Sandbox; and (d)
Consumer and Worker Protection. The Director-General shall, within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, submit a staffing pattern and budget
proposal to the Department of Budget and Management.

       SEC. 9. Qualifications of the Director-General. – The Director-General
shall be a natural-born Filipino citizen of good moral character with at least ten
(10) years of relevant experience in AI, computer science, law, public policy, or a
related field.

      SEC. 10. National AI Registry. – There is hereby established a National
AI Registry, which shall record all high-risk AI systems required to be registered
under Section 19. Registration shall not be required for limited-risk or minimal-
risk AI systems. The Registry shall be made publicly accessible in a manner that
protects legitimate trade secrets and proprietary information.

      SEC. 11. Rule-Making and Public Consultation. – All proposed rules and
regulations of NAIRA shall undergo public notice and consultation for not less
than thirty (30) calendar days and shall be published in the Official Gazette, in
a newspaper of general circulation, or on the official NAIRA website prior to
taking effect.

      SEC. 12. Funding. – The initial funding requirement for the establishment
of NAIRA shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DICT.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued operation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

                                CHAPTER IV
               National AI Multi-Stakeholder Advisory Council

      SEC. 13. Creation of the Council. – There is hereby created the National
AI Multi-Stakeholder Advisory Council, which shall serve as an independent
advisory body to NAIRA, composed of eleven (11) members appointed by the
President upon recommendation of the DICT Secretary, as follows:

      (a) One (1) AI researcher or machine learning expert;

      (b) One (1) ethicist, philosopher, or scholar with a demonstrated body of
          work on AI ethics;

      (c) One (1) representative of a national labor federation or trade union;

      (d) One (1) human rights or data privacy legal expert;

      (e) One (1) sociologist, anthropologist, or public-interest technologist;

      (f) One (1) representative of the youth or academic sector under thirty (30)
          years of age;

      (g) One (1) representative of MSMEs or the startup sector;

      (h) One (1) representative of a consumer protection organization;

      (i) One (1) media practitioner or researcher on disinformation or
          algorithmic harms;
      (j) One (1) representative from Visayas, and one (1) representative from
          Mindanao, rotating every three (3) years.

      SEC. 14. Functions of the Council. – The Council shall: (a) issue non-
binding opinions on proposed high-risk classifications and major NAIRA rules;
(b) conduct public hearings and consultations on proposed AI regulations; (c)
submit an annual AI Ethics and Public Impact Report to Congress and the
President; and (d) receive a written explanation from NAIRA whenever the
Authority adopts a position contrary to the Council's recommendation.

      SEC. 15. Term and Secretariat Support. – Members of the Council shall
serve a term of three (3) years, renewable once, and shall receive honoraria as
may be determined by the Department of Budget and Management, subject to
availability of funds. The DICT shall provide secretariat support to the Council.

                                CHAPTER V
                   Risk-Based Classification of AI Systems

       SEC. 16. Risk Tiers. – AI systems shall be classified into the following
tiers: (a) Unacceptable Risk, which is prohibited outright under Section 17; (b)
High Risk, subject to the requirements of Section 19; (c) Limited Risk, subject to
the transparency requirements of Section 20; and (d) Minimal Risk, subject to
Section 21.

      SEC. 17. Prohibited AI Practices. – The following AI practices are
prohibited:

      (a) The use of AI by a public authority to evaluate or classify the
         trustworthiness of persons based on their social behavior or personal
         characteristics, leading to detrimental or unfavorable treatment
         unrelated to the context in which the data was originally generated;

      (b) The use of AI techniques that materially distort a person's behavior
          through subliminal, manipulative, or deceptive means in a manner
          likely to cause significant harm;

      (c) The use of real-time, indiscriminate biometric identification of persons
          in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement purposes, except
          upon judicial authorization or as otherwise provided by law;

      (d) The use of AI to exploit the vulnerabilities of children, persons with
          disabilities, or the elderly in a manner likely to cause harm;

      (e) The use of AI to create or distribute synthetic content depicting a real,
          identifiable person without their consent, where such content is
          deceptively presented as authentic and is likely to cause reputational,
          financial, or electoral harm; and

      (f) The use of AI to infer the emotional state of an employee or student
          without their consent, except where necessary for safety or medical
          purposes.
       SEC. 18. High-Risk AI Systems. – NAIRA shall classify an AI system as
high-risk based on: (a) its use in critical sectors such as health, finance,
education, employment, credit, law enforcement, government benefits, or critical
infrastructure; (b) its degree of autonomy and the extent of human oversight
available; (c) its use for biometric identification or profiling; and (d) the scale of
its deployment and the number of persons potentially affected. NAIRA shall issue
and periodically update an indicative list of high-risk AI use cases.

       SEC. 19. Obligations for High-Risk AI Systems. – A deployer of a high-
risk AI system shall, prior to deployment: (a) register the system with the National
AI Registry; (b) conduct and document a risk assessment, including bias and
fairness testing; (c) implement an effective human oversight mechanism; (d)
establish post-deployment monitoring and incident-reporting procedures; and
(e) adopt commensurate cybersecurity safeguards. Failure to comply shall be
subject to the sanctions under Section 35.

      SEC. 20. Limited-Risk AI Systems. – A deployer of a limited-risk AI
system, including a generative AI system, shall disclose to users, in a clear and
conspicuous manner, that they are interacting with an AI system or that content
has been generated or materially altered by AI, and shall, where feasible, provide
an accessible means of obtaining human assistance.

      SEC. 21. Minimal-Risk AI Systems. – A minimal-risk AI system shall not
be subject to mandatory registration or pre-deployment review under this Act.
NAIRA may issue voluntary codes of conduct for the responsible development of
such systems.

                                CHAPTER VI
                    Cross-Cutting and Sectoral Obligations

       SEC. 22. Alignment with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. – Republic Act
No. 10173 shall apply suppletorily to this Act. The National Privacy Commission
shall retain jurisdiction over complaints involving the unlawful processing of
personal data by an AI system. NAIRA and the National Privacy Commission
shall jointly issue guidelines on automated decision-making and profiling within
one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.

      SEC. 23. AI in Government Services. – A government agency deploying
a high-risk AI system for public-facing decisions shall register the system with
NAIRA, publish a plain-language notice of the system's function and available
appeal channels, and ensure that a duly authorized human officer remains
accountable for the final government action.

      SEC. 24. Protection of Workers from AI-Driven Displacement. – No
employer shall dismiss, demote, or reduce the compensation or benefits of an
employee based solely on the output of an AI or automated system, without
human review by a duly authorized officer. An employer intending to adopt an
AI system reasonably expected to result in the redundancy or displacement of
workers shall provide affected workers, and their recognized labor organization
where one exists, written notice of not less than sixty (60) calendar days prior to
implementation, and shall, in coordination with the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), offer reasonable retraining, upskilling, or transition
assistance to displaced workers. DOLE, in coordination with NAIRA, shall issue
implementing guidelines within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 25. AI in Recruitment and Employment Decisions. – An employer
using AI in recruitment, performance evaluation, or termination shall disclose
such use to applicants and employees, conduct periodic bias testing, and ensure
that final adverse decisions remain subject to human review upon request.

       SEC. 26. AI in Credit, Insurance, and Financial Services. – A financial
institution using AI for credit scoring, underwriting, or claims processing shall
disclose the use of automated processing to affected clients and provide a right
to request human review of an adverse decision, without prejudice to the
continuing prudential authority of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the
Securities and Exchange Commission, and the Insurance Commission, which
shall coordinate with NAIRA on applicable technical standards.

      SEC. 27. AI in Elections and Political Communication. – Synthetic or
AI-generated campaign content shall be conspicuously labeled as such. This Act
shall be construed to complement, and not to diminish, the authority of the
Commission on Elections to regulate the use of AI in electoral activities.

       SEC. 28. AI in Health Care. – An AI system intended for clinical diagnosis,
triage, or treatment recommendation shall be reviewed with the Department of
Health prior to deployment and shall remain assistive to, and shall not replace,
the independent judgment of a duly licensed health professional.

      SEC. 29. AI in Education. – An AI system used for student grading,
ranking, or profiling shall not be the sole basis of an academic decision. The
Department of Education and the Commission on Higher Education, in
coordination with NAIRA, shall issue standards for the use of AI in basic and
higher education and shall integrate AI literacy and ethics into relevant
curricula.

      SEC. 30. AI Data Centers and Environmental Disclosure. – An operator
of an AI training or inference data center exceeding a capacity threshold set by
NAIRA, in coordination with the Department of Energy and the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources, shall disclose its annual energy and water
consumption, secure applicable environmental compliance certificates, and
consult host communities prior to the commencement of operations.

       SEC. 31. Advanced AI Systems and Future Preparedness. – NAIRA shall
establish a foresight function to monitor the development of advanced or highly
general-purpose AI systems and shall submit a strategic risk report to Congress
at least once every three (3) years. NAIRA may issue precautionary advisories or
time-bound restrictions, not exceeding twelve (12) months unless renewed, on
emerging AI applications presenting significant and unregulated risk to rights,
safety, or national security.

                                 CHAPTER VII
                 Regulatory Sandbox and Innovation Support

      SEC. 32. Regulatory Sandbox Program. – NAIRA shall establish a
regulatory sandbox program allowing startups, researchers, and MSMEs to test
AI systems under time-bound, supervised conditions with conditional
exemptions from specified requirements of this Act, subject to a risk mitigation
plan, reporting requirements, and defined geographic or functional limits.

      SEC. 33. Support for Domestic AI Development. – The State shall
promote the development of locally built AI systems, including Filipino-language
models, through grants, research support, and partnerships coordinated with
the Department of Science and Technology, the National AI Center for Research
and Innovation, and the Commission on Higher Education. NAIRA shall establish
a simplified compliance track for MSMEs.

      SEC. 34. AI Safety and Research Fund. – There is hereby created an AI
Safety and Research Fund, to be administered by NAIRA and funded through the
annual General Appropriations Act, to support independent safety and bias
audits, adversarial testing, and the development of national AI safety
benchmarks.

                              CHAPTER VIII
                    Enforcement, Penalties, and Remedies

      SEC. 35. Administrative Sanctions. – NAIRA may impose the following
administrative sanctions, without prejudice to civil or criminal liability: (a) a fine
not exceeding Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00) per violation for the
unauthorized deployment of a high-risk AI system without registration, with a
grace period for first-time violators who have not caused substantial harm; (b) a
fine not exceeding One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00) for failure to disclose the
use of AI or to label synthetic content as required under this Act; (c) suspension
or revocation of registration; and (d) disqualification from government
procurement.

       SEC. 36. Criminal Liability. – The willful commission of a prohibited AI
practice under Section 17, or the malicious use of AI to enable fraud, identity
theft, or the non-consensual creation of deceptive synthetic content depicting a
real person, shall be punished by imprisonment of six (6) months to six (6) years,
or a fine of not more than Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000.00), or both, at the
discretion of the court, without prejudice to prosecution under the Revised Penal
Code, Republic Act No. 10175, or other applicable law.

      SEC. 37. Liability of Juridical Persons. – Where a violation is committed
by a juridical person, the officer or employee who authorized, directed, or
knowingly allowed the violation, and the compliance officer who failed to act
despite knowledge thereof, shall be held solidarily liable with the entity.

      SEC. 38. Civil Remedies. – Any person whose rights are violated under
this Act may file an action for injunctive relief, actual, moral, or exemplary
damages, correction or deletion of data, or such other relief as the courts may
deem just and equitable.
      SEC. 39. Whistleblower Protection. – A person who reports a violation
of this Act in good faith shall be protected from retaliation. An entity that
voluntarily discloses and promptly corrects a violation may, at the discretion of
NAIRA, be granted mitigation of penalties.

                                  CHAPTER IX
                                Final Provisions

      SEC. 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, NAIRA and the DICT, in
consultation with the National Privacy Commission, the Department of Justice,
DOLE, the Department of Trade and Industry, and the Council, shall promulgate
the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

       SEC. 41. Periodic Review. – The appropriate committees of the Senate
and the House of Representatives shall review this Act at least once every three
(3) years to account for technological developments and emerging risks.

       SEC. 42. Suppletory Application. – Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy
Act of 2012), Republic Act No. 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012), the
Labor Code of the Philippines, and other applicable laws shall apply suppletorily
to this Act.

       SEC. 43. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 44. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 45. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
