                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

   Do not have two differing weights in your bag—one heavy, one light. Do not have two

The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates consumer protection through explicit provisions
promoting welfare, safety, and fair trade practices. Meanwhile, Article XIII, Section 12 directs
the State to recognize consumers' right to health and safety, quality standards, information,
choice, representation, redress, education, and a healthy environment. Finally, Article XVI,
Section 9 requires the State to protect consumers from trade malpractices and substandard
or hazardous products.

These constitutional guarantees are given statutory effect under Republic Act No. 7394, as
amended, or the Consumer Act of the Philippines. The law provides for the protection of
consumers against hazards to health and safety, deceptive and unfair sales acts and
practices, and other forms of abuse in the marketplace. It also sets standards on product
quality, warranties, and labeling, and provides remedies such as refund or replacement at no
additional cost.

In the context of residential utilities, consumer protection remains limited in scope. The
clearest existing example is the Magna Carta for Residential Electricity Consumers issued by
the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) under ERC Resolution No. 10, Series of 2004, as
amended. However, this applies only to residential electricity customers under approved rate
schedules. To date, there is still no single and comprehensive charter of rights for household
consumers across essential utility services.

This gap is significant because electricity, water, and internet services are indispensable to
the daily lives of Filipino households. These services are closely tied to the fulfillment of basic
needs and to the welfare and well-being of families. Electricity is necessary for lighting, food
storage, communication, and other essential household activities. Water is vital for sanitation,
hygiene, health, and everyday domestic use. Internet access has likewise become
increasingly important for communication, learning, access to information, online transactions,
and participation in daily life.
However, many Filipino households still encounter obstacles in obtaining reliable utility
services. They struggle with high expenses, frequent service disruptions, subpar quality, and
ineffective complaint systems. Most consumers are either unaware of their rights or vulnerable
to utility providers who function with minimal oversight and accountability.

This bill seeks to address these issues by empowering residential consumers through the
creation of a Magna Carta that provides a clear and comprehensive framework governing the
rights and duties of residential utility consumers and the obligations of utility providers. It
introduces mechanisms for complaint resolution, defines service quality standards, and
enhances regulatory supervision of electricity, water, and internet providers. Through this
measure, the bill reinforces the State’s duty to safeguard public welfare by guaranteeing fair
access to essential utilities, fostering accountability and transparency, and preventing unjust
and exploitative practices.

By reaffirming the State’s responsibility to safeguard the well-being of its people, this measure
goes beyond a legal framework; it represents a commitment to honor God by defending the
rights of every Filipino, strengthening communities through access to essential, high-quality
services, and advancing the nation by embedding principles of equity, accountability, and
empowerment.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
    PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF RESIDENTIAL UTILITY CONSUMERS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                           CHAPTER I – GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of Residential Utility
Consumers Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote and safeguard
the welfare of residential electricity, water, and internet consumers. These household utilities
are basic necessities essential to everyday life and must be provided in a manner that is
affordable, reliable, safe, and transparent. The State shall promote fair business practices,
responsive service delivery, and meaningful access to redress for grievances. The State shall
ensure that all Filipino households, regardless of geography or socioeconomic status, are
protected from abuse, inefficiency, and discriminatory practices in utility services.

SECTION 3. Scope and Application. — This Act shall apply to residential consumers of
electricity, water, and internet services nationwide, and to all utility providers engaged in the
distribution and delivery of such services, whether privately-owned, cooperatives, or
government-owned or operated.

                           CHAPTER II – DEFINITION OF TERMS

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act, the following terms are hereby
defined, as follows:

   a) “Bill Deposit” refers to a refundable amount collected by a utility service provider from
      a consumer as a guarantee for future payment of utility services.

   b) “Billing Adjustment” refers to the amount charged to the consumer for the unbilled
      utility service consumed resulting from a stoppage or defect, conspicuous or otherwise,
      in the device, provided that there is no evidence of tampering thereon.

   c) “Differential Billing” shall mean the amount to be charged to the consumer for the
      unbilled consumption of utility service caused by illegal means, as determined through
      the use of reasonable methodologies of the concerned utility service provider or as
      prescribed by law.
   d) “Disconnection” means the discontinuance of a utility service due to lawful causes.

   e) “High Billings” or “Unusual Consumption” refers to monthly billings or
      consumption that are more than two hundred percent (200%) of the consumers’
      immediately preceding billing.

   f)   “Meter” refers to a device that measures and records the amount of electrical energy
        or the volume of water consumed by a consumer over a specific period, for usage
        tracking and determination of applicable charges.

   g) "Modem" shall refer to a device that connects directly to an Internet Service Provider
      (ISP), translating internet signals into a format usable by consumer devices, thereby
      enabling internet connectivity

   h) “Registered Customer” shall mean the customer who has a valid service contract
      with the utility service provider.

   i)   “Residential Utility Consumer, Customer or End-user” shall mean any person who
        is the registered customer of the electric, water, or internet utility service or any person
        authorized by the registered customer to occupy the premises and enjoy the said
        service.

   j)   “Retirement of Service” shall mean the removal of all facilities necessary for the
        provision of electric, water, or internet service.

   k) "Router" shall refer to a device that receives internet signals from a modem and
      distributes such signals to multiple consumer devices within a premises through wired
      or wireless connections.

   l)   “Service Contract” refers to the agreement between the consumer and provider
        governing utility use and services.

   m) “Termination of Service Contract” shall mean the cancellation of the service
      contract whereby the utility service provider and the registered consumer are released
      from their respective obligations in the contract, without prejudice to the performance
      of existing obligations prior to termination.

   n) “Utility Service Provider” means any entity that has an exclusive franchise or is
      authorized by law to provide electricity, water, or internet services to residential
      consumers.

For purposes of this Act, the terms "meter”, "modem”, and "router”, as herein defined, shall
collectively be referred to as "device."

              CHAPTER III – RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF CONSUMERS

SECTION 5. Basic Rights. — All residential consumers shall be entitled to the following
basic rights:

   a) To have quality, affordable, safe, reliable, and uninterrupted utility services;

   b) To be provided with accurate, fair, timely, and transparent billing, including a
      transparent and reasonable price for utility service;
   c) To be accorded courteous, prompt, and non-discriminatory service, including quality,
      accessible, and effective customer service;

   d) To file complaints and be accorded prompt and speedy resolution of complaints within
      a reasonable time;

   e) To be given adequate access to information on matters affecting the electric service of
      the consumer concerned; and

   f)   To be provided with safe installation services and proper maintenance of service
        equipment, and to have a quality, reliable, accurate, and functioning device installed.

SECTION 6. Basic Obligations. — Every consumer must comply with the following
obligations and responsibilities:

   a) To observe the terms of the contract, including, among other things, paying monthly
      bills promptly and honestly;

   b) To allow the faithful and accurate recording of consumption to be reflected in the
      appropriate device, if applicable;

   c) To grant access to the consumer's premises to employees or authorized
      representatives of the utility for lawful and valid purposes, including, but not limited to,
      reading or inspection of devices, service lines, or wires.

   d) To take proper care of the devices and service lines that the utility service provider has
      installed within the premises;

   e) To inform the utility service provider of any improvements made or renovations to be
      undertaken on the consumer’s premises, if such changes would affect compliance with
      the conditions set forth under Section 9 of this Act. In cases where proper and timely
      notice is given, the relocation of the device shall be at no cost to the consumer.
      However, failure to provide such notice shall render the consumer liable for the full cost
      of the relocation; and

   f)   To inform the utility service provider, upon discovery, of any theft, pilferage of, or any
        damage caused to the devices installed.

SECTION 7. Right to Service Connection; Presumption. — Consumers shall have the right
to apply for utility services, subject only to reasonable and standardized requirements. For
purposes of this section, the utility service provider shall act on the application within five (5)
days from the submission of complete requirements. Failure to do so shall give rise to the
presumption that the application has been unreasonably denied or unduly delayed. In such
cases, the consumer may file a complaint with the appropriate government agency exercising
regulatory or supervisory authority over the utility service provider, which shall take appropriate
action to address the delay or denial and impose sanctions, if warranted, in accordance with
law.

SECTION 8. Prompt Connection; Reconnection. — Within five (5) days after approval of
the consumer’s application, the utility service provider shall ensure proper connection to the
system.

In cases of disconnection due to non-payment, the utility service provider shall reconnect the
consumer’s service upon full payment of the outstanding amount, subject to the following
periods:
       a) Within three (3) hours from full payment, for smart meters, systems or services with
       remote reconnection or restoration capability; and

       b) Within six (6) hours from full payment, for analog meters, digital meters without
       remote reconnection capability, or other meters and services requiring physical
       reconnection.

For purposes of this section, the applicable reconnection period shall be reckoned from the
time the utility service provider confirms receipt of full payment.

SECTION 9. Proper Installation of Device. — For electricity and water consumers, their
respective meters shall be installed in a clean place, free of vibration, and where it will be
easily accessible and visible for reading and testing for both the service utility provider and the
consumer: Provided, That for justifiable reasons, such as in areas where the risk of meter
tampering is high or if there is no adequate space for the installation of the meter, the service
utility provider shall be allowed to install and cluster the meter in an elevated or reasonable
space.

For internet consumers, the consumer has the right to choose the place where the modem
and router are to be installed within the residence.

SECTION 10. Unjust Disconnection. — No utility service shall be disconnected without due
process. For disconnections due to non-payment, a written notice must be served at least
forty-eight (48) hours prior to disconnection.

When disconnection is physically carried out by the utility service provider, it shall only be
made in the presence of the consumer, his or her duly authorized representative, or someone
of suitable age and discretion residing in the premises.

Where disconnection is carried out remotely, the utility service provider shall duly serve the
required prior notice, immediately notify the consumer after the remote disconnection is carried
out, and maintain a verifiable record thereof.

Disconnection of utility service, whether physically or remotely carried out, shall only be made
under the following circumstances:

   a) Non-payment of electric bills within the proper period, if the same is not contested by
      the consumer or pending investigation by the utility service provider, and only after
      exhaustion of the bill deposit, and the same is not enough to cover the subject billing;

   b) Illegal use of the utility service;

   c) Upon lawful orders of government agencies and/or the courts;

   d) When public safety so requires; or

   e) Refusal of the consumer, without any justifiable reason, to allow the utility service
      provider’s employees or representatives to enter into the premises to inspect the
      installed device.

Whenever a utility service provider disconnects a consumer's service, it may opt to retire,
remove, or recover its service facilities, meters, lines, equipment, if the service remains
disconnected for a period exceeding one hundred eighty (180) days.
SECTION 11. Suspension of Disconnection. — Notwithstanding the service of notice,
disconnections of service shall not be made on any weekday beyond three o'clock in the
afternoon (3:00 P.M.) to eight o’clock in the morning (8:00 A.M.), or on Saturdays, Sundays,
and official holidays, and under the following circumstances:

   a) One of the permanent occupants is sick and dependent on a life support system:
      Provided, That the customer shall present a medical certificate issued by a duly
      licensed physician or public health official certifying such fact: Provided, Further, That
      the suspension shall only be during the dependency of the patient on the life support
      system but which shall not exceed one () month from such suspension;

   b) During the funeral wake of a deceased who was a resident of the premises: Provided,
      That the consumer shall present a copy or a duly certified true copy of the death
      certificate of the deceased issued by the Local Civil Registry of the city or municipality
      concerned: Provided, Further, That the suspension shall only be during the period of
      the wake which shall not exceed seven (7) days from the suspension;

   c) One of the permanent occupants is either a newborn child less than six (6) months old
      or a senior citizen: Provided, That the suspension shall only be for a period of five (5)
      days;

   d) The customer tenders payment of the unpaid bill to the agent or employee of the utility
      service provider who is to effect the disconnection;

   e) The customer indubitably proves that he or she did not receive a Statement of Account
      and/or a Disconnection Notice, unless the non-receipt is caused by the refusal of the
      customer to accept such bill or notice;

   f)   The customer is being billed in a single statement for consumption covering several
        months due to the failure of the utility to issue a timely monthly billing statement to the
        consumer; and

   g) There is a pending case or unresolved complaint between the consumer and the utility
      service provider involving differential billings, billing adjustments, disputed estimated
      consumptions, or other billing errors of similar nature, until the final resolution of the
      case.

SECTION 12. Refund. — Consumers are entitled to refunds in cases of overbilling, billing
errors, or payments made for services not delivered.

Any bill deposit existing, if not yet returned or consumed, shall also be refunded within one (1)
month from the termination of service, provided that all obligations due have been paid.

SECTION 13. Exemption from Payment of Meter Deposits or Installation Fee. — All
consumers shall be exempt from the payment of meter deposits or installation fees for the
utility service availed of.

In cases of loss and/or damage to the installed device, the consumer shall bear the
replacement cost of the device if the loss or damage is due to his or her fault, otherwise, it is
the utility service provider who shall bear the cost.

For consumers who have already paid a meter deposit, the respective service utility provider
shall automatically refund the same within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.
SECTION 14. Transparent Billing. — All utility bills must clearly state charges, rates, usage,
payment due dates, and any adjustments made therein: Provided, That bills which are based
on estimates shall be clearly and conspicuously marked as such and explained to the
consumer when the bill is delivered.

Rate formulas and component breakdowns shall be published online and posted at the utility
service provider’s service centers, website, and social media pages.

SECTION 15. Accurate Meter or Functional Modem and Router. — No device shall be
installed or placed in service unless it has been tested, certified, and sealed by the utility
service provider and the government agency having regulatory control over it, if applicable.

The service utility provider shall present and explain to the consumer the device test report
before the same is installed or placed in service. In case of failure to present the said report,
the consumer may opt to refuse installation and demand that a different device be installed.

For purposes of this section, a consumer has the right to require the service utility provider to
test annually, free of charge, the accuracy or functionality of the device installed in their
premises.

SECTION 16. Refund for Overbillings; Overpayments. — Customers of electricity and
water services have the right to a refund in cases of overbilling by the service utility provider
arising from a meter test showing that the said meter was inaccurately fast or in cases of other
billing errors where overpayments are declared. The refund obtained shall then be applied
fully to the consumer's next immediate billing.

In cases of overpayments or payments made by mistake, the consumer shall have the option
to either: (a) request for a full refund of the overpaid amount; or (b) have the same amount
credited to future billings. If the consumer opts for a refund, it shall be implemented within
seven (7) calendar days from the date the consumer formally exercises the option.

SECTION 17. Utility Service Connection Despite Arrearages of the Previous Tenant. —
A service utility provider shall not refuse or discontinue service to a consumer who is not in
arrears, even though there are unpaid bills or charges due from the premises occupied by the
consumer-applicant on account of an unpaid bill of a prior tenant: Provided, That there is no
conspiracy between the previous tenant and the new consumer-applicant to defraud the utility
service provider.

SECTION 18. Bill Deposit. — A bill deposit may be required from consumers to guarantee
the payment of utility bills. The amount of the deposit shall be equivalent to the estimated
billing for one (1) month, based on the consumer’s projected consumption. The bill deposit
shall earn interest at a rate prescribed by the appropriate regulatory agency, and such interest
shall be credited to the consumer’s account annually.

In the event that the bill deposit is partially or fully consumed, the utility service provider may
require the consumer to replenish the same to its original amount, subject to prior notice and
reasonable terms.

Upon termination of service and full settlement of all outstanding obligations, the utility service
provider shall refund the unused portion of the bill deposit, together with any accrued interest,
within one (1) month from termination.

SECTION 19. Payment of Bills. — Consumers shall pay their utility bills not later than fifteen
(15) days from receipt of the monthly bill.
In cases of high or unusual billings, or other billing irregularities, the consumer may defer
payment of the disputed amount, provided that a formal complaint has been filed with the utility
service provider. During the pendency of the complaint, the consumer shall not be subjected
to disconnection in accordance with Section 11 of this Act.

                CHAPTER IV – DUTIES OF UTILITY SERVICE PROVIDERS

SECTION 20. Service Standards. — Providers must ensure continuous, quality, and safe
service. Interruptions must be limited, scheduled, and announced in advance whenever
possible. In case of unscheduled interruptions or service outages, providers shall explain the
cause, estimated time to resolve, and compensation, if any, within twenty-four (24) hours.

SECTION 21. Customer Service. — Providers must establish hotlines, grievance desks, and
online platforms to receive complaints, which must be resolved within a period not exceeding
fifteen (15) working days. If unresolved within the prescribed period, the matter may be
elevated to the appropriate regulatory agency or, in their absence, to the regular courts.

SECTION 22. Equipment and Installation. — All equipment shall be properly installed,
regularly inspected, and meet regulatory safety standards.

In line with this provision, all utility service providers must conduct an inspection/clean up
within their respective jurisdictions/authorized areas for all non-operational, tangled, or
sagging wires and remove, replace, or fix the same. In the same manner, all utility consumers
shall have the right to demand inspection/clean up of their respective installed equipment,
subject to a refund for overbillings and overpayments, if applicable.
In cases of permanent termination or retirement of service facilities, utility service providers
shall be obligated to remove any wires, cables, devices, or other equipment they have installed
within or connected to the consumer’s premises or public utility infrastructure, including poles,
posts, or service lines.

The removal shall commence within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of the termination or
retirement of service, without additional charge to the consumer. Failure to comply shall
subject the utility service provider to administrative sanctions and penalties as may be
imposed by the appropriate regulatory agency.

                             CHAPTER V – FINAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 23. Administrative Sanctions. — Utility service providers that violate any provision
of this Act shall be subject to fines or penalties as may be provided under existing laws, rules,
or regulations, and in the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act.

SECTION 24. Penalty for Illegal Disconnection. — Aside from the penalty that may be
imposed by the concerned regulatory agency, any disconnection of electricity, water, or
internet services that do not comply with the due process requirements under this Act shall
entitle the consumer to a rebate equivalent to the estimated average daily consumption based
on the consumer’s annual average billing, multiplied by the number of days the disconnection
occurred.

Such rebate shall be automatically credited to the consumer’s account in the next billing cycle,
without need of demand and without prejudice to other remedies available under law.

SECTION 25. Prohibition Against Predatory Pricing and Bundling. — No internet service
provider shall impose tied contracts, unreasonable lock-in periods, or bundle services that
cannot reasonably be disentangled by consumers.
For purposes of this section, real estate developers, condominium corporations, and
homeowner associations shall be prohibited from imposing or providing restrictions on, or
limiting the option of, condominium unit buyers and owners as well as homeowners, to choose
their preferred internet service providers. The Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development, in coordination with the NTC, shall be responsible for monitoring compliance
and prosecuting violations of this provision.

SECTION 26. Mandatory Public Hearings for Rate Increases. — No rate increase shall be
approved by any regulatory agency without first conducting public hearings with affected
stakeholders.

SECTION 27. No Wrong Door Policy. — To ensure accessible and efficient redress for
household utility complaints, a “No Wrong Door” policy shall apply to the Department of Energy
(DOE), the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the National
Water Resources Board (NWRB), local government units (LGUs), and such other government
agencies, offices or instrumentalities concerned.

Any of these agencies or LGUs that receive a consumer complaint involving electricity, water,
or internet services — regardless of whether it falls within their jurisdiction shall: (a)
acknowledge the complaint; (b) refer or endorse it to the appropriate regulatory agency within
five (5) working days; and (c) notify the consumer of such referral and provide relevant contact
information of the agency to which the complaint was endorsed.

This policy shall apply to all forms of communication, including but not limited to walk-in
inquiries, phone calls, emails, text messages, and online platforms such as social media.

For purposes of this Act, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) shall be the appropriate
agency for electricity-related concerns; the Local Water Utilities Administration (LWUA) for
water consumers outside Metro Manila; the Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System
– Regulatory Office (MWSS-RO) for water consumers within Metro Manila; and the National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) for internet-related concerns.

SECTION 28. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from effectivity of this Act, the ERC, LWUA, MWSS-RO, and NTC, in coordination with
the DOE, DICT, NWRB, and other relevant agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.

As part of the implementing rules and regulations, the ERC, LWUA, MWSS-RO, and NTC
shall formulate and adopt sector-specific minimum quality and performance standards
applicable to electricity, water, and internet service providers under their jurisdiction. These
standards shall cover, at the minimum, key performance indicators, consumer service levels,
and operational benchmarks, including, but not limited to, outage duration and frequency,
response time, service restoration, and billing accuracy.

Compliance with these minimum service standards shall be mandatory. Any failure by a utility
service provider to meet such standards, unless justified by force majeure or other valid
causes, shall constitute a violation of this Act.

SECTION 29. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in force and effect and will not
diminish rights already granted in other laws, rules, or regulations, provided that they do not
conflict with the provisions of this Act.
SECTION 30. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation contrary to, or inconsistent
with, the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended accordingly.

SECTION 31. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in at
least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
