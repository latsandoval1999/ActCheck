                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 7900, or the High-Value Crops Development Act of 1995, established
the national framework for the production, processing, marketing and distribution of high-value
crops. Three decades later, climate change, biotechnology, plant-variety innovation, stricter
product standards, supply-chain disruptions and growing demand for renewable and bio-
based materials require a more integrated, science-based, market-oriented and institutionally
sustained approach.

        This bill expressly includes bamboo as a high-value crop and recognizes it as a
strategic industrial crop. Bamboo can provide food, fiber, biomass, engineered and
construction materials, furniture, packaging, bioenergy, biochar, biomaterials and other value-
added products. Developed through appropriate agroforestry and multi-storey farming
systems, it can diversify rural livelihoods and support climate, soil, water and ecosystem
objectives, subject to environmental, biodiversity, land-use, agrarian reform and ancestral-
domain laws.

        The measure establishes within the Department of Agriculture the Office of the
Undersecretary for High-Value Crops Development, Innovation and Bioeconomy. The Office
shall provide policy guidelines on technology management and innovation, exercise direct
oversight over the National High-Value Crops and Bioeconomy Development Program, and
supervise the functional roles, mandates and performance of its component offices.

         Two Assistant Secretaries shall support the Undersecretary. The Assistant Secretary
for High-Value Crops Program Management shall coordinate implementation, technical
services, farming systems, bamboo and industrial-crop development, agribusiness,
processing, standards, investment and markets. The Assistant Secretary for High-Value
Crops Knowledge and Agricultural Statistics shall lead the National High-Value Crops
Information System, agricultural statistics and data science, knowledge management, market
intelligence, geospatial services, monitoring, evaluation and results reporting.

        The bill further integrates research, innovation, biotechnology, genetic resources,
plant-variety development, quality planting materials, climate-resilient production, integrated
development areas, processing, enterprise development, financing, standards, trade and
agricultural bioeconomy applications. It strengthens the High-Value Crops Development Fund
and permits the Department to propose an indicative initial program envelope of Five Hundred
Million Pesos for strategic industrial bamboo development, subject to the regular budget
process and without creating an automatic appropriation.
        The proposed structure is designed as an integrating and coordinating mechanism. It
preserves the statutory mandates of the Philippine Statistics Authority and other competent
regulatory, research, standards, environmental and local-government institutions, while
requiring compliance with government-optimization, civil service, position-classification,
budgeting, accounting and auditing laws.

       In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

                              AN ACT
EXPRESSLY INCLUDING BAMBOO AS A HIGH-VALUE CROP, RECOGNIZING IT AS AN
INDUSTRIAL CROP, AND STRENGTHENING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE HIGH-
VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT ACT BY INSTITUTIONALIZING THE NATIONAL HIGH-
VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT AND BIOECONOMY OFFICE AND THE NATIONAL
HIGH-VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT AND BIOECONOMY PROGRAM, AMENDING
FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7900, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “HIGH-
VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT ACT OF 1995”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Enhanced High-Value Crops
Development and Bioeconomy Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to accelerate the
sustainable, inclusive, science-based, climate-resilient, and market-driven development of
high-value crops as a principal engine of agricultural modernization, agro-industrialization,
rural employment, nutrition security, export diversification, and a regenerative and circular
bioeconomy. The State shall promote productive landscapes and competitive enterprises that
create value from biological resources while conserving biodiversity, restoring ecosystems,
improving soil and water conditions, reducing waste, and increasing the incomes and
resilience of farmers, Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples, agrarian reform
beneficiaries, cooperatives, associations, workers, micro, small and medium enterprises, and
rural communities.
        Towards this end, bamboo is expressly included as a high-value crop and recognized
as an industrial crop because of its renewable, multipurpose, and scalable uses for food, feed,
fuel, fiber, engineered materials, furniture, construction, bioenergy, biochar, carbon
sequestration, and other value-added products and ecosystem services. The State shall
strengthen the governance, financing, research and innovation, production, processing,
logistics, enterprise development, and domestic and export market systems for all high-value
crops through the National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Office and the
National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Program.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – Section 4 of Republic Act No. 7900 is hereby amended to read
as follows:
        “SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
            a) ‘Program’ refers to the National High-Value Crops Development and
               Bioeconomy Program established under Section 7-A of this Act.

           b) ‘High-value crops’ or ‘HVCs’ refer to crops other than traditional staple
              crops which have demonstrated or potential high economic value,
              value-adding, employment generation, nutritional, industrial, export,
              environmental, or climate-resilience benefits. These include, but are not
               limited to: BAMBOO; coffee and cacao; fruit crops, including citrus,
               cashew, guyabano, papaya, mango, pineapple, strawberry, jackfruit,
               rambutan, durian, mangosteen, guava, lanzones, and watermelon; root
               and tuber crops, including potato, sweet potato, cassava, taro, and ube;
               vegetable crops, including asparagus, broccoli, cabbage, celery,
               carrots, cauliflower, radish, tomato, bell pepper, and patola; legumes,
               pole sitao, snap beans, and garden pea; spices and condiments,
               including black pepper, garlic, ginger, and onion; cut flowers,
               ornamental foliage plants, herbs, medicinal and aromatic crops; and
               such other crops as the Department of Agriculture may identify, after
               consultation with stakeholders, based on agro-climatic suitability, food
               and nutrition requirements, market demand, value-adding potential,
               resource-use efficiency, and contribution to the bioeconomy.

          c) ‘Bamboo’ refers to plants belonging to the family Poaceae, subfamily
             Bambusoideae, including clumping and running species suitable for
             food, agricultural, environmental, industrial, construction, energy, and
             other lawful uses.

          d) ‘Industrial crop’ refers to a crop cultivated or managed as a renewable
             biological raw material for processing, manufacturing, construction,
             energy, pharmaceuticals, nutraceuticals, textiles, chemicals,
             engineered materials, and other industrial or commercial applications,
             without excluding its food, feed, ecological, cultural, and livelihood
             uses.

          e) ‘Bioeconomy’ refers to the sustainable and circular production,
             mobilization, processing, and use of renewable biological resources,
             knowledge, science, technology, and innovation to produce food, feed,
             fiber, materials, energy, services, and value-added products, while
             conserving biodiversity, maintaining ecosystem integrity, minimizing
             waste and pollution, and generating inclusive economic opportunities.

          f)   ‘Idle and abandoned land’ refers to agricultural land as defined under
               existing agrarian reform and land-use laws.

          g) ‘Alienable and disposable lands’ refer to lands of the public domain
             classified and declared as not needed for forest purposes, subject to
             existing laws.

          h) ‘Forest lands’ refer to lands of the public domain classified as forest
             land under existing laws. Their inclusion in any activity under this Act
             shall not alter their legal classification or dispense with the permits,
             tenurial instruments, safeguards, and approvals required by law.”

SEC. 4. Recognition of Bamboo as an Industrial Crop. – A new Section 4-A is hereby inserted
after Section 4 of Republic Act No. 7900, to read as follows:

       “SEC. 4-A. Bamboo as a High-Value and Industrial Crop. — Bamboo is hereby
       expressly included as a high-value crop and recognized as an industrial crop
       under the Program. This recognition shall provide the basis for integrated
       government support across the bamboo value chain, including quality planting
       materials, sustainable farm and landscape establishment, harvesting,
       aggregation, primary and secondary processing, product standards, design
       and engineering, financing and insurance, enterprise incubation,
      commercialization, public procurement, and domestic and export market
      development.
      “Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the conversion or clearing
      of natural forests, protected areas, critical habitats, or other environmentally
      critical areas; alter land classification or tenure; impair vested or ancestral
      domain rights; or dispense with free and prior informed consent, environmental
      compliance, biodiversity safeguards, or any permit required under existing
      laws.”

SEC. 5. National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Program. – A new Section
7-A is hereby inserted after Section 7 of Republic Act No. 7900, to read as follows:

      “SEC. 7-A. National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy
      Program. — There is hereby established a comprehensive National High-
      Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Program, hereinafter referred to
      as the Program, which shall serve as the national framework for the
      coordinated development of priority high-value crop industries and value
      chains. The Program shall consolidate and harmonize relevant existing and
      future DA programs, projects, and activities, without diminishing the mandates
      of other agencies.

      “The Program shall include:
      (a) national and commodity roadmaps, spatial and agro-climatic suitability
      planning, supply-and-demand programming, and investment plans;
      (b) seed, nursery, tissue-culture, propagation, accreditation, certification, and
      traceability systems for quality planting materials;
      (c) research, biotechnology, breeding, mechanization, digital agriculture,
      demonstration, extension, and technology transfer;
      (d) climate-resilient, diversified, multi-storey, regenerative, organic, protected-
      cultivation, and sustainable agroforestry farm-enterprise models;
      (e) irrigation, water management, farm-to-market logistics, aggregation, post-
      harvest, cold-chain, processing, testing, storage, and distribution facilities;
      (f) affordable credit, guarantees, crop insurance, grants, blended finance,
      public-private partnerships, and investment facilitation;
      (g) farmer clustering and consolidation, block farming, cooperatives, contract
      growing with equitable terms, agribusiness incubation, and enterprise
      upgrading;
      (h) food safety, plant health, grades and standards, certification, intellectual
      property, geographic indications, digital traceability, and quality assurance;
      (i) institutional procurement, domestic market development, export promotion,
      market intelligence, and stable and transparent market linkages;
      (j) circular use of biomass and by-products, renewable energy, bio-based
      materials, waste minimization, carbon management, and payment for
      ecosystem services, consistent with applicable standards and safeguards;
      (k) development of women, youth, Indigenous Peoples, agrarian reform
      beneficiaries, and smallholders as producers, innovators, workers, and
      entrepreneurs; and
      (l) a unified information, monitoring, evaluation, and learning system with
      commodity, geographic, beneficiary, investment, income, employment,
      nutrition, environmental, and market indicators.

      “The Secretary of Agriculture shall approve, and update at least once every
      five (5) years, a National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy
      Roadmap containing measurable targets, priority commodities and production
      zones, investment requirements, agency accountabilities, financing strategies,
      safeguards, and a results framework.”


SEC. 6. Bamboo Development Component. – A new Section 7-B is hereby inserted after
Section 7-A of Republic Act No. 7900, to read as follows:

      “SEC. 7-B. Bamboo Development Component. — The Program shall maintain
      a dedicated Bamboo Development Component covering the complete farm-to-
      industry ecosystem. In addition to the interventions under Section 7-A, it shall
      support:
      (a) mapping and registration of bamboo resources, farms, nurseries,
      processors, and enterprises;
      (b) accreditation of nurseries and protocols for true-to-type, healthy, and site-
      appropriate planting materials;
      (c) species- and site-specific production, harvesting, treatment, preservation,
      grading, and chain-of-custody protocols;
      (d) sustainable agroforestry and multi-storey systems on appropriate
      agricultural, idle, marginal, degraded, and other legally available lands;
      (e) community nurseries, plantation and farm establishment, rehabilitation, and
      maintenance;
      (f) machinery, common service facilities, drying, treatment, engineered-
      bamboo and bio-based processing, product design, testing, and certification;
      (g) development of bamboo shoots, fiber, furniture, construction materials,
      energy, biochar, and other lawful products;
      (h) research, innovation, skills development, technology demonstration, and
      extension;
      (i) market aggregation, long-term supply arrangements, responsible public
      procurement, domestic promotion, and export development; and
      (j) scientifically credible measurement, reporting, and verification of carbon and
      ecosystem services.

      “The DA shall coordinate this component with the Department of Environment
      and Natural Resources, Department of Trade and Industry, Department of
      Science and Technology, Department of Public Works and Highways,
      Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development, Technical
      Education and Skills Development Authority, Commission on Higher
      Education, local government units, state universities and colleges, farmer and
      industry organizations, Indigenous Peoples, academe, financing institutions,
      and the private sector.

      “Bamboo activities on forest lands, protected areas, ancestral domains,
      agrarian reform lands, and other regulated areas shall be undertaken only
      under the appropriate land-use, tenure, environmental, social, and cultural
      safeguards and the authority of the agency having jurisdiction.”

SEC. 7. National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Office. – Section 11 of
Republic Act No. 7900 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “SEC. 11. National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Office. —
      The National High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Office,
      hereinafter referred to as the Office, is hereby institutionalized within the
      Department of Agriculture as the principal policy, planning, coordinating,
      implementing, monitoring, and investment-facilitation office for the Program. It
      shall absorb and succeed to the high-value crops program functions of the
      agribusiness development group referred to in this Act and such relevant
      functions, personnel, appropriations, records, property, and obligations as may
      be identified in the implementing rules and approved reorganization plan.

      “The Office shall be headed by an Undersecretary for High-Value Crops
      Development and Bioeconomy, appointed in accordance with law, who shall
      exercise executive direction and supervision over the Program. The
      Undersecretary shall be assisted by an Assistant Secretary who shall
      concurrently serve as National Program Director and head the National
      Program Management Office, and by at least two (2) Directors responsible for:
      (a) policy, planning, investment, markets, data, and results management; and
      (b) farming systems, research, innovation, extension, enterprise, and
      bioeconomy development. The Secretary may establish divisions, commodity
      directorates, technical units, and time-bound program teams as necessary,
      subject to applicable civil service, compensation, position classification,
      budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws.

      “The Office shall: (1) formulate and recommend national policies, roadmaps,
      standards, investment priorities, and annual plans and budgets; (2) coordinate
      implementation by DA operating units, attached agencies and corporations,
      regional field offices, and local government units; (3) convene commodity and
      industry councils or technical working groups with balanced representation
      from smallholders, cooperatives, industry, academe, civil society, Indigenous
      Peoples, women, and youth; (4) develop, appraise, package, and monitor
      public, private, and blended-finance investments; (5) integrate production,
      logistics, processing, market, and environmental data and maintain a public-
      facing sector information system, subject to data-privacy and confidentiality
      laws; (6) establish performance standards, beneficiary targeting, monitoring
      and evaluation, and grievance and accountability mechanisms; (7) coordinate
      research, innovation, extension, standards, financing, insurance, market
      development, and international cooperation; and (8) perform such other
      functions necessary to implement this Act.

      “The Regional Executive Director of each DA Regional Field Office shall
      establish or designate a Regional High-Value Crops Development and
      Bioeconomy Program Management Office with qualified technical personnel
      and counterpart focal units at the provincial and local levels, in coordination
      with local government units. National and regional organizational structures
      shall ensure commodity expertise and area-based implementation.”

SEC. 11. Program Convergence and Local Implementation. – A new Section 11-C is hereby

       “SEC. 11-C. Program Convergence and Local Implementation. — National

SEC. 12. Safeguards and Equitable Benefit-Sharing. – A new Section 11-D is hereby inserted
after Section 11-C of Republic Act No. 7900, to read as follows:

      “SEC. 11-D. Safeguards and Equitable Benefit-Sharing. — Projects under the
      Program shall comply with environmental, land-use, agrarian reform, labor,
      competition, consumer protection, Indigenous Peoples’ rights, access and
      benefit-sharing, biosafety, food safety, and data-privacy laws. Government
      support shall prioritize organized smallholders and viable enterprises that
      demonstrate fair contracting, transparent pricing or pricing formulas, timely
      payment, responsible resource use, occupational safety, and equitable sharing of
      value and risks. No activity under this Act shall result in involuntary displacement,
      unlawful land conversion, or the introduction of invasive species.”

SEC. 13. Organizational Transition. – Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Secretary of Agriculture, in coordination with the Department of Budget and
Management and the Civil Service Commission, shall prepare and implement an
organizational and staffing plan for the Office and the national and regional Program
Management Offices. The transition shall, to the greatest extent practicable, protect security
of tenure and utilize qualified existing personnel. No permanent employee shall be separated
or demoted except for a valid cause and in accordance with civil service laws, rules, and due
process.

SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount of Five Hundred Million Pesos (₱500,000,000.00) is
hereby authorized for the initial implementation of this Act, including organizational transition,
national and regional program management, bamboo and other priority commodity
development, quality planting materials, research and innovation, enterprise and common
service facilities, digital information systems, and monitoring and evaluation. The amount
actually required for the first year shall be charged against available appropriations of the
Department of Agriculture, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws.
Thereafter, the amounts necessary for continued implementation shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.
“The DA and participating agencies may access other lawful sources, including official
development assistance, grants, donations, counterpart funds, income from authorized
activities, public-private partnerships, and financing from government financial institutions.
Projects demonstrably contributing to the Sustainable Development Goals may be proposed
for funding from applicable SDG allocations under Section 288-A of the National Internal
Revenue Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 11467 and subsequent laws, subject to the
determination of the appropriate national planning authority and the regular budget
preparation, authorization, release, utilization, and reporting requirements. Nothing herein
shall be construed as an automatic earmark or direct appropriation from such revenues.”

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred twenty (120) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DA, through the Office and in consultation with the Inter-Agency
Committee, local government units, farmers and industry stakeholders, Indigenous Peoples,
academe, civil society, and other concerned sectors, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation. Failure to issue the rules within the prescribed
period shall not prevent the implementation of provisions that are self-executing.

SEC. 16. Congressional Oversight Review. – The appropriate committees of the Senate and
the House of Representatives shall conduct a joint review of the implementation of this Act
not later than three (3) years after its effectivity and every three (3) years thereafter, including
the adequacy of appropriations, performance of the Office and Program, results for
smallholders and enterprises, and effectiveness of environmental and social safeguards.

SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and
other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
