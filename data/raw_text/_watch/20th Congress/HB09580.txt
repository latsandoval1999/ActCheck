                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The right to bail is a constitutional guarantee anchored on the presumption of
innocence. Thousands of detainees remain in congested jails, not because they pose
a danger to society, but because they are too poor to post bail.

       Republic Act No. 10389, otherwise known as the “Recognizance Act of 2012,”
was enacted to address this inequity by allowing the release of indigent accused
persons on recognizance. While the law provides a framework for such release, it does
not establish a clear and standardized mechanism for determining indigency or
assessing whether posting bail would result in undue hardship. The absence of uniform
standards has resulted in inconsistent applications across jurisdictions.

        Recent policy discussions have highlighted the urgency of reform. During the
Jail Decongestion Summit convened by the Justice Sector Coordinating Council (JSCC)
composed of the Supreme Court, the Department of Justice, and the Department of
the Interior and Local Government, stakeholders underscored that jail congestion
remains a systemic concern largely driven by prolonged pretrial detention and the
inability of low-income accused persons to post bail. The Summit emphasized the need
for evidence-based release mechanisms, improved bail reform policies, and structured
risk assessment tools to ensure that detention prior to conviction is used only when
necessary to secure court appearance or protect public safety.

        The Summit further recognized that a significant proportion of persons deprived
of liberty are charged with non-violent offenses and remain incarcerated solely due to
poverty. It recommended stronger coordination among courts, prosecutors, public
attorneys, and local government units to standardize procedures for indigency
determination and pretrial release.

       This bill responds directly to those policy recommendations by strengthening
and operationalizing the Recognizance Act through the institutionalization of a
Financial Capacity Assessment prior to the grant of recognizance.

        By introducing financial-based standards, this measure promotes fairness,
uniformity, and transparency in the administration of pretrial release. It strengthens
judicial discretion through structured guidelines while safeguarding the constitutional
rights of the accused.

        More importantly, the bill contributes to the urgent national effort to decongest
detention facilities. Rational and evidence-based release mechanisms will help
alleviate overcrowding, reduce detention costs, improve case flow management, and
uphold the dignity of persons awaiting trial. This proposed amendment affirms a
simple but fundamental principle: liberty before conviction should not depend on
wealth, but on justice and risk.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                AN ACT
     STRENGTHENING AND EXPANDING THE APPLICATION OF RELEASE ON
     RECOGNIZANCE, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10389,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “RECOGNIZANCE ACT OF 2012”


 1   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
 2   Congress assembled:
 3
 4         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Expanded Release
 5   on Recognizance Act.”
 6
 7          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 8   State to uphold the constitutional presumption of innocence, promote social justice,
 9   and ensure that poverty shall not be a ground for deprivation of liberty. The State
10   shall strengthen release on recognizance as a primary mode of pretrial release for
11   indigent and near-indigent accused who pose minimal risk of flight or danger to the
12   community, while ensuring accountability and the orderly administration of justice.
13
14          SECTION 3. Definition of terms. - For purposes of this Act:
15      (a) “Indigent” shall refer to an accused who qualifies for free legal assistance under
16          the standards of the Public Attorney’s Office or the Department of Justice.
17
18      (b) “Near-indigent” shall refer to an accused whose income or financial resources
19          marginally exceed the applicable poverty threshold or indigency cut-off but are
20          insufficient to reasonably afford the required bail without impairing basic
21          subsistence needs, as determined by the court.

 2          SECTION 4. Expanded Coverage of Persons Qualified for Release on
 3   Recognizance. – Section 6 of Republic Act No. 10389 is hereby amended to read as
 4   follows:

 6         “SECTION. 6. Requirements. — The competent court where a criminal case
 7         has been filed against a person covered under this Act shall, upon motion, order
 8         the release of the detained person on recognizance to a qualified custodian:
 9         Provided, That the court, after hearing, determines that the accused is indigent
10         or near-indigent and poses a low risk of flight or danger to the community, and
11         that all of the following requirements are complied with:
12
13         (a) The accused is indigent or near-indigent, as determined by the court

42          SECTION 5. Financial Capacity Assessment - In determining financial
43   capacity, the court shall consider, among others, the accused’s income, employment
44   status, number of dependents, existing debts and liabilities, and other relevant
45   financial obligations.
46
 1          (a) Financial Capacity Assessment. — Prior to granting release on recognizance,
 2   the court shall conduct a summary financial capacity assessment to determine whether the
 3   accused is indigent or near-indigent and unable to post bail without undue hardship.
 4   In conducting such assessment, the court shall consider:
 5       1. The gross monthly household income of the accused from all lawful sources;
 6       2. The number of dependents and total household size;
 7       3. Mandatory deductions and basic subsistence expenses, including food, shelter,
 8          utilities, education, transportation, and medical needs;
 9       4. Available liquid assets and real or personal property, excluding the primary
10          residence and tools of livelihood;
11       5. The amount of bail fixed and its proportion to the disposable income of the
12          accused.
13
14          (b) Undue Hardship. — Posting bail shall be deemed to cause undue hardship
15   when payment thereof would substantially impair the ability of the accused or the
16   accused’s household to maintain minimum basic subsistence needs for a reasonable
17   period as determined by the court.
18
19         (c) Nature of Proceedings. — The financial capacity shall be summary in
20   nature and shall not require a full evidentiary trial. The prosecution shall be given the
21   opportunity to be heard and to present relevant evidence.
22
23          (d) Burden of Proof. — The accused shall initially establish indigency or near-
24   indigency through sworn declaration and supporting documentation. The prosecution
25   may rebut the same. The court shall make written findings supporting its
26   determination.”
27
28         The Supreme Court is hereby authorized to promulgate rules adopting a
29   standardized risk assessment tool consistent with this Act.

31         SECTION 6. Automatic Review of Prolonged Detention. - The court shall
32   conduct an automatic review of the custody status of an accused detained beyond the
33   minimum imposable penalty or due to delays not attributable to the accused.

36   implement this Act within ninety (90) days from effectivity.
