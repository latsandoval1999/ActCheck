      PARTICIPATORY GRASSROOTS BUDGETING, STRENGTHENING (CSOs),
    EMPOWERING CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS, INSTITUTIONALIZING A
    BOTTOM-UP BUDGETING MECHANISM IN DEVELOPMENT PLANNING AND
            BUDGETING, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR


                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Section 23, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides that the State shall
encourage non-governmental, community-based, or sectoral organizations that promote the
welfare of the nation. Furthermore, Section 16, Article XIII mandates that the right of the
people and their organizations to effective and reasonable participation at all levels of social,
political, and economic decision-making shall not be abridged, and the State shall facilitate the
establishment of adequate consultation mechanisms.

A study by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) found that evaluations of
early participatory budgeting initiatives must guide our laws today. The study highlighted
bottlenecks that hinder effective community consultation and the lack of formal integration
between community action plans and local investment frameworks. 1

Moreover, the persistent corruption scandals and irregularities that have destroyed public
infrastructure and public funds over the past years mean that funds meant for classrooms,
hospitals, and basic public services vanished into questionable contracts while ordinary
families continue to struggle with basic needs.

This is what happens when public funds are allocated through ambiguous, top-down channels
without direct public oversight; they become highly susceptible to misuse and diversion.

This bill seeks to institutionalize a permanent bottom-up budgeting mechanism in local and
national development planning. The primary goal of this bill is to promote inclusive
development by ensuring that government funds are allocated to projects that directly address
the needs and priorities of local communities.

By empowering Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) to actively participate in project
formulation, budget prioritization, and transparent third-party monitoring, public governance
becomes more transparent, responsive, and aligned with grassroots needs.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                    AN ACT STRENGTHENING
   PARTICIPATORY GRASSROOTS BUDGETING, STRENGTHENING (CSOs),
 EMPOWERING CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS, INSTITUTIONALIZING A
 BOTTOM-UP BUDGETING MECHANISM IN DEVELOPMENT PLANNING AND
         BUDGETING, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Grassroots Participatory Budgeting
Act.”

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to recognize, protect, and enhance
the right of the people and their organizations to meaningful participation at all levels of
social, political, and economic decision-making. Further, the State shall promote a
bottom-to-top approach in development planning, policy formulation, and governance by
institutionalizing direct collaboration between government institutions and accredited Civil
Society Organizations (CSOs).

Sec. 3. Grassroots Participatory Budgeting (GPB). – Grassroots Participatory Budgeting
(GPB), formerly known as Bottom-Up Budgeting (BUB), is hereby institutionalized as a
fundamental approach in the preparation of the National Budget. Under this process,
community-identified priority poverty reduction projects shall form part of the annual agency
budget proposals submitted to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) for
inclusion in the General Appropriations Act (GAA).

Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. - For the purposes of this Act:

   a. Grassroots Participatory Budgeting System – refers to an institutionalized planning and
      budgeting approach wherein local development needs and priority projects identified
       by cities and municipalities, through genuine community consultations led by basic
       sector organizations and Civil Society Organizations, are systematically integrated into
       local and national expenditure programs.
   b.​ Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) – refer to any non-government, non- profit,
       voluntary group or organized citizens' association operating independently from the
       State to promote public interest, local development, and social welfare. This includes
       Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs), People's Organizations (POs),
       cooperatives, faith- based groups, and academic or sectoral associations.
   c.​ Non-Government Organizations (NGO) – refers to a non-stock, non-profit voluntary
       organization, duly registered with appropriate government agencies, formed to provide
       service, advocacy, or development interventions.
   d.​ People's Organization (PO) – refers to a bona fide, community-based, or
       member-governed association of citizens representing basic sectors (e.g. farmers,
       fisherfolk, urban poor, workers, women, youth, persons with disabilities, indigenous
       peoples, senior citizens) established to advance the direct rights and collective welfare
       of its members.
   e.​ Local Poverty Reduction Action Plan (LPRAP) or Local Development Action Plan
       (LDAP) – refers to the official local government plan containing programs and
       projects collectively drawn through a mandatory participatory process with CSOs,
       directly addressing the needs of marginalized and vulnerable communities.

Sec. 5. Institutionalization of Grassroots Consultations. – All Barangays, Municipalities,
Cities, and Provinces shall conduct structured participatory consultation assemblies at least
twice a year with accredited Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) operating within their
territorial jurisdictions. Priorities identified during these grassroots assemblies shall form the
core component of Local Poverty Reduction Action Plans, Local Development Investment
Programs, and annual local budget proposals.

Sec. 6. Alignment with Cash-Based Budgeting and Procurement. – To ensure compliance
with the Annual Cash-Based Budgeting System, grassroots community plans and Local
Poverty Reduction Action Plans shall be finalized not later than July thirty-first (31st) of the
preceding fiscal year for inclusion in local and national budget preparation. The Department of
the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM) shall provide technical templates and advance engineering guidance to prevent
procurement and implementation delays.

Sec. 7. Public Transparency and Monitoring. – All LRAPs, approved project lists,
procurement documents, and implementation progress reports shall be published on the
official websites of the LGUs, DILG, and the DBM.

Sec. 8. Appropriations. - The funding required for the implementation of projects identified
under the GPB process shall be incorporated in the annual General Appropriations Act
(GAA).

Sec. 9. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act shall be declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
Sec. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, memorandum orders,
memorandum circulars, administrative orders, ordinances or parts thereof which are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby deemed repealed or modified
accordingly.


Sec. 11. Non-Accredited CSOs. - Non-accredited CSOs may still participate in the budget
process by submitting position papers for the consideration of the NGA or GOCC and by
attending and observing public consultations. They may be invited to expound on their criåque
or proposals when said institutions deem it fit. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
deprive non-accredited CSOs of the rights, privileges, and incentives accorded to them by the
Constitution, existing laws, executive orders, rules and regulations, and other issuances.

Sec. 12. Public and Timely Disclosure of Budget Documents and Other Related Data. - For
CSOs to participate in an informed manner, the NGA or GOCC shall post on their websites or
other public posting areas, budget documents to which CSOs shall have access, as provided
for under Section 7 of this Act.

Sec. 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act shall be declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining part or provisions not otherwise affected shall remain in force.

Sec. 14. Repealing Clause. - Any law, decree, ordinance, or administrative circular not
consistent with any provision of this Act is hereby amended, repealed, or modified
accordingly.

Sec. 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
