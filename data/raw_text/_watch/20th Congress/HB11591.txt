EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine sardine industry stands at the crossroads of fisheries, agriculture, food
manufacturing, and coastal economic growth. It nourishes millions of Filipinos and supports the
livelihoods of fisherfolk, workers, manufacturers, processors, transporters, distributors, tfaders,
and a vibrant spectrum of enterprises, from micro to large-scale.

At the heart of the industry lies the City of Zamboanga, celebrated as the “Sardines Capital
of the Philippines.” Here, a thriving hub of sardine-related activities pulses with energy, from
fishing and fish landing to warehousing, processing, canning, packaging, transportation, and
distribution.

Across Zamboanga’s coastal barangays, communities are woven into the fabric of the
sardine industry. These neighborhoods host and support a network of facilities for storage,
warehousing, canning, processing, packaging, logistics, and more. The sardine industry is aot just
a fisheries pursuit, but a dynamic coastal industrial ecosystem that generates jobs and economic
vitality throughout the city and neighboring areas. depend solely on increasing the quantity of
sardines harvested or processed. The Philippines must move toward a science-driven, innoyation-
led, higher-value sardine economy.

The fundamental principle of this measure is straightforward: we must harness precise
science and bold innovation to unlock greater economic value from every sustainably sourced
sardine.

By embracing scientific research, accurate stock assessments, cutting-edge fisheries
technology, smarter post-harvest handling, modern processing, automation, artificial intelligence,
energy-saving manufacturing, innovative packaging, creative product development, by-product
utilization, digital traceability, and sharper market intelligence, the industry can boost productivity
while trimming waste and unnecessary costs.

These advancements translate directly into economic gains.

When science and innovation empower enterprises to create more value from the same or
fewer resources, profit margins rise. These stronger, more sustainable margins unlock new
opportunities to reinvest in machinery, research, workforce training, business growth, new
products, and untapped markets. Innovation increases productivity and value addition. Higher
productivity and value addition improve profitability. Higher profitability encourages
reinvestment and expansion. Expansion creates more jobs. Greater production of higher-value

===== [page 2] =====
goods expands domestic economic activity and exports. Increased domestic economic activity and
exports contribute to Philippine GDP growth.

This measure sees science and innovation not just as research costs, but as powerful tools
for job creation, investment growth, enterprise profitability, export expansion, and a stronger
national economy.

The proposed Support for Sardine Science and Innovation Program will offer a unified
framework to realize these goals. It will champion research and development, fisheries s¢ience,
technology transfer, modernized processing, sustainable packaging, energy efficiency, high-value
product creation, digitalization, workforce training, export growth, and the commercialization of
new ideas.

Particular emphasis shall be placed on value addition. Instead of relying primarily on
increasing fishing volume, the industry shall be encouraged to generate greater economié value
from sustainably harvested resources through improved processing, premium products, innovative
packaging, better quality, new food products, sardine-based ingredients, utilization of by-products,
and access to higher-value markets.

This strategy empowers Philippine enterprises to compete not just on price and volume,
but on quality, technology, innovation, sustainability, reliability, and true value.

The measure also honors the vital role of Zamboanga City. Its thriving sardine industry,
coastal communities, processing plants, canneries, warehouses, packaging operations, skilled
workforce, and allied enterprises form a solid foundation for building a national Sardine Science
and Innovation Center.

By boosting Zamboanga’s capacity for research, technology, processing innovation, skills
training, export promotion, and investment, the city can shine as a model for science-driven
fisheries industrialization, inspiring and supporting other sardine-producing regions acr@ss the
Philippines.

At its core, sustainability remains essential. Scientific innovation should never be a license
for overexploiting marine resources. Instead, science must guide the nation in setting sustainable
fishing levels, then harness technology, productivity, efficiency, and value addition to maximize
economic returns within those boundaries. The long-term profitability of the sardine industry
depends upon the continued availability of healthy sardine stocks and productive marine
ecosystems.

The proposed legislation aims to build an economic model where sustainability and
profitability go hand in hand, each strengthening the other.

With better science, the country gains a clearer understanding of its resources. With
innovation, it processes them more efficiently. With value addition, it earns more from sustainable
production. With higher profits, enterprises grow and create jobs. With rising exports and
economic activity, the industry makes a stronger mark on Philippine GDP.

It is to generate greater economic value, more jobs, stronger Filipino enterprises, inéreased
investments, expanded exports, and a deeper contribution to the Philippine economy, all while
safeguarding sardine resources for generations to come.

Passing this measure will lay the groundwork to transform the Philippine sardine ;
into a science-based, innovation-driven powerhouse that is globally competitive, creates jobs, and
drives economic growth.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bi is earnestly sought.

NA REIKO CHUA-TAI

Innovation Act.”

SEC. 12. Coastal Community and Barangay Participation. — The Program shall ensure

meaningful participation of coastal communities and barangays in sardine-producing areas.

Particular support shall be provided to qualified coastal barangays hosting or affected by
sardine-related fishing, landing, storage, warehousing, processing, canning, packaging,

transportation, and other ancillary activities.

Science and innovation programs shall seek to create additional livelihood and employment
opportunities in these communities through skills development, technology adoption, processing,

value addition, entrepreneurship, logistics, environmental services, and other related activities.

SEC. 13. Sustainable Fisheries Management. — All programs under this Act shall be

consistent with science-based fisheries management and conservation measures.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize or encourage overfishing, illegal fishing,

destructive fishing practices, or exploitation beyond scientifically established sustainable levels.

The economic objective of this Act shall be achieved primarily through greater
productivity, efficiency, value addition, technology, innovation, and better market returns, rather

than through unsustainable increases in fishing effort.

SEC. 24. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DA, DOST, and DTI, in consultation with the Council and stakeholders,

shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

SEC. 25. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this

Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General

Appropriations Act.

The Sardine Science and Innovation Fund may be augmented by grants, donations,

SEC. 26. Congressional Oversight. — The appropriate committees of the House of
Representatives and the Senate shall exercise continuing oversight over the implementation of this

SEC. 27. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or

unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 28. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and
other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,

amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 29. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
