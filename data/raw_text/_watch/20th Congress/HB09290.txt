                              AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PROHIBITION AGAINST ILLEGAL WITHHOLDING OF
PHILIPPINE PASSPORTS, PARTICULARLY OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS,
PROVIDING FOR ENFORCEMENT MECHANISMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

A Philippine passport is not just a travel document, it is a lifeline for Overseas Filipino
Workers (OFWs). It allows them to move freely, seek help from Philippine authorities,
leave abusive employers, and return home safely. Yet many OFWs and job applicants
still face a common abuse: their passports are taken or withheld by recruiters,
agencies, or employers. This practice traps them, limits their freedom, and makes
them more vulnerable to exploitation.

Our laws already make it clear that a passport belongs to the Philippine government
and must never be taken by private individuals or companies. It is illegal to con iscate
or use a passport as collateral for a loan or obligation, and such acts can lead to serious
penalties. The courts have also recognized that withholding passports is a form of
coercion and is often linked to illegal recruitment and even human traf icking.

This bill strengthens these existing protections by making it easier to report cases of
passport withholding and ensuring faster action to help victims recover their
documents. It promotes better coordination among government agencies so that
affected workers can quickly get assistance. It also reinforces the rule that passports
must never be used as leverage for debt or control, and that those who commit such
acts will be held accountable under the law.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Illegal Withholding of
Passport (OFW Protection) Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the rights
and promote the welfare of all Filipinos, especially Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs) and applicant workers, and to prevent coercion and abuse committed
through the illegal con iscation, retention, or withholding of Philippine passports and
travel documents.

Consistent with the New Philippine Passport Act, a Philippine passport is issued by
the State through the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), and its unlawful
withholding undermines personal liberty, mobility, and protection of migrant
workers.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act, the following terms shall
mean:



                                                                           3|Page
   (a) “Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) or Migrant Worker” refers to a person
       who is engaged, or has been engaged, in a remunerated activity in a State of
       which he or she is not a citizen, including those on board sea-going vessels,
       consistent with the Migrant Workers and Overseas Filipinos Act.

   (b) “Applicant worker” refers to a person who is in the process of applying for
       overseas employment, including those undergoing documentation, placement,
       or deployment processing.

   (c) “Passport” refers to any valid Philippine passport issued by the DFA.

   (d) “Without legal authority” means without authority granted by law, by lawful
       order of a competent court, or by lawful order/requirement of the DFA or
       other competent government authority expressly authorized by law.

SECTION 4. Prohibited Act; Relation to Existing Offenses. —

   (a) Illegal Withholding of Passport. Any person or entity who, without legal
       authority, con iscates, retains, or withholds any Philippine passport issued by
       the DFA, including the passport of an OFW or an Applicant Worker, shall be
       liable under this Act.

   (b) No diminution of liability for illegal recruitment and related
       offenses. The withholding or denial of passports or travel documents
       from applicant workers before departure for monetary or inancial
       considerations, or for any other reasons other than those authorized under the
       Labor Code and its implementing rules and regulations, shall continue to
       constitute illegal recruitment under the Migrant Workers and Overseas
       Filipinos Act.

   (c) Without prejudice clause. Prosecution under this Act shall be without
       prejudice to prosecution under RA 11983 (2024), RA 8042 (1995) as
       amended by RA 10022 (2010), and other applicable laws.

SECTION 5. Penalties. —

   (a) Basic penalty. Any person or entity found guilty of Illegal Withholding of
       Passport shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of not less than twelve (12)
       years and one (1) day but not more than twenty (20) years and a ine of not

      less than One million pesos (P1,000,000.00) but not more than Two million
      pesos (P2,000,000.00), consistent with the New Philippine Passport Act.

   (b) Public of icers. If the offender is a public of icer or employee, he or she shall,
       in addition to the penalties in subsection (a), suffer the accessory penalties
       provided by law, including administrative liability, without prejudice to
       dismissal and disquali ication pursuant to applicable civil service rules.

   (c) Juridical entities. If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, or
       other juridical entity, the of icers who authorized, directed, knowingly
       permitted, or participated in the act shall be criminally liable. Regulatory
       consequences (including cancellation of license/accreditation where
       applicable) shall be referred to the proper government agency.

   (d) Aliens. If the offender is an alien, he or she shall, after service of sentence, be
       subject to deportation in accordance with immigration laws.

SECTION 6. Reporting, Complaint, and Assistance Mechanism. —

   (a) Any person with personal knowledge of a violation may ile a complaint with
       the Of ice of the Prosecutor, the DOJ, the Philippine National Police (PNP), the
       National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), or Philippine foreign service posts for
       endorsement to the proper authorities.

   (b) Government agencies concerned shall provide a mechanism for OFWs and
       applicant workers to report illegal withholding and obtain assistance for the
       recovery of passports.

SECTION 7. Extraterritorial Application; Venue. —

   (a) This Act shall apply to offenses committed within the Philippines. It shall
       likewise apply to offenses committed outside the Philippines when the
       offender is a Filipino, or when the victim is a Filipino passport holder and the
       offender is found in the Philippines, subject to the Constitution, applicable
       treaties, and generally accepted principles of international law.

   (b) When the offense is committed outside the Philippines, the criminal action
       may be instituted in the Regional Trial Court (RTC) of:



                                                                              5|Page
       (1) the place where the offender is arrested or found in the Philippines; or
       (2) the place where the victim resides; or
       (3) Metro Manila, at the option of the public prosecutor, when venue cannot be
           reasonably determined.
       (4) No double jeopardy for the same conduct. No prosecution shall be
           commenced under this Act when the accused has been inally convicted or
           acquitted by a court of competent jurisdiction for the same act that
           constitutes the offense, consistent with constitutional protections.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DOJ, DFA, DOLE, and the Department of Migrant Workers (if
applicable), shall issue the necessary rules to operationalize reporting, assistance,
referral, and coordination for prosecution, consistent with this Act and RA 11983
(2024).

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
