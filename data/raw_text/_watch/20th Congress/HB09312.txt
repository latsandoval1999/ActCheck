                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to promote local participation and equitable benefit-sharing
in the development, ownership, and operation of indigenous, potential, and
renewable energy resources. It grants qualified local government units (LGUs),
communities, electric cooperatives (ECs), and distribution utilities (DUs) the
right of first refusal over energy projects located within their jurisdictions.

        Despite the Philippines' abundant energy resources, many communities
continue to experience high electricity costs, inadequate power supply, and
limited economic participation in energy projects operating within their
localities. While energy investments contribute significantly to national
development, the direct benefits to local consumers and communities remain
limited.

      This measure recognizes the vital role of LGUs, ECs, DUs, and organized
communities as valuable partners in achieving national energy security.
Strengthening local participation in energy development is expected to
encourage investments, expand employment opportunities, stimulate local
economic activity, and improve public support for responsible energy projects.

       The bill grants qualified local entities the right of first refusal in the grant,
transfer, operation, or sale of covered energy projects, subject to technical, legal,
and financial standards. It also provides a priority framework among qualified
local parties, encourages joint ventures, and promotes partnerships that can
mobilize capital and expertise for long-term energy sustainability.

      The measure further provides that a reasonable portion of locally
generated power shall first be offered to the EC or DU serving the host area. This
aims to improve the reliability of supply, stabilize electricity rates, and prioritize
the welfare of consumers in areas that host energy facilities.

       The bill likewise mandates community benefit programs such as local
employment preference, scholarships, livelihood assistance, rural electrification
support, environmental protection, and infrastructure development.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROMOTING LOCAL PARTICIPATION IN THE DEVELOPMENT AND
OPERATION OF INDIGENOUS, POTENTIAL, AND RENEWABLE ENERGY
RESOURCES, GRANTING QUALIFIED LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS,
COMMUNITIES, ELECTRIC COOPERATIVES, AND DISTRIBUTION
UTILITIES THE RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL OVER ENERGY PROJECTS
WITHIN THEIR JURISDICTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Local Energy
Participation and Right of First Refusal Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the declared policy of the State to
ensure energy security, affordable electricity rates, sustainable development, and
inclusive economic growth through the responsible utilization of indigenous,
potential, and renewable energy resources.

       The State likewise recognizes the right of local communities to participate
in the development of natural resources within their jurisdictions and the vital
role of Local Government Units (LGUs), Electric Cooperatives (ECs), and
Distribution Utilities (DUs) in providing reliable electric service. Toward this end,
the State shall promote local ownership, local participation, and equitable access
to energy resources and benefits arising therefrom.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Community refers to host communities, indigenous peoples,
       people’s organizations, cooperatives, and residents directly affected by
       energy projects;

          b. Distribution Utility (DU) refers to an entity authorized to distribute


                                           1
      electricity within a franchise area;

         c.​ Electric Cooperative (EC) refers to a duly registered electric
      cooperative operating under existing laws;

         d.​ Indigenous Energy Resources refers to energy resources naturally
      occurring within the Philippines, including but not limited to hydro,
      geothermal, solar, wind, biomass, natural gas, and other local resources;

        e.​ Local Government Unit (LGU) refers to the province, city,
      municipality, or barangay affected by or hosting the energy project;

         f.​ Potential Energy Resources refers to identified but undeveloped
      resources capable of commercial power generation;

         g.​ Renewable Energy Resources refers to energy resources as defined
      under Republic Act No. 9513, otherwise known as the “Renewable Energy
      Act of 2008”; and

         h.​ Right of First Refusal refers to the preferential right to match the
      terms and conditions offered to third parties for the development,
      operation, or purchase of power from an energy project;

       SEC. 4. Promotion of Local Participation. – The Department of Energy (DOE),
in coordination with concerned agencies, shall promote and facilitate the
participation of LGUs, local communities, ECs, and DUs in the exploration,
development, financing, ownership, and operation of energy projects. Preference
shall be given to projects demonstrating substantial local equity participation,
employment generation, and direct community benefits.

       SEC. 5. Grant of Right of First Refusal. – In the grant, transfer, assignment,
joint venture, operation, or sale of any indigenous, potential, or renewable
energy project located within a province, city, municipality, or franchise area,
qualified LGUs, communities, ECs, and DUs shall be granted the right of first
refusal. Such entities may match the terms and conditions offered to any third
party, subject to legal, technical, and financial qualifications.

       SEC. 6. Priority Order. – Where multiple qualified parties seek to exercise
the right of first refusal, the following order of priority shall apply:

          a.​ Host    Community         Cooperatives      or     Community-Based
              Organizations;

          b.​ Concerned Electric Cooperative or Distribution Utility;

          c.​ Concerned Municipality or City;
          d.​ Concerned Province; and

          e.​ Consortium of the foregoing entities:

      Provided, That joint ventures among qualified local entities shall be
encouraged.

       SEC. 7. Conditions for Exercise. – To exercise the right granted under this
Act, the interested party must:

        a. Submit written notice within sixty (60) days from formal offer or
      publication;

          b. Demonstrate technical and financial capability;

          c. Comply with all regulatory requirements; and

          d. Undertake to operate the project in the public interest.

        SEC 8. Power Supply Preference for Local Consumers. – At least a reasonable
portion of dependable capacity generated by projects covered under this Act
shall first be offered to the local EC or DU serving the host area, subject to Energy
Regulatory Commission (ERC) rules and existing contracts.

      SEC. 9. Community Benefits. – All projects covered by this Act shall adopt
programs for host communities, including but not limited to:

          a. Employment preference for qualified local residents;

          b. Skills training and scholarships;

          c. Rural electrification assistance;

          d. Environmental protection and watershed management;

          f.​ Livelihood and enterprise support; and

          g.​ Infrastructure assistance.

       SEC. 10. Environmental and Indigenous Peoples Protection. – All covered
projects shall comply with environmental laws, land use regulations, and the
rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples, including Free
and Prior Informed Consent (FPIC) where applicable.

      SEC. 11. Regulatory Authority. – The DOE, ERC, National Electrification
Administration (NEA), and relevant agencies shall issue rules to ensure
transparent implementation, fair pricing, open competition, and protection of
consumer welfare.

       SEC. 12. Incentives. – Qualified local entities participating in projects under
this Act may avail of incentives under existing investment and renewable energy
laws, subject to applicable regulations.

       SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOE, in consultation with the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), ERC, NEA, National Commission on
Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations in the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

       SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
