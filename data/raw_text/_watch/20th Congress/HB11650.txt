                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
Confidential funds serve a legitimate purpose where peace and order operations require
secrecy. Yet the same secrecy that protects sensitive operations also makes these funds
particularly vulnerable to abuse and misuse, with fewer opportunities for public scrutiny.
For this reason, confidential funds must be treated as an exception justified by actual
necessity, rather than as an ordinary component of local government spending.
The experience of local government units exhibits that the amount spent in confidential
funds does not necessarily determine the quality of peace and order. Davao City reported
₱530 million in confidential expenses in 2023, after spending ₱460 million annually from
2019 to 2022. Cagayan de Oro City allocated ₱200 million in 2023 and ₱250 million in
2024, while Cavite Province proposed ₱193.5 million for confidential expenses in 2024,
following approximately ₱183 million in 2023. By contrast, in 2024, eight Metro Manila
cities— Las Piñas, Navotas, Makati, Pasig, Taguig, Mandaluyong, Malabon and
Valenzuela—reported no confidential-fund expenditures at all. Quezon City LGU likewise
reduced its confidential funds from ₱100 million in 2023 to ₱75 million in 2024, even as its
index crimes declined from 2,359 to 1,958 over the same period. Other LGUs have likewise
chosen to rely less on confidential spending. Naga City announced the total removal of
confidential funds from its budget, while the LGU of Capas, Tarlac chose to forego its
₱8-million confidential fund for 2026, with the amount instead intended for programs
benefiting senior citizens and public school students.
This measure seeks to institutionalize safeguards requiring LGU confidential funds to be
supported by necessity, proportionate to legitimate peace and order requirements, and
subject to audit, reporting, and safeguards against misuse. This is not intended to diminish
or unduly restrict confidential funds where genuinely necessary. Rather, it seeks to ensure
that such funds are used only when justified by actual peace and order requirements and
subject to transparency and accountability.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
  INSTITUTIONALIZING SAFEGUARDS IN THE APPROPRIATION, RELEASE,
UTILIZATION, REPORTING, AND AUDIT OF CONFIDENTIAL FUNDS OF LOCAL
           GOVERNMENT UNITS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Local Government
Confidential Funds Accountability Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold local autonomy while ensuring fiscal responsibility, transparency, and
accountability in the use of public funds.
The State recognizes that certain legitimate peace and order and public safety
activities may require confidentiality. However, confidentiality shall be the exception
and shall be justified only by operational necessity. It shall not exempt any
expenditure of public funds from lawful appropriation, accounting, audit, and
appropriate government oversight.
Accordingly, confidential funds shall be resorted to only when the intended public
purpose cannot reasonably and effectively be accomplished through regular
appropriations and ordinary government procedures without compromising a
lawful confidential activity.
SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall apply to confidential funds appropriated by
provinces, cities, and municipalities for lawful peace and order and public safety
purposes.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed as granting any local government unit
intelligence, investigative, surveillance, police, or law-enforcement powers not
otherwise conferred by law.
Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:
   (a)​ Confidential Fund refers to a lump-sum appropriation in the budget of a
        local government unit intended for confidential expenses directly related to
        lawful peace and order and public safety activities;
   (b)​ Confidential Expense refers to an expenditure for which confidentiality is
        reasonably necessary to prevent the compromise of a lawful activity, the
        disclosure of confidential sources or methods, or a substantial risk to public
        safety or to any person;
   (c)​ Local Government Unit or LGU refers to a province, city, or municipality;
        and
   (d)​ Peace and Order Program refers to the programs, activities, and projects
        relating to peace and order and public safety contained in the duly approved
        plans and budget of the LGU.
An expenditure shall not be considered confidential solely because it relates to
peace and order or public safety.
Sec. 5. Necessity of Confidential Expenditure. Before any confidential fund may
be appropriated or utilized, the LGU shall establish that:
   (a)​ the expenditure directly relates to a lawful peace and order or public safety
        purpose within the functions of the LGU;
   (b)​ confidentiality is reasonably necessary to accomplish such purpose;
   (c)​ the activity cannot reasonably be undertaken through a regular
        appropriation without compromising its lawful objective, confidential source,
        operational method, or the safety of any person; and
   (d)​ the amount proposed is reasonable and proportionate to the identified need.
The use of regular appropriations shall be preferred whenever the intended public
purpose can reasonably be achieved without confidential treatment.
SEC. 6. Ceiling on Confidential Funds. The total annual appropriation for
confidential funds of an LGU shall not exceed thirty percent (30%) of the total
annual amount appropriated for its Peace and Order Program.
The foregoing ceiling is a maximum limitation and shall not be construed as an
entitlement, automatic allocation, or presumptively necessary amount.
The amount actually appropriated shall be based on demonstrated need and shall
be reasonable and proportionate to the peace and order and public safety
requirements of the LGU.
Any amount appropriated in excess of the ceiling provided herein shall be
inoperative.
SEC. 7. Requirements for Appropriation, Release, and Utilization. No
confidential fund shall be appropriated, released, or utilized unless:
   (a)​ it is specifically identified as a confidential fund in the annual or duly
        authorized supplemental appropriation ordinance;
   (b)​ the underlying peace and order or public safety concern is reflected in the
        appropriate local peace and order and public safety plan;
   (c)​ a Physical and Financial Plan is prepared stating the general purposes,
        activities, and amounts for which the confidential fund is intended;
   (d)​ the proposed appropriation is supported by a current peace and order
        assessment issued by the local Philippine National Police office or other
        appropriate government agency exercising relevant law-enforcement or
        public safety functions;
   (e)​ the release and utilization of the fund is authorized by a resolution approved
        by at least two-thirds (2/3) of the total membership of the Local Peace and
        Order Council; and
   (f)​ all applicable budgeting, accounting, auditing, and documentary
        requirements are complied with.
The peace and order assessment required under paragraph (d) shall serve as a
supporting document and shall not transfer to the issuing agency the authority of
the LGU to determine its lawful budgetary priorities.
SEC. 8. Authorized Uses. Confidential funds may be utilized only for expenses
directly and reasonably necessary for lawful confidential activities relating to peace
and order and public safety, including:
    (a)​ obtaining confidential information necessary for the prevention or
         suppression of criminal activity or serious threats to public safety;
    (b)​ payment of duly authorized rewards or compensation to confidential
         informants;
    (c)​ expenses necessary to preserve the confidentiality of a lawful operation,
         including temporary transportation, communications, supplies, materials, or
         facilities when ordinary procedures would reasonably compromise such
         operation; and
    (d)​ other analogous confidential expenses expressly authorized by law or
         applicable rules and regulations.
Where an activity principally falls within the jurisdiction of a national
law-enforcement agency, the LGU shall, as far as practicable, coordinate with the
appropriate agency.
SEC. 9. Prohibited Uses. Confidential funds shall not be used, directly or
indirectly, for:
   (a)​ salaries, wages, bonuses, allowances, honoraria, overtime pay, or other
        additional compensation or benefits of public officials or employees, except
        when expressly authorized by law;
   (b)​ representation, entertainment, gifts, donations, celebrations, or ceremonial
        activities;
   (c)​ construction or acquisition of buildings, real property, permanent facilities,
        or capital expenditures properly chargeable against regular appropriations;
   (d)​ political, partisan, electoral, or campaign activities;
   (e)​ personal expenses of any public official or employee;
   (f)​ activities intended to monitor, intimidate, harass, or gather information
        against any person solely on account of lawful political activity, criticism of
        government, membership in a lawful organization, or the exercise of
        constitutionally protected rights;
   (g)​ expenditures which can reasonably and effectively be undertaken through
        regular appropriations without compromising a lawful confidential activity;
   (h)​ fictitious, simulated, or non-existent activities or transactions; or
   (i)​ any other purpose prohibited by the Constitution or existing law.
SEC. 10. Prohibition Against Circumvention. No LGU or public officer shall
circumvent the limitations under this Act by transferring, realigning, dividing,
reclassifying, concealing, or charging a confidential expenditure against another
appropriation.
For purposes of this Act, the actual nature and purpose of an expenditure, rather
than its budgetary designation or nomenclature, shall determine whether it
constitutes a confidential expense.
An expenditure shall be considered confidential when confidentiality is reasonably
necessary to its execution or liquidation and its actual nature falls within the
authorized purposes under this Act.
SEC. 11. Supplemental Appropriations. Additional confidential funds may be
provided through a supplemental budget only when:
   (a)​ the requirements governing supplemental appropriations under existing law
        are satisfied;
   (b)​ the total confidential fund remains within the ceiling prescribed under
        Section 6;
   (c)​ the additional requirement is supported by an updated peace and order
        assessment and Physical and Financial Plan;
   (d)​ the Local Peace and Order Council approves its release and utilization by the
        vote required under Section 7; and
   (e)​ all applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing requirements are
        complied with.
No appropriation whose use is restricted by law for another purpose shall be
transferred or realigned to confidential funds.
SEC. 12. Accountability and Reporting. The local chief executive shall ensure the
timely preparation and submission of all confidential fund accountability reports
required by the Commission on Audit and other appropriate oversight agencies.
Such reports shall contain, at a minimum:
   (a)​ the amount appropriated, released, and utilized;
   (b)​ the general categories of activities funded;
   (c)​ the remaining balance;
   (d)​ appropriate accomplishment indicators; and
   (e)​ such other information as may be required under applicable accounting and
        auditing rules.
Operational information whose disclosure may compromise a lawful confidential
activity, reveal a confidential source or method, or endanger any person shall be
contained in a separate confidential annex and handled in accordance with secure
procedures prescribed by the Commission on Audit.
SEC. 13. Public Fiscal Disclosure. The confidential nature of an activity shall not
extend to the existence and aggregate fiscal dimensions of the appropriation.
Every LGU appropriating confidential funds shall disclose, through its existing Full
Disclosure Policy mechanism or other official transparency platform:
   (a)​ the total amount appropriated;
   (b)​ the total amount released and utilized;
   (c)​ the percentage of the Peace and Order Program represented by the
        confidential fund; and
   (d)​ the unobligated or unutilized balance at the end of the applicable fiscal
        period.
Nothing herein shall require public disclosure of confidential sources, operational
methods, specific operational targets, ongoing lawful activities, or information
whose disclosure would reasonably endanger any person or prejudice public safety.
SEC. 14. Audit by the Commission on Audit. Nothing in the confidential
character of any fund, transaction, or supporting record shall impair or diminish
the constitutional authority of the Commission on Audit.
Confidentiality shall not constitute a ground for denying duly authorized
Commission on Audit personnel access to records necessary for the proper exercise
of their constitutional functions.
The Commission shall prescribe secure procedures for the submission,
examination, custody, and preservation of records relating to confidential
expenditures.
SEC. 15. Effect of Failure to Submit Accountability Reports. Except when
necessary to respond to an immediate and substantial threat to public safety, no
subsequent release from an LGU confidential fund shall be made while a required
accountability report for a preceding reporting period remains unsubmitted beyond
the period prescribed by applicable rules.
Subsequent releases may resume upon compliance with the reporting
requirements, without prejudice to any audit suspension, disallowance, refund,
restitution, or administrative, civil, or criminal liability under existing law.
SEC. 16. Accountability of Public Officers. Every public officer who authorizes,
approves, certifies, releases, disburses, receives, utilizes, liquidates, or accounts for
confidential funds shall be responsible for the faithful performance of his or her
respective duties under this Act and existing law.
Approval by another official, office, council, or government agency shall not excuse
a public officer from liability for an expenditure which such officer knew to be
unlawful.
Nothing herein shall be construed as creating criminal liability for conduct not
otherwise punishable under existing penal laws.
SEC. 17. Integration Into Existing Disclosure and Reporting Systems. The
Department of the Interior and Local Government, Department of Budget and
Management, and Commission on Audit shall, as far as practicable, integrate the
reporting and disclosure requirements under this Act into existing budgeting,
accounting, auditing, and Full Disclosure Policy systems.
No separate reporting platform shall be created unless necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.
SEC. 18. Annual Consolidated Report. The Department of the Interior and Local
Government, in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management and
the Commission on Audit, shall prepare an annual consolidated report on the
utilization of confidential funds by LGUs.
The report shall contain, to the extent lawfully disclosable:
   (a)​ the LGUs that appropriated confidential funds;
   (b)​ the total amounts appropriated, released, and utilized;
   (c)​ compliance with the statutory ceiling and accountability requirements;
   (d)​ significant audit observations that may lawfully be disclosed; and
   (e)​ recommendations for improving the administration and accountability of
        local confidential funds.
   (f)​
The report shall be submitted to Congress and made accessible through existing
government transparency platforms.
SEC. 19. Periodic Review. Within three (3) years from the effectivity of this Act,
and every three (3) years thereafter, the Department of the Interior and Local
Government and the Department of Budget and Management, in consultation with
the Commission on Audit and representatives of the leagues of local government
units, shall review the implementation of this Act.
The review shall consider, among others, confidential fund utilization according to
income classification, population, geographic conditions, prevailing peace and order
circumstances, and demonstrated operational requirements.
The agencies shall submit to Congress recommendations on whether the ceiling
and safeguards established under this Act should be retained or modified.
SEC. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Audit, Department of Budget and
Management, and Department of the Interior and Local Government shall jointly
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its implementation, in
consultation with the Philippine National Police, other appropriate government
agencies, and representatives of the leagues of provinces, cities, and municipalities.
The implementing rules shall, as far as practicable, utilize existing government
reporting, accounting, budgeting, and disclosure systems and avoid unnecessary
duplication of documentary requirements.
No implementing rule shall expand the authorized purposes of confidential funds,
increase the statutory ceiling, or diminish the minimum safeguards established
under this Act.
SEC. 21. Suppletory Application of Existing Rules. Existing laws, rules, and
regulations governing confidential funds shall continue to apply insofar as they are
not inconsistent with this Act.
SEC. 22. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
SEC. 23. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative
issuances, rules, regulations, or portions thereof inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 24. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
