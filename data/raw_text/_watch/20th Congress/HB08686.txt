                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Optical Media Board (OMB), established by Republic Act No.
9239 (the Optical Media Act of 2003), was originally intended to regulate the
production and distribution of physical media, such as compact discs (CDs),
and video compact discs (VCDs), and digital versatile discs (DVDs). While
effective during the height of the disc-based economy, this mandate is now
fundamentally mismatched with the current technological landscape.

        Over the last twenty years, piracy has undergone a massive digital
migration. Today’s intellectual property violations occur primarily through, 1.)
Illegal streaming services and torrent networks; 2.) Cloud-based storage and file-
sharing platforms; 3.) Online marketplaces. Since the OMB’s legal jurisdiction is
strictly "tethered" to physical replication plants and tangible carriers, these
digital-native threats fall outside its statutory reach.

       In contrast, the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL),
governed by Republic Act No. 8293, possesses a technology-neutral mandate.
IPOPHL is better equipped to handle the complexities of the digital age, with
the authority to adjudicate disputes, deputize law enforcement, and maintain
specialized copyright offices. Furthermore, its institutional capacity to retain
fees and fines ensures its operational sustainability.



      To eliminate government redundancy and create a unified, more potent

front against piracy in all its forms, this measure proposes the transfer of the
OMB’s powers and resources to IPOPHL. This consolidation will streamline
enforcement and ensure our laws remain relevant in a borderless digital
economy.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                AN ACT
     ABOLISHING      THE    OPTICAL    MEDIA     BOARD CREATED
     UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9239, TRANSFERRING ITS POWERS AND
     FUNCTIONS TO THE INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY OFFICE OF THE
     PHILIPPINES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1            SECTION 1. Abolition of the Optical Media Board. – The Optical Media
 2      Board (OMB) created under the Republic Act No. 9239, otherwise known as the
 3      Optical Media Act of 2003, is hereby abolished.

 4            SEC. 2. Transfer of Powers and Functions to IPOPHL. – All powers,
 5      functions, and responsibilities of the OMB under Section 10 of RA 9239 relating to:
 6            a. licensing, regulation, and inspection of establishments engaged in
 7                reproduction and distribution of copyrighted works in physical media or
 8                their digital equivalents, including visitorial powers;
 9            b. enforcement of administrative and penal provisions relating to
10                unauthorized reproduction of copyrighted works; and




                                                                                         1
 1          c. database and registry maintenance on licensed establishments and
 2             reproduction equipment, are hereby transferred to the Intellectual
 3             Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL).
 4
 5   A separate division may be created under the IPOPHL’s Bureau of Copyright and
 6   Other Related Rights may be created, as may be deemed necessary by the IPOPHL,
 7   in order to perform the absorbed functions of the OMB.
 8
 9          SEC. 3. Absorption of Affected Personnel, Separation and Retirement
10   Benefits. – The Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL) shall, as
11   needed, absorb the employees of the Secretariat of the abolished OMB, without
12   diminution of their salaries and other benefits. Employees who are separated from
13   the service as a result of the abolition of the OMB and its Secretariat shall receive
14   separation benefits under applicable laws and regulations: Provided, That those who
15   are qualified to retire under existing laws shall be allowed to retire and receive
16   retirement benefits to which they may be entitled under applicable laws, rules and
17   regulations.
18          SEC. 4. Transfer of Properties and Resources to IPOPHL. – The IPOPHL
19   shall, by virtue of this Act, be subrogated to all the rights and assume all the
20   obligations and liabilities of the OMB, and all records, property, assets, equipment,
21   and funds of the OMB, including unexpended appropriations or allocations, shall be
22   transferred to the IPOPHL.
23          SEC. 5. Appropriations. – Funds necessary to implement this Act shall be
24   sourced from current appropriations of the OMB. Thereafter, appropriations shall
25   be included in IPOPHL’s annual budget.
26          SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is held
27   invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
28   remain in full force and effect.
