                       AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10533, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE ENHANCED BASIC EDUCATION ACT OF 2013, FOR THE
INTEGRATION OF AN AGRICULTURAL STRAND IN THE
ACADEMIC TRACK OF THE SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL PROGRAM
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1, Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education and to establish,
maintain, and support a complete, adequate, and integrated system of education
responsive to the needs of national development. As agriculture remains a
cornerstone of the Philippine economy and a critical pillar of national food
security, the basic education curriculum must be responsive to the long-term
sustainability and modernization of this sector.

       Republic Act No. 10533, or the Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013,
established the K-12 program in the Philippines, mandating one year of
kindergarten, six years of elementary, and six years of secondary education. It
aimed to make education learner-oriented, globally competitive, and flexible
through reforms to specialized tracks in senior high school. The current K to 12
Program provides two (2) years of Senior High School (SHS) to equip students
with the necessary knowledge and skills to become lifelong learners and to
prepare graduates for tertiary education, middle-level skills development,
employment, and entrepreneurship. While the existing SHS curriculum offers
Agri-Fishery Arts and related specializations (i.e., Aquaculture) under the
Technical-Vocational-Livelihood (TVL) Track, these programs are primarily
structured for skills certification and immediate employability. They do not
provide a dedicated academic preparatory pathway for students who intend to
pursue higher education, research, innovation, agribusiness development,
agricultural economics, food systems management, and agricultural policy.

       The absence of an Agricultural Strand under the Academic Track creates a
structural gap in the curriculum. Students who aspire to become agricultural
scientists, researchers, farm technologists, agribusiness leaders, or public sector
experts in food security are compelled to enroll in general strands such as Science,
Technology, Engineering, Mathematics (STEM) or Accountancy, Business, and
Management (ABM), which do not provide focused foundational exposure to
agricultural systems, rural development, climate-resilient farming, and agri-
innovation.

       This bill seeks to institutionalize and promote agriculture as a viable
academic pathway for SHS students by integrating an Agricultural Strand under
the Academic Track. It aligns with the State policy to sustain and provide support
to the agricultural industry through appropriate technology, research, adequate
financial and production support systems, and comprehensive education and
training. By strengthening agricultural education at the secondary level, the State
develops a sustained pipeline of future professionals capable of modernizing the
sector and ensuring intergenerational continuity.

      The Philippine agricultural sector continues to face structural challenges,
including an aging farmer population, declining youth participation, productivity
constraints, and increasing pressures on food security. Elevating agriculture to
the Academic Track recognizes it not merely as a vocational occupation but as a
science-based, innovation-driven, and strategically vital discipline essential to
national survival and economic resilience.

       In the long term, this measure contributes to national food security and
rural economic development by cultivating a new generation of agriculturists,

                       AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10533, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE ENHANCED BASIC EDUCATION ACT OF 2013, FOR THE
INTEGRATION OF AN AGRICULTURAL STRAND IN THE
ACADEMIC TRACK OF THE SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL PROGRAM
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Senior High
School Agricultural Strand Act.”


      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to
sustain and provide support to the agricultural industry through the provision of
appropriate technology and research; adequate financial, production, marketing,
and other support services; and, comprehensive education and training to
agriculturalists, their families, and other interested individuals to enable them to
utilize resources to their full potential and enable them to participate in the
planning, organization, and management of the programs affecting them.
       It is the policy of the state to ensure food security for the country through
the modernization of agricultural systems, the creation and improvement of
agricultural educational institutions, and the encouragement of youth to engage,
sustain, and develop the agricultural industry.


       To this end, the State shall integrate an Agricultural Strand under the
Academic Track of the Senior High School curriculum in all educational
institutions.


       Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
       (a) Agricultural Strand – refers to the basic course subjects in the field of
          agriculture.
       (b) Academic Track – refers to one of the fields in the curricula of Senior
          High School level preparatory to earning a degree in college.


       Section 4. Amendment. – Section 6 of Republic Act No. 10533 or the
Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013, is hereby amended to read as follows:


                “Sec. 6. Curriculum Consultative Committee. – There shall be
       created a curriculum consultative committee chaired by the DepEd
       Secretary or his/her duly authorized representative and with members
       composed of, but not limited to, a representative each from the CHED, the
       TESDA, the DOLE, THE DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE, the
       PRC, the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and a
       representative from the business chambers such as the Information
       Technology–Business Process Outsourcing (IT-BPO) industry association.
       The consultative committee shall oversee the review and evaluation on the

      implementation of the basic education curriculum and may recommend to
      the DepEd the formulation of necessary refinements in the curriculum.


             THE DEPARTMENT AND THE COMMITTEE SHALL GIVE
      PRIORITY TO AGRICULTURE IN THE DEVELOPMENT AND
      REVIEW OF THE CURRICULUM. TO THIS END, THERE SHALL
      BE AN AGRICULTURAL STRAND INTEGRATED UNDER THE
      ACADEMIC TRACK IN THE CURRICULA OF THE SENIOR
      HIGH SCHOOL IN ALL EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS.
      FUTURE        AMENDMENTS            OF     THE     CURRICULA       MUST
      INCLUDE AN AGRICULTURAL STRAND.”


      Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the date of effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education
(DepEd) shall formulate and design the agricultural strand subjects, modules, and
other necessary details to implement this Act.


      Section 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation
of this Act shall be appropriated from the budget of the DepEd. Subsequently, the
appropriations shall be included in the Annual General Appropriations Act.


      Section 7. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or
provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.


      Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, executive orders,
presidential decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with any provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
