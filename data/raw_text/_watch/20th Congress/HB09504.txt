                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall protect and promote the
right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. While
devolution has enabled LGUs to directly manage and deliver healthcare services
tailored to local needs, disparities in healthcare capacity, infrastructure, funding,
workforce availability, and administrative capability continue to persist across
provinces, cities, and municipalities. These inequalities are particularly pronounced
in low-income LGUs and geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs),
where access to quality healthcare remains limited.

      Many LGU-managed hospitals, rural health units (RHUs), and barangay
health stations (BHSs) continue to face challenges such as inadequate medical
equipment, insufficient health personnel, fragmented health information systems,
uneven service quality, weak emergency preparedness, and limited fiscal capacity.


                                                                                      1
        Thus, this bill seeks to address these challenges by establishing a
 comprehensive national framework to strengthen devolved healthcare systems while
 respecting local autonomy. The measure provides for national standards and
 performance benchmarks for healthcare delivery, sustained technical and financial
 assistance to LGUs, institutional and human resource development programs, digital
 health integration, and mechanisms for transparency, accountability, and evidence-
 based monitoring.

       The bill likewise establishes the Health Systems Strengthening Fund (HSSF)
 to support infrastructure upgrading, procurement of medical equipment, healthcare
 workforce development, emergency preparedness, and other critical health system
 investments, with priority given to underserved communities and disadvantaged
 LGUs.

       By strengthening local health systems, improving governance and
 accountability, and ensuring equitable support for all LGUs, this measure seeks to
 build a more resilient, responsive, and inclusive healthcare system capable of
 meeting the present and future health needs of the Filipino people.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Local Health Systems
Strengthening Act”.


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Consistent with Article II, Section 15 of the
Constitution, the Universal Health Care Act, and the Local Government Code of



                                                                               1
1991, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and promote the right to
health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.


The State recognizes the crucial role of local government units (LGUs) in the
delivery of devolved health services and shall strengthen local health systems
through appropriate standards, technical guidance, fiscal support, institutional
development, and performance monitoring mechanisms to ensure equitable,
efficient, accessible, resilient, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:


   a. “Devolved health services” refer to healthcare functions, facilities, programs,
      and personnel transferred to LGUs pursuant to the Local Government Code
      of 1991 and other relevant laws;
   b. “LGU-managed health facilities” refer to provincial, district, city, and
      municipal hospitals, rural health units (RHUs), barangay health stations
      (BHSs), and other healthcare facilities operated by LGUs;
   c. “Health systems strengthening” refers to interventions aimed at improving
      governance, financing, service delivery, infrastructure, human resources,
      information systems, and health outcomes within local health systems;
   d. “Performance benchmarks” refer to measurable standards and indicators
      established by the Department of Health (DOH) to evaluate healthcare
      accessibility, quality, efficiency, responsiveness, and health outcomes;
   e. “Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs)” refer to
      communities identified by the DOH as having limited access to healthcare and




                                                                                       2
      basic services due to geographic, socioeconomic, or security-related factors;
      and
   f. “Centers of Excellence” refer to LGU-managed health facilities recognized
      by the DOH for exemplary healthcare service delivery, operational efficiency,
      innovation, and health outcomes.


SEC. 4. Objectives. – This Act aims to:


         a. Establish national standards for service delivery, operations,
            infrastructure, and patient care in devolved healthcare facilities;
         b. Provide sustained national government support to LGU-managed
            hospitals, RHUs, and local health systems through funding, technical
            assistance, and institutional support;
         c. Strengthen the institutional, managerial, and human resource capacities
            of local health systems;
         d. Develop a national performance benchmarking system to monitor,
            evaluate, and incentivize improved healthcare delivery and health
            outcomes;
         e. Promote equitable healthcare access, particularly in underserved areas
            and GIDAs; and
         f. Improve coordination among the DOH, PhilHealth, LGUs, and other
            stakeholders in the implementation of Universal Health Care.


SEC. 5. National Standards and Performance Benchmarks. – The DOH, in
consultation with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
PhilHealth, LGUs, healthcare workers’ organizations, civil society organizations,


                                                                                  3
patient advocacy groups, academic institutions, and other relevant stakeholders,
shall develop and regularly update national standards and performance benchmarks
for devolved healthcare services, including but not limited to:


         a. Service availability and readiness;
         b. Health outcome indicators;
         c. Infrastructure and medical equipment adequacy;
         d. Human resource staffing, competencies, and retention;
         e. Financial and operational efficiency;
         f. Emergency preparedness and response capacity;
         g. Digital health integration and health information systems; and
         h. Patient safety, satisfaction, and continuity of care.


The standards and benchmarks established under this section shall be reviewed at
least once every three (3) years and shall be published through publicly accessible
platforms.


SEC. 6. Health Systems Strengthening Fund (HSSF). – There is hereby
established a Health Systems Strengthening Fund (HSSF), which shall provide
supplemental financial support to LGU-managed health systems and facilities. The
HSSF shall be used for the following purposes:


         a. Infrastructure development, rehabilitation, and upgrading of healthcare
             facilities;
         b. Procurement, leasing, and maintenance of medical equipment and
             health technologies;


                                                                                 4
         c. Digital health integration, including electronic medical records,
            telemedicine systems, and health information systems;
         d. Hiring, deployment, training, and retention of healthcare personnel;
         e. Preventive, promotive, maternal, emergency, and primary healthcare
            programs;
         f. Emergency preparedness, stockpiling, and disease outbreak response
            capacity; and
         g. Other health systems strengthening initiatives as may be determined by
            the DOH.


The HSSF shall be administered by the DOH in coordination with the DBM and
DILG. Allocation of funds shall consider:


   1) Population size;
   2) Disease burden and health indicators;
   3) Service coverage gaps;
   4) Income classification of LGUs;
   5) Presence of GIDAs and underserved populations;
   6) Disaster and public health vulnerability; and
   7) Performance ratings and measurable improvements in healthcare delivery.


Priority shall be given to low-income LGUs, underserved areas, and GIDAs.


All disbursements and utilization of the HSSF shall be subject to existing
government accounting and auditing rules and regulations and oversight by the
Commission on Audit (COA).


                                                                                   5
SEC. 7. Support Programs for LGUs. – The DOH shall implement support
programs for LGUs to strengthen local healthcare delivery systems, including:


          a. Technical assistance in local health planning, budgeting, procurement,
             governance, and financial management;
          b. Deployment of regional support teams to assist LGUs in compliance,
             capacity-building, and performance improvement;
          c. Access to centralized procurement, shared services, or leasing
             arrangements for medical equipment and supplies;
          d. Digital health integration support for electronic medical records, data
             reporting systems, interoperability, and telemedicine services;
          e. Scholarship, return-service, and retention incentive programs for
             healthcare workers serving in underserved and geographically isolated
             areas; and
          f. Training and continuing professional development programs for
             healthcare workers and local health administrators.


SEC. 8. Integration with Universal Health Care and PhilHealth. – The DOH and
PhilHealth shall coordinate to ensure that the implementation of this Act aligns with
the Universal Health Care framework, including Konsulta programs, case rates,
primary care provider networks, health financing reforms, and other national
healthcare initiatives.




                                                                                   6
Performance benchmarks established under this Act shall, whenever practicable, be
harmonized with PhilHealth accreditation standards and healthcare financing
mechanisms.


SEC. 9. Participation of Local Health Boards. – Local Health Boards established
under existing laws shall participate in the planning, prioritization, monitoring, and
evaluation of programs and projects funded under this Act.


The DOH shall encourage participatory governance and community engagement
mechanisms to strengthen accountability and responsiveness in local health systems.


SEC. 10. Performance-Based Incentives. – LGUs that meet or exceed established
performance benchmarks, or demonstrate significant measurable improvements in
healthcare delivery and health outcomes, shall be eligible for:


         a. Additional financial grants and technical assistance;
         b. Recognition and accreditation as Centers of Excellence;
         c. Priority inclusion in national healthcare programs and pilot initiatives;
             and
         d. Eligibility for participation in international health partnerships and
             grants facilitated by the national government.


Incentive mechanisms shall consider both absolute performance and measurable
improvements relative to baseline conditions to ensure fairness and equity among
LGUs.




                                                                                    7
SEC. 11. Transparency and Public Accountability. – The DOH shall establish
and maintain a publicly accessible digital information system containing:


         a. HSSF allocations, releases, and utilization reports;
         b. Performance benchmark results and LGU assessments;
         c. Compliance reports and audit findings; and
         d. Annual local health systems assessments and program evaluations.


The DOH shall ensure timely publication and public accessibility of reports under
this section, subject to applicable laws on data privacy and confidentiality.


SEC. 12. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DOH shall submit an Annual Report
to Congress detailing:


         a. HSSF allocations and utilization;
         b. LGU performance assessments and benchmark results;
         c. Progress in strengthening local health systems;
         d. Identified implementation gaps and challenges; and
         e. Recommendations for policy and program improvements.


The DOH may coordinate with state universities and colleges, public health
institutions, civil society organizations, and independent evaluators in conducting
periodic assessments of the implementation and effectiveness of this Act.


SEC. 13. Non-Partisanship and Merit-Based Personnel Administration. – The
hiring, appointment, deployment, promotion, and utilization of healthcare personnel


                                                                                 8
funded under this Act shall be merit-based and consistent with civil service laws,
rules, and regulations.


Healthcare personnel and resources funded under this Act shall not be used for
partisan political activities.


SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and other
available sources of funding. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.


SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the DBM, DILG,
PhilHealth, DICT, COA, and other relevant agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, administrative orders,
rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
