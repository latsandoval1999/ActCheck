                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines is among the countries most vulnerable to climate change, experiencing
increasingly severe typhoons, flooding, sea-level rise, and coastal erosion, as shown in global
climate risk assessments.¹ These hazards place growing strain on public infrastructure and
expose the limitations of conventional “gray” infrastructure such as seawalls, drainage systems,
and flood control facilities, which are often rigid, costly to maintain, and increasingly
insufficient under intensifying climate conditions.²

In response, Green Infrastructure (GI) and Nature-based Solutions (NbS) have emerged as
globally recognized approaches to climate adaptation and disaster risk reduction. International
institutions such as the World Bank, Organisation for Economic Co-operation and
Development (OECD), United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), and International
Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) recognize that these approaches can complement or
outperform traditional infrastructure by enhancing resilience while delivering multiple
environmental and socio-economic benefits.³ These systems use natural assets such as
mangroves, wetlands, river corridors, watershed forests, and urban green spaces to manage
water flows, reduce flooding, and strengthen ecosystem resilience.

In the Philippines, these approaches are already being applied in practice and gaining
institutional traction. The Asian Development Bank has supported Nature-based Solutions in
selected river basin flood risk management programs in the country, demonstrating their
technical and economic relevance under local hydrological and climate conditions.⁴ At the local
             government level, Quezon City has recently expanded its network of flood-mitigating water
             catchment ponds within designated urban green spaces to temporarily store stormwater and
             reduce flooding during intense rainfall events, illustrating a practical and scalable form of urban
             Nature-based Solutions.⁵ These emerging practices reflect a growing shift among national and
             local stakeholders toward integrating ecological systems into infrastructure planning as a
             complement or alternative to conventional flood control measures.

             The constitutional mandate to protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and
             healthful ecology provides the legal foundation for this policy direction. This is reinforced by
             Oposa v. Factoran, which recognized the doctrine of intergenerational responsibility and
             affirmed the State’s duty to safeguard the environment for present and future generations, and
             is further strengthened by Republic Act No. 11995, which institutionalizes ecosystem valuation
             in national planning and investment.

             Despite these advances, there remains no unified national framework that treats ecosystems as
             infrastructure assets or requires systematic evaluation of green, gray, and hybrid options in
             public investment decisions. This gap has led to underinvestment in natural systems that could
             improve resilience and reduce long-term costs.

             This bill therefore seeks to institutionalize Green Infrastructure and Nature-based Solutions as
             a core national strategy for sustainable development, climate adaptation, and disaster risk
             reduction. It establishes a national policy framework, strengthens inter-agency coordination
             through a National Green Infrastructure Council, and introduces sustainable financing
             mechanisms such as a Green Infrastructure Fund and Payment for Ecosystem Services.

             These proposed reforms are designed to ensure that environmental assets are no longer treated
             as incidental public goods but as strategic infrastructure that delivers measurable economic
             value and risk reduction benefits. By embedding nature-based investments into local and
             national planning and financing systems, the State is expected to reduce long-term disaster
             recovery costs, improve the efficiency of public infrastructure spending, and build a more
             resilient and climate-adaptive development pathway for all levels of governments.

             In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is respectfully sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Title. This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Green Infrastructure Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. In fulfillment of the constitutional mandate of the State to protect
and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with the rhythm and
harmony of nature, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to pursue a transformative shift toward
Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions as a cornerstone of sustainable development.

This approach seeks to optimize the management of the nation’s natural resources, reduce lifecycle
costs of public infrastructure, enhance the effectiveness and climate resilience of national and local
investments, and secure long-term environmental integrity and economic sustainability.

Accordingly, this Act institutionalizes Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions as the preferred
and primary framework for national development, climate change adaptation, and disaster risk
reduction. It shall be implemented in alignment with the Philippine Development Plan (PDP), the
National Adaptation Plan (NAP), the Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC), the national
strategy for a Blue Economy, and the long-term vision articulated in AmBisyon Natin 2040.

The State shall:

    a) Recognize natural ecosystems such as forests, wetlands, mangrove forests, and riverine
       floodplains as a vital class of public infrastructure that provides essential ecosystem services
       critical for public welfare and national development;
   b) Mainstream the valuation of these ecosystem services into national and local development
      planning, policy formulation, and public investment decisions, ensuring that the full lifecycle
      costs and benefits of all infrastructure options are properly accounted for;

   c) Establish clear mandates, foster inter-agency coordination, and build the capacity of national
      and local government institutions to plan, implement, and manage Green Infrastructure
      effectively;

   d) Create innovative, market-based financial mechanisms and incentives that make the protection,
      restoration, and establishment of Green Infrastructure economically competitive and financially
      sustainable; and

   e) Ensure a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach, fostering collaboration among
      government agencies, local government units (LGUs), the private sector, civil society, and local
      communities to build a climate-resilient and sustainable Philippines.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. For the purpose of this Act, the following terms are defined:

   a) Ecosystem Services – The direct and indirect contributions of ecosystems to human well-being.
      These include provisioning services (e.g., food, water), regulating services (e.g., flood control,
      climate regulation), supporting services (e.g., nutrient cycling), and cultural services (e.g.,
      recreation, spiritual enrichment).

   b) Gray Infrastructure – Engineered infrastructure that uses manufactured materials such as
      concrete and steel to manage natural systems, characterized by single-purpose design and hard,
      impervious surfaces. Examples include concrete seawalls, drainage canals, piped sewer
      systems, and dams.

   c) Green Infrastructure (GI) – A strategically planned network of natural and semi-natural areas
      with other environmental features, designed and managed to deliver a wide range of ecosystem
      services. It includes natural ecosystems, restored habitats, and engineered systems that mimic
      natural processes. Examples range from large-scale assets like national parks and coastal
      greenbelts to urban-scale features like green roofs, permeable pavements, constructed wetlands
      for wastewater treatment, phytoremediation sites for solid waste management, and urban parks
      designed for flood retention.

   d) Hybrid Infrastructure – A planned system that strategically combines Green and Gray
      Infrastructure elements to leverage the benefits of both approaches, enhancing overall
      performance, resilience, and cost-effectiveness.

   e) Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA) – A method of project evaluation that considers all costs
      associated with an asset over its entire lifespan, from planning and construction to operation,
      maintenance, and end-of-life disposal or decommissioning. For the purposes of this Act, LCCA
      shall include the monetization of all quantifiable ecosystem service co-benefits generated by an
      infrastructure option.

   f) Nature-Based Solutions (NbS) – Actions to protect, sustainably manage, and restore natural or
      modified ecosystems, which address societal challenges effectively and adaptively,
          simultaneously providing human well-being and biodiversity benefits, consistent with
          internationally recognized standards such as the IUCN Global Standard for NbS.

     g) Payment for Ecosystem Services (PES) – A voluntary transaction where a well-defined
        ecosystem service, or a land use likely to secure that service, is bought by at least one buyer
        from at least one provider on the condition that the provider secures the provision of that
        service.

                                         ARTICLE I
                          INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK AND MANDATES

SECTION 4. Creation of the National Green Infrastructure Council. To ensure a whole-of-government
approach, there is hereby created a National Green Infrastructure Council (NGIC), which shall function
as the primary body for inter-agency coordination, policy integration, and monitoring for the
implementation of this Act.

The NGIC shall be composed of the following:

 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) - Chairperson
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) - Co-Chairperson
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) - Co-Chairperson
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of National Defense (DND), as Chairperson of the NDRRMC - Member
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) - Member
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Agriculture (DA) - Member
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) - Member
 ▪    Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF) - Member
 ▪    Chairperson of the Climate Change Commission (CCC) - Member
 ▪    Three (3) representatives from the private sector, academe, and civil society, to be appointed by the President -
      Members


SECTION 5. Functions of the NGIC. The NGIC shall have the following coordinating and oversight
functions:
    a) Facilitate convergence of programs and projects among member agencies;
    b) Review and endorse the National Green Infrastructure Master Plan;
    c) Serve as a forum to resolve conflicts and implementation bottlenecks;
    d) Recommend national policies, standards, and guidelines;
    e) Monitor and evaluate progress and submit annual reports to the President and Congress.

SECTION 6. NGIC Secretariat. The NGIC shall be supported by a permanent Secretariat housed in
the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), which shall provide technical
and administrative support.

SECTION 7. Mandates of Key National Government Agencies. To operationalize this Act:

     a.   DEPDev – Shall lead the formulation and periodic updating of national plans for Green Infrastructure,
          issue guidelines for Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA) for all relevant infrastructure projects, and ensure
          the systematic mainstreaming of Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions across national,
          regional, and sectoral development plans and public investment programming;
    b.   DPWH – Shall develop, adopt, and promulgate engineering and design standards that integrate Green
         Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions as preferred approaches in the planning, construction, and
         retrofitting of public works and infrastructure systems;

    c.   DENR – Shall serve as the primary scientific and technical authority for Green Infrastructure, and shall
         lead the national mapping, inventory, and valuation of ecosystem assets, establish and regulate Payment
         for Ecosystem Services (PES) mechanisms, and develop standards to ensure ecological integrity and
         sustainability;

    d.   DILG – Shall ensure the integration of Green Infrastructure into local governance and development
         planning processes, and shall design and implement comprehensive capacity-building programs to equip
         local government units with the necessary technical and institutional capabilities;

    e.   DA – Shall promote the adoption of agro-ecological approaches and climate-resilient agricultural
         practices, and support the integration of Green Infrastructure within agricultural landscapes to enhance
         productivity, sustainability, and rural resilience;

    f.   DHSUD – Shall incorporate Green Infrastructure standards into national housing, land use, and urban
         development policies, plans, and guidelines, and provide direction to local government units on their
         implementation;

    g.   DOF – Shall develop and oversee financing strategies and enabling policy frameworks to support
         investments in Green Infrastructure, and shall manage the National Green Infrastructure Fund in
         accordance with this Act;

    h. CCC – Shall ensure that all Green Infrastructure strategies, plans, and programs are aligned with national
         climate policies, including adaptation and mitigation commitments.

SECTION 8. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). Local Government Units (LGUs) shall serve
as the frontline implementers of this Act and shall ensure the integration of Green Infrastructure (GI)
into their respective local development plans, including but not limited to Comprehensive Land Use
Plans (CLUPs), Provincial Development and Physical Framework Plans (PDPFPs), and Local Climate
Change Action Plans (LCCAPs), consistent with Republic Act No. 7160.

                            ARTICLE II
 MAINSTREAMING GREEN INFRASTRUCTURE IN PLANNING AND DEVELOPMENT

SECTION 9. Mandatory Green-First Infrastructure Options Analysis. All public infrastructure
projects related to climate and disaster risk management shall undergo a Green-First Options Analysis
comparing gray, green, and hybrid solutions, using Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA). DENR
consultation shall be required, and results shall form part of project approval and ECC issuance.

SECTION 10. National Green Infrastructure Inventory and Valuation. The DENR, with the Philippine
Statistics Authority (PSA), shall establish a nationwide inventory and valuation system consistent with
Republic Act No. 11995 (Philippine Ecosystem and Natural Capital Accounting System Act).

SECTION 11. Declaration and Protection of Designated Green Infrastructure. Ecosystems may be
declared as Designated Green Infrastructure by the President upon NGIC recommendation, granting
legal protection and priority land-use status.
SECTION 12. Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) System. The DENR shall establish an
MRV system to track the condition and performance of GI assets using tools such as GIS, remote
sensing, and satellite imagery, ensuring long-term effectiveness and accountability.

                                ARTICLE III
               ECONOMIC INSTRUMENTS AND SUSTAINABLE FINANCING

SECTION 13. Authority to Implement Stormwater Management Fees. LGUs are hereby authorized to
create, through local ordinance, a system of stormwater management fees. Such fees shall be structured
as a user fee based on a property's contribution to stormwater runoff, which may be measured by its
area of impervious surface. The revenue generated shall be placed in a local special account to be used
exclusively for the planning, construction, and management of local Green Infrastructure for stormwater
management.

SECTION 14. Establishment of Payment for Ecosystem Services (PES) Mechanisms. Pursuant to its
mandate under Section 7(c) of this Act, the DENR is hereby authorized to establish, facilitate, and
regulate PES mechanisms for Designated Green Infrastructure assets. Commercial, industrial, and other
entities who are direct and measurable beneficiaries of the ecosystem services provided by such assets,
including but not limited to, water purification and supply, shall be required to enter into PES
agreements.

The fees collected shall be deposited into a dedicated trust fund for each Designated Green
Infrastructure, to be managed by a multi-stakeholder governing body. The revenue shall be used for the
conservation, maintenance, and enhancement of the asset and to provide a sustainable source of revenue
and livelihood for its recognized stewards, including local communities and indigenous peoples.

SECTION 15. National Green Infrastructure Fund. There is hereby established a special fund to be
known as the National Green Infrastructure Fund, hereinafter referred to as the Fund. The Fund shall
be capitalized through an initial appropriation in the General Appropriations Act and shall be
supplemented by annual appropriations thereafter. It may also be augmented by grants, donations, and
other contributions from local and foreign sources.

The Fund shall be used to provide grants and concessional financing to Local Government Units, civil
society organizations, and community enterprises for the development, restoration, and management of
a wide range of Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions projects. It shall also provide funding
for technical assistance and project preparation to develop a robust pipeline of bankable projects.

The National Green Infrastructure Council shall serve as the Governing Board of the Fund, while the
Department of Finance shall act as its manager and secretariat.

SECTION 16. Fiscal and Non-Fiscal Incentives for Private Sector Adoption. To encourage private
sector investment in Green Infrastructure, the following incentives are hereby established:

    a. Fiscal Incentives under the CREATE Act. – The Board of Investments (BOI), upon the
       recommendation of the NGIC, shall include the establishment, restoration, and management of
       Green Infrastructure as a priority activity in the Strategic Investment Priority Plan (SIPP).
       Pursuant to Republic Act No. 11534, otherwise known as the Corporate Recovery and Tax
       Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act, and its subsequent amendments, investments by
        registered business enterprises in NGIC-certified Green Infrastructure projects shall be eligible
        for fiscal incentives, including but not limited to, Enhanced Deductions on capital investment
        and labor expense, as administered by the BOI and other Investment Promotion Agencies.

    b. Non-Fiscal Incentives. – Pursuant to their powers under Republic Act No. 7160, and guided by
       standards issued by the DHSUD, relevant LGUs are hereby mandated to establish a system of
       non-fiscal incentives for private developers. Such incentives may include, but are not limited
       to, density bonuses, increased floor area ratios, and expedited permitting for projects that
       incorporate significant and high-quality GI features that exceed minimum national and local
       standards.

                                  ARTICLE IV
              CAPACITY BUILDING, RESEARCH, AND PUBLIC AWARENESS

SECTION 17. National Capacity Building Program. The DILG, in partnership with the DENR,
DPWH, State Universities and Colleges, and qualified technical institutions, shall design and implement
a continuous national capacity-building program for LGU officials, planners, and engineers on the
principles, design, implementation, and maintenance of Green Infrastructure.

SECTION 18. Research and Development. The Department of Science and Technology (DOST), in
coordination with the DENR and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), shall develop and
fund a National Green Infrastructure Research and Development Program. This program shall focus on
developing locally appropriate technologies, materials, and best practices for GI and NbS tailored to the
diverse ecosystems and climatic conditions of the Philippines.

SECTION 19. Public Awareness and Education. The NGIC, through the Philippine Information
Agency (PIA) and in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), shall develop and implement a sustained national public information,
education, and communication campaign to build broad-based support and understanding of the value
and benefits of Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions.

                                           ARTICLE V
                                        FINAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the DEPDev, as Chairperson of the NGIC, shall convene the Council to develop and
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 21. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act,
including the operational budget for the NGIC Secretariat and the initial capitalization of the National
Green Infrastructure Fund, shall be included in the General Appropriations Act following the enactment
of this law. Thereafter, each member agency of the NGIC shall include in their respective annual budget
proposals the necessary funding to carry out their mandated functions under this Act.

SECTION 22. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid,
the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
SECTION 23. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 24. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
