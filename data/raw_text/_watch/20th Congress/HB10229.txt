                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

More than a decade after the passage of Republic Act No. 10586, or the “Anti-Drunk and
Drugged Driving Act of 2013,” incidents of impaired driving continue to claim lives. The
issue lies not in the absence of penalties but in weak enforcement and operational gaps.

Law enforcers often lack proper equipment, drivers refuse testing without consequence,
hospitals fail to conduct timely examinations, and modern evidence, such as CCTV or
dashcam footage, is underutilized. Local traffic authorities remain under-empowered,
while establishments serving alcohol bear no responsibility in preventing impaired driving.

This measure seeks to amend RA 10586 by strengthening enforcement standards,
mandating equipment, ensuring admissibility of evidence, requiring post-accident testing,
deputizing local traffic enforcers, and assigning preventive duties to establishments.

By closing these gaps, the law will move from merely penalizing offenders to being
effectively enforced nationwide. Road safety is a shared responsibility, and this
amendment aims to protect lives, promote responsible driving, and restore public
confidence in traffic enforcement.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

SECTION. 1. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

      (e) Probable Cause refers to the standard to be used by the law
      enforcement officer (LEO) in apprehending the person driving the motor
      vehicle is under the influence of alcohol, dangerous drugs and/or other
      similar substances when he personally witnesses or observes erratic driving
      (e.g lane straddling, sudden stops, over speeding, swerving, weaving)
      indicating impairment, confirmed by alcohol smell, slurred speech,
      bloodshot eyes, poor coordination, or failed sobriety test.

      (f) Driving under the influence of alcohol refers to the act of operating a
      motor vehicle by a person in a state of intoxication as determined by the
      following Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) thresholds:

           (1) 0.0% for novice drivers (within the first three years of licensing),
           professional drivers, drivers of trucks, buses, motorcycles, and
           operators of public utility vehicles;
           (2) 0.02% for all other drivers of private motor vehicles.
          Any level of alcohol above these thresholds shall constitute prima
          facie evidence of driving under the influence.

      (k) Vulnerable Zones refer to high-risk areas where the presence of
      pedestrians or specific groups is concentrated and where the road
      infrastructure provides no physical barriers for protection, which areas
      include but are not limited to: school zones, hospital vicinities, public
      markets, playgrounds, and residential areas with high population density.

SEC. 2. Section 6-A of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby added to read as follows:

      SECTION 6-A. Use of Secondary Evidence for Proof of Violation. – In
      addition to the field sobriety, chemical, and confirmatory tests required
      under Section 6 herein, secondary evidence shall be gathered by the law
      enforcement officer to prove a violation of this Act when primary tests are
      unavailable, infeasible, inadmissible, or refused by the driver, including but
      not limited to:

      (a) Testimony from credible witnesses attesting to the driver’s manifest
          intoxication, erratic driving, or physical signs of impairment such as
          slurred speech, unsteady gait, or odor of alcohol;
      (b) Video or photographic recordings from body-worn cameras, dashcams,
          or surveillance capturing behavior indicative of drunkenness or drug
          influence; or
      (c) Circumstantial evidence, such as refusal to submit to chemical testing
          (deemed presumptive admission under this Act), vehicle collision
          patterns consistent with impairment, or documented field sobriety test
          failures.

      Law enforcement officers shall document secondary evidence as soon as
      practicable and contemporaneously with the incident, including timestamps
      where available.

SEC. 3. Section 7 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SECTION 7. Mandatory Alcohol Screening, Implied Consent, and
      Presumption of Guilt. - Any driver of a motor vehicle in a vehicular accident
      resulting in the loss of human life, or physical injuries shall be subjected to
      chemical tests and shall be deemed to have given their consent to have
      their breath, urine, saliva, blood, or urine tested for alcohol or drug content.

      (a) Mandatory Alcohol Screening (MAS): Law enforcement officers are
          authorized to demand a breath sample for field screening from any
          driver who has been personally determined to have probable cause of
          being intoxicated or under the influence of dangerous drugs.
      (b) Presumption of Intoxication and Prima Facie Evidence of Guilt: If a
          driver refuses to submit to the required tests despite being informed of
          the implied consent rule, such refusal shall give rise to a disputable
          presumption that the driver was under the influence of alcohol or drugs
          at the time of the operation of the vehicle, and shall be deemed as prima
          facie evidence of guilt.

SEC. 4. Section 7-A shall be incorporated in Republic Act No. 10586 to read as follows:

      SECTION 7-A. Post-Accident Testing Requirements. – In the event of a
      road vehicular accident resulting in physical injury or death:

      (a) Mandatory Testing: All drivers involved shall be subjected to a chemical
          test for alcohol and drugs within two (2) hours of the incident.
      (b) Law Enforcement Duty: Responding officers are required to facilitate this
          testing immediately. Failure of an officer to initiate testing without a valid
          medical justification shall be grounds for administrative disciplinary
          action.
      (c) Mandatory Cooperation of Medical Facilities: All government and private
          medical facilities, including attending physicians and medical staff, are
          mandated to cooperate with law enforcement in the administration of
          chemical tests following a vehicular accident involving injury or death.

      All medical facilities shall conduct tests, as practicable, without
      compromising patient care, and to prevent the natural dissipation of alcohol,
      within two (2) hours where feasible, subject to DOH guidelines. Any medical
      facility, medical professional, or medical staff who wilfully refuses, delays,
      or obstructs the conduct of a mandatory test without a valid justification
      (e.g., immediate life-saving intervention) shall be subject to applicable
      administrative, civil, and criminal sanctions.

      For this purpose, the results of chemical tests conducted during DUI
      investigations are subject to the provisions of the Data Privacy Act, provided
      that the disclosure of the said results is permitted when necessary for the
      investigation and for the protection of lawful rights and interests.

SEC. 5. Section 9 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby further amended to read as
follows:

      SECTION 9. Acquisition of Equipment and Training. – Within six (6) months
      from the effectivity of this act, the land transportation office (LTO) and the
      Philippine national police (PNP) shall acquire and maintain an adequate
      number of breath analyzers to be utilized by law enforcement officers and
      deputized local traffic enforcement officers nationwide.
      The number of breath analyzers considered 'adequate' shall be determined
      based on a needs assessment conducted jointly by the LTO and PNP, in
      consultation with relevant traffic safety experts and public health agencies.
      The needs assessment shall consider, among others, the number of traffic
      enforcement personnel, historical data on road crashes involving
      intoxication, general deterrence principles in road policing, and geographic
      and population needs per region.

      A broader use of breath analyzers in the field shall be conducted through
      random breath testing in public transport terminals, random sobriety
      checkpoints, and targeted enforcement during high-risk events.

      The acquisition and maintenance of these devices shall be a continuous
      process, subject to periodic review of LTO and PNP every two (2) years to
      ensure the sufficiency and functionality of the equipment.

      Law enforcement personnel and deputized local traffic enforcement officers
      equipped with breath analyzers shall undergo regular training on the proper
      conduct of field sobriety tests and the use of breath analyzers.

      Funding for the acquisition, maintenance, periodic upgrade, and training for
      the use of these devices shall be sourced from the annual budget
      appropriations of the LTO and PNP, supplemented, if necessary, by grants,
      donations, or special appropriations authorized by Congress.

      The LTO and PNP shall divide costs proportionally based on the number of
      personnel each agency deploys for road safety enforcement duties and the
      volume of traffic incidents managed in their respective jurisdictions.

      The LTO and PNP shall provide an annual report to Congress detailing the
      number of breath analyzers acquired, the distribution across regions, and
      any challenges encountered in equipment deployment and maintenance.

SEC. 6. Section 12 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SECTION 12. Penalties. - A driver found to have been driving a motor
      vehicle while under the influence of alcohol shall be penalized as follows:

      a. If the violation of Section 5 did not result in physical injuries or homicide,
         the penalty of three (3) months imprisonment, and a fine ranging from
         Twenty thousand pesos (Php 20,000.00) to Eighty thousand pesos (Php
         80,000.00) shall be imposed. However, if the violation did not result in
         physical injuries or homicide but is committed within a designated
         Vulnerable Zone, the penalty shall be increased to six (6) months
         imprisonment and a fine ranging from Fifty thousand pesos (Php
         50,000.00) to One hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00).
      b. If the violation of Section 5 resulted in physical injuries, the penalty
         provided in Article 263 of the Revised Penal Code or the penalty
         provided in the next preceding subparagraph, whichever is higher, and
         a fine ranging from one hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) to
         two hundred thousand pesos (Php 200,000.00) shall be imposed. But
         when the physical injuries are sustained within a Vulnerable Zone, the
         maximum period of the imposable prison term shall be applied, and the
         fine shall be increased by fifty percent (50%).
      c. If the violation of Section 5 resulted in homicide, the penalty provided in
         Article 249 of the Revised Penal Code and a fine ranging from three
         hundred thousand pesos (Php 300,000.00) to five hundred thousand
         pesos (Php 500,000.00) shall be imposed. But if the homicide occurred
         within a Vulnerable Zone, the penalty shall be reclusion perpetua and
         the fine shall be increased to a range of Seven hundred thousand pesos
         (Php 700,000.00) to One million pesos (Php 1,000,000.00).

      e. Rehabilitative Pathway: In addition to the penalties of imprisonment and
         fine, any person found in violation of this Act shall be required to enroll
         in and successfully complete an Impaired Driver Intervention Program
         (IDIP). The IDIP shall include behavioral therapy, defensive driving
         education, and victim impact seminars. Failure to complete the IDIP
         shall be a ground for the permanent revocation of the driver's license.

SEC 7. A new section designated as Section 18 of Republic Act No.10586 is hereby
inserted to read as follows:

      SECTION 18. Proactive measures to prevent impaired driving. – Private
      and public establishments, such as bars, clubs, and other establishments
      serving alcohol, shall institute proactive measures to prevent impaired
      driving, such as engaging designated drivers, alternative modes of
      transportation for impaired drivers, and other innovative measures to
      prevent impaired driving and promote safe transportation.

      Local government units shall require and provide support to establishments
      that serve alcohol to adopt programs that train staff to recognize intoxication
      and support designated driver practices, refusal of service to intoxicated
      individuals, and provide information on the risks of drunk driving. Failure to
      comply with these proactive measures will result in non-issuance or non-
      renewal of the business permit.

SEC 8. A new section designated as Section 19 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby
inserted to read as follows:

      Section 19. Appropriations. - The initial amount of one billion and six

       Thereafter, such amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this act

SEC 9. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any section of this Act shall be deemed
unconstitutional and invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof shall not be affected
and shall remain in force and effect.

SEC 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, decrees, instructions, rules, and
regulations, contrary to or inconsistent with any provisions of this Act are repealed or
amended accordingly.

SEC 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
