                            AN ACT
 INSTITUTIONALIZING THE RULES ON SYSTEM LOSS CAP FOR ELECTRIC
                    DISTRIBUTION UTILITIES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        In our system of electricity distribution, system loss refers to the electrical
energy lost between the point of generation and its delivery to end-users. These losses
may be technical, arising from the physical condition, design, and operation of
transmission and distribution facilities, or non-technical, resulting from electricity theft,
illegal connections, meter tampering, and inaccurate billing or measurement. System
loss remains a serious national concern because a portion of its cost is passed on to
consumers, contributing to already high electricity rates. Persistent losses also reduce
the efficiency and reliability of power distribution, waste energy that could otherwise
serve households and businesses, and require additional generation to compensate
for electricity that never reaches paying consumers.

       While the recovery of system losses is necessary to ensure the continued
operation and financial viability of distribution utilities and electric cooperatives,
consumers should not be burdened with unlimited charges arising from losses that are
largely beyond their control or awareness. This bill excludes transmission losses from
recoverable system losses and prohibits their recovery from end-users. The bill also
sets a maximum recoverable system loss limit of 5% for private distribution utilities
and establishes a tiered cap for electric cooperatives based on operational conditions.

      In light of the foregoing, support for this bill, which is a counterpart measure
of Senate Bill 495 is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Distribution System
 2   Loss Act”.
 3
 4            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It shall be the policy of the State to protect
 5   the rights of every electric consumer without compromising the State's obligation to
 6   ensure the viability of electric distribution utilities. Towards this end, the State shall
 7   ensure that the imposition of electricity distribution charges to electricity end-users is
 8   justifiable and equitable, and that no unnecessary cost is recovered by distribution
 9   utilities.
10
11          SEC. 3. Transmission Losses. Transmission losses are hereby disaggregated
12   from the computation of allowable system loss charges. These losses shall not form
13   part of the passed-on costs to electricity end-users.

14            For the purposes of this Act, transmission loss shall refer to the difference in
15   kilowatt-hours (kWh) between the electricity delivered by a generation company to a
16   distribution utility as indicated in the powerplant billing meter installed at the plant
17   gate and the electricity actually received by the substation of an individual distribution
18   utility as indicated in the billing meter installed at the delivery point.

19          Sec. 4. Distribution System Loss Cap for Private Distribution Utilities.
20   Private distribution utilities are hereby allowed to pass on system losses to their
21   consumers only to the extent that these system losses result from design fault,
22   technical loss, and non-technical losses as defined by the Energy Regulatory
23   Commission (ERC), which in no case shall be more than five percent (5%).
24

 1          Sec. 5. Distribution System Loss Caps for Electric Cooperatives.
 2   Distribution utilities are hereby allowed to pass on system losses to their consumers
 3   only to the extent that these system losses result from design fault, technical loss,
 4   and non-technical loss as defined by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), which
 5   in no case shall exceed the following:
 6
 7          (a) For electric cooperatives in Cluster 1 - 12.00%;
 8
 9          (b) For electric cooperatives in Cluster 2 - 10.25%; and
10
11          (c) For electric cooperatives in Cluster 3 - 8.25%.
12
13          A system loss charge shall be determined annually based on the previous year's
14   costs and revenues of the distribution utilities, which shall be verified by the ERC to
15   ensure that only legitimate costs are borne by consumers. For the purpose of this Act,
16   system loss shall mean the difference in kWh between the electricity purchased and/or
17   generated and the electricity actually sold by a distribution utility.

12          SEC. 7. Powers and Functions of the Energy Regulatory Commission.
13   In addition to its powers and functions under Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known
14   as the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001, and other relevant laws, the ERC
15   shall perform the following:
16
17                 (a) Determine, as often as is necessary, whether the caps shall be
18          reduced further on the basis of load density, sales mix, cost of service, delivery
19          voltage, and other technical considerations, as well as international
20          benchmarks, taking into account the viability of distribution utilities and electric
21          cooperatives and the interest of the consumers;
22
23                 (b) Exercise the emergency power, when deemed necessary, to increase
24          the system loss rates caps as prescribed by this Act;
25
26                   (c) Review and verify the system loss charges reported by distribution
27          utilities to ensure that only legitimate costs are borne by the consumers.
28
29          Subsequently and every year thereafter, the ERC shall review the system loss
30   rates prescribed by this Act based on parameters such as load density, sales mix, cost
31   of service, delivery voltage and any other technical considerations, as provided in the
32   EPIRA, necessary for establishing different caps for distribution utilities.
33
34            SEC. 8. System Loss Reduction Program and Incentives. The ERC shall
35   develop an incentive mechanism to reward distribution utilities whose actual system
36   losses are below the prevailing approved caps. The incentive mechanism adopted may
37   be incorporated by the ERC in the performance incentive scheme under the
38   performance-based regulation for private distribution utilities and in the benchmarking
39   methodology for electric cooperatives. Towards this end, expenses associated with
40   any system loss reduction program duly approved by the ERC shall be considered part
41   of the capital, expenditure plans or programs, and operating costs of distribution
42   utilities.

 1          SEC. 9. Emergency Clause. The application of allowable system loss rates
 2   provided in this Act may be suspended upon the occurrence of the following: acts of
 3   God, riots, war, terrorist act, epidemic, pandemic, civil commotion, natural
 4   catastrophes, and other emergency circumstances wherein the distribution utility is
 5   unable to accurately determine the energy sold because of damage or loss of
 6   consumers billing meters and other facilities used in the determination of the
 7   consumers energy consumption, as determined by the ERC.
