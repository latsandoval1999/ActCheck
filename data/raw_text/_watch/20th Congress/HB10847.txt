                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Our schools should be the safest rooms in the Republic. In the span of fifty-seven
days, two of them became crime scenes. On June 22, 2026, two students aged fourteen
and fifteen opened fire inside San Jose National High School in Tacloban City, killing
three of their classmates and wounding twenty. On August 18, 2026, a Grade 9 student
walked into the Ateneo de Zamboanga University junior high school with a pistol and
a rifle, killed a schoolmate, and then took his own life. In both tragedies, the guns came
from the adult world around the children. In Tacloban, one weapon was the service
pistol of a police officer, and the other a revolver registered to a security agency, and
the strongest charge the State could bring against the firearm keeper was malversation.
When a child dies, and the law can speak only of property, the law is insufficient.

       The numbers confirm what the tragedies revealed. The Philippine National
Police estimates that around 1.7 million loose firearms circulate against about 4.12
million registered firearms, that more than 700,000 registrations have lapsed, and that
almost all gun crimes are committed with loose firearms. Since 1980, the government
has declared at least thirteen firearms amnesties. All of them failed, because each
offered forgiveness without finality and without the consequence that must follow.

        This bill does what those amnesties did not. First, it grants one final, non-
extendible amnesty of one hundred twenty days, with penalties and surcharges waived,
fees reduced, registration brought by mobile teams to rural and island communities,
and every firearm test-fired and screened against crime gun databases so that no
weapon used against our people is ever laundered into legality. Second, it ends the rule
that lets an offender who uses an unlicensed gun escape a separate charge for the gun
itself: unlawful possession of a loose firearm becomes a separate and distinct offense.
Third, it reserves the gravest, non-bailable penalty for the gravest conduct: using a loose
firearm in a crime, trafficking in firearms, bringing a firearm into a school, and arming
a minor. Fourth, it makes safe storage a legal duty, holds adults to account when their
negligence places a gun in the hands of a child, and brings privately made and 3D-
printed firearms under the law.

       The Constitution permits a non-bailable offense only where the penalty reaches
reclusion perpetua, and it forbids cruel and disproportionate punishment. This
measure honors both commands. The farmer with an inherited shotgun is given a door
to legality. The trafficker, the smuggler, and the one who hands a gun to a child meet
the full weight of the law. One last open door, then a locked gate.

The approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT GRANTING A FINAL FIREARMS AMNESTY, STRENGTHENING
    ACCOUNTABILITY FOR LOOSE FIREARMS, PROSECUTING THE
    UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF A LOOSE FIREARM AS A SEPARATE AND
    DISTINCT OFFENSE, ESTABLISHING GUN-FREE SCHOOL ZONES,
    IMPOSING SAFE STORAGE AND ADULT LIABILITY DUTIES,
    REGULATING PRIVATELY MADE AND UNTRACEABLE FIREARMS,
    AMENDING FOR THESE PURPOSES REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10591,
    OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "COMPREHENSIVE FIREARMS AND
    AMMUNITION REGULATION ACT," AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Final Firearms Amnesty
and Loose Firearms Accountability Act of 2026."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State affirms the right of qualified citizens
to own and possess firearms for lawful purposes, and the duty of every firearm holder
to keep those firearms within the reach of the law. The State likewise declares that
schools shall remain safe spaces for learning. To these ends, the State grants a single,
final, and non-extendible amnesty for the registration of unregistered and loose
firearms, prosecutes the unlawful possession of a loose firearm as a distinct wrong,
imposes the gravest penalties on those who use, traffic, or place firearms where they
cause the most harm, and observes at all times the constitutional guarantees of
proportionate punishment, equal protection, and the right to bail.

      SEC. 3. Definitions. – Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10591 is hereby amended by
adding the following new paragraphs:

       "(hh) Gun-free school zone refers to the premises of any public or private school
       offering pre-elementary, elementary, secondary, or technical-vocational
       education, or tertiary education, including its grounds and buildings, and the
       area within fifty (50) meters from the outermost perimeter of the school grounds,
       excluding private residences located within that area;
       "(ii) Privately made firearm refers to a firearm, including one produced through
       three-dimensional printing or assembled from an unfinished frame or receiver,
       manufactured or assembled without the serialization, licensing, and registration
       required under this Act, and lacking a unique serial number recorded with the
       Firearms and Explosives Office of the Philippine National Police; and

       "(jj) Firearm trafficking refers to the illegal importation, exportation, acquisition,
       sale, delivery, movement, or transfer of firearms, their major parts, or
       ammunition, or the facilitation of any of these acts."

       SEC. 4. Final Firearms Amnesty; Grant and Duration. – A Final Firearms
Amnesty is hereby granted for a single, non-extendible period of one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
During this period, any person in possession of an unregistered firearm, or holding an
expired or unrenewed license or registration, may register or renew the same free from
criminal liability for unlawful possession under Republic Act No. 10591, as amended,
and free from any delinquency fee, surcharge, or penalty otherwise due.

       SEC. 5. Amnesty Fees. – All delinquency fees, surcharges, and penalties are
waived during the amnesty period. Registration and licensing fees during the period
shall be set at reduced rates by the Chief of the Philippine National Police, and shall be
waived for indigent applicants upon proof of indigency.

        SEC. 6. Ballistic Screening. – No firearm shall be registered under the amnesty
until it has undergone test-firing and ballistic examination by the Philippine National
Police and has been cleared against the national databases of crime guns, stolen
firearms, and government-issued firearms. A firearm that matches any such record
shall be denied amnesty and shall be dealt with under applicable laws.

       SEC. 7. Exclusions. – The amnesty shall not extend to: (a) firearms used in the
commission of a crime; (b) stolen firearms; (c) firearms issued to or owned by the
Government, including those of the Armed Forces of the Philippines, the Philippine
National Police, and other agencies; (d) firearms in the possession of persons
disqualified under Republic Act No. 10591, as amended; and (e) firearms which the law
prohibits from private ownership, which shall instead be surrendered for safekeeping
or disposition.

       SEC. 8. One-Stop Centers and Mobile Registration. – The Philippine National
Police shall establish one-stop processing centers in every provincial capital and highly
urbanized city, and shall deploy mobile registration teams to rural, island, upland, and
agricultural communities, so that no applicant is denied the amnesty by reason of
distance or cost.

       SEC. 9. Heirloom and Customary Weapons. – The implementing rules and
regulations shall provide a simplified and affordable procedure for the registration or
lawful retention of heirloom firearms and of weapons of cultural, customary, or
indigenous significance, consistent with public safety and with Republic Act No. 8371.
       SEC. 10. Information Campaign. – The Philippine National Police, the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, and the Presidential
Communications Office shall conduct an intensive nationwide information campaign
in Filipino, English, and the principal regional languages throughout the amnesty
period.

        SEC. 11. Post-Amnesty Report; Intensified Enforcement. – Within sixty (60) days
from the close of the amnesty period, the Philippine National Police shall report to both
Houses of Congress the number of firearms registered, renewed, surrendered,
screened, and denied, and the compliance rate by region. Upon the close of the amnesty
period, the Philippine National Police shall intensify enforcement against all remaining
loose firearms. No further amnesty shall thereafter be granted.

      SEC. 12. Unlawful Possession as a Separate and Distinct Offense. – Section 29
of Republic Act No. 10591 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 29. Unlawful Possession and Use of a Loose Firearm in the Commission of
      a Crime. The unlawful possession of a loose firearm is a separate and distinct
      offense from any other crime committed by the possessor. Where a person
      unlawfully possesses a loose firearm and commits any crime punishable under
      the Revised Penal Code or special laws, the person shall be prosecuted and, upon
      conviction, penalized separately for the unlawful possession under Section 28 of
      this Act and for the other crime committed. The rule that unlawful possession is
      absorbed by the other crime, or is reduced to a mere aggravating circumstance,
      is hereby abolished.

      "When a loose firearm is used in the commission of any crime, such use shall
      further be appreciated as a special aggravating circumstance in that crime, and
      shall not be offset by any mitigating circumstance.

      "If the violation of this Act is in furtherance of, or incident to, or in connection
      with the crime of rebellion, insurrection, or attempted coup d'etat, such violation
      shall be absorbed as an element of the said crime."

     SEC. 13. Qualified Circumstances; Non-Bailable Offenses. – Section 28 of
Republic Act No. 10591 is hereby amended by adding the following paragraphs:

      "Notwithstanding the preceding paragraphs, the penalty of reclusion perpetua
      shall be imposed, and the offense shall be non-bailable when evidence of guilt is
      strong, in any of the following qualified circumstances:

      "(1) When a loose firearm is used in the commission of a crime resulting in death
      or serious physical injuries, or of any crime punishable by reclusion temporal or
      a higher penalty;

      "(2) When the offense constitutes firearm trafficking as defined in this Act;

      "(3) When a loose firearm is carried, possessed, or discharged within a gun-free
      school zone; and
       "(4) When a firearm, its major part, or ammunition is delivered, sold, or
       otherwise made available to a minor, to a person known to be disqualified under
       Section 4 of this Act, or to a person with a pending criminal case for an offense
       punishable by imprisonment of more than six (6) years.

       "Unlawful acquisition or possession not falling under any qualified circumstance
       shall remain bailable and shall be penalized in accordance with the graduated
       schedule of this Section."

        SEC. 14. Gun-Free School Zones. – No person shall carry, possess, or discharge
a firearm within a gun-free school zone, except a law enforcement officer in the actual
performance of official duty, a school security guard acting under a duly approved duty
detail order, or a person transporting a licensed and unloaded firearm in a locked
container through the zone without stopping within it. Violation of this Section shall be
punished by reclusion perpetua where the firearm is a loose firearm, and by prision
mayor in its maximum period where the firearm is licensed. Schools shall post visible
notices designating their premises and perimeter as gun-free school zones.

        SEC. 15. Safe Storage; Adult Liability. – Every person who owns or lawfully
possesses a firearm shall store it, when not carried or in use, unloaded and secured in a
locked container or vault or with a tamper-resistant locking device, separate from its
ammunition, and beyond the access of any minor or unauthorized person. An owner
or possessor whose negligence in storage allows a minor or unauthorized person to
gain access to a firearm shall be punished by prision correccional where no crime is
committed with the firearm; by prision mayor where the firearm is used to inflict
physical injuries; and by reclusion temporal where the firearm is used to cause death.
This liability is without prejudice to civil liability and to any greater liability under other
laws.

       SEC. 16. Privately Made and Untraceable Firearms. – The unlawful
manufacture, assembly, acquisition, possession, sale, or transfer of a privately made
firearm, of an unfinished frame or receiver intended for that purpose, or of digital files
primarily designed to enable the three-dimensional printing of a firearm or its major
part, shall be punished by reclusion temporal to reclusion perpetua. The Firearms and
Explosives Office shall issue rules requiring the serialization and registration of all
frames, receivers, and privately made firearms.

       SEC. 17. Election Gun Ban. – Nothing in this Act shall diminish the authority of
the Commission on Elections to regulate the bearing, carrying, or transporting of
firearms during the election period.

       SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government,
through the Philippine National Police, and in consultation with the Department of
Education, the Commission on Higher Education, the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority, the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples, and
recognized organizations of responsible firearm owners, dealers, and sport shooters,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
        SEC. 19. Appropriations. – The amounts necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act, including the one-stop centers, mobile registration teams, ballistic screening,
and the information campaign, shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Philippine National Police. Thereafter, the amounts necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. – Republic Act No. 8294 and the remaining
provisions of Presidential Decree No. 1866, as amended, insofar as they are inconsistent
with this Act, and all other laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act, are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 22. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
