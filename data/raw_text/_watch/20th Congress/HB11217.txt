                                              AN ACT
CREATING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR EDUCATION,
DEFINING ITS MANDATE, POWERS, AND FUNCTIONS, AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR
                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Philippine education system stands at a critical juncture. Despite significant investments
and continuing efforts by the government, persistent challenges in learning outcomes, teacher
development, workforce preparation, access, and the transition of learners across different levels of
education continue to affect the country’s capacity to develop a highly capable and productive
citizenry. These challenges are complex and interconnected, requiring not only reforms within
individual agencies but also stronger and more sustained coordination across the entire education
and workforce development system.

        At present, education and workforce development concerns are addressed by several
government institutions, each with distinct mandates, programs, priorities, and resources. While
such specialization is necessary, the absence of a permanent, high-level mechanism that can
consistently bring these efforts together may result in fragmented policies, overlapping initiatives,
gaps in implementation, and difficulties in sustaining reforms. Changes in administration may
likewise affect the continuity of priorities and programs, making it more difficult to pursue long-
term reforms whose benefits may only become evident over many years.

       The passage of the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) Act is therefore
necessary to establish a durable institutional mechanism for coordination, policy coherence, and
accountability in the education and workforce development sector. The proposed measure
recognizes that improving education outcomes cannot be achieved through isolated interventions. It
requires a whole-of-government approach in which agencies work toward common goals, share
information, align resources, and collectively address problems that extend across basic education,
higher education, technical and vocational education, employment, science and technology, and
national development planning.

         The proposed Council is intended to strengthen the continuity and coherence of education
reforms by providing a permanent platform for collaboration at the highest levels of government.
This is particularly important in addressing the learning crisis and ensuring that reforms are not
dependent solely on the priorities or organizational arrangements of a particular administration.
Institutionalizing coordination can help ensure that important reforms are sustained, evaluated,
improved, and carried forward over time.

       The measure is likewise important because education outcomes are closely connected with
the country’s economic and workforce needs. Students must be equipped not only with foundational
knowledge but also with skills relevant to a changing labor market. Stronger coordination between
education institutions and agencies responsible for employment, economic planning, science and
technology, and public resources can help bridge the gap between what the education system
provides and what society and the economy require.
        Equally important is the need for greater accountability in the implementation of education
reforms. A coordinated national mechanism can provide clearer lines of responsibility, strengthen
monitoring of outcomes, and promote evidence-based decision-making. It can also facilitate the use
of reliable and integrated information in determining priorities and allocating public resources,
thereby helping ensure that government investments in education produce meaningful and
measurable results.

        The proposed legislation also complements ongoing efforts to improve the Philippine
education system, including reform initiatives arising from the work of the Second Congressional
Commission on Education (EDCOM II). Rather than allowing recommendations and reforms to
remain isolated within individual institutions, there is a need for an institutional structure capable of
translating national priorities into coordinated and sustained government action.

       Ultimately, education is a long-term national investment. The quality of today’s education
system will shape the country’s workforce, productivity, competitiveness, social mobility, and
capacity for inclusive development in the years ahead. Addressing the country’s education
challenges therefore requires more than additional programs; it requires stronger institutional
coordination and a sustained commitment to common national objectives.

        The passage of the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) Act will
provide the government with a permanent framework through which education and workforce
development reforms can be coordinated, sustained, and held accountable. By strengthening the
unity of purpose among government institutions, the measure seeks to ensure that education remains
a consistent and enduring national priority, regardless of changes in administration.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                                   AN ACT
     CREATING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR EDUCATION,
     DEFINING ITS MANDATE, POWERS, AND FUNCTIONS, AND APPROPRIATING
     FUNDS THEREFOR
     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "National Coordinating Council for
 2   Education (NCCE) Act."
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and promote
 4   the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such
 5   education accessible to all. The State recognizes the need to address the learning crisis and ensure
 6   the seamlessness of the education system. To this end, the State shall establish a permanent
 7   mechanism to ensure strong, effective, and continuous coordination among the agencies involved in
 8   education and workforce development, ensuring that reforms and priorities remain consistent
 9   regardless of changes in national administration.
10   SEC. 3. Creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education. – There is hereby created
11   under the Office of the President a high-level inter-agency body to be known as the National
12   Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE), hereinafter referred to as the Council, which shall
13   serve as the primary mechanism for directing, coordinating, and monitoring education and
14   workforce development reforms across the Executive Branch.
15   The Council shall operate under the supervision of the Office of the President and shall ensure
16   alignment of agency policies, plans, programs, and budgets with the National Education and
17   Workforce Development Plan, and other subsequent midterm and long-term plans on education and
18   workforce development.
19   SEC. 4. Composition of the Council. – The President of the Philippines shall serve as Chairperson
20   of the Council. The President may designate a Cabinet Secretary as Vice Chairperson to oversee
21   day-to-day coordination, with the following as members:
22      (a) Secretary, Department of Education (DepEd);
23      (b) Chairperson, Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
24      (c) Director General, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA);
25      (d) Secretary, Department of Science and Technology (DOST);
26      (e) Secretary, Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE);
27      (f) Secretary, Department of Budget and Management (DBM); and
28      (g) Secretary, Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev).
29   SEC. 5. Powers and Functions. – The Council shall have the following powers and functions:
30      (a) Serve as venue for regular collaboration and coordination to ensure harmonized policies and
31          programs of the education agencies, and other relevant government bodies;
32      (b) Implement, refine, and update the National Education and Workforce Development Plan, to
33          inform and ensure coherence of targets, priorities, and resource allocations of member
34          agencies;
35      (c) Ensure alignment and convergence with existing inter-agency mechanisms, including those
36          established under Administrative Order No. 36, s. 2025, and harmonize policies, plans, and
37          programs across relevant bodies to avoid duplication, strengthen coordination, and maximize
38          the efficient use of government resources;
39      (d) Oversee, coordinate, and monitor the implementation of priority education and workforce
40          development reforms, including those identified by the Second Congressional Commission
41          on Education (EDCOM II), and such other reform initiatives as may be adopted by the
42          Council, in order to ensure coherence, continuity, and sustained impact across
43          administrations;
44      (e) Develop and implement a comprehensive human resource in education development plan,
45          including the supply and demand of teachers and non-teaching professionals and personnel
46          required to support the needs of the education sector;
47      (f) Review and endorse major education sector budget proposals prior to submission to DBM;
48      (g) Propose and jointly implement, through relevant agencies, scholarship programs, research,
49          grants, trainings, and other initiatives, to support the above plans and to strengthen the
50          capacity of the system as a whole;
51      (h) Upgrade the country's labor market information (LMI) system to better inform student and
52          school choice, including offerings in Senior High School, TVET, higher education, and other
53          lifelong learning opportunities, and to serve as evidence-based in the strategic identification
54          of government priorities for scholarship programs and other grants;
55      (i) Establish, implement, and maintain an interoperable data system that tracks learner progress
56          beginning in early childhood, ensuring integration of data across relevant agencies, in
57          partnership with, but not limited to, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Department
58          of Health (DOH), National Nutrition Council (NNC), Department of Social Welfare and
59          Development (DSWD), and Department of Information and Communications Technology
60          (DICT);
61      (j) Review and propose mechanisms to rationalize interagency bodies participated in by the
62          education agencies, in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management,
63          consistent with Republic Act (RA) No. 12231 or the Government Optimization Act;
64      (k) Recommend to the Legislative-Executive Advisory Council (LEDAC) key legislative
65          measures related to education and workforce development;
66      (l) Implement and monitor key government reforms in education, including the
67          recommendations of EDCOM II;
68      (m) Submit to the Office of the President, the Senate of the Philippines, and the House of
69          Representatives an annual report on the status of the implementation of the National
70          Education and Workforce Development Plan, including its policy recommendations and the
71          progress and accomplishments in relation to the Council's goals and objectives.
72   SEC. 6. Technical Working Groups. – To support the work of the Council, the body may create
73   Technical Working Groups in identified priority areas. Said Groups shall be composed of senior
74   officials from member agencies, with at least the level of Assistant Secretary or Director IV of the
75   relevant offices handling these concerns.
76   Further, the Council may invite or enjoin relevant heads of departments and agencies to join the
77   Council and/or Groups, as necessary. This includes other executive agencies, heads of industry and
78   educational associations and other organizations, as well as representatives of relevant sectoral
79   groups, to ensure overall efficiency and effectiveness.
80   SEC. 7. Convergence and Accountability. – All Heads of the member-agencies of NCCE, including
81   their respective attached agencies, are hereby directed to render their full and timely assistance and
82   cooperation to the Council, and provide such information and data as may be required to carry out
83   its mandates. The Council may call upon the assistance of such other agencies of the Government,
84   as may be necessary in the performance of its functions.
85   All member agencies shall be accountable for the timely and effective implementation of programs,
86   projects, and activities aligned with the National Education and Workforce Development Plan.
 87   Furthermore, the Council shall establish a results-based monitoring and evaluation framework, with
 88   clear targets, performance indicators, and reporting mechanisms. Member agencies, as well as those
 89   invited or enjoined to support the work of the Council, shall regularly submit such data, reports, and
 90   information as may be required to assess progress and ensure accountability.
 91   SEC. 8. The Council Secretariat. – The Secretariat shall serve as the central technical, analytical,
 92   and program management arm of the Council, and shall be responsible for policy analysis, program
 93   coordination, monitoring and evaluation, data integration, and the provision of strategic and
 94   administrative support necessary for the effective discharge of the Council's mandate. It shall be
 95   headed by an Executive Director with the rank of Undersecretary, who shall be appointed by the
 96   President.
 97   The Secretariat may be staffed by a core complement of permanent personnel and augmented, as
 98   necessary, by technical experts, seconded personnel from member and other government agencies,
 99   and contracted specialists, subject to existing civil service, budgeting, procurement, and auditing
100   laws, rules, and regulations.
101   SEC. 9. Rationalization and Convergence of Inter-Agency Bodies. – To promote policy coherence
102   and administrative efficiency, the Council shall serve as the primary inter-agency platform for
103   matters relating to education and workforce development. Accordingly, existing inter-agency
104   councils, committees, and similar bodies with substantially similar mandates or memberships shall,
105   to the extent practicable, conduct their coordination, deliberations, and decision-making through the
106   Council or its Technical Working Groups.
107   Meetings convened by the Council or its duly authorized Technical Working Groups shall be
108   deemed compliance with the meeting and coordination requirements of such bodies, subject to
109   applicable laws, rules, and regulations.
110   SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall
111   be charged against the current appropriations of the Office of the President. Thereafter, the amount
112   necessary for its continuous implementation shall be included in the budget proposal of the Office
113   of the President in the annual General Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
114   and auditing rules and regulations.
115   SEC. 11. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act which shall be held to be invalid
116   or unconstitutional, other parts or provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall continue to
117   be in force and effect.
118   SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative order, rules and
119   regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended
120   accordingly.
121   SEC. 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
122   Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national circulation.
