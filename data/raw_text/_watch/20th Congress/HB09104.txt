                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Let each of you look out not only for his own interests, but also for the interests of
others.

                                                                       — Philippians 2:4

Republic Act No. 7432, otherwise known as the Senior Citizens Act, and Republic Act
No. 7277, otherwise known as the Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities, were
enacted as social protection measures to help ease the everyday financial burden
faced by senior citizens and persons with disabilities. Through mandatory discounts
and tax exemptions, these laws seek to make basic goods and essential services more
accessible to sectors that are often more vulnerable to rising costs and economic
shocks.

Social protection is most effective when it is reliable and consistently applied. In daily
transactions, however, the benefits granted under these laws are often diluted when
goods and services are offered under promotional discounts. Senior citizens and
persons with disabilities are frequently informed that the promotional price already
replaces the statutory discount, even though the law does not intend for temporary
marketing offers to substitute for legally mandated benefits. This practice weakens the
protective purpose of existing laws and creates uncertainty at the point of purchase.

This bill seeks to address that gap by clearly providing that the discounts and tax
exemptions granted under Republic Act No. 7432 and Republic Act No. 7277 shall
apply even when goods and services are offered under promotional discounts. It
expressly states that promotions extended to the general public cannot be treated as
compliance with the discount privileges mandated by law. The measure also clarifies
the tax treatment of such discounts to ensure consistent implementation while allowing
business establishments to properly account for them in accordance with existing tax
laws.

At its core, the measure reaffirms the State’s responsibility to uphold meaningful social
protection for vulnerable sectors. Preferential treatment for senior citizens and persons
with disabilities is a deliberate policy choice grounded in equity and social justice. By
removing ambiguity and closing a commonly encountered loophole, this bill
strengthens the implementation of existing laws and ensures that the protection they
offer is felt in everyday life.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
ENHANCING THE DISCOUNT ON THE PURCHASE OF GOODS AND SERVICES
       OF SENIOR CITIZENS AND PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall promote a just and dynamic
social order that shall ensure the nation’s prosperity and independence as well as free
its people from poverty. The State shall provide social justice in all phases of national
development, and value the dignity of every human person, and guarantee full respect
for human rights.

The State shall adopt an integrated approach to health and development which shall
endeavor to make essential goods, health, and other social services available to all at
affordable cost. There shall be priority for the needs of the underprivileged, sick,
elderly, and persons with disabilities.

To this end, the State shall preserve the preferential treatment accorded to senior
citizens and persons with disabilities, and proportionately distribute its costs between
the government and the people.

SEC. 2. Applicability of Discounts of Senior Citizens and Persons with
Disabilities to Goods and Services on Promotional Offer. – The discount granted
to senior citizens and persons with disabilities, including the twenty percent (20%)
discount and exemption from the value-added tax (VAT) on goods and services, or the
special discount on purchase of basic necessities and prime commodities, as provided
under Section 4 of Republic Act (RA) No. 7432, otherwise known as “Senior Citizens
Act,” as amended, and Section 32 of RA No. 7277, otherwise known as “Magna Carta
for Disabled Persons with Disabilities,” as amended, shall in addition to any prevailing
promotional offers or discounts extended by business establishments, if any, be
provided, but in no way that it will require presentation of booklets in the procurement
of goods.

In no case shall a promotion or discount offered by a business establishment to the
general public be considered in compliance with Section 4 of RA No. 7432, as
amended, and Section 32 of RA No. 7277, as amended.
SEC. 3. Tax Treatment of Discounts. – The twenty percent (20%) discount for senior
citizens or persons with disabilities applied on certain goods and services, the input
tax attributable to the VAT-exempt sale to senior citizens and persons with disabilities,
and the special discount on basic necessities and prime commodities, shall be treated
as part of deductible expense pursuant to Section 34 of the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended.

The establishments may claim the discounts granted under subsections (a), (c), and
(j) of Section 4 of RA No. 7432, as amended, and subsection (a), paragraphs (1), (2),
(3), (5), (6), (7) and (8) and subsection (d) of Section 312 of RA No. 7277, as amended,
as deduction based on the net cost of goods sold or services rendered: Provided, That
the cost of the discount shall be allowed as deduction from gross income for the same
taxable year that the discount is granted: Provided, further, That the total amount of
the claimed tax deduction net of VAT, if applicable, shall be included in their gross
sales receipts for tax purposes and shall be subject to proper documentation and to
the provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended.

SEC. 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The National Commission for
Senior Citizens and the National Council on Disability Affairs, in consultation with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, Department of Trade and Industry,
Department of Information and Communications Technology, Department of Health,
Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and other relevant agencies and
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 6. Repealing Clause. – The pertinent provisions of the following laws are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly:

   (a) Section 4 of RA No. 7432, as amended by RA No. 9994, otherwise known as
       the “Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010,” insofar as purchase of goods and
       services which are on promotional discount is concerned;

   (b) Section 32 of RA No. 7277, as amended by RA No. 10754, insofar as higher
       discount granted by the commercial establishment, other existing laws or in
       combination with other discount programs is concerned.

   (c) Section 32(j) of RA No. 9442, entitled “An Act Amending Republic Act No. 7277,
       Otherwise Known as the ‘Magna Carta for Disabled Persons,’ and for Other
       Purposes”;

   (d) Section 4(l) of RA No. 9257, entitled “An Act Granting Additional Benefits and
       Privileges to Senior Citizens Amending for the Purpose Republic Act No. 7432,
       Otherwise Known as ‘An Act to Maximize the Contribution of Senior Citizens to
       Nation Building, Grant Benefits and Special Privileges for the Other Purposes’”.
All other laws, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof, which are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
