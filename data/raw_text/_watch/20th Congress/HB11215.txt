                              AN ACT
FURTHER STRENGTHENING THE INCENTIVES FOR PRIVATE SECTOR
PARTICIPATION IN PUBLIC EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS BY AMENDING
CERTAIN PROVISIONS IN REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8525, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
"ADOPT-A-SCHOOL ACT OF 1998"

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

Education plays an important role in preparing Filipino learners for further education, employment,
and participation in society. While the government continues to provide the necessary support for
public education, partnerships with the private sector can provide additional resources, expertise,
and opportunities that complement existing government efforts. Republic Act No. 8525, otherwise
known as the “Adopt-a-School Act of 1998,” established a framework for such partnerships. With
the changing needs of learners and educational institutions, there is an opportunity to further
strengthen and update the program.

The continuing demand for additional educational resources highlights the value of sustained
cooperation between the public and private sectors. As of June 2026, the Department of Education
reported an estimated nationwide classroom backlog of 143,543 classrooms, underscoring the
continuing need to expand and improve learning spaces. 1At the same time, recent assessments by
the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) found that approximately one in
two learners from Grades 1 to 10 remained below grade-level reading proficiency by the end of
School Year 2025–2026.2 These conditions highlight opportunities for additional partnerships in
areas such as school facilities, learning resources, teacher development, and learner support.

This measure seeks to enhance the Adopt-a-School Program by expanding the forms of assistance
that private individuals and entities may provide to educational institutions. These may include
teacher and personnel development, scholarships, school facilities, learning materials and
technologies, technical-vocational equipment, health and nutrition support, and assistive learning
devices for learners with disabilities. The expanded areas of assistance are intended to give
participating institutions greater flexibility in addressing their particular educational needs.

The measure also recognizes the importance of strengthening the connection between education and
employment. The National Education and Workforce Development Plan 2026–2035 reports
baseline employment rates of 60% among higher education graduates and 70% among TVET
graduates.3 The Plan also notes that approximately 20% of Senior High School graduates entered
the labor force in 2025, while more than 70% proceeded to further education.³ These figures

providing learners with relevant training, certifications, and opportunities as they transition from
school to employment or further education.

The measure further provides incentives for qualified expenses related to scholarships, training, and
other forms of assistance under the Adopt-a-School Program. It also provides appropriate
exemptions from customs duties, taxes, and other fees for qualified donations. These measures are
intended to encourage sustained participation while ensuring that assistance is directed toward
legitimate educational purposes.

The bill also strengthens coordination among the Department of Education, Commission on Higher
Education, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, Department of Trade and
Industry, Department of Labor and Employment, and other relevant agencies. Through closer
coordination, the implementation of the program can be better aligned with the needs of educational
institutions, learners, and participating private-sector partners.

Overall, this measure seeks to build on the existing framework of the Adopt-a-School Program by
encouraging more meaningful and sustained partnerships in education. By bringing together the
resources, expertise, and opportunities of the public and private sectors, the program can provide
additional support to educational institutions, strengthen learning and training opportunities, and
help learners gain access to the facilities, skills, and employment opportunities they need to pursue
their goals.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
