                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

According to the 1987 Constitution, the State shall promote a just and dynamic social order that
frees the people from poverty through adequate social services and an improved quality of life.
Republic Act No. 11310, or the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program Act, likewise institutionalizes
cash assistance as a core tool of social protection for poor households.

Rising prices hit the poorest families first and foremost. According to the Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), inflation for the bottom thirty percent (30%) of income households rose to nine
nine point five percent (9.5%) in October 2026, its highest since early 2023, against a headline rate
of seven point eight percent (7.8%). It has stayed at eight percent (8%) or higher every month
since April 2026, while the same group saw prices fall a year earlier.

Many of these families are not even counted as poor. According to the PSA, the 2025 poverty
threshold for a family of five is only ₱14,634 a month, or about ₱96 per person a day for both
food and non-food needs. The PSA itself has admitted that its food threshold is insufficient.
Families earning slightly above this line receive no protection, even when price increases push
them back into hardship.

Notwithstanding the said irregular threshold, the law already recognizes automatic triggers. While
Republic Act No. 12316 allows the President to suspend or reduce fuel excise taxes once Dubai
crude reaches US$80 per barrel, no similar trigger exists for the prices that poor households
actually pay. Hence, relief remains ad hoc, discretionary, and often late.

The proposed measure seeks to close the said gap by mandating the automatic release of cash
assistance to poor and near-poor households whenever inflation for the bottom thirty percent
(30%) of income households reaches 6 percent nationally, or 8 percent in a region, for two
consecutive months. The assistance ranges from ₱1,000 to ₱2,000 a month depending on the
severity of inflation, and is released by the Department of Social Welfare and Development within
thirty days of the PSA's certification, without need of further executive approval. The said bill also
prohibits the use of the names and images of public officials on the assistance.

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE AUTOMATIC GRANT OF CASH ASSISTANCE TO POOR
AND NEAR-POOR HOUSEHOLDS DURING INFLATION OR PRICE SURGES,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Siguradong Ayuda Para sa
Abang Tahanan (SAPAT) Act of 2026."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to free the people from
poverty and to protect the purchasing power of poor and vulnerable families. Toward this end,
the State shall provide timely, predictable and non-discretionary cash assistance to poor and near-
poor households whenever rising prices threaten their ability to meet basic needs.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, and unless indicated otherwise,
the following terms shall be defined as:

    a. Bottom Thirty Percent (30%) Inflation Rate refers to the year-on-year rate of change of
       the Consumer Price Index for the Bottom 30% Income Households, national or regional,
       as compiled and published monthly by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) pursuant
       to Republic Act No. 10625, or the Philippine Statistical Act of 2013;

    b. Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) refers to the system of generating
       household-level data established under Republic Act No. 11315, or the Community-Based
       Monitoring System Act, administered by the PSA and conducted in coordination with local
       government units;

    c. Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) Social Registry refers to the
       National Beneficiary Identification System for Vulnerable Populations maintained by the
       Social Registry Office of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
       which consolidates CBMS data with the data of DSWD programs, or its successor
       database;




                                                                                                     1
   d. Official Poverty Threshold refers to the regional per capita poverty threshold most
      recently released by the PSA in its official poverty statistics;

   e. Poor Household refers to a household whose per capita income falls below the Official
      Poverty Threshold, as identified through the CBMS and recorded in the DSWD Social
      Registry;

   f. Near-Poor Household refers to a household whose per capita income is at or above the
      Official Poverty Threshold but does not exceed one hundred thirty percent (130%)
      thereof, as identified through the CBMS and recorded in the DSWD Social Registry;

   g. Trigger Period refers to any period during which a national or regional trigger under
      Section 4 is in effect; and

   h. Inflation Relief Grant refers to the monthly cash assistance provided under this Act.

      SEC. 4. Automatic Trigger. – The grant of the Inflation Relief Grant shall be
automatically triggered as follows:

   a. National Trigger – When the national Bottom 30% Inflation Rate reaches or exceeds six
      percent (6.0%) for two (2) consecutive months, all eligible households nationwide shall
      receive the grant;

   b. Regional Trigger – When the Bottom 30% Inflation Rate of a region reaches or exceeds
      eight percent (8.0%) for two (2) consecutive months, all eligible households in that region
      shall receive the grant, even if the national trigger has not been reached; and

   c. Certification – Within five (5) working days from the release of the data for the second
      qualifying month, the PSA shall certify that the trigger has been reached and transmit the
      certification to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the
      Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and both the House of Representatives
      and Senate.

       The release of the grant upon certification by the PSA is mandatory and ministerial. It shall
not require any further recommendation, declaration or approval from any office.

        SEC. 5. Eligible Households. – The following households shall be eligible for the Inflation
Relief Grant:

   a. Household beneficiaries of the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program implemented by the
      DSWD under Republic Act No. 11310;
   b. Poor Households and Near-Poor Households recorded in the DSWD Social Registry;
   c. Households of indigent senior citizens receiving the social pension under Republic Act
      No. 7432, as amended by Republic Act Nos. 9994 and 11916; and
   d. Near-Poor Households not yet recorded in the DSWD Social Registry, upon application
      with the city or municipal social welfare and development office and validation by the
      Social Registry Office of the DSWD.

        Each household shall receive only one (1) grant per month regardless of the number of
    categories under which it qualifies.


       SEC. 6. Amount of the Grant. – The monthly Inflation Relief Grant per household shall
be based on the applicable Bottom 30% Inflation Rate for the month, as follows:

         Bottom 30% Inflation Rate                        Monthly grant per household
 6.0% to below 8.0%                               ₱1,000.00
 8.0% to below 10.0%                              ₱1,500.00
 10.0% and above                                  ₱2,000.00

Where a regional trigger applies, the regional rate shall determine the amount for households in
that region. Every three (3) years, the DSWD, in coordination with the Department of Economy,
Planning, and Development (DEPDev), shall review these amounts and may increase, but not
reduce, them.

       SEC. 7. Duration. – The grant shall be released for every month that the Trigger Period
remains in effect. A Trigger Period ends when the applicable Bottom 30% Inflation Rate falls
below the trigger level for two (2) consecutive months.

         SEC. 8. Mode and Timeline of Release. – The DSWD shall release the first grant within
thirty (30) days from receipt of the PSA certification, and every month thereafter during the Trigger
Period. Release shall be made through existing cash cards, government depository banks,
accredited electronic wallets or, where these are unavailable, direct payout

        SEC. 9. Relation to Other Programs. – The Inflation Relief Grant shall be in addition to,
and shall not be deducted from, the regular grants under Republic Act No. 11310 and the social
pension under Republic Act No. 7432, as amended. A household receiving other emergency cash
assistance for the same month, by reason of inflation or fuel price increases, shall receive only
whichever amount is higher.

        SEC. 10. Prohibition on Political Branding. – No name, image, logo or symbol of any
public official or candidate shall appear on any cash card, envelope, signage or material related to
the grant. No elected official shall personally distribute the grant. Any violation shall be punished
with a fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred
thousand pesos (₱200,000.00), without prejudice to administrative liability.

        SEC. 11. Transparency and Reporting. – The DSWD shall publish on its website, every
month during a Trigger Period, the number of beneficiaries and the total amount released per city
and municipality. Within sixty (60) days after a Trigger Period ends, the DSWD shall submit a
report to both Houses of Congress. The Commission on Audit shall conduct a special audit of
each Trigger Period.

       SEC. 12. Tax Exemption. – The Inflation Relief Grant shall be exempt from income tax
and shall not be subject to garnishment, levy or execution.

        SEC. 13. Appropriations. – An Inflation Relief Standby Fund shall be included annually
in the budget of the DSWD under the General Appropriations Act. The amount shall be sufficient
to fund at least three (3) months of the grant under a national trigger, based on estimates of the
DSWD and DEPDev. For the initial year, the amount needed shall be charged against the current
appropriations of the DSWD and the Contingent Fund. Where the Standby Fund is insufficient
during a Trigger Period, the DBM shall release the deficiency from the Contingent Fund or
unprogrammed appropriations.

         SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the PSA, DBM, DEPDev, Department of
the Interior and Local Government and the Land Bank of the Philippines, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations to implement this Act. Delay in their issuance shall not prevent the
implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any provision of this Act, or any part
thereof, is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof not otherwise affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved.
