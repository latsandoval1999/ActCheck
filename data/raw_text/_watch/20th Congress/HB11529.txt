20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   The Philippines is, by geography and by livelihood, a nation deeply connected to the
23   sea. With a coastline stretching approximately 36,289 kilometers, and more than 70
24   million Filipinos living in coastal areas, the health of our seas and coastlines is
25   inseparable from the welfare of our people.
26
27   Our coastal ecosystems are likewise among the country's most valuable natural assets.
28   According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), as of 2025, the Philippines had
29   approximately 2.15 million hectares of coastal resources, consisting of 1.34 million
30   hectares of coral reefs (or at least 62.3%) 496,560 hectares of seagrass (or at least
31   23%) and seaweed areas, and 318,160 hectares of mangrove forests (or at least
32   14.8%)
33
34   Likewise, the PSA, in 2024, reported that the country's ocean-based industries
35   generated ₱943.05 billion in 2023, contributing 3.9% to the country's Gross Domestic
36   Product, with the ocean economy growing by 9.9% from the previous year. Ocean
37   fishing alone accounted for 28.6% of the total ocean economy.
38
39   However, undeniably, these resources and industries face increasing, if not constant,
40   pressures from development, pollution, climate change, habitat degradation, and poorly
41   coordinated activities across political boundaries.
42
43   Hence, this proposed measure establishes Integrated Coastal Management (ICM) as a
44   national strategy to coordinate national agencies, LGUs, communities, and other
45   stakeholders in protecting and managing coastal resources. Aside from the creation of
46   a National Coordinating Committee on ICM to lead this initiative, it also creates a
47   National Coastal Greenbelt Action Plan to identify and restore vulnerable coastal areas,
 1   revert abandoned, undeveloped, and underutilized fishponds to mangroves, and
 2   remove illegal structures in protected areas.
 3
 4   In short, the proposed measure combines coordinated governance, science-based
 5   planning, and ecosystem restoration—including penalties for unauthorized structures
 6   and other violations within designated coastal greenbelts. By institutionalizing
 7   integrated coastal management, the measure aims to ensure that the Philippines'
 8   coastal resources remain productive and capable of sustaining communities and the
 9   economy for generations to come.
10
11   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

36        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Integrated Coastal
37   Management Act.”
38
39          SEC. 2. Policy Statement. – It shall be the policy of the State to ensure
40   the sustainable management of the country’s coastal and marine environment and
41   resources by looking at the interconnectivity of ecosystems from the watersheds to the
42   coastal and marine areas. Towards this end, it shall adopt Integrated Coastal
43   Management as a framework in regulating different human activities, restoring or
44   rehabilitating ecosystems, and ensuring a multi-stakeholder participation and ridge-to-
45   reef approach to ecosystem management, with due consideration to ecological
46   connectivity and their impact on social and ecological systems. The framework shall
47   support the country’s achievement of sustainable development, promotion of blue
48   economy, food security, just transition to sustainable livelihood, poverty alleviation
49   and its resilience and risk reduction, from sea-level rise and other coastal stresses,
50   while respecting the traditional resource rights of indigenous peoples and gender
51   equality.
52
 1           SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. – Integrated Coastal Management shall
 2   be implemented in all local government units (LGUs) addressing the inter-linkages
 3   among ecosystems from ridge-to-reef, by all relevant government structures at the
 4   national and local levels in consultation and partnership with all stakeholders through
 5   participatory governance.

 7         SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
 8
 9         (a) Adaptation refers to the adjustment in natural or human systems in
10         response to actual or expected climatic stimuli or their effects, which
11         moderates harm or exploits beneficial opportunities;
12
13         (b) Annual Investment Program (AIP) refers to the annual slice of the
14         Local Development Investment Program (LDIP), which constitutes the total
15         resource requirements for all programs, projects, and activities consisting of
16         the annual capital expenditure and regular operating requirements of the LGU;
17
18         (c) Beach forest refers to mixed association of creepers, shrubs, and trees
19         above the high tide line along the coast which fall into various groups
20         morphologically based on the following forms:
21
22                    (1) Herbaceous Beach – creepers with sand-binding abilities, e.g.,
23                    Ipomoea pes-caprae;
24
25                    (2) Beach Scrub –     shrubs and low         trees, e.g.,   Scaevola
26                    taccada, Dendrolobium umbellatum;
27
28                    (3) Casuarina equisetifolia (Agoho) Forest – common pioneer on
29                    sandy beaches; and
30
31                    (4) Mixed Littoral Forest – plentiful vines, palms and trees, e.g.,
32                    Barringtonia asiatica, Millettia pinnata, and Pandanus tectorius;
33
34         (d) Biological diversity or Biodiversity refers to the variability among living
35         organisms from all sources including terrestrial, marine, and other aquatic
36         ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part. This
37         includes diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems;
38
39         (e) Climate Change refers to a change of climate which is attributed directly
40         indirectly to human activity that alters the composition of global atmosphere
41         and which is in addition to natural climate variability observed over comparable
42         time periods;
43
44         (f) Coastal area or zone refers to a band of dry land and adjacent ocean
45         space (water and submerged land) in which terrestrial processes and uses
46         directly affect oceanic processes and uses, and vice versa; its geographic
47         extent may include areas within a landmark limit of one (1) kilometer from the
48         shoreline at high tide to include mangrove swamps, brackish water ponds, nipa
49         swamps, estuarine rivers, sandy beaches, and other areas within a seaward
50         limit of 200 meters isobath to include coral reefs, algal flats, seagrass beds, and
51         other soft-bottom areas;
 1
 2   (g) Coastal greenbelts refer to at least 100-meter- wide strip of natural or
 3   planted coastal vegetation extending from the seaward edge of mangroves
 4   (middle intertidal zone) towards land, or extending from the seaward edge of
 5   beach forest (high tide line) towards land, in cases where mangroves are
 6   absent. Its function is to absorb wave energy during storms thereby
 7   reducing wave damage, preventing coastal erosion, and protecting human lives
 8   and property;
 9
10   (h) Comprehensive Development Plan (CDP) refers to a six-year multi-sectoral
11   plan formulated at the city or municipal level embodying the vision, sectoral
12   goals, objectives, development strategies, and policies within the terms of the
13   officials of theLGU, and the        medium-term.       It     contains
14   ecological      profile, sectoral development    plan,    and    implementation
15   instruments;
16
17   (i) Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) refers to the document formulated
18   by the cities and municipalities, in consultation with its stakeholders, that
19   defines or provides guidelines on the allocation, utilization, development, and
20   management of all lands within a given territory or jurisdiction according
21   to the inherent qualities of the land itself and supportive economic,
22   demographic, socio-cultural, and environmental objectives as defined in
23   Republic Act No. 11201, otherwise known as the “Department of Human
24   Settlements and Urban Development Act”;
25
26   (j) Disaster risk reduction refers to the concept and practice of reducing
27   disaster risks through systematic efforts to analyze and manage the causal
28   factors of disasters, through reduced exposure to hazards, lessened
29   vulnerability of people and property, wise management of land and the
30   environment, and improved preparedness for adverse events;
31
32   (k) Ecosystems services refer to the benefits people obtain from the
33   ecosystems such as:
34
35         (1) provisioning which refers to the services that provide food, water,
36         timber, and fiber;
37         (2) regulating which refers to the services that affect climate, floods,
38         disease, wastes, and water quality;
39         (3) cultural which refers to the services that provide recreational,
40         aesthetic, and spiritual benefits; and
41         (4) supporting which refers to the essential services such as soil
42         formation, photosynthesis, nutrient cycling, and carbon sequestration;
43
44   (l) Holistic and sustainable management refers to             the
45   management of all components of the ecosystem and the services it provides in
46   a way and at a rate that maintains and enhances its current state and resilience,
47   thereby maintaining its potential to meet the needs of the aspirations of
48   present and future generations. This approach involves all stakeholders in
49   collaborative decision-making, including scientists, national and local
50   government, private sector, local residents, and the public towards the
 1   protection, conservation, restoration, and sustainable use of the ecosystem and
 2   the resources therein;
 3
 4   (m) Integrated Coastal Management (ICM) refers to a holistic and
 5   integrative management that incorporates the following components: (1)
 6   connectivity of various ecosystems – environment and resources; (2)
 7   various sectors and stakeholders;    (3)   institutional arrangements
 8   that are      relevant;   and (4) assessment of drivers of coastal
 9   ecosystem degradation, management planning and implementation, including
10   research and education, to address coastal degradation and biodiversity
11   losses,       and to      improve    the   well-being    of communities in
12   coastal and interconnected areas;
13
14   (n) Local Climate Change Action Plan (LCCAP) refers to the action plan
15   formulated by LGUs to address climate change concerns. It focuses on both
16   climate change adaptation and mitigation and describes how LGUs plan to
17   respond to the impacts of climate change and mainstream them into mandated
18   local plans, i.e., land use plan, sectoral development plan, investment
19   program, pursuant to Section 14 of Republic Act No. 9729, or the “Climate
20   Change Act of 2009,” as amended;
21
22   (o) Mangrove forest refers to the forested wetland growing along tidal mudflats
23   and along shallow water coastal areas extending inland along rivers, streams,
24   and their tributaries where the water is generally brackish and composed
25   of low elevation species such as Avicennia marina, A. alba, and Sonneratia
26   alba. The mid        zone is      dominated by        Rhizophora species,
27   Sonneratia caseolaris, Xylocarpus granatum and Nypa fruticans while the
28   landward zone or areas with higher elevation are inhabited by Avicennia
29   rumphiana, A. officinalis, and Bruguiera gymnorrhiza;
30
31   (p) Mitigation, in the context of climate change, refers to human intervention
32   to address anthropogenic emissions by sources and removals by sinks of
33   all greenhouse gases, including ozone-depleting substances and their
34   substitutes;
35
36   (q) Municipal waters refer not only to streams, lakes, inland bodies of water, and
37   tidal waters within the municipality which are not included within the protected
38   areas as defined under Republic Act No.11038, otherwise known as the
39   “Expanded National Integrated Protected Areas System Act of 2018”, public
40   forest, timber lands, forest reserves, or fishery reserves, but also marine
41   waters included between two (2) lines drawn perpendicular to the general
42   coastline from points where the boundary lines of the municipality touch the
43   sea at low tide and a third line parallel with the general coastline including
44   offshore islands and fifteen (15) kilometers from such coastline. Where two
45   (2) municipalities are so situated on opposite shores and when with less than
46   thirty (30) kilometers of marine waters between them, the third line shall be
47   equally distant from opposite shore of the respective municipalities;
48
49   (r) Provincial Development and Physical Framework Plan (PDPFP) refers to the
50   plan document formulated at the provincial level that merges the traditionally
51   separate provincial physical framework plan and provincial development plan
 1         to address the disconnect between spatial and sectoral factors and between
 2         medium and long-term concerns. It contains the long-term vision of the province,
 3         and identifies development goals, strategies, objectives or targets and
 4         corresponding programs, projects, and activities which serve as primary
 5         inputs to provincial investment programming and subsequent budgeting and
 6         plan implementation;
 7
 8         (s) State of the Coasts (SoC) reporting system refers to an assessment tool for
 9         LGUs to be used in measuring the progress and benefits of ICM
10         implementation. It allows the LGUs to document and measure the
11         effectiveness and impacts of policy      and management interventions in
12         support        of   sustainable coastal development and evaluate progress
13         towards local, national, and international targets for sustainable development;
14
15         (t) Watershed refers to a land area drained by a stream or fixed body of water
16         and its tributaries having a common outlet for surface run-off. It is a
17         topographically delineated area of land from which rainwater can drain as
18         surface run-off, via a specific stream or river system to a common outlet point.
19         It encompasses ridge-to-reef areas and may include the uplands, lowlands, and
20         coastal areas;
21
22         (u) Wetlands refer to a wide variety of inland habitats such as marshes,
23         peatlands, floodplains, rivers and lakes, and coastal areas such as saltmarshes,
24         mangroves, intertidal mudflats; and seagrass beds; and also, coral reefs and
25         other marine areas no deeper than six (6) meters at low tide, as well as human-
26         made wetlands such as dams, reservoirs, rice paddies, and wastewater
27         treatment ponds and lagoons.

38   Sec. 31. National Coastal Greenbelt Action Plan. – The NCC shall identify and
39   convene all national government agencies responsible for foreshore management,
40   mangrove and beach forest protection and utilization, coastal land and sea-
41   use planning, coastal tourism development, social welfare of coastal communities, and
42   other relevant mandates, to prepare an integrated National Coastal Greenbelt Action
43   Plan (NCGAP).
44
45          The NCGAP shall, at the minimum, contain the following:
46
47         (a) Spatial representation, or if feasible, quick or rapid national inventory of the
48         status of coastlines and offshores, including the status of mangroves, beach
49         forests, settlements, structures and fishponds within 100 meters therein;
50
 1   (b) The action plan shall indicate that the assessment of priority areas shall be
 2   completed within twelve (12) months from the adoption of the NCGAP;
 3
 4   (c) Designation of priority areas for coastal greenbelts that are already included
 5   either as a protected area under Republic Act No. 11038 or as a fish refuge or
 6   sanctuary under Republic Act No. 8550, otherwise known as “The
 7   Philippine Fisheries Code of the Philippines”, as amended by Republic Act
 8   No. 10654, or as a local marine protected area as may be declared by
 9   municipalities and cities through ordinances. The designation of a priority
10   area shall be completed within six (6) months from the completion of the
11   applicable    biophysical, socioeconomic,         and geological
12   assessment contemplated under Section 32 of this Act. Designation shall also
13   be done through a proposal to the appropriate agency, municipality or city as
14   may be deemed necessary. In the event an area is designated as a priority
15   area for coastal greenbelts, no structure shall be allowed therein unless it is
16   approved by the DENR or DA through the BFAR: Provided, That with respect
17   to the Province of Palawan, the necessary clearance from the PCSD shall also
18   be required pursuant to Republic Act No. 7611, otherwise known as “Strategic
19   Environmental Plan (SEP) for Palawan Act”;
20
21   (d) Operational plan for the rehabilitation, reforestation, or afforestation of
22   designated priority coastal greenbelts with ecologically appropriate mangrove
23   and beach forest species, not less than 100 meters in width. Existing forests or
24   plantations greater than the minimum width shall not be reduced. The
25   program for rehabilitation, reforestation or afforestation shall be community-
26   based, long-term,      and implemented through           the   municipality or
27   city government. The operational plan shall indicate that the program
28   for rehabilitation, reforestation, and afforestation must cover a minimum target
29   area of twenty percent (20%) of the designated priority areas in the first five (5)
30   years for maximum protection of the most vulnerable communities in the city or
31   municipality. The remaining priority areas must be completed within ten (10)
32   years from the designation of the priority area;
33
34   (e) All fishponds that are declared by the DENR and DA-BFAR, through a joint
35   assessment, as abandoned, undeveloped or underutilized (AUU) shall be
36   turned over to the DENR within one (1) year after its declaration. The DENR
37   will then revert the AUU fishponds to their original mangrove state through
38   natural or artificial reforestation in accordance with Section 49 of Republic
39   Act No. 8550 as amended by Republic Act No. 10654. All AUU fishponds
40   turned over to the DENR shall be declared as part of the coastal greenbelt.
41   The minimum target for the first year of implementation of the NCGAP will
42   include all AUU fishpond areas turned over to the DENR for reversion to their
43   original mangrove state;
44
45   (f) Operational Plan for the removal of illegal structures such as breakwaters,
46   permanent residential or commercial structures, and the like, in the identified
47   priority coastal greenbelts. The NCGAP shall indicate that the declaration of
48   and notice to persons responsible for the removal of illegal structures shall be
49   completed within twelve (12) months upon issuance of Notice to Vacate. The
50   removal of illegal structures and the start of rehabilitation, reforestation or
 1         afforestation of these areas shall form part of the minimum target for the first
 2         year of implementation of the NCGAP; and
 3
 4         (g) Monitoring and evaluation plan, with quantitative and qualitative targets
 5         consistent with (d), (e), and (f) above, appropriate indicators and reasonable
 6         means of verification. The plan shall become an integrated part of the
 7         NCGAP-responsive LCCAP.
 8
 9         The NCGAP and all of its relevant components shall be incorporated into
10         the mandated local plans of the LGUs, such as, but not limited to, CLUP and
11         CDP.

15   or mangrove areas, AUU fishponds turned over to the DENR,          site        specific

13          SEC. 35. Prohibited Acts. – The following shall constitute prohibited
14   acts punishable under this Act, without prejudice to accrued liability for acts and
15   omissions punishable under existing environmental laws:
16
17         (a) Any person who unjustly obstructs the LGU, DENR, or DA through BFAR
18         and PCSD in the Province of Palawan in the removal of illegal structures in
19         the process of reversion of AUU fishponds, shall be liable to the agency for
20         an administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) per day that the
21         agency is prevented from performing its function.
22
23         (b) No structures shall be built within the identified coastal greenbelts without
24         a permit from DENR, or DA through the BFAR, or PCSD in the Province of
25         Palawan, consistent with their mandates. Any person who violates this
26         prohibition shall, be liable to the agency for an administrative fine of Fifty
27         Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) per day from the time the structure was built
28         until its removal.
29
30         (c) A crime or offense committed within designated coastal greenbelt areas
31         shall subject the offender to a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand
32         Pesos (₱100,000.00)       and    not    more than       Five Million Pesos
33         (₱5,000,000.00), depending on the gravity of the offense.
34
35         (d) Any violation of the provisions of this Act and its implementing rules
36         and regulations duly promulgated and published in accordance with Section 39
37         of this Act, shall be subject to an administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos
38         (₱50,000.00) upon any person or entity found guilty thereof.
39
40          Proceeds from the administrative fines imposed under this Section shall be
41   remitted to the Bureau of the Treasury.
42
43          SEC. 36. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
44   of the Act shall be charged against the current year appropriations of the departments
45   or agencies concerned. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continuing
46   implementation of this Act shall be sourced from and included in the annual General
47   Appropriations Act.
48
49         The LGUs concerned may provide the necessary funds for the purpose, in their
50   respective annual budgets.
 1           SEC. 37. Other Funding Options. – The NCC, the local development councils,
 2   the LGUs, and the national government agencies may source local and international
 3   grants, bequests, donations, and other sustainable financing mechanisms in support
 4   of ICM implementation. LGUs to be prioritized in the allocation of these funds shall
 5   include sixth- up to third-class municipalities and LGUs with existing ICM and/or related
 6   policies.
 7
 8          The Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the Philippines,
 9   the People's Credit and Finance Corporation, and other relevant government
10   financial institutions shall formulate and identify loan and financing mechanisms that
11   shall be made available to support local ICM-responsive programs, including
12   alternative livelihood projects for small-scale fisherfolks and cooperatives.
13
14          SEC. 38. Tax exemption. – All grants, bequests, endowments, donations, and
15   contributions made to the NCC, the local development councils, the LGUs, the DA,
16   the DENR, and the PCSD to be used actually, directly, and exclusively for the
17   implementation of the ICM shall be exempt from donor’s tax and the same shall
18   be considered as allowable deductions from gross income for purposes of computing
19   the taxable income of the donor in accordance with the provisions of the National
20   Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
21
22          SEC. 39. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DA, DENR, CCC,
23   DHSUD, and DILG, in consultation with concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall
24   promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within one hundred eighty
25   (180) days from its effectivity: Provided, That failure to issue the implementing rules and
26   regulations will not prevent the implementation of this Act upon effectivity.
27
28          SEC. 40. Annual Report. – The NCC shall submit an annual report to Congress
29   of the Philippines and the Office of the President on its compliance with its functions
30   under this Act, the implementation of the ICM Framework, and the SoC Report on
31   or before March 30 of every year following the effectivity of this Act.
32
33          SEC. 41. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee. – There is hereby
34   created a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee (JCOC) to monitor the
35   implementation of this Act and to review the implementing rules and regulations
36   promulgated by the DA, DENR, DHSUD, and CCC for a period not exceeding five
37   (5) years from the effectivity of this Act. The Committee shall be composed of five
38   (5) Senators and five (5) Representatives to be appointed by the Senate President
39   and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively. The Oversight
40   Committee shall be co-chaired by the Chairpersons of the Senate Committee
41   on Environment,     Natural        Resources and Climate          Change     and
42   the    House of Representatives Committee on Climate Change.
43
44         The secretariat of the JCOC shall be drawn from existing personnel of
45   the Senate Committee on Environment, Natural Resources and Climate Change,
46   and House of Representatives Committee on Climate Change.
47
48           SEC. 42. Separability Clause. – If any of the sections or provisions of this Act
49   is held invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid.
1          SEC. 43. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, orders, resolutions,
2   instructions, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which are
3   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
4   accordingly.
5
6           SEC. 44. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
7   its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
