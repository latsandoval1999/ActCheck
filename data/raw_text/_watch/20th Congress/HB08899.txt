                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

   The Katarungang Pambarangay (Barangay Justice System) plays a central
role in decongesting courts and promoting peaceful, community-based resolution
of disputes. Yet, many barangays face gaps in training, resources, and access to
legal assistance. These weaknesses undermine the ability of the Lupong
Tagapamayapa to deliver fair, effective, and timely justice.

   The bill strengthens the Barangay Justice System through:

   1. The Barangay Legal Aid Fund, ensuring free legal assistance for indigent
      residents;

   2. The Paralegal Training Program, capacitating barangay officials and
      volunteers with essential legal knowledge;

   3. Expanded Lupong Tagapamayapa training, covering alternative dispute
      resolution, human rights, gender sensitivity, and restorative justice; and
      4. Institutional support structures, including barangay justice support units
         and focal points of the Department of Justice.

         In addition, this proposed measure introduces penalties and accountability
   mechanisms to prevent corruption, misuse of funds, and abuse of authority,
   thereby protecting the integrity of the Barangay Justice System.

          This bill also advances the country’s commitments under the United
   Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 16: Peace, Justice, and Strong
   Institutions, which promotes inclusive access to justice and the strengthening of
   accountable institutions.

          By enacting the Barangay Justice System Strengthening Act, we reinforce
   the role of barangays as the foundation of peace and order, while ensuring that
   justice remains accessible, fair, and community-driven.

         In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Justice
System Strengthening Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote access to justice at the grassroots level by strengthening the Barangay
Justice System, enhancing the capacity of the Lupong Tagapamayapa, and
providing legal assistance to communities. In line with the constitutional mandate
to ensure a just and humane society, the State shall institutionalize mechanisms
that empower barangays to resolve disputes fairly, efficiently, and peacefully.


SEC. 3. Barangay Legal Aid Fund. — There is hereby established a Barangay
Legal Aid Fund, to be administered by the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG) in coordination with the Department of Justice (DOJ), to
support:
         (a) Free legal assistance for indigent barangay residents in disputes brought
         before the Lupong Tagapamayapa;
         (b) Access to legal advice and documentation for barangay-level cases;
         (c) Honoraria for accredited volunteer lawyers and paralegals who provide
         community-based legal services.


SEC. 4. Paralegal Training Program. — The DILG, in coordination with the
DOJ and the Philippine Association of Law Schools, shall establish a Paralegal
Training Program to capacitate barangay officials, Lupong Tagapamayapa
members, and community volunteers in basic legal concepts, mediation, and
conflict resolution.


Graduates of the program shall be accredited as Barangay Paralegals and may
assist in barangay conciliation proceedings, subject to supervision of the Lupong
Tagapamayapa.


SEC. 5. Expansion of Lupong Tagapamayapa Training                 – The DILG shall
develop and implement an expanded training curriculum covering:
   (a) Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR);
   (b) Human rights and gender sensitivity;
   (c) Child protection and anti-violence laws;
   (d) Restorative justice practices; and
   (e) Basic paralegal and legal aid services.


Continuing education programs shall be conducted at least once every two (2)
years.
SEC. 6. Institutional Support. – The DILG shall establish a Barangay Justice
Support Unit (BJSU) at the provincial and city levels to provide technical, legal,
and administrative assistance to barangays in implementing this Act.


The DOJ shall designate focal persons in every province to coordinate referrals
from barangays and facilitate access to formal courts when necessary.


SEC. 7. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DILG and
DOJ. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


SEC. 8. Penalties and Accountability. —
   (a) Misuse of Barangay Legal Aid Fund — Any barangay official, employee,
      or any person who misappropriates, diverts, or improperly utilizes the
      Barangay Legal Aid Fund for purposes other than those provided under
      this Act shall be:
         1. Administratively liable under the Local Government Code and Civil
             Service rules;
         2. Criminally liable under existing laws, including but not limited to
             the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (R.A. 3019), and the
             Revised Penal Code provisions on malversation of public funds; and
         3. Required to return or reimburse the misused funds, without
             prejudice to other penalties.


   (b) Failure to Implement. — Barangay Captains, Lupong Tagapamayapa
      members, or other responsible officials who, without justifiable reason, fail
      to implement the provisions of this Act, including the establishment of
      legal aid programs or participation in mandated training, shall be subject
      to:
            1. First Offense – Written reprimand and mandatory corrective
               training;
            2. Second Offense – Suspension of one (1) to three (3) months from
               office;
            3. Third Offense – Administrative and criminal liability, including
               possible removal from office, subject to due process.


   (c) Volunteer Lawyers and Paralegals. — Any accredited volunteer lawyer or
      barangay paralegal who is found to have committed gross negligence,
      misconduct, or abuse of authority in providing legal services under this Act
      shall:
            1. Be delisted from accreditation by the DILG and DOJ;
            2. Be barred from re-accreditation for a period of three (3) years; and
            3. Be referred to the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) or the
               Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), as appropriate, for
               disciplinary proceedings.


   (d) Reporting Mechanism. — The DILG shall establish a confidential
      complaint and reporting mechanism for citizens to report misuse of funds,
      abuse of authority, or non-compliance with this Act at the barangay level.


SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG and DOJ, in consultation with the
Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP), civil society organizations, and other
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.
SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of this
Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof not
affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations,
rules and regulations, and other issues or parts thereof contrary to or inconsistent
with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
