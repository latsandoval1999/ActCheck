                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Education is a fundamental right guaranteed by the 1987 Constitution,
which declares education as a primary concern of the State. Article XIV, Section
1 mandates the State to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality
education at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such education
accessible to all. Complementing this mandate, Article XIV, Section 2(3) directs
the State to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, student loan
programs, subsidies, and other incentives for deserving students in both public
and private schools, particularly those belonging to underprivileged sectors.
Furthermore, Article XIV, Section 5(5) requires the State to assign the highest
budgetary priority to education and ensure that the education sector is
adequately supported in order to promote quality learning outcomes.

      Despite these constitutional commitments, many Filipino families
continue to experience barriers that prevent them from fully benefiting from
educational opportunities. Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)1,
through the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI), has consistently shown that
educational attainment remains one of the most prevalent forms of deprivation
among Filipino households. Earlier MPI data revealed that a significant
proportion of families had at least one member aged eighteen (18) years or older
who had not completed basic education.2 More recent poverty and education
reports likewise indicate that economic hardship continues to be a major factor
contributing to school non-completion, particularly among households belonging
to lower-income sectors.

       Over the past decade, the government has introduced major reforms aimed
at improving access to education. The enactment of the Universal Access to
Quality Tertiary Education Act (Republic Act No. 10931) provides free tuition and
other school fees in state universities and colleges, while the Pantawid Pamilyang
Pilipino Program (Republic Act No. 11310) incentivizes school attendance among
children from economically disadvantaged households. However, despite these
measures, many students continue to face substantial financial challenges in
meeting the daily and indirect costs of schooling, including expenses for food,
medicine, school supplies, and other basic necessities.

       Recent discussions on learning recovery and educational participation
following prolonged disruptions in schooling have further underscored the
vulnerability of underprivileged students. Education sector assessments have
consistently highlighted that poverty-related costs associated with schooling
remain a key driver of absenteeism, delayed educational attainment, and early
withdrawal from school. These realities demonstrate that ensuring access to
education requires not only tuition-related support but also broader social
support mechanisms that address the everyday needs of students from
disadvantaged households.

       This proposed measure seeks to address these challenges by granting
discount privileges for underprivileged students on essential commodities such
as food, medicine, school supplies, and other basic necessities. By reducing the
everyday financial burden faced by economically disadvantaged students, the
measure aims to create an enabling environment that encourages school
participation and completion while easing the economic pressures faced by their
families.

       The proposed policy does not conflict with existing education laws but
instead complements and strengthens current assistance programs under
Republic Act No. 10931 and Republic Act No. 11310. By providing additional
support mechanisms beyond tuition assistance, the measure reinforces the
State’s broader commitment to ensuring that no Filipino student is denied the
opportunity to pursue education due to poverty.

       Education remains one of the most powerful instruments for social
mobility, poverty reduction, and national development. Ensuring that every
Filipino student has both the opportunity and the means to pursue education is
essential to fulfilling the constitutional promise of equitable and accessible
learning for all.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
    GRANTING DISCOUNTS ON BASIC AND EDUCATION SERVICES TO
   UNDERPRIVILEGED STUDENTS AT ALL LEVELS, INCLUDING THOSE
   ENROLLED IN TECHNICAL-VOCATIONAL (TECH-VOC) INSTITUTIONS

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Underprivileged
Student Discount Act".

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to prioritize quality education and take appropriate steps to make such
education accessible to all.

      SECTION 3. Underprivileged Students. — For this purpose of this Act,
“underprivileged student” shall refer to a Filipino student enrolled in basic
education, post-secondary non-degree technical-vocational courses, and those
enrolled in bachelor's Degree Programs at the college level, whose parents have
a gross annual income of not more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P200,000.00) subject to review by the National Economic and Development
Authority (NEDA) every three years: Provided, that a Filipino student who opts
to take part-time jobs to subsidize the student's education and whose income
combined with the annual gross income of the student's parents do not exceed
Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000,00), subject to review by the NEDA
every three (3) years, is deemed an Underprivileged Student;

     SECTION 4. Discounts for Underprivileged Students. — Underprivileged
students shall be entitled to the following:

   a. On Food Establishments — A five percent (5%) discount shall be granted
      to underprivileged students by food establishments such as food chains,
     canteens, and restaurants anywhere in the country: Provided, that private
     food establishments may claim the cost as an allowable tax deduction from
     the gross income in the computation of the income tax in accordance with
     the provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as
     amended;
  b. On Medicines — A five percent (5%) discount shall be granted to
     underprivileged students by pharmacies or drug stores anywhere in the
     country: Provided, that these pharmacies or drug stores may claim the
     cost as an allowable tax deduction from the gross income in the
     computation of the income tax in accordance with the provisions of the
     NIRC of 1997, as amended;
  c. On Textbooks and School Supplies — A five percent (5%) discount shall be
     granted to underprivileged students by establishments anywhere in the
     country that sells reference books and schools supplies: Provided, that
     these establishments may claim the cost as an allowable tax deduction
     from the gross income in the computation of the income tax in accordance
     with the provisions of the NIRC of 1997. as amended;
  d. On Tuition Fees, Miscellaneous, and Other School Fees — A five percent
     (5%) discount shall be granted to underprivileged students by public and
     private schools where they are enrolled, without prejudice to their right to
     avail themselves of other educational assistance given by the school and
     the government, such as those provided under Republic Act No. 6728, as
     amended by Republic Act No. 8545 or the "Government Assistance to
     Students and Teachers in Private Education (EGASTPE", Republic Act No.
     9442 or the law amending the "Magna Carta for the Disabled", Republic
     Act No. 10687 or the "Unified Student Financial Assistance System for
     Tertiary Education (UniFAST)", and Republic Act No. 10931 or the
     "Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education (UAQTE) Act": Provided,
     that the schools may claim the cost as an allowable tax deduction from
     the gross income in the computation of the income tax in accordance with
     the provisions of the NIRC of 1997, as amended; and
  e. On Museums, Theaters, and Cultural Events — A five percent (5%)
     discount shall be granted to underprivileged students on entrance fees to
     museums, theaters, and cultural events sanctioned by the National
     Commission on Culture and Arts anywhere in the country: Provided, that
     private museums, theaters, and producers of cultural events may claim
     the cost as an allowable tax deduction from the gross income in the
     computation of the income tax in accordance with the provisions of the
     NIRC of 1997, as amended.

      SECTION 5. Limitations. — The Department of Education (DepEd),
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) may identify grounds for
disqualification of students in availing of the aforementioned discounts, in
accordance with the implementing rules and regulations of this Act: Provided,
that these grounds will not defeat the purpose of liberally granting such
discounts to underprivileged students.

      SECTION 6. Role of the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD). — Qualified primary and secondary students and tertiary or college
beneficiaries, shall be determined by DSWD. This agency shall have the following
responsibilities:

         a. Screen applicants, receive documentary proof, and identify who is
            the eligible underprivileged primary, secondary, tertiary, and
            technical-vocational students based on the provisions of this Act;
         b. Closely coordinate with the NEDA in determining the income
            brackets of Filipino families for the purpose of identifying
            underprivileged primary, secondary, tertiary, and technical-
            vocational students;
         c. Issue identification cards which shall be valid anywhere in the
            country as proof of the eligibility of the underprivileged primary,
            secondary, tertiary, and technical-vocational students to avail
            themselves of the discounts;
         d. Maintain and regularly update, on a quarterly basis, the list of
            underprivileged primary, secondary, tertiary, and technical-
            vocational students who avail themselves of the discounts;
         e. Provide information to the affected establishments under Section 4
            of this Act for any questions regarding the eligibility of any
            underprivileged primary, secondary, tertiary, and technical-
            vocational students claiming the discounts;
         f. Monitor the extent of implementation of this Act and coordinate with
            the concerned agencies regarding the imposition of penalties on
            violators of this Act; and

      SECTION 7. Tax Incentives. — Discounts granted by establishments to
underprivileged students under this Act shall be treated as an allowable
deduction from the gross income in the computation of the income tax, in
accordance with the provisions of NIRC of 1997, as amended.

        SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DSWD, in
coordination with the DepEd, TESDA, CHED, NEDA, the Department of Health
(DOH), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Department of Finance
(DOF), and such other agencies concerned with the establishments which may
be affected by this Act, shall issue the implementing rules and regulations to
carry out the objectives of this Act within thirty (30) days after this law takes
effect.

      SECTION 9. Penalties. — The first violation of any provisions of this Act
shall cause the suspension of the license to operate or the business permit of
the establishment or person concerned for not less than one (1) week but not
more than four (4) weeks and a fine of not less than Twenty Thousand Pesos
(P20,000.00) but not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00).

      The second violation of any provisions of this Act shall cause the
suspension of the license to operate or the business permit of the establishment
or the person concerned for not less than four (4) weeks and a fine of not less
than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not exceeding Two Hundred Fifty
Thousand Pesos (P250,000.00): Provided, however, that the procedure of notice
and hearing shall have been complied with prior to the imposition of the said
penalties.

       If the offender is a corporation, organization, or any similar entity, the
officers thereof shall suffer the same penalty imposed on any person found
violating this Act.

       SECTION 10. Enforcement. — Pursuant hereto, the DSWD, DepEd, DTI,
DOH, and local government units (LGUs), in coordination with the CHED and
TESDA shall have the mandate to implement the provision of this Act. After filing
an appropriate complaint, and after due notice and hearing, the proper
authorities may also cause the cancellation or revocation of the business permit,
permit to operate, franchise, and other similar privileges granted to any business
entity that fails to abide by the provisions of this Act.

       SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
