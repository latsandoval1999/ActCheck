                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Blood is an essential component of the health care system. Every day, blood and
blood products may be needed in the treatment of patients suffering from severe trauma,
complications of childbirth, major surgery, cancer, and other serious illnesses, as well as
in emergency and disaster situations. The availability of a safe and adequate blood supply
is therefore an essential public health concern.

       A sustainable blood services system cannot depend solely on periodic blood
donation campaigns or on the capacity of individual hospitals and institutions. It requires
a coordinated national system capable of supporting blood collection and distribution
while also developing the current local capacity to respond to the needs of their respective
communities.

        This bill seeks to establish the statutory mandate of the Philippine Blood Center
(PBC), Subnational, and Regional Blood Centers in order to rationalize and improve the
provision of an adequate, safe, and accessible supply of blood and blood components,
and to set the standards for scientific and professional operations of blood service
facilities nationwide.

       Republic Act (RA) No. 7719, otherwise known as the "National Blood Services Act
of 1994," established the framework for a national policy on voluntary blood donation.
However, the rising incidence of medical emergencies, trauma cases, maternal mortality
challenges, and complex hematologic conditions necessitates a re-engineered legal
framework. There is an urgent need to institutionalize the Philippine Blood Center as the
apex national reference and coordinating hub, strengthen subnational and regional blood

centers, actively engage private corporations, and empower Local Government Units
(LGUs) to establish and maintain local blood service facilities.

      Section 4(d) of RA No. 7719 directs that blood centers be strategically established
nationwide within a National Blood Transfusion Service Network spearheaded by the
Department of Health (DOH). This Act operationalizes that statutory mandate through a
multi-pronged approach anchored on existing health and tax laws:

   1) Empowerment of Local Government Units (LGUs): Pursuant to the devolution
      of health services under Section 17 of RA No. 7160 or the Local Government Code
      of 1991 and the revenue-capacity classifications under RA No. 11964 otherwise
      known as the Automatic Income Classification of Local Government Units Act,
      this bill mandates Provinces, Cities, and Municipalities to establish local blood
      service facilities. It also operationalizes Inter-LGU cooperation pursuant to Section
      33 of RA No. 7160 and authorizes local blood funding through the Special Health
      Fund under Sections 19–21 of RA No. 11223 or the Universal Health Care Act.

      Not all local government units are equally situated. The bill therefore takes a
graduated approach based on the political classification of LGUs the Local Government
Code of 1991 and their income classification under RA No. 11964:

       a) Highly urbanized cities, independent component cities, and first- to third-
class provinces shall establish and maintain at least one (1) DOH-licensed Hospital-Based
Blood Center or Blood Bank capable of collection, component processing, and cross-
matching blood center or hospital-based blood banks that can serve as referral points for
nearby LGUs;

       b) Component cities, fourth- and fifth-class provinces, and first- to third-class
municipalities shall establish and maintain a Blood Collection Unit (BCU) or Blood
Station (BS) attached to their city or municipal health offices or local district hospitals;
and

       c) Fourth- and fifth-class municipalities shall establish and maintain Blood
Stations (BS) or partner with adjacent LGUs to form an Inter-LGU Blood Service Network.
They may also enter into cooperative or clustering arrangements with their province or
neighboring LGUs where an independent facility is not practicable.

       So that no local government unit is discouraged from taking part because of
limited resources, the DOH shall give priority technical assistance, capacity-building,
and, subject to available funds, financial support to lower-income LGUs.

   2) Corporate and Private Sector Engagement: Consistent with the non-profit and
      non-remunerated voluntary donation principles of RA No. 7719 and corporate

       social responsibility (CSR) frameworks under Republic Act No. 11232 (Revised
       Corporation Code of the Philippines), this bill incentivizes corporate participation
       through tax mechanisms. Contributions of cash, mobile vans, or testing equipment
       are granted 100% income tax deductions pursuant to Section 34(H) of the National
       Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as amended, and exempted from Donor's
       Tax under Section 101(A)(2) of the NIRC. Furthermore, duty-free importation of
       specialized medical equipment is provided pursuant to Section 800 of Republic
       Act No. 10863 (Customs Modernization and Tariff Act).

       The measures incorporated in this bill recognize the important role of local
government units in ensuring access to health services. Local government units are in a
good position to identify the blood requirements of their communities and to mobilize
local resources. Accordingly, the bill seeks to empower provinces, cities, and
municipalities to establish and maintain appropriate local blood service facilities, subject
to the standards and regulations prescribed by the Department of Health. Moreover, it
allows cooperation among local government units where the establishment of an
independent facility may not be practicable.

       The private sector also has an important role in strengthening the country’s blood
supply through organized workplace and community-based blood donation programs.
Rather than being an individual effort, the measures under this bill establish mechanisms
through which private corporations may participate in and support the national blood
services program while preserving the principle that blood donation must remain
voluntary and non-remunerated. It further provides appropriate incentives and
recognition mechanisms to encourage sustained private-sector participation.

      By aligning corporate CSR incentives and local government health mandates with
national reference centers, this bill guarantees a sustainable, equitable, and safe blood
supply for all Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing considerations, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE PHILIPPINE BLOOD CENTER (PBC),
 SUBNATIONAL, AND REGIONAL BLOOD CENTERS AND APPROPRIATING
                       FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                                  ARTICLE I
                           INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS

              SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Institutionalized
       Philippine Blood Center and Local Blood Services Act.”

              SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
       to protect and promote the right to health of the people by ensuring the
       availability, accessibility, adequacy, safety, and quality of blood and blood
       components for all persons who require transfusion.

              Towards this end, the State shall strengthen and institutionalize the
       Philippine Blood Center as the national coordinating institution for the country’s
       blood services program and provide it with the necessary mandate, resources, and
       mechanisms to effectively coordinate the collection, processing, storage,
       distribution, and utilization of safe and quality blood and blood components
       nationwide.

              The State shall likewise strengthen the participation of local government
       units in the national blood services program by empowering them to establish and
       maintain appropriate local blood service facilities, subject to national standards


                                                                                            4
and regulations, in every province and city nationwide. It shall also promote
voluntary and non-remunerated blood donation and encourage the participation
of the private sector through voluntary blood donation and other forms of support
for the national blood services program.

       The State shall ensure that all blood services, including the collection,
processing, testing, storage, distribution, and utilization of blood and blood
components, are undertaken in accordance with established standards of safety,
quality, and public health.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

      (a) Blood and Blood Components refer to human whole blood and the
separated components thereof intended for transfusion or other medically
recognized purposes (i.e., platelets, red blood cells, and plasma) ;

        (b) Blood Service Facility refers to a facility authorized by the Department of
Health to perform one or more blood service functions such as blood collection,
processing, testing, storage, and distribution, in accordance with applicable laws,
rules, regulations, and standards;

       (c) Local Blood Service Facility refers to a blood service facility established,
operated, or maintained by a local government unit, either independently or in
cooperation with other local government units or qualified health institutions,
subject to the standards and requirements prescribed by the Department of Health;

      (d) Philippine Blood Center or PBC refers to the existing Philippine Blood
Center under the Department of Health, which is hereby institutionalized as the
national coordinating institution for the country’s blood services program;

        (e) National Blood Services Program refers to the national program for the
provision, coordination, and regulation of blood services, including the voluntary
donation, collection, processing, testing, storage, distribution, and appropriate
utilization of blood and blood components;

      (f) Voluntary and Non-Remunerated Blood Donation refers to the donation of
blood willfully and freely given by an individual without receiving monetary
payment or other material compensation in exchange for such donation.

                                      ARTICLE II
                           THE PHILIPPINE BLOOD CENTER

       SEC. 4. Institutionalization of the Philippine Blood Center. - The existing
Philippine Blood Center (PBC) in Quezon City, Metro Manila, is hereby institutionalized
as the national blood center of the Philippines, to be called the Philippine Blood Center
(PBC) under the direct control and supervision of the Department of Health (DOH) to
serve as the national blood center, subnational blood center for Luzon, and regional blood
center for the National Capital Region.

       SEC. 5. Powers and Functions. – In accordance with Republic Act No. 7719,
otherwise known as the “National Blood Services Act of 1994,” and other applicable laws,
the Philippine Blood Center (PBC), as the national coordinating institution and reference
blood service facility of the Philippines, shall have the following powers and functions:

      (a) Centrally recruit, motivate, and screen voluntary blood donors;

      (b) Collect, test for transfusion-transmissible infections, process, store, issue, and
          transport blood and blood components for transfusion;

      (c) Perform specialized immunohematologic testing and other reference
         laboratory services;

      (d) Provide information, education, counseling, and public awareness programs
          on blood donation, transfusion safety, and transfusion-transmissible
          infections;

      (e) Establish and maintain a secure database of blood donors in accordance with
          Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,”
          and other applicable laws and regulations;

      (f) Conduct quality assurance, monitoring, and surveillance of blood service
          facilities in both the public and private sectors;

      (g) Establish and maintain a national hemovigilance system for the monitoring and
          prevention of adverse events associated with blood transfusion;

      (h) Conduct and support training, continuing education, research, and
         development in blood banking, transfusion medicine, and related technologies
         and fields as a basis for national policy and program development;

      (i) Strengthen and provide technical assistance and capacity-building to regional
          blood centers and other blood service facilities;

(j) Manufacture, procure, and provide blood-typing sera, red cell reagent panels,
    and other necessary laboratory reagents, subject to applicable standards and
    regulations;

(k) Provide or facilitate specialized training in blood banking and transfusion
    medicine for clinical pathologists and other qualified health professionals;

(l) Prepare and provide special plasma derivatives, subject to applicable standards,
     regulations, and licensing requirements;

(m) Establish, maintain, and coordinate the national blood services network in
   accordance with the framework established under Republic Act No. 7719;

(n) Develop and administer a resource stratification and allocation framework for
    national blood services in coordination with regional blood centers, local
    government units, and other blood service facilities;

(o) Facilitate the coordination and equitable distribution of blood and blood
    components among blood service facilities, particularly during emergencies,
    disasters, and periods of critical shortage;

(p) As an integral part of the Department of Health, the PBC shall carry out its
    mandate and functions under this Act, subject to existing laws, rules, and
    regulations; and

(q) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the purposes of
    this Act or as may be assigned by the Secretary of Health, consistent with law.

                             ARTICLE III
               SUBNATIONAL AND REGIONAL BLOOD CENTERS

       SEC. 6. Establishment of Subnational and Regional Blood Centers. - There shall
be established Subnational Blood Centers in Visayas and Mindanao, and Regional Blood
Centers in every region of the Philippines under the direct control and supervision of the
DOH. Provided, that in Visayas and Mindanao, the establishment of additional
subnational or regional blood centers may be dispensed with where existing blood
centers are determined by the DOH to be qualified and capable of performing the
corresponding functions, subject to the applicable standards and requirements.

       SEC. 7. Powers and Responsibilities. - Subnational and Regional Blood Centers
shall have the following powers and responsibilities:

   a) Perform blood collection;
   b) Test for Transfusion Transmissible Infections, component preparation, storage,
      and distribution within their respective jurisdictions as a blood service facility;
   c) Serve as regional coordinating hubs for Local Blood Service Networks.


                               ARTICLE IV
              EMPOWERMENT OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS (LGUs)

      SEC. 8. Mandate on LGUs to Establish Blood Service Facilities. - Local
Government Units (LGUs) are hereby mandated to establish, staff, and operate local
blood service facilities within their health systems, as follows:

          (a) Provinces and Highly Urbanized Cities (HUCs) - shall establish and maintain
              at least one (1) DOH-licensed Hospital-Based Blood Center or Blood Bank
              capable of collection, component processing, and cross-matching;
          (b) Component Cities and 1st to 3rd Class Municipalities - shall establish and
              maintain a Blood Collection Unit (BCU) or Blood Station (BS) attached to
              their city or municipal health offices or local district hospitals;
          (c) 4th to 6th Class Municipalities - shall establish Blood Stations (BS) or partner
              with adjacent LGUs to form an Inter-LGU Blood Service Network.

       Income classes shall refer to the classifications effective under Republic Act No.
11964. Each facility shall perform only the functions covered by its DOH authorization.

      SEC. 9. Inter-LGU Blood Service Networks (BSNs). - LGUs are authorized and
encouraged to form inter-LGU cooperative agreements to jointly build, equip, and




                                                                                            8
maintain shared blood service facilities, cold-chain transport networks, and donor
databases.

       SEC. 10. Local Voluntary Donor Registry. - Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) and
City or Municipal Health Offices shall establish and update a voluntary donor database
(“Walking Blood Donor Registry”) to facilitate emergency donor recruitment during local
blood shortages or disasters. Registry enrollment shall not establish fitness to donate;
each donation remains subject to required consent, screening, testing, and applicable
DOH emergency conditions under Republic Act No. 7719. Processing and disclosure of
donor information shall comply with existing and applicable laws.

       SEC. 11. Local Funding for Blood Services. - LGUs shall allocate a dedicated line
item in their annual budget, chargeable against their Special Health Fund under Republic
Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) or local health development funds specifically
for blood cold-chain logistics, donor recruitment, testing kits, and staff training.

                              ARTICLE V
              PRIVATE SECTOR AND CORPORATE ENGAGEMENT

   SEC. 12. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) in Voluntary Blood Donation. -
Private corporations and commercial establishments are encouraged to incorporate
voluntary blood donation drives into their annual CSR programs. Corporations
employing more than fifty (50) personnel are encouraged to organize at least one (1)
annual workplace voluntary blood drive in partnership with the PBC, DOH Regional
Blood Centers, or local government blood collection units. Individual participation by
employees shall remain strictly voluntary and non-remunerated.

   SEC. 13. Tax Incentives and Deductions for Corporate Donors. -

          (a) Income Tax Deduction - All cash or material donations, grants, or
              equipment (including mobile blood collection vans, cold-chain equipment,
              and testing reagents) contributed by private corporations to the PBC,
              Subnational/Regional Blood Centers, or LGU-operated blood facilities may
              be deducted in computing the donor corporation’s taxable income, subject
              to the eligibility, limits, valuation, and substantiation requirements of
              Section 34(H) of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as
              amended.
          (b) Exemption from Donor’s Tax - Direct donations of equipment, real estate,
              or capital funds to the PBC or accredited government blood facilities shall
              be exempt from Donor’s Tax under Section 101 of the NIRC.

          (c) Deductibility of Corporate Blood Drive Expenses - Corporations hosting
              accredited voluntary blood donation drives may claim administrative and
              logistical expenses incurred for such drives as deductible ordinary and
              necessary business expenses, subject to Section 34(A)(1) of the NIRC and
              applicable substantiation requirements, without duplicate deduction of the
              same expense.

       SEC. 14. Duty-Free Importation of Medical Equipment. - Notwithstanding the
applicable provisions of the NIRC, as amended, the importation of specialized blood
processing, apheresis, cold-chain storage, and blood-testing equipment by or consigned
to the Republic through the DOH for the PBC, Subnational or Regional Blood Centers, or
to an LGU for its authorized blood centers, exclusively for their blood services, shall be
exempt from customs duties and import value-added tax upon certification by the DOH
and the Department of Finance, subject to verification by the Bureau of Customs and the
Bureau of Internal Revenue. Other exemptions independently available under law,
including Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7719, shall remain governed by their own terms.

      SEC. 15. Recognition and Corporate Seals. - The DOH, in coordination with the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall establish the “National Humanitarian
Corporate Award” to recognize corporations demonstrating outstanding support for the
National Voluntary Blood Services Program.

                              ARTICLE VI
        ORGANIZATION, ADMINISTRATION, AND FINANCIAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 16. Leadership and Staffing. – The PBC, Subnational, and Regional Blood
Centers shall be headed by Directors who are board-certified Clinical Pathologists in
good standing with the Philippine Society of Pathologists, Inc., appointed by the
Secretary of Health. Staffing structures shall be crafted in coordination with the Civil
Service Commission (CSC) and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).

        SEC. 17. Non-Profit Operation and Service Fees. – All blood centers shall operate
on a non-profit basis. Blood service facilities may collect standard processing and service
fees strictly limited to recovering operational costs (testing kits, reagents, blood bags, and
storage), as prescribed and capped by the DOH. Blood shall never be sold as a commercial
commodity.

     SEC. 18. Program for Indigents. – Indigent patients needing blood and blood
components shall be fully exempt from blood processing fees upon assessment by
Medical Social Services in government hospitals, in accordance with DOH guidelines.

       SEC. 19. Special Fund for Blood Service Fees. – Notwithstanding Section 65 of
Presidential Decree No. 1445, income from blood service fees collected by the PBC and
Subnational or Regional Blood Centers shall be retained in a special account in the
General Fund in the National Treasury. Fees collected by LGU blood facilities shall be
recorded separately in the appropriate local accounts under Republic Act No. 7160 and,
where applicable, the Special Health Fund under RA No. 11223. Expenditure shall require
the applicable national or local appropriation and comply with budget, Treasury,
accounting, and COA audit rules, solely for operational maintenance, procurement of
reagents, equipment upgrades, and staff capacity development; retention alone shall not
constitute spending authority.

      SEC. 20. Annual Report. – The PBC, Subnational, and Regional Blood Centers shall
submit an annual report to the Department of Health.

       SEC. 21. Appropriations. – The amount of Eight Hundred Million Pesos (Php
800,000,000.00) is hereby authorized as initial funding for the institutionalization of the
PBC, Subnational, and Regional Blood Centers, to be charged against the current year’s
budget of the DOH. Thereafter, the required budget shall be included as a distinct line
item in the National Expenditure Program (NEP) and the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA).

                                       ARTICLE VII
                                    FINAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the DILG, DOF, DTI, DBM,
and the Philippine Red Cross, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 23. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

      SEC. 24. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act, including relevant provisions of Republic Act No. 7719, are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 25. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
