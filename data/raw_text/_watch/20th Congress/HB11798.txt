EXPLANATORY NOTE

In recent years, the transformation of the Philippine labor market has given rise to a
growing number of Filipinos earning a living through freelance work, online selling, delivery
services, and other forms of self-employment. Unlike employees whose contributions are
ordinarily deducted and remitted through their employers, these workers must personally keep
track of how much to pay, when payments are due, and whether their contributions have been
properly recorded. For a freelance graphic designer, an online seller, a delivery rider, or a self-
employed professional, this often means navigating different government systems and completing
separate transactions simply to maintain their social protection coverage.

The Social Security System (SSS), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth),
and Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund) already provide various payment channels
and online services. However, these systems generally operate separately, requiring workers to
access different platforms, follow different procedures, and monitor their contributions across
multiple accounts. For those balancing irregular income, multiple clients, or the daily demands of
running a small business, these additional steps can become an unnecessary burden. Missed or
delayed contributions may also affect their access to benefits, subject to the applicable rules of
each agency.

Social protection should not be more difficult to maintain simply because a Filipino earns
a living outside a traditional employer-employee arrangement. This measure therefore seeks to
establish an Integrated Social Protection Contribution Service within the government's existing
eGovPH platform, or its successor, through which freelancers and self-employed workers may
conveniently view their applicable contributions, obtain payment references, make consolidated
payments, and monitor their payment status across SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG Fund.

Consistent with Republic Act No. 12254, otherwise known as the E-Governance Act, the
proposal seeks to bring together existing government contribution services through a common
access point, rather than create another application, payment system, or government office. While
the E-Governance Act already provides for the integration of government frontline services and
digital payment systems, this measure specifically addresses the payment and monitoring of social
protection contributions by workers who manage their own remittances.

In particular, it seeks to enable these workers to pay contributions to multiple agencies
through a single transaction, rather than requiring separate payment transactions for each agency.
The measure builds on the existing digital infrastructure of the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) and the participating social protection agencies, while
preserving their respective responsibilities over membership, contribution assessment, collection,
and official records.

Importantly, this bill does not impose new contributions, increase existing contribution
rates, or change the benefits and eligibility requirements established under current social protection

laws. These shall remain governed by Republic Act No. 11199, or the Social Security Act of 2018;
Republic Act No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care Act; Republic Act No. 9679, or the Home

concerns the manner by which workers access, pay, and monitor their existing contributions, not
the amount they are legally required to contribute.

The measure also recognizes that not all workers have reliable internet access or are
comfortable using digital platforms. Participation in the integrated service shall therefore be
voluntary, and existing payment channels and assistance through government service offices shall
remain available. Appropriate safeguards for data privacy, payment security, transaction
verification, and correction of payment errors shall likewise be established to protect workers using
the service. Workers must also be able to determine whether their payments have been successfully
received and recorded, and know where to seek assistance when a payment fails or is not properly
reflected in their contribution records.

For Filipino workers who already shoulder the responsibility of finding clients, earning
their income, and sustaining their livelihoods, keeping their social protection contributions up to
date should not require navigating several separate government systems. They should be able to
spend less time managing payment transactions and more time working, earning, and caring for
their families. By making these transactions simpler and more accessible, this measure seeks to
help freelancers and self-employed workers maintain their contributions and better protect
themselves and their families against illness, loss of income, and other uncertainties.

AN ACT
FACILITATING THE PAYMENT AND MONITORING OF SOCIAL PROTECTION
CONTRIBUTIONS OF FREELANCERS AND SELF-EMPLOYED WORKERS
THROUGH AN INTEGRATED SOCIAL PROTECTION CONTRIBUTION SERVICE
WITHIN THE GOVERNMENT'S EXISTING E-GOVERNANCE INFRASTRUCTURE,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Unified Social Protection
Contributions for Freelancers and Self-Employed Workers Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is the policy of the State to promote accessible,
efficient, and continuous social protection and to reduce unnecessary administrative burdens in
government transactions. Consistent with Republic Act No. 12254, otherwise known as the “E-
Governance Act,” the State shall promote the integration and interoperability of government
services so that freelancers and self-employed workers may conveniently determine, pay, and
monitor their applicable social protection contributions through a unified access mechanism,
without altering the substantive rights and obligations established under existing laws.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “Freelancer” refers to a natural person who independently performs work, renders services,
or delivers an agreed output for one or more clients without an employer-employee
relationship;

(b) "Self-employed worker" refers to a natural person who derives income from the exercise
of a trade, business, profession, occupation, calling, or other independent economic activity
and is covered or eligible for coverage under the applicable individual membership
classification of a participating agency;

(c) “Participating agencies” refer to the Social Security System (SSS), Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG
Fund); and

(d) “Integrated Social Protection Contribution Service” or “Service” refers to the integrated
contribution facility within the existing government e-governance infrastructure, including
the eGovPH Application or its successor platform, through which freelancers and self-
employed workers may access the services provided under this Act.

SECTION 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to freelancers and self-employed workers
who are covered or eligible for coverage under one or more of the participating agencies. Their
membership classification, contribution obligations, and eligibility to make particular

SECTION 5. Integrated Social Protection Contribution Service. — The Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the participating
agencies, shall make available an Integrated Social Protection Contribution Service within the
existing eGovPH platform or its successor platform. The Service shall utilize existing government
digital infrastructure and shall not constitute a separate social insurance program, fund, mobile
application, or government entity.

Subject to applicable laws and appropriate identity verification, the Service shall enable a
covered user, through a single access point, to:

(a) Link or verify existing membership information with the participating agencies;

(b) View the applicable contribution amount or amounts determined in accordance with the
rules of each participating agency;

(c) Generate or obtain the payment references or equivalent information required by the
participating agencies;

(d) Pay contributions to two (2) or more participating agencies through a single payment
transaction, with the corresponding amounts allocated to the respective agencies;

(e) Receive confirmation of payment and the posting status of contributions with each
participating agency; and

(f) View a consolidated summary of contributions paid through the Service.

The Service shall clearly indicate the status of each payment, including whether the amount
has been successfully transmitted and officially posted by the concerned participating agency.

SECTION 6. Computation of Contributions. — The Social Security System (SSS),
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-
IBIG Fund) shall each determine the applicable contribution of a freelancer or self-employed
worker in accordance with their respective governing laws, rules and regulations, and prevailing
contribution schedules. The Integrated Social Protection Contribution Service shall consolidate
and present through the eGovPH platform the contribution amounts determined by the
participating agencies.

The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall provide
the necessary technical infrastructure and interoperability to enable the secure exchange and
presentation of such information. Nothing in this Act shall authorize the DICT or the Service to
determine or modify contribution rates, income classifications, membership status, or other
substantive obligations prescribed by law.

Nothing in this Act shall authorize the DICT or the Service to determine or modify
contribution rates, membership classifications, or other substantive obligations prescribed by law.

SECTION 7. Consolidated Payment and Contribution Records. — The Service shall
facilitate the allocation and transmission of payments to the participating agencies in accordance
with applicable collection, accounting, and remittance rules. Each participating agency shall
remain responsible for the official validation, posting, and maintenance of its members’
contribution records.

The Service shall provide members with a transaction reference, an itemized summary of
payments, and information on the posting status of their contributions. Such information shall not
replace the official records maintained by the participating agencies. Mechanisms for reporting
and resolving failed, duplicate, incorrectly allocated, or unposted payments shall be established in
accordance with the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

SECTION 8. Use of Existing Systems and Non-Duplication. — The DICT and the
participating agencies shall utilize and integrate existing government platforms, payment systems,

SECTION 9. Preservation of Existing Contribution and Remittance Arrangements. —
Use of the Service shall be voluntary. Its availability shall not prevent members from using existing
over-the-counter, online, authorized collecting agents, or other lawful payment channels of the
participating agencies.

The participating agencies shall continue to provide reasonable assistance through their
existing service offices and authorized channels for members unable to access the Service.

SECTION 10. Data Privacy and Security. — The DICT and the participating agencies shall
ensure that the collection, processing, and exchange of personal and contribution information
under this Act comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act
of 2012," Republic Act No. 12254, and other applicable data protection and cybersecurity laws,
rules, and regulations.

SECTION 11. Implementing Agencies. — The DICT shall be responsible for the technical
integration and operation of the Service within the existing government e-governance
infrastructure. The participating agencies shall retain authority over their respective membership,
contribution, collection, posting, and benefit rules and shall provide the necessary information and
technical coordination for the implementation of this Act.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DICT, SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG Fund shall jointly promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary for its implementation. The rules shall provide for system
interoperability, payment allocation and reconciliation, transaction verification, correction of
payment errors, data privacy, cybersecurity, and other necessary implementation procedures. The
Service shall be operational within one (1) year from the effectivity of the rules and regulations.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the DICT and the participating
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
included in their respective annual appropriations.

SECTION 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
