                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Cell and gene therapies, stem-cell-based and tissue-engineered products,
medicines made through biotechnology (such as recombinant proteins, monoclonal
antibodies, and therapeutic peptides), and new vaccine technologies such as mRNA
and viral-vector vaccines are changing how many diseases are treated and
prevented. Many of these products are made from living cells or genetic material,
and some keep acting in the body for years after a single dose. Regulating them well
requires specialized scientific review, close oversight of how they are made, and
long-term monitoring of the patients who receive them.

        These products raise questions that conventional drug review was not
designed to answer: how to confirm that a living-cell product is what it claims to be,
that it is pure and potent, that every batch is made the same way, and that patients
stay safe over many years. At the same time, the public needs protection from
unproven “stem cell” and similar treatments that are promoted without adequate
evidence. Both concerns call for specialized scientific and regulatory expertise within
the Philippine Food and Drug Administration.

         Under Republic Act No. 3720 (the Foods, Drugs and Devices, and Cosmetics
Act), as amended by Executive Order No. 175 (1987) and Republic Act No. 9711
otherwise known as the “Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Act of 2009,” the
FDA has four (4) product-based Centers and is headed by a Director-General assisted
by two (2) Deputy Directors-General. Veterinary medicines, vaccines, and biologicals
all fall under the Center for Drug Regulation and Research (CDRR).

       Given the rapid growth and scientific complexity of these products, this bill
creates a fifth Center, the Center for Life Sciences Regulation and Research (CLSRR),
and the position of Deputy Director-General for Life Sciences. Conventional
vaccines, as well as plasma-derived and other traditional biologicals, will stay with
the CDRR unless the FDA assigns them to the new Center under published criteria.
Veterinary medicines will remain with the CDRR.

       This measure gives the FDA clear legal authority for risk-proportionate
review of these products, reliance on the work of trusted foreign regulators,
independent scientific advice, long-term safety monitoring, specialist staffing, and
safeguards against conflicts of interest. It provides for a phased transition so that
existing registrations and pending applications are not disrupted. It does not expand
FDA jurisdiction over academic research laboratories, and it does not change the
existing oversight of biosafety, research ethics, health facilities, or professional
practice.

         In view of the vital role of regulatory modernization in safeguarding public
health and fostering innovation in the life sciences, the immediate approval of this
bill is respectfully requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “FDA Life Sciences
and Biotechnology Regulatory Modernization Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — Consistent with Section 15, Article II, and Section
12, Article XIII of the Constitution, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the Filipino people by maintaining an
effective, science-based, and risk-proportionate regulatory system for life science
health products, with decisions based on evidence and free from undue influence.
The State shall strengthen the regulatory capacity of the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) to ensure the safety, efficacy, purity, and quality of these
products, to protect the public from unproven and unsafe products, and to facilitate
transparent, predictable, and timely patient access to safe and effective innovations.

Republic Act No. 3720 that this Act amends or adds, the following terms shall mean:

     SEC. 9. Scienti c Advisory Committees. — The FDA may convene scienti c
     advisory committees of independent experts in elds such as biotechnology, clinical
     medicine, immunology, genetics, biostatistics, and bioethics to advise it on complex
     questions involving life science health products. These committees may also include
     patient representatives. The advice of these committees shall not bind the FDA. Each
     member shall disclose any nancial or professional interest relevant to the matters
     under review, sign a con dentiality undertaking, and inhibit from any deliberation
     in which the member has a con ict of interest. The FDA shall publish the
     membership of each committee and a summary of the declared interests of its
     members, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data
     Privacy Act of 2012.”

     SEC. 10. Risk-Proportionate Review and Regulatory Reliance. — The FDA may
     adopt review pathways for life science health products that match the depth of
     review to the risk of the product and the strength of the available evidence,
     including priority review for products intended for serious conditions that have no
     satisfactory treatment. The FDA may attach conditions to an authorization, such as
     the completion of ongoing studies, long-term follow-up of patients, or use only in
     quali ed health facilities licensed by the DOH.

             In reaching its decisions, the FDA may apply regulatory reliance and may
     enter into cooperation and reliance arrangements with reference regulatory
     authorities. Reliance shall not relieve the FDA of responsibility for its decisions. No
     pathway under this Section shall dispense with adequate evidence of the quality,
     safety, and ef cacy of a product for its intended use. The FDA shall publish the
     requirements and target timelines for each pathway.

     SEC. 11. Biovigilance and Enforcement. — The FDA may require holders of
     authorizations for life science health products to:

     (a) Maintain systems that trace each product, and, where applicable the donor cells
         or tissues used to make it, from source to patient;

     (b) Report adverse events to the FDA within the periods it prescribes; and

     (c) Conduct long-term follow-up of patients and maintain patient registries, where
         the nature of the product requires it.

              Personal and health information collected under this Section shall be
     processed in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173. The prohibited acts, penalties,
     and administrative sanctions under Republic Act No. 3720, as amended, shall apply
     to life science health products.

 SEC. 13. Staf ng and Appropriations. — The FDA shall prepare the organizational
 structure, staf ng pattern, and position titles of the CLSRR and of the support staff
 of the Deputy Director-General for Life Sciences, subject to the approval of the
 Secretary of Health under Section 7 of Republic Act No. 3720, as amended, and to the
 approval by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) of the new positions
 and their compensation. The quali cation standards for the new positions shall be
 submitted to the Civil Service Commission (CSC) for approval. As far as practicable,
 the CLSRR shall rst be staffed by FDA personnel who currently handle the products
 assigned to it. The FDA may engage scienti c and technical specialists, and may use
 the services of other government or accredited testing laboratories, subject to
 applicable civil service, procurement, accounting, and auditing laws and rules.

          SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120)
          days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Health, upon the
          recommendation of the FDA Director-General and after consultation with the DBM,
          the CSC, the DOST, industry, health professional organizations, patient groups, and
          other stakeholders, shall promulgate the IRR of this Act. The FDA shall issue the
          product-classi cation guidelines required under Section 5(g) of Republic Act No.
          3720, as amended by this Act, within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of the IRR.

          SEC. 16. Congressional Oversight and Reporting. — The FDA shall submit to the
          committees on health of the Senate and of the House of Representatives, and publish
          on its website, an annual report on the implementation of this Act. The report shall
          cover, among others, the staf ng of the CLSRR, the number of applications received
          and decided, actual review times compared with target timelines, the use of
          regulatory reliance, biovigilance actions, and the use of funds.

          SEC. 17. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
          unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
          continue in full force and effect.

          are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ed accordingly.
