               MENUS OF FOOD SERVICE ESTABLISHMENTS

                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

Food has always been an integral part of Filipino culture. We take pride in our flavorful
and unique dishes, many of which have since gained international recognition. Each
household has their own version of their favorite Filipino food, from sinigang, adobo,
kare-kare, and many others, which we fondly refer to as “lutong-bahay.”

Through the years, however, more and more Filipinos have made it a habit to dine
out,1 while others frequent fast food chains for cheap and convenient meals. However,
studies report that food away from home is generally less healthy and is associated
with a higher prevalence of obesity.2 While we greatly support the food service industry
for expanding Filipino culture and bringing people together, we also wish to highlight
people’s health and instill health consciousness among Filipinos, as part of the State’s
mandate under the Constitution.3

Based on the 2023 National Nutrition Survey of the Department of Science and
Technology–Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI), obesity remains a
problem in the Philippines. Data shows that 1 in every 10 school-age children and
adolescents are overweight or obese, and 4 in every 10 adults aged 20-59 years old

One way to motivate consumers to make healthier choices is by ensuring that relevant
nutritional information is available for their consideration. A Research Specialist at the
DOST-FNRI has emphasized the need to monitor the nutritional quality of food sold in
stores and other establishments, to help Filipinos adopt healthier lifestyles and prevent
lifestyle-related diseases.6

Studies have shown that consumers tend to underestimate calorie content in food,
which implies that menu items are thought to be healthier than they actually are.7
Therefore, making available the nutritional information on menus may help consumers
make informed choices, and steer their decisions towards the healthier option. In
Canada, a study showed that a nutrition labeling program was associated with more
consumers noticing and using nutrition information during meal selection, compared
to those in other restaurants.8 Previous research has also shown that calorie labeling
in menus has increased the attention to and use of nutritional information.9

Backed by these findings, this measure seeks to require food service establishments
which are part of 10 or more establishments doing business nationally (e.g. chain
restaurants, franchises) to disclose the calorie content information on their menus, and
other nutritional information (e.g. carbohydrates, sugar, protein, saturated fat, sodium)
through brochures or online platforms made available at the point of sale. Our goal is
to present Filipinos with all the right information they need to switch to the healthier
option.

 AN ACT REQUIRING THE DISCLOSURE OF NUTRITIONAL INFORMATION IN
            MENUS OF FOOD SERVICE ESTABLISHMENTS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Healthy Meals Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. The
State recognizes that proper nutrition is essential in the promotion of health and
prevention of diseases. Toward this end, the State shall ensure that consumers are
provided with accurate nutritional information for food items offered and sold in food
service establishments, to enable them to make informed choices and encourage
healthier eating habits.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms are defined as
follows:

   (a) Calorie content information means the total number of calories per standard
       menu item, as that item is usually prepared and offered for sale;

   (b) Covered food service establishment refers to a food service establishment that
       is one of a group of 10 or more food service establishments doing business
       nationally, offering for sale substantially the same menu items, in servings that
       are standardized for portion size and content, that operate under common
       ownership or control, or as franchised outlets of a parent business, or do
       business under the same name. This Act excludes Micro, Small, and Medium



                                           4
       Enterprises (MSMEs). However, it does not preclude MSMEs from complying
       with the standards as may be provided pursuant to this measure;

   (c) Drive-thru means an area where a customer may order from and receive
       standard menu items while occupying a motor vehicle;

   (d) Menu refers to a printed list or pictorial display of a food item or items, and their
       price(s), that are available for sale from a covered food service establishment
       and shall include menus distributed or provided outside of the establishment;

   (e) Menu board shall mean any list or pictorial display of a food item or items and
       their price(s) posted in and visible within or outside of a covered food service
       establishment for the purpose of ordering from a drive-through window;

   (f) Nutritional information includes, but is not limited to, all of the following, per
       menu item:

         (i)   Total number of calories
        (ii)   Total number of grams of carbohydrates
       (iii)   Total number of grams of saturated fat
       (iv)    Total number of grams of protein
        (v)    Total number of grams of sugar
       (vi)    Total number of milligrams of sodium

       The Department of Health (DOH) may require additional nutritional information
       that it may deem necessary for the public to know;

   (g) Reasonable basis means any reasonable means recognized by the DOH in
       determining nutritional and calorie content information for a menu item, as
       usually prepared and offered for sale, including, but not limited to, nutrient
       databases and laboratory analyses; and

   (h) Standard menu item shall mean any individual food item, or combination of food
       items, listed or displayed on a menu board or menu that is offered for sale or
       sold by a covered food service establishment through a menu, menu board, or
       display tag at least 180 days per calendar year.

SEC. 4. Menu Labeling. - Covered food service establishments shall disclose in a
clear and conspicuous manner on their menus and menu boards the calorie content
information, which shall be displayed adjacent to the name of the standard menu item,
to be clearly associated with such item. In the case of display tags, the calorie content
information of the standard menu item identified on the display tag shall be stated
thereon.

Covered food service establishments shall disclose the other nutritional information,
as well as the suggested daily caloric intake of the average Filipino as specified by the


                                             5
Secretary of Health through the implementing rules and regulations to be issued
pursuant to this Act, in a clear and conspicuous manner through a brochure or online
platform made available at the point of sale prior to or during the placement of an
order.

In the case of food and beverages sold at a self-service facility, a covered food service
establishment shall place adjacent to each food offered a sign that lists calories per
displayed food item or per serving.

The disclosure of calorie content information next to a standard menu item that is
intended to serve more than one individual, shall include the number of individuals
intended to be served by the standard menu item and the calorie content information
per individual serving.

The nutritional information and calorie content information required by this section
shall be determined on a reasonable basis. A reasonable basis determination of
nutritional information and calorie content information shall be required only once per
standard menu item, provided that portion size is reasonably consistent and the
covered food service establishment follows a standardized recipe and follows a
consistent method of preparation.

SEC. 5. Disclosure in Drive-thru Food Service Establishments. - A covered food
service establishment that has a drive-thru area and uses a menu board to display or
list standard menu items at the point of sale shall, for purposes of the drive-thru area
only, disclose the nutritional information in a clear and conspicuous manner through a
brochure or online platform made available upon request, and shall conspicuously
display a notice at the point of sale that reads: "NUTRITION INFORMATION IS
AVAILABLE UPON REQUEST".

SEC. 6. Noncompliance with Menu Labeling Requirement - The following shail be
considered non-compliance with menu labeling:

   (1) The sale of any food or beverage item by covered establishments without the
       required calorie content information and availability of nutritional information
       displayed on the menu, menu board, or display tag;

   (2) The sale of any food or beverage item by covered establishments without the
       availability of the required nutritional information for all standard menu items;

   (3) The declaration of calorie content information and nutritional information
       without reasonable basis therefor; and

   (4) The misbranding or erroneous disclosure of calorie content information and
       nutritional information of standard menu items.



                                           6
SEC. 7. Penalties. - The Department, in coordination with other concerned
government agencies, after notice and hearing, shail impose any or all of the following
administrative sanctions in cases of non-compliance:

   (a) Suspension of registration; and
   (b) A fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) for the first violation;
       not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) for the second
       violation; and not more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00) and
       possible revocation of business permit for the third and succeeding violations.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within six (6) months from the date
of effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health and the Food and Drug
Administration, in coordination with other concerned agencies, non-governmental
organizations, private sector and consumer groups involved in nutrition, shall
promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations to implement the
provisions of this Act.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain valid and subsisting.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
