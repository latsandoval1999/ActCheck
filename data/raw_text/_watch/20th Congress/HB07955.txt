                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Article II, Section 13 of the 1987 Constitution declares it a State policy to
recognize the vital role of the youth in nation-building and to promote and protect
their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. It further
mandates the State to encourage their meaningful participation in public and civic
affairs. This constitutional directive requires the State to adopt coherent,
sustainable, and institutional mechanisms that respond effectively to the needs of
Filipino youth.

       Despite existing programs and legislative measures, a significant number of
young Filipinos remain out of school, unemployed or underemployed, and without
completion of tertiary or post-secondary education. According to data from the
Philippine Statistics Authority, approximately four (4) million youth fall within this
vulnerable demographic. The persistence of this condition underscores the need for
a strengthened and integrated national framework for intervention.

       In 2014, the Department of Education, in coordination with the National
Youth Commission and other agencies, initiated the Abot-Alam Program as an inter-
agency convergence mechanism aimed at reducing the number of out-of-school
youth (OSY) through targeted identification, profiling, and needs-based intervention.
However, the program remains administrative in nature and lacks statutory
institutionalization, resulting in fragmented coordination and the absence of
guaranteed long-term funding and accountability mechanisms.

        This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize the Abot-Alam Program as a
convergence and coordination framework that complements, and does not duplicate,
existing statutory mandates, particularly those under Republic Act No. 11510, or the
“Alternative Learning System Act,” and Republic Act No. 8044, or the “Youth in
Nation-Building Act.” The Program shall function as a mechanism for systematic
identification, referral, case management, and inter-agency coordination, while the
delivery of formal and non-formal education services shall remain within the
authority of the Department of Education through the Alternative Learning System
and other existing modalities authorized by law.

       By clarifying its role as an integrative and facilitative framework rather than a
parallel education system, the proposed measure ensures statutory harmony, avoids
duplication of institutional mandates, and strengthens programmatic coherence
across agencies.

                              AN ACT
 INSTITUTIONALIZING THE ABOT-ALAM PROJECT OF THE DEPARTMENT OF
           EDUCATION AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Abot-Alam Act”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State
to be more proactive in addressing the issues that face out-of-school youth (OSY),
recognizing their role in nation-building and in both public and civic affairs. As such,
the State shall undertake the necessary steps to ensure that these OSYs are fully
equipped with the necessary knowledge and skills that they need to become
productive members of society.

       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:
       a) “Out of School Youth (OSY) – refers to individuals aged 15 to 30 years old
          who:
             a. Have not yet finished basic education and are currently not in
                school, whether employed or unemployed;
             b. Have not yet finished higher education or any technical/vocational
                skills training and are currently not in school, whether employed or
                unemployed.

       b) OSYs Mapped – refers to OSYs who have been mapped and registered in
          the Abot-Alam database either through the household targeting done by
          the local DepEd or barangay, or online through the Abot-Alam website.

       c) Alternative Learning System (ALS) – is a parallel learning system to provide
          a viable alternative to the existing formal education instruction. It
          encompasses both the nonformal and informal sources of knowledge and
          skills.

       d) ALS Partner – refers to an individual or group, organization and agency
          (e.g., LGUs, NGOs, private institutions/companies, religious groups, etc.)
          that implements ALS programs in different areas using their own resources
          and/or that provides technical assistance or other resources in support of
          ALS programs at any level.

                                           3
       SECTION 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all OSYs as the primary
beneficiaries.

       SECTION 5. Objective. – This Act seeks to enroll OSYs in programs where they
will acquire their desired knowledge and skills to enable them to become more
productive members of society and to help improve their current economic
conditions. This shall be done through two phases: the data-gathering phase and the
program intervention phase.

       The Data Gathering phase of the program consists of the organization or
convening of the local alliances headed by the LGUs and the Schools Division Office,
the data gathering and community mapping of the barangays and the OSYs therein,
the mapping of programs for OSYs by the National Abot-Alam Council, and the
program matching of the OSYs to their desired programs by the local alliances. The
data gathered in community mapping shall be inputted into a National Abot-Alam
database. Data on children 14 years old and below and adults above 30 years old
who are out-of-school and have not yet finished basic education may be included in
the mapping but will be enlisted in the existing ALS data collection processes

        The Program Intervention phase shall consist of the enrollment or referral of
OSYs Mapped to their desired programs, capacity-building for newly hired/assigned
teachers for the implementation of the program, and learner assessment and
certification.

       All formal and non-formal education delivery, learner assessment,
accreditation, and certification under this Act shall be undertaken in coordination
with and subject to the authority of the Department of Education pursuant to
Republic Act No. 11510 and related laws. The Program established herein shall
function primarily as a convergence, referral, and coordination framework and shall
not create a parallel education system.

       SECTION 6. The National Abot-Alam Council. – There shall be created a
National Abot-Alam Council, hereon referred to in this Act as the National Council,
to oversee the implementation and realization of the directives as set forth in this
Act. The council shall be composed of the following:

      a) Secretary of Education

      b) Chairperson of the National Youth Commission

      c) Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education

      d) Director-General of the Technical Education and Skills Development
         Authority

      e) Secretary of the Labor and Employment

      f)   Secretary of Social Welfare and Development

      g) Convenor of the National Anti-Poverty Commission


                                         4
      h) Chairperson of the Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor.

      The National Council shall be co-chaired by the Secretary of Education and
the Chairperson of the National Youth Commission. The Department Secretaries and
other members may not designate alternate representatives other than their
respective undersecretaries or the equivalent of such to the meetings of the National
Council.

     SECTION 7. Functions of the National Abot-Alam Council. – The National
Council shall have the following functions:

      a) Develop goals and key performance indicators in the implementation of
         this Act;

      b) Develop strategies to ensure smooth and effective implementation of this
         Act’s objectives;

      c) Map existing programs and services that are offered by various
         organizations or agencies at each locality and provide such to the
         appropriate Multi-Sectoral Local Alliance. Such menu of programs must
         be furnished to the Multi-Sectoral Local Alliances within sixty (60) days
         from the constitution of the National Council;

      d) Monitor and assess the effectiveness of the program as laid out by this Act;

      e) Administer the appropriations as established in this Act;

      f)   Ensure equitable prioritization and selection of recipients of the program;

      g) Submit reports and recommendations to Congress for needed adjustments
         in the existing legal framework to better carry out the policy of the State as
         declared in this Act.

       SECTION 8. Secretariat to the National Abot-Alam Council. - A Secretariat to
the National Council shall be established within the National Youth Commission, to
be headed by an Executive Director who shall be appointed by the President and
shall have the rank of Undersecretary. The Executive Director shall have at least
seven (7) years of experience and practice in the fields of education, social work, or
community development to qualify for appointment. The Executive Director shall be
nominated by the National Council.

       The Secretariat of the National Council is hereby established whose staffing
structure and complement shall be submitted to the Department of Budget and
Management for funding. Pending the creation of the appropriate plantilla positions,
the National Council shall constitute an interim Secretariat within one (1) month
from the approval of this Act, through the temporary detail of personnel from its
member agencies.

      The Secretariat shall have the following functions:

      a) Provide secretariat support to the National Council, including the
         preparation of draft strategies, priorities, and other outputs to be adopted;



                                           5
      b) Consult with experts who can offer vital insights on policymaking and
         priority setting;

      c) Coordinate with various agencies of the government, the private sector,
         and other sector stakeholders to produce a comprehensive menu of
         programs and interventions available for OSYs;

      d) Perform other functions as may be directed by the National Council.

      SECTION 9. Multi-Sectoral Local Alliances. – There shall be Local Multi-
Sectoral Alliances created at the provincial and city/municipal levels headed by the
Schools Division Office (SDO) and the LGU. It shall be further composed of both
government and non-governmental agencies/organizations and other stakeholders
as would be determined by the SDO and the LGU. The commitment of relevant
private organizations and stakeholders shall be formalized through a Memorandum
of Agreement or Understanding among them.

       The LGUs shall, in coordination with the SDOs and other relevant
stakeholders, conduct the data gathering activities to map the households in all
barangays and record information on OSYs. Such information shall be encoded
appropriately for integration in the National Abot-Alam database. The information
gathered shall, at the minimum, produce a list of OSYs per barangay, with all the
data necessary to assess their needs. Such mapping of OSYs shall be done
continuously until all barangays have been covered and all OSYs have been
registered to the program.

      The Multi-Sectoral Local Alliances shall oversee the matching of OSYs to the
appropriate or desired programs from the menu of available services provided by the
National Council to their locality and ensure the delivery of the matched programs
and services to OSYs.

       SECTION 10. Appropriations. – Such amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation
for the concerned agencies. Thereafter, the funds necessary for this Act’s continuous
and effective implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

       SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The National Council
shall formulate and promulgate the implementing rules and regulations and other
issuances as may be necessary to ensure the effective implementation of the
provisions of this Act within sixty (60) days from the approval thereof. The
implementing rules and regulations issued pursuant to this section shall take effect
(30) days after its publication in any newspaper of general circulation.

      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
proclamations, rules, and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act, are
hereby amended or modified accordingly.

        SECTION 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in
full force and effect.
