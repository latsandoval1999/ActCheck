                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE


       In the landmark case of Jennifer C. Josef v. Evalyn G. Ursua (G.R. No. 267469, February
5, 2025), the Supreme Court clarified that same-sex cohabitants are not governed by
Article 147 of the Family Code because they are not legally capacitated to marry each
other. Nevertheless, their property relations may be governed by Article 148, provided
that actual contribution to the acquisition of property is established. The Court further
held that documentary acknowledgments of contribution constitute competent evidence
of ownership and that a party who has expressly recognized another's proprietary
interest may be estopped from subsequently denying such interest.

        While the decision significantly advances the protection of equitable property
rights, its application remains dependent upon judicial interpretation on a case-by-case
basis. There is currently no express statutory provision recognizing documentary
acknowledgments of contribution or establishing clear evidentiary standards for
cohabiting persons whose unions are not covered by Article 147.

       This measure seeks to codify the doctrine established by the Supreme Court by
expressly recognizing that any person who has actually contributed money, property, or
industry to the acquisition, improvement, preservation, or amortization of property
during cohabitation may establish co-ownership through competent evidence, regardless
of the legal nature of the parties' relationship, consistent with Article 148 of the Family
Code.

      It must be clarified that this measure neither alters the existing laws on marriage
nor creates a new form of civil union. It merely protects legitimate property rights
founded upon actual contribution and equity.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                           AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PROPERTY RIGHTS OF PERSONS WHO HAVE
ACTUALLY CONTRIBUTED TO THE ACQUISITION OF PROPERTY DURING
COHABITATION NOT GOVERNED BY ARTICLE 147 OF THE FAMILY CODE,
AMENDING ARTICLE 148 OF EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. 209, OTHERWISE KNOWN
AS THE “FAMILY CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES,” AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Equitable Property Rights of
Cohabitants Act."


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to protect property rights,
uphold fairness and equity, prevent unjust enrichment, and recognize ownership based
on actual contribution in accordance with the Constitution and existing civil laws.

The State likewise recognizes that persons who have actually contributed to the
acquisition or improvement of property should not be deprived of their lawful
proprietary interests solely because legal title is registered in another person's name.



SEC. 3. Article 148 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Article 148. In cases of cohabitation not falling under the preceding Article, only
       properties acquired through the actual joint contribution of money, property,
       industry, labor, or services shall be owned by the parties in common in
       proportion to their respective contributions. In the absence of proof to the
       contrary, their contributions and corresponding shares are presumed to be
       equal.
       ACTUAL CONTRIBUTION MAY BE ESTABLISHED THROUGH ANY
       COMPETENT EVIDENCE, INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED TO:
      (A) WRITTEN AGREEMENTS;
      (B) ACKNOWLEDGMENTS OF CONTRIBUTION;
      (C) RECEIPTS, BANK RECORDS, AND FINANCIAL DOCUMENTS;
      (D) PROOF OF PAYMENT OF PURCHASE PRICE, LOAN
      AMORTIZATIONS, TAXES, INSURANCE, RENOVATIONS, REPAIRS, OR
      IMPROVEMENTS;
      (E) TESTIMONIAL AND OTHER ADMISSIBLE EVIDENCE.
      AN ACKNOWLEDGMENT EXECUTED BY ONE PARTY RECOGNIZING
      THE OWNERSHIP INTEREST OR FINANCIAL CONTRIBUTION OF
      ANOTHER SHALL CONSTITUTE PRIMA FACIE EVIDENCE OF SUCH
      CONTRIBUTION.
      A PARTY WHO VOLUNTARILY ACKNOWLEDGES THE PROPRIETARY
      INTEREST OF ANOTHER SHALL BE ESTOPPED FROM SUBSEQUENTLY
      DENYING SUCH ACKNOWLEDGMENT ABSENT PROOF OF FRAUD,
      MISTAKE, INTIMIDATION, UNDUE INFLUENCE, OR FALSIFICATION.
      UPON PROOF OF ACTUAL JOINT CONTRIBUTION, OWNERSHIP SHALL
      BE PRESUMED EQUAL UNLESS COMPETENT EVIDENCE ESTABLISHES
      A DIFFERENT PROPORTION. The same rule and presumption shall apply to
      joint deposits of money and evidences of credit.

      If one of the parties is validly married to another, his or her share in the co-
      ownership shall accrue to the absolute community or conjugal partnership
      existing in such valid marriage. If the party who acted in bad faith is not validly
      married to another, his or her shall be forfeited in the manner provided in the
      last paragraph of the preceding Article.

      The foregoing rules on forfeiture shall likewise apply even if both parties are in
      bad faith.


SEC. 4. Right to Partition. Any co-owner whose ownership has been established under
this Act or Article 148 of the Family Code shall possess the right to demand partition of
the property at any time, unless partition is prohibited by law or by a valid agreement.



SEC. 5. Annotation of Equitable Interest. A person claiming ownership under this Act
may cause the annotation of an adverse claim or notice of equitable interest upon the
certificate of title in accordance with existing laws governing land registration.



SEC. 6. Clarificatory Clause. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as:

(a) recognizing or creating marriage or civil union between persons not authorized
under existing laws;

(b) modifying the requisites for marriage under the Family Code; or
(c) diminishing rights otherwise granted under the Civil Code, Family Code, or other
special laws.

This Act solely governs proprietary rights arising from actual contribution.



SEC. 7. Presumption of Contribution. In case of doubt in the existence of cohabitation
or actual contribution in the acquisition of property, co-habitation and equal
contribution shall be presumed to prevent unjust enrichment and to give effect to the
true intent of the parties.



SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice, in coordination with the Land
Registration Authority and the Supreme Court, insofar as procedural matters are
concerned, shall formulate the necessary implementing rules and regulations.



SEC. 9. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.



SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, rules and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.



SEC. 11. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
