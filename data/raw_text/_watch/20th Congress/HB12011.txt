                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


In the Philippines, thousands of Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDLs) are released
from our correctional facilities every year after serving their sentences, being
granted parole, or receiving a pardon. While the state’s justice system works to
rehabilitate these individuals during their confinement, the true test of their
reformation begins upon their release and re-entry into society.

Unfortunately, reality consistently show that former PDLs face immense,
sometimes insurmountable, barriers to finding employment. The pervasive
stigma against hiring individuals with criminal records creates a cycle of poverty
and marginalization. Without proper intervention, a former PDL is highly likely to
return to a life of crime, leading to high recidivism rates that burden the justice
system and compromise public safety.

Employment is a critical factor in a former PDL's successful reintegration. It
promotes socialization, provides a structured daily routine, and grants them the
dignity of self-reliance. It is imperative that the State not only protects the rights
of these individuals to equal employment opportunities but also actively
encourages the private sector to participate in their social reintegration.

This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive employment and reintegration
program for released PDLs.       To overcome the hesitation of the private sector,
this measure proposes tax incentives for business establishments that hire and
retain former PDLs. Furthermore, it prohibits employment discrimination based
solely on an individual's prior conviction, ensuring they are given a fair chance to
rebuild their lives.
By providing former PDLs with dignified opportunities to work, we are not merely
extending compassion; we are taking a practical step toward building safer
communities and a more inclusive economy.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT PROMOTING THE EMPLOYMENT AND REINTEGRATION OF
RELEASED PERSONS DEPRIVED OF LIBERTY, PROVIDING INCENTIVES TO
   EMPLOYERS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
                                  PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Released Persons
Deprived of Liberty (PDL) Employment and Reintegration Act."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote social
justice and human rights by creating economic opportunities based on freedom
of initiative and self-reliance. The State recognizes that the rehabilitation of
Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDLs) does not end upon their release. Therefore,
the State shall facilitate their successful reintegration into society by providing
access to gainful employment, protecting them from unjust discrimination, and
encouraging the active participation of the private sector in their reformation.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   1. Released Person Deprived of Liberty (Released PDL) refers to any individual
      who has been convicted by final judgment and has subsequently been
      released from confinement on probation, parole, pardon, or after having
      fully served their sentence.
   2. Private Entity refers to any business establishment, corporation,
      partnership, or enterprise operating in the private sector that employs
      workers.
   3. Recidivism refers to the act of a person repeating an undesirable behavior
      after they have either experienced negative consequences of that behavior
      or have been trained to extinguish that behavior.
SEC. 4. The PDL Reintegration and Employment Program. — The Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the Department of Justice
(DOJ), the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor), the Bureau of Jail Management and
Penology (BJMP), and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), shall establish a comprehensive PDL Reintegration and Employment
Program.

The Program shall include, but not be limited to:

      a) Skills training and vocational certification for PDLs prior to and
      immediately after their release;

      b) Job matching and placement assistance through the Public Employment
      Service Offices (PESO) in all Local Government Units (LGUs); and

      c) Livelihood and entrepreneurial support for Released PDLs who opt to
      start micro-businesses.


SEC.5. Incentives for Employers. — To encourage the active participation of the
private sector in promoting the social reintegration of Released PDLs, private
entities that employ Released PDLs for a minimum period of one (1) year shall be
entitled to an additional deduction from their gross income. The deduction shall
be equivalent to fifteen percent (15%) of the total amount paid as salaries and
wages to such Released PDL employees, subject to the rules and regulations
formulated by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and the Department of
Finance (DOF).

SEC.6. Protection from Discrimination. — It shall be an unlawful employment
practice for any employer to fail or refuse to hire, or to discharge, any individual,
or otherwise discriminate against any individual with respect to compensation,
terms, conditions, or privileges of employment, solely because of the individual's
previous criminal offenses, provided that the applicant is qualified for the position
and their prior conviction does not inherently conflict with the nature of the work.

SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, DOJ, DOF, and TESDA, in consultation with
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 8. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's
appropriations of the DOLE and the DOJ. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
