EXPLANATORY NOTE

The family is a crucial cornerstone of society. For many Filipino workers, caring for their
families does not end when they report for work. When a child falls seriously ill, a parent needs to
undergo treatment, or a spouse is rushed to the hospital, being present for their loved ones becomes
just as important as fulfilling their responsibilities at work. Yet, for employees who have exhausted
their leave credits or have no available paid leave, attending to these family emergencies may
mean risking their jobs and the very livelihood that supports their families. While existing labor
laws provide for maternity, paternity, and solo-parent parental leave, these benefits address
specific circumstances. There remains no general statutory entitlement to protected leave for
employees who must accompany or assist an immediate family member during hospitalization,
serious illness, or significant medical treatment. In the absence of such protection, workers may
find themselves having to choose between being there for their loved ones when they are needed
most and fulfilling their obligations to their employers.

This bill seeks to address this gap by granting covered private-sector employees up to ten
(10) working days of family medical care leave per calendar year when their presence or assistance
is reasonably necessary due to a qualifying medical circumstance involving an immediate family
member. More importantly, it seeks to ensure that employees who avail of such leave are protected
against dismissal, disciplinary action, or other adverse employment consequences. Recognizing
the operational and financial considerations of employers, particularly small businesses, the
proposed measure does not impose an additional mandatory paid leave benefit. Employees may
choose to use their available service incentive leave, vacation leave, or other applicable paid leave
credits, while any remaining portion shall be unpaid. The bill likewise establishes reasonable
notice and verification requirements to ensure that the leave is availed of for legitimate medical
circumstances, without imposing unnecessary administrative burdens on employers.

Family and livelihood are important areas of one’s life and attending to personal and
professional responsibilities need not come into conflict. At its core, this measure recognizes a
simple reality: workers are also parents, children, and spouses who have responsibilities beyond
the workplace. No employee should have to fear losing their job simply because they need to
accompany their loved ones undergoing serious medical treatment. Protecting workers in these
circumstances is not only a matter of humane working conditions, but also of recognizing the
importance of family in the lives of Filipino workers.

AN ACT
GRANTING EMPLOYEES PROTECTED LEAVE TO ATTEND TO THE
HOSPITALIZATION, TREATMENT, OR OTHER MEDICAL CARE OF IMMEDIATE
FAMILY MEMBERS WITH SERIOUS MEDICAL CONDITIONS, PROHIBITING
EMPLOYMENT-RELATED RETALIATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Family Medical Care Leave
and I‘mployment Protection Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the family as a basic social
institution and affirms the right of workers to humane conditions of work. It shall promote
reasonable accommodation of necessary family caregiving responsibilities arising from
hospitalization, serious medical conditions, and significant diagnostic or therapeutic procedures,
while recognizing the legitimate operational needs of employers.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — F or purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “Covered employee” means a private-sector employee who has commenced actual work,
regardless of employment status or length of service;

(b) "Immediate family member" means the employee's spouse, child, parent, or legal ward,
including a person for whom the employee has been legally designated as guardian;

(c) "Qualifying family medical circumstance" means the hospitalization, emergency
treatment, surgery, or significant diagnostic or therapeutic procedure of an immediate
family member arising from or related to a serious medical condition, for which the
employee's presence or assistance is reasonably necessary; and

(d) “Serious medical condition” means an illness, injury, impairment, or physical condition
requiring hospitalization, surgery, significant or continuing medical treatment, or
significant diagnostic or therapeutic procedures, as determined by a licensed medical
professional.

Routine medical consultations, preventive health examinations, and diagnostic procedures
not related to a serious medical condition shall not, by themselves, constitute a qualifying family
medical circumstance under this Act.

SECTION 4. Family Medical Care Leave. — A covered employee whose presence or
assistance is reasonably necessary due to a qualifying family medical circumstance involving an
immediate family member shall be entitled to up to ten (10) working days of family medical care
leave in a calendar year.

The leave may be taken continuously or intermittently, including in half-day increments,
depending on the medical needs of the immediate family member, provided that the total leave
availed of shall not exceed ten (10) working days in a calendar year.

The employee may choose to charge the leave against available service incentive leave,
vacation leave, or other applicable paid leave credits. An employer shall not require the employee
to use or exhaust such paid leave credits before availing of family medical care leave under this
Act. Any portion not charged against available paid leave credits shall be unpaid.

Absence from work pursuant to this Section shall be considered an authorized absence and
shall not constitute absence without leave, abandonment, neglect of duty, or other analogous
ground for disciplinary action or termination of employment.

Unused family medical care leave shall not be cumulative or convertible to cash.
Availment of leave under this Act shall not diminish any separate leave entitlement granted under
existing laws, collective bargaining agreements, employment contracts, or company policies.
Where the employee elects to charge an absence against an applicable paid leave entitlement, the
same period of absence shall be counted against both entitlements, without duplication of payment.

Nothing in this Act shall require an employer to provide additional paid leave beyond that
required by law or voluntarily provided under a collective bargaining agreement. company policy,
or employment contract.

SECTION 5. Notice and Verification. - The employee shall notify the employer as soon
as reasonably practicable of the need to avail of family medical care leave and the expected period
of absence.

For scheduled diagnostic or therapeutic procedures, the employee shall give notice as soon
as the schedule is known. Failure to give advance notice shall not affect the employee’s entitlement
when circumstances reasonably prevent such notice.

The employer may require reasonable documentation establishing the qualifying family
medical circumstance and, where reasonably necessary, the employee's need to provide assistance
or accompaniment. Such documentation may include a hospital record, appointment or procedure
schedule, or certification from a licensed medical professional.

The employee shall submit the required proof within five (5) working days after returning
to work, unless prevented by circumstances beyond the employee's control, in which case a
reasonable extension shall be allowed. The employer shall require only such information as is
reasonably necessary to determine eligibility under this Act and shall not require disclosure of the
patient's specific diagnosis or other confidential medical information beyond what is necessary for
such determination. Any medical information obtained shall be treated confidentially and
processed in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act
of 2012."

SECTION 6. Employment Protection and Non-Diminution of Benefits. — No employee
shall be dismissed, suspended, demoted, discriminated against, threatened, harassed, or subjected
to adverse changes in employment conditions for requesting or availing of family medical care
leave under this Act.

Upon return to work, the employee shall be restored to the same position or an equivalent
position without loss of seniority or benefits accrued prior to the leave. The accrual of additional
benefits during any unpaid portion of the leave shall be governed by applicable laws, collective
bargaining agreements, employment contracts, or company policies.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to reduce or impair any more favorable benefit
granted under existing laws, collective bargaining agreements, employment contracts, or company

SECTION 7. Enforcement and Remedies. — The Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE) shall oversee compliance with this Act in accordance with its existing visitorial,
inspection, and enforcement powers under applicable labor laws.

Complaints involving dismissal, suspension, discrimination, or other adverse employment
actions arising from the exercise of rights under this Act shall be resolved by the appropriate labor
authorities or tribunals, in accordance with their respective jurisdiction under existing laws.

An employee whose rights under this Act have been violated shall be entitled to such
reinstatement, backwages, restoration of benefits, or other appropriate relief as may be warranted
under applicable labor laws, without prejudice to other remedies available under existing laws.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in consultation with
the Department of Health (DOH) and other relevant government agencies, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary for its implementation.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
