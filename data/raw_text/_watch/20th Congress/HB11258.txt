                             AN ACT
      STRENGTHENING GOVERNMENT SUPPORT FOR THE DIGITAL
    TRANSFORMATION OF MICRO, SMALL, AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES
 (MSMEs) THROUGH E‑COMMERCE AND RESPONSIBLE USE OF ARTIFICIAL
             INTELLIGENCE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Micro, small, and medium enterprises constitute 99.63 percent of the Philippines’ 1.24
million business establishments and generate 6.35 million jobs—roughly two-thirds of
total employment nationwide. Yet only 15,998 of these firms were actively transacting
online in 2023, a fraction of the sector’s potential market reach. This participation gap
persists even as the digital economy contributed ₱2.25 trillion, or about 8.5 percent of
gross domestic product, in 2024.

Household connectivity barriers are receding: the Women and ICT Development Index
survey recorded a surge in home internet access from 17.7 percent in 2019 to 76.9
percent in 2022, confirming that physical access is no longer the primary hurdle to
online commerce. Instead, MSMEs cite cost, skills shortages, logistics frictions, and
mounting cyber-risks. Fortinet’s 2024 threat-landscape report found that six in ten
Philippine companies experienced a three-fold increase in cyber-attacks in 2023. At
the same time, the Philippines climbed nine places to 56th out of 188 economies in
Oxford Insights’ 2024 Government AI Readiness Index, evidence of growing
institutional capacity to deploy artificial intelligence responsibly. Harnessing these twin
trends — rapid connectivity gains and improving AI readiness — demands a coherent
policy response that lowers the cost of digital entry, embeds cybersecurity, and
accelerates skills formation for the country’s smallest firms.

The MSME E-Commerce Support Act delivers that response.
First, it creates a Digital and AI Transformation Grant that defrays the costs of web
storefronts, AI-enabled marketing tools, smart-logistics interfaces, and mandatory
data-protection safeguards.

Second, it mandates a nationwide capacity-building programme — jointly
implemented by DTI, DICT, TESDA, and CHED — to upskill entrepreneurs and
employees in e-commerce operations, AI fundamentals, and cybersecurity
compliance.

Third, it institutionalises a logistics-access scheme that links MSMEs to affordable
warehousing and last-mile delivery services, especially in underserved regions.

Fourth, it establishes voluntary AI sandboxes where firms can pilot emerging
technologies under regulatory guidance from the DICT and National Privacy
Commission.

Fifth, the bill directs the MSME Development Council to publish open-data dashboards
tracking digital-adoption rates, sales growth, and risk-reduction outcomes, ensuring
transparency and evidence-based course correction.

The measure aligns squarely with Article XII, Section 1 of the Constitution, which
obliges the State to promote industrialization and full employment. By lowering the
threshold for digital participation, the bill empowers the sector that already provides
the majority of jobs to reach new domestic and export markets, strengthens consumer
protection through mandatory cybersecurity upgrades, and positions Philippine
enterprises to capitalize on responsible artificial-intelligence tools. Enactment will not
only modernize commerce but also deepen economic resilience and broaden the tax
base—benefits that accrue to workers, consumers, and the national treasury alike.

Passage of this bill is therefore earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
      STRENGTHENING GOVERNMENT SUPPORT FOR THE DIGITAL
    TRANSFORMATION OF MICRO, SMALL, AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES
 (MSMEs) THROUGH E‑COMMERCE AND RESPONSIBLE USE OF ARTIFICIAL
             INTELLIGENCE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “MSME E‑Commerce Support

Act.”

        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote inclusive

economic growth by enhancing the digital and artificial‑intelligence competitiveness of

MSMEs. The government shall provide targeted support in digital capacity‑building,

responsible AI adoption, online presence, e‑commerce logistics, and cybersecurity

compliance.

        Sec. 3. Statement of Objectives. – This Act shall have the following objectives:

   1. Provide financial and technical assistance enabling MSMEs to integrate

        e‑commerce platforms and AI‑powered tools for marketing, customer support,

        inventory management, and data analytics;
   2. Improve the digital and AI literacy of MSME owners and employees through

      accredited training programs;

   3. Promote cybersecurity, data privacy, and ethical AI standards that safeguard

      consumers and enterprises;

   4. Establish a coordinated support mechanism involving national agencies, LGUs,

      and private partners in accelerating responsible digital and AI transformation;

   5. Incentivize Filipino MSME participation in global value chains and AI‑enabled

      online marketplaces.

Sec. 4. Coverage. – This Act applies to all duly registered MSMEs operating in the

Philippines, including cooperatives, social enterprises, and informal enterprises

undergoing formalization.

      Sec. 5. Digital and AI Transformation Grants. – A Digital and AI

Transformation Grant is hereby created to support qualified MSMEs. Eligible

expenditures include:

   1. Website development, e‑commerce platform subscriptions, and AI‑enabled

      storefront or chatbot services;

   2. Digital marketing tools (including AI‑driven customer targeting and content

      generation) and campaigns;

   3. Inventory, payment, and supply‑chain systems with embedded AI forecasting

      or optimization features;

   4. Access to logistics service platforms, smart warehousing, and AI‑assisted route

      optimization;

   5. Cybersecurity     solutions,   data‑protection   compliance   measures,     and

      risk‑scoring AI services.
       Sec. 6. MSME Digital and AI Skills Training Program. – The Department of

Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with TESDA, DICT, and the Commission on

Higher Education (CHED), shall offer nationwide training that covers:

   1. E‑commerce operations, digital entrepreneurship, and AI fundamentals for

       business;

   2. Customer relations, digital branding, and AI‑enhanced marketing analytics;

   3. Supply‑chain management and AI‑driven demand forecasting;

   4. Data privacy, cybersecurity basics, and responsible‑AI frameworks consistent

       with international best practice.

       Sec. 7. Responsible‑AI Compliance and Sandbox Program. – The DICT,

together with the National Privacy Commission (NPC) and DOST, shall establish

voluntary AI Sandboxes where MSMEs can pilot new AI solutions under regulatory

guidance, supported by standardized ethical‑AI and data‑governance checklists.

       Sec. 8. E‑Commerce Logistics Access Program. – The DTI, in partnership

with logistics providers, shall facilitate MSME access to affordable nationwide delivery

systems and encourage local logistics hubs and warehousing cooperatives that

employ AI‑assisted inventory tracking and last‑mile optimization, with priority to

underserved areas.

       Sec. 9. Public‑Private Partnerships. – The DTI and concerned agencies are

encouraged to partner with technology companies, AI developers, logistics firms, and

digital banks to extend subsidized or pro‑bono AI and e‑commerce services to

MSMEs.
       Sec. 10. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The MSME Development Council shall

create a multi‑stakeholder monitoring framework to assess digital‑ and AI‑adoption

rates, productivity gains, sales growth, and risk‑reduction outcomes of beneficiaries,

using open‑data dashboards where feasible.

       Sec. 11. Lead Agency. – The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall be

the lead agency for this Act in coordination with the DICT, DOST, NPC, and other

relevant agencies.

       Sec. 12. Appropriations. – Funds necessary for initial implementation shall be

charged against the current‑year budgets of concerned agencies. Thereafter, required

sums shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       Sec. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DTI, in consultation

with concerned agencies and MSME stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and

regulations within thirty (30) days from effectivity.

       Sec. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive issuances, rules, or

regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       Sec. 15. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is

declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force

and effect.

       Sec. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after its publication in at least

two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
