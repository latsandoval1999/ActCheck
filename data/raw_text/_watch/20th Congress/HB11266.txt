15                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE
16
17           Article II, Section 3 of the 1987 Constitution declares that civilian authority is, at all times,
18   supreme over the military. Article XVI, Section 6 thereof mandates the State to establish and
19   maintain one police force, national in scope and civilian in character, to be administered and
20   controlled by a national police commission. Both provisions proceed from a common
21   constitutional premise: that an armed and uniformed service of the Republic must remain
22   accountable to an institutional authority beyond its own chain of command.
23
24          The Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), notwithstanding its character as an armed and
25   uniformed service, has never been accorded such an institutional mechanism.
26
27           Originally constituted as a major unit of the Philippine Navy under Republic Act No. 5173
28   in 1967, transferred to the then Department of Transportation and Communications in 1998, and
29   subsequently established as an armed and uniformed service under Republic Act No. 9993 in 2010,
30   the PCG has expanded from a force of approximately three thousand (3,000) personnel into one of
31   the largest maritime law enforcement agencies in the region. It now comprises tens of thousands
32   of officers and non-officers, maintains a fleet of ocean-going vessels and aircraft, and performs a
33   frontline role in the assertion of the country's sovereign rights and jurisdiction in the West
34   Philippine Sea. Its personnel bear arms, exercise powers of arrest, search, and seizure, board and
35   detain vessels, and operate in contested waters where the decision of a single officer may carry
36   consequences of national significance.
37
38            The institutional governance of the PCG, however, has not kept pace with the growth of its
39   organization and mandate. Under existing law, the command of the service and the administration
40   of its personnel are vested in a single office. The Commandant directs operations and, at the same
41   time, determines matters of promotion, assignment to major commands, and discipline. Unlike the
42   Philippine National Police, whose administration and control are vested in the National Police
43   Commission (NAPOLCOM) under Republic Act No. 6975, as amended by Republic Act No.
44   8551, the PCG has no specialized statutory body that reviews these matters independently of the
45   chain of command.
46
47          This deficiency is most pronounced at the highest ranks. Under Article VII, Section 16 of
48   the Constitution, as construed by the Supreme Court in Manalo v. Sistoza (G.R. No. 107369, 11
49   August 1999), only officers of the armed forces from the rank of colonel or naval captain require
 1   confirmation by the Commission on Appointments. Flag officers of the Armed Forces of the
 2   Philippines are thus subject to legislative scrutiny, while senior officers of the Philippine National
 3   Police are subject to the screening and endorsement of the NAPOLCOM. Flag officers of the PCG
 4   are subject to neither. No professional body evaluates their qualifications prior to appointment, nor
 5   reviews the exercise of their authority thereafter.
 6
 7           This measure is introduced while Congress undertakes a comprehensive revision of the
 8   statutory framework of the PCG. On 11 August 2026, the Senate approved on third and final
 9   reading Senate Bill No. 2116, or the proposed Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law, which
10   reorganizes the PCG into functional, support, and technical commands, prescribes clearer rules on
11   ranks, appointments, and promotions, and strengthens internal accountability through a Coast
12   Guard Internal Affairs Service. In the House of Representatives, the Committee on Transportation
13   is deliberating on a Substitute Bill to House Bills Nos. 426, 1120, 3390, 4693, 4758, 5127, 5131,
14   5471, 5552, 5758, 6104, 6173, 6244, 6386, 8027, and 9204, which seeks to strengthen the PCG
15   through policy and organizational reforms.
16
17            Mindful of these parallel efforts, this bill is crafted to complement, and not to duplicate,
18   the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law. It does not establish a separate organizational, personnel,
19   promotion, disciplinary, or career-management system. It contains an express harmonization
20   clause and rule of construction; it requires that professional standards and career progression
21   criteria conform to the functional organization adopted under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard
22   Law; it relies upon the Internal Affairs Service for investigation and fact-finding, in lieu of creating
23   a competing investigative body; and it limits any transfer of functions to those expressly vested in
24   the Commission by law.
25
26           Neither does this bill place the Commission within, or above, the chain of command of the
27   PCG. The Commission is constituted as an independent personnel governance and oversight body,
28   not as an administrative superior of the Commandant, and is expressly denied any authority over
29   the operations, budget, finances, procurement, and assets of the service. Its powers are confined to
30   those enumerated by law, namely: the promulgation of governance standards and the review of
31   compliance therewith; the validation of the promotion of senior officers; the validation of the
32   assignment of flag officers to major unit commands; the professional screening of nominees for
33   Commandant for the consideration of the President; and the exercise of appellate and review
34   authority in administrative disciplinary cases.
35
36           The distinction between command and governance is deliberate. Command, being the
37   direction of personnel, vessels, aircraft, and resources in the accomplishment of the mission, is
38   vested in, and shall remain with, the Commandant. The PCG shall continue to administer its
39   personnel system, including recruitment, training, assignment, career management, and personnel
40   records, while the Commission shall safeguard the integrity of that system. The Commandant
41   recommends; the Commission independently validates; and the President appoints or promotes
42   where so required by the Constitution or by law. In no instance may the Commission substitute its
43   own choice for the recommendation of the Commandant or for the appointing power of the
44   President.
45
46           The NAPOLCOM serves as a precedent, not as a template. The PCG is not a police force
47   and is not organized as one. The Commission proposed herein is accordingly adapted to a maritime
48   service whose functions encompass maritime safety, marine environmental protection, and
49   maritime security, and whose officers are trained for duty at sea. The measure preserves the
50   operational command of the Commandant and the constitutional appointing power of the


                                                                                                           2
 1   President, while providing the service with an institutional safeguard for merit, professionalism,
 2   accountability, and continuity.
 3
 4          Commandants, Secretaries, and administrations succeed one another; the institution,
 5   however, must endure beyond any single tenure. Sound command is indispensable to the Coast
 6   Guard, and sound governance renders such command sustainable. This measure does not seek to
 7   diminish the authority of the Commandant, but to ensure that such authority is exercised within a
 8   professional institutional framework that strengthens the service for succeeding generations.
 9
10          In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

          AN ACT CREATING THE PHILIPPINE COAST GUARD COMMISSION AS AN
     INDEPENDENT PERSONNEL GOVERNANCE AND OVERSIGHT BODY FOR THE
     PHILIPPINE COAST GUARD, DEFINING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS,
     PROVIDING FOR ITS HARMONIZATION WITH THE REVISED PHILIPPINE COAST
     GUARD LAW, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9993,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "PHILIPPINE COAST GUARD LAW OF 2009,"
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

                                           CHAPTER I
                                       GENERAL PROVISIONS

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Coast Guard
 2   Commission Act."
 3
 4           SECTION. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   maintain the Philippine Coast Guard as a professional, competent, and accountable armed and
 6   uniformed service dedicated to maritime safety, marine environmental protection, and maritime
 7   security.
 8
 9          Toward this end, the State shall preserve the command authority of the Commandant of
10   the Philippine Coast Guard, while establishing an independent governance framework that
11   safeguards merit, professionalism, accountability, institutional continuity, and continuous
12   improvement in the administration of the service.
13
14          The State shall further ensure that such governance framework complements, and does not
15   duplicate or conflict with, the organizational, personnel, career, promotion, and accountability
16   systems established under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law, and that it is exercised in a
17   manner that is merit-based, transparent, and insulated from undue political or personal influence.
18
19          SECTION. 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
20   defined as follows:



                                                                                                     1
 1            (a) Command – refers to the authority and responsibility of the Commandant and
 2   subordinate commanders to direct, control, deploy, and employ the personnel, vessels, aircraft,
 3   facilities, and resources of the PCG, and to maintain its operational readiness, in the performance
 4   of its mission and functions;
 5            (b) Commandant – refers to the Commandant of the Philippine Coast Guard;
 6            (c) Commission – refers to the Philippine Coast Guard Commission created under this Act;
 7            (d) Department – refers to the Department of Transportation;
 8            (e) Flag Officer – refers to an officer of the PCG holding the rank of Commodore or higher;
 9            (f) Internal Affairs Service – refers to the Coast Guard Internal Affairs Service, or its
10   equivalent, established under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law;
11            (g) Major Unit Command – refers to a functional command, Coast Guard District, or other
12   command or office of the PCG which, under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law, or as
13   determined by the Commission upon the recommendation of the Commandant, requires an officer
14   holding the rank of Commodore or higher;
15            (h) PCG or Coast Guard – refers to the Philippine Coast Guard established under Republic
16   Act No. 9993, as amended or revised;
17            (i) Personnel Governance – refers to the promulgation of governance standards for the
18   personnel systems of the PCG, the review of compliance therewith, the validation of the promotion
19   of senior officers and of assignments to major unit commands, the professional screening of
20   nominees for Commandant, and the exercise of appellate and review authority in administrative
21   cases, as expressly provided in this Act. The term shall not include recruitment, training,
22   assignment, career management, personnel records management, and other day-to-day human
23   resource functions, which shall remain with the PCG;
24            (j) Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law – refers to the law enacted by Congress revising
25   or replacing Republic Act No. 9993, and, pending the effectivity thereof, Republic Act No. 9993,
26   as amended;
27            (k) Secretary – refers to the Secretary of Transportation; and
28            (l) Senior Officer – refers to an officer of the PCG holding, or being considered for
29   promotion to, the rank of Captain or higher.

30                                      CHAPTER II
31                       THE PHILIPPINE COAST GUARD COMMISSION
32
33          SECTION. 4. Creation and Nature of the Commission. There is hereby created a
34   Philippine Coast Guard Commission, hereinafter referred to as the "Commission," which shall
35   exercise independent personnel governance, oversight, review, validation, and appellate authority
36   over such personnel matters as are expressly provided under this Act. The Commission shall be
37   attached to the Department for purposes of policy and program coordination.
38
39          The Commission shall not form part of the chain of command of the PCG. Nothing in this
40   Act shall be construed as placing the Commission in a position of hierarchical or operational
41   superiority over the Commandant.
42
43           SECTION. 5. Composition. The Commission shall be a collegial body composed of a
44   Chairperson and four (4) Commissioners, as follows:
45           (a) A Chairperson, who shall serve on a full-time basis and shall act as the chief executive
46   officer of the Commission;
47           (b) One (1) Commissioner who is a retired flag officer of the PCG and who has been retired
48   from the service for at least two (2) years at the time of appointment;



                                                                                                       2
 1           (c) One (1) Commissioner who is a member of the Philippine Bar, has been engaged in the
 2   practice of law for at least ten (10) years, and possesses demonstrated competence in
 3   administrative, constitutional, maritime, or international law;
 4           (d) One (1) Commissioner who comes from the academe or the private sector and possesses
 5   recognized expertise in maritime governance, public administration, public-sector accountability,
 6   or human resource and organizational management; and
 7           (e) The Undersecretary of the Department in charge of maritime affairs, as an ex officio
 8   Commissioner.
 9
10           The Commandant shall sit with the Commission as an ex officio member without the right
11   to vote: Provided, That the Commandant shall not participate in the deliberations of the
12   Commission on any matter concerning the promotion of senior officers, the assignment of flag
13   officers to major unit commands, the screening of nominees for Commandant, or any
14   administrative case in which the Commandant is a party or is the deciding authority whose decision
15   is on appeal.
16
17           SECTION. 6. Qualifications; Conflict of Interest. The Chairperson and the regular
18   Commissioners shall be natural-born citizens of the Philippines, at least thirty-five (35) years of
19   age at the time of appointment, of proven integrity and independence, and shall not have been
20   candidates for any elective position in the election immediately preceding their appointment. The
21   appointments to the Commission shall, as far as practicable, ensure that its members collectively
22   possess demonstrated competence in maritime governance, personnel and organizational
23   management, administrative and constitutional law, public-sector accountability, institutional
24   development, and maritime security, safety, or environmental protection.
25
26          Except as provided in Section 5(b) hereof, no person who is a member of the PCG at the
27   time of appointment, or who has been retired from the PCG for less than two (2) years, shall be
28   appointed to the Commission.
29
30           A member of the Commission shall inhibit from any deliberation, validation, screening, or
31   case in which such member has a personal or financial interest, or is related to any party within
32   the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or affinity, or has participated in an official capacity prior
33   to appointment.
34
35           SECTION. 7. Appointment and Term of Office. The Chairperson and the regular
36   Commissioners shall be appointed by the President for a term of six (6) years without
37   reappointment: Provided, That of the first appointees, the Chairperson shall serve for six (6) years,
38   the Commissioner under Section 5(b) hereof for five (5) years, the Commissioner under Section
39   5(c) hereof for four (4) years, and the Commissioner under Section 5(d) hereof for three (3) years:
40   Provided, further, That any person appointed to fill a vacancy shall serve only for the unexpired
41   term of the predecessor.
42
43           SECTION. 8. Removal from Office. The Chairperson and the regular Commissioners may
44   be removed from office by the President only for cause, after due notice and hearing, on any of
45   the following grounds: serious misconduct, gross neglect of duty, physical or mental incapacity,
46   or conviction by final judgment of a crime involving moral turpitude.
47
48           SECTION. 9. Rank, Compensation, and Privileges. The Chairperson shall have the rank,
49   salary, and privileges of a Department Undersecretary, and the regular Commissioners shall have



                                                                                                          3
 1   the rank, salary, and privileges of a Department Assistant Secretary. They shall be subject to the
 2   prohibitions and disqualifications applicable to public officers and employees under existing laws.
 3
 4           SECTION. 10. Powers and Functions. The Commission shall have the following powers
 5   and functions:
 6           (a) Promulgate governance standards for the personnel systems of the PCG, consistent with
 7   the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law and other applicable laws;
 8           (b) Review the compliance of the personnel systems, policies, and practices of the PCG
 9   with such standards and applicable laws, and issue its findings and recommendations to the
10   Commandant and the Secretary;
11           (c) Establish and oversee professional standards and career progression criteria in
12   accordance with Section 14 hereof;
13           (d) Ensure that career progression in the PCG is merit-based, transparent, and insulated
14   from undue political or personal influence;
15           (e) Validate the promotion of senior officers recommended by the Commandant, in
16   accordance with Section 15 hereof;
17           (f) Validate the assignment of flag officers to major unit commands, in accordance with
18   Section 16 hereof;
19           (g) Conduct the professional screening and evaluation of qualified flag officers for
20   appointment as Commandant, in accordance with Section 17 hereof;
21           (h) Exercise appellate and review authority over decisions of the Commandant in
22   administrative disciplinary cases involving officers of the PCG, and original jurisdiction over
23   administrative cases against flag officers, in accordance with Section 18 hereof;
24           (i) Monitor institutional professionalism in the PCG through periodic governance reviews
25   of its units and offices on matters of personnel administration, discipline, and professional
26   standards;
27           (j) Access such personnel records, reports, and information of the PCG as may be
28   reasonably necessary for the performance of its functions under this Act;
29           (k) In cases within its jurisdiction, administer oaths, issue subpoena ad testificandum and
30   subpoena duces tecum, and refer to the Internal Affairs Service for investigation any matter
31   affecting the integrity, professionalism, or discipline of PCG personnel;
32           (l) Submit an annual report to the President and to both Houses of Congress on the state of
33   the officer and non-officer corps of the PCG, the activities of the Commission, and any systemic
34   deficiency in the personnel systems of the PCG; and
35           (m) Perform such other functions as may be expressly provided by law.
36
37           Recruitment, training, personnel administration, assignment, career management,
38   personnel records management, and other day-to-day human resource functions shall remain with
39   the PCG in accordance with the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law. The Commission shall not
40   establish or maintain a parallel personnel, career-management, or investigative system.
41
42           SECTION. 11. Limitation of Authority. The Commission shall exercise no power over
43   the budget, appropriations, funds, procurement, or financial management of the PCG, nor over the
44   acquisition, disposition, or employment of its vessels, aircraft, facilities, and other assets, which
45   matters shall continue to be governed by the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law and other
46   existing laws, rules, and regulations.
47
48           Nothing in this Act shall be construed as vesting in the Commission any operational,
49   tactical, deployment, employment, or command authority over the personnel, vessels, aircraft,



                                                                                                        4
 1   facilities, or other assets of the PCG, or as authorizing the Commission to direct, review, or
 2   interfere with the operations of the PCG.
 3
 4           SECTION. 12. Meetings, Quorum, Secretariat, and Staff. The Commission shall meet
 5   regularly at least twice every month, and as often as may be necessary. Three (3) voting members
 6   shall constitute a quorum, and the concurrence of a majority of the voting members present, there
 7   being a quorum, shall be necessary for the adoption of any resolution or decision.
 8
 9           The Commission shall be assisted by a Secretariat headed by an Executive Director, who
10   shall be appointed by the Commission and shall have the rank of Director IV. The Commission
11   shall determine its staffing pattern, subject to the approval of the Department of Budget and
12   Management, and may avail itself of the services of personnel detailed from the PCG, the
13   Department, and other government agencies: Provided, That personnel detailed from the PCG
14   shall not be assigned to any function involving the validation of promotions or the adjudication of
15   administrative cases.

16                                    CHAPTER III
17                        PERSONNEL GOVERNANCE AND DISCIPLINE
18
19           SECTION. 13. Command of the Coast Guard. The Commandant shall continue to
20   exercise command and control over the PCG and shall be responsible for the direction,
21   deployment, employment, and operational readiness of its personnel, vessels, aircraft, facilities,
22   and resources. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as vesting in the Commission, or as
23   diminishing, transferring, or impairing in the Commandant, the authority to command the PCG or
24   to direct its operations.
25
26           SECTION. 14. Professional Standards and Career Progression. The Commission shall
27   establish and oversee professional standards and career progression criteria consistent with the
28   functional organization, career structure, and personnel classification established under the
29   Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law. Such standards and criteria shall recognize the core
30   functions of the PCG in maritime safety, marine environmental protection, and maritime security,
31   and shall promote technical specialization, competence, and continuity of service within each
32   function. The Commission shall promulgate such standards and criteria within one (1) year from
33   its organization.
34
35          SECTION. 15. Validation of the Promotion of Senior Officers. The Commandant shall
36   submit to the Commission the list of officers recommended for promotion to the rank of Captain
37   and to all higher ranks, together with the complete records, performance evaluations, and
38   supporting documents of each officer so recommended.
39
40           The Commission shall independently determine whether each officer recommended
41   possesses the merits and qualifications prescribed for the rank, on the basis of competence,
42   integrity, performance, completion of the required schooling and training, compliance with tour-
43   of-duty and functional requirements, and fitness for higher responsibility. For this purpose, the
44   Commission may require the appearance of any officer recommended and may require the
45   Commandant to explain the basis of any recommendation.
46
47           The Commission shall act upon the list within sixty (60) days from receipt thereof. Where
48   the Commission finds an officer qualified, it shall so certify. Where the Commission finds an
49   officer not qualified, it shall return the name of such officer to the Commandant with a written


                                                                                                      5
 1   statement of the grounds therefor, and no such officer shall be promoted unless the grounds are
 2   subsequently cured and the name of the officer is resubmitted and certified.
 3
 4          Promotion to the rank of Captain shall be made by the Commandant only upon certification
 5   by the Commission. Promotion to flag rank shall be made by the President, where so required by
 6   the Constitution or by law, upon the recommendation of the Commandant, as certified and
 7   endorsed by the Commission through the Secretary.
 8
 9          The Commission shall not substitute its own nominees for those recommended by the
10   Commandant. The results of every validation, including the criteria applied, shall be made
11   available to the officers concerned.
12
13          SECTION. 16. Validation of the Assignment of Flag Officers to Major Unit Commands.
14   The selection and designation of flag officers as commanders of major unit commands shall be the
15   prerogative of the Commandant: Provided, That every such assignment or designation shall be
16   submitted to the Commission for validation prior to its effectivity.
17
18          The Commission shall determine whether the officer so designated satisfies the established
19   professional, career, functional, and qualification standards, taking into account the training,
20   experience, and service record of the officer and the mission and requirements of the command
21   concerned. The Commission shall act within thirty (30) days from submission. Failure of the
22   Commission to act within the said period shall be deemed a validation of the assignment.
23
24           Where the Commission finds that the assignment does not conform to such standards, it
25   shall return the same to the Commandant with a written statement of the grounds therefor,
26   whereupon the Commandant shall designate another officer for the position. In no case shall the
27   Commission select or designate the commander of any major unit command.
28
29           In case of emergency, or where the exigencies of the service require the immediate filling
30   of a vacancy in a major unit command, the Commandant may make a temporary designation
31   effective immediately: Provided, That such designation shall be submitted to the Commission for
32   validation within fifteen (15) days therefrom: Provided, further, that no such temporary
33   designation shall exceed ninety (90) days unless validated.
34
35           SECTION. 17. Selection of the Commandant. The President shall appoint the
36   Commandant in accordance with the Constitution and the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law.
37   The Commission shall conduct the professional screening and evaluation of qualified flag officers
38   and shall submit to the President, through the Secretary, a list of not less than three (3) qualified
39   nominees, together with its evaluation of each, for the consideration of the President. Nothing in
40   this Section shall be construed as a limitation upon the appointing power of the President.
41
42          In addition to the qualifications prescribed under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law,
43   a nominee for Commandant shall have completed a minimum of three (3) years of cumulative sea
44   duty aboard a PCG vessel in a junior officer billet: Provided, That no requirement of prior
45   command of a PCG vessel shall be imposed as a condition for eligibility, it being the intent of this
46   Act that fitness for command of the service be assessed on the breadth of competence across the
47   functions of the PCG and not on any single operational specialization.
48
49          SECTION. 18. Administrative Discipline. The investigation, fact-finding, and integrity
50   functions pertaining to the discipline of PCG personnel shall be performed by the Internal Affairs


                                                                                                        6
 1   Service in accordance with the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law. The Commission shall not
 2   establish a separate investigative body.
 3
 4          The Commandant shall retain original disciplinary authority over officers and non-officers
 5   of the PCG as provided by law. Any decision of the Commandant in an administrative case
 6   involving an officer, where the penalty imposed is suspension for more than thirty (30) days,
 7   demotion in rank, forfeiture of pay, or dismissal from the service, may be appealed to the
 8   Commission within fifteen (15) days from receipt of a copy thereof.
 9
10           The Commission shall have original jurisdiction over administrative cases against flag
11   officers, and may require the Internal Affairs Service to conduct the investigation thereof and to
12   submit its report and recommendations to the Commission. Decisions of the Commission shall be
13   final and executory, subject to appeal to the Court of Appeals under Rule 43 of the Rules of Court.
14
15           The Commission shall promulgate rules of procedure for the expeditious disposition of
16   cases, which shall guarantee due process and shall not be bound by the technical rules of evidence.
17   The pendency of an administrative case before the Commission shall be a ground for the deferment
18   of the promotion of, or of the validation of the assignment of, the officer concerned until the final
19   resolution of the case.
20
21           SECTION. 19. Institutional Coordination. The Commandant shall furnish the
22   Commission with such personnel records, reports, and information as may be reasonably necessary
23   for the performance of its functions under this Act. The Commission shall coordinate with the
24   Commandant in the formulation of governance standards affecting the personnel of the PCG, and
25   shall consult the Commandant prior to the adoption of any standard affecting the operational
26   readiness of the service.
27
28           The relationship between the Commission and the Commandant under this Act shall be
29   one of institutional coordination, and shall not be construed as placing the Commandant in a
30   relationship of operational or hierarchical subordination to the Commission.

31                                      CHAPTER IV
32                            TRANSITORY AND FINAL PROVISIONS
33
34           SECTION. 20. Interim Personnel and Professionalization Board. Within sixty (60) days
35   from the effectivity of this Act, and pending the organization of the Commission, the Secretary
36   shall constitute an Interim Personnel and Professionalization Board within the Department, to be
37   chaired by the Undersecretary in charge of maritime affairs. The Board shall prepare the
38   organizational and procedural requirements for the establishment of the Commission and shall
39   exercise, on an interim basis, the functions provided in Section 10(a) and (b) hereof. The Board
40   shall cease to exist upon the appointment of a majority of the members of the Commission.
41
42           SECTION. 21. Organization of the Commission. The President shall appoint the
43   Chairperson and the regular Commissioners within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
44   effectivity of this Act. The Commission shall be deemed organized upon the appointment and
45   qualification of a majority of its voting members.
46
47           SECTION. 22. Transfer of Functions and Records. Only such functions and records of
48   the PCG as pertain to matters expressly vested in the Commission under this Act shall be
49   transferred to the Commission within six (6) months from its organization, in accordance with a


                                                                                                        7
 1   transition plan to be approved by the Commission upon the recommendation of the Commandant.
 2   Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authorizing the Commission to absorb or duplicate any
 3   function assigned to the PCG under the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law. No personnel of the
 4   PCG shall suffer any diminution in rank, salary, or benefits by reason of such transfer.
 5
 6           SECTION. 23. Pending Cases, Promotions, and Assignments. Administrative cases
 7   pending before the Commandant upon the organization of the Commission shall continue to be
 8   heard and decided by the Commandant, and the decisions thereon shall be appealable to the
 9   Commission in accordance with Section 18 hereof. Promotions and assignments to major unit
10   commands effected prior to the organization of the Commission shall remain valid and shall not
11   require validation under this Act.
12
13           SECTION. 24. Harmonization with the Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law; Rule of
14   Construction. The provisions of this Act shall be construed and implemented in harmony with the
15   Revised Philippine Coast Guard Law and other laws governing the PCG. The Commission shall
16   exercise independent personnel governance, oversight, review, validation, and appellate functions
17   only to the extent expressly provided by law.
18
19         Nothing in this Act shall be construed as diminishing, transferring, or impairing the
20   command authority of the Commandant over the PCG, including operational command,
21   deployment, employment, readiness, and direction of its personnel and assets.
22
23           Where the provisions of this Act concern personnel standards, career development,
24   promotion, discipline, organizational classification, or other matters governed by the Revised
25   Philippine Coast Guard Law, the provisions of both laws shall, insofar as legally possible, be
26   interpreted in a manner that gives effect to both and avoids duplication, inconsistency, or
27   conflicting lines of authority.
28
29           SECTION. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
30   effectivity of this Act, the Secretary, in consultation with the Commandant, the Civil Service
31   Commission, and the Department of Budget and Management, shall promulgate the rules and
32   regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act. Upon its organization, the
33   Commission may amend, supplement, or supersede such rules and regulations insofar as they
34   pertain to matters within its competence.
35
36           SECTION. 26. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
37   this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriations of the Department. Thereafter,
38   such sums as may be necessary for the continued operation of the Commission shall be included
39   in the annual General Appropriations Act as a separate line item, and shall not be charged against
40   the appropriations of the PCG.

42           SECTION. 27. Amendatory Clause. The provisions of Republic Act No. 9993, as
43   amended or revised, relating to the appointment of the Commandant, the promotion of officers,
44   the assignment of flag officers, and the administrative discipline of PCG personnel are hereby
45   amended insofar as they are inconsistent with this Act.

47           SECTION. 28. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
48   regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
