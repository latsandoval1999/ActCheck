                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and
   promote the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake
   appropriate health human resource development responsive to the country’s
   needs and problems (Article XIII, Section 12).

         Radiologic technologists play a critical role in the health care system as
   the “eyes of medicine,” providing essential diagnostic imaging that underpins
   accurate clinical decision-making and effective treatment. Their work is
   indispensable in ensuring patient safety and in supporting a wide range of
   medical interventions, from routine diagnostics to complex, life-saving
   procedures. Moreover, the profession entails exposure to occupational hazards,
   including radiation, which requires a high level of technical competence and
   adherence to strict safety protocols.

         Despite their vital contributions, radiologic technologists in government
   service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of compensation relative
   to the demands, risks, and responsibilities of their profession. Many are
   overworked and underpaid, while the continuing global demand for radiologic
   technologists has contributed to the outmigration of skilled Filipino health care
   professionals seeking better compensation abroad. This trend undermines the
   capacity of our public healthcare system to deliver timely and quality services.

          This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government radiologic technologists from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15,
   thereby promoting equitable compensation, improving retention, and aligning
   their remuneration with similarly situated healthcare professionals.

         Ultimately, the proposed measure affirms the State’s commitment to
   uphold decent work standards in the public health sector and to strengthen
   the delivery of accessible and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
   earnestly sought.

                                “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT RADIOLOGIC TECHNOLOGISTS FROM
            SALARY GRADE 11 TO SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Government
Radiologic Technologists’ Salary Upgrading Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government radiologic
technologists in order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public
health sector, consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality
healthcare services to all Filipinos.

     SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Radiologic Technologists’
Minimum Salary Grade Level.- The minimum salary grade of government radiologic
technologists occupying entry-level positions (Radiologic Technologist I) is hereby
upgraded from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of radiologic technologists occupying higher positions shall
be correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all radiologic technologists
employed in public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and
operated by the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
