                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize tax literacy and responsibility in
intermediate, secondary, technical-vocational, and tertiary education in order to
strengthen voluntary tax compliance and foster a culture of accountability among
future taxpayers.

       The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has consistently reported gaps between
revenue targets and actual collections, while the Department of Finance (DOF) has
emphasized that improving voluntary compliance is more sustainable and
cost-efficient than relying solely on enforcement measures1.

        In the Philippines, where tax-to-GDP ratio has historically lagged behind
many regional peers,2 building tax morale early in life becomes imperative. These
realities underscore the need for structured and age-appropriate taxpayer education
that not only explains how taxes are collected, but also clarifies where public funds
go and how citizens can demand accountability.

      This proposed measure integrates tax education into the formal curriculum
and aligns it with youth development initiatives. This cultivates informed,
responsible, and engaged citizens. These future taxpayers understand that paying
the correct taxes on time supports essential public services, infrastructure, and
national development.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                        AN ACT
MANDATING THE TEACHING OF TAX LITERACY, CONSCIOUSNESS AND
RESPONSIBILITY     IN      INTERMEDIATE,       SECONDARY,
TECHNICAL-VOCATIONAL, AND TERTIARY EDUCATION, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Tax Consciousness and
Responsibility Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is a constitutionally declared policy of the
State to “promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and
independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies that provide
adequate social services, promote full employment, rising standard of living, and improved
quality of life for all”. Toward this end, the State shall responsibly and responsibly
exercise its inherent power of taxation in order to mobilize resources for the
common good.

       Corollary to this, the State shall also inculcate in its citizens the overriding
importance of regularly and promptly paying the correct amount of taxes. To this
end, an age-appropriate curriculum shall be included in intermediate, secondary,
technical-vocational, and tertiary education in both public and private schools,
colleges, and universities for the teaching of the importance of taxes as the lifeblood
of the nation and tax consciousness and responsibility of every taxpayer.

       Through education, to which is assigned the highest budgetary priority, the
State shall instill in the youth the citizens' patriotic duty to promptly pay the correct
amount of taxes as the citizens' valuable contribution to economic development and
nation-building.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:




                                            1
          a.​ Tax refers to the monetary contribution levied on individuals or
      firms/corporations by a government entity whether local or national to
      finance government activities and services to the people. Tax Compliance
      refers to the ability and willingness of taxpayers to comply with the tax laws,
      declare the correct income for each year, and pay the right amount of taxes on
      time;

         b.​ Tax Consciousness refers to the awareness and understanding of the
      concept of taxation and the importance of paying taxes on time;

          c.​ Tax Education refers to the inclusion of tax related lessons/courses in
      the curricula of educational institutions aimed at instilling tax consciousness,
      literacy, and compliance as a civic duty or social responsibility;

         d.​ Tax Literacy refers to the ability to know and understand the tax
      concepts, laws and related issues, and be updated on developments on tax
      and taxation;

         e.​ Tax Morale refers to the intrinsic motivation to pay taxes, it measures
      taxpayer perceptions, judgments, and attitudes towards paying or evading
      taxes;

         f.​ Tax Return refers to a form filed with the tax authority on which the
      taxpayer states his/her income, expenses and other tax information;

          g.​ Taxation refers to the power of the State to levy charges or burdens
      upon persons, property or property rights for the use, operation, and support
      of the government to enable it to discharge its functions; amd

         h.​ Voluntary Tax Compliance refers to the ability and willingness of
      taxpayers to file and report honest and accurate annual returns without
      proactive action by the government.

       SEC. 4. Mandatory Tax Education. — The State shall provide for the mandatory
teaching of tax literacy; instilling tax consciousness, compliance, and responsibility;
and improving tax morale, as defined in the preceding Section of this Act as a civic
duty or social responsibility in all intermediate and secondary education classes as
well as in all graduating classes in technical-vocational and tertiary education in
both public and private schools, colleges, and universities.
​
       SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department of Education. — The Department of
Education (DepEd) is mandated to prepare age-appropriate tax education
curriculum and modules, in coordination with the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA) and in consultation with the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR); the Bureau
of Customs (BoC); recognized tax experts, lawyers, and academicians; and local and
national youth organizations: Provided, That private educational institutions shall
adopt these curriculums and modules or modify them so long as that the

curriculums or modules they craft remain in accord with the objectives of this Act:
Provided, further, That the DepEd shall take full responsibility for the strict, effective,
and sustained implementation of the tax education curriculum and modules.
​
         SEC. 6. Tax Consciousness Week. — The observance of the “Tax Consciousness
Week” on the first week of April of every year as declared under Proclamation 960,
s. of 1972 shall be heightened. Week-long activities shall be conducted under the
auspices of the DoF in collaboration with relevant government agencies and private
entities notably educational institutions and the print, broadcast, social and web
media.
​
         SEC. 7. Complementary Role of Parents. — Consistent with the constitutional
recognition of the “natural and primary right and duty of parents in the rearing of
the youth for civic efficiency and the development of moral character”, parents shall
instill tax consciousness in their children at the appropriate age.
​
         SEC. 8. Inculcation of Tax Consciousness in Youth Development Plans. —
Inculcation of tax consciousness, literacy and compliance shall be included in the
Philippine Youth Development Plan and the Local Youth Development Plans of the
Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) councils and the Local Youth Development Councils
(LYDCs).
​
         SEC. 9. Tax Education Committee. — A Tax Education Committee is hereby
created to monitor and ensure the full and strict implementation of this Act. The
Committee shall be headed by the DepEd Secretary as Chairperson, the DoF
secretary as Co-chairperson, and the CHED Chairperson, TESDA Director-General,
and the Chairperson and Chief Executive Officer of the National Youth Commission
(NYC) as members. It shall initially convene within fifteen (15) days from the
effectivity of this Act. The Committee shall submit an annual report on the
implementation of the Act to the Office of the President of the Senate and the Office
of the Speaker of the House of Representatives.
​
         SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — DepEd, in coordination with
the CHED, TESDA, BIR, and BoC, shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR) of this Act within thirty (30) days from its effectivity.

        SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
