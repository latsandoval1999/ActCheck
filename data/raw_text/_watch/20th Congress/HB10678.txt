                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE



        The 1987 Constitution mandates that taxation shall be uniform and equitable, and
that Congress shall evolve a progressive system of taxation.1 This bill gives effect to that
constitutional command by updating the individual income tax schedule under Section
24(A)(2) of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended, to reflect present economic
realities and to protect low- and middle-income earners from tax burdens that have
become heavier in real terms.



        Under the Republic Act (“R.A.”) No. 10963 (2017), or the Tax Reform for
Acceleration and Inclusion Act, the first ₱250,000 of annual taxable income was
effectively placed under a zero-percent rate, while the personal income tax schedule was
further reduced beginning January 1, 2023 for individuals earning ₱8,000,000 and below.2
These reforms were intended to increase disposable income and make the tax system
more progressive. However, the ₱250,000 zero-rate threshold has remained fixed despite
the cumulative rise in the cost of living. As of April 2026, the Philippine Statistics

       The effect is felt most sharply by workers and small earners whose income growth
does not necessarily translate to real purchasing power. PSA data show that in 2023, the
average annual income of Filipino families was ₱353,230.00, while average annual family
expenditure reached ₱258,050.00.4 At the same time, minimum wage rates remain
calibrated regionally, with the National Capital Region’s current daily minimum wage
ranging from ₱755.00 to ₱780.00, while Western Visayas rates range from ₱520.00 to
₱550.00.5 These figures show that many Filipino workers and families continue to operate
within narrow margins, where additional take-home pay directly supports food,
transportation, utilities, education, rent, medicines, and other basic household needs.



       This bill therefore proposes to increase the income tax exemption ceiling from
₱250,000 to ₱350,000 annually, and correspondingly recalibrates the graduated tax
brackets. The measure also updates the optional eight percent tax regime for qualified
purely self-employed individuals and professionals by allowing the eight percent tax to
apply only to gross sales or receipts and other non-operating income in excess of
₱350,000, instead of ₱250,000. This preserves parity between compensation earners and
qualified self-employed individuals, while maintaining the simplified tax option for
small professionals and micro-entrepreneurs whose gross receipts remain below the VAT
threshold.

the point where income becomes taxable, ensuring that subsistence and modest earnings

       SEC 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance shall, upon the recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to
implement the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.
