                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK,
ENSURING THE SAFETY AND WELL-BEING OF LEARNERS AND SCHOOL

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Every learner has the right to study in an environment that is safe, healthy, inclusive, and
supportive. Schools should not only provide quality education but also protect learners and school
personnel from violence, abuse, bullying, discrimination, harassment, disasters, health
emergencies, and other threats to their safety and well-being.

        The continuing prevalence of violence and bullying among children underscores the need
to strengthen school safety measures. The 2015 National Baseline Study on Violence Against
Children, conducted by the Council for the Welfare of Children and UNICEF, found that 65% of
Filipino children and youth aged 13 to 24 experienced peer violence, while 64.2%
experienced physical violence and 61.5% experienced psychological violence during
childhood. More recently, the Department of Education reported 2,500 bullying cases in the
National Capital Region during School Year 2024–2025, demonstrating that bullying and
violence continue to pose significant concerns within the school environment.

       These circumstances show that school safety must go beyond physical security. It must also
address the mental, emotional, psychosocial, and digital well-being of learners and school
personnel. With the increasing use of digital technologies, schools must likewise be prepared to
address cyberbullying, online abuse, cybersecurity risks, and other emerging threats.

        This measure seeks to institutionalize a Comprehensive School Safety Framework for
public and private basic education institutions. It provides common standards for the prevention of
risks, preparedness for emergencies, response to incidents, and recovery and support for affected
members of the school community.

        Under the proposed measure, every school shall develop a School Safety Plan and
maintain a School Safety Handbook containing appropriate procedures for emergency response,
learner protection, reporting and referral, mental health and psychosocial support, digital safety,
disaster preparedness, and off-campus activities.

       The bill also strengthens the roles of school personnel and promotes coordination among
the Department of Education, Department of Health, Department of Social Welfare and
Development, Department of the Interior and Local Government, Philippine National Police,
local government units, and other concerned agencies and stakeholders. It further provides for
appropriate safety infrastructure, risk assessments, emergency drills, disaster preparedness, and
mechanisms for addressing bullying, abuse, violence, discrimination, and other threats.
        By establishing a unified and preventive approach to school safety, this measure seeks to
ensure that schools are equipped not only to respond to incidents, but also to identify and prevent
risks before they result in harm.

         Ultimately, a safe learning environment is essential to quality education. Protecting
learners and school personnel from violence, hazards, and other threats will allow schools to better
fulfill their fundamental role in developing healthy, capable, and resilient Filipino children and
youth.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                                TITLE I

                                       GENERAL PROVISION

             SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “ Comprehensive
      School Safety Act”.

              SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall protect and promote the right of all
      learners and school personnel to an enabling, safe, healthy, and supportive learning and
      working environment where all learners and school personnel are protected and secured
      from any form of abuse, violence, or harm.

              The State recognizes that health promotion and healthy learning environments are
      integral to school safety, and shall therefore promote conditions, policies, practices, and
      environments that prevent harm, support physical, mental, and social well-being, and
      enable learners and school personnel to thrive.

              This policy shall be consistent with the Philippines' commitment to attaining the
      Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 4 (Quality Education), Target
      4.a, which calls for education facilities that are child, disability, and gender sensitive and
      provide safe, non-violent, inclusive, and effective learning environments for all, and SDG
      16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions), Target 16.2, which calls for an end to abuse,
      exploitation, trafficking, and all forms of violence against and torture of children.

             To this end, the State shall:

              (a) Adopt a comprehensive framework that fosters a whole-of-society and
      all-hazards approach the development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of
      school safety policies, programs, and plans;

               (b) Institutionalize an integrated school safety system for basic education
      institutions that prevents, responds to, or recovers from threats and other untoward
      incidents;
       (c) Strengthen coordination among the Department of Education (DepEd), national
government agencies, local government units (LUs), and other community stakeholders in
the implementation of school safety measures;

       (d) Ensure the provision of adequate human resources, infrastructure, equipment,
technology, financial support, and other resources necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act; and

       (e) Promote learner-centered, fully inclusive, evidence-based, and developmentally
appropriate approaches in the design and implementation of school safety programs and
interventions.

       SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public and private
elementary and secondary schools and other basic education institutions as may be covered
by the DepEd.

       The provisions of this Act shall apply to all learners, school personnel, and other
members of the school community, and encompass measures for the prevention of,
response to, and recovery from hazards and threats to their safety and well-being within
school premises or during school activities, whether conducted on or off campus, including
those conducted through or involving digital platforms and technologies.

       Acts such as violence, child abuse, neglect, exploitation, bullying, cyber-bullying,
online abuse, cybersecurity risks, other forms of digital harm, discrimination, harassment,
gender-based violence, corporal punishment, and retaliation committed by students or
school personnel against students or school personnel are prohibited.

        For purposes of this Act, school safety encompasses the protection and promotion
of the physical, mental, emotional, psychosocial, and digital safety and well-being of
learners, school personnel, and other members of the school community, as well as the
prevention and appropriate management of violence, abuse, bullying, discrimination,
harassment, exploitation, emergencies, disasters, and other threats to their safety and
well-being.

                                    TITLE II

             SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK AND POLICIES

                                  CHAPTER I

                      SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Comprehensive School Safety
Framework. - The DepEd, in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH),
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), Philippine National Police (PNP), LGUs, National Youth
Commission (NYC), and other relevant government stakeholders, shall establish, maintain,
and periodically update a National Comprehensive School Safety Framework (the
Framework) to guide the implementation of this Act in all schools.

        The Framework shall prescribe minimum standards for the prevention of, response
to, or recovery from threats and incidents relating to school safety harmonizing and
integrating for this purpose existing laws, policies, and mechanisms on the same. Such
standards shall include:

       (a) The development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of School Safety
Plans and School Safety Handbooks;

       (b) ﻿School safety personnel, staffing guidelines, competency standards, and
capacity building;
      (c) ﻿School safety protocols, infrastructure, facilities, equipment, and security
measures;

       (d) ﻿Learner protection, violence prevention, and anti-bullying mechanisms;

       (e) ﻿Digital safety, responsible technology use, and cybersecurity;

       (f) Standards for safe, responsible, and ethical reporting and journalism on
school-related incidents and mass tragedies;

      (g) Learner well-being, mental health promotion, counseling services, and
psychosocial support;

      (h) Emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction and management, public health
emergencies, crisis response and recovery, and business continuity;

       (i) Periodic engagement with parents and other community stakeholders;

        (j) Meaningful participation and empowerment of learners and the youth in matters
affecting school safety and well-being;

        (k) Reporting school safety concerns such as those involving school bullying and
other forms of violence;

       (l) Detection of health and safety concerns, as well as assessment, referral, case
management, and inter-agency coordination to address health and safety concerns and
mitigate risk;

       (m) Monitoring, evaluation, reporting, and continuous improvement; and

       (n) Such other standards as may be necessary to effectively implement this Act.

       The DepEd, in consultation with other stakeholders, shall develop and periodically
update policies, templates, manuals, protocols, reporting forms, training modules,
assessment tools, and other implementation resources necessary to assist schools in
complying with this Act.

        The Framework shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years, or as may be
necessary, to ensure that it remains responsive to new and emerging challenges in school
safety.

        SEC. 5. Establishment of School Safety Plans. - Every school shall develop and
update every three (3) years, or as may be necessary, a School Safety Plan that adopts the
Framework to the school's unique needs, risks, and resources, emphasizing the safety,
protection, and wellbeing of learners, as well as school personnel.

        The School Safety Plan shall be harmonized with other school plans related to
safety, school improvement, Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM), child
protection, and healthy learning institutions, among others.

       The Plan shall include:

       (a) School safety standards, including standards for prevention, response, and
recovery;

        (b) Protocols and manuals for emergencies, including hotlines for reporting,
designated hospitals, police stations, safety zones, evacuation areas, referrals for mental
health needs, and a fixed-timeline referral pathway for serious threats and abuse;

       (c) Clear roles and responsibilities of members of the school community;

       (d) ﻿Protective safety measures, including security infrastructure and equipment;
      (e) ﻿Items identified as strictly prohibited in school premises, including the
management protocols for confiscation of such items;

        (f) Policies on safe, responsible, developmentally appropriate, and healthy use of
digital technologies, including gadgets, social media, gaming, artificial intelligence, and
other emerging technologies, as well as measures addressing digital safety, cybersecurity,
and digital wellness, on and off campus;

        (g) Clear mechanisms for recovery, such as intervention and reintegration plans for
learners and affected personnel, coordination referrals to government agencies, and
post-incident reviews;

       (h) Guidelines and protocols for off-campus activities;

       (i) Community engagement and partnership strategies to strengthen school safety,
including coordination with parents and guardians, LGUs, community organizations, health
and mental health service providers, security and emergency response groups, and other
relevant stakeholders; and

       (j) Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.

       The School Safety Plan shall be developed in consultation with the school
community, including school personnel, learners, parents or guardians, and other relevant
stakeholders.

        The School Head shall oversee the implementation of the School Safety Plan within
the school. The School Head shall submit the Plan to the Schools Division Office (SDO)
for review and approval every three (3) years or in case of material revisions thereto. The
SDO shall ensure that the Plan is aligned with the Framework and complies with the
minimum standards set forth in this Act. Upon approval, the Plan shall be presented to the
Local School Board to facilitate coordination with the LGU and inform the development
and updating of relevant local plans and programs.

        SEC. 6. School Safety Handbook. - Every school shall prepare, maintain, and
regularly update a School Safety Handbook, as part of the school's Learner Handbook, to
communicate essential school safety information to learners, parents or guardians, school
personnel, visitors, and other members of the school community.

       The School Safety Handbook shall present, in a clear, concise, accessible,

child-sensitive and user-friendly manner, the school safety policies, procedures, emergency

information, and learner support services that members of the school community need to

know to promote safety and respond appropriately during emergencies and other school

safety situations. It shall be made available in Filipino and prevailing regional languages.

       At a minimum, the School Safety Handbook shall contain:

       (a) The school's commitment to safety and learner well-being;

       (b) The School Code of Conduct and key school safety policies for learners and

school personnel;

       (c) Procedures for reporting bullying, violence, abuse, discrimination, harassment,

emergencies, and other school safety concerns;
        (d) Confidentiality protocols and procedures to protect learner identity and privacy
and to handle information, in accordance with data privacy laws;

       (e) Prohibited items in school premises, including confiscation and management
protocols;

       (f) Guidelines for securely documenting and recording cases and maintaining legal
compliance;

      (g) Referral and escalation mechanisms to relevant agencies, including criteria,
documentation, and follow-up;

       (h) Roles, responsibilities, and functions of school leaders, teachers, guidance
counselors, other school officials, learners, and parents;

      (i) Emergency procedures for fire, earthquakes, severe weather, lockdowns, medical
emergencies, evacuations, and other applicable situations;

       (j) Emergency contact information for school officials, emergency responders,
designated hospitals, and relevant government agencies;

        (k) School maps showing evacuation routes, emergency exits, assembly and
evacuation areas, clinics, School Care Centers, guidance offices, nearest hospitals, and
other important locations;

       (1) Visitor management procedures and campus access guidelines;

       (m) Learner support services, including counseling, psychosocial support, referral
services, and School Care Centers;

         (n) Digital safety guidelines, digital wellness, and responsible technology use,
including developmentally appropriate use of digital devices, social media, online gaming,
artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies; healthy digital habits; prevention of
digital harms; and pathways for help-seeking and referral when technology use adversely
affects learner health, development, education, relationships, or daily functioning; and

      (o) Such other practical school safety information as may be prescribed by the
DepEd.

        The School Safety Handbook shall be made readily available in printed and
electronic formats, and shall be disseminated to learners, parents or guardians, school
personnel, and other members of the school community at the beginning of every school
year and whenever significant revisions are made.

       Schools shall also conduct an annual orientation on the School Safety Handbook to
ensure that members of the school community understand their rights, responsibilities, and
the procedures for preventing and responding to safety concerns.

        The School Safety Handbook shall be reviewed periodically in consultation with
learners, parents or guardians, school personnel, and other relevant stakeholders.

                                   CHAPTER II

                         SCHOOL SAFETY POLICIES

        SEC. 7. Policy on Safe School Culture. - Every school shall foster a safe,
inclusive, respectful, and supportive school culture that cultivates socio-emotional learning,
empathy, peer connectedness, and the preservation of human dignity, in accordance with
applicable DepEd and DOH standards on the social and physical environment of schools.
Schools shall promote positive relationships, mutual respect, responsibility, resilience, and
meaningful participation among all members of the school community through preventive,
developmentally appropriate, and evidence-based policies, programs, and practices that
strengthen positive behavior, respectful conflict resolution, and a shared commitment to
maintaining a safe learning environment.

        SEC. 8. Policy Against Violence and Harm. - Every school shall adopt and enforce
a policy against all forms of violence and harm, including physical violence, child abuse,
exploitation, bullying, discrimination, harassment, sexual and gender-based violence,
corporal punishment, retaliation, and other acts that threaten the safety, dignity, or
well-being of learners and school personnel, whether occurring in person or through digital
or other means. Violence or harmful conduct perpetrated by school personnel against
learners shall be subject to applicable child protection laws and appropriate administrative,
disciplinary, and legal measures. Incidents of violence and harmful conduct involving
learners shall be promptly addressed by the school through interventions that are fair,
proportionate, developmentally appropriate, trauma-informed, and restorative, with due
regard to the safety and rights of all persons involved.

         Schools shall prohibit the establishment, operation, recruitment, and initiation
activities of fraternities and sororities, in accordance with Republic Act No. 11053 or the
"Anti-Hazing Act of 2018", and shall only promote the participation of learners in duly
recognized school organizations that foster safe spaces, camaraderie, inclusion, leadership,
and respect.

        SEC. 9. Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy. - Pursuant to Republic Act
No. 10627 or the "Anti-Bullying Act of 2013", every school shall adopt and implement a
comprehensive Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy that promotes the safety,
dignity, rights, and well-being of all learners and ensures the prevention, early
identification, prompt reporting, timely intervention, and appropriate resolution of
incidents involving bullying, cyber-bullying, harassment, including technology-facilitated
harassment and abuse, violence, child abuse, neglect, exploitation, discrimination,
impersonation, non-consensual sharing of personal or intimate content, retaliation, and
other acts that threaten the safety or welfare of learners. The Policy shall provide for
prompt mental health and psychosocial assessment, support, and appropriate intervention
for learners affected by bullying, including both the subject of bullying and the learner who
has engaged in bullying behavior, in coordination with their respective families and
licensed professionals, as appropriate.

        SEC. 10. School Code of Conduct. - The School Safety Handbook shall include a
School Code of Conduct that promotes a culture of respect, responsibility, integrity,
inclusivity, and accountability among members of the school community. The Code shall
clearly define unacceptable acts, corresponding disciplinary and restorative interventions,
and the rights and responsibilities of all members of the school community. In
implementing the Code, schools shall ensure that disciplinary measures are fair,
proportionate, developmentally appropriate, non-discriminatory, and restorative in nature,
with due regard for the best interests of the child, due process, and the rights of school
personnel.

        SEC. 11. Learner Participation and Voice. - Every school shall recognize and
promote the right of learners to participate meaningfully in matters affecting their safety,
well-being, and school life, consistent with their evolving capacities and the best interests
of the child.

        Schools shall establish age-appropriate, inclusive, which and learner-responsive
mechanisms through which learners may express their views, report safety concerns,
contribute to school safety initiatives, and participate in the development, implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation of school safety policies and programs. Schools shall provide
opportunities for the student council and other recognized learner organizations to
participate in school safety initiatives and such processes.
         Learner participation shall be safe, respectful, and free from discrimination,
retaliation, or intimidation.

        SEC. 12. Consistency with Existing Laws and Policies. - The policies, programs,
standards, and procedures adopted and implemented pursuant to this Act shall be consistent
with existing laws, rules, regulations, and applicable policies of the DepEd and other
relevant agencies on child protection, anti-bullying, positive discipline, mental health,
gender-based violence, safe spaces, learner welfare, and other matters relating to the safety,
dignity, rights, and well-being of learners and school personnel.

                                     TITLE III

                      SCHOOL SAFETY GOVERNANCE

        SEC. 13. School Safety Governance. - Every school shall establish a
comprehensive school safety governance system to ensure the effective implementation of
this Act and to promote a safe, secure, inclusive, supportive, and resilient learning
environment. This may utilize or expand on existing school governance mechanisms, with
clear designation of school safety functions and responsibilities.

       Every school shall organize or designate an appropriate school safety governance
mechanism composed of school personnel responsible for learner protection, counseling
and psychosocial support, school safety and security, health services, disaster risk reduction
and management, and other functions as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        SEC. 14. Key School Safety Personnel. - Every school shall ensure the availability
of qualified school personnel necessary to effectively implement this Act.

        The DepEd shall prescribe staffing standards, competency requirements,
qualifications, deployment guidelines, training, and continuing professional development
requirements for key school safety personnel. Schools shall progressively strengthen the
staffing of key personnel in public schools, consistent with national staffing standards and
subject to the availability of funds. Key school safety personnel may be designated from
among qualified existing school personnel.

      (a) School Head - The School Head shall serve as the overall lead for the
implementation of this Act at the school level, and shall:

       (1) Oversee the implementation of the School Safety Plan;

       (2) Ensure compliance with national school safety standards;

       (3) Provide leadership in fostering a safe, inclusive, and supportive school
environment;

       (4) Allocate school resources to support school safety initiatives;

       (5) Ensure coordination among school personnel, parents, LGUs, concerned
agencies, and relevant stakeholders;

       (6) Oversee incident management and post-incident recovery; and

       (7) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        (b) School Counselor - The School Counselor, consisting of either a registered
guidance counselor under Republic Act No. 9258 or the "Guidance and Counseling Act of
2004" or a registered psychologist under Republic Act No. 10029 or the "Philippine
Psychology Act of 2009", shall head the School Care Centers, deliver school-based mental
health services and learner support programs, and ensure the accessibility of guidance and
psychosocial services pursuant to Republic Act No. 12080 or the "Basic Education Mental
Health and Well-Being Promotion Act': Provided, That when there is no available School
Counselor or School Counselor Associate, the DepEd may assign teachers or other DepEd
personnel, supervised by the Schools Division Counselor, to deliver school-based mental
health services in line with the limitations and conditions of professional practice under
Republic Act No. 12080.

        (c) School Counselor Associates - School Counselor Associates shall assist the
School Counselor in implementing learner support programs and facilitate access to
appropriate guidance and psychosocial services, subject to the conditions of professional
practice prescribed under Republic Act No. 12080. Where a School Counselor is not
available, the School Counselor Associates shall facilitate referral to the Schools Division
Counselor for counseling.

        (d) Learner Formation Officer - Consistent with Republic Act No. 10627, the
Learner Formation Officer shall serve as the primary focal person for learner formation and
first responder to reports of bullying and other learner-related incidents. The Learner
Formation Officer shall:

   (1)​﻿Receive, document, and facilitate the appropriate initial response to reports of
        bullying, peer violence, harassment, and other incidents involving learners;

   (2)​﻿Ensure that reported incidents are promptly referred to the School Head, guidance
        or counseling personnel, School Care Center, Child Protection Committee (CPC),
        or other appropriate authorities, as warranted;

   (3)​Coordinate the implementation of preventive and formative programs that promote
       safe school culture;

   (4)​ Support the implementation of learner-led and peer-based initiatives, including
       peer mentoring, buddy systems, bystander and upstander programs, and other
       learner formation activities;

   (5)​ Promote learners' awareness of their rights and responsibilities, the School Code of
       Conduct, reporting mechanisms, and available protection and support services;

   (6)​ Monitor patterns and recurring concerns involving bullying, peer violence, and
       learner behavior, and recommend appropriate preventive interventions;

   (7)​Regularly monitor actions taken on referrals of incidents of bullying, peer violence,
       and other learner-related incidents; and

   (8)​ Coordinate with classroom advisers, guidance or counseling personnel, parents or
       guardians, and other appropriate school personnel in addressing learner safety and
       formation concerns.

       (e) School Safety Officer - Every school shall designate a School Safety Officer
who shall serve as the principal coordinator for the implementation of school safety
programs under this Act. The School Safety Officer shall:

       (1) Coordinate implementation of the School Safety Plan;

       (2) ﻿Monitor compliance with school safety standards;

       (3) ﻿Coordinate emergency preparedness activities;

       (4) ﻿Conduct campus safety audits and oversee school safety inspections and
preventive maintenance;

       (5) Manage a non-threatening reporting system within the school;

       (6) ﻿Coordinate reporting and documentation;
       (7) ﻿Facilitate coordination with government agencies and emergency responders;

       (8) ﻿Support school safety training and drills; and

       (9)﻿Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        (f) School Health Personnel - School nurses, physicians, dentists, and other
qualified health personnel shall provide health services and support school health and
wellness programs consistent with existing laws and DepEd policies.

       They shall:

   (1)​Provide appropriate school health services;

   (2)​﻿Participate in emergency preparedness and response;

   (3)​ Support mental health and psychosocial interventions;

   (4)​) Assist in health promotion and disease prevention activities;

   (5)​Coordinate referrals to health facilities;

   (6)​﻿Coordinate and report to the LGUs cases of disease outbreaks in the school; and

   (7)​Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        School health personnel should be trained to recognize and identify: physical or
somatic complaints, including recurrent headaches, abdominal pain, sleep difficulties;
tendencies for violence and self-harm that are possible signs of bullying, anxiety, or abuse;
and signs of psychological distress, and facilitate referral to the School Counselor, other
qualified school personnel, or appropriate health or mental health professionals.

        Where qualified school health personnel are not available on-site, schools shall
establish linkages with health and mental health professionals and facilities, including
through teleconsultation or other arrangements with DOH-retained or accredited facilities.

        (g) Security Personnel - Security guards and other authorized security personnel
shall help maintain a safe and a secure school environment. They shall:

       (1)﻿Regulate access to school premises;

       (2) ﻿Implement visitor management procedures;

       (3) ﻿Assist in emergency response;

       (4) ﻿Report security incidents;

       (5) ﻿Support the implementation of campus safety measures; and

       (6) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        Schools shall have adequate security guards or trained security personnel, taking
into consideration school size, enrollment, operating hours, location, and security risk.

       In accordance with applicable DILG issuances, LGUs may extend support through
the deployment of barangay tanods or local law enforcement to secure the vicinity and
immediate public perimeters of schools, subject to local risk assessment, capacity, and the
availability of local funds, including the Special Education Fund (SEF), as may be
authorized under applicable laws.

        (h) Classroom Adviser - The Classroom Adviser shall promote learner well-being
within the classroom and serve as the primary point of contact for learners and parents on
routine learner welfare concerns. The Classroom Adviser shall:
           (1)​ Monitor learner well-being and classroom climate;

           (2)​ Be trained in identifying signs of psychological distress such as self-harm
               or violent behavior;

           (3)​ Identify learners requiring additional support based on indicators such as
               chronic or sudden absence, repeated tardiness, withdrawal, and abrupt
               decline in participation or performance, among others;

           (4)​ Coordinate with the School Counselor and other school personnel on
               appropriate interventions;

           (5)​Maintain regular and periodic communication with parents or guardians
               regarding learner welfare;

           (6)​﻿Promote positive discipline and learner participation; and

           (7)​ Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        Recognizing their critical role in safeguarding the safety and well-being of learners,
every Classroom Adviser shall be provided with the commensurate teaching loads
dedicated exclusively to learner safety and well-being responsibilities, including learner
monitoring, parent engagement, case documentation, home visitation, when necessary,
participation in case conferences, implementation of interventions, and coordination with
other school personnel for student safety.

        The DepEd shall ensure that the corresponding teaching load reduction, staffing
requirements, and funding necessary to implement this provision are reflected in its
staffing standards and annual appropriations.

        (i) Student Leaders - Student leaders, including student council officers and
recognized learner organizations, shall support the promotion of a safe, inclusive, and
respectful school environment. They shall:

       (1) Assist in raising awareness on school safety policies and reporting mechanisms;

       (2) Promote positive behavior, peer support, and responsible bystander action;

       (3) Help identify and communicate learner safety concerns to appropriate school
personnel;

        (4) Be trained to recognize peers experiencing safety or psychosocial concerns,
refer them to appropriate school personnel or support services, and encourage them to seek
assistance; and

       (5) Participate in the planning and implementation of school safety initiatives.
Student leaders shall not be tasked with investigating incidents, imposing disciplinary
measures, or performing functions that are the responsibility of authorized school
personnel.

       SEC. 15. Responsibilities of Government Agencies. - For the effective
implementation of this Act, the concerned government agencies shall be responsible for the
following:
       (a) DepEd - The DepEd shall serve as the lead agency responsible for the
implementation of this Act and shall exercise overall policy direction, coordination,
supervision, monitoring, and evaluation of the Framework.

       In addition to its responsibilities under other applicable laws, the DepEd shall:

           (1)​Formulate, harmonize, and periodically update national policies, standards,
               guidelines, and operational manuals for school safety;
           (2)​Prescribe minimum standards for school safety infrastructure, equipment,
               staffing, programs, and services;

           (3)​ Provide schools with technical assistance, capacity building, and
               implementation support;

           (4)​ Develop a model School Safety Plan, School Safety Handbooks, reporting
               systems, and other implementation tools;

           (5)​ Establish national systems for school safety monitoring, reporting, and
               evaluation;

           (6)​Provide financial and technical support to strengthen school safety
               implementation;

           (7)​ Coordinate with relevant government agencies and stakeholders;

           (8)​Develop and periodically update, in coordination with relevant government
               agencies and stakeholders, national standards and operational guidelines on
               digital safety and responsible technology use in schools; and

           (9)​Perform such other functions as may be necessary to implement this Act.

           (b) DOH - The DOH shall serve as the technical authority for health, health
       promotion, health services, mental health promotion, disease prevention, and
       healthy school environments, consistent with its mandate. It shall support the
       DepEd in the implementation and strengthening of school mental health and
       psychosocial services, and in supporting emergency medical preparedness. The
       DOH shall provide guidance in establishing referral mechanisms to DOH-retained
       hospitals, facilities, and health professionals.

          (c) DSWD - The DSWD shall provide technical assistance and support to schools
in handling cases involving children in conflict with the law and victims of abuse. It shall
also assist in providing psychosocial support and rehabilitation services to affected
learners, families, and personnel, and shall coordinate with schools for the proper case
management and referral of incidents.

       (d) DILG - The DILG shall facilitate coordination between schools and LGUs and
encourage local support for school safety initiatives.

       (e) PNP - The PNP shall assist schools in maintaining campus security and in
responding to incidents of violence and other threats. It shall coordinate with schools and
LGUs for the deployment of security personnel, the conduct of security audits, and the
enforcement of laws on the prohibition of deadly weapons in school premises.

        (f) LGUs - LGUs shall support implementation of school safety programs,
encourage schools to participate in local emergency planning, strengthen community
partnerships, and assist schools in disaster preparedness and emergency response,
consistent with their respective mandates and available resources. They shall also
coordinate with the schools in the implementation of community diversion programs
relative to children in conflict with the law.

       LGUs may also provide security personnel equipment to schools, which may be
funded through the school's SEF or other authorized local funds.

        The DepEd may enter into partnerships with other government agencies, state
universities and colleges, civil society organizations, professional organizations, and the
private sector to strengthen implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 16. Community Engagement. - Schools shall promote meaningful
participation among learners, parents or guardians, families, LGUs, leagues of LGUs, civil
society organizations, community organizations, youth organizations, faith-based
organizations, and other stakeholders in the planning, implementation, monitoring, and
continuous improvement of school safety programs.

       The DepEd shall encourage community participation and partnerships, which shall
complement and strengthen school-based efforts to create safe, secure, inclusive,
supportive, and resilient learning environments. Schools shall establish appropriate
mechanisms to ensure that stakeholders are regularly informed, consulted, and engaged in
school safety initiatives.

        (a) Parents and Guardians - Parents and legal guardians are recognized as essential
partners of schools in promoting the safety, well-being, and holistic development of
learners. Schools shall conduct a mandatory orientation session for parents and guardians
on the School Safety Plan, safety protocols, and other pertinent information regarding
school safety. Parents and guardians shall be encouraged to support the implementation of
school safety policies, reinforce positive behavior and learner well-being at home,
promptly communicate safety concerns to the school, participate in school safety
orientations, consultations, and emergency preparedness activities, and cooperate with
school authorities in implementing interventions affecting their children.

        Schools shall establish mechanisms to ensure meaningful parent and guardian
participation in the implementation of the Framework through regular communication,
consultation, and collaborative interventions.

        (b) Parents-Teachers Association (PTA) - The PTA shall serve as the primary
partner organization of the school in promoting school safety and learner well-being.

        The PTA shall participate in the development, implementation, and periodic review
of the School Safety Plan, assist in promoting school safety awareness among parents and
families, support school programs on school safety, assist the school in mobilizing
community participation and volunteer support for school safety initiatives, and provide
feedback on school safety concerns affecting learners and parents, subject to guidelines
prescribed by the DepEd.

        (c) Child Protection Committee (CPC) - Every school shall establish a CPC,
composed of the School Head, School Safety Officer, Learner Formation Officer, and
designated representatives of learners, teachers, parents, and the community. The CPC
shall support the school in the prevention of and response to child abuse, violence,
exploitation, discrimination, bullying, and other child protection concerns. The CPC shall
assist in the implementation of child protection policies and preventive programs; facilitate
the appropriate handling, referral, and follow-up of child protection concerns; coordinate
with relevant school personnel, parents or guardians, the Barangay Council for the
Protection of Children (BCPC), the City or Municipal Social Welfare and Development
Office (C/MSWDO), and other appropriate authorities; and recommend measures to
strengthen the school's child protection mechanisms.

        The establishment or functioning of a CPC shall not relieve the School Head or any
other responsible school personnel of their duties and accountability under this Act and
other applicable child protection laws, rules, and policies.

                                     TITLE IV

                                  PREVENTION

                                   CHAPTER I

SCHOOL SAFETY INFRASTRUCTURE AND PROTECTIVE MEASURES
       SEC. 17. Safety Infrastructure and Equipment. – Schools shall be equipped with
appropriate safety infrastructure and security equipment, including, as applicable:

           (a) Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) – CCTV cameras shall be installed in
common areas, entrances and exits, perimeter areas, and other locations identified through
the school’s risk assessment but shall not be installed in areas where there is a reasonable
expectation of privacy. Designated and authorized personnel shall monitor the CCTV
system during school hours and shall promptly identify and address incidents requiring
immediate attention. The collection, storage, access, use, and disposal of CCTV footage
shall comply with applicable data privacy laws and regulations and shall be used solely to
investigate documented bullying or security incidents;

        (b) Screening Equipment – Schools shall have access to appropriate screening
equipment, including metal detectors, where warranted by the school’s risk profile.
Screening shall be conducted by authorized and appropriately trained personnel in a
reasonable, proportionate, non-discriminatory, and child-sensitive manner;

         (c) Visitor Management System – Schools shall establish appropriate systems to
regulate and monitor visitor entry into and movement within school premises, including
visitor identification, registration, verification, and access control;

         (d) Security Personnel and Protective Presence – Schools shall ensure appropriate
security and protective presence based on their size, enrollment, location, and risk profile.
This may include licensed security guards and coordinated support from the PNP, barangay
tanods, safety officers, community volunteers, and other authorized personnel; and

        (e) Perimeter and Access Security – Schools shall maintain appropriate physical
measures to secure school boundaries and regulate access, including, as appropriate, gates,
fencing, lighting, locks, barriers, and other protective infrastructure.

       SEC. 18. Emergency, Health, and Life-Safety Equipment. – Schools shall be
equipped with appropriate emergency, health, and life-safety equipment, including, as
applicable:

        (a) first aid kits and essential emergency medical supplies;

        (b) fire extinguishers and other required fire safety equipment;

         (c) emergency lighting, flashlights, batteries, emergency power supplies, and other
disaster preparedness supplies;

        (d) rescue and evacuation equipment appropriate to identified hazards; and

        (e) emergency communication and alert systems, including public address systems,
alarms, emergency notification systems, two-way radios, and other equipment.

          All equipment under this section shall be regularly inspected, tested, and
maintained, and shall be replaced or replenished as needed. The results of such inspections
shall be documented and included in the annual School Safety Audit under Section 19 of
this Act.

       SEC. 19. Preventive Safety Measures. – Every school shall implement preventive
safety measures appropriate to its circumstances to reduce risks, strengthen preparedness,
and promote a safe and secure learning environment. At a minimum, schools shall
implement:

       (a) Risk Mapping and Assessment – Regularly identify physical, social, behavioral,
environmental, and digital risks, including locations and circumstances that may increase
the likelihood of violence, bullying, abuse, harassment, accidents, emergencies, digital
harm, harmful or unsafe technology use, or other threats to learner safety, health,
development, and well-being. The findings shall guide the development and
implementation of the School Safety Plan and appropriate preventive interventions;

        (b) Campus Security Monitoring – Maintain appropriate monitoring measures,
including security patrols, monitoring of entrances and exits, observation of common and
high-risk areas, and review of security camera footage where available, consistent with the
school’s risk profile and applicable privacy and child protection laws;

        (c) Visitor Screening – Establish procedures for visitor identification, registration,
verification, issuance of passes where appropriate, and monitoring of visitor access, while
ensuring access for emergency responders and other authorized persons;

        (d) Vehicle Inspection – All motor vehicles entering the campus shall be subject to
plain-view inspection. Drivers and riders shall present a valid identification or driver’s
license, open all compartments for inspection, and, in the case of motorcycles, remove
helmets and masks. Dangerous or prohibited items shall be surrendered for safekeeping at a
designated secure location. Licensed firearms shall be unloaded and surrendered with the
required permits and may be reclaimed upon exit. Visitors who refuse inspection shall be
denied entry, while learners and personnel who fail to comply shall be subject to applicable
administrative sanctions;

      ​(e) Mandatory Screening and Inspection – Conduct mandatory screening of persons
and belongings, including bag inspections or use of metal detectors, where authorized and
warranted by the school’s risk assessment and applicable DepEd standards. Screening shall
be conducted by authorized personnel in a respectful, non-discriminatory, child-sensitive,
and developmentally appropriate manner;

   ​    (f) Confiscation and Disposition of Prohibited Items – Establish procedures for the
identification, confiscation, documentation, safekeeping, turnover, and lawful disposition
of prohibited items, including appropriate due process, notification of parents or guardians
when necessary, and coordination with authorities when required by law;

     ​ (g) Emergency Drills – Regularly conduct developmentally appropriate,
trauma-informed, and inclusive drills for fires, earthquakes, severe weather, medical
emergencies, active attacks, security incidents, and other relevant hazards. Such drills shall
be designed to prepare learners and school personnel to respond appropriately to
emergencies while minimizing unnecessary fear or distress and shall be conducted with
appropriate advance notice and consultation with relevant professionals;

  ​    (h) Safety Orientation and Awareness – Provide developmentally appropriate and
role-appropriate safety orientation for learners, school personnel, parents and guardians,
and other members of the school community. These orientations shall cover the Framework
and the School Safety Plan, protocols for prevention from, response to, and recovery from
school safety threats and incidents, digital safety, and responsible technology use;

    ​    (i) Safety Audits and Preventive Maintenance – Conduct an annual School Safety
Audit and regular inspections of infrastructure, facilities, equipment, emergency systems,
and other safety measures, and implement corrective and preventive measures based on
identified hazards and deficiencies. The findings shall guide the updating of the School
Safety Plan and the prioritization of resources; and

   ​ (j) Incident Tracking – Schools shall maintain a centralized database recording all
reported incidents, actions taken, and outcomes to ensure transparency, timely follow-ups,
and accountability. The database shall include, but not be limited to:

       (1) Number of incidents;


       (2) Nature of incidents;
       (3) Number of learners involved;


       (4) Setting (time, date, location);


       (5) Actions taken post-incident;


       (6) Preventive measures implemented;


       (7) Learner details (age, grade, address, parents’ background); and


       (8) Current case status.


        SEC. 20. General Standards for School Safety Measures. – All school safety
policies, infrastructure, equipment, and measures under this Chapter shall be appropriate to
the school’s size, enrollment, location, risk profile, specific needs, and subject to the
availability of funds.

       The DepEd, in coordination with relevant national government agencies and other
stakeholders, shall establish national standards and operational guidelines for the provision,
implementation, use, maintenance, and monitoring of infrastructure and equipment under
this Chapter, including, where applicable, their installation, inspection, replacement, and
data management and disposal.

       SEC. 21. Nature- and Climate-Related School Safety Protocols. – The DepEd, in
coordination with the DILG, DOST, DOH, Department of Environment and Natural
Resources, DPWH and other relevant agencies, shall establish and periodically update
national policies for the assessment, management, risk-reduction and recovery from nature-
and climate-related hazards affecting schools. These standards shall cover extreme heat
conditions, tropical cyclones, flooding, earthquakes, air quality, and other related hazards
that may endanger the safety of learners and school personnel.

        The policy shall include a systemic plan for the construction of disaster-resilient
infrastructure, and assessment and prioritization for retrofitting or replacement of unsafe
building structures, taking into consideration the nature- and climate-related hazards of the
area.

                                    CHAPTER II

               SAFETY DURING OFF-CAMPUS ACTIVITIES

        SEC. 22. Safety During Off-Campus Activities. – Schools conducting authorized
off-campus activities shall adopt and implement measures to ensure the safety, security,
health, and well-being of learners and school personnel participating in such activities.
Schools shall take reasonable and appropriate measures to prevent foreseeable harm to
learners and school personnel throughout the activity.

        Prior to any authorized off-campus activity, the school shall conduct an appropriate
risk assessment and prepare an activity-specific safety plan, which shall include learner
supervision arrangements and designated personnel-in-charge, transportation safety
measures, emergency communication and response procedures, emergency contact
information for participating learners, and such other safety measures as may be prescribed
by the DepEd.

       Schools shall also ensure full disclosure to parents or guardians of information
regarding the activity, including the objectives of the itinerary, transport arrangements,
accommodations, identified risks, emergency procedures, and contact information of
responsible personnel. Schools shall likewise ensure that reasonable safety equipment is
made available during the activity, and that appropriate informed consent is secured in
accordance with applicable laws and DepEd policies.

         The DepEd shall prescribe minimum operational standards governing educational
tours, work immersion, athletic competitions, cultural activities, community engagement
activities, and other authorized off-campus activities.

                                  CHAPTER III

       DIGITAL SAFETY AND RESPONSIBLE TECHNOLOGY USE

       SEC. 23. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use. – Schools shall adopt
and implement comprehensive policies and programs to promote digital wellness and
responsible technology engagement, and to ensure the safe, responsible, ethical,
age-appropriate use of digital technologies in teaching, learning, communication, and
school operations, consistent with the best interests and developmental needs of learners.

       Schools shall establish measures to protect learners and school personnel from
cyber-bullying, online abuse, cybersecurity risks, and other forms of digital harm. Such
measures shall include, the prevention and early identification of digital-related harm and
harmful or unsafe technology use, appropriate intervention and referral mechanisms, media
and information literacy programs, orientation programs for parents and guardians,
procedures for reporting and responding to digital safety incidents, and other digital safety
measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        Implementation of this section shall be consistent with applicable laws on child
protection, data privacy, cybersecurity, and online safety.

        SEC. 24. Prohibition on Mobile Devices and Other Electronic Gadgets. – Schools
and Community Learning Centers shall prohibit the use within school premises of mobile
devices and other portable electronic gadgets by learners and personnel during instructional
hours, and by teachers during their designated instructional periods, except when necessary
for legitimate educational, health, safety, or school-related purposes. This policy shall be
implemented to minimize distractions and promote a safe, focused, and conducive learning
environment.

Exceptions may be allowed under the following circumstances:

         (a) Learning and Authorized Educational Activities – When the use of such
devices is necessary for classroom presentations, technology-assisted instruction,
class-based learning activities, or reasonable adjustments to accommodate a learner with a
learning disability or difficulty;

         (b) Health and Well-Being – When a learner has a medical or health condition
requiring the use of a mobile phone or other electronic device, as certified by a licensed
physician and documented with the approval of the School Head;

          (c) Emergency, Safety, and Risk Management – When necessary for
communication, coordination, or access to information during emergencies, disasters, or
other safety and risk management situations, including in response to a perceived threat or
danger; and

          (d) School-related Activities Outside School Premises – During field trips,
educational excursions, and other authorized school-related activities conducted outside
school premises.

                                  CHAPTER IV

                           LEARNER WELL-BEING
        SEC. 25. Learner Well-Being and Psychosocial Support. – Schools shall establish
and implement a comprehensive, coordinated, and age-appropriate system for promoting
learner health and well-being, providing mental health services, and ensuring psychosocial
support, consistent with the principles of prevention, early identification, and appropriate
protection of learners. referral, inclusion, and protection of learners.

    ​    (a) Health and Well-Being Promotion – Schools shall promote the physical,
mental, emotional, and social health and well-being of learners through healthy learning
environments, health-promotion policies and practices, health education and skills
development, and measures addressing factors affecting learner health and well-being.
Such measures shall form an integral component of school safety and be guided by
applicable policies of the DepEd and the DOH.

         (b) Mental Health Services – Schools shall facilitate access to appropriate mental
health services, including prevention, early identification, assessment, treatment, referral,
and other professional interventions, in accordance with Republic Act No. 12080 and other
applicable laws, rules, standards, and guidelines. Professional mental health services shall
be provided by appropriately qualified and authorized mental health professionals within
their respective scopes of practice.

​      (c) Psychosocial Support – Schools shall provide or facilitate appropriate
psychosocial support to learners experiencing stress, distress, crisis, trauma, loss, violence,
abuse, displacement, disasters, or other circumstances affecting their well-being. Such
support may include psychological first aid, supportive and responsive school-based
interventions, peer and social support, referral and linkage to appropriate services, and
other non-clinical measures that strengthen learners’ coping, resilience, connectedness, and
sense of safety and belonging. Psychosocial support shall complement, but not replace,
professional mental health services when such services are required.

                                      TITLE V

                                    RESPONSE

       SEC. 26. Incident Reporting, Assessment and Immediate Response. – Every
school shall establish and maintain responsive mechanisms and protocols for the reporting,
assessment, protection interventions, and immediate response to school safety concerns
involving learners, school personnel, and other members of the school community. Such
mechanisms shall form part of the School Safety Plan and shall be implemented in
accordance with this Act and applicable laws.

        Schools shall ensure that the implementation of this section is guided by the
principles of child protection and the best interests of the child, confidentiality and respect
for privacy, timely intervention, trauma-informed and developmentally appropriate
practices, due process, coordinated inter-agency response, and survivor-centered,
rights-based, and restorative approaches, where appropriate.

        Schools shall ensure the availability of multiple reporting channels, which may
include in-person reporting, anonymous reporting systems, digital reporting platforms,
designated reporting officers, emergency hotlines, and such other reporting mechanisms as
may be prescribed by the DepEd.

Upon receipt of a report, the school shall:


       (a) Immediately record the report;


       (b) Conduct an initial risk assessment;
       (c) Determine the necessity of immediate protective measures;


       (d) Notify the School Head and appropriate school personnel;


       (e) Notify parents or guardians, where appropriate;


       (f) Activate emergency response procedures when necessary;


       (g) Coordinate with the PNP, LGU, and other relevant local authorities in cases

involving violence, imminent threats to safety or security, and other emergencies;


       (h) Document actions taken; and


       (i) Determine the propriety of referral or case management.


         Where the initial risk assessment identifies indicators of suicide risk, self-harm,
active psychosis, or imminent danger to self or others, the school shall immediately
activate emergency protocols and refer the learner for same-day psychiatric evaluation, in
coordination with parents or guardians and the nearest DOH-accredited mental health
facility or service provider.

        Every school shall ensure that all members of the school community are informed
of the reporting mechanisms, available support services, and emergency procedures
established under this Act. Schools shall likewise ensure that reporting mechanisms are
accessible to learners with disabilities and other learners requiring additional support.

       At the beginning of the school year, every school shall maintain and update
emergency contact information and establish procedures for the immediate notification of
parents or guardians, emergency responders, LGUs, and other appropriate authorities
during emergencies and critical incidents.

         SEC. 27. Referral, Case Management, and Coordination. – Every school shall
establish a formal case management and referral system for learners requiring continuing
intervention and support.

The system shall provide for:

       (a) Designation of case managers, where appropriate;

       (b) Multidisciplinary case conferences involving relevant school personnel;

       (c) Counseling and psychosocial interventions;

        (d) The establishment of a tiered referral pathway to hospitals, mental health and
well-being offices, health professionals, psychologists, psychiatrists, social workers, child
protection authorities, law enforcement agencies, or other service providers, consistent
with Republic Act No. 12080 and other applicable laws and policies;

       (e) Development of individualized intervention plans, where appropriate;

       (f) Regular monitoring and follow-up;

       (g) Coordination with parents or guardians;

       (h) Documentation and secure case records; and
       (i) Safe reintegration into the school community.

        The DepEd shall establish standardized case management forms, referral protocols,
documentation procedures, and inter-agency coordination mechanisms to promote
consistency across schools.

       Schools shall coordinate with the DSWD, the Department of Justice, the PNP, and
LGUs for the proper handling of cases, including children in conflict with the law, and for
the provision of medical, legal, and psychosocial support.

                                    TITLE VI

                                    RECOVERY

        SEC. 28. School Recovery. – Schools shall establish and implement recovery and
reintegration measures for learners, school personnel, and other affected members of the
school community.

        Recovery measures shall aim to restore safety, promote healing, minimize
disruption to learning, strengthen resilience, and facilitate the safe return of affected
persons to school.

Schools shall, at a minimum:

        (a) Provide timely counseling, psychosocial support, psychological first aid, and
other appropriate interventions;

       (b) Develop individualized intervention and reintegration plans for learners or
school personnel requiring continuing support, where appropriate;

        (c) Implement academic accommodations and learning continuity measures for
affected learners;

        (d) Engage parents or guardians in planning and supporting the recovery of affected
learners; Coordinate referrals to appropriate government agencies, health professionals,
and community-based service providers;

        (f) Monitor the progress of affected learners and school personnel following
reintegration;

        (g) Implement measures to prevent retaliation, stigmatization, discrimination, or
further trauma; and

        (h) Conduct post-incident reviews to identify lessons learned and improve school
safety systems.     Recovery and reintegration measures shall be developmentally
appropriate, trauma-informed, culturally responsive, and consistent with the best interests
of the child.

        SEC. 29. National Support for School Recovery and Continuous Improvement. –
The DepEd shall establish a National School Recovery and Support Program to strengthen
the capacity of schools to respond to and recover from incidents affecting school safety and
learner well-being. This shall be integrated into the School Safety Plan.

       The Program shall include:

       (a) Technical assistance to affected schools;

       (b) Deployment of specialized psychosocial support teams, where appropriate;

       (c) Development of standardized recovery and reintegration protocols;

       (d) Capacity building for school personnel on post-incident recovery;
       (e) Technical assistance for learning continuity and academic recovery;

       (f) Collection and analysis of post-incident data and lessons learned;

       (g) Dissemination of good practices and model recovery interventions; and

       (h) Such other support measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

       The DepEd shall coordinate with the DOH, the DSWD, LGUs, and other
appropriate agencies to strengthen community-based recovery and reintegration services.

        The DepEd shall, subject to available appropriations, prioritize technical assistance
and specialized support for schools significantly affected by major emergencies, disasters,
violence, or other critical incidents. The DepEd may organize or designate regional or
division-level multidisciplinary support teams to provide technical assistance, psychosocial
support, crisis intervention, and other specialized services to schools affected by major
incidents, emergencies, or disasters. These shall include or have rapid access to a
psychiatrist, alongside psychologists and social workers, for post-incident deployment as
needed.

                                    TITLE VII

                  PROTECTION OF SCHOOL PERSONNEL

       SEC. 30. Protection and Support for School Personnel. – All schools shall ensure
that their personnel are equipped with appropriate protection, support, and resources
necessary to safely and effectively perform their responsibilities under this Act.

Such measures shall include:

 ​     (a) Access to counseling, psychosocial support, employee wellness, and mental
health services, including mental health leave certification, psychological or psychiatric
assessments, or fitness-to-return assessments following a critical incident;

        (b) Training and continuing professional development on school safety, learner
protection, socio-emotional learning, emergency preparedness, crisis management, and
trauma-informed response;

         (c) Appropriate legal assistance and institutional support for school personnel
acting in good faith in the performance of official duties;

       (d) Protection against retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or threats arising from
the implementation of school safety measures;

    (e) Provision of appropriate personal protective equipment, emergency equipment, and
occupational safety measures where necessary;

       (f) Access to post-incident debriefing and recovery support following critical
incidents;

         (g) Recognition of school safety responsibilities in workload planning and
administrative support; and

        (h) Such other measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

        Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish existing rights, benefits, and
protections available to school personnel under existing laws.

                                    TITLE VIII

         MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY
        SEC. 31. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DepEd, in coordination with the
DOH, DSWD, DILG, PNP, LGUs, NYC, and other concerned agencies, shall establish a
comprehensive national monitoring and evaluation system to assess compliance with this
Act, measure the effectiveness of school safety programs, and support evidence-based
planning, policymaking, budgeting, and continuous improvement. These shall be
harmonized with existing indicators and assessment, self-appraisal and reporting
mechanisms to avoid duplicative reporting requirements.

The monitoring and evaluation system shall include:

 ​      (a) Monitoring of compliance with the Framework and the minimum standards
established under this Act;

       (b) Performance targets for the agencies identified in this section and those that are
responsible for the implementation of this Act;

       (c) Periodic review of School Safety Plans and School Safety Handbooks;

       (d) Assessment of school safety infrastructure, equipment, personnel, and services;

      (e) Monitoring of school safety incidents, trends, response times, recovery
outcomes, and implementation of corrective actions;

        (f) Collection and analysis of data on learner well-being, school climate, violence
prevention, bullying, digital safety, emergency preparedness, and other school safety
indicators;

       (g) Monitoring of capacity-building activities and competency development of
school personnel;

        (h) Monitoring of appropriate indicators of digital wellness and emerging digital
risks, including learner-reported digital well-being, harmful or unsafe technology use,
gaming or social media use, technology-associated sleep disruption, and other
digital-related behavioral health concerns, subject to applicable child protection and data
privacy laws;

       (i) Periodic compliance assessments of public and private schools; and

       (j) Such other monitoring mechanisms as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

       SEC. 32. National School Safety Information System. – The DepEd shall establish
and maintain a National School Safety Information System, integrated with existing DepEd
information systems, to support planning, monitoring, research, policy development, and
resource allocation under this Act.

       The System shall collect, consolidate, analyze, and report information on:

      (a) Incidents involving violence, bullying, abuse, discrimination, harassment,
emergencies, disasters, and other school safety concerns;

       (b) Learner well-being, school climate, and behavioral trends;

       (c) Implementation of school safety programs, infrastructure, equipment, and
personnel;

       (d) Emergency preparedness and disaster resilience;

        (e) Digital safety and technology-related incidents and behavioral health trends,
including emerging risks associated with digital technologies, subject to applicable child
protection, confidentiality, and data privacy laws;

       (f) Implementation of recovery and reintegration measures;
       (g) Capacity building and professional development activities;

       (h) School safety performance indicators; and

       (i) Such other information as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

       Information collected under this section shall be used to support evidence-based
policymaking, strategic planning, budget preparation, program evaluation, and continuous
improvement of school safety programs.

        The collection, processing, storage, sharing, and publication of information under
this section shall comply with applicable laws on child protection, confidentiality, and data
privacy.

        SEC. 33. Annual School Safety Report. – Every school shall submit an Annual
School Safety Report to the DepEd, through the SDO, summarizing the implementation of
its School Safety Plan, including the status of school safety implementation, significant
incidents and trends, actions taken, implementation challenges, resource needs, and such
other information as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

       The SDO shall review the reports submitted by schools and provide appropriate
technical assistance, recommendations, and other support necessary to address identified
gaps and strengthen the implementation of School Safety Plans.

       The DepEd shall prescribe the form, content, submission schedule, and reporting
procedures for the Annual School Safety Report.

      A copy of the Annual School Safety Report shall also be furnished to the Local
School Board, LGU, and the BCPC for coordination purposes and inclusion of
community-based safety measures in the local plans.

       SEC. 34. National School Safety Report to Congress. – The DepEd shall submit a
National School Safety Report to Congress every two (2) years providing a summary of the
nationwide implementation of this Act and the Framework, including national school
safety trends, significant findings, implementation status, emerging challenges, and
recommendations for policy, programmatic, and budgetary improvements.

        SEC. 35. Non-Compliance and Administrative Sanctions. – A School Head or
responsible school official who, without justifiable cause, fails or refuses to comply with
the reportorial requirements, or with the minimum standards established under this Act,
shall be subject to administrative sanctions under existing civil service rules and
regulations. For private schools, persistent or willful non-compliance may be a ground for
the suspension or non-renewal of the government permit to operate, without prejudice to
other applicable laws: Provided, That non-compliance attributable to the unavailability of
funds or resources beyond the school’s control shall not, by itself, be a ground for sanctions
under this section.

                                     TITLE IX

                              FINAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 36. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary for the full implementation of this Act shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act. LGUs shall support the implementation of this Act
through their respective local budgets and other available and authorized sources of funds,
including the SEF and local disaster risk reduction and management funds, as may be
applicable, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.
       SEC. 37. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The rules and regulations
necessary for the effective and efficient enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall be
formulated by the DepEd, in consultation with the DOH, DSWD, DILG, other relevant
government agencies, leagues of LGUs, LGUs, the private sector, and other stakeholders,
and shall be implemented within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act.

       Unjustified or willful failure or refusal of the concerned government agencies to
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) within the specified period shall
subject the heads of the aforementioned government agencies to administrative penalties
under applicable civil service laws.

       Should the IRR contain any provision contrary to this Act, the heads of the
government agencies responsible therefor shall, upon a showing of bad faith or gross
negligence, be held administratively liable.

        SEC. 38. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remainder hereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

         SEC. 39. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations,
other issuances, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 40. Effectivity. – Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing rules
and regulations, this Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
