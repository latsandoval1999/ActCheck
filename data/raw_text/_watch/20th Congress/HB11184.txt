INSTITUTIONALIZING   THE   RECOGNITION,   QUALITY    ASSURANCE,

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Philippines continues to face a growing mismatch between the skills possessed by its
workforce and those required by a rapidly changing economy. The Second Congressional
Commission on Education (EDCOM II), in its Year Two Report, found that skills mismatches
persist across all levels of education. Around one-third of employed Technical and Vocational
Education and Training (TVET) graduates are working in positions that do not fully utilize their
qualifications, while senior high school graduates record the highest unemployment rate among
educational cohorts. Moreover, from 2021 to 2022, approximately 35% of the labor force was
engaged in the informal sector, many of whom have limited access to portable and recognized
credentials that can be readily accepted by employers, educational institutions, and government
programs.

         Despite these challenges, the country still lacks a unified legal framework governing
microcredentials and short courses. The Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF), established
under Executive Order No. 83 in 2012 and institutionalized through Republic Act No. 10968 in
2018, was intended to provide a national system for recognizing and building upon qualifications
acquired through various learning pathways. However, EDCOM II reported that little substantial
progress has been made in implementing the PQF since its establishment. The National Coordinating
Council became nonfunctional in 2017 due to staffing and funding limitations, while the framework
continued to lack a permanent secretariat, coordinated financing, and a comprehensive strategic plan
at the time of the Year Two Report.

        As a result, skills and competencies gained through short courses, workplace-based training,
and other forms of non-formal learning remain difficult to recognize, accumulate toward higher
qualifications, or transfer across employers and educational institutions. EDCOM II likewise
identified the lack of a clear national framework for the equivalency and recognition of non-formal
and informal learning as a major barrier to lifelong learning and workforce development. Workers
who acquire new skills through industry training, online learning platforms, or community-based
learning programs often have no reliable mechanism through which these competencies can be
converted into academic credit or formal certification.

        This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by formally integrating microcredentials
and short courses into the Philippine education and training system and aligning them with the PQF.
It establishes minimum quality standards and requires the accreditation of qualified providers by the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), and the Department of Education (DepEd). It also provides for the creation of
a Digital Credential Registry to facilitate the verification, portability, and interoperability of
credentials.

        The proposed measure further safeguards learner rights, provides protections for employed
individuals pursuing additional education or training, and institutionalizes access to Recognition of
Prior Learning. An Inter-Agency Coordination Committee composed of CHED, TESDA, DepEd,
and the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) will be established to harmonize national
standards. Its work will be complemented by an Industry Advisory Council and mechanisms for
scholarships, training vouchers, and employer tax incentives. Through these reforms, the measure
seeks to establish a unified and coherent national system for flexible learning that recognizes diverse
forms of education and training and enables Filipinos to acquire, build upon, and carry their skills
throughout their education and careers.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

 1          Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Abot-Kayang Pangarap Act."

 2          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote response to
 3   technological change and evolving labor market demands.

 4           Toward this end, the State shall recognize microcredentials and short courses as legitimate
 5   and integral parts of the national education and training system; ensure their quality, stackability,
 6   and portability within the Philippine Qualifications Framework; and align them with national
 7   workforce development strategies, so that learning and skills development remain accessible to all
 8   Filipinos at every stage of life and career.

 9          Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

10      (a) Accredited Provider refers to an institution, organization, or entity duly authorized by the
11          Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
12          Development Authority (TESDA), or the Department of Education (DepEd) to offer
13          microcredentials or short courses under this Act;

14      (b) Credit Equivalency refers to the conversion of demonstrated learning outcomes into
15          academic or training credits;

16      (c) Employed Student refers to a learner engaged in wage employment, self-employment,
17          informal work, or livelihood activities while enrolled in a microcredential or short course;

18      (d) Learning Outcomes refer to measurable statements of what a learner is expected to know
19          and demonstrate upon completing a microcredential or short course, which serve as the basis
20          for curriculum design, assessment, and quality assurance under this Act;

21      (e) Microcredential refers to a competency-based certification attesting to specific, verified
22          competencies acquired over a short duration, assessed against defined standards, and
23          satisfying the minimum requirements under this Act;

24      (f) Portability refers to the recognition and transferability of microcredentials across
25          institutions, sectors, employers, and jurisdictions;
26      (g) Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) refers to the formal assessment and recognition of
27          competencies acquired through formal, non-formal, or informal learning, including work
28          experience;

29      (h) Short Course refers to a structured learning program of limited duration, which may be
30          modular, stackable, or standalone, delivered through any mode; and

31      (i) Stackability refers to the accumulation and sequencing of microcredentials or short courses
32          toward academic credit, diplomas, degrees, or national qualifications.

33          Sec. 4. Legal Recognition. - Microcredentials and short courses are recognized as lawful
34          and integral parts of the Philippine education and training system. They shall complement,
35          and not replace, formal degree and diploma programs, and shall serve as flexible entry, exit,
36          or progression pathways for all learners.

37          No educational institution, employer, or government agency shall refuse to consider a duly
38   accredited microcredential solely because of its delivery mode or duration, subject to compliance
39   with quality assurance standards under this Act.

40          Sec. 5. Alignment with the Philippine Qualifications Framework. - All microcredentials
41   and short courses accredited under this Act shall, where appropriate, be referenced to the Philippine
42   Qualifications Framework (PQF) to facilitate recognition, stackability, and progression within the
43   national education and training system. CHED, TESDA, and DepEd shall, in coordination with the
44   PQF National Coordinating Council, promulgate joint guidelines for the referencing of
45   microcredentials and short courses to the appropriate PQF levels within their respective sectors.

46   Stackable credentials shall be designed to enable coherent progression across PQF levels toward
47   higher qualifications, consistent with applicable education and training standards.

48          Sec. 6. Minimum Standards for Microcredentials. - No credential shall be issued or
49   recognized as a microcredential under this Act unless it satisfies all of the following minimum
50   requirements:

51      (a) At least fifteen (15) notional learning hours;

52      (b) Clearly defined and measurable learning outcomes aligned with recognized competency or
53          academic standards;

54      (c) Assessment conducted by a qualified assessor;

55      (d) Issuance by an accredited provider; and

56      (e) Registration in the Digital Credential Registry established under Section 14 of this Act.

57           CHED, TESDA, and DepEd may prescribe higher sector-specific standards within their
58   respective mandates. The Inter-Agency Coordination Committee, established under Section 19 of
59   this Act, shall periodically review these minimum standards at least once every three (3) years.

60           Sec. 7. Quality Assurance and Provider Accreditation. - CHED, TESDA, and DepEd shall
61   each establish and enforce quality assurance standards for microcredentials and short courses within
62   their respective mandates, covering provider accreditation, learning outcomes, assessment,
63   instructional design, and learner support. They shall harmonize these standards through the Inter-
64   Agency Coordination Committee to ensure comparability across sectors.

65           No person or entity shall offer microcredentials or short courses under this Act without prior
66   accreditation from the appropriate agency. Each agency shall maintain a publicly accessible registry
67   of accredited providers, updated at least annually. Accreditation shall be subject to periodic renewal
68   and performance review.

69           Sec. 8. Stackability, Portability, and Credit Recognition. - Accredited microcredentials and
70   short courses may be credited toward formal education programs and national qualifications, subject
 71   to rules issued by CHED and TESDA within their respective jurisdictions. Higher education
 72   institutions shall publish clear policies on credit conversion, accumulation limits, and validity
 73   periods.

 74           CHED shall promulgate rules on the credit equivalency and academic recognition of
 75   microcredentials toward undergraduate and graduate degrees. No institution shall impose credit
 76   transfer requirements more restrictive than CHED standards without written justification on
 77   academic grounds.

 78          No learner shall be required to repeat learning already demonstrated through an accredited
 79   microcredential or recognized prior learning, except where required by licensure, public safety, or
 80   substantial academic grounds, which must be disclosed in writing upon the learner's request.

 81           Sec. 9. Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL). - CHED and TESDA shall each establish,
 82   within their respective jurisdictions, a framework for the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL),
 83   consistent with Republic Act No. 12313, otherwise known as the Lifelong Learning Development
 84   Framework (LLDF) Act. Such framework shall provide for the assessment and recognition of
 85   competencies acquired through formal, non-formal, or informal learning, including industry-based
 86   and work-based learning.

 87   Industry-recognized credentials may be considered for academic or training credit only upon
 88   evaluation in accordance with standards prescribed by the appropriate agency. Industry recognition
 89   alone shall not confer academic or training credit.

 90          Sec. 10. Industry Participation. - An Industry Advisory Council, composed of
 91   representatives from relevant industries, employer organizations, professional associations, and
 92   worker groups, shall be constituted under the Inter-Agency Coordination Committee to:

 93      (a) Identify skills needs and labor market gaps;

 94      (b) Co-design and validate competency standards; and

 95      (c) Recommend updates to the quality assurance framework.

 96         The Council shall serve in an advisory capacity only. Authority over accreditation and
 97   academic matters shall remain with CHED, TESDA, and DepEd. Government reskilling and
 98   employment programs shall recognize accredited microcredentials as valid training credentials.

 99            Sec. 11. Learner Rights and Duties of Institutions. - All learners enrolled in accredited
100   microcredentials or short courses have the right to transparent information on credit conversion
101   policies, stackability pathways, RPL procedures, and assessment requirements prior to enrollment.
102   Institutions and accredited providers shall make this information publicly accessible.

103          Institutions shall adopt flexible, learner-centered delivery policies, including blended or
104   asynchronous options where applicable, advance notice of assessments, and access to academic
105   advising, without compromising academic standards or credential integrity.

106            Sec. 12. Protections for Employed Students. - Employed students enrolled in accredited
107   microcredentials or short courses shall not be denied enrollment, continuation, or completion solely
108   by reason of employment status or work schedule, provided academic requirements are met. They
109   shall be entitled to reasonable academic accommodations, including flexible pacing and alternative
110   assessment modes consistent with program standards.

111          Employers are encouraged to support employed students through flexible work
112   arrangements, adjusted schedules, or educational leave, subject to operational requirements and
113   management prerogative exercised in good faith and consistent with the Labor Code of the
114   Philippines.

115          No employer shall dismiss, demote, or otherwise prejudice an employed student solely by
116   reason of enrollment in an accredited program, absent willful neglect of employment duties or
117   violation of a lawful company policy. Violations may be brought before the Department of Labor
118   and Employment (DOLE), without prejudice to remedies available under existing laws. Nothing in
119   this section shall be construed to diminish existing labor rights, employer prerogatives, or collective
120   bargaining agreements.

121           Sec. 13. Complaints and Redress. - Complaints involving educational institutions or
122   accredited providers under this Act shall be filed with CHED or TESDA, as appropriate. Complaints
123   involving employers shall be filed with DOLE, without prejudice to remedies available under
124   existing laws.

125          No filing fee shall be required for complaints filed under this Act. No person shall be
126   subjected to retaliation, intimidation, or adverse action for filing a complaint or participating in any
127   proceeding under this Act.

128           Sec. 14. Digital Credential Registry. - A centralized, secure, and interoperable Digital
129   Credential Registry is hereby established, administered by CHED in coordination with TESDA,
130   DepEd, and DOLE. The Registry shall serve as the national repository of all accredited
131   microcredentials and short courses issued under this Act, including learner completion records,
132   provider accreditation data, and credential verification information. Only credentials recorded in the
133   Registry shall be recognized for academic credit, employment, promotion, or workforce certification
134   : under this Act.

135          The Registry shall be interoperable with existing national data systems and shall comply
136   with the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

137           Sec. 15. Transition of Existing Credentials. - Microcredentials and short courses issued
138   before the effectivity of this Act may be recognized under this framework if, within two (2) years
139   from effectivity, the issuing provider obtains accreditation and the credential meets the minimum
140   standards under Section 6 and is registered in the Digital Credential Registry. Credentials that do
141   not qualify within this period shall not be recognized under this Act but remain valid under any other
142   law or agreement under which they were issued.

143            Sec. 16. Incentives and Financial Assistance. - The State may provide scholarships, training
144   vouchers, grants, or subsidies to support participation in accredited microcredentials and short
145   courses, with priority for learners from disadvantaged sectors, workers in industries undergoing
146   technological disruption, and those seeking to re-enter the workforce. Employers supporting
147   employee upskilling through accredited programs may be eligible for tax incentives under applicable
148   fiscal laws, including the National Internal Revenue Code and the CREATE Act.

149           Sec. 17. Credential Fraud. - Any person who knowingly issues, submits, or uses a fraudulent
150   or unregistered credential as an accredited microcredential or short course under this Act shall be
151   liable under the Revised Penal Code and other applicable laws. Accredited providers found to have
152   knowingly issued, facilitated, or tolerated fraudulent credentials shall be subject to administrative
153   sanctions under this Act, including suspension or revocation of accreditation, without prejudice to
154   criminal or civil liability under existing laws.

155           Sec. 18. Regulatory Sanctions, - CHED, TESDA, and DepEd may, after due notice and
156   opportunity to be heard, impose the following sanctions on accredited providers for violations of
157   this Act:

158      (a) Written warning with directed corrective action;

159      (b) Suspension of accreditation;

160      (c) Revocation of accreditation; and

161      (d) Disqualification from re-applying for a period not exceeding five (5) years.
162         Sec. 19. Inter-Agency Coordination Committee. - There is hereby created an Inter-Agency
163   Coordination Committee composed of CHED as lead agency, TESDA, DepEd, and DOLE. The
164   Committee shall:

165      (a) Harmonize quality assurance standards, accreditation procedures, and credit equivalency
166          rules across agencies;

167      (b) Oversee the Digital Credential Registry;

168      (c) Convene and oversee the Industry Advisory Council;

169      (d) Issue joint guidelines as necessary for the implementation of this Act; and

170      (e) Review the minimum standards and quality assurance framework at least every three (3)
171          years.
172           The Committee shall meet at least quarterly and submit an annual implementation report
173          to the Senate and House of Representatives.

174           Sec. 20. Monitoring and Evaluation. - The Inter-Agency Coordination Committee shall
175   track the outcomes of this Act, including but not limited to: enrollment and completion rates
176   disaggregated by sector, gender, and region; employment outcomes of completers; credit
177   equivalency and RPL decisions; and complaint and sanction statistics.
