                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Life expectancy is widely recognized as a key indicator of a nation’s quality
of life and level of development. Countries with higher life expectancy rates
generally demonstrate stronger economies, better access to health care and
social services, and improved living conditions for their citizens. Recent data
from the World Bank indicates that the average life expectancy of Filipinos
remains at approximately seventy-one (71) years.

      While this reflects gradual improvements in public health and living
standards, many Filipino senior citizens continue to face significant challenges
associated with aging. Rising healthcare costs, the increasing price of basic
commodities, and the growing need for maintenance medicines place
considerable financial strain on elderly individuals, many of whom rely on limited
or fixed sources of income. These realities underscore the importance of
strengthening existing policies that recognize and support elderly Filipinos who
have reached advanced ages.

      In recognition of the remarkable achievement of reaching one hundred
(100) years of age, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 10868, which grants
monetary incentives and public recognition to Filipino centenarians, both in the
Philippines and abroad. The law honors these individuals as living symbols of
wisdom, resilience, and longevity, acknowledging their invaluable role in society
and their contributions to the nation’s collective heritage.

      Since the enactment of the Centenarians Act, demographic trends indicate
that the Philippines is steadily transitioning toward an aging society. According
to data from the Philippine Statistics Authority, more than one million Filipinos
are now aged seventy (70) years and above, and a substantial number are
reaching ages well beyond eighty (80). These figures reflect the increasing
longevity of the Filipino population and highlight the need for policies that
respond more effectively to the realities of aging.

       Consistent with the policy of honoring the wisdom and longevity of our
elders, this bill seeks to expand the coverage of the Centenarians Act by
extending recognition and assistance to more senior citizens who have reached
significant age milestones. By doing so, the State affirms its commitment to
ensuring that elderly Filipinos are not only celebrated for their longevity but are
also supported in meeting the practical challenges associated with aging.

       The measure is likewise consistent with the State’s broader policy
framework on the protection and welfare of senior citizens, including Republic
Act No. 9994 and Republic Act No. 11350, which collectively recognize the rights,
dignity, and well-being of older persons. By providing additional recognition and
modest financial assistance to elderly Filipinos who have reached advanced ages,
this bill strengthens existing social protection measures without duplicating or
conflicting with current statutory benefits.

       At a time when the cost of healthcare and essential goods continues to
rise, the modest assistance provided under this measure can help alleviate some
of the immediate burdens faced by elderly Filipinos, particularly with respect to
maintenance medicines, healthcare needs, and daily living expenses. More
importantly, it affirms society’s respect and gratitude toward those who have
lived long lives and whose experiences and wisdom continue to enrich our
communities.

      The Constitution envisions a just and humane society that respects the
dignity of every person and ensures the protection and care of the elderly. By
expanding the recognition and support provided to our senior citizens, this
measure advances that constitutional mandate and reaffirms the nation’s
commitment to valuing the contributions of its aging population.

     It is for these foregoing premises that the approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT GRANTING ADDITIONAL BENEFITS TO FILIPINO CENTENARIANS,
     RECOGNIZING OCTOGENARIANS AND NONAGENARIANS, AND
  APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10868, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE ‘CENTENARIANS
                          ACT OF 2016’

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 10868, otherwise known as the
Centenarians Act of 2016, is hereby amended to read as follows:

     “Sec. 2. Benefits for Centenarians. - All Filipinos who reach the age of one
     hundred (100) years old, whether residing in the Philippines or abroad,
     shall be honored with:

        a) A letter of felicitation from the President of the Philippines
           congratulating the celebrant on one’s longevity; and

        b) A Centenarian Gift in the amount of one hundred thousand pesos
           (P100,000.00)

        c)    All Filipino Centenarians who reach the age of one hundred and one
             (101) years old shall receive a cash gift in the amount of one million
             pesos (P 1,000,000.00) on their birthday.

     SECTION 2. A new section denominated as Section 3 of the same Act is
added to read as follows:

     “Sec. 3. Benefits for Octogenarians and Nonagenarians. - All Filipinos who
     reach the age of eighty (80), eighty-five (85), ninety (90) and ninety-five (95)
      years old shall also receive a letter of felicitation from the President and a
      cash gift of twenty-five thousand pesos (P 25,000.00)

      SECTION 3. Sections 3 and 4 of the same Act are renumbered as Sections
4 and 5, respectively.

     SECTION 4. A new section denominated as Section 6 of the same Act is
added to read as follows:

      “Sec. 6. Lead Agency. - The National Commission of Senior Citizens (NCSC)
      shall implement the provisions of this act.”

    SECTION 5. Section 5 of the same Act is renumbered as Section 7 and
amended to read as follows:

      “Sec. 7. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of
      this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.”

    SECTION 6. Section 6 of the same Act is renumbered as Section 8 and
amended to read as follows:

      “Sec 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within thirty (30) days from
      the effectivity of this Act, the Chairperson of the NCSC shall, in
      consultation with the Secretary of Social Welfare and Development, the
      Secretary of Interior and Local Government, the Secretary of Health, the
      Executive Director of the Commission on Filipino Overseas, and the
      Undersecretary of the Philippine Statistics Office, promulgate the
      necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this
      act.”

      SECTION 7. Sections 7 and 8 of the same Act are renumbered as Sections
9 and 10, respectively.

      SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
proclamations, rules, and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
