                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines faces an increasingly competitive global landscape for
higher education, research, and innovation. While the country produces a large
pool of skilled and English-proficient graduates, it continues to experience
outward migration of top talent and limited inflow of world-class academic
institutions. At the same time, global demand for cross-border education, medical
training, research collaboration, and educational tourism is rapidly expanding.

       This bill seeks to address these structural challenges by establishing a clear
legal and regulatory framework for the creation of Global Education Cities
(GECs)—designated geographic zones exclusively developed to host Foreign
Higher Education Institutions (FHEIs) of recognized international standing. The
proposed framework positions higher education as a strategic economic sector
that generates high-value jobs, research output, technology transfer, and
international mobility, while remaining fully consistent with constitutional
safeguards on educational integrity and national interest.

       The measure authorizes the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in
coordination with relevant agencies, to designate and regulate GECs with
integrated, climate-resilient, and smart infrastructure. Within these zones,
qualified FHEIs are allowed full foreign ownership and operational control,
subject to strict accreditation, reporting, and student protection standards. To
prevent unfair competition with domestic institutions, the bill introduces a
Filipino enrollment safeguard mechanism, ensuring that FHEIs maintain their
  international character while prioritizing niche, high-demand, and under-supplied
  academic fields.

         The bill further establishes specialized academic clusters—including
  medicine, marine and maritime sciences, engineering, information technology,
  business, tourism, and governance—each supported by targeted fiscal and
  operational incentives. These incentives are performance-based and aligned with
  national development priorities such as healthcare capacity, disaster resilience,
  digital transformation, maritime leadership, and ASEAN policy engagement. In
  the medical and health sciences cluster, the bill directly addresses the country’s
  persistent healthcare brain drain by mandating localization plans, competitive
  compensation, and structured knowledge transfer.

         Fiscal incentives under the measure are harmonized with existing
  constitutional provisions and investment laws, balancing competitiveness with
  accountability. These include time-bound income tax incentives for proprietary
  institutions, duty-free importation of specialized educational equipment, real
  property tax exemptions for educational use, and donor tax incentives to
  encourage philanthropic and research funding. Robust compliance, audit, and
  claw-back mechanisms are included to protect public interest and government
  revenues.

         Ultimately, this bill aims to transform the Philippines into a premier
  education, research, and educational tourism hub in Asia, attract foreign direct
  investment in knowledge industries, create globally competitive career pathways
  for Filipinos, and reverse long-standing talent outflows. By doing so, it advances
  inclusive growth, strengthens national competitiveness, and operationalizes the
  State’s constitutional mandate to prioritize education, science, and technology for
  sustainable development.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

ADMINISTRATION, AND REGULATION OF GLOBAL EDUCATION
CITIES, RECOGNIZING THEIR INTERNATIONAL CHARACTER,
AND PROVIDING INCENTIVES TO ATTRACT WORLD-CLASS
FOREIGN HIGHER EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS, THEREBY
PROMOTING    EDUCATIONAL   TOURISM   AND NATIONAL

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

                              CHAPTER 1
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Global
Education City Act of 2025.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
provide quality education relevant to the changing needs of the people and
society, positioning higher education as a principal instrument for generating
productive knowledge, innovation, and technology needed to compete in the
knowledge economy.

Recognizing that globalization and the liberalization of services create a climate
for borderless teaching and learning, the State shall actively encourage, promote,
and accelerate the establishment of Foreign Higher Education Institutions
(FHEIs). This strategy is designed to achieve two core goals: first, to promote
internationalization and modernization of the Philippine higher education sector
by attracting world-class academic standards, expertise, and research capacity;
and second, to position the Philippines as a premier destination for educational
tourism, particularly in high-demand and niche fields of Philippine excellence.

Finally, the State shall create high-value, locally competitive career opportunities
for Filipino talent and researchers, thereby reversing brain drain. This strategy is
anchored in the constitutional mandate of Article II, Section 17 to give priority to
education, science, and technology for national development, while also
upholding the requirement under Article XIV, Section 4(2) on educational control
and administration. To comply, the Act includes strict regulatory safeguards
necessary to protect the interests of domestic HEIs and national educational
integrity.

SEC. 3. Creation of Global Education Cities (GECs). – The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), in consultation with the Philippine Economic Zone
Authority (PEZA) and the Board of Investments (BOI), shall designate specific,
contiguous geographical areas as Global Education Cities (GECs). These GECs
shall be established exclusively for Foreign Higher Education Institutions
(FHEIs) of recognized international repute that offer specialized programs.

SEC. 4. GEC Master Planning and Sustainable Infrastructure. – To ensure that
GECs serve as models of modern, sustainable urban living, the designation of any

                                                                                   2
area as a GEC shall be contingent upon the submission and approval of a
Comprehensive Master Plan. This Master Plan must adhere to the following
standards:

     1. Green and Smart Infrastructure. All buildings and facilities within the GEC
        shall comply with the Philippine Green Building Code and integrate
        “Smart City” technologies, including high-speed digital backbones, smart
        grids, and automated campus management systems.

     2. Integrated Mobility. The GEC must be designed with a pedestrian-centric
        layout, featuring dedicated bike lanes, electric vehicle (EV) charging
        stations, and seamless connectivity to major public transport hubs.

Resilience. The infrastructure planning must incorporate climate-resilient
engineering standards to withstand typhoons, flooding, and seismic events,
serving as a benchmark for disaster-resilient urban planning in the Philippines.

SEC. 5. Ownership and Administration of the GEC. — To ensure the integrity,
security, and world-class standard of the GEC, its ownership structure shall be
governed by the following provisions:

1.      Condominium Corporation Ownership. The entire land area of the GEC
shall be owned by and registered in the name of a Condominium Corporation
organized under Philippine laws.

2.      Horizontal Condominiums. The GEC shall be divided into horizontal
condominium units, hereinafter referred to as “Estate Units,” which shall
constitute the alienable and disposable portions of the estate.

3.      Institutional Estate Units. Estate Units covering up to thirty percent (30%)
of the total land area of the GEC may be designated as Institutional Estate Units.
                                                                                   3
These units shall be utilized primarily for the FHEI's educational campus,
research centers, and directly related facilities.

4.    Residential Estate Units. Estate Units covering up to ten percent (10%) of
the total land area of the GEC may be designated as Residential Estate Units.
These units shall be developed for residential purposes and may be acquired by
employees or students residing in the GEC and their families.

5.    Foreign Ownership of Estate Units. Notwithstanding the provisions of
Republic Act No. 4726, otherwise known as the “Condominium Act,” as
amended, foreign ownership of Estate Units within the GEC shall be allowed as
follows:

a.    Institutional Estate Units may be acquired and fully owned by the FHEI
operating within the GEC; and

b.    Residential Estate Units may be acquired and fully owned by foreigners
who can establish a medium to long-term tie or connection with an FHEI or other
businesses related thereto operating within the GEC.

                           CHAPTER 2
           THE FOREIGN HIGHER EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS

SEC. 6. Recognition of International Status. – To achieve the policy of
educational tourism and internationalization, FHEIs establishing a branch
campus within a designated GEC shall be classified as an “Educational Institution
of International Character” established primarily for “foreign temporary
residents.”




                                                                                4
An FHEI operating solely within a designated GEC shall be allowed 100%
foreign equity and full operational control of its branch campus, provided it
adheres strictly to the enrollment safeguard stipulated in Section 15.

The FHEI shall have the prerogative to determine its own curriculum, academic
calendar, standards, and medium of instruction, provided it is accredited by a
recognized international body and offers degrees from the parent institution.

SEC. 7. Designated Fields of Philippine Excellence. – FHEIs shall be prioritized
for establishment in GECs if they offer specialized, world-class programs in the
following fields where the Philippines seeks regional distinction:

1.    Medicine and Health Sciences;

2.    Coastal and Marine Resource Management;

3.    Maritime Sciences;

4.    Engineering and Architecture;

5.    International Business, Finance and Economics;

6.    Information and Data Technology; and

7.    Asian Studies and Governance.

                       CHAPTER 2
      SPECIALIZED ACADEMIC CLUSTERS AND INCENTIVES

SEC. 8. Medical Education and Healthcare Cluster. – To maximize impact on
the national healthcare sector and directly combat the exodus of highly skilled
Filipino medical professionals, FHEIs establishing medical programs shall be
allowed to establish and operate an accompanying world-class teaching and
research hospital within the GEC.


                                                                                5
The government shall streamline the process and provide necessary support for
the construction and operation of such facilities by FHEIs offering advanced
medical and allied health programs within the GEC through:

   1. Single-Window Permitting. The Department of Health (DOH) and the
      CHED shall create a single-window process for the issuance of all
      necessary permits (including Certificate of Need and Operation) for the
      hospital and medical school, with processing times guaranteed not to
      exceed thirty (30) days.

   2. Utility Infrastructure Funding. The national government shall prioritize
      funding for the connection of the GEC hospital and medical school to
      essential utilities (power, water, and digital infrastructure) at subsidized,
      non-commercial rates not to exceed a maximum of fifty percent (50%) of
      the prevailing commercial rate during the construction phase.

   3. Fast-Track Accreditation. CHED, in coordination with the Professional
      Regulation Commission (PRC) and the DOH, shall establish a fast-track
      system for the recognition and accreditation of the FHEI's medical and
      allied health programs and the hospital's residency and fellowship
      programs, ensuring immediate alignment with international regulatory
      bodies.

   4. Fiscal Incentives. The hospital and medical facilities shall be entitled to the
      Real Property Tax Exemption and Duty-Free Importation of construction
      materials      and      equipment       provided       in     Section      17.




                                                                                    6
   5. Medical Tourism Support. These high-standard healthcare facilities shall
      be integrated into the national strategy for medical tourism, providing
      services accessible to foreign medical travelers.

As a condition for receiving the aforementioned specialized incentives, the FHEI
must adhere to the following hiring quotas to address local brain drain and retain
highly skilled professionals:

   1. Non-Physician Staff. The FHEI hospital must allocate a minimum of
      eighty percent (80%) of non-physician staff positions (nurses, allied
      medical professionals, and administrators) to ASEAN/Asian nationals,
      with priority given to Filipino citizens, offering globally competitive
      salaries.

   2. Physicians and Consultants. To ensure the transfer of specialized medical
      knowledge while gradually integrating local expertise, the FHEI shall
      submit an escalating “Physician Localization Plan” to the DOH and
      Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE). The plan shall ensure that
      Filipino/ASEAN/Asian nationals comprise:

         a. At least fifteen percent (15%) of the medical staff/consultants by the
             5th year of operation; and

         b. At least thirty percent (30%) of the medical staff/consultants by the
             10th year of operation.

Compliance with these limits must be certified annually by the DOLE as a
condition for the continued enjoyment of the incentives under this Act.




                                                                                 7
SEC. 9. Coastal and Marine Science Cluster. – To leverage the Philippines’
position as a global center of marine biodiversity and promote educational
tourism, FHEIs specializing in coastal and marine research shall be granted the
following incentives:

   1. Exclusive Field Research Authority. FHEIs shall be granted authority to
      establish and operate exclusive marine field laboratories or coastal research
      stations within the GEC boundaries or nearby coastal areas, subject to
      existing     environmental and territorial    laws    and Department of
      Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) regulations.

   2. Resource Management Focus. The curricula shall prioritize programs in
      Tropical Marine Biology, Coastal Resource Management, Blue Economy
      Development, and Sustainable Ecotourism.

   3. Coastal Tourism Integration. The Department of Tourism (DOT) shall
      prioritize and support the FHEI in utilizing its research stations and
      facilities for specialized educational tourism packages and field training
      programs aimed at attracting international students and researchers.

Other Fiscal and Operational Incentives. The FHEI shall be entitled to the Real
Property Tax Exemption and Duty-Free Importation of specialized equipment
and vessels provided in Section 17, to the extent such equipment is necessary for
marine research.

SEC. 10. Maritime Cluster. – To capitalize on the Philippines' global standing in
maritime and technical industries, the Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA)
shall grant exclusive authority to FHEIs in GECs specializing in Advanced
Maritime Sciences, Naval Architecture, Marine Engineering, and specialized
training programs for modern, autonomous, and green shipping technologies to
                                                                                  8
self-certify its graduates for specific advanced local professional requirements
(excluding PRC licensing), effectively removing the time-consuming and
inconsistent government bottleneck that foreign shipping companies currently
face when hiring Filipino seafarers, provided:

   1. Curriculum and Audit Commitment. The FHEI shall prioritize curricula
      designed to upgrade local certification standards and integrate state-of-the-
      art simulator technologies. In exchange for the FHEI submitting to an
      annual independent quality audit; and

   2. Guaranteed Placement. The FHEI must establish formal, legally binding
      agreements with at least two international shipping companies for the
      guaranteed internship placement of its Filipino and ASEAN graduates to
      ensure practical training and employment pathways are immediately
      available.

SEC. 11. Engineering and Architecture Cluster. – Recognizing the Philippines
as a primary location for studying tropical climates, climate change adaptation,
and disaster resilience, the following incentives shall be provided to FHEIs
offering specialized degrees in Engineering and Architecture that focus on
“Tropical, Green, and Resilient Infrastructure”:

   1. Living Lab Permits. Priority access to partnerships with the Department of
      Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and Local Government Units
      (LGUs) to utilize specific public infrastructure projects as case studies or
      field laboratories for students.

   2. Enhanced R&D Tax Deductions. FHEIs in this cluster shall be entitled to
      one hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on research and
      development     expenses     incurred   for   developing   disaster-resilient
                                                                                  9
      construction materials or low-cost sustainable housing technologies
      relevant to the Philippine context.

   3. Power Expense Deduction. Recognizing the energy-intensive nature of
      engineering laboratories and simulations, FHEIs in this cluster shall be
      entitled to one hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on power
      expenses incurred for their educational and research operations.

SEC. 12. Business, Finance, and Tourism Cluster. – To leverage the
Philippines' status as a leader in Service Exports, Business Process Outsourcing
(BPO), and Global Tourism, FHEIs offering programs in Business, Finance,
Economics, and Hospitality shall be prioritized and given the following
incentives if they focus on Emerging Markets, Fintech, and/or Sustainable
Tourism:

   1. Training Hotels and Facilities. FHEIs offering Hospitality and Tourism
      programs shall be allowed to operate commercial “Training Hotels” within
      the GEC. These facilities shall be entitled to Duty-Free Importation of
      specialized hotel equipment, kitchen machinery, and furniture not locally
      available in sufficient quantity or comparable quality.

   2. Tourism Reinvestment Allowance. FHEIs in this cluster shall be entitled
      to a fifty percent (50%) reinvestment allowance on net income reinvested
      in the expansion or upgrade of tourism-related educational facilities or
      training equipment.

   3. Fintech Regulatory Sandbox. In coordination with the Bangko Sentral ng
      Pilipinas (BSP) and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the
      FHEI’s research centers shall be granted “Observer Status” in government


                                                                              10
      Fintech regulatory sandboxes, allowing faculty and students access to real-
      time data on digital finance policy and innovation.

   4. Regional Headquarters Integration. The BOI shall facilitate partnerships
      between the FHEI and Multinational Corporations with Regional
      Headquarters (RHQs) in the Philippines, creating a direct pipeline for
      internships and executive education tailored to Asian market nuances.

SEC. 13. Information and Data Technology Cluster. – Recognizing the
Philippines as a global powerhouse in the Information Technology-Business
Process Management (IT-BPM) sector, FHEIs offering programs in Data
Science, Artificial Intelligence (AI), Cybersecurity, and Cloud Computing shall
be prioritized to sustain the country’s climb up the global value chain. To attract
leading technology institutions, the State grants the following incentives:

   1. Digital Infrastructure Subsidy. The national government shall ensure the
      GEC is connected to the national fiber backbone. FHEIs in this cluster shall
      be entitled to a subsidy on high-bandwidth internet connectivity costs for
      their first five (5) years of operation.

   2. Tech Hardware Importation. In addition to standard educational duty-free
      privileges, FHEIs in this cluster shall be entitled to Duty-Free Importation
      of high-performance computing equipment, supercomputers, servers, and
      data center infrastructure necessary for advanced AI and data research.

   3. Power Expense Deduction. Recognizing the energy-intensive nature of
      data centers and server farms, FHEIs in this cluster shall be entitled to one
      hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on power expenses incurred
      for their data laboratories and research facilities.


                                                                                11
   4. IT-BPM Industry Linkage. The Board of Investments (BOI) shall facilitate
      formal academic partnerships between the FHEI and top-tier IT-BPM
      locators in the Philippines to co-develop curricula and guarantee internship
      placements, effectively integrating the FHEI into the country's largest
      export industry.

SEC. 14. Asian Studies and Governance Cluster. – To leverage the Philippines'
status as a vibrant democracy in Southeast Asia and its strategic role in ASEAN
affairs, FHEIs offering programs in Political Science, International Relations,
Public Administration, and Asian Studies shall be entitled to the following
incentives:

         1. Policy Research Partnerships. FHEIs in this cluster shall be granted
              priority status to enter into Memoranda of Agreement (MOA) with
              key government agencies, such as the Department of Economy,
              Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Department of Foreign
              Affairs (DFA), and Department of the Interior and Local
              Government (DILG) for the commissioning of policy papers,
              utilization of government data for research, and internship
              placements for students.

         2. Visiting Scholar Visas. To facilitate high-level academic exchange,
              the Bureau of Immigration (BI) shall establish a streamlined
              “Visiting Scholar” visa category for guest lecturers, diplomats-in-
              residence, and policy experts invited by the FHEI for short-term
              engagements, exempting them from standard Alien Employment
              Permit (AEP) requirements for tenures under six (6) months.

         3. ASEAN Conference Support. The DOT and the DFA shall provide
              logistical and promotional support for international conferences and
                                                                                12
               summits hosted by the FHEI within the GEC, positioning the
               institution as a regional hub for policy dialogue.

                              CHAPTER 4
                       SAFEGUARDS AND STANDARDS

SEC. 15. Safeguard Mechanism and Filipino Enrollment Limitation. – To
ensure that the institutions operating within an GEC maintain their “international
character” and do not compete unfairly with local Filipino HEIs, the total
enrollment of Filipino citizens in a single FHEI branch campus within an GEC
shall adhere to the following limits:

   1. Enrollment in Baccalaureate and Master’s Degree programs that are
      substantially offered by existing local educational institutions shall not
      exceed one-third (1/3) of the total student population for these programs;
      and

   2. The Filipino enrollment limit shall be waived entirely for Doctoral (Ph.D.)
      programs and for Master’s Degree programs in the fields identified in
      Section 7, provided that CHED certifies that the specific program or
      specialization is not substantially offered by existing local HEIs, or that
      there is a demonstrable national demand for additional graduates in that
      field.

For purposes of this Section, CHED shall issue detailed technical guidelines in
the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) to define programs that are
“substantially offered.” These guidelines shall consider any or all of the
following:

   1. Saturation Rate. The number of existing graduates from local HEIs relative
      to current industry demand;
                                                                               13
   2. Program Availability. The number of local HEIs offering the specific
      degree program within the region; and

   3. Employability Data: The absorption rate of graduates from local HEIs in
      the specific field. Programs with high local graduate unemployment or
      high saturation shall be deemed “substantially offered,” thereby triggering
      the 1/3 enrollment cap for the FHEI.

FHEIs shall submit mandatory enrollment reports to CHED and the BI annually.
Failure to comply with the foreign enrollment minimum necessary to comply with
the Filipino enrollment limits stipulated above shall be grounds for suspension or
revocation of the international character status and the incentives under this Act.

In the event that the incentives are suspended or revoked due to non-compliance
with the safeguard mechanisms or other provisions of this Act, the FHEI shall be
liable to reimburse the National Government the monetary equivalent of all fiscal
incentives (including waived Income Taxes, Custom Duties, and VAT) availed
of during the period of verified non-compliance.

SEC. 16. Student Protection. – To safeguard the interest of all students, the FHEI
shall adhere to the following protection measures:

   1. Termination or Closure of Program. In the event of program phaseout or
      closure, the FHEI branch campus shall ensure that commitments made to
      enrolled students are met, including offering students suitable alternative
      pathways for completing the program to which they have been accepted.

   2. Termination or Closure of the FHEI branch. The FHEI shall provide a
      guarantee, approved by the CHED, ensuring that in the event of program
      termination, campus closure, or loss of international accreditation, enrolled

                                                                                 14
      students shall be provided a clear and funded pathway to complete their
      degree without additional cost, including potential credit transfer to the
      FHEI’s home campus or an alternative partner institution.

                              CHAPTER 5
                 FISCAL AND OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK

SEC. 17. General Incentives and Taxation Regime. FHEIs operating within a
GEC shall be governed by the following fiscal and tax provisions, which are in
addition to the specialized incentives provided for Academic Clusters in Chapter
3 of this Act:

      1. Tax Exemptions for Non-stock, Non-profit FHEIs. Pursuant to Section
      4, Article XIV of the Constitution, all revenues and assets of non-stock,
      non-profit FHEIs which are used actually, directly, and exclusively for
      educational purposes shall be exempt from taxes and duties.

      2. Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Incentives and Special Preferential Rate
      for Proprietary FHEIs. FHEIs which do not satisfy the definition of ‘non-
      stock, non-profit educational institutions’ are deemed proprietary and shall
      be granted a CIT holiday for ten (10) years from the date of commercial
      operations, followed by a special preferential tax rate of five percent (5%)
      thereafter. This rate shall apply to the FHEI's taxable income derived from
      its activities within the GEC.

      3. Duty-Free Importation. Exemption from customs duties and taxes on the
      importation of specialized laboratory equipment, research apparatus,
      library resources, and construction materials required for the educational
      program and accompanying facilities. The FHEI shall be entitled to the



                                                                               15
      most favorable duty and tax-free importation scheme provided under
      existing laws for economic zones or investment promotion agencies.

      4. Real Property Tax Exemption. In accordance with Section 28, Article
      VI of the Constitution, all lands, buildings, and improvements, actually,
      directly, and exclusively used by a FHEI for educational purposes shall be
      exempt from Real Property Tax (RPT). This exemption shall accrue to the
      owner/lessor of the land or property, provided that the said property is
      under a valid and current lease or use agreement with the FHEI.

      5. Tax Deductions and Exemptions for Donors. All grants, bequest,
      endowments, donations and contributions made to the FHEI to be used
      actually, directly and exclusively by the FHEI shall be exempt from
      donor’s tax and the same shall be allowed as allowable deduction from the
      gross income of the donor for purposes of computing the taxable income
      of the donor in accordance with the provisions of the National Internal
      Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as amended.

      6. Taxation and Harmonization for Proprietary FHEIs. FHEIs that are
      proprietary in nature shall be subject to the taxes, exemptions, and other
      duties and privileges under the NIRC, specifically Section 27, Title II,
      Chapter IV thereof, and Bureau of Internal Revenue implementing rules
      and regulations. In the event of conflict or overlap between the incentive
      package under this Act and other existing laws (including the NIRC, the
      Omnibus Investments Code of 1987, and the Export Development Act of
      1994), the FHEI shall be entitled to avail itself of the package that provides
      the most preferential fiscal treatment.

SEC. 18. Operational Incentives. – FHEIs operating within a GEC shall also be
granted the following operational incentives:
                                                                                 16
   1. Long-term Land Leases. FHEIs shall be allowed to secure long-term land
      leases for periods up to ninety-nine (99) years for educational and related
      facilities. For government-owned land within the GEC, the lease shall be
      at a preferential rate, defined as a rate substantially lower than commercial
      market value, such as a nominal fee or long-term rate based on non-profit
      educational use. For private land leases, the long-term lease shall be
      secured in accordance with the Investors' Lease Act, and the parties may
      mutually agree on preferential rates.

   2. Special Non-Immigrant Status. Foreign faculty, researchers, and key
      administrative staff, along with their spouses and unmarried children, shall
      be granted Special Non-Immigrant Status under Section 47(a)(2) of the
      Philippine Immigration Act of 1940, as amended, and shall be exempt from
      all visa and immigration fees; processing times shall be streamlined and
      prioritized. Spouses seeking employment shall be granted an expedited
      Alien Employment Permit (AEP) process, and unmarried children under
      twenty-one (21) shall be exempt from securing Special Study Permits
      (SSP) or student visas for local enrollment.

SEC. 19. Retirement Benefits of Foreign FHEI Employees. – To enhance
retention of essential foreign personnel, foreign FHEI employees shall be eligible
for the following retirement incentives:

   1. Special Resident Retiree’s Visa (SRRV) Eligibility. Foreign personnel
      who have been employed by an FHEI within a GEC for a cumulative period
      of at least five (5) years and who opt for retirement in the Philippines shall
      be automatically eligible for a simplified Special Resident Retiree's Visa
      (SRRV) or equivalent permanent residency status. This SRRV eligibility
      shall be extended to the retiree’s spouse, unmarried children below the age

                                                                                 17
      of twenty-one (21) and other legal dependents without the requirement of
      additional deposit, and grants dependents exemption from student visas
      and special study permits.

   2. Pension and Fund Repatriation. The repatriation of their retirement funds
      and earned benefits shall be expedited and guaranteed through central bank
      regulations.

                         CHAPTER 6
             ADMINISTRATION AND IMPLEMENTATION

SEC. 20. Financial Guarantees and Capital Repatriation. – Repatriation of
capital and remittance of dividends, profits and earnings arising from foreign
investments in GECs may be done, using foreign exchange sourced from
authorized agent banks (AAB) or AAB subsidiary or affiliate foreign exchange
corporations: Provided, That such foreign investments have been previously
registered with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP). For this purpose, the BSP
rules and regulations governing registration of foreign investments shall be
complied with.

SEC. 21. Regulatory Coordination and Inter-Agency Roles. – Effective
coordination between national and local government agencies is essential for the
seamless operation and success of the GEC. Consistent with the policy and
objective of encouraging, promoting, and accelerating the establishment of FHEIs
and programs in the country, all concerned government agencies and LGUs are
hereby mandated to establish mechanisms to expedite the processing of the
relevant applications, documents, and permits.

   1. SEC and BIR. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the
      Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) are hereby mandated to establish a
      mechanism to facilitate and expedite the processing of the applications for
                                                                               18
      registration and tax identification of FHEI branch campuses and/or
      auxiliary services.

   2. LGUs. The concerned LGUs are hereby directed to establish a mechanism
      to facilitate and expedite the processing of business permits for FHEI
      branch campuses.

   3. BI and DOLE. The BI and the DOLE are hereby directed to establish a
      mechanism to facilitate and expedite the processing of visa and other
      immigration requirements for foreign faculty, academic personnel, and all
      staff and students.

   4. PRC. The PRC is hereby directed to establish a mechanism to facilitate and
      expedite the processing of certificate of registration or license and
      professional identification cards for foreign faculty.

   5. DOT. The DOT is hereby mandated to integrate the GEC and its associated

SEC. 22. Labor and Management Relations. – Labor and management relations
in all FHEI branch campuses shall be governed by existing labor laws, consistent
with the territorial application of the Labor Code. Employees and personnel of
FHEIs shall enjoy working conditions not less than those provided under
Presidential Decree No. 442, or the “Labor Code of the Philippines,” as amended,
and other relevant laws, issuances, rules and regulations of the Philippine
government and the DOLE. Provided, That if the foreign employment contract
contains provisions regarding compensation, tenure, benefits, or working
conditions that are more favorable to the employee than the minimum standards
provided by Philippine labor law, those more favorable terms shall apply.

SEC. 23. Continuing Research. – The CHED shall conduct continuing research
and development on the improvement of GECs and the internationalization of
higher education for the purpose of enhancing knowledge creation and revenue
generation.

SEC. 24. Appropriations. – The national government shall appropriate funds
necessary to establish the core infrastructure and digital connectivity of the
designated GECs, as well as to carry out the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Chairperson of the CHED, with technical support
of the Technical Committee for Transnational Education and the Technical Panel
for Distance Education, and in consultation with the PRC, BI, SEC, BIR, BOI,
DOT, DOH, MARINA, DICT, DepDev, and other stakeholders and relevant
government agencies, shall issue the implementing rules and regulations of this

                                                                             20
Act. Such rules and regulations shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines.

SEC. 26. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, presidential decrees, executive
orders, proclamations or administrative regulations which are inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act are hereby amended, modified, superseded or repealed
accordingly.

SEC. 28. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
