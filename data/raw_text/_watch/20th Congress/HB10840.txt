                            AN ACT
MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION OF A NATIONAL
    BUDGET BLOCKCHAIN SYSTEM TO ENHANCE TRANSPARENCY,
       ACCOUNTABILITY, AND PUBLIC PARTICIPATION IN THE
                  PHILIPPINE BUDGET PROCESS

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The 1987 Constitution recognizes transparency and accountability as essential principles
in the administration of public affairs and in the use of public funds. The national budget involves
billions of pesos appropriated for programs, projects, and services intended to benefit the Filipino
people. However, the complexity of government financial systems and the fragmentation of
budget, procurement, disbursement, accounting, and auditing records can make it difficult for
citizens and oversight institutions to independently and promptly trace the movement and
utilization of public funds.

         The rapid development of digital technologies presents an opportunity to strengthen
existing mechanisms for fiscal transparency and accountability. Blockchain technology, through
its distributed, cryptographically secured, and tamper-resistant ledger, can provide a reliable
means of recording and verifying transactions across the government budget cycle. When
properly designed and implemented, such a system can create a continuous and auditable record
from the appropriation of funds under the General Appropriations Act to their release,
disbursement, utilization, and audit.

       This measure therefore seeks to establish the Philippine National Budget Blockchain, a
blockchain-based budget system that will serve as a government-managed digital ledger for
national budget records. The proposed system shall record budget-related transactions as Digital
Public Assets (DPAs), providing immutable and verifiable records that can be independently
examined by citizens, government agencies, oversight bodies, and other authorized stakeholders.

         Under the measure, the Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT), in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the
Commission on Audit (COA), shall establish and administer the blockchain-based budget
system. The system shall cover the budget cycle from preparation and legislation to execution
and audit. It shall also provide a public-facing portal through which citizens and oversight
institutions may access and verify relevant budget information in a timely manner.

        The measure further recognizes the importance of integrating the proposed system with
existing government financial and information systems. Government budgeting, accounting,
disbursement, procurement, and reporting platforms shall therefore comply with interoperability
standards and ensure that relevant financial data are anchored to the blockchain. This will prevent
the proposed system from operating as an isolated platform and will instead allow it to
complement and strengthen existing government processes.

         To promote accountability, the proposed system shall incorporate key features such as
immutability, decentralization, traceability, security and resilience, and interoperability. These
features are intended to preserve the integrity of budgetary records, prevent unauthorized
alteration or manipulation, enable the tracking of public funds from appropriation to actual
utilization, and ensure the continuity and security of the system.

       The measure also assigns specific responsibilities to concerned government agencies.
DICT shall serve as the lead implementing agency and may engage qualified private-sector
partners, technology providers, and academic institutions, subject to applicable procurement
laws and regulations. DBM shall ensure the integration and timely submission of national budget
data, while COA shall incorporate blockchain-based records into its audit systems and processes.
All government agencies, bureaus, offices, commissions, and government-owned or controlled
corporations shall likewise integrate their financial management systems into the blockchain-
based budget system and submit the required budget, procurement, and expenditure information.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:
 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act will be known as the “Philippine National Budget
 2   Blockchain.”
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Principles and State Policies. – It is the policy of the State to uphold
 4   transparency, accountability, and good governance in the management and disbursement of
 5   public funds. The State shall safeguard honesty and integrity in public service, adopt effective
 6   measures against graft and corruption, and institutionalize public disclosure through open,
 7   verifiable, and citizen-accessible digital systems. To this end, the government shall establish a
 8   digital platform that provides timely, accurate, tamper-resistant, interoperable, and traceable
 9   publication of government budget transactions. The system shall guarantee that all government
10   appropriations, disbursements, procurements, and expenditures are disclosed and made publicly
11   accessible to citizens and oversight institutions.
12   SEC. 3. Definition of terms. – As used in this Act:
13      (a) Blockchain refers to a distributed and cryptographically secured digital ledger that
14          records transactions in a verifiable, immutable, and transparent manner;
15      (b) Blockchain-based Budget System refers to a government-managed blockchain ledger that
16          records, in a real-time and immutable form, all transactions across the budget cycle;
17      (c) Digital Public Asset (DPA) refers to any budget-related record placed on blockchain,
18          representing public funds as immutable and verifiable assets of the people;
19      (d) National Budget refers to the General Appropriations Act (GAA), which is the law that
20          authorizes the government to spend a specific amount of money for various programs
21          and projects;
22      (e) Smart Contract refers to self-executing code deployed on blockchain to enforce
23          predefined conditions, including budget releases and compliance requirements; and
24      (f) Validator Nodes refer to independent verifying entities, including government agencies,
25          academic institutions, and accredited organizations, that maintain and secure the
26          blockchain's decentralization.
27   Sec. 4. Establishment of the Blockchain-based Budget System. – The Department of
28   Information and Communications Technology ("DICT"), in coordination with the Department
29   of Budget and Management ("DBM") and Commission of Audit ("COA"), shall establish a
30   blockchain-based budget system where all records of the national budget are recorded as DPAs.
31   All records of the national budget, from budget preparation and legislation to execution and
32   audit, shall be recorded in DPAs. Smart contracts may automate fund releases upon the
33   fulfillment of verifiable milestones such as the approval process and other documentation.
34   A public-facing portal shall provide real-time access to DPAs, to enable citizens, COA, and
35   oversight bodies to independently verify the flow of funds down to agencies, projects, and
36   beneficiaries.
37   All government financial systems, including budgeting systems, disbursement tools, accounting
38   applications, and reporting software, shall be required to comply with blockchain interoperability
39   standards. All data generated by such systems must be anchored to the blockchain as DPAs,
40   ensuring immutability, traceability, and public auditability.
41   Sec. 5. Features of the Blockchain-Based Budget System. – The blockchain-based budget
42   system shall, at a minimum, embody the following features to ensure transparency,
43   accountability, and reliability:
44      (a) Immutability – All transactions and entries recorded in the system shall be permanent and
45          shall not be subject to alteration, deletion, or manipulation, thereby preserving the
46          integrity of official budgetary data.
47      (b) Decentralization – Validation functions shall be distributed across multiple independent
48          institutions, agencies, or designated entities to prevent concentration of control and to
49          strengthen institutional accountability.
50      (c) Traceability – The system shall provide a verifiable chain of records that allows public
51          funds to be tracked from their appropriation in the General Appropriations Act down to
52          their utilization in specific projects, programs, or beneficiaries.
53      (d) Security and Resilience – The system shall employ robust cryptographic safeguards,
54          redundancy mechanisms, and disaster recovery protocols to ensure continuous operation
55          and to protect all data, processes, and accounts against tampering, breaches, or system
56          failures.
57      (e) Interoperability – The system shall be designed to be compatible with, and adaptable to,
58          existing and future government information systems and emerging technologies, thereby
59          ensuring scalability, efficiency, and seamless integration across platforms.
60   Sec. 6. Data Quality Standards. – Mandates of Implementing Agencies. -
61      (a) DICT shall serve as the lead implementing agency and is hereby authorized to engage
62          and contract qualified private sector partners, technology providers, and academic
63          institutions for the design, development, deployment, and maintenance of said system,
64          subject to government procurement laws and regulations.
65      (b) DBM shall ensure the integration of the national budget process into the blockchain-
66          based budget system including provision of timely and accurate data in coordination with
67          DICT and other concerned agencies.
68      (c) COA shall integrate blockchain technology into its audit systems and processes and
69          ensure that audit standards are aligned with blockchain-based recordkeeping.
 70      (d) All government agencies, bureaus, office, commissions, and government-owned or
 71          controlled corporations shall integrate their financial management systems into the
 72          blockchain-based budget system and ensure timely submission of budget, procurement,
 73          and expenditure reports.
 74   Sec. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60) days from the
 75   effectivity of this Act, DICT, in coordination with DBM, COA, the DOJ, and other concerned
 76   agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
 77   of this Act. The formulation of the IRR shall be undertaken in consultation with representatives
 78   from civil society organizations, the academe, media, and relevant industry stakeholders.
 79   Sec. 8. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee (JCOC). – Upon the effectivity of this Act,
 80   the Joint Congressional Oversight Committee (JCOC) is hereby constituted. The JCOC shall be
 81   composed of eight (8) members with the Chairpersons of the Committee on Science and
 82   Technology of the Senate and the House of Representatives as Co-Chairpersons; Chairpersons
 83   of the Committee on Finance of the Senate and Committee on Appropriations of the House of
 84   Representatives as members; and two (2) members from each House to be designated by the
 85   Senate President and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively.
 86   The JCOC shall monitor the effective implementation of this Act, recommend the necessary
 87   remedial or administrative measures, and perform such other duties and functions as may be
 88   necessary to attain the objectives of this Act.
 89   Sec. 9. Appropriations. – The amount of Five Hundred Million Pesos (P500,000,000.00) is
 90   hereby appropriated from available funds in the National Treasury for the initial implementation
 91   of this Act. The amount necessary to carry out this Act shall be charged against the current
 92   appropriations of DICT for digital-governance and transparency programs. Subsequent funding
 93   requirements shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
 94   SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – Should any provision or part of this Act be declared
 95   unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions and parts hereof, insofar as they are separable
 96   from the invalid ones, shall remain in full force and effect.
 97   SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders or parts thereof inconsistent with this
 98   Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended accordingly.
 99   SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
100   Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
