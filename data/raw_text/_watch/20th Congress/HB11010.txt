                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to impose higher penalties for acts of animal cruelty,
maltreatment, and neglect; permanently prohibit persons convicted of violations
under Republic Act (RA) No. 8485, otherwise known as the “Animal Welfare Act of
1998”, as amended, from owning animals; and establish a National Animal Offender
Registry under the Department of Agriculture.

       In February 2025, public outrage followed the discovery of a dead dog inside
a government-run dog pound in Masbate, prompting renewed calls for stronger
accountability and animal welfare protections1. In March 2025, the Philippine
Animal Welfare Society (PAWS) likewise called for stronger enforcement following
the fatal stabbing of a dog in Subic, Zambales. These incidents shows the challenges
in reporting and prosecuting animal cruelty cases, particularly due to weak
enforcement and limited public awareness of legal remedies2. Highly publicized
cases of animal cruelty shared on social media have further stressed the need for
stronger safeguards. “Tiktok”, a dog from Negros Occidental, was shot multiple
times with arrows, while “Axle”, an American Bully from Mountain Province, was
brutally beaten and killed after allegedly urinating on the perpetrator3.

       These cases show the continuing need to strengthen the country’s legal
framework on animal welfare and ensure that such acts of cruelty are met with
penalties commensurate with their severity. While RA No. 8485, as amended,
prescribes criminal penalties for acts of animal cruelty, its existing safeguards remain
limited in preventing convicted offenders from gaining access to animals in the

        The proposed measure increases the penalties for animal cruelty to ensure
that the punishment reflects the gravity of the offense and strengthens its deterrent
effect. It also permanently prohibits convicted offenders from owning animals and
establishes a National Animal Offender Registry to enable law enforcement agencies,
regulatory authorities, animal shelters, breeders, rescue organizations, and other
authorized stakeholders to verify a person’s history of animal welfare violations.

       A nation’s compassion is measured not only by how it treats its people, but
also by the mercy it extends to those without a voice. Animals depend entirely on
human conscience for their protection, and when the law allows convicted abusers to
regain access to them without meaningful safeguards, innocent lives remain
vulnerable to repeated cruelty.

        As stewards of the environment and of creatures that cannot protect
themselves, we have a duty to ensure that cruelty has no place in a civilized society.
Advocating for the welfare of animals is not merely an act of kindness, but is a
reflection of our collective commitment to justice and humanity.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
INCREASING THE PENALTIES FOR AND BANNING THOSE CONVICTED
OF ANIMAL CRUELTY FROM OWNING ANIMALS, STRENGTHENING
ANIMAL WELFARE PROTECTION, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8485, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE ANIMAL WELFARE
ACT OF 1988, AS AMENDED

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Section 9 of Republic Act No. 8485, as amended by Republic Act
No. 10631, otherwise known as the “Animal Welfare Act of 1988” is hereby further
amended to read as follows:

            “SEC. 9. Any person who subjects any animal to cruelty,
      maltreatment or neglect shall, upon conviction by final judgment, be
      punished by imprisonment and/or fine, as indicated in the following
      graduated scale:

                    “(1) DEATH OF AN ANIMAL - Imprisonment of [one
             (1) year and six (6) months and one (1) day] TWO (2) YEARS
             AND ONE (1) DAY to [two (2) years] THREE (3) YEARS
             and/or a fine [not exceeding] RANGING FROM One hundred
             thousand pesos (P100,000.00) TO TWO HUNDRED FIFTY
             THOUSAND PESOS (P250,000.00), if the animal subjected to
             cruelty, maltreatment or neglect dies;

                    “(2) GRAVE INJURY TO AN ANIMAL - Imprisonment
             of one (1) year and one (1) day to [one (1) year and six (6)
             months] TWO (2) YEARS and/or a fine [not exceeding Fifty
             thousand pesos (P50,000.00)] OF ONE HUNDRED
             THOUSAND PESOS (P100,000.00), if the animal subjected to
             cruelty, maltreatment or neglect survives but is severely injured
             with loss of its natural faculty to survive on its own and
             needing human intervention to sustain its life; and
