                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish a Public Veterinary Clinic in every city and
municipality in the country. It aims to provide preventive, diagnostic, curative, and
emergency animal health services, strengthen disease surveillance and response,
promote responsible pet ownership, support livestock and poultry raisers, and
advance animal welfare as an essential component of public health and food
security.

        Despite the passage of Republic Act (RA) No. 8485, otherwise known as the
“Animal Welfare Act of 1988”, as amended; and RA No. 9482, otherwise known as the
“Anti-Rabies Act of 2007”, access to veterinary services remains uneven across the
country. Many municipalities still lack permanent veterinary offices, adequate
facilities, and sufficient veterinary personnel, leaving pet owners, farmers, and
livestock raisers with limited access to essential animal health services. The
Department of Health reported two hundred and sixty (260) human rabies cases
from January to September 2025, with ninety-five percent (95%) of infections
involving animals that were either unvaccinated or had an unknown vaccination
status1. Likewise, veterinary experts have emphasized that many municipalities
continue to operate without permanent veterinarians, making sustained vaccination
campaigns, disease surveillance, and animal health planning difficult to implement
effectively2.

       The proposed measure establishes fully operational public veterinary clinics
in every city and municipality to expand vaccination and sterilization programs,
improve disease surveillance and emergency response, enhance animal rescue and
welfare services, provide technical assistance to livestock and poultry producers, and
institutionalize accessible veterinary care as a regular local government service. It
also reinforces local preparedness against zoonotic diseases while promoting
healthier communities and protecting agricultural productivity.

food security, yet countless Filipinos continue to struggle to obtain even the most
basic veterinary care because these services remain unavailable or financially out of
reach in many communities. Every missed vaccination, untreated illness, or delayed
response to disease increases the risk to both animal and human lives and places
greater economic burdens on households and local governments. Establishing public
veterinary clinics throughout the country transforms animal healthcare into an
essential public service that protects families, strengthens rural economies,
safeguards the nation’s food supply, and promotes a more compassionate and
healthier society.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A PUBLIC VETERINARY CLINIC IN EVERY CITY AND
MUNICIPALITY IN THE COUNTRY, PROVIDING FOR THEIR OPERATION
AND MAINTENANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Animal Medical Center
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State to
protect and promote the welfare of every animal, recognize their intrinsic value, and
ensure that their basic health and safety needs are adequately met. Towards this end,
the State shall establish and strengthen accessible and affordable animal health and
veterinary services in all local government units nationwide, thereby contributing to
public health, responsible pet ownership, and a humane society.

      SEC. 3. Establishment. – There shall be established, operated, and continuously
maintained a public veterinary clinic in every city and municipality in the country.
These clinics shall be under the direct control and supervision of the respective city
or municipal local government unit (LGU), in consultation and coordination with
the Department of Agriculture - Bureau of Animal Industry (DA-BAI).

       SEC. 4. Provision of Facilities and Equipment. – The concerned city or municipal
LGU shall provide an adequate, accessible, and suitable lot or space where the
public veterinary clinic shall be constructed or established. The clinics shall be
equipped with essential machines, equipment, instruments, and supplies necessary
for providing comprehensive basic veterinary services.

       SEC. 5. Functions and Services. – The public veterinary clinics shall perform
functions and provide services including, but not limited to:
          a. Provide routine vaccinations (e.g., anti-rabies, distemper) and
       deworming services;



                                           1
          b.​ Conduct spay and neuter programs for pet population control;

          c.​ Offer basic veterinary consultations,        diagnoses,   and   medical
      treatments for common animal illnesses;

          d.​ Perform minor surgical procedures;

         e.​ Assist in the control and prevention of zoonotic diseases in
      coordination with local health offices;

         f.​ Conduct animal welfare awareness and responsible pet ownership
      campaigns;

         g.​ Maintain a database of registered pets and vaccinated animals within
      the LGU’s jurisdiction;

         h.​ Support, alongside the Municipal Agriculturist, farmers and livestock
      and poultry raisers with technical assistance and health management
      programs;

          i.​ Provide basic emergency care for animals; and

         j.​ Coordinate with local animal welfare organizations and law
      enforcement for animal rescue and cruelty cases.

       SEC. 6. Organizational Structure and Staffing. – The local chief executive shall
appoint a qualified Chief Veterinarian to head the public veterinary clinic, who shall
have the necessary professional qualifications and experience as prescribed by the
Civil Service Commission and the Professional Regulation Commission. The Chief
Veterinarian shall render full-time service and be responsible for the clinic’s overall
administration and operation. The local chief executive shall also appoint the
necessary veterinary medical, technical, and administrative personnel and staff,
proportionate to the needs of the LGU and in accordance with relevant civil service
laws, rules, and regulations.

      SEC. 7. Design, Policies, Terms and Conditions. – The LGU shall prepare the
design and layout of the clinic structure in consultation with the DA-BAI.

The LGU shall also formulate and implement appropriate local policies, guidelines,
fees (if any, for non-indigent cases, and subject to LGU ordinances), and terms and
conditions relative to the efficient operation and maintenance of the public
veterinary clinic, ensuring accessibility and affordability of services.

       SEC. 8. National Government Support. – The National Government, through the
Department of Agriculture (DA), shall extend technical, financial, and material
assistance to LGUs for the effective operation of municipal veterinary offices. In
cases of animal disease outbreaks and other public animal health emergencies, the
DA shall, in coordination with the concerned LGUs, immediately mobilize

emergency funds, personnel, vaccines, equipment, and other resources necessary for
containment, prevention, and eradication efforts.

      The DA shall likewise provide continuing capacity-building, surveillance
support, and access to diagnostic facilities to strengthen local preparedness and
response systems.

      SEC. 9. Funding. – For the initial establishment and construction or renovation
of public veterinary clinics, an initial appropriation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act for the ensuing fiscal year following the approval of this
Act. This initial funding shall be allocated as a subsidy from the National
Government, through the Department of Agriculture, to assist cities and
municipalities in meeting the capital outlay requirements for these facilities.

      The city or municipal local government unit shall primarily be responsible for
providing the necessary funds for the continuous operation, maintenance, and
personnel services of their respective public veterinary clinics. Such funds shall be
included in their annual regular appropriations. The Department of Agriculture may
provide supplementary financial assistance or technical support for specific
programs (e.g., mass vaccination, spay/neuter drives) as part of its regular mandate.

        SEC. 10. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DA-BAI, in coordination with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and relevant professional
organizations, shall establish a robust system for monitoring and evaluating the
establishment, operation, and effectiveness of the public veterinary clinics. This
system shall ensure compliance with national standards, promote best practices, and
facilitate continuous improvement of animal health services nationwide.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after the
approval of this Act, the DA-BAI, in coordination with the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG) and such other relevant agencies and
professional organizations, shall prepare and issue the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved,
