                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


The Constitution mandates that the State shall protect and promote the right of
all citizens to quality education at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to
make such education accessible to all. Crucial to this mandate is ensuring that
our learning institutions remain safe spaces where students, teachers, and school
personnel can thrive without fear of violence, environmental hazards, or
emotional trauma.

Recent tragedies in public school vicinities highlight critical gaps in our current
school safety infrastructure and response frameworks. The increasing complexity
of both man-made security threats and climate-driven natural disasters demands
a comprehensive, modern approach to campus safety.

This bill seeks to institutionalize standard safety protocols across all public basic
education institutions by establishing three core pillars:

   1. Physical and Infrastructure Security: Mandating secured perimeters,
      controlled access points, and routine safety audits.

   2. Crisis and Emergency Response: Implementing uniform emergency
      response protocols and mandatory training for natural disasters, fires, and
      active-threat situations.

   3. Mental Health and Psycho-social Support: Allocating dedicated personnel
      to early intervention, peer-mediation, and counseling to address the root
      causes of behavioral crises before they escalate.
By integrating physical infrastructure upgrades with proactive mental health care
and rigorous training, this legislative measure shifts our public school systems
from a reactive stance to a culture of constant readiness and prevention.

In view of the urgent need to protect our nation’s most valuable resource—our
children—the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING COMPREHENSIVE SAFETY PROTOCOLS,
SECURITY INFRASTRUCTURE, AND CRISIS INTERVENTION MECHANISMS
       IN ALL PUBLIC BASIC EDUCATION SCHOOLS NATIONWIDE AND
                      APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Safe Schools and
Learning Environments Act."

      Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect the life, liberty, and property of its citizens, particularly the youth. The
State recognizes that an environment free from violence, hazards, and
psychological distress is indispensable to effective learning. To this end, the State
shall implement systemic safety protocols, robust physical defenses, and
proactive psycho-social intervention strategies in all public elementary and
secondary schools.
      Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage – This Act shall apply to all public basic
education institutions, including elementary, junior high, and senior high schools
under the jurisdiction of the Department of Education (DepEd).

      Sec. 4. The School Safety and Security Framework - The DepEd, in
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
the Philippine National Police (PNP), and the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), shall
formulate a Comprehensive School Safety Framework divided into three core
components:

      (a) Physical Infrastructure and Perimeter Defense: All public schools shall
          be mandated to maintain secured boundaries. This includes perimeter
          fencing, functional main gates with strictly controlled access logs, high-
          definition closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras at entry points, and
          well-lit walkways.
      (b) Standardized Emergency Response Protocols (SERP): Every school must
          adopt a uniform protocol for specific threat levels, including, but not
          limited to, active shooter scenarios, localized civil unrest, fires,
          earthquakes, and severe weather events.

      (c) Early Warning and Communication Systems: Schools shall implement
          dedicated public address systems and a localized, secure messaging
          network to communicate rapidly with parents, guardians, and local
          authorities during emergencies.

       Sec. 5. Mandatory School Safety Audits. – Within twelve (12) months from
the effectivity of this Act, and every two years thereafter, all public schools shall
undergo a mandatory safety and structural integrity audit. These audits will be
spearheaded by the DepEd’s Engineering Division in collaboration with local
government unit (LGU) engineers and disaster risk reduction officers to identify
and retroactively fix physical vulnerabilities.

      Sec. 6. Integration of School-Based Mental Health Services. – To prevent
internal behavioral crises and violence, DepEd shall accelerate the hiring and
strategic placement of Registered Guidance Counselors and Mental Health
Associates in all public school districts. Schools shall establish localized "Safe
Space Offices" offering confidential counseling, peer-mediation groups, and
mental health first aid.

       Sec. 7. School Safety and Crisis Management Committees. — Every public
school shall establish a School Safety and Crisis Management Committee chaired
by the School Principal and composed of faculty representatives, parent-teacher
association officers, local barangay health/security personnel, and a designated
school safety officer. The committee shall oversee quarterly drills and enforce
strict visitor screening protocols.
       Sec. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to initially implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations
of the Department of Education. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
its continued implementation and structural enhancements shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

      Sec. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from
the approval of this Act, the Department of Education, in consultation with the
DILG, DOH, PNP, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

       Sec. 10. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      Sec. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
presidential issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      Sec. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
