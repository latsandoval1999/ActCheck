                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes the vital role of arts and culture in nation-building, identity
formation, and inclusive development. The promotion of Filipino creativity is not only a
constitutional mandate but also a strategic investment in the country’s cultural and
creative industries. However, despite the abundance of talent across the country, many
Filipino artists, particularly those in the regions and those at the early stages of their
careers, continue to face significant barriers in accessing platforms where they can
publicly exhibit their work.

        One of the most persistent challenges confronting local artists is the high cost and
limited availability of exhibition spaces. Traditional galleries are often concentrated in
urban centers and may be financially inaccessible to emerging artists. As a result, many
are deprived of opportunities to showcase their work, connect with audiences, and
participate meaningfully in the creative economy. This lack of access not only constrains
artistic development but also limits the public’s exposure to diverse forms of Filipino
expression.

       At the same time, numerous public spaces across the country, such as parks,
plazas, government buildings, and other communal areas, remain underutilized or are
not fully optimized for cultural engagement. These spaces present an opportunity to
bring art closer to the people by transforming them into accessible venues for artistic
expression and appreciation. By integrating art into everyday public environments, the
State can foster a more vibrant, inclusive, and participatory cultural landscape.

       This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by allowing the free use of
available public spaces for art exhibits of local artists. Through the coordinated efforts of
the National Commission for Culture and the Arts and the Department of Tourism, in
partnership with local government units, the program will establish regional and local
platforms where artists can exhibit their works without the burden of prohibitive costs.
In doing so, the bill democratizes access to artistic spaces and ensures that opportunities
for creative expression are not limited by geography or financial capacity.

        Beyond supporting artists, this measure advances broader social and economic
objectives. Public art enriches communities by making culture more visible and
accessible, fostering a deeper sense of identity, pride, and shared heritage among
Filipinos. It transforms public spaces into dynamic cultural hubs that encourage
engagement, dialogue, and creativity. Moreover, by enhancing the cultural character of
localities, art exhibitions in public spaces can contribute to tourism development and
stimulate local economic activity, benefiting small businesses and surrounding
communities.

       The measure is likewise timely and necessary in light of the ongoing recovery of
the creative sector. Many artists continue to experience limited income opportunities and
reduced exposure following disruptions in recent years. Providing accessible venues for
exhibitions serves as a practical and immediate intervention to help revitalize artistic
livelihoods and sustain creative production across the country.

        Ultimately, this bill represents a low-cost yet high-impact approach to cultural
development. By maximizing existing public infrastructure, it creates opportunities for
artistic expression, strengthens the creative economy, and promotes inclusive access to
culture for all Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT ESTABLISHING A PROGRAM FOR THE USE OF PUBLIC AND
       PARTICIPATING PRIVATE SPACES FOR ART EXHIBITS OF LOCAL
       ARTISTS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sining sa
Pampublikong Lugar Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote and
support the talents of the Filipino people in arts, aiming to cultivate creativity and
enhance the cultural identity of the nation. Towards this end, the State shall establish a
coordinated program for the promotion of public art exhibits in accessible public spaces,
in partnership with Local Government Units (LGUs), cultural agencies, and stakeholders
in the creative industries.

       The State shall accordingly facilitate and provide support through the National
Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), in coordination with the Department of
Tourism (DOT) and Local Government Units (LGUs), the establishment of regional and
local platforms showcasing such talents and allowing the public to have access to local
arts.

        SEC. 3. Use of Public Spaces and Participating Private Establishments for Art
Exhibits. — LGUs are hereby encouraged to designate appropriate public spaces within
their respective jurisdictions as venues for art exhibits of local artists, subject to existing
laws, ordinances, and public safety regulations.

      Public Spaces and Participating Private Establishments refer to parks, plazas,
government buildings, cultural centers, sidewalks, open grounds, and other publicly
accessible areas owned, controlled, or managed by Local Government Units (LGUs) or
national government agencies, as well as privately owned but publicly accessible venues,
including malls, commercial centers, and similar establishments, that voluntarily
participate in the Program, which may be designated as venues for art exhibits, subject
to existing laws, ordinances, and public safety regulations.

      The use of such public spaces for art exhibits may be granted free of charge or at
subsidized rates, as may be determined by the concerned LGU through appropriate
ordinances or issuances.

      The DOT and the NCCA shall provide technical and programmatic support to
LGUs in the identification, development, and promotion of such public art spaces.

       SEC. 4. Participation of Private Establishments. — Privately-owned
establishments, including malls and commercial centers, may voluntarily participate in
the Program by making designated publicly accessible spaces available for art exhibits of
local artists, subject to guidelines to be issued under this Act.

       Participating private establishments shall coordinate with the concerned LGU and
the NCCA for program alignment, scheduling, and compliance with safety and
accessibility standards.

      SEC. 5. Incentives for Participating Private Establishments. —

          (a) Participating private establishments may avail of applicable fiscal and non-
              fiscal incentives under existing laws, subject to the requirements of such
              laws, for activities undertaken in support of the Program.

          (b) Expenses directly incurred for the hosting, promotion, and maintenance of
              art exhibits under this Act may be treated as allowable deductions from
              gross income, subject to existing tax rules and regulations.

          (c) Local Government Units are encouraged to provide non-fiscal incentives,
              including recognition programs, streamlined permitting, and promotional
              support, and may grant reductions in local fees or charges, subject to the
              Local Government Code and existing ordinances.

          (d) The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of
              Tourism (DOT), in coordination with the NCCA, may develop recognition
              and accreditation programs for participating establishments.

       SEC. 6. Venues and Schedule. — The DOT and the NCCA shall identify suitable
venues within each region and locality for the conduct of the art exhibits. The schedule
of exhibits shall be determined jointly by the LGUs, with technical support from the DOT
and NCCA, upon consultation with relevant stakeholders, including local artists and
cultural organizations.

       SEC. 7. Program Framework and Artist Selection. — The NCCA, in coordination
with the DOT and LGUs, shall develop a program framework for the implementation of
public art exhibits under this Act, which shall include:
           (a) Transparent and inclusive mechanisms for the selection and participation
               of local artists;
           (b) Guidelines for curation, installation, and display of artworks;
           (c) Standards for the maintenance, preservation, and security of exhibited
               works; and
           (d) Measures to ensure accessibility and public engagement.

      SEC. 8. Safeguards and Liability. — LGUs, in coordination with the NCCA and
DOT, shall ensure that the use of public spaces for art exhibits complies with public safety
standards and does not unduly disrupt the primary use of such spaces.

       Appropriate measures shall be adopted to protect exhibited artworks from
damage, theft, or vandalism. The terms and conditions governing liability for loss or
damage shall be defined in the implementing rules and regulations or in agreements with
participating artists.

       SEC. 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to execute the aims and
purposes of this Act shall be allocated by Congress annually and shall be managed and
disbursed by the NCCA. Such funds shall be used for program development, artist
support, logistical requirements, capacity-building for LGUs, and promotional activities
related to the Art in Public Places Program.

       SEC. 10. Inter-agency Coordination. — The NCCA shall serve as the lead
implementing agency of this Act and shall coordinate with the DOT, LGUs, and other
relevant agencies to ensure the effective implementation of the program.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
date of effectivity of this Act, the DOT and NCCA, in coordination with the Department
of Interior and Local Government (DILG) and concerned LGUs, shall promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.
      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
