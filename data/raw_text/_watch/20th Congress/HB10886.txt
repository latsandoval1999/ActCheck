                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Tax compliance is essential to the delivery of public services. Unpaid tax liabilities,
however, may become increasingly difficult to settle as interest, surcharges, and penalties
accumulate over time. What may once have been a manageable obligation can eventually
become difficult to settle. When that happens, the Government is left with a liability on
paper, while the taxpayer remains outside full compliance.

       This legislative proposal provides a practical way forward by granting a one-time
amnesty on interest, surcharges, compromise penalties, and other additions to unpaid or
underpaid national internal revenue taxes for taxable year 2025 and prior years. In return,
the taxpayer must pay one hundred percent (100%) of the basic tax lawfully due.

       This measure provides citizens and businesses a clear opportunity to settle past
obligations without the accumulated burden of penalties and interest. It covers
unassessed liabilities, pending assessments, and final and executory liabilities. It also
provides reasonable installment arrangements for individuals and smaller taxpayers,
while preserving validation and safeguards against fraudulent availment.

        For the Government, the measure can convert outstanding liabilities into actual
collections while bringing taxpayers back into the formal tax system. This is particularly
significant given the central role of tax revenues in funding public services. In 2024, the
Bureau of Internal Revenue collected ₱2.852 trillion, underscoring the central role of tax
revenues in financing government operations and public services.1

       A tax amnesty should not diminish the value of voluntary compliance. Neither
should accumulated penalties become a permanent barrier to payment. By requiring full
and final settlement of the basic tax while providing a reasonable path back to compliance,
this measure is able to strike a balance between granting meaningful relief to taxpayers
and preserving the fiscal interests of the Government to fund essential public services.

                                  AN ACT
          GRANTING A ONE-TIME GENERAL TAX AMNESTY ON INTEREST,
      SURCHARGES, COMPROMISE PENALTIES, AND OTHER ADDITIONS TO THE
        TAX WITH RESPECT TO UNPAID OR UNDERPAID NATIONAL INTERNAL
         REVENUE TAXES FOR TAXABLE YEAR 2025 AND PRIOR YEARS, UPON
           PAYMENT OF THE BASIC TAX DUE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

            Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “General Tax Amnesty Act
 2   of 2026.”
 3
 4           Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   enhance revenue administration and collection, broaden the tax base, promote voluntary
 6   compliance, and provide qualified taxpayers a reasonable and time-bound opportunity to
 7   settle unpaid or underpaid national internal revenue tax liabilities.
 8
 9          Toward this end, the State shall establish a one-time, liability-specific general tax
10   amnesty that requires payment of the basic tax lawfully due while granting relief from
11   interest, surcharges, compromise penalties, and other additions to the tax directly
12   attributable to the failure to timely file a return or other required document, correctly
13   declare the tax due, withhold or remit a tax, or pay the tax due.
14
15          The amnesty granted under this Act shall apply only to the specific tax type,
16   taxable period, transaction, and basic tax settled under this Act. Any undisclosed or
17   unsettled liability shall remain subject to assessment and collection under the National
18   Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, within the applicable prescriptive period.
19
20          Section 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
21
22          (a) Amnesty application refers to the sworn application filed by a taxpayer to obtain

23           Section 4. Coverage. – Subject to the exclusions under this Act, there is hereby
24   granted a one-time general tax amnesty on interest, surcharges, compromise penalties,
25   and other additions to the tax directly attributable to the failure to timely file a return or
26   other required document, correctly declare the tax due, withhold or remit a tax, or pay
27   the tax due with respect to all national internal revenue taxes imposed under the NIRC
28   for the covered period, whether collected by the BIR or the BOC, and whether assessed or
29   unassessed.
30
31          The tax amnesty may cover a liability that is (a) unassessed, whether or not under
32   audit or investigation; (b) covered by a formal assessment that has not become final and
33   executory, including a liability under administrative protest or pending judicial
34   proceeding; (c) Final and executory; or (d) subject to collection proceedings.
35
36         Section 5. General Rule on Availment. – A qualified taxpayer may avail of the tax
37   amnesty by:
38         (a) Filing the applicable original, amended, or supplemental tax return or other
39             prescribed schedule, as applicable, together with the amnesty application;
40         (b) Truthfully identifying the tax type, taxable period, transaction, and basic tax
41             being settled; and
42         (c) Paying one hundred percent (100%) of the basic tax due, subject to the
43             installment arrangements authorized under this Act.

 6          Section 7. Withholding Taxes. – A withholding agent who failed to withhold a tax

 8   withholding tax that should have been withheld.

22          Section 8. Installment Payment. – An individual not engaged in trade or business,
23   an estate or trust, or a micro or small taxpayer may pay at least twenty-five percent (25%)
24   of the basic tax upon filing and the balance in equal monthly installments over a period
25   not exceeding twelve (12) months.
26
27          A medium taxpayer may pay at least fifty percent (50%) of the basic tax upon filing
28   and the balance in equal monthly installments over a period not exceeding six (6) months.
29
30          A large taxpayer shall pay the basic tax in full upon filing.
31
32          For purposes of this section, business taxpayers shall be classified in accordance
33   with Section 21(b) of the NIRC.

35          No interest, surcharge, or other addition to the tax shall accrue on any installment
36   timely paid in accordance with this section.
37
38          Failure to pay any installment within thirty (30) days from its due date shall result
39   in the cancellation of the installment arrangement. Payments already made shall be
40   applied to the basic tax. The unpaid balance shall become immediately due, and the

 4           Section 9. Availment Period; Filing; Effect on Prescription. – The period for
 5   availment shall commence on the one hundred twentieth (120th) day following the
 6   effectivity of this Act and shall continue for twenty-four (24) months.
 7
 8          Applications shall principally be filed electronically in accordance with procedures
 9   prescribed by the collecting bureau. Manual and assisted digital filing shall be made
10   available to taxpayers who are unable to use the electronic system.
11
12         The filing of an amnesty application shall not revive any liability for which the
13   Government’s right to assess or collect had prescribed before the effectivity of this Act.
14
15           For a liability for which the applicable prescriptive period remains open, the
16   complete filing of an amnesty application shall suspend the running of the period for
17   assessment or collection solely with respect to the liability covered by the application until

19   case for more than eighteen (18) months from complete filing, excluding any period
20   attributable to a remedy initiated by the taxpayer.
21
22          Section 10. Validation and Correction of Deficiencies. – The collecting bureau may
23   validate an amnesty application within twelve (12) months from complete filing.
24
25          Notwithstanding Section 222(e) of the NIRC, a return, schedule, or document filed

27   validate the availment and only within the prescribed validation period.

30   inquire into bank deposits, foreign currency deposits, financial accounts, or other
31   information protected by law. Access to such information shall remain subject to existing
32   law and other independently applicable legal authority.
33
34           If the collecting bureau finds a discrepancy, it shall issue a written Notice of

36   basic tax declared, the additional basic tax proposed, and the documents or information
37   relied upon.
38
39         The taxpayer shall have thirty (30) days from receipt of the notice within which to
40   submit an explanation, corrected return, or supporting documents.
41
42          A taxpayer may voluntarily correct an application before receipt of a Notice of
43   Discrepancy and pay any resulting deficiency basic tax without interest, surcharge,
44   compromise penalty, or additional amnesty charge.

13           Section 13. Immunities and Privileges. – Upon final availment, the taxpayer shall,
14   with respect only to the tax type, taxable period, transaction, and basic tax specified in the
15   Certificate of Availment, be:
16
17          (a) Relieved from payment of all interest, surcharges, compromise penalties, and
18              other additions to the tax directly attributable to the covered failure;
19          (b) Immune from civil and administrative liability under the NIRC arising solely
20              from the covered failure;
21          (c) Immune from criminal liability under the NIRC consisting solely of the failure
22              to file a return or other required document, supply correct and accurate
23              information, pay a tax, withhold a tax, or remit a tax required to be withheld,
24              provided that the violation did not involve fraud, falsification, a fictitious
25              transaction, or an affirmative attempt to evade or defeat the tax;
26          (d) Protected from further assessment or collection of the settled basic tax and the
27              condoned additions thereto; and
28          (e) Protected from further examination of the settled liability, except as expressly
29              provided under this Act.
30
31          The immunity granted under this Act shall not extend to any undisclosed or
32   unsettled tax type, taxable period, transaction, or basic tax.
33
