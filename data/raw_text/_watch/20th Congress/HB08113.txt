                              AN ACT

AND TRANSPARENT REMITTANCE PRICING, STRENGTHENING CONSUMER
PROTECTION AGAINST FRAUD AND CONVERSION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) make great sacri ices to support their families and
the country. The money they send home pays for food, schooling, housing, and small
businesses, and helps keep the economy strong. Because remittances are so
important, the State has long promoted the use of safe and formal remittance services
with reasonable fees. Still, many OFWs and their families lose part of their hard-
earned money because of hidden charges, unfair exchange rates, excessive fees, or
even fraud.

Today, remittance services are offered not only by banks but also by many non-bank
providers and digital platforms. While this gives OFWs more options, it also creates
risks. Some providers do not clearly disclose the true cost of sending money, while
others engage in abusive practices or allow funds to be diverted or misused. These
problems reduce the amount families actually receive and weaken trust in the
remittance system.

This bill protects OFWs by setting clear and fair rules for remittance services. It
requires full disclosure of all fees and exchange rates before a transaction is made,
promotes fair pricing through proper regulation, and strengthens penalties against
fraud and misuse of remitted funds. By making remittance services more transparent,
competitive, and secure, the measure helps ensure that more of an OFW’s hard-

deserves immediate passage.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs) Remittance Fair Practices Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the signi icant
contribution of overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) to the national economy through
foreign exchange remittances. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
OFW remittances by ensuring fair, transparent, and competitive pricing of remittance
services; preventing fraud, misappropriation, and unauthorized taking of
remittances; and promoting inancial education for OFWs and their families.

The State shall implement this policy in coordination with the Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas (BSP) and relevant agencies, consistent with existing laws that promote safe
and prompt delivery of of icial remittances and reasonable charges.

SECTION 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all remittance transactions of OFWs
sent to bene iciaries in the Philippines through banks and non-bank inancial
institutions, including money service businesses and other remittance and transfer


                                                                           3|Page
companies, to the extent supervised or regulated by the BSP and other relevant
regulators.

SECTION 4. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Overseas Filipino Worker” refers to a person who is to be engaged, is
       engaged, or has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a State of which the
       person is not a citizen, including seafarers, consistent with applicable rules.

   (b) “Remittance” refers to the foreign exchange earnings sent by OFWs or on their
       behalf to bene iciaries in the Philippines through formal channels, including
       those required to be remitted through the Philippine banking system under
       existing rules.

   (c) “Remittance Service Provider (RSP)” refers to banks and non-bank inancial
       institutions, including money service businesses and similar entities, that
       provide remittance and transfer services to OFWs and their bene iciaries.

   (d) “Total Remittance Cost” refers to all charges borne by the sender and/or
       bene iciary in relation to a remittance transaction, including service fees,
       commissions, foreign exchange spread, and any other charge directly linked to
       the remittance.

   (e) “Posted Exchange Rate” refers to the peso equivalent rate offered by an RSP
       for the covered transaction, as displayed in accordance with this Act.

SECTION 5. Mandatory Disclosure; Exchange Rate and Fee Transparency. —

   (a) Every RSP shall clearly disclose, prior to the con irmation of a remittance
       transaction, the Total Remittance Cost, including
       (i)    the service fee,
       (ii)   the exchange rate to be applied, and
       (iii) the net peso amount to be received by the bene iciary.

   (b) Every RSP shall conspicuously post in its premises and digital channels (as
       applicable) its exchange rates and applicable fees for remittance corridors
       commonly used by OFWs, in the form and manner prescribed by the BSP.

   (c) The net peso amount disclosed and con irmed to the sender shall be the same
       net peso amount to be received by the bene iciary, unless the sender expressly
       authorizes deductions identi ied and disclosed in advance.

SECTION 6. Fair Pricing Standard; BSP Benchmark and Fee Cap Authority. —

   (a) Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the BSP,
       in coordination with the DOF, DMW, and concerned industry stakeholders,
       shall issue regulations establishing:

      (i)    corridor-based benchmarks for Total Remittance Cost, taking into
             account market structure, cost of compliance, anti-money laundering
             measures, and consumer protection; and

      (ii)   a mechanism for setting maximum allowable Total Remittance Cost for
             speci ied remittance corridors where market abuse, excessive pricing,
             or lack of effective competition is demonstrated.

   (b) In setting benchmarks or caps, the BSP shall consider the policy of ensuring
       reasonable charges for remittance services and the promotion of of icial
       remittance channels.

SECTION 7. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any person or RSP to:

   (a) misappropriate, convert, or divert remittances received in trust, on
       commission, for administration, or under any obligation to deliver to the OFW
       bene iciary, or deny receipt thereof;

   (b) take, transfer, or apply an OFW remittance without the consent or authority of
       the sender or the bene iciary;

   (c) impose or collect fees or charges not disclosed as required under Section 5 of
       this Act;

   (d) apply an exchange rate different from the Posted Exchange Rate or from the
       exchange rate disclosed and con irmed to the sender for the covered
       transaction;

   (e) refuse or fail to make required disclosures or postings under Section 5; and


                                                                          5|Page
   (f) violate BSP-issued benchmarks or maximum allowable Total Remittance Cost
       under Section 6, where applicable.

SECTION 8. Consultation and Notice for Fee Adjustments. —

   (a) RSPs shall provide at least sixty (60) days prior public notice of material
       changes in fees or pricing structures for OFW remittance products, in the
       manner prescribed by the BSP.

   (b) The BSP shall maintain a mechanism for receiving comments from the DMW,
       DOF, OFW organizations, and industry groups on material fee changes
       affecting OFW remittances.

SECTION 9. Enforcement; Coordinating Roles. —

   (a) The BSP shall be the primary regulator for purposes of supervising compliance
       of BSP-supervised institutions with Sections 5 to 8 of this Act, without
       prejudice to the powers of other agencies under their respective charters.

   (b) The DMW, in coordination with OWWA and the DOF, shall lead the
       implementation of the inancial education program under Section 11.

   (c) The DOF and the Bureau of Internal Revenue shall issue such guidance as may
       be necessary on any tax treatment provided by law, subject to iscal policy and
       existing tax rules.

SECTION 10. Penalties. —

   (a) Any person who commits any act under Section 7(a) or 7(b) shall, upon
       conviction, suffer the penalty of imprisonment of six (6) months and one (1)
       day to six (6) years and a ine of Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) to Seven
       hundred ifty thousand pesos (Php 750,000.00), without prejudice to
       prosecution under the Revised Penal Code and other special laws.

   (b) Any RSP that violates Sections 5, 6, 7(c) to 7(f), or 8 shall be subject to
       administrative sanctions by the BSP, including ines, suspension, restriction, or
       other measures authorized under BSP laws and regulations, without prejudice
       to criminal liability where fraud or conversion is proven.

   (c) If the violation is committed by a juridical entity, the responsible directors,
       of icers, or employees who authorized, knowingly tolerated, or participated in
       the violation shall be held liable, subject to due process.

SECTION 11. OFW Financial Education Program. —

   (a) The DMW, as lead agency, in coordination with OWWA, the DOF, and the BSP,
       shall provide a free and continuously updated inancial education program for
       OFWs and their families covering, at a minimum: budgeting and savings;
       remittance management; avoidance of scams; fair borrowing and interest
       awareness; basic investment and insurance products; and consumer
       complaint mechanisms.

   (b) Financial education shall form part of the Pre-Departure Orientation Seminar
       (PDOS) and shall be made available through online channels for OFWs and
       families, with appropriate coordination with Philippine overseas posts where
       feasible.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the BSP and the DMW, in coordination with the DOF
and other stakeholders, shall issue the necessary rules and regulations to implement
this Act.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
