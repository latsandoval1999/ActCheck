                             AN ACT
PROTECTING AND PROMOTING THE RIGHTS AND WELFARE OF COMMUTERS,
SETTING MINIMUM SERVICE AND INFRASTRUCTURE STANDARDS FOR PUBLIC
TRANSPORT AND MOBILITY FACILITIES, CREATING MECHANISMS FOR
COMMUTER REPRESENTATION AND COMPLAINTS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mobility is essential to everyday life. Millions of Filipinos rely on public transportation
to get to work, school, hospitals, and other basic services. However, many commuters
continue to face daily challenges such as heavy traf ic, unsafe or poorly maintained
facilities, lack of reliable passenger information, and limited opportunities to raise
concerns or seek assistance. These conditions not only make commuting stressful and
unsafe, but they also limit economic opportunities and worsen inequality, especially
for low-income workers who depend heavily on public transport.

This bill, to be known as the “Magna Carta of Commuters,” seeks to protect and
promote the rights and welfare of the commuting public. It establishes clear standards
for safe, accessible, and commuter-friendly public transportation services and
facilities. The measure ensures that commuters have access to clean terminals and
sanitary facilities, adequate passenger information during service disruptions, safe
infrastructure, and proper channels for complaints and assistance. It also strengthens
the participation of commuters in transport planning and policy discussions so that
their voices are heard in decisions that directly affect their daily lives.

The bill builds on existing laws that already recognize commuter rights. For example,
the Free Public Toilets Act (Republic Act No. 11311) guarantees that passengers may

use regular sanitary facilities in transport terminals free of charge. It also reinforces
accessibility standards under the Accessibility Law (Batas Pambansa Blg. 344) to
ensure that persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups can travel safely
and conveniently. At the same time, the measure harmonizes with the roles of key
transport agencies, including the Department of Transportation and the Metropolitan
Manila Development Authority, to ensure coordinated and effective implementation.

Ultimately, this Magna Carta seeks to shift transportation policy toward a system that
prioritizes people rather than cars—one that provides safe, reliable, and inclusive
mobility for all while supporting economic growth and environmental sustainability.
By recognizing and protecting the rights of commuters, this measure aims to uplift the
everyday experience of millions of Filipinos who depend on public transportation.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                                   CHAPTER I
                               GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “The Magna Carta of
Commuters.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure safe,
digni ied, accessible, reliable, and affordable mobility for the commuting public, and
to guarantee meaningful participation of commuters in policy and decision-making
processes affecting public transportation and mobility infrastructure.

In all transport planning, regulation, enforcement, and service delivery, the welfare
and safety of commuters shall be a paramount consideration, consistent with law and
the respective mandates of national agencies, local government units (LGUs), and
metropolitan authorities.



                                                                           3|Page
SECTION 3. Scope. — This Act shall apply to: (a) national government agencies
involved in transportation and mobility infrastructure; (b) LGUs; (c) government-
owned and controlled corporations engaged in transport; (d) public utility vehicle
(PUV) operators and transport network service providers, where applicable; and (e)
owners, operators, or administrators of public transport terminals, stations, stops,
rest areas, and similar facilities.



                                    CHAPTER II
                               DEFINITION OF TERMS

SECTION 4. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

   (a) Commuter refers to a person who travels from one place to another for work,
       study, livelihood, access to services, or other lawful purpose, using public
       transport, active transport, or a combination thereof.

   (b) Active Transport refers to non-motorized travel, including walking and
       cycling.

   (c) Mobility Infrastructure refers to physical facilities supporting movement,
       including sidewalks, crossings, bicycle lanes, PUV stops and terminals, rail
       stations, ports, and intermodal facilities.

   (d) Public Transport refers to public sector and franchised or authorized private
       transport services over land, rail, and water, including, as applicable, jeepneys,
       buses, UV express, railways, ferries, taxis, and tricycles, subject to regulation.

   (e) High-Volume Transport Corridor refers to a corridor, facility, or terminal with
       passenger low of at least two thousand (2,000) persons per hour per direction
       during peak periods, as determined under the IRR.

   (f) Advance Notice refers to notice of a public consultation or hearing, given at
       least ten (10) working days prior to the activity, stating the subject, proposed
       action, venue/platform, and how to submit comments.

                              CHAPTER III
               RIGHTS OF COMMUTERS AND CORRELATIVE DUTIES

SECTION 5. Right to Safe, Accessible, and Digni ied Mobility. — Commuters shall
have the right to public transport services and mobility infrastructure that are safe
and dignified and that reasonably respond to mobility needs, subject to applicable
planning standards and available resources.

All public transport facilities and services shall comply with accessibility
requirements for persons with disabilities (PWDs) and other persons with impaired
mobility, consistent with existing accessibility law. No permit or authorization for
covered facilities shall be issued without compliance with such accessibility
requirements.

SECTION 6. Minimum Standards for Terminals, Stations, Stops, and Rest Areas.
— Owners, operators, or administrators of land transport terminals, stations, stops,
and rest areas shall provide clean, adequate, and safe sanitary facilities for
passengers, consistent with applicable standards.

It shall be unlawful to collect fees from passengers for the use of regular sanitary
facilities in such land transport terminals, stations, stops, rest areas, and roll-on/roll-
off (RORO) terminals, subject to the exceptions provided by law.

SECTION 7. Right to Information and Assistance. — Commuters shall have the
right to timely, accurate, and accessible information on routes, fares, service
advisories, and disruptions. Transport operators and terminal/station
administrators shall post service information at conspicuous places and through
digital channels, as practicable, consistent with standards to be issued in the IRR.

SECTION 8. Service Disruptions; Minimum Passenger Assistance. — In case of a
service breakdown or disruption attributable to the transport operator or facility
administrator, affected passengers shall be entitled to: (a) timely advisories; (b)
reasonable assistance to secure onward travel; and (c) access to a safe waiting area
and sanitary facilities.

For land transport terminals and similar facilities, access to regular sanitary facilities
shall remain free as provided by law, upon compliance with reasonable veri ication
measures.



                                                                               5|Page
SECTION 9. Participatory Rights; Public Consultations. — Commuters and
commuter groups shall be given advance notice and reasonable opportunity to submit
comments on proposed routes, service rationalization, fare changes, major
infrastructure projects, and other actions that materially affect commuter welfare, in
the manner provided in the IRR.

The concerned agency shall publish a summary of received comments and a response
matrix within a reasonable period to be set in the IRR.



                                 CHAPTER IV
                          INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISMS

SECTION 10. Of ice of Commuter Affairs (OCA). — There is hereby created an Of ice
of Commuter Affairs under the Department of Transportation (DOTr), which shall
serve as the central commuter assistance, complaints referral, and policy
coordination unit.

LGUs are encouraged to establish a local commuter assistance desk or unit, which may
be lodged in an existing of ice, consistent with local autonomy and budgetary capacity.

SECTION 11. Powers and Functions of the OCA. — The OCA shall:

   (a) operate commuter assistance desks and receive complaints;
   (b) refer complaints to the appropriate regulator or enforcement body and
       monitor resolution;
   (c) develop commuter service standards and templates for advisories and public
       consultations;
   (d) coordinate with concerned agencies on commuter welfare programs; and
   (e) prepare an annual “State of Commuters Report” with key indicators, subject to
       data availability.

SECTION 12. Harmonization with Existing Mandates; Metro Manila. — Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to diminish the powers and functions of agencies as
provided by existing law.

For Metro Manila, transport and traf ic management functions with metro-wide
impact shall be exercised in accordance with the MMDA’s statutory mandate, and LGU
participation in traf ic enforcement shall be subject to deputation and coordination
as provided by law and jurisprudence.

                             CHAPTER V
            IMPLEMENTING RULES, ENFORCEMENT, AND PENALTIES

SECTION 13. Implementing Agencies. — The DOTr shall be the lead implementing
agency. The DPWH, DILG, MMDA, DENR, DTI, and concerned LGUs shall provide
support within their respective mandates.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from effectivity, the DOTr, in consultation with concerned agencies,
transport operators, commuter groups, civil society, and the academe, shall
promulgate the IRR.

The IRR shall include measurable standards and phased compliance schedules, taking
into account sectoral regulators and the practice of issuing sector-speci ic rules where
appropriate.

SECTION 15. Delegation and Standards. — The IRR shall operationalize this Act by
setting (a) minimum service information standards; (b) minimum assistance during
disruptions; (c) consultation procedures; and (d) complaint handling protocols,
consistent with the principle that administrative rules must be anchored on suf icient
legislative standards.

SECTION 16. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are prohibited:

   (a) refusal to comply with the free-use-of-regular-sanitary-facilities requirement
       where applicable;

   (b) willful posting or dissemination of materially false service advisories that
       endanger commuters; and (c) retaliation against complainants or witnesses
       who ile good-faith complaints under this Act.

SECTION 17. Penalties. — Any person or entity found to have willfully violated this
Act or its IRR, after due notice and hearing, shall be subject to:

   (a) for establishments or operators: a ine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos
       (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos (₱200,000.00)
       per offense, without prejudice to suspension or cancellation of
       permits/franchises under existing laws; and

   (b) for public of icials: administrative liability under existing civil service and
       related laws, without prejudice to criminal liability if the act constitutes an
       offense under other laws.

SECTION 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 19. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, regulations, and other issuances
or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied
accordingly.

SECTION 20. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
