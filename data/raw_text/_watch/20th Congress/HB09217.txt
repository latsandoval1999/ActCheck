                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution expressly requires the State to safeguard workers'
rights, promote their overall welfare, and strictly prohibit combinations in restraint
of trade alongside unfair market competition.

      At present, private sector employers hold the exclusive authority to dictate
the specific banking institution where employee salaries are deposited. This
entrenched practice creates unintended negative consequences on worker
convenience and broader economic competition. Certain banks achieve an unfair
competitive advantage, securing a captive market simply because a singular
corporate entity handpicked them, rather than earning those clients through
superior service.

        All employees must be granted the absolute freedom to select which
traditional bank or Electronic Money Issuer they will trust for their wage deposits. In
choosing an ideal depository institution, individuals consider vital factors like
attractive financial products, higher interest rates, digital service availability, and
geographical accessibility. If financial institutions were compelled to actively
compete for individual payroll deposits, they would be strongly incentivized to
provide superior goods and minimize account fees

       The financial landscape has evolved dramatically. Today, real-time inter-bank
transfer networks and advanced digital wallets make cross-institution payroll
disbursements completely seamless and technically effortless. This updated measure
aims to grant private sector employees the undisputed right to designate their
preferred bank or digital wallet for wage deposits, firmly promoting true market
competition and financial autonomy. Crucially, the bill mandates that electronic
transfer fees incurred during disbursement must be fully borne by the employer,
guaranteeing that workers' salaries remain intact.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

 AN ACT GRANTING EMPLOYEES IN THE PRIVATE SECTOR THE RIGHT TO
DESIGNATE THE FINANCIAL OR ELECTRONIC MONEY ACCOUNT TO WHICH
THEIR WAGES OR COMPENSATION SHALL BE DEPOSITED, AMENDING FOR
     THE PURPOSE ARTICLE 102 OF PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 442,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE LABOR CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                                    TITLE I
                              GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Payroll Account
Portability Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – A second paragraph shall be inserted to Article 102,
Chapter III, Title I, Book Three, of Presidential Decree No. 442 as amended and
renumbered, otherwise known as the Labor Code of the Philippines, so that the
entire first paragraph shall read as follows:

        "ART. 102. Forms of Payment.- No employer shall pay the wages of an
        employee by means of promissory notes, vouchers, coupons, tokens, tickets,
        chits, or any object other than legal tender, even when expressly requested
        by the employee.

             AN EMPLOYEE SHALL HAVE THE RIGHT TO DESIGNATE THE
        BANK OR ELECTRONIC MONEY ISSUER (EMI) ACCOUNT TO


                                                                                      1
           WHICH THEIR WAGES SHALL BE DEPOSITED. IN THE EVENT THAT
           INTER-BANK OR ELECTRONIC TRANSFER FEES ARE INCURRED TO
           FACILITATE THE DEPOSIT OF WAGES INTO THE EMPLOYEE'S
           DESIGNATED ACCOUNT, SUCH FEES SHALL BE BORNE BY THE
           EMPLOYER AND SHALL NOT BE DEDUCTED FROM THE
           EMPLOYEE'S WAGES: PROVIDED, THAT IF THE EMPLOYEE'S
           DESIGNATED ACCOUNT IS NOT TECHNOLOGICALLY FEASIBLE
           FOR ELECTRONIC TRANSFER, THE EMPLOYER MAY PAY THE
           EMPLOYEE'S WAGES BY ISSUING A COMPANY CHECK, BY
           DISBURSING CASH TO THE EMPLOYEE OR BY DEPOSITING THE
           WAGES IN AN ALTERNATIVE ACCOUNT CHOSEN BY THE
           EMPLOYEE, WHICH MAY EITHER BE A DEFAULT ACCOUNT
           PROVIDED BY THE EMPLOYER OR ANOTHER ACCOUNT OF THE
           EMPLOYEE'S OWN CHOOSING.

                Payment of wages by check or money order shall be allowed when
           such manner of payment is customary on the date of effectivity of this Code,
           or is necessary because of special circumstances as speci ed in appropriate
           regulations to be issued by the Secretary of Labor and Employment or as
           stipulated in a collective bargaining agreement.”

SEC. 3. Implementing Rules. – The Department of Labor and Employment shall,
within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, promulgate such rules and
regulations as may be necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with this Act, are hereby
repealed, amended or modi ed accordingly.

SEC 5. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of cial Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
