                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 15 of Article II of the 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall
protect and promote the right to health of the people, while Section 20 recognizes
the indispensable role of the private sector in national development and the
importance of encouraging private enterprise and needed investments. Consistent
with these mandates, this measure seeks to establish a specialized framework for
hospitals that provide healthcare services to international patients by strengthening
patient safety, quality of care, hospital capacity, and institutional competitiveness.
International research indicates that healthcare infrastructure and institutional
capacity are important factors in the development and competitiveness of medical
tourism destinations.

       Philippine research demonstrates that medical tourism remains affected by
institutional and service-delivery challenges. Studies involving Philippine
healthcare providers found that trust and network building are important in

addressing uncertainty and strengthening relationships with international patients.1
Research on medical tourism facilitators in the Philippines likewise emphasized the
importance of patient coordination, information dissemination, consultation, and
support services throughout the medical tourism process.2 Philippine policy research
further identified concerns involving regulatory coordination, healthcare
infrastructure, international accreditation, price transparency, and health information
systems as relevant considerations in strengthening the country's medical tourism
sector.3

       International research further shows that accreditation, quality management,
and continuity of care are significant considerations in cross-border healthcare. A
study of foreign patients in Colombia found that treatment in an internationally
accredited facility was associated with higher perceptions of healthcare quality.4
Research on international medical travelers has also highlighted the potential of
digital health and video consultations to support pre-travel and post-travel
coordination and continuity of care.5 More recent Philippine research identified
fragmented governance, limited international accreditation, infrastructure
deficiencies, healthcare workforce concerns, and digital health gaps as continuing
challenges in the development of medical tourism.6

      Recognizing these findings, this proposed legislation establishes
supplemental national standards for medical tourism hospitals covering international
patient coordination, patient information and transparency, informed consent,
language and cultural competency, post-operative care, medical records, patient
support, quality management, patient safety, and grievance mechanisms. It further
creates a Medical Tourism Hospital Development and Upgrading Program to

promote the right to health and to support the development of a safe, quality-driven,

      international patients;

      medical tourism hospitals;

      (d) Facilitate the attainment and maintenance of recognized international
      healthcare accreditation;

      (e) Strengthen coordination among the DOH, DOT, Board of Investments

                                    CHAPTER II
                    ENHANCED NATIONAL STANDARDS

      SEC. 5. Establishment of Enhanced National Standards.              The DOH, in
coordination with the DOT and in consultation with concerned government
agencies, healthcare professionals, hospitals, patients, industry stakeholders, and
other relevant organizations, shall establish the Enhanced National Standards for
Qualified Medical Tourism Hospitals.

      The Enhanced National Standards shall supplement applicable hospital
licensing, health regulatory, and tourism accreditation requirements.

      The standards established under this Act shall not constitute a separate
hospital licensing system or a separate tourism accreditation system.

      SEC. 6. Scope of the Enhanced National Standards.                 The Enhanced
National Standards shall include appropriate requirements on the following:


                                                                                   4
(a) International Patient Intake and Coordination.       Procedures for patient
intake, scheduling, admission, clinical coordination, discharge, and
coordination of services;

(b) Patient Information and Transparency. Clear and accessible information
regarding the nature of the healthcare service, treatment process, material
risks, expected recovery period, payment arrangements, and other relevant
conditions of treatment;

(c) Informed Consent.       Procedures that ensure that an international patient
receives understandable information necessary for informed decision-making
in accordance with applicable law and professional standards;

(d) Language and Cultural Competency.             Appropriate mechanisms to
address language, communication, and cultural needs of international
patients;

(e) Post-Operative Care and Continuity of Care.       Procedures for follow-up,
post-operative monitoring, referral, discharge planning, and continuity of
care, including arrangements relevant to an international patient's return to the
patient's place of residence;

(f) Medical Records and Health Information.           Secure creation, storage,
access, transfer, and exchange of relevant medical information in accordance
with applicable health information, privacy, and data protection laws;

(g) Patient Support Services.       Appropriate patient-support arrangements
related to admission, treatment, recovery, communication, and discharge;




                                                                               5
      (h) Quality Management and Patient Safety.     Systems for quality assurance,
      patient safety, incident reporting, risk management, and continuous
      improvement;

      (i) Complaints and Grievance Mechanisms.       Accessible procedures through
      which international patients may raise concerns and seek appropriate hospital-
      level assistance; and

      (j) Emergency and Care Coordination.            Appropriate procedures for
      emergency response, referral, transfer, and coordination with other health
      facilities.

      SEC. 7. Recognition as a Qualified Medical Tourism Hospital. The DOH,
in coordination with the DOT, shall establish a procedure for verifying compliance
with the Enhanced National Standards and recognizing a hospital as a Qualified
Medical Tourism Hospital.

      The recognition under this section shall be used solely for purposes of the
programs, support measures, and incentive facilitation established under this Act.

      The recognition shall not replace or amend the license to operate issued by the
DOH or the accreditation issued by the DOT.

      SEC. 8. Periodic Review of Standards.       The DOH and DOT shall review
the Enhanced National Standards at least once every three (3) years or as may be
necessary to consider developments in healthcare delivery, medical technology,
patient safety, digital health, international healthcare practices, and recognized
international standards.

                                   CHAPTER III
 MEDICAL TOURISM HOSPITAL DEVELOPMENT AND UPGRADING
                                    PROGRAM

      SEC. 9. Medical Tourism Hospital Development and Upgrading Program.
  The DOH, in coordination with the DOT, BOI, other appropriate IPAs, and
concerned government agencies, shall establish a Medical Tourism Hospital
Development and Upgrading Program.

      The Program shall support qualified hospital projects that improve their
capacity to comply with the Enhanced National Standards and provide safe,
efficient, and internationally competitive healthcare services to international
patients.

      The Program shall provide separate implementation mechanisms, as
appropriate, for public and private hospitals.

      SEC. 10. Eligible Modernization Measures.      The following activities may
qualify as Eligible Modernization Measures:

      (a) Acquisition, installation, replacement, or upgrading of medical equipment
      and technology;

      (b) Construction, renovation, expansion, or modernization of hospital
      facilities;

      (c) Development or improvement of patient-support infrastructure;

      (d) Establishment or upgrading of quality management and patient-safety
      systems;


                                                                                 7
      (e) Development or upgrading of digital health and medical information
      systems;

      (f) Development of international-patient coordination and support facilities;

      (g) Expenses directly attributable to the attainment, maintenance, or renewal
      of recognized international healthcare accreditation; and

      (h) Other capital investments or upgrading activities that the DOH and DOT
      determine to be necessary for compliance with the Enhanced National
      Standards.

      SEC. 11. Technical Assistance.       The DOH and DOT shall, within their
respective mandates and subject to available appropriations, provide technical
assistance to hospitals seeking recognition as Qualified Medical Tourism Hospitals.

      Technical assistance may include standards orientation, compliance guidance,
capacity-building, assessment, quality-improvement assistance, and coordination
with concerned government agencies.

      SEC. 12. International Healthcare Accreditation. The DOH and DOT shall
facilitate the attainment and maintenance of recognized international healthcare
accreditation by Qualified Medical Tourism Hospitals.

      The standards and procedures under this section shall not require accreditation
by a particular private accreditation organization unless authorized by law.

      Expenses directly attributable to recognized international healthcare
accreditation may qualify as an Eligible Modernization Measure under this Act.

and avail of the fiscal and non-fiscal incentives available under such laws.

                                   CHAPTER VI
                       FUNDING AND FINAL PROVISIONS

        SEC. 25. Funding.     The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act of the concerned implementing
agencies, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, procurement, and auditing
laws.

        The implementing agencies may also receive and utilize grants, donations,
and other legally available resources consistent with law.

        SEC. 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations.          Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Health and the Secretary of Tourism,
in coordination with the Secretary of Finance, the BOI, FIRB, other appropriate
IPAs, and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary
for the effective implementation of this Act.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall provide for:

      (a) The Enhanced National Standards;

      (b) The procedure for recognition and renewal of Qualified Medical Tourism

      (c) The Medical Tourism Hospital Development and Upgrading Program;

      Measures;

      (e) The inter-agency coordination mechanism;

      (f) The registry and monitoring system;

      (g) The procedure for coordination with IPAs; and

      SEC. 27. Non-Diminution of Regulatory Authority.          Nothing in this Act
shall diminish or transfer:

      (a) The authority of the DOH over hospital licensing, health facility
      regulation, clinical standards, and patient safety;

      (b) The authority of the DOT over tourism standards, accreditation, and
      tourism promotion; and

      (c) The authority of the BOI, FIRB, and other appropriate IPAs over
      investment registration and the administration of incentives.

      SEC. 30. Effectivity.     This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
