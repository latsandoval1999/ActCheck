                             EXPLANATORY NOTE
        Republic Act No. 11036, or the “Mental Health Act,” was a landmark step
in recognizing mental health as a matter of national policy. Section 21 of the
law directs the Department of Health (DOH) to establish twenty-four hour,
toll-free, and accessible hotlines for suicide prevention. Since its passage, this
mandate remains only partly realized. Several agencies and private
organizations operate separate helplines using different numbers, protocols,
and hours of operation, resulting in a fragmented system where a person in
crisis may not know which number to call, or may reach a line that cannot
respond in time.
       This proposed Act does not attempt to overhaul the country’s entire
mental health crisis infrastructure, but focuses on a single, achievable, and
urgently needed reform, one that establishes a three-digit national hotline that
is easy to remember, free of charge on all networks, and capable of receiving
calls, text messages, and the like. Consistent with how mental health crises
actually are, the hotline is intended to serve any person experiencing a mental
health crisis, whether they are under overwhelming stress, anxiety, panic,
grief, trauma, or other acute emotional distress, regardless of whether this
leads to thoughts of self-harm or suicide. In light of the prevailing climate of
uncertainty, escalating violence, and disruptions brought about by an
always-connected, online, and overstimulating world, more and more are
having to navigate complex mental health landscapes. This necessitates
convenient platforms for listening and helping address such concerns.
      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this Bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
   ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS HOTLINE,
   MANDATING ITS OPERATION, INTEROPERABILITY, AND ADEQUATE
              FUNDING, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National
Mental Health Crisis Hotline Act.”
       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State, consistent
with Republic Act No. 11036, to protect and promote the right to mental health
of all Filipinos and to value the psychological well-being of the citizenry as
much as their physical well-being. Toward this end, the State recognizes that
timely, accessible, and confidential support during a mental health crisis saves
lives and prevents the escalation of psychological distress. The State shall
therefore establish and maintain a single, unified, and adequately resourced
national hotline for mental health crises, distinct and separate from, but
interoperable with, existing response systems.
      SECTION 3. Definitions. – As used in this Act:
         a) Mental health crisis refers to any situation in which a person’s
            thoughts, feelings, or behavior place the person at risk of harm to
            self or others, or otherwise severely compromise the person’s
            ability to cope or function, requiring timely support or intervention.
            This includes, but is not limited to, acute stress, anxiety or panic
            episodes, grief, trauma, psychosis, and thoughts of self-harm or
            suicide. A mental health crisis need not involve suicidal ideation to
            warrant a response under this Act;
         b)​ National Mental Health Crisis Hotline or Hotline refers to the
             three-digit telephone, text, and chat-based service established
             under this Act;
         c)​ Mental Health refers to the state of well-being in which an
             individual realizes one's own abilities and potentials, scopes
             adequately with the normal stresses of life, displays resilience in
             the face of extreme life events, works productively and fruitfully,
             and is able to make a positive contribution to the community;
         d)​ Crisis counselor refers to a person trained and certified under
             standards issued by the DOH to receive and respond to calls,
             messages, or chats to the Hotline;
      SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Mental Health Crisis Hotline. –
There is hereby established a National Mental Health Crisis Hotline, which
shall be the primary, unified point of contact for any person experiencing a
mental health crisis as defined in this Act, or for any person seeking help on
behalf of another:
      a)​ The DOH, through the Philippine Council for Mental Health (PCMH),
          shall operate the Hotline twenty-four (24) hours a day, seven (7) days
          a week, through voice call, short messaging service (SMS), and web-
          or application-based chat;
      b)​ Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in
          coordination with the National Telecommunications Commission
          (NTC) and Department of Information and Communications
          Technology (DICT), shall secure and activate a single, nationwide,
          easy-to-remember three-digit number for the Hotline, which shall be
          free of charge when called, texted, or accessed from any mobile or
          fixed-line network;
      c)​ Existing hotlines operated by government agencies and accredited
          private or non-government organizations may continue to operate
          and shall be encouraged to interconnect or refer calls to the Hotline,
          but the Hotline established under this Act shall be promoted as the
          primary national number for purposes of public awareness and
          emergency interoperability;
      d)​ The Hotline shall be staffed at all times by adequately trained crisis
          counselors, in a ratio sufficient to meet the response standards under
          Section 5, and shall have access to on-call licensed mental health
          professionals for escalation and consultation; and
      e)​ The Hotline shall receive and respond to calls, messages, and chats
          from any person in a mental health crisis, regardless of whether the
          crisis involves thoughts of self-harm or suicide. No caller shall be
          denied initial support, triage, or referral solely because the caller’s
          distress has not escalated to suicidal ideation.
     SECTION 5. Response Standards. – The PCMH shall adopt and enforce
minimum service standards for the Hotline, including, at the minimum:
      a)​ Answering of calls, messages, or chats within twenty (20) seconds or
          the shortest practicable time;
      b)​ Initial triage by a qualified crisis counselor within two (2) minutes of
          being answered;
      c)​ Referral to emergency medical services, the nearest DOH-retained or
          accredited facility, or local government health services for callers
          assessed to be in imminent danger; and
      d)​ Where the caller consents, a follow-up contact within twenty-four (24)
          to seventy-two (72) hours after a high-risk call.
      SECTION 6. Regional Coordination. – In lieu of creating new regional
command centers, the existing DOH Centers for Health Development shall
serve as the regional coordination points for the Hotline, tasked with
monitoring call volume and response times within their respective regions,
coordinating referrals to local health facilities and local government units, and
reporting regional performance to the PCMH.
       SECTION 7. Interoperability. – The DICT, in coordination with the DOH,
the NTC, and the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
shall issue within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act
the technical standards necessary to:
      a)​ Enable call transfer and data-sharing protocols between the Hotline
          and the 911 national emergency hotline and local emergency response
          systems, subject to Section 8;
      b)​ Ensure the Hotline is accessible through basic mobile phones without
          internet access, in addition to smartphone tand web-based channels;
          and
      c)​ Ensure continuity of service during natural disasters and network
          disruptions,     including      redundancy     arrangements     with
          telecommunications providers.
       SECTION 8. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. – All calls, messages, and
chats to the Hotline, and all records generated in the course of providing a
response, shall be treated as confidential and shall be processed in accordance
with Republic Act No. 10173, or the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other
applicable laws. Caller information shall not be disclosed except: (a) with the
caller’s consent; (b) when necessary to prevent imminent harm to the caller or
another person, consistent with existing legal and ethical standards for mental
health professionals; or (c) when required by law. The DOH shall adopt privacy
safeguards, and any identified data used for monitoring or research under
Section 5 and Section 6 shall not permit re-identification of individual callers.
      SECTION 9. Public Awareness Campaigns. – The DOH, in coordination
with the Philippine Information Agency, Department of Education (DepEd), the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and local government units, shall
conduct a continuing public awareness campaign to promote the Hotline
number, including through mandatory posting of the Hotline number in public
schools, health facilities, and government offices, and through partnerships
with telecommunications companies for periodic SMS or network-based public
service announcements.
       SECTION 10. PhilHealth Coordination. – The Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth) is encouraged to study and develop, within two (2)
years from the effectivity of this Act, an appropriate benefit or reimbursement
mechanism for crisis assessment and stabilization services directly linked to
referrals made through the Hotline.
       SECTION 11. Funding. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act, including the acquisition of the three-digit number,
hiring and training of crisis counselors, communications technology, and
system interoperability, shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued operation of the Hotline shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act. The DOH may accept grants, donations, and technical
assistance from local and international organizations for purposes of this Act,
subject to existing accounting and auditing rules.
       SECTION 12. Reporting. – The PCMH shall submit an annual report to
Congress on the operations of the Hotline, including call volume, response
times, and outcomes, without disclosing information that could identify
individual callers.
       SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one
hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, through
the PCMH, in consultation with the DICT, NTC, DILG, PhilHealth, and relevant
civil society and mental health advocacy groups, and people’s organizations,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations necessary to carry
out the provisions of this Act.
       SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of laws, presidential
decrees, letters of instruction, and other presidential issuances that are
incompatible or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby deemed
amended or repealed.
       SECTION 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act, or part
hereof, is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or provision
not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.
      SECTION 16. Effectivity Clause – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
