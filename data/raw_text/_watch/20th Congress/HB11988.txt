                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution commands the State to protect
and promote the right to health of the people. Republic Act No. 11036, or the Mental
Health Act, gave that command a mental health dimension in 2018, and Republic Act
No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care Act, promised every Filipino financial
protection against the cost of care. For many families, however, mental health care is
still paid out of pocket, one consultation and one prescription at a time, until the money
runs out and treatment stops.

      The need is not small. The World Health Organization estimates that between
11.3 and 11.6 percent of Filipinos live with a mental disorder.1 The National Center for
Mental Health (NCMH) received 7,189 suicide-related calls from January to
September 2025 alone, already more than it received in the whole of 2024, and most
callers were between 18 and 30 years old.2 These are students, young workers and
new parents: the people whose recovery matters most to the households that depend
on them.

      The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) has taken a first step
through its outpatient mental health package, worth ₱9,000 for the general package
and ₱16,000 for the specialty package. Yet PhilHealth itself has reported that of some
3,000 physicians trained by the Department of Health (DOH) under the Mental Health

16         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive
17   Mental Health Benefit Act of 2026.”

18         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
19   promote the right to health of the people, including the right to mental health, and to
20   ensure that every Filipino has financial protection against the cost of mental health
21   care. Consistent with Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health
22   Act,” and Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care
23   Act,” the State shall ensure that mental health services are covered by the National
24   Health Insurance Program on an equal footing with other health services, are available
25   as close as possible to where people live, and are delivered in a manner that respects
26   the dignity, privacy and autonomy of service users.

27         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

28           (a)   Accredited provider refers to a health care provider or facility accredited
29                 by PhilHealth to deliver services under the mental health benefit
30                 package, including primary care facilities, community-based mental
31                 health facilities, hospitals, and individual licensed professionals;




                                                 1
 1           (b)   Mental health condition refers to a neurologic or psychiatric condition as
 2                 defined under Republic Act No. 11036, determined on the basis of
 3                 scientifically accepted medical nomenclature, including the International
 4                 Classification of Diseases of the World Health Organization;

 5           (c)   Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) refers to the program
 6                 of the World Health Organization for scaling up care for mental,
 7                 neurological and substance use conditions through non-specialist health
 8                 workers;

 9           (d)   Mental health professional refers to a medical doctor, psychologist,
10                 nurse, social worker, or any other appropriately trained and qualified
11                 person with specific skills relevant to the provision of mental health
12                 services, as defined under Republic Act No. 11036;

13           (e)   Perinatal mental health condition refers to a mental health condition
14                 arising during pregnancy or within twelve (12) months after childbirth or
15                 pregnancy loss, including postpartum depression and postpartum
16                 psychosis;

17           (f)   PhilHealth refers to the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation; and

18           (g)   Psychiatric emergency refers to a condition presenting a serious and
19                 immediate threat to the health and well-being of a service user or of
20                 others, requiring immediate intervention, including acute suicidal risk,
21                 acute psychosis and severe agitation.

22         SEC. 4. Comprehensive Mental Health Benefit Package. – Within one
23   hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, PhilHealth, in consultation
24   with the DOH, the Philippine Council for Mental Health, the Health Technology
25   Assessment Council, professional organizations, organizations of service users and
26   carers, and local government units (LGUs), shall develop and implement a
27   comprehensive mental health benefit package covering all mental health conditions.
28   The package shall be available to all PhilHealth members and their qualified
29   dependents, including minors.

 1         SEC. 5. Minimum Covered Services. – The comprehensive mental health
 2   benefit package shall cover, at a minimum, the following services:

 3           (a)   Screening, assessment and diagnosis, including psychological testing
 4                 and other diagnostic services required by the treatment plan;

 5           (b)   Outpatient consultations with psychiatrists, other physicians trained
 6                 under mhGAP, and licensed psychologists;

 7           (c)   Psychotherapy and other evidence-based psychosocial interventions
 8                 delivered by licensed mental health professionals, individually or in
 9                 groups, including family interventions;

10           (d)   Essential medicines for mental health conditions listed in the Philippine
11                 National Formulary, dispensed in quantities sufficient to ensure
12                 continuity of treatment;

13           (e)   Emergency care and crisis stabilization for psychiatric emergencies,
14                 including emergency room care and short-stay observation;

15           (f)   Inpatient care for acute mental health conditions in general hospitals and
16                 specialty mental health facilities;

17           (g)   Day treatment, partial hospitalization, psychosocial rehabilitation and
18                 community reintegration services;

19           (h)   Telepsychiatry and other telehealth services, subject to DOH standards
20                 on quality, safety and data privacy;

21           (i)   Screening, diagnosis and treatment of perinatal mental health
22                 conditions;

23           (j)   Age-appropriate assessment, treatment and continuing care for children
24                 and adolescents, including developmental and neurodevelopmental
25                 conditions; and

26           (k)   Treatment of substance use disorders and co-occurring conditions,
27                 consistent with existing laws.




                                                 3
 1          SEC. 6. Tiered Benefit Levels. – PhilHealth shall structure the benefit package
 2   into tiers corresponding to the severity and clinical needs of the service user. Each tier
 3   shall provide, per beneficiary per year, not less than the following:

 4           (a)   Tier 1 (Basic), for mild conditions and early intervention: six (6) outpatient
 5                 consultations, six (6) psychotherapy or psychosocial intervention
 6                 sessions, and medicines prescribed under the treatment plan;

 7           (b)   Tier 2 (Standard), for moderate conditions: ten (10) outpatient
 8                 consultations, twelve (12) psychotherapy or psychosocial intervention
 9                 sessions, psychological testing, and medicines prescribed under the
10                 treatment plan; and

11           (c)   Tier 3 (Comprehensive), for severe, complex or chronic conditions: such
12                 outpatient consultations as the treatment plan requires, twenty (20)
13                 psychotherapy or psychosocial intervention sessions, medicines
14                 prescribed under the treatment plan, and access to rehabilitation, day
15                 treatment and inpatient services.

16          A beneficiary may move between tiers upon reassessment by the attending
17   accredited provider. Emergency care, crisis stabilization and inpatient care for acute
18   conditions shall be covered regardless of tier. PhilHealth may increase the
19   entitlements under this Section and determine the corresponding payment rates on
20   the basis of actuarial studies and costing, but shall not reduce the minimum
21   entitlements provided herein.

22          SEC. 7. Eligibility and Access. – A member or dependent shall be eligible for
23   the benefit package upon assessment by any accredited provider. No prior certification
24   from a specialist, a DOH hospital or any other office shall be required as a condition
25   for availing of Tier 1 or Tier 2 benefits or of emergency care. Referral to a specialist
26   for Tier 3 benefits shall be made by the attending accredited provider in accordance
27   with the referral pathways of the province-wide or city-wide health system. PhilHealth
28   shall allow the use of electronic and simplified claims processes and shall not require
29   the service user to submit documents already in the possession of the accredited
30   provider.

17                 under this Act within ninety (90) days from receipt. An application not

20         SEC. 9. Contracting Through Local Health Systems. – PhilHealth shall
21   contract the delivery of the benefit package through the province-wide and city-wide
22   health systems established under Republic Act No. 11223, and shall develop payment
23   mechanisms that pay these systems for population-based and individual-based
24   mental health services, including capitation or bundled payments for community-
25   based care. Payments received by an LGU under this Section shall be retained in its
26   Special Health Fund and used for health services, including mental health services, in
27   accordance with Republic Act No. 11223.

28         SEC. 10. No Balance Billing. – No other fees or expenses shall be charged to
29   indigent members, sponsored members, senior citizens and persons with disability
30   who avail of the benefit package in government facilities, consistent with the no

 1   balance billing policy of PhilHealth. For other members, PhilHealth shall publish fixed
 2   co-payment ceilings for services covered under this Act.

 3          SEC. 11. Confidentiality and Non-Discrimination. – All information on the
 4   mental health condition of a beneficiary obtained in the processing of claims shall be
 5   treated as confidential and processed in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173,
 6   otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and Republic Act No. 11036. No
 7   member shall be denied membership, benefits or any other privilege under the
 8   National Health Insurance Program, nor be charged higher premiums, on account of
 9   a past or present mental health condition.

10          SEC. 12. Grievance and Appeals. – PhilHealth shall establish a mechanism
11   for the prompt resolution of complaints and the appeal of denied claims under this Act.
12   An appeal shall be decided within thirty (30) days from filing.

13          SEC. 13. Information Campaign. – PhilHealth, in coordination with the DOH,
14   the Philippine Information Agency and LGUs, shall conduct a continuing nationwide
15   information campaign on the benefit package, including how and where it may be
16   availed of, with particular attention to young people, mothers before and after
17   childbirth, and families of persons with mental health conditions.

18          SEC. 14. Periodic Review. – The benefit package, including its payment rates,
19   shall be reviewed at least every two (2) years by PhilHealth with the Health Technology
20   Assessment Council, taking into account utilization, costs, clinical evidence and the
21   financial sustainability of the National Health Insurance Program, subject to Section 6
22   of this Act.

23          SEC. 15. Reportorial Requirement. – PhilHealth shall submit to Congress,
24   within ninety (90) days after the end of each year, a report on the implementation of
25   this Act. The report shall include the number of beneficiaries served by tier, condition,
26   age group, sex, province and city or municipality; the number and distribution of
27   accredited providers; claims paid and denied; and total expenditures. A summary of
28   the report shall be published on the PhilHealth website, without disclosing personal
29   information.

30          SEC. 16. Funding. – The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
31   charged against the funds of PhilHealth, consisting of premium contributions of direct

 6          SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
 7   from the effectivity of this Act, PhilHealth, in coordination with the DOH and in
 8   consultation with the stakeholders mentioned in Section 4, shall promulgate the rules
 9   and regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act.

10          SEC. 18. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
11   or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
12   and effect.

13          SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
14   regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended
15   or modified accordingly.

16          SEC. 20. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
17   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
