                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The proposed measure seeks to amend Article 95 of Presidential Decree (“P.D.”
No. 442, otherwise known as the Labor Code of the Philippines, by increasing the Service
Incentive Leave (“SIL”) from five (5) days to ten (10) days with pay, in recognition of the
evolving demands of the modern workplace and the State’s constitutional duty to protect
labor. Under Article XIII, Section 3 of the 1987 Constitution of the Philippines, the State is
mandated to afford full protection to labor, promote humane working conditions, and
ensure the well-being of workers. However, the current statutory minimum of five (5)
days SIL has remained unchanged for decades, despite significant shifts in labor
conditions, increased work intensity, and growing awareness of the importance of
employee health and work-life balance

       SIL, a mandatory benefit for employees with at least one year of service, provides
essential paid leave for rest and personal contingencies.The Philippine Supreme Court
has reinforced SIL as a mandatory benefit that must be provided or converted to cash if
unused.1,2

      The proposed increase to ten (10) days is grounded on both policy and empirical
considerations. Studies across jurisdictions show that adequate rest periods significantly
improve employee productivity, reduce burnout, and enhance overall well-being. The
World Health Organization has classified burnout as an occupational phenomenon
linked to chronic workplace stress, 3 while research literature demonstrates that paid

        From an economic perspective, increasing the SIL is not merely a cost imposition
on employers but a strategic investment in human capital. Evidence shows that
employees who are given sufficient rest are less likely to incur absenteeism, more likely
to remain in their jobs, and generally perform better.6 Thus, enhanced leave benefits
reduce turnover costs and increase organizational efficiency. This is consistent with the
State’s policy of promoting both labor welfare and industrial productivity.

       This proposed measure complements the objectives of the Republic Act No. 11058,
which mandates the provision of safe and healthy working conditions, and the Republic
Act No. 11036, which recognizes the importance of mental health in the workplace. By
expanding leave benefits, the State reinforces these policies and provides workers with
the time they need to recover from physical and psychological stress.

       Furthermore, the bill retains important safeguards for employers by maintaining
existing exemptions, such as for establishments with fewer than ten (10) employees and
those already providing equal or superior leave benefits. This ensures that the measure
remains balanced, taking into account both labor protection and business viability. At the
same time, the non-diminution clause embedded in labor law principles is preserved,
ensuring that employees continue to enjoy benefits already granted to them.

       Increasing the Service Incentive Leave to ten (10) days is a necessary and timely
reform that responds to contemporary labor realities. It upholds the constitutional
mandate to protect labor, aligns domestic policy with international standards, promotes
worker health and productivity, and ensures a more humane and equitable workplace.
By strengthening this fundamental labor benefit, the State affirms its commitment to the
dignity, welfare, and overall well-being of Filipino workers.

AN ACT INCREASING THE SERVICE INCENTIVE LEAVE OF EMPLOYEES,
    AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE, ARTICLE 95 OF PRESIDENTIAL DECREE
    NO. 442, AS AMENDED, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE LABOR CODE OF THE
    PHILIPPINES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

     SECTION 1. Article 95 of Presidential Decree No. 442, as amended, is hereby
amended to read as follows:

       “Article 95. Right to service incentive leave.
          (a) Every employee who has rendered at least one year of service shall be entitled
              to a yearly service incentive leave of [five] TEN days with pay.
          (b) This provision shall not apply to those who are already enjoying the benefit
              herein provided, those enjoying vacation leave with pay of at least [five] TEN
              days, and those employed in establishments regularly employing less than ten
              employees or in establishments exempted from granting this benefit by the
              Secretary of Labor after considering the viability or financial condition of such
              establishment.
          (c) The grant of benefit in excess of that provided herein shall not be made a
              subject of arbitration or any court or administrative action.”

      SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
approval of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment shall promulgate the rules
and regulations implementing the provisions of this Act, which shall take effect thirty (30)
days after its publication in any newspaper of general circulation.

       SEC. 3. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, and other
issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 4. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
