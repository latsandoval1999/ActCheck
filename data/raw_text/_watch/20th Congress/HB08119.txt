                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution clearly states that the State must protect both the life of the mother
and the life of the unborn from conception. This means that from the very beginning
of human life, the law has a duty to give protection. The Supreme Court has explained
that “conception” happens at fertilization, not later, and that the Constitution does not
allow abortion or the use of drugs or devices that destroy a fertilized human life. This
policy re lects the nation’s commitment to uphold the dignity and value of every
human life, while also caring for mothers.

Despite existing laws, confusion and disputes continue. There is still uncertainty
about what products may be considered abortifacients and how they should be
regulated, creating gaps that can weaken constitutional protections. At the same time,
there is concern that protecting the unborn should never prevent women from
receiving proper medical care, especially emergency treatment that saves the life of
the mother during pregnancy or childbirth. Both concerns must be addressed clearly
and responsibly.

This bill provides clear guidance that follows the Constitution and existing law. It
af irms protection of unborn life from fertilization, strengthens safeguards against
abortion and abortifacients, and at the same time guarantees access to necessary and

measure protects human life, supports women’s health, and gives doctors, regulators,
and families clear rules to follow. It is a balanced and compassionate approach that
honors both mother and child.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Unborn Child Protection
and Maternal Care Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the sanctity of family life
and shall protect and strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social institution.
It shall equally protect the life of the mother and the life of the unborn from
conception, and shall support the natural and primary right and duty of parents in the
rearing of the youth for civic ef iciency and the development of moral character.

It is hereby declared the policy of the State to: (a) protect prenatal life from
conception, understood as fertilization; (b) prevent and penalize acts of abortion as
de ined and punished under the Revised Penal Code; (c) ensure that maternal health
services during pregnancy are delivered in accordance with evidence-based medical
standards, with due regard to the State’s duty to protect prenatal life from conception;
and (d) promote informed, lawful, and ethical health care that respects family
autonomy and religious convictions consistent with law.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Conception refers to fertilization”, i.e., the union of the sperm and the egg
       resulting in a zygote.

   (b) “Unborn Child” refers to the human organism from conception (fertilization)
       until complete birth.

   (c) “Abortion” refers to intentional acts punished as abortion under Articles 256
       to 259 of the Revised Penal Code, and other acts that the law treats as abortion.

   (d) “Abortifacient ” refers to any drug, device, substance, or procedure that is
       intended to cause abortion or is designed to primarily destroy the life of the
       unborn from conception or prevent the implantation of the fertilized ovum,
       consistent with existing law and jurisprudence.

   (e) “Maternal Emergency Care” refers to medically indicated procedures or
       treatments necessary to prevent the death of, or serious permanent
       impairment of a major bodily function of, the pregnant woman, provided such
       care is performed in accordance with accepted medical standards and without
       intent to cause abortion.

SECTION 4. Af irmation of Protection of Prenatal Life; Construction. — This Act
shall be construed:

   (a) to af irm the constitutional duty to protect the life of the unborn from
       conception and the life of the mother; and

   (b) not to diminish any constitutional right, including religious freedom and the
       autonomy of the family, as recognized by the Constitution and jurisprudence.

SECTION 5. Prohibited Acts. — In addition to acts already punishable under existing
laws, it shall be unlawful for any person to:

   (a) Knowingly manufacture, distribute, dispense, advertise, or administer an
       abortifacient, except as may be allowed under existing regulatory laws and
       standards consistent with the Constitution; or

   (b) Knowingly aid or abet the commission of acts punishable as abortion under
       the Revised Penal Code.

                                                                            4|Page
SECTION 6. Maternal Care; No Criminalization of Legitimate Medical Treatment.
— Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted to:

   (a) penalize obstetric, prenatal, or emergency medical care that is medically
       indicated, performed in good faith, and consistent with accepted standards of
       care, where there is no intent to cause abortion; or

   (b) prohibit the management of ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, or other
       pregnancy-related complications in accordance with accepted medical
       practice, subject to existing laws.

SECTION 7. Relationship with Existing Laws; Non-Diminution. — This Act shall be
read in harmony with existing penal laws on abortion and with Republic Act No.
10354, which declares a national policy on responsible parenthood and reproductive
health while proscribing abortion and requiring protection of the unborn from
conception.

SECTION 8. Implementing Coordination. — The Department of Health (DOH), the
Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and other concerned agencies shall, within one
hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, issue joint guidelines
consistent with: (a) the constitutional protection of the unborn from conception; and
(b) the Supreme Court’s rulings on the standards for determining and regulating
abortifacients and the State’s interest in protecting prenatal life.

SECTION 9. Penalties. —

   (a) Acts constituting abortion shall remain punishable under Articles 256 to 259
       of the Revised Penal Code.

   (b) Violations of Section 5(a) of this Act (manufacture, distribution, dispensing,
       advertising, or administration of abortifacients) shall be punished by
       imprisonment of [insert range] and/or a ine of [insert range], without
       prejudice to prosecution under other applicable laws.

   (c) If the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible of icers who participated in
       or authorized the violation shall be liable.

   (d) Administrative sanctions, including license suspension or revocation, may be
       imposed under existing professional and regulatory laws.
