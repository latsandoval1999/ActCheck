                             AN ACT
  PROVIDING FOR OPTIONAL EARLY RETIREMENT FOR GOVERNMENT
    EMPLOYEES WITH DISABILITIES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
  REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8291, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “GOVERNMENT
 SERVICE INSURANCE SYSTEM ACT OF 1997”, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Persons with disabilities continue to make invaluable contributions to the Philippine civil
service despite facing barriers and additional disability-related costs throughout their careers.
Many government employees with disabilities devote decades of public service while
managing expenses for rehabilitation, medicines, assistive devices, accessible transportation,
personal assistance, and other support necessary to perform their duties and participate fully in
society.

While the Government Service Insurance System provides retirement benefits to qualified
government employees, existing retirement laws do not recognize the distinct circumstances
faced by persons with disabilities who often experience greater physical, financial, and health-
related demands over the course of their employment. As more persons with disabilities enter
and remain in government service through inclusive employment policies, there is a
corresponding need to ensure that the country’s retirement system likewise becomes more
responsive to their circumstances.

This proposed measure seeks to provide qualified government employees with disabilities the
option of early retirement upon reaching fifty-five (55) years of age and rendering at least ten
(10) years of government service. The measure is entirely voluntary and does not compel
retirement. Rather, it provides an additional retirement option for those who, after years of
dedicated public service, choose to retire earlier in recognition of the unique circumstances
associated with disability.
The bill likewise reflects the Philippines’ commitment under the United Nations Convention
on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD), which calls upon States Parties to adopt
appropriate measures that promote equality, inclusion, and social protection for persons with
disabilities. By recognizing disability-responsive retirement as a reasonable and equitable
social protection measure, the State affirms that equality sometimes requires policies that
respond to different circumstances rather than treating everyone identically.

This proposed legislation honors the dedication and sacrifices of government employees with
disabilities by providing them greater flexibility and security as they transition from public
service. It strengthens the government’s commitment to inclusive employment, responsive
social protection, and the dignity and well-being of persons with disabilities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “Optional Early Retirement for
Government Employees with Disabilities Act”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold the
dignity, promote the well-being, and protect the rights of persons with disabilities pursuant to
the Constitution, Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the Magna Carta for Persons
with Disabilities, as amended, and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons
with Disabilities.

The State recognizes the valuable contributions of persons with disabilities in public service
and affirms their right to equal employment opportunities, reasonable accommodation, social
protection, and meaningful participation in nation-building.

Towards this end, the State shall establish responsive retirement policies that recognize the
unique circumstances of qualified government employees with disabilities by providing an
option for early retirement after long years of public service, while ensuring the sustainability
of the Government Service Insurance System.

Section 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
   a) Disability: is an evolving concept that results from the interaction between
      persons with impairments and attitudinal and environmental barriers that hinders
      their full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with others;
   b) Disability Assessment and Determination: means the process by which disability
      of an individual is officially recognized for the purpose of eligibility
      determination to diverse entitlements. It is based on a human rights model of
      disability, with a focus on functional limitations, support requirements and
      barriers restricting inclusion and participation;
   c) Disability-related needs: Disability-related needs include disability-related
      medical supplies (e.g., medicines, catheters, diapers), medical-related services
      (e.g., rehabilitation services, etc.) assistive products, personal assistance, sign
      language interpreter, higher cost of accessing services because of lack of
      accessibility (e.g., transportation cost), and others;
   d) Persons with Disabilities: include those who have long-term physical, mental,
      intellectual or sensory impairments which in interaction with various barriers
      may hinder their full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with
      others. Persons who may fall in this definition are, but not limited to, the
      following: persons with psychosocial disability, person with autism, person with
      down syndrome, person who are blind, person with low vision, person with hard
      of hearing, deaf, person with rare diseases, and all other person who will be
      certified under the disability assessment and determination system or as may be
      defined or classified by the Department of Health;
   e) Reasonable accommodation: means necessary and appropriate modification and
      adjustments not imposing a disproportionate or undue burden, where needed in a
      particular case, to ensure to persons with disabilities the enjoyment or exercise
      on an equal basis with others of all human rights and fundamental freedoms;

Section 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all civilian officials and employees of the
National Government, including government-owned or controlled corporations with original
charters, state universities and colleges, constitutional commissions, the Judiciary, and local
government units, who are covered by the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS),
regardless of whether their appointment is permanent, temporary, casual, coterminous,
substitute, as may be recognized under existing civil service and GSIS laws, rules, and
regulations. Uniformed personnel of the Armed Forces of the Philippines, the Philippine
National Police, the Bureau of Fire Protection, the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology,
and other government personnel covered by separate retirement laws shall not be covered by
this Act.

Section 5. Optional Early Retirement. – Qualified government employees with disabilities
who are members of the Government Service Insurance System may, at their option, retire and
receive the retirement benefits provided under Section 13 of Republic Act No. 8291, subject to
the following qualifications:

   a) Has rendered at least ten (10) years of government service;
   b) Is certified as a person with disability by the appropriate government authority; and
   c) Has reached at least fifty-five (55) years of age.

Retirement under this Act shall be without prejudice to other benefits granted under existing
laws.
Section 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Government Service Insurance System, in coordination with the
Civil Service Commission, the National Council on Disability Affairs, the Department of
Health, the Department of Budget and Management, and other relevant government agencies
and organizations of persons with disabilities, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

Section 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
