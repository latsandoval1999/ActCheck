                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution firmly declares that public office is a public trust. Article
XI, Section 1 provides that public officers and employees must, at all times, be
accountable to the people, serve them with utmost responsibility, integrity, loyalty, and
efficiency, act with patriotism and justice, and lead modest lives. Complementing this
mandate, Article II, Section 27 directs the State to maintain honesty and integrity in the
public service and to take positive and effective measures against graft and corruption.
These constitutional commands are clear: government authority must always be
exercised in the open, and those entrusted with public power must remain answerable
to the Filipino people.

       Yet despite these principles, corruption continues to erode public institutions,
weaken democratic trust, and divert resources meant for social services, infrastructure,
healthcare, education, and decent livelihoods. Public funds that should uplift workers,
farmers, students, and marginalized communities are too often lost through illicit
enrichment, hidden assets, and abuse of office. When corruption is allowed to persist, it
is ordinary Filipinos who bear the heaviest burden through poor services, lost
opportunities, and deepening inequality.

       One enduring obstacle to meaningful accountability is the misuse of financial
secrecy laws as a shield against legitimate scrutiny. Republic Act No. 1405, otherwise
known as the Secrecy of Bank Deposits Law, was enacted to encourage confidence in
the banking system and protect lawful privacy. However, a law designed for financial

stability must never be distorted into a refuge for dishonesty in public office.
Confidentiality in private banking transactions serves a valid purpose, but secrecy
cannot be absolute where public accountability is at stake. Those who exercise the
powers of the State must be held to a higher standard than private citizens because they
manage authority and resources that belong to the people.

       This measure, therefore, seeks to harmonize the legitimate interest in financial
privacy with the superior constitutional demand for transparency and integrity in
government. It provides carefully defined exceptions to bank confidentiality rules for
elected and appointed public officials, allowing lawful examination of deposits,
accounts, investments, and related financial records by competent authorities acting
within their constitutional or statutory powers. It further strengthens existing
accountability mechanisms by requiring the truthful disclosure of relevant financial
interests and by authorizing verification consistent with law. In doing so, the bill
supports lifestyle checks, anti-corruption investigations, asset recovery efforts, and the
prosecution of offenses involving public funds and public office. These safeguards
recognize a simple principle: those who honestly serve the public have nothing to fear
from lawful accountability, while those who exploit public office should not be allowed
to hide behind technical barriers and outdated protections.

       A government that asks sacrifice from workers and taxpayers must itself be
transparent. A nation that seeks inclusive development must ensure that public
resources reach the people for whom they are intended. By closing avenues for
concealment and strengthening mechanisms of accountability, this measure advances
clean governance, protects the public treasury, and affirms that no office is above
scrutiny. More importantly, it gives life to the constitutional promise that power is held
in trust for the people and never for private gain.

      In solidarity with the working class, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
  PROVIDING EXCEPTIONS TO THE CONFIDENTIALITY OF BANK DEPOSITS
 FOR PUBLIC OFFICIALS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO.
   1405, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “SECRECY OF BANK DEPOSITS LAW,”
    REQUIRING THE DISCLOSURE AND VERIFICATION OF CERTAIN BANK
   DEPOSITS AND INVESTMENTS OF SUCH OFFICIALS, AND PRESCRIBING
                 PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Amendment to the Bank Secrecy Law. – Section 2 of Republic Act No. 1405,
otherwise known as the “Secrecy of Bank Deposits Law,” is hereby amended to read as
follows:

“SEC. 2. All deposits of whatever nature with banks or banking institutions in the
Philippines including investments in bonds issued by the Government of the
Philippines, its political subdivisions and its instrumentalities, are hereby considered as
of an absolutely confidential nature and may not be examined, inquired or looked into
by any person, government official, bureau or office, except[ upon written permission of
the depositor, or in cases of impeachment, or upon order of a competent court in cases
of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials, or in cases where the money
deposited or invested is the subject matter of the litigation.]:

        (A) UPON WRITTEN PERMISSION OF THE DEPOSITOR; OR

    (B) IN CASES OF IMPEACHMENT; OR

    (C) UPON ORDER OF A COMPETENT COURT IN CASES OF BRIBERY OR

     (D) IN CASES OF PLUNDER, GRAFT AND CORRUPTION, UNEXPLAINED

PUBLIC FUNDS OR PUBLIC OFFICE; OR

     (E) IN CASES WHERE THE MONEY DEPOSITED OR INVESTED IS THE

OF THE GOVERNMENT, WHETHER PERMANENT, TEMPORARY, CAREER,
NON-CAREER, COTERMINOUS, CONTRACTUAL, CASUAL, ELECTIVE,
APPOINTIVE, CIVILIAN, OR UNIFORMED, INCLUDING MEMBERS OF THE
ARMED FORCES OF THE PHILIPPINES, THE PHILIPPINE NATIONAL POLICE,
THE BUREAU OF FIRE PROTECTION, THE BUREAU OF JAIL MANAGEMENT
AND PENOLOGY, AND OTHER UNIFORMED SERVICES, WHETHER OR NOT
RECEIVING COMPENSATION, REGARDLESS OF AMOUNT: PROVIDED, THAT
THE DEPOSITS, INVESTMENTS, ACCOUNTS, RECORDS, AND RELATED
BANKING INFORMATION OF SUCH PUBLIC OFFICIAL MAY BE LOOKED

THAT THIS EXCEPTION SHALL INCLUDE FOREIGN CURRENCY DEPOSITS,
TRUST ACCOUNTS, DEPOSIT SUBSTITUTES, AND OTHER ANALOGOUS
FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS MAINTAINED WITH PHILIPPINE BANKS,

SECTION 2. Disclosure of Financial Interests of Public Officials. – All public officials
covered by this Act shall disclose in their Statements of Assets, Liabilities and Net
Worth, or in such other reportorial instruments as may be required by law, the existence
of their bank deposits, investments, joint accounts, foreign currency deposits, trust
accounts, and deposit substitutes, in the manner prescribed by applicable law and
regulations. The Office of the Ombudsman and the Anti-Money Laundering Council
shall have authority to verify such disclosures in accordance with law.

SECTION 3. Penalties. – Any public official who willfully conceals, misdeclares, omits,
or fails to disclose any bank deposit, investment, account, or other financial interest
required to be reported under this Act, or who knowingly causes the same to be held,
transferred, or recorded in the name of another person or entity for the purpose of
evading disclosure, shall be subject to dismissal or removal from service, perpetual
disqualification from holding public office, and criminal, civil, and administrative
liability under existing anti-graft, forfeiture, anti-money laundering, tax, and other
applicable laws.

SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), the Office of the
Ombudsman, and the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC), in consultation with
the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the Department of Justice (DOJ), and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders, implementing
rules and regulations, issuances, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication,
in English and Filipino, in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.
