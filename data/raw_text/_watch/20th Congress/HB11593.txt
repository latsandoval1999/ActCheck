EXPLANATORY NOTE

Technical education and skills development are essential to building a productive,
adaptable, and competitive workforce. Through relevant and accessible technical-vogational
education and training, Filipino workers and jobseekers can acquire competencies that improve
their employability, productivity, livelihood opportunities, and capacity for entrepreneurship.
Republic Act No. 7796, otherwise known as the “Technical Education and Skills Development
Act of 1994,” established the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
and mandated it to develop and implement technical education and skills development programs
responsive to the requirements of the Philippine economy and the labor market.

The Zamboanga City Special Economic Zone and Freeport, or ZAMBOECOZONE, was
established pursuant to Republic Act No. 7903, otherwise known as the “Zamboanga City Special
Economic Zone Act of 1995.” The law established the Zamboanga City Special Economic Zone
Authority to manage and operate the economic zone and envisioned the ZAMBOECOZONE as
an agro-industrial, commercial, financial, investment, and tourist center and free port capable of
attracting investments, generating employment, and promoting the economic and social
development of Zamboanga City and surrounding areas.

As the ZAMBOECOZONE continues to grow, demand for a workforce equipped with the
right skills to support its thriving enterprises also grows. To meet the needs of industries, investors,
and employers within the Freeport, technical-vocational education and skills training must align
closely with real-world manpower requirements. Establishing a TESDA training facility within
the heart of the ZAMBOECOZONE brings skills development directly to the doorsteps of workers,
jobseekers, businesses, and local communities.

This proposed measure paves the way for the Zamboanga City TESDA Training Center to
take root within the ZAMBOECOZONE, forging a strong and lasting connection Between
TESDA’s mission and the Freeport’s economic ambitions. The Center will become a hub for
technical-vocational education, offering training, competency assessment, reskilling, upskilling,
retraining, entrepreneurship support, and services designed to empower the local workforée.

Placing the Center within the ZAMBOECOZONE is a strategic move that allows TESDA
to collaborate closely with enterprises and industry leaders to pinpoint the exact skills and
qualifications employers seek. The Center can open doors to apprenticeships, enterprise-based

and other partnerships that help bridge the gap between training and real employment.

The proposed legislation also considers the existing cooperation between TESDA and the

establishing a TESDA Provincial Training Center within its First Industrial Park in Sitio San

The existing cooperation provides a practical foundation. This ongoing partnership lays a
solid groundwork for the proposed legislation. Instead of creating a separate facility, the measure
aims to institutionalize, expand, and sustain the TESDA training center within the
ZAMBOECOZONE. By integrating existing agreements, facilities, personnel, programs, and
resources, the Center can maximize the impact and efficiency of government efforts. Seetors of
manufacturing, industrial operations, construction, welding and metal fabrication, electri¢al and
electronics technology, automotive servicing, logistics, information and communications
technology, food processing, agriculture and agribusiness, fisheries-related activities, tourism and
hospitality, culinary services, renewable energy, machine operation and maintenance, and other
occupations identified by TESDA and industry stakeholders as in demand.

The Center will open doors for workers and jobseekers eager to learn new skills or enhance
their current abilities. Beneficiaries may include the unemployed, underemployed, out-o “school
youth, persons with disabilities, returning overseas Filipino workers, displaced workers, those
employed within the ZAMBOECOZONE, and other eligible individuals. With TESDA’s
scholarship and financial assistance programs, qualified trainees can access the support they need,
depending on program guidelines and available funding.

The measure preserves the respective mandates of TESDA and the ZAMBOECOZONE
Authority. TESDA shall retain administrative and technical supervision over the Center, ineluding
its training programs, competency standards, personnel, assessment and certification eons
and operational policies. Consistent with Republic Act No. 7903, the ZAMBOECOZONE
Authority shall coordinate appropriately and may make available land, facilities, utilities,
infrastructure support, security, and other assistance necessary for the Center's operation. The
arrangement is intended to create institutional cooperation without transferring TESDA's statutory
functions to the ZAMBOECOZONE Authority.

The legislation also calls on TESDA and the ZAMBOECOZONE Authority to establish
systems to monitor and evaluate the Center’s progress. These efforts will help guarantee that public
resources are invested in training programs that truly build relevant skills and open doors to
meaningful jobs, livelihoods, and business opportunities.

By establishing the Zamboanga City TESDA Training Center witliin the
ZAMBOECOZONE, this measure aims to strengthen the link between skills development and
economic growth. The Center will serve as a lasting foundation for equipping workers to seize
both current and future job opportunities within the Freeport, Zamboanga City, and the broader
Zamboanga Peninsula.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

KAJRINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Zamboanga City TESDA

Training Center Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and strengthen technical education and skills development as an essential
component of national development, employment generation, poverty reduction,

productivity enhancement, and inclusive economic growth.

Towards this end, the State shall ensure accessible, relevant, quality-aSsured,
industry-responsive, and employment-oriented technical-vocational education and
training, particularly in areas where economic and industrial activities generate existing

and emerging demand for skilled workers.

SEC. 3. Establishment and Institutionalization of the Zamboanga City TESDA
Training Center. — There is hereby established and institutionalized, under the
supervision and administration of the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), a technical-vocational education and training center within the
Zamboanga City Special Economic Zone and Freeport (ZAMBOECOZONEB), to be Known

as the Zamboanga City TESDA Training Center, hereinafter referred to as the “Center.”

The Center shall form part of the TESDA training delivery system and shall serve
as a dedicated technical-vocational education, training, competency assessment,
certification, and skills development facility for qualified beneficiaries from Zamboanga
City, the ZAMBOECOZONE, the Zamboanga Peninsula, and such other areas as may be

determined by TESDA.

The Center shall complement, and shall not unnecessarily duplicate, existing
TESDA Regional Training Centers, Provincial Training Centers, district offices, accredited

technical-vocational institutions, and other TESDA-supported training facilities.

SEC. 13. Coordination with the ZAMBOECOZONE Authority. — TESDA and
the ZAMBOECOZONE Authority shall enter into such agreements as may be necessary
to ensure the efficient establishment, operation, maintenance, and development of the

Center.

The agreements may cover the use of land and facilities, utilities, security, access,
site development, maintenance, infrastructure support, industry coordination, emergency
response, environmental management, and other matters necessary for the operation of the

Center.

The ZAMBOECOZONE Authority may designate an appropriate office or focal
person to coordinate with TESDA concerning the operations and requirements of the

Center.

SEC. 14. Scholarships and Financial Assistance. — Qualified beneficiaries of the
Center may avail themselves of TESDA scholarships, training allowances, assessment
assistance, toolkits, and other forms of financial or material assistance under existing
TESDA programs and other government-funded programs, subject to applicable eligibility

requirements and availability of funds.

Priority may be given, subject to existing program rules, to qualified residents of
Zamboanga City, workers and dependents of workers employed within the
ZAMBOECOZONE, unemployed and underemployed persons, out-of-school youth,
displaced workers, persons with disabilities, returning overseas Filipino workers, and other

disadvantaged sectors.

SEC. 15. Monitoring and Evaluation. — TESDA, in coordination with the
ZAMBOECOZONE Authority, shall establish a system for monitoring and evaluating the

performance and effectiveness of the Center.
