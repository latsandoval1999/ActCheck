                                              AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING SUPPORT MECHANISMS FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS

MECHANISMS FOR CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT AND PROVIDING FOR THEIR

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


         Article XIV, Section 2(1) of the 1987 Philippine Constitution affirms the State’s duty to
“establish, maintain, and support a complete, adequate, and integrated system of education relevant
to the needs of the people and society.” This constitutional mandate recognizes education not merely
as the transmission of knowledge, but as a fundamental institution in the development of responsible
and productive citizens.

        For a substantial part of their formative years, children are immersed in the school
environment under the care, supervision, and guidance of teachers and other school personnel. The
experiences they gain, the lessons they learn, and the values they develop during their time in school
significantly contribute to shaping their future.

        Schools consequently serve as an extension of the home, providing an environment where
students are guided toward becoming responsible, capable, and socially conscious members of the
community. Teachers and other school personnel who interact directly with students assume
responsibilities that go beyond academic instruction. In many respects, they serve in the capacity of
second parents by helping students develop not only the knowledge and skills necessary for their
future endeavors, but also the discipline, values, and character needed to become responsible
Filipino citizens.

        Such responsibility necessarily includes the proper management of student behavior. As
partners of parents and guardians in raising children, school personnel cannot be expected to remain
passive when students engage in misconduct or violate established school rules. Teachers, in
particular, have the dual responsibility of educating students and preparing them to become
respectful, responsible, and law-abiding members of society. This includes the authority, subject to
applicable laws and regulations, to impose appropriate disciplinary measures when students violate
school policies or exhibit improper conduct.

        Disciplinary interventions are intended not merely as punishment, but as opportunities for
students to understand the consequences of their actions, correct inappropriate behavior, and develop
respect for others. This is particularly important in instances involving disrespect toward teachers,
school personnel, visitors, or fellow students. The ability of teachers to effectively manage their
classrooms and address misconduct is therefore an essential component of maintaining a safe,
orderly, and productive learning environment.

        The legal foundation for this responsibility may be found in the doctrine of in loco parentis.
Article 218 of the Family Code recognizes the special parental authority and responsibility of
schools, administrators, teachers, and other institutions engaged in child care over minors while they
are under their supervision, instruction, or custody. Similarly, Article 349 of the Civil Code
recognizes teachers and professors among those who exercise substitute parental authority. These
provisions demonstrate that the law recognizes the legitimate role of educators in supervising
students and promoting proper conduct within the school environment and during school-sanctioned
activities.

        The law likewise imposes responsibilities upon school personnel. Article 2180 of the Civil
Code provides for liability in certain circumstances for damages caused by pupils, students, or
apprentices while under the custody of teachers or educational institutions. In light of these
responsibilities, teachers and school personnel must also be afforded reasonable protection when
they exercise their lawful authority and perform their duties in good faith. They should not be placed
in a situation where fear of unfounded complaints or punitive consequences prevents them from
addressing student misconduct appropriately.

        The rapid growth of technology and social media has further complicated this concern.
Allegations involving teachers and school personnel can now be disseminated to a wide audience
almost instantaneously, often before the facts have been properly established. False, malicious, or
misleading accusations can subject educators to public humiliation, online harassment, and
reputational damage. Even when an allegation is subsequently proven unfounded, the damage to an
educator’s professional standing and personal reputation may already be difficult to reverse.

       These circumstances highlight the need for safeguards that protect both the rights of students
and the legitimate authority of educators. Teachers should be able to perform their responsibilities
as educators and persons exercising special parental authority without being unduly exposed to
malicious attacks simply for carrying out their lawful duties. At the same time, mechanisms must
remain in place to ensure that legitimate complaints involving abuse, misconduct, or violations of
students’ rights are properly investigated and resolved.

        This measure therefore seeks to provide appropriate protection to teachers and other school
personnel against demonstrably false and malicious accusations that may undermine the teaching
process, disrupt the learning environment, and cause lasting harm to their professional reputation.
An allegation, even when eventually disproven, may continue to circulate online and remain
accessible long after the person concerned has been cleared. Such circumstances can have
consequences that extend beyond the resolution of the complaint itself, including damage to one’s
career, credibility, and standing in the community.

        The protection of children and the preservation of their rights must remain paramount.
However, such protection should be balanced with the equally important right of teachers and school
personnel to due process and protection from malicious or demonstrably false imputations. The
existence of legitimate accountability mechanisms should not result in educators being subjected to
public condemnation before the facts have been established.

       Accordingly, teachers and school personnel who act within the bounds of law and in the
legitimate performance of their duties should be afforded sufficient authority and protection to
maintain discipline and safeguard the learning environment. This measure seeks to strike a proper
balance between protecting students from abuse and ensuring that educators can exercise their lawful
responsibilities without being subjected to intimidation, harassment, or trial by publicity based on
unfounded accusations.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

 1         Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Classroom Management Support
 2   and Protection for Teachers Act."

 3            Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - In all actions relating to children, the State shall accord
 4   special protection to children from all forms of abuse, exploitation, cruelty, and other conditions
 5   prejudicial to their development. Furthermore, the State has the duty to establish and maintain a
 6   complete and adequate system of public basic education geared towards the holistic development of
 7   a child.

 8           These policies are understood to encompass the State's responsibility to provide appropriate
 9   instruction and guidance to students about school discipline, their duties and responsibilities with
10   respect to their peers, to their teachers and other persons of authority, to members of their
11   community, and to society.

12           To this end, the State shall institutionalize measures to implement in all public schools
13   clearly defined regulations on appropriate student behavior towards other students, teachers, and
14   school staff and personnel, during classes, and within and outside school premises.

15           The State is also hereby bound to establish and maintain support systems, including training
16   on classroom management and child discipline, as well as legal assistance, for teachers and school
17   staff and personnel, who come in daily direct contact with students, and thus find themselves in
18   situations that could expose them to false accusations of child abuse in the course of the discharge
19   of their duties.

20           It is hereby also declared that the State shall promote and improve the working conditions of
21   public school teachers and other school personnel, which are understood to determine whether or
22   not the physical and emotional environments of children and teachers are conducive to learning and
23   teaching.

24            Sec. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall be applicable to all teaching personnel of all public school
25   institutions, including non-teaching personnel who stand in direct contact with students in the course
26   of the discharge of their duties.

27          Sec. 4. Classroom Management Guidelines. - The Department of Education

28   (DepEd) shall formulate guidelines for classroom management for teachers and school personnel.
29   Said guidelines shall include the following:
30      (1) The rules and regulations to be observed by teaching and non-teaching personnel who
31          interact directly with students in the course of the discharge of their duties, with respect to
32          students, whenever inside the premises of public schools, or even outside in case of school-
33          sanctioned activities;

34      (2) The rights and responsibilities of teaching and non-teaching personnel who interact directly
35          with students, with respect to student discipline and classroom management, to the end of
36          fostering a positive school climate and safe environment conducive to instruction and
37          learning.

38           School administrators and teachers exercise special parental authority over their students
39   while under their supervision, instruction and custody. As such, the Guidelines to be produced by
40   the DepEd shall recognize their authority in the schools and in school-sanctioned events, and the
41   need to conduct their classes in an organized manner that is conducive to learning. It is likewise
42   recognized that the teachers have the authority to ensure that their students behave, respond and treat
43   their teachers and classmates in a respectful manner. They have the right to call out disorderly or
44   disrespectful behavior, and deal with the same consistent with the law and the Guidelines to be
45   implemented;

46      (3) Permissible, appropriate, and effective responses and interventions that will address
47          violations of school rules and regulations to be administered by teachers, the school head,
48          and other school staff and personnel; and

49      (4) A clear description of the disciplinary procedure and clear designation of the authorities
50          competent to investigate, propose, and apply the responses and interventions.

51           Sec. 5. Acts Not Considered as Child Abuse. - Any act committed by teaching and non-
52   teaching personnel who stand in direct contact with students in the course of the discharge of their
53   duties pursuant to the Guidelines, provided they are within the bounds of law, good morals, good
54   customs, public order and public policy, shall not be deemed as child abuse, cruelty, and exploitation
55   as prohibited by Republic Act No. 7610, otherwise known as the "Special Protection of Children
56   Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act."

57           Sec. 6. Consultation with Stakeholders. - Students, parents, teachers, other school
58   personnel, and the community, represented by their respective organizations, as well as professionals
59   in child behavior and social work, shall each have meaningful involvement in the formulation of the
60   Guidelines.

61          Sec. 7. Information Dissemination of the Guidelines. - The DepEd shall provide
62   explanations, training, and workshops in order to familiarize teachers, students and families with the
63   Guidelines, explain the expectations of various sectors involved, and describe the various preventive
64   and positive responses and interventions that may be used. At the beginning of each school year,
65   copies of the Guidelines shall be provided to each student as well as their parents or legal guardians.

66           Sec. 8. Training of Teachers and School Personnel on the Guidelines. - All public school
67   teachers and school personnel shall receive trainings and instructions related to the specific contents
68   of the Guidelines.

69           Sec. 9. Professional Development Relating to Student Discipline and Classroom
70   Management. - The DepEd shall provide teachers the appropriate pre-service and in-service training
71   on child behavior and psychology, classroom management techniques, positive discipline, and other
72   related fields, as well as the Guidelines under Section 4 and Section 5 of this Act and the scope of
73   their duties and responsibilities with respect to student discipline and classroom management.

74           Sec. 10. Support Personnel. - The DepEd, with the Department of Budget and Management
75   (DBM), shall also ensure that there is a sufficient number of items for guidance counselors, who
76   will serve as support personnel to teachers and school administrators in matters of student discipline.
 77   For purposes of this Act, the DepEd shall endeavor to meet the recommended ratios of guidance
 78   counselors to students in public elementary and high schools.

 79           Sec. 11. Other Support Mechanisms for Student Discipline and Classroom Management.
 80   - The DepEd shall provide such other mechanisms necessary to capacitate its teachers and other
 81   personnel on matters regarding child behavior and psychology, classroom management techniques,
 82   positive discipline, and other related fields. These measures shall include but not be limited to the
 83   conduct of periodic seminars and formulation of primers and other explanatory materials.

 84           Sec. 12. Protection to Teachers and School Personnel in Charges Related to Student
 85   Discipline and Classroom Management. - In all cases related to student discipline and classroom
 86   management, including criminal and administrative charges of child abuse, the DepEd shall extend
 87   to its teachers and other personnel the necessary protection, including but not limited to legal
 88   assistance and representation.

 89          The DepEd shall ensure that respondents, in all stages of the disciplinary proceedings, are
 90   amply represented by the accredited union, or registered union, where there is no existing accredited
 91   union, of teachers or non-teaching employees, as the case may be.

 92           Violation of this Section is a violation of due process and shall render the proceedings void,
 93   and all responsible persons administratively, civilly, and criminally liable.

 94           Sec. 13. Confidentiality of Identity of Parties and Proceedings under this Act. - No person,
 95   whether public or private and whether a party to the case or otherwise, shall publicize a case during
 96   its pendency. In no case shall the identity of complainants and respondents, or any information that
 97   may reasonably identify them, be disclosed to the public during the pendency of the proceedings.

 98          Violation of this Section shall subject the person to the appropriate administrative, civil,
 99   criminal, and other liabilities.

100   The DepEd shall issue guidelines for the media with regard to the handling of cases within the
101   contemplation of this Act.

102           Sec. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effective
103   date of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with other implementing agencies, shall promulgate the
104   rules and regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act. The implementing rules and
105   regulations (IRR) provided for under this Act shall be reviewed periodically, and revised, if
106   necessary.

107           Sec. 15. Separability Clause. - Should any provision of this Act be found unconstitutional
108   by a court of law, such provision shall be severed from the remainder of this Act, and such action
109   shall not affect the enforceability of the remaining provisions of this Act.

110           Sec. 16. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, letters of instruction, resolutions, orders or
111   parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified or
112   amended accordingly.

113           Sec. 17. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
114   in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

115          Approved,
