14                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE
15
16   Republic Act No. 11697, otherwise known as the “Electric Vehicle Industry
17   Development Act” (EVIDA), established the national policy for the development and
18   adoption of electric vehicles in the Philippines.
19
20   As electric mobility expands, the emergency-response capability of government must
21   develop alongside it.
22
23   Electric vehicles equipped with rechargeable traction batteries present emergency-response
24   considerations different from those encountered in conventional vehicle fires. Battery
25   thermal runaway may involve extreme heat, hazardous gases, prolonged incident
26   management, and the possibility of reignition even after visible flames have been controlled.
27
28   Republic Act No. 9514, otherwise known as the “Revised Fire Code of the Philippines of
29   2008”, mandates the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) to prevent and suppress destructive
30   fires and enforce appropriate fire-safety standards.
31
32   Republic Act No. 11589, otherwise known as the “Bureau of Fire Protection
33   Modernization Act”, further strengthens the capabilities of the BFP through the acquisition
34   of modern fire suppression, rescue, hazardous-materials, specialized equipment, training,
35   research and other resources necessary for effective emergency response.
36
37   There is therefore a need to ensure that the continuing growth of the electric vehicle
38   industry is accompanied by a corresponding strengthening of the capability of the BFP to
39   respond to electric vehicle fires and battery-related emergencies.
40
41   This measure establishes a statutory public-safety responsibility requiring manufacturers,
42   assemblers, importers and, where applicable, distributors that first introduce covered
43   electric vehicles into the Philippine market to provide the BFP with one (1) approved
44   Electric Vehicle Fire Response Safety Set (EVFRSS) for every ten (10) covered electric
45   vehicles introduced into the Philippine market.
46
47   Each EVFRSS shall include an appropriate BFP-approved Electric Vehicle Fire
48   Containment Device (EVFCD) and such complementary equipment as may be required by
49   the BFP.
50
51   The measure adopts technology-neutral terminology and does not mandate any particular
52   brand, design or proprietary product. Technical requirements shall be determined and
53   periodically reviewed by the BFP, in coordination with concerned government agencies,
54   based on prevailing fire science, applicable safety standards and technological
55   developments.
56
 57   The measure likewise provides safeguards against double counting, establishes reporting
 58   and accountability mechanisms, requires proper government inventory and accounting of
 59   equipment received, mandates technical cooperation and firefighter training, and provides
 60   administrative due process.
 61
 62   As the number of electric vehicles on Philippine roads increases, the country’s capacity to
 63   safely respond to electric vehicle emergencies must increase with it.
 64
 65   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

126      AN ACT ESTABLISHING AN ELECTRIC VEHICLE FIRE SAFETY AND
127          EMERGENCY RESPONSE SUPPORT PROGRAM, REQUIRING
128   MANUFACTURERS, ASSEMBLERS, IMPORTERS AND, WHERE APPLICABLE,
129    DISTRIBUTORS OF ELECTRIC VEHICLES TO PROVIDE THE BUREAU OF
130    FIRE PROTECTION WITH ELECTRIC VEHICLE FIRE RESPONSE SAFETY
131      EQUIPMENT FOR EVERY TEN (10) COVERED ELECTRIC VEHICLES
132       INTRODUCED INTO THE PHILIPPINE MARKET, STRENGTHENING
133      FIREFIGHTER TRAINING AND INDUSTRY COOPERATION, AND FOR
134                          OTHER PURPOSES

135         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
136   Congress assembled:

137   SECTION 1. Short Title.
138         This Act shall be known as the “Electric Vehicle Fire Safety and Emergency
139   Response Support Act.”
140
141   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.
142          It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote environmentally sustainable
143   transportation while protecting life, property, communities and emergency responders from
144   emerging fire and safety risks associated with electric vehicle technologies.
145
146         The State recognizes that the development and adoption of electric vehicles must be
147   accompanied by appropriate fire prevention, containment, suppression, rescue, recovery and
148   emergency-response capabilities.
149
150          Toward this end, entities introducing covered electric vehicles into the Philippine
151   market shall share a reasonable and proportionate responsibility for strengthening the
152   capability of government to respond to electric vehicle and traction-battery emergencies.
153
154          Implementation of this Act shall be technology-neutral, transparent, competitive,
155   equitable and non-discriminatory and shall not favor any particular manufacturer, supplier,
156   brand, technology or country of origin.
157
158   SEC. 3. Objectives.
159         This Act shall:
160
161         (a) Strengthen the capability of the BFP to respond to electric vehicle fires and
162         battery-related emergencies;
163
164         (b) Establish a predictable and proportionate industry contribution to EV fire-
165         response capability;
166
167         (c) Equip BFP personnel with appropriate fire containment, suppression, monitoring
168         and responder-safety equipment;
169
170         (d) Provide firefighters with appropriate technical information and specialized
171         training;
172
173         (e) Promote evidence-based and technology-neutral standards for EV fire response;
174
175         (f) Protect firefighters and the public from thermal runaway, reignition, hazardous
176         gases, electrical hazards and related risks; and
177
178         (g) Integrate EV emergency preparedness into the country’s transition toward
179         electric mobility.
180
181   SEC. 4. Definition of Terms.
182         For purposes of this Act:
183
184         (a) BFP refers to the Bureau of Fire Protection;
185
186         (b) Covered Electric Vehicle or Covered EV refers to a four-wheeled or larger
187         electric vehicle equipped with a rechargeable traction battery, including battery
188         electric vehicles, hybrid-electric vehicles and plug-in hybrid-electric vehicles as
189         defined under Republic Act No. 11697, and such other classes of electric vehicles as
190         may subsequently be included pursuant to this Act;
191
192         (c) Electric Vehicle Fire Containment Device or EVFCD refers to a purpose-
193         designed, high-temperature-resistant device, system or equipment intended to
194         contain, isolate, control or limit the spread of heat, flame, smoke and other hazards
195         arising from an electric vehicle or traction-battery fire, including devices designed
196         for deployment over, under or around a vehicle, subject to technical standards and
197         operational protocols prescribed or recognized by the BFP;
198
199         (d) Electric Vehicle Fire Response Safety Set or EVFRSS refers to a standardized
200         package of BFP-approved equipment intended for the containment, control,
201         suppression, isolation, monitoring or management of electric vehicle fires and
202         battery-related emergencies;
203
204         (e) Importer and Manufacturer or Assembler shall have the meanings assigned to
205         them under Republic Act No. 11697;
206
207         (f) Distributor refers to a natural or juridical person engaged in the commercial
208         distribution of Covered EVs in the Philippines who directly imports or otherwise
209         first places such vehicles in the Philippine market when no manufacturer, assembler
210         or importer has previously assumed the obligation imposed under this Act;
211
212         (g) Covered Entity refers to the manufacturer, assembler, importer or, where
213         applicable, distributor responsible for first introducing a Covered EV into the
214         Philippine market; and
215
216         (h) Introduced into the Philippine Market refers to the importation for commercial
217         distribution, first sale or commercial disposition of a Covered EV in the Philippines,
218         subject to the anti-double-counting provisions of this Act.
219
220   SEC. 5. Coverage.
221          This Act shall apply to Covered Entities that manufacture, assemble, import or
222   directly introduce Covered EVs for sale, distribution, lease, fleet operation or other
223   commercial use in the Philippines.
224
225          Light electric vehicles weighing less than fifty kilograms (50 kg), including electric
226   bicycles, electric scooters and similar micromobility devices, shall initially be excluded
227   from the mandatory contribution under this Act.
228
229          The BFP, DOE, DTI and DOTr may recommend to Congress a separate
230   proportionate fire-safety framework for light electric vehicles based on battery capacity, fire
231   risk, market volume and available scientific evidence.
232
233         Covered EVs imported exclusively for research, testing, exhibition, diplomatic use,
234   temporary admission or subsequent re-exportation, and which are not placed in ordinary
235   commercial use in the Philippines, shall be exempt, subject to verification under the
236   Implementing Rules and Regulations.
237
238   SEC. 6. Mandatory Electric Vehicle Fire Safety Contribution.
239          For every ten (10) Covered EVs introduced into the Philippine market by a Covered
240   Entity, such entity shall provide to the BFP, without cost to the National Government, at
241   least one (1) BFP-approved Electric Vehicle Fire Response Safety Set (EVFRSS).
242
243          Each EVFRSS shall include at least one (1) appropriate Electric Vehicle Fire
244   Containment Device (EVFCD) and such complementary equipment as may be prescribed
245   by the BFP.
246
247          The obligation imposed under this section shall constitute a statutory in-kind public-
248   safety contribution and shall not require payment or reimbursement by the BFP or any other
249   government agency.
250
251          Only completed groups of ten (10) Covered EVs shall give rise to an immediate
252   contribution obligation.
253
254          Any remainder of fewer than ten (10) vehicles at the end of a reporting period shall
255   be carried forward to succeeding reporting periods until the cumulative total reaches ten (10)
256   vehicles.
257
258   SEC. 7. No Double Counting.
259         A Covered EV shall be counted only once for purposes of determining the obligation
260   under Section 6 of this Act.
261
262   An imported EV subsequently sold or distributed by a dealer or distributor shall not create a
263   second contribution obligation when the importer has already accounted for the vehicle.
264
265   For locally manufactured or assembled EVs, responsibility shall attach to the manufacturer
266   or assembler upon first commercial disposition.
267
268           A distributor shall become responsible only when it directly imports or otherwise
269   first introduces the Covered EV into the Philippine market and no upstream Covered Entity
270   has accounted for the vehicle.
271
272          The BFP, DOE, DTI, Bureau of Customs and Land Transportation Office shall
273   establish an appropriate verification mechanism to prevent duplication of obligations.
274
275   SEC. 8. Composition of the Electric Vehicle Fire Response Safety Set.
276         Each EVFRSS shall contain equipment determined by the BFP to be reasonably
277   necessary for safe and effective response to EV and traction-battery emergencies.
278
279          The set shall include an appropriate EVFCD and may include:
280
281          (a) Thermal-imaging or temperature-monitoring equipment;
282
283          (b) Equipment for vehicle isolation and exposure protection;
284
285          (c) Specialized water-delivery equipment, nozzles or adapters;
286
287          (d) Battery-fire incident warning, marking or isolation devices;
288
289          (e) Appropriate electrical-safety equipment;
290
291          (f) Appropriate responder personal protective equipment;
292
293          (g) Post-fire monitoring equipment; and
294
295          (h) Such other equipment determined by the BFP to be necessary for containment,
296          suppression, monitoring, recovery or responder protection.
297
298          All specifications shall be performance-based, technology-neutral and non-brand-
299   specific.
300
301         Nothing in this Act shall authorize the government to require compliance through the
302   purchase or provision of a particular commercial brand or proprietary product.
303
304   SEC. 9. Standards and Operational Use of Electric Vehicle Fire Containment Devices.
305         The BFP shall prescribe operational protocols governing the deployment and use of
306   EVFCDs.
307
308          Such protocols shall take into consideration battery thermal runaway, accumulation
309   of flammable or hazardous gases, firefighter positioning and safety, ventilation, temperature
310   and thermal monitoring, appropriate extinguishing or cooling agents, exposure protection,
311   device deployment and removal, reignition risk, post-fire monitoring, towing and temporary
312   storage, and other relevant operational considerations.
313
314           Nothing in this Act shall require an incident commander to deploy an EVFCD when,
315   based on conditions at the incident scene, such deployment would increase the danger to
316   firefighters, vehicle occupants, bystanders or property.
317
318         The BFP may recognize or approve safer or more effective successor technologies or
319   equivalent equipment as fire science and EV technology develop.
320
321   SEC. 10. Technical Standards and Certification.
322          Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the BFP, in
323   coordination with the DTI-Bureau of Philippine Standards, DOE, DOTr, DOST, DENR and
324   other appropriate agencies, shall establish minimum technical specifications, testing
325   requirements and certification procedures for EVFRSS and EVFCD equipment.
326
327          Standards shall consider heat and flame resistance; thermal performance; mechanical
328   strength and durability; electrical safety; dimensions appropriate to different vehicle classes;
329   hazards associated with thermal runaway and flammable gases; compatibility with BFP
330   operational procedures; inspection, maintenance, storage and replacement requirements;
331   applicable Philippine National Standards; recognized international standards where
332   appropriate; and independently validated scientific and operational research.
333
334         The standards shall be periodically reviewed to account for advances in EV battery
335   technology and fire science.
336
337   SEC. 11. Acceptance, Ownership, Inventory and Accountability.
338         Upon inspection and formal acceptance by the BFP, equipment provided pursuant to
339   this Act shall become property of the National Government under the custody and
340   accountability of the BFP.
341
342          All equipment shall be inspected, recorded, valued, inventoried and accounted for in
343   accordance with applicable Commission on Audit rules and government accounting and
344   property-management standards.
345
346           The BFP shall maintain an electronic national inventory identifying the quantity,
347   classification, location, condition, service life and operational status of equipment received.
348
349        No government official or employee shall solicit, demand or receive any equipment,
350   money or consideration beyond that authorized by this Act or other applicable laws.
351
352   SEC. 12. Strategic Allocation.
353          The BFP shall determine the allocation and deployment of equipment received under
354   this Act based upon objective fire-risk and operational criteria.
355
356           Priority may be given to areas with high concentrations of registered EVs; highly
357   urbanized and densely populated areas; major highways and expressways; EV charging
358   hubs; parking buildings and large parking facilities; ports, airports and transportation
359   terminals; areas operating substantial electric public transportation fleets; BFP stations
360   strategically positioned for highway or regional emergency response; and other areas
361   identified through BFP risk assessment.
362
363          No Covered Entity may condition its compliance upon the deployment of its
364   contribution to a particular locality, fire station or political jurisdiction.
365
366   SEC. 13. Industry Technical Cooperation.
367          Manufacturers, assemblers and importers shall make available to the BFP
368   appropriate emergency-response information for Covered EV models introduced into the
369   Philippines, including, where applicable:
370
371          (a) Emergency Response Guides;
372
373          (b) Battery location and configuration;
374
375          (c) High-voltage isolation procedures;
376
377          (d) Emergency vehicle shutdown procedures;
378
379          (e) Thermal-runaway considerations;
380
381          (f) Towing and recovery precautions;
382
383          (g) Post-fire storage and reignition precautions; and
384
385          (h) Such other technical information reasonably necessary to protect emergency
386          responders and the public.
387
388          Nothing in this section shall require disclosure of proprietary information or trade
389   secrets beyond information reasonably necessary for life safety and emergency response,
390   subject to applicable law.
391
392   SEC. 14. Training and Capability Development.
393         The BFP shall establish and periodically update a standardized Electric Vehicle Fire
394   and Battery Emergency Response Training Program.
395
396         Training shall include EV identification, high-voltage and electrical hazards, vehicle
397   and battery isolation, battery thermal runaway, containment and suppression strategies,
398   EVFCD deployment, exposure protection, firefighter safety, hazardous gases, post-fire
399   monitoring and reignition, towing and recovery coordination, and battery-related
400   hazardous-materials considerations.
401
402           Covered Entities shall provide reasonable model-specific technical assistance and
403   safety information necessary for BFP training without cost to the government.
404
405   SEC. 15. Reporting and Interagency Verification.
406         Covered Entities shall submit periodic electronic compliance reports containing:
407
408         (a) The number and classification of Covered EVs introduced into the Philippine
409         market;
410
411         (b) The cumulative vehicle count;
412
413         (c) The number of EVFRSS obligations generated;
414
415         (d) The number and description of EVFRSS delivered;
416
417         (e) Applicable certificates of conformity or compliance; and
418
419         (f) Such other information reasonably necessary to verify compliance.
420
421         The BFP, DOE, DTI, DOTr, Bureau of Customs and Land Transportation Office
422   may exchange information necessary to verify compliance.
423
424          Any processing of personal information shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173,
425   otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” including the principles of
426   transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality.
427
428         Only information reasonably necessary to implement this Act shall be collected,
429   processed, retained or exchanged.
430
431   SEC. 16. Compliance Certification and Ease of Doing Business.
432         The BFP shall establish an electronic system for recording compliance.
433
434          Government agencies shall, to the greatest extent practicable, utilize existing
435   databases and interoperable government systems and shall not require Covered Entities to
436   repeatedly submit information already lawfully available to another government agency.
437
438          Implementation shall be consistent with Republic Act No. 11032, otherwise known
439   as the “Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018.”
440
441   SEC. 17. Administrative Due Process.
442         No administrative penalty shall be imposed without written notice and reasonable
443   opportunity to be heard.
444
445         Upon determination of a compliance deficiency, the BFP shall issue a written Notice
446   of Compliance specifying the nature and factual basis of the deficiency, the corrective
447   action required, the period for compliance, and the consequences of continued
448   noncompliance.
449
450           The Covered Entity shall be given thirty (30) days from receipt to comply or submit
451   its explanation.
452
453        For justifiable reasons and where public safety will not be materially prejudiced, the
454   BFP may grant a reasonable extension.
455
456         A final administrative decision shall state its factual and legal basis and may be
457   appealed to the Secretary of the Interior and Local Government, without prejudice to
458   remedies available under law.
459
460   SEC. 18. Administrative Penalties.
461          After notice and opportunity to be heard, a Covered Entity that willfully or
462   repeatedly fails to comply with this Act may be subject to an administrative fine of not less
463   than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
464   (P500,000.00) for each reporting period of noncompliance.
465
466          Payment of the administrative fine shall not extinguish the obligation to provide the
467   required EVFRSS.
468
469          Continued noncompliance after a final administrative order may constitute a separate
470   violation for each succeeding reporting period.
471
472           Where authorized by existing law, the appropriate regulatory agency may, after due
473   process, impose appropriate regulatory sanctions directly related to the activity under its
474   jurisdiction.
475
476          Falsification, fraud, corruption, misrepresentation or other unlawful acts committed
477   in connection with implementation of this Act shall be prosecuted under applicable laws.
478
479   SEC. 19. Corporate Social Responsibility and Sustainability Recognition.
480          Compliance with the statutory contribution established under this Act may be
481   recognized by a Covered Entity as part of its corporate social responsibility, environmental,
482   social and governance, sustainability or public-safety program, subject to applicable
483   accounting, taxation and regulatory rules.
484
485          Such recognition shall not convert the mandatory statutory obligation into a
486   voluntary donation and shall not reduce or substitute for the minimum contribution required
487   under this Act.
488
489   SEC. 20. Research, Testing and Periodic Technical Review.
490           The BFP, in coordination with the DOST, DTI-BPS, DOE, DOTr, academic
491   institutions and qualified technical organizations, shall encourage continuing research,
492   testing and evaluation involving EV fires and battery emergencies.
493
494         At least once every two (2) years, or earlier when significant scientific or
495   technological developments warrant, the concerned agencies shall review:
496
497         (a) EVFCD technical specifications;
498
499          (b) EVFRSS composition;
500
501          (c) Changes in traction-battery chemistry and vehicle design;
502
503          (d) EV fire suppression and containment research;
504
505          (e) BFP operational experience;
506
507          (f) Firefighter injuries and safety incidents;
508
509          (g) Post-fire and reignition incidents; and
510
511          (h) International fire-safety standards and best practices.
512
513          Technical specifications may thereafter be updated through regulations without
514   requiring amendment of this Act, provided that the statutory purpose and contribution ratio
515   established herein are preserved.
516
517   SEC. 21. Harmonization with EVIDA and Existing Regulatory Framework.
518           Implementation of this Act shall be harmonized with the Comprehensive Roadmap
519   for the Electric Vehicle Industry under Republic Act No. 11697.
520
521           The appropriate interagency mechanisms established under EVIDA may be utilized
522   for harmonizing EV policies under this Act, with the BFP participating on matters involving
523   fire safety and emergency response.
524
525         Nothing herein shall diminish or transfer the existing statutory authority of the DOE,
526   DTI, DOTr, DOST, DENR, DILG, BFP, Bureau of Customs, Land Transportation Office,
527   Commission on Audit or other competent government agencies.
528
529   SEC. 22. Equal and Non-Discriminatory Application.
530           The requirements of this Act shall apply equally to Covered EVs meeting the same
531   classification and safety criteria, whether locally manufactured, assembled or imported,
532   subject only to reasonable distinctions based upon vehicle class, battery characteristics, fire
533   risk or other objective safety considerations.
534
535          No implementing rule shall discriminate against a vehicle, manufacturer or supplier
536   solely on the basis of country of origin.
537
538   SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
539          Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG,
540   through the BFP, in coordination with the DOE, DTI, DOTr, DOST, DENR, Bureau of
541   Customs, Land Transportation Office, Commission on Audit and other concerned agencies,
542   shall promulgate the necessary Implementing Rules and Regulations after appropriate
543   stakeholder consultation.
544
545           The IRR shall provide for vehicle-counting and anti-double-counting procedures;
546   reporting periods; EVFRSS and EVFCD technical standards; product testing and
547   certification; inspection and acceptance; property recording and inventory; strategic
548   allocation; training requirements; compliance verification; administrative enforcement and
549   appeals; transitional arrangements; and other matters necessary to implement this Act.
550
551   SEC. 24. Transition Period and Prospective Application.
552          Covered Entities shall be given six (6) months from the effectivity of the IRR to
553   establish the necessary compliance systems.
554
555          The mandatory contribution shall apply prospectively only to Covered EVs
556   introduced into the Philippine market after expiration of the transition period.
557
558          Covered EVs introduced before such date shall not be retroactively counted.
559
560   SEC. 25. Appropriations.
561          The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged
562   against the current appropriations of the BFP and other concerned agencies.
563
564          Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for administration, training, testing,
565   inspection, maintenance, storage and implementation shall be included in the annual
566   General Appropriations Act, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting and auditing laws,
567   rules and regulations.
568
569          Nothing in this section shall authorize government reimbursement for an EVFRSS
570   required as a statutory contribution under Section 6 of this Act.
571
572   SEC. 26. Construction with Existing Laws.
573          This Act shall be construed in harmony with Republic Act No. 11697, the Electric
574   Vehicle Industry Development Act; Republic Act No. 9514, the Revised Fire Code of the
575   Philippines of 2008; Republic Act No. 11589, the Bureau of Fire Protection Modernization
576   Act; Republic Act No. 6969, the Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes
577   Control Act of 1990; Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012; Republic Act
578   No. 11032, the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of
579   2018; Republic Act No. 12009, the New Government Procurement Act, where applicable;
580   and other applicable laws, treaties, rules and regulations.
581
582   SEC. 27. Separability Clause.
583         If any provision or part of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the
584   remaining provisions or portions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
585
586   SEC. 28. Repealing Clause.
587          All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative orders, rules, regulations and
588   other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended or
589   modified accordingly.
590
591   SEC. 29. Effectivity.
592           This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette
593   or in a newspaper of general circulation.
