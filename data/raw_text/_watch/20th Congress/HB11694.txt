EXPLANATORY NOTE

In many Filipino communities in the mapped and poorly served areas of the country,
agricultural machinery and heavy equipment are either scarce or not available. Livestock and
working animals form a vital part of the agricultural, economic, and social structure. They serve
the purpose of providing food, offering a means of livelihood, supplying transportation,
contributing to agricultural labor, and carrying out a number of other essential functions. However,
when typhoons, floods, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides, droughts, and other disasters
and calamities occur, these animals are very susceptible to being injured, falling ill, being
displaced, starving, being abandoned, and dying.

This is evident in the farthest communities of the First Legislative District of Zamboanga
City.

Although the present laws in the Philippines include general provisions concerning disaster
risk reduction and management, as well as animal welfare, veterinary services, and the functions
of local government, there is still the need for a clear and coordinated framework which
specifically covers the protection and welfare of livestock and working animals in the case of
disasters. The Local Government Code also acknowledges veterinary activities that arise both
during and after natural and man-made disasters. The proposed measure aims at strengthening and
harmonizing these existing responsibilities by means of uniform national guidelines, without
setting up a separate or redundant disaster-response structure.

The bill requires the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council
(NDRRMC) to draw up and issue national guidelines relating to the rescue, evacuation, protection,
provision of temporary shelter, emergency veterinary care, supply of medicines and medical
supplies, and the rehabilitation of livestock and working animals which are affected by disasters

and other calamities.

===== [page 2] =====
The guidelines must set out the minimum standards and procedures concerning the kind
and proper treatment of affected animals, including their rescue, evacuation, transport, sheltering,
feeding, watering, treatment and rehabilitation. They must also include suitable arrangements for
disease prevention, biosecurity, quarantine, identification, documentation and, where possible, for
the reunification of the rescued animals with their owners or caregivers.

The law takes into account the separate duties and expertise of the Department of
Agriculture and the Bureau of Animal Industry with regard to livestock and animal health, as well
as the coordinating function of the Department of the Interior and Local Government and the
current responsibilities of the local government units in dealing with disasters and providing
veterinary services.

The bill also promotes the involvement of socio-civic organizations, animal welfare
organizations, veterinary professionals, associations of livestock and animal owners, and private-
sector organizations in both disaster preparedness and response. In this way, they can provide extra
personnel, transport, temporary shelter, food, water, veterinary medicines, medical supplies, and
other resources which are needed in order to ensure that the response to animal disasters is both
timely and effective.

The protection of livestock and working animals is also a vital part of community
resilience; for families whose means of livelihood are based on these animals, losing them can lead
to serious economic difficulties and slow down the process of recovering after. Timely action is
therefore necessary to make sure that rescue operations, veterinary care, and rehabilitation take
place in a timely manner, since this contributes both to animal welfare and to the recovery and
resilience of the affected households and communities.

This measure aims to make sure that livestock and working animals are properly included
in disaster preparedness, response, rehabilitation, and recovery activities across the country by
setting up uniform national guidelines and improving coordination between agencies and across
different sectors.

On the basis of what has been said, there is a strong appeal for this bill to proceed

immediately.

KATRANA REIKO CHUA-TAI

AN ACT
MANDATING THE NATIONAL DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT
COUNCIL TO DEVELOP AND PROMULGATE NATIONAL GUIDELINES FOR THE
RESCUE, EVACUATION, PROTECTION, AND PROVISION OF EMERGENCY
VETERINARY CARE, MEDICINES, AND MEDICAL SUPPLIES TO LIVESTOCK AND
WORKING ANIMALS DURING CALAMITIES AND OTHER DISASTERS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Livestock and Working

Animals Disaster Protection Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. —\t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the
welfare of livestock and working animals affected by calamities and other disasters and to
recognize their contribution to agriculture, food security, transportation, livelihood, and the

economic well-being of families and communities.

Towards this end, the State shall ensure that disaster risk reduction and management
policies, plans, and programs provide appropriate measures for the timely and humane rescue,
evacuation, protection, temporary shelter, feeding, watering, transportation, emergency veterinary
care, provision of medicines and medical supplies, and rehabilitation of livestock and working

animals affected or threatened by calamities and other disasters.

Animal Disaster Response refers to the organized and coordinated measures
undertaken before, during, and after a calamity or disaster to prevent or minimize
injury, illness, displacement, suffering, and loss among livestock and working animals,
including rescue, evacuation, temporary shelter, feeding, watering, transportation,
emergency veterinary care, provision of medicines and medical supplies,
identification, rehabilitation, and, when practicable, reunification with their owners or

caretakers;

Calamity or Disaster refers to a serious disruption of the functioning of a community
resulting from natural or human-induced hazards that causes or threatens to cause
casualties, damage to property, economic losses, displacement, environmental

damage, or other significant impacts;

Livestock refers to domesticated animals raised, maintained, or used for agricultural,
food production, breeding, commercial, or livelihood purposes, including cattle,
carabaos, horses, goats, sheep, swine, poultry, and other farm animals as may be

determined by the appropriate government agency;

Working Animals refers to domesticated animals used for transportation, agricultural

activities, hauling, security, rescue, or other lawful work-related purposes;

Emergency Veterinary Care refers to immediate veterinary assessment, first aid,
diagnosis, treatment, stabilization, wound management, disease prevention,
vaccination when indicated, and other necessary veterinary interventions provided by
duly licensed veterinarians or other persons legally authorized to provide such

SEC. 4. Scope. — This Act shall apply to livestock and working animals that are affected,
displaced, injured, sick, or at risk as a consequence of calamities and other disasters within the

territorial jurisdiction of the Republic of the Philippines.

The national guidelines to be developed and promulgated under this Act shall establish

minimum standards and procedures concerning:
(a) disaster preparedness and contingency planning for livestock and working animals;
(b) animal rescue, evacuation, and transportation;

(c) identification and designation of suitable animal evacuation centers and temporary

shelter facilities;

(d) provision of adequate food, potable water, emergency veterinary care, medicines,

medical supplies, and other essential needs;

(e) humane handling, restraint, transportation, sheltering, and treatment of animals;

SEC. 5. Roles and Responsibilities. — The National Disaster Risk Reduction and

Management Council (NDRRMC) shall:

(a) develop and promulgate national guidelines for the rescue, evacuation, protection,
temporary shelter, emergency veterinary care, provision of medicines and medical
supplies, and rehabilitation of livestock and working animals during calamities and

other disasters;

(b) ensure that the protection of livestock and working animals is appropriately integrated

into relevant national disaster risk reduction and management policies, plans, and

programs;

(c) establish minimum standards and protocols for animal rescue, evacuation,
transportation, temporary shelter, emergency veterinary response, and animal health

protection;

(d) facilitate the development of training and capacity-building programs for government

personnel, animal disaster response teams, and other qualified responders;

SEC. 6. Inter-Agency Collaboration. — The NDRRMC shall establish appropriate

instrumentalities, including the Department of Agriculture, Bureau of Animal Industry,

The NDRRMC may create an Animal Disaster Response Technical Working Group

SEC. 7. Participation of Socio-Civic Organizations and Private Sector. — The
NDRRMC, concerned national government agencies, and local government units shall encourage
and facilitate the participation of duly registered socio-civic organizations, animal welfare
organizations, veterinary organizations, agricultural organizations, livestock and animal owners’
associations, private veterinary practitioners, businesses, and other qualified private entities in

animal disaster preparedness, response, rehabilitation, and recovery activities.

Subject to applicable laws, rules, regulations, and coordination with the appropriate

government authorities, participating organizations and private entities may:
(a) provide trained personnel and volunteers for animal rescue and evacuation;

(b) provide or assist in providing temporary shelter, transportation, animal feed, potable

water, veterinary medicines, medical supplies, and other essential resources;

(c) provide emergency veterinary services through duly licensed veterinarians and other

legally qualified personnel;

(d) assist in the identification, documentation, rehabilitation, and reunification of rescued

animals with their owners or caretakers;
(e) assist in public information, education, training, and simulation exercises; and

(f) provide other lawful assistance consistent with the national guidelines promulgated

under this Act.

Participation under this section shall be voluntary and shall not confer upon any private
person or organization the authority to exercise governmental regulatory or enforcement powers

unless otherwise authorized by law.

of Animal Industry, Department of the Interior and Local Government, concerned national
government agencies, local government units, veterinary professionals, animal welfare

organizations, socio-civic organizations, private-sector representatives, and other relevant
