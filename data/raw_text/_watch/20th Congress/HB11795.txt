                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The modernization of government vehicles is essential to the country's pursuit
of environmental sustainability, energy security, and efficient public administration.
Government agencies, local government units, and government-owned or controlled
corporations continue to rely heavily on gasoline and diesel-powered vehicles,
contributing to air pollution, greenhouse gas emissions, and recurring public
expenditures on fuel.

       The country's dependence on petroleum exposes government operations to
fluctuating oil prices, supply disruptions, and rising maintenance costs. Transitioning
to electric vehicles (EVs) offers an opportunity to reduce these expenses, minimize
carbon emissions, and promote energy efficiency while supporting the development
of the local electric mobility industry.

       However, the transition requires adequate financing, charging infrastructure,
technical support, and trained personnel. A systematic and phased approach is
therefore necessary to ensure that modernization remains financially sustainable and
does not disrupt government operations.

        This proposed measure seeks to establish a National Government Fleet
Electrification and Modernization Program, mandating government agencies and local
government units to gradually replace conventional vehicles with electric vehicles. It
further directs the Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with other concerned
agencies, to develop charging infrastructure, financing mechanisms, and technical
standards to support the transition.

      By adopting this measure, the government can lead the country's shift toward
cleaner transportation, reduce dependence on imported petroleum, and ensure more
efficient utilization of public funds while advancing environmental protection and
energy security.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL GOVERNMENT FLEET MODERNIZATION
     PROGRAM, PROVIDING FOR THE GRADUAL TRANSITION FROM FUEL-
     POWERED GOVERNMENT VEHICLES TO ELECTRIC VEHICLES, PROVIDING
     FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Government
 2   Fleet Modernization Act."
 3
 4           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 5   State to promote sustainable transportation, energy efficiency, environmental
 6   protection, and the responsible utilization of public funds through the gradual
 7   transition of government-owned and government-operated vehicles from conventional
 8   fuel-powered vehicles to electric vehicles.
 9
10           The State shall pursue the modernization of government vehicle fleets to reduce
11   dependence on imported petroleum, minimize greenhouse gas emissions and air
12   pollution, lower long-term government operating expenditures, and promote the
13   development of the country's electric mobility industry.
14
15          For this purpose, the State shall establish a comprehensive and coordinated
16   framework for the acquisition, operation, maintenance, and eventual replacement of
17   conventional government vehicles with electric vehicles, while ensuring that the
18   delivery of public services remains efficient, accessible, and uninterrupted.
19
20
21         SECTION 3. National Government Fleet Electrification and
22   Modernization Program. – There is hereby established a National Government
23   Fleet Modernization Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program, which shall
 1   provide the framework for the gradual transition of government vehicle fleets to
 2   electric mobility.
 3
 4          The Program shall be anchored on the following principles:
 5
 6          (a) Gradual and systematic transition from conventional fuel-powered
 7   government vehicles to electric vehicles;
 8          (b) Reduction of government expenditures on gasoline, diesel, and other
 9   petroleum-based fuels;
10          (c) Environmental sustainability and reduction of greenhouse gas emissions;
11          (d) Energy efficiency and promotion of renewable energy utilization;
12          (e) Financial sustainability and responsible use of public funds;
13          (f) Availability of adequate charging infrastructure and maintenance facilities;
14          (g) Protection of government employees and workers affected by the transition;
15   and
16          (h) Continuity, accessibility, and efficiency of government operations and public
17   services.
18
19          SECTION 4. Coverage and Scope. – This Act shall apply to all national
20   government agencies, departments, bureaus, offices, instrumentalities, state
21   universities and colleges, local government units, and government-owned or -
22   controlled corporations, including their respective regional and field offices.
23
24         The Program shall cover government-owned, leased, or otherwise government-
25   operated motor vehicles used for official government functions, including:
26         (a) Administrative and service vehicles;
27         (b) Passenger and utility vehicles;
28         (c) Inspection, monitoring, and field operation vehicles;
29         (d) Delivery and logistics vehicles;
30         (e) Public service and maintenance vehicles; and
31         (f) Other vehicles used in the performance of official government functions, as
32   may be determined by the implementing agencies.
33
34          SECTION 5. Government Fleet Inventory and Electrification Plan. – All
35   covered government entities shall conduct a comprehensive inventory of their existing
36   vehicle fleets within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.
37
38          The inventory shall include:
39          (a) The number and classification of existing government vehicles;
40          (b) The age, condition, and remaining useful life of each vehicle;
41          (c) Average annual fuel consumption and maintenance expenditures;
42          (d) The frequency and nature of vehicle utilization;
43          (e) The estimated cost of replacement with electric vehicles; and
44          (f) The availability of charging infrastructure and electricity supply within the
45   areas of operation.
46
 1         Based on the inventory, each covered government entity shall prepare a
 2   Government Fleet Electrification Plan identifying its priorities, implementation
 3   schedule, funding requirements, and infrastructure needs.
 4
 5         The plans shall be submitted to the Department of Energy (DOE) and the
 6   Department of Budget and Management (DBM) for consolidation, monitoring, and
 7   evaluation.
 8
 9           SECTION 6. Phased Electrification and Modernization. – All covered
10   government entities shall undertake the gradual replacement of their conventional
11   fuel-powered vehicles with electric vehicles in accordance with the following
12   guidelines:
13           (a) Priority shall be given to the acquisition of electric vehicles for
14   administrative, passenger, utility, and other government operations where electric
15   vehicles are technically and operationally suitable;
16           (b) All new procurement of government vehicles shall prioritize battery electric
17   vehicles, subject to technical feasibility, operational requirements, availability, and the
18   approved Government Fleet Electrification Plan;
19           (c) The replacement of existing conventional vehicles shall be undertaken
20   according to their age, condition, maintenance costs, and remaining useful life, subject
21   to the availability of funds;
22           (d) Government entities shall progressively reduce the acquisition and use of
23   new gasoline- and diesel-powered vehicles, except in cases covered by the exemptions
24   under this Act; and
25           (e) Covered government entities shall endeavor to complete the transition of
26   their eligible vehicle fleets within ten (10) years from the effectivity of this Act.
27
28          The DOE, in coordination with the DBM and other concerned agencies, shall
29   establish annual electrification targets and performance indicators to ensure the
30   orderly implementation of the Program.
31
32          SECTION 7. Government Charging Infrastructure. – The DOE, in
33   coordination with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), Department
34   of Transportation (DOTr), and other concerned agencies, shall facilitate the
35   development and installation of electric vehicle charging infrastructure in government-
36   owned and government-operated facilities.
37
38   For this purpose, covered government entities shall:
39   (a) Identify suitable locations within government premises for the installation of
40   charging stations;
41   (b) Integrate charging infrastructure requirements into the planning and development
42   of new government buildings and facilities;
43   (c) Retrofit existing government facilities, where practicable, to accommodate electric
44   vehicle charging systems;
45   (d) Establish appropriate safety, maintenance, and operational standards for charging
46   facilities; and
47   (e) Explore partnerships with qualified private entities for the installation, operation,
48   and maintenance of charging infrastructure.
 1
 2   The DOE shall ensure that government charging facilities comply with applicable
 3   electrical safety, energy efficiency, and technical standards.
 4
 5           SECTION 8. Financial and Institutional Support. – Government financial
 6   institutions (GFIs), particularly the Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) and the
 7   Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP), in coordination with the DBM and DOE, shall
 8   develop financing mechanisms to support the implementation of the Program.
 9
10          Such mechanisms may include:
11          (a) Low-interest loans for the acquisition of electric vehicles;
12          (b) Financing arrangements for the installation of charging infrastructure;
13          (c) Lease-to-own and other financing schemes for government entities;
14          (d) Credit facilities for local government units with limited financial resources;
15   and
16          (e) Other financing arrangements that may facilitate the transition to electric
17   mobility.
18
19           The DBM shall establish appropriate budgetary guidelines and mechanisms to
20   assist covered government entities in incorporating vehicle electrification requirements
21   into their respective annual budgets and procurement plans.
22
23          SECTION 9. Procurement of Electric Vehicles. – The Government
24   Procurement Policy Board (GPPB), in coordination with the DOE, DBM, and other
25   concerned agencies, shall establish procurement guidelines that prioritize the
26   acquisition of electric vehicles by covered government entities.
27
28          Such guidelines shall consider the following:
29          (a) Total cost of ownership, including acquisition, operation, maintenance, and
30   eventual disposal costs;
31          (b) Energy efficiency and environmental performance;
32          (c) Availability of spare parts, technical support, and maintenance services;
33          (d) Battery capacity, vehicle range, and suitability for intended government
34   operations;
35          (e) Availability and accessibility of charging infrastructure; and
36          (f) Compliance with applicable safety, quality, and technical standards.
37
38          Procurement specifications shall be performance-based and shall encourage
39   fair competition among qualified suppliers, manufacturers, and service providers.
40
41         The government shall, where practicable, prioritize locally assembled or
42   manufactured electric vehicles and components, consistent with applicable
43   procurement and trade laws.
44
45         SECTION 10. Capacity-Building and Technical Training Programs. –
46   The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in coordination
47   with the DOE, DOTr, and other concerned agencies, shall develop training and
 1   certification programs on electric vehicle operation, maintenance, repair, and charging
 2   infrastructure management.
 3
 4          Covered government entities shall ensure that their personnel responsible for
 5   vehicle fleet management and maintenance receive appropriate training to support
 6   the implementation of the Program.
 7
 8         The training programs shall include:
 9         (a) Basic and advanced electric vehicle maintenance;
10         (b) Battery management and safety procedures;
11         (c) Charging station operation and maintenance;
12         (d) Electrical safety and emergency response; and
13         (e) Fleet management and energy efficiency practices.
14
15           SECTION 11. Exemptions and Special Considerations. – The mandatory
16   transition requirements under this Act shall not apply to government vehicles where
17   the acquisition or operation of electric vehicles is technically, operationally,
18   geographically, or economically impracticable.
19
20          Exemptions may be granted for:
21          (a) Emergency response vehicles, including ambulances, fire trucks, and
22   disaster response vehicles, when the available electric alternatives cannot adequately
23   meet operational requirements;
24          (b) Military, police, and other national security vehicles requiring specialized
25   capabilities;
26          (c) Heavy-duty vehicles and specialized equipment for which suitable electric
27   alternatives are not commercially available;
28          (d) Vehicles assigned to geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas where
29   adequate charging infrastructure and reliable electricity supply are unavailable; and
30          (e) Other vehicles as may be determined by the DOE, in consultation with the
31   concerned government entity.
32
33          Any exemption shall be supported by a written justification and shall be subject
34   to periodic review to determine whether the relevant operational limitations have been
35   addressed.
36
37         SECTION 12. Disposal and Management of Retired Government
38   Vehicles. – Conventional fuel-powered vehicles replaced under the Program shall be
39   disposed of in accordance with existing government property disposal, accounting,
40   and auditing rules and regulations.
41
42          The disposal process shall ensure transparency, accountability, and the proper
43   valuation of government assets.
44
45         The DOE, in coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural
46   Resources (DENR), shall establish guidelines for the environmentally sound
47   management and disposal of batteries, vehicle components, and other hazardous
48   materials generated from electric vehicle operations.
 1
 2         Government entities shall ensure that retired electric vehicle batteries are
 3   handled, collected, recycled, or disposed of through authorized facilities and in
 4   accordance with applicable environmental laws and regulations.

 7   created a Government Fleet Modernization Council, hereinafter referred to as the
 8   Council, which shall be chaired by the DOE.
 9
10           The Council shall be composed of the following:
11           (a) Department of Energy;
12           (b) Department of Transportation;
13           (c) Department of Budget and Management;
14           (d) Department of Public Works and Highways;
15           (e) Department of Environment and Natural Resources;
16           (f) Government Procurement Policy Board;
17           (g) Development Bank of the Philippines;
18           (h) Land Bank of the Philippines;
19           (i) Government Service Insurance System;
20           (j) Representatives from the League of Provinces of the Philippines, League of
21   Cities of the Philippines, and League of Municipalities of the Philippines; and
22           (k) Representatives from the electric vehicle industry and other relevant
23   stakeholders.
24
25         The Council shall:
26         (a) Oversee and coordinate the implementation of the Program;
27         (b) Consolidate and evaluate government fleet electrification plans;
28         (c) Establish national targets and performance indicators;
29         (d) Recommend policies and financing mechanisms to accelerate the transition;
30         (e) Monitor the development of government charging infrastructure;
31         (f) Facilitate coordination among national government agencies and local
32   government units; and
33         (g) Submit an annual report to the Office of the President and Congress on the
34   implementation and progress of the Program.
35
36         SECTION 14. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DOE, in coordination with
37   the DBM and other concerned agencies, shall establish a monitoring and evaluation
38   system to assess the implementation of this Act.
39
40           All covered government entities shall submit annual reports containing
41   information on:
42           (a) The number of conventional and electric vehicles in their respective fleets;
43           (b) Annual expenditures on fuel, electricity, vehicle maintenance, and charging
44   infrastructure;
45           (c) The number and location of government charging stations;
46           (d) Reductions in petroleum consumption and estimated greenhouse gas
47   emissions;
48           (e) Progress in meeting established electrification targets; and
 1         (f) Other relevant information as may be required by the Council.
 2
 3         The Council shall publish an annual consolidated report to ensure transparency
 4   and accountability in the utilization of public funds and the implementation of the
 5   Program.

 8   provisions of this Act shall be included in the appropriations of the DOE and other
