                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


Scientific evidence has shown that exposure to tobacco smoke causes death, diseases
and disability to non- smokers.1

According to the Global Adult Tobacco Survey of 20212, in the Philippines, 12.9 % of
adults are exposed to tobacco smoke in enclosed areas at the workplace, 2.7 % are
exposed to tobacco smoke in healthcare facilities, 6.6 % are exposed in government
buildings or offices, 12.2 % are exposed to tobacco in public transport, 3.4 % are
exposed in universities, and 2.4 % are exposed to tobacco in schools.

By creating smoke-free public spaces, we will be able to uphold and protect the right to
health of everyone and instill health consciousness among them as guaranteed by the
1987 Philippine Constitution.

This bill provides for an evidence-based smoke-free public place legislation designed to
protect individuals from tobacco smoke exposure and mitigate associated health risks.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
    MANDATING A SMOKE-FREE ENVIRONMENT IN ALL PUBLIC AND
  WORKPLACES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9211,
   OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "TOBACCO REGULATION ACT OF 2003"

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Smoke-Free Environment Act".

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
The State also recognizes that effective measures to provide protection from exposure to
tobacco smoke, as envisioned by Article 8 of the World Health Organization Framework
Convention on Tobacco Control, require the total elimination of smoking and tobacco
smoke in a particular space or environment in order to create a 100% smoke free
environment. It is, therefore, the State's policy to protect all people from exposure to
tobacco smoke.

      SEC. 3. Construction – In case of doubt, the interpretation of any of the
provisions of this Act, including its implementing rules and regulations (IRRD), shall be
construed liberally in favor of the protection and promotion of public health.

      SEC. 5 Section 5 of Republic Act No. 9211 is hereby amended to read as follows:
      “SEC. 5. Smoke-free Public Places. - Smoking shall be prohibited in all public
and work places:

  a. Centers of youth activity such as playschools, preparatory schools, elementary
     schools, high schools, colleges and universities, youth hostels, and recreational
     facilities for persons under eighteen (18) years old;
  b. Elevator and stairwells;
  c. Location in which fire hazards are present, including gas stations and storage areas
     for flammable liquids, gas, explosives, or combustible materials;
  d. Within the buildings and premises of public and private hospitals, medical, dental,
     and optical clinics, health centers, nursing homes, dispensaries and laboratories;
  e. Public conveyance including all terminals and public facilities;
  f. Food preparation areas;
  g. Government offices; and
  h. Other public places as may be designated as smoke-free through an ordinance to
     promote general welfare.

       SEC. 6. Section 6 of Republic Act No. 9211 is hereby amended to read as

        "SEC 6. Non-Smoking Areas and Smoking Areas - In all enclosed places that are
open to the general public, workplaces, and other places not covered under the preceding
section, where smoking may expose a person other than the smoker to tobacco smoke,
the owner, proprietor, operator, possessor, manager or administrator of such places may
establish smoking areas. Such area must be in an outdoor space with the following
requirements:

  a. It shall be located in an open space with no permanent or temporary roof or walls in
     an outdoor area;
  b. It shall not be located within 10 meters of entrances, exits or any place where
     people pass or where people congregate;
  c. It shall not have an area larger than 10 square meters;
  d. No more than one smoking area shall be designated in a building or public place;
  e. No food or drinks shall be served in the designated smoking area; and,
  f. Every smoking area shall have a highly visible and prominently displayed:
     f.1) "Smoking Area" signage;
     f.2) Graphic health warnings on the effects of tobacco use, and
       f.3) Prohibition on the entry of minors.

       Nothing in this Act shall compel persons-in-charge to establish designated
smoking areas nor prevent them from instituting more stringent measures in their
buildings and establishments to better ensure a smoke-free environment.

        These smoking and non-smoking signages should be made part of the
requirements for the issuance or renewal of a business permit to operate an establishment,
health facilities or schools. Failure to comply therewith is a ground for the denial or
revocation of such permit, Provided, that for schools, these signages should be
prominently displayed whenever youth activities are being conducted declaring that the
area is a smoke-free zone.

      SEC. 7. Obligations of Persons-in-Charge. Persons-in-charge, operators and
owners of establishments within public and workplaces shall:

  a. Prominently post and display "No Smoking" Signage in locations most visible to
      the public. The sign shall be at least 8 x 11inches in size;
  b. Remove ash trays and other receptacles for disposing cigarette refuse in place
      where smoking is prohibited;
  c. Establish internal procedures and measures in which this Act shall be implemented
     and ordered within his or her areas of jurisdiction;
  d. Ensure that all employees are aware of this Act before implementation and
     enforcement; and,
  e. Have several signages versions, including those written in local dialects or in
     English.


       SEC. 8. Prohibited Acts. The following are declared unlawful:

  a. Smoking in public and workplaces;
  b. Inducing, allowing; or tolerating smoking in smoke-free areas provided in Section
     5 and 6 of this Act;
  c. Establishment of smoking areas in smoke-free public places pursuant to Section 5;
  d. Failure to comply with the requirements provided under Section 6, and,

  e. Other analogous acts or omissions in violation of this Act.
  SEC. 9. Persons Liable. - The following are liable and shall be punished in
    accordance with the governing provisions of this Act:

  a. Any person or entity who commits prohibited acts; and
  b. Persons-in-charge who knowingly allow, abet, authorize, or tolerate the prohibited
     acts, or have failed to fulfill their duties and obligations enumerated in this Act.

       SEC 10. Creation of Smoke-Free Task Forces, — All provinces, cities and
municipalities are mandated to establish a Smoke-Free Task Force. The Smoke-Free
Task Force, in coordination with Philippine National Police, shall be responsible in the
implementation, enforcement, and monitoring of this Act; provided that the effectivity
and enforcement of this Act shall not be prejudiced by the lack of a Smoke Free Task
Force in the government unit.

       Local government units shall allocate a 3% dedicated fund from the Local Health
Budget for the operation of the Smoke Free Task Force and shall enact measures
necessary to implement the provisions of this Act including the development and
implementation of supportive programs to promote smoke-free environment. The Task
Force is mandated to meet on a quarterly basis, or whenever necessary, to carry out the
provisions of this Act and other relevant tobacco control laws and ordinances.

      The Smoke-Free Task Force shall be chaired by the Mayor and co-chaired by the
Municipal / City Health Office. The Task Force shall have the following members:
 a. Business Processing and Licensing Office
 b. Engineering Office
 c. Offices involved in Peace and Order
 d. Association of barangay Captains Chair
 e. SK Federation President
 f. School District Representative
 g. Tertiary Institution Representatives
 h. Environmental Protection Officer

The Smoke-Free Task Force shall include at least one representative from civil society
organizations, community and people organizations promoting public health which
should not be connected, directly or indirectly, or represent the interest of the tobacco
industry.

        SEC. 11 - Awards and Recognition. The Department of Health (DOH) and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) are hereby mandated to
include the faithful implementation of this Act, particularly the performance of the
Smoke-Free Task Force, as a requisite in the grant of the Red Orchid Awards and the
Seal of Good Local Governance awards, or any other incentives program for the private
sector for promoting smoke-free environment, as the Department of Health may approve.

remain valid and subsisting.

        SEC 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
