20                                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   When a family has nowhere else to turn, when a child needs protection, or when a community
23   is struck by disaster, it is often a social worker who stands between a vulnerable person and
24   the government assistance they need.
25
26   Yet, the work does not end when the case is closed. Social workers are exposed to the
27   emotional weight of difficult cases while still responding with the professionalism and technical
28   competence demanded by their profession, as well as with empathy and resilience.
29
30   It is only just, therefore, that their compensation reflects the modern responsibilities of their
31   profession.
32
33   Beyond compensation, this measure also recognizes that government social work has evolved
34   far beyond traditional “social welfare.” Social workers now perform essential functions in
35   community development, disaster risk management, gender and development, labor and
36   employment, peace, early childhood development, youth development, and assistance to
37   veterans, among others.
38
39   The same principle should apply to registered social workers engaged through job orders or
40   contracts of service. A social worker does not become less of a professional because of the
41   form of their engagement. If they perform the same essential functions, they deserve the same
42   recognition and protection under the Magna Carta.
43
44   Ultimately, the protection of the Magna Carta should follow the social worker. This measure
45   seeks to bring the law closer to the realities of modern government service and to give social
46   workers the recognition that the weight of their work deserves.
47
48   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 6   in government service, INCLUDING THOSE WHOSE SERVICES ARE ENGAGED
 7   THROUGH JOB ORDERS OR CONTRACTS OF SERVICE.”

 8          SEC. 3. Section 12 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

 9                      “SEC. 12. Compensation. — [The existing law] APPLICABLE
10                 LAWS, RULES AND REGULATIONS on [the] salary scaleS of
11                 government employees shall apply in determining the salaries of public
12                 social workers[.]: PROVIDED, THAT THE SALARY GRADE OF ENTRY-
13                 LEVEL JUNIOR OFFICER PUBLIC SOCIAL WORKERS SHALL BE AT
14                 LEAST SALARY GRADE 13: PROVIDED, HOWEVER, THAT NOTHING
15                 IN THIS ACT SHALL BE CONSTRUED TO ELIMINATE OR IN ANY
16                 WAY DIMINISH BENEFITS BEING ENJOYED BY PUBLIC SOCIAL
17                 WORKERS AT THE TIME OF THE EFFECTIVITY OF THIS ACT:
18                 PROVIDED, FURTHER, THAT [In] IN case of violations of this provision,
19                 the public social worker concerned shall file the necessary complaint to
20                 the CSC or NLRC through the Social Work Management and Consultative
21                 Council: PROVIDED, FINALLY, THAT FOR LOCAL GOVERNMENT
22                 UNITS WHICH DO NOT HAVE ADEQUATE OR SUFFICIENT FUNDS
23                 SHALL ONLY PARTIALLY IMPLEMENT THE ESTABLISHED RATE
24                 AS MAY BE APPROVED BY THE SOCIAL WORK MANAGEMENT
25                 AND CONSULTATIVE COUNCIL.”

26      SEC. 4. The Department of Budget and Management, in consultation with the Social
27   Work Management and Consultative Council, shall, within ninety (90) days after the
28   approval of this Act, modify the Index of Occupational Services, Occupational Groups,
29   Classes and Salary Grades to adjust the salary grades of public social workers.
30
31      SEC. 5. All other laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, issuances, and rules and
32   regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby
33   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
