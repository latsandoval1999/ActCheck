                                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Home to a diverse ecosystem, the Philippines is rich in natural resources and
hosts countless animal species. But nature also holds danger. According to one study1,
roughly 33 species of venomous snakes live in the Philippines and its surrounding
coastal waters, alongside dangerous fish and jellyfish, toxic snails, and spiders and
insects.

        Snakebites and marine envenomation claim many Filipino lives each year.
According to news reports, tragic cases occur regularly, including one instance where
a 14-year-old girl died shielding her cousins from a snake attack2. Marine
envenomation threats are equally severe; jellyfish stings, which can prove fatal, take
an estimated 40 Filipino lives annually3.

        Snakebites and marine envenomation are urgent public health concerns,
particularly in agricultural, coastal, and indigenous communities where exposure is
high. Thus, the immediate administration of antivenom is both critical and life-saving.
However, many health care facilities, especially those in remote areas, lack both the
supply and the facilities required to keep antivenom viable.

        This measure seeks to ensure that health care facilities, whether public or
private, maintain an accessible supply of antivenom and adequate storage capacity to
preserve its efficacy. It also aims to guarantee timely medical intervention and prevent
venom-related illnesses and deaths. Additionally, it seeks to equip high-risk
communities with the knowledge needed to prevent and respond to venom-related
incidents through proper first aid and information.

        Coexistence with animals means safeguarding communities while respecting
nature and all its creatures.
       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
MANDATING THE AVAILABILITY OF ANTIVENOM IN ALL HOSPITALS,
INFIRMARIES, AND RURAL HEALTH UNITS, PROVIDING GUIDELINES
  FOR STORAGE AND DISTRIBUTION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. -      This Act shall be known as the “Accessible
Antivenom Act of 2026”

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State in
accordance with the 1987 Philippine Constitution, to protect and promote the right to
health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.

       The State likewise recognizes the need to ensure the availability and
accessibility of life-saving medical interventions, including antivenom, for victims of
snakebites or marine envenomation, especially those living in vulnerable and far-
flung communities.

       Section 3. Definition of Terms- As used in this Act, the following terms are
defined as follows:

    A.    Antivenom- refers to a biological product used to treat venomous bites or
         stings by neutralizing the venom;

    B.    Hospitals- refer to any healthcare facility that provide primary medical care
         and basic emergency services but are not licensed as hospitals;

    C. Rural Health Unit (RHU)- refer to a primary-level healthcare facility
       established by the Department of Health (DOH) in a municipality; and

    D. Venomous Species- refer to animals that inject venom, including but not
       limited to snakes, spiders, and marine animals.

      Section 4. Expanded Distribution of Anti-Venom- The Department of Health
(DOH), in coordination with the local government units (LGUs), shall ensure the
expanded and regular distribution of appropriate antivenom in all Rural Health Units


                                           2
(RHUs), infirmaries, and public and private hospitals particularly those located in
areas identified as high-risk for envenomation from venomous snakes and marine
species.

        Section 5. Identification of High-Risk Areas for Venomous Snakes and
Marine Species- The DOH, through its Epidemiology Bureau and in consultation
with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), and local
health offices, shall establish a comprehensive list and regularly update the
identification of municipalities and barangays considered as high-risk areas for
venomous snakes and marine species based on epidemiological data, geographical
characteristics and reported incidences of snakebites and marine envenomation.

       Section 6. Stockpiling and Inventory.-

       a) The DOH shall maintain a strategic national stockpile of antivenom to
ensure adequate supply;

         b) All RHUs, infirmaries, and hospitals in identified high-risk areas for
venomous snakes and marine species shall maintain a minimum stock of appropriate
anti-venom, as determined by the DOH based on the prevalence of venomous species
in their respective localities; and

       c) Regular inventory and replenishment mechanisms shall be established to
prevent stock-outs and ensure the viability of the anti-venom supply.

        Section 7. Training and Capacity Building.- The DOH shall develop and
implement a comprehensive training for healthcare professionals in RHUs, infirmaries,
and hospitals on the proper administration, storage, and management of anti-venom,
as well as on the diagnosis and management of snakebites and marine envenomation.
This training shall include protocols for referral to higher-level facilities when
necessary.

        Section 8.Public Awareness and Education Campaign.- The DOH, in
coordination with the LGUs, shall conduct a continuous public awareness and
education campaign in high-risk areas for venomous snakes and marine species,
prevention, first aid measures, and the importance of seeking immediate medical
attention at healthcare facilities equipped with antivenom.

       Section 9. Funding.- The initial funding for the implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of Health.
Thereafter, such sums may be necessary for the continuous implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       Section 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Health, in consultation with relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

         Section 11. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held

       Section 12. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential
