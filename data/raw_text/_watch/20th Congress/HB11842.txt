                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The arrest of veteran journalist Philip Lustre Jr. on 24 October 2026, on a
cyberlibel warrant issued by a court in Davao City for a complaint he says he never
received, is the latest reminder that criminal libel punishes long before any verdict is
handed down.1 The arrest, the detention, the bail and the burden of defending a case
far from home fall on the accused whether or not the charge is ever proven. Nor is the
danger confined to the press.

       As the National Union of Journalists of the Philippines (NUJP) has warned,
criminal libel can be weaponized against any ordinary citizen who openly criticizes
those in power, and its toll can outlast the case itself. The organization has recounted
how a journalist remained traumatized by a cyberlibel complaint even after it was
dismissed.2 Criminal libel thus operates less as a remedy for injured reputation than
as an instrument to deter speech.

       This bill seeks to remove defamation from our penal statutes. It aims to repeal
the provisions of the Revised Penal Code on libel and oral defamation, as well as the
cybercrime of libel under Republic Act No. 10175. In doing so, it follows the guidance
of the United Nations Human Rights Committee, which has urged States to
decriminalize defamation. The UN body has in fact found that jailing a Filipino
broadcaster for libel violated the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.3

        Decriminalization, however, must not leave the injured without recourse.
Reputation is, at bottom, a personal interest, and the remedy for injuring it belongs in
a civil court rather than in a criminal prosecution brought in the name of the State. As
the Center for International Law (CenterLaw) has argued, the person whose reputation
is at stake should proceed with litigation through an action for damages4 on account
of defamation, in the same way that our Civil Code has already recognized
independent civil actions for damages in a number of actionable wrongs.5

       This bill introduces a civil law framework, one that reinforces existing Civil Code
provisions on torts and damages. It defines the actionable conduct and provides
appropriate remedies. It protects opinion and comment, including satire and parody,
so long as they do not assert false facts. It adopts the “actual malice standard” for
statements about public officials, public figures, and matters of public concern, so that
those who hold power, or seek public attention, cannot recover for criticism unless
they prove a knowing or reckless falsehood. It limits liability to authors and to those
who approve publication, sparing the platforms that carry speech and the readers who
innocently read the same. It requires that damages be proportionate to the injury.

        The bill keeps the existing venue rules. An action may be filed only where the
statement was first published or where the offended party resides or holds office, and
the first court to take the case keeps it to the exclusion of all others. A single-
publication rule governs the one-year prescriptive period, so that a statement does not
become a new cause of action every time it is read online.

        Extortion through the threat of publication remains a crime. Pending criminal
cases are dismissed, and the offended parties keep the right to pursue their claims in
a civil court.

        A democracy is measured by how it treats those who question power. For too
long, our libel laws have answered criticism with handcuffs, and speech with the threat
of a prison cell. Journalists and private citizens alike have learned that one post, one
column, or one broadcast can set off years of litigation in a court far from home. Many

       This bill does not ask the State to stop caring about reputation. It asks the State
to stop treating speech as a crime. Honor will still be protected in civil courts, enforcing
proportionate remedies. No Filipino, however, should lose his or her liberty for words
alone. The vigor of our democracy largely lies in the ability of our people to speak truth
to power, to hold public figures to account, and to contribute to meaningful
conversation about matters of public concern.

       The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Libel
 2   Decriminalization and Civil Defamation Act of 2026.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Our Constitution guarantees that no law shall
 5   be passed abridging the freedom of speech, of expression, or of the press. In the same
 6   manner, the State values the dignity of every human person, protecting them against
 7   baseless and unlawful attacks on honor and reputation. Toward these ends, the State
 8   shall abolish criminal liability for defamation and shall instead provide effective and
 9   proportionate civil remedies to those whose reputations are unlawfully injured.
10
11         SEC. 3. Definitions. – As used in this Act:
12
13               (a)       Actual malice refers to the making of a statement with knowledge
14           that it was false or with reckless disregard of whether it was false or not;
15
16              (b)     Computer system shall have the meaning given to it under
17           Republic Act No. 10175;

 2           depiction of fact, whether written, oral or made by any other means, including
 3           through a computer system, which tends to cause the dishonor, discredit or
 4           contempt of an identifiable natural or juridical person or to blacken the memory
 5           of one who is dead;
 6
 7                (d)     Matter of public concern refers to any matter relating to the
 8           conduct of public officials, the use of public funds, the exercise of public
 9           authority, the operation of government or any of its subdivisions or
10           instrumentalities, elections and electoral campaigns, public health and safety,
11           or any similar subject matter of legitimate interest to the community or the
12           general public;
13
14               (e)       Publication refers to the communication of a statement or content
15           to at least one person other than the person to whom it refers; and
16
17               (f)     Public figure refers to a person who, by reason of fame, position
18           or voluntary participation in a public controversy, has invited public attention
19           or comment.

 1          SEC. 7. Civil Action for Defamation. – Any person injured by the publication of
 2   a defamatory statement or content may bring a civil action for damages against the
 3   persons responsible under Section 10 of this Act. This action is in addition to, and shall
 4   not diminish, the remedies available under Articles 19, 20, 21, 26 and 33 of Republic
 5   Act No. 386, otherwise known as the Civil Code of the Philippines.
 6
 7          SEC. 8. Elements. – The plaintiff in a civil action for defamation shall prove that:
 8
 9                   (a) The defendant authored or published a defamatory statement or
10           depiction of fact;
11                   (b) The statement or depiction referred to, or can be reasonably
12           concluded as referring to, the plaintiff;
13                   (c) The defendant acted with actual malice, where the statement or
14           depiction related to a public official, a public figure or a matter of public
15           concern. In all other cases, the defendant acted negligently as to the truth or
16           falsity of the statement or depiction; and
17                   (d) The plaintiff suffered injury as a result of the publication.
18
19          SEC. 9. Privileged Communications. – No liability shall attach to:
20
21                 (a) A private communication made by any person to another in the
22           performance of any legal, moral or social duty, unless made with actual
23           malice;
24
25                   (b) A fair and true report, made in good faith, of any judicial,
26           legislative or other official proceedings which are not of a confidential nature,
27           or of any statement, report or speech delivered or submitted therein, or of any
28           other act performed by public officers in the exercise of their functions; and
29
30                  (c) Any discussion of a matter of public concern, or any criticism of
31           the conduct of public officials or public figures, unless made with actual malice.
32
33          SEC. 10. Persons Responsible. – The author of a defamatory statement or
34   content shall be liable. Unless specifically shown to have taken part in the defamatory
35   post, by reviewing and approving and/or adopting the statement or content as his/her
36   own, the editor, publisher, or producer shall not be held solidarily liable with the author.
37
38         In the same manner, no person shall be liable solely for providing or hosting a
39   venue, platform or facility, nor for storing or recording a statement or content authored
40   by another, unless the former actively and knowingly endorses the said statement or
41   content, or otherwise espouses the same as his/her own.
42
43        SEC. 11. Damages. – The court may award actual, moral, nominal or temperate
44   damages in accordance with the Civil Code of the Philippines: Provided, That such

 2   plaintiff.
 3
 4           SEC 12. Prescription; Single Publication. – A civil action for defamation shall
 5   be filed within one (1) year from the publication of the statement, in accordance with
 6   Article 1147 of the Civil Code of the Philippines. The continued availability of the
 7   statement or content in print, online, or in any archive shall not constitute a new
 8   publication. In relation to an online or social media post, only one cause of action may
 9   arise from a publication regardless of the number of times that a post is shared.
10
11            SEC. 13. Venue. – A civil action for defamation shall be filed with the proper
12   first-level court:
13
14               (a)    Where the plaintiff is a public officer, of the province or city where
15         he or she held office at the time of the publication, or where the defamatory
16         statement or content was printed and first published; or
17
18               (b)    Where the plaintiff is a private individual, of the province or city
19         where he or she actually resided at the time of the publication, or where the
20         defamatory statement or content was printed and first published.
21
22                 Where there are several plaintiffs, the action may be filed in any venue
23         available to any of them. The court where the action is first filed shall acquire
24         jurisdiction to the exclusion of other courts. In all other respects, venue shall be
25         governed by the Rules of Court.
26
27          SEC. 14. Transitory Provisions. – Upon the effectivity of this Act:
28
29                 (a)    All pending criminal actions and preliminary investigations for
30         libel, oral defamation, intriguing against honor, or the cybercrime of libel shall
31         be dismissed, and all outstanding warrants of arrest issued therein shall be
32         recalled;
33
34               (b)    Persons serving sentence for any of the said offenses shall be
35         released, consistent with Article 22 of the Act No. 3815, or the Revised Penal
36         Code;
37
38                 (c)   The plaintiff in any action dismissed under paragraph (a) may file
39         a civil action for defamation under this Act within one (1) year from such
40         dismissal, and the period during which the criminal action or investigation was
41         pending shall not be counted against the prescriptive period; and
42
43               (d)    Judgments for civil damages that have become final and
44         executory before the effectivity of this Act shall not be affected.
