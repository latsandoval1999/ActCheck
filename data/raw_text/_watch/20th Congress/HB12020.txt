ng isang seaman na nasa laot, ang kaniyang e-wallet upang bayaran ang upa. Kulang na ang laman.
Sa listahan ng transaksiyon, sunod-sunod ang bayad sa isang online game: P95, P190, P475, P95S0,
lahat sa loob ng isang hapon. Ang lumang cellphone na ibinigay niya sa siyam na taong gulang na

Si Liza ay tauhang binuo upang ilarawan ang isang suliraning kinakaharap ng maraming

Recent laws do not fill the gap. Republic Act No. 12010 makes financial institutions liable
for restitution, but only for losses from the scams and money-muling offenses it defines.* Republic
Act No. 11765 requires a bank or e-wallet only to suspend fees and charges while it investigates an
unauthorized transaction; it gives no right to a refund against the seller of the game item.’ Republic
Act No. 11967 excludes online media content from its coverage, and its refund remedies concern

defective goods, not purchases that the account holder never made."°

This bill complements measures already before Congress. House Bill No. 11566, approved
by the House on third reading and now in bicameral conference, requires online gaming platforms to
disable purchases and gambling-like mechanisms for children.'' That protection attaches to the child's
own account. It does not reach a purchase charged to the account of a parent when the child uses the
parent's phone, nor does it provide a refund. House Bill No. 11464, the "Bantay-Laro Act" of this
Representation, addresses contact between children and strangers in online games.” This bill does not

touch that subject.

The bill rests on five measures. First, every seller of digital content must require the account
holder's confirmation for each purchase by default, may not keep a hidden purchase window open,
must provide free settings to turn off purchases and set spending limits, and must notify the account
holder of each purchase with its price in pesos. Second, banks, e-wallets and telecommunications
companies must allow the account holder, free of charge, to block or cap payments for digital content.
Third, the account holder may claim a refund of a purchase made by a minor without consent within
ninety days, with the burden on the seller to prove consent; a child's claim to be of age does not bar
the parent's claim. Fourth, the seller must decide the claim within fifteen days, after which the account

holder may go to the consumer arbitration officers of the Department of Trade and Industry or, for

The bill also states what it does not do. It does not prohibit in-game purchases by adults, and
it does not regulate the content or design of games. It applies only to purchases made after its
effectivity. It does not relieve parents of the duty to supervise their children: an account holder who

AN ACT PROVIDING A REFUND REMEDY FOR DIGITAL PURCHASES MADE
BY MINORS WITHOUT THE CONSENT OF THE ACCOUNT HOLDER,
REQUIRING PURCHASE AUTHENTICATION IN ONLINE GAMES,
APPLICATION STORES, AND PAYMENT CHANNELS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION I. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Balik-Bayad Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article II, Sections 12 and 13, Article
XV, Section 1, and Article XVI, Section 9 of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State to:

(a) Protect families from charges for digital purchases made by minors without the consent of

the account holder;

(b) Require that every digital purchase charged to an account holder be confirmed by that

account holder;

(c) Provide a simple, prompt, and inexpensive remedy for the refund of unauthorized minor

purchases; and

(d) Protect the personal data of account holders and minors, consistent with Republic Act No.

10173, otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012.”
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Account holder refers to a natural person of legal age in whose name a store account, game
account, or payment channel is registered and who is liable for the charges made through
it;

(b) Application store refers to a digital platform through which online games and other

applications, and digital content within them, are offered to and paid for by users;

(c) Authentication refers to an act by which a specific digital purchase is confirmed, such as
the entry of a password or personal identification number, a biometric confirmation, or

the entry of a one-time code sent to the account holder;

(d) Digital purchase refers to the purchase, through a payment channel, of virtual currency, a
virtual item, a randomized reward, a subscription, a season or game pass, a paid
application, or other digital content in or for an online game or other application, including

a top-up made through a reseller;

(e) Merchant of record refers to the person that receives payment from the account holder for
a digital purchase, including an application store that processes the payment, a game

publisher or developer that sells directly, and a reseller of game credits or top-ups;
(f) Minor refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age;

(g) Payment channel refers to a credit card, debit card, deposit account, electronic money
account, prepaid load, subscriber billing account, or other means of payment used for a

digital purchase;

(h) Payment service provider refers to a bank, an electronic money issuer, or another
institution supervised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) that provides a payment
channel, and to a public telecommunications entity that allows charges for digital

purchases to be billed to a subscriber's account or deducted from prepaid load; and

(i) Unauthorized minor purchase refers to a digital purchase made by a minor and charged to
an account holder without the account holder’s authentication of, or other express consent

to, that purchase.

SECTION 4. Scope and Construction. — This Act applies to digital purchases charged to

account holders in the Philippines by merchants of record that offer digital content to persons in the
Philippines, whether or not the merchant of record maintains an office in the Philippines, and whether
or not the digital content constitutes online media content under Republic Act No. 11967, otherwise

known as the "Internet Transactions Act of 2023."

This Act shall be construed in harmony with the Civil Code of the Philippines and Republic

Act Nos. 7394, 10173, 11765, 11967, and 12010. Nothing in this Act shall diminish any right or
remedy available to an account holder or a minor under those laws, but no account holder shall recover

more than once for the same purchase.

SECTION 5. Purchase Authentication and Controls. — Every merchant of record shall:

(a) Require authentication for each digital purchase by default;

(b) Not allow further digital purchases without authentication for any period after an
authentication, unless the account holder has expressly elected such a period after a clear

disclosure of its effect. The period shall not exceed fifteen (15) minutes;

(c) Provide, free of charge and in Filipino and English, settings that allow the account holder
to turn off digital purchases, to require authentication for every digital purchase, and to

set daily or monthly spending limits;

(d) Send the account holder, within twenty-four (24) hours, a notice of each digital purchase
stating the merchant of record, the item, the amount charged in Philippine pesos, and the

manner of claiming a refund under this Act;

(e) State, at the time of purchase, the price in Philippine pesos of virtual currency and the peso

equivalent of every item sold for virtual currency; and

(f) Not use a purchase screen designed to cause a digital purchase through a single unintended

act.

SECTION 6. Duties of Payment Service Providers. — Subject to the regulations of the
BSP and of the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) within their respective

jurisdictions, every payment service provider shall:

(a) Allow the account holder, free of charge, to block or to set a limit on payments to

merchants of digital content;

(b) Send the account holder a notice of each payment to a merchant of digital content at the

time it is made;

(c) Receive a refund claim under this Act through its financial consumer protection assistance
mechanism under Republic Act No. 11765, forward it to the merchant of record, and,
pending resolution of the claim, suspend the imposition of interest, fees, and charges on

the disputed amount; and

(d) In the case of a public telecommunications entity, disable the billing of digital purchases
to a subscriber's account or prepaid load upon the subscriber's request, free of charge, and

require the subscriber's confirmation through a one-time code for each such charge.

SECTION 7. Right to Refund. — An account holder may claim from the merchant of record
the refund of an unauthorized minor purchase within ninety (90) days from the date of the purchase
or from receipt of the notice under Section 5(d), whichever is later, but in no case later than one
hundred eighty (180) days from the date of the purchase. The claim shall identify the purchases and
shall be supported by a statement under oath, which may be signed electronically, that the purchases

were made by a minor without the consent of the account holder.

The merchant of record shall grant the refund, subject to the following rules:

(a) Where the merchant of record failed to comply with Section 5 in respect of the purchase,

the refund shal! be granted in full;

(b) Where the merchant of record complied with Section 5 and the purchase was confirmed
with the credentials of the account holder, the refund shall be granted for the first claim
filed by the account holder within any period of twelve (12) months. A further claim
within the same period shall be granted only if the account holder shows that the minor

obtained the credentials without the fault or negligence of the account holder;

(c) A series of digital purchases charged to the same account within twenty-four (24) hours
in an online game or application rated or marketed for minors, or exceeding One thousand
pesos (P 1,000.00) in the aggregate within that period, shall be presumed to have been
made by a minor, unless the merchant of record proves otherwise by substantial evidence.
The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) may adjust the amount once every three (3)

years on the basis of the consumer price index; and

(d) The fact that a minor entered a false age or confirmed that he or she is of legal age shall

not bar the claim of the account holder.

Upon granting the refund, the merchant of record may revoke the digital content and virtual
currency purchased. The account holder shall not be liable for digital content already consumed. No

fee shall be charged for a claim or a refund.

The refund shall be paid to the original payment channel, or by another mode chosen by the
account holder, within fifteen (15) days from the grant. An amount not paid within that period shall

earn legal interest from the date of the grant until full payment.

SECTION 8. Non-Waiver. — Any stipulation in terms of service or any other agreement
that waives or limits a right under this Act, or that requires the account holder to resolve a refund

claim under this Act exclusively outside the Philippines, shall be void.

SECTION 9. Claims Procedure. —- Every merchant of record shall maintain a free refund
channel, accessible within the online game or application and on its website, in Filipino and English.
It shall acknowledge a claim within two (2) days and decide it in writing, stating the reasons, within

fifteen (15) days from its filing. A claim not decided within that period shall be deemed granted.

Where an application store processes the payment for a digital purchase, the application store
shall be the merchant of record and shall be answerable for the refund, without prejudice to its right

of recovery against the publisher or developer.

A merchant of record without an office in the Philippines shall designate a representative in

SECTION 10. Review and Adjudication. — If a claim is denied in whole or in part, or the
refund is not paid within the period under Section 7, the account holder may, within sixty (60) days,
file a complaint with the DTI. The consumer arbitration officers of the DTI under Republic Act No.
7394, otherwise known as "The Consumer Act of the Philippines,” shall mediate and adjudicate the
complaint and decide it within thirty (30) days from its filing. They may order the refund, with legal
interest, and impose the administrative fines under Section 13 of this Act. The decision may be
appealed to the Secretary of Trade and Industry, whose decision shall be subject to review in the
manner provided in Article 166 of Republic Act No. 7394.

Complaints against a payment service provider for violation of Section 6 shall be filed with
the BSP under Republic Act No. 11765 or, in the case of a public telecommunications entity, with the
NTC.

The DTI may use the online dispute resolution platform established under Section t7 of
Republic Act No. 11967. Nothing in this Section shall bar the account holder from filing an action in

court, including a small claims case.

SECTION 11. Non-Retaliation. — No merchant of record shal] suspend, terminate, or
restrict the account of an account holder or of a minor, or deny access to digital content lawfully
purchased, by reason of a refund claim, a complaint, or a payment dispute filed in good faith. The

revocation of digital content under Section 7 shall not constitute retaliation.

SECTION 12. False Claims. — An account holder who knowingly files a false claim under
this Act shall return the refund received, with legal interest, and shall be liable for an administrative
fine of not less than Five thousand pesos (?5,000.00) nor more than Fifty thousand pesos
(P50,000.00), without prejudice to criminal liability under other laws.

SECTION 13. Administrative Penalties. — A merchant of record that violates Section 5,
7, 8,9, or 11 of this Act shall be liable for an administrative fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos
(P50,000.00) nor more than One million pesos (P1,000,000.00) for each violation, and of not more
than Ten thousand pesos (P10,000.00) for each day that a violation continues after notice. In fixing
the fine, the DTI shall consider the gravity of the violation, the number of account holders affected,
and the good faith of the merchant of record. The DTI may also issue compliance orders under Section

14 of Republic Act No. 11967.

Violations of Section 6 by payment service providers shall be sanctioned by the BSP or the
NTC under the laws they administer.

Administrative fines collected under this Act shall accrue to the National Treasury.

SECTION 14. Data Privacy. —- Merchants of record, payment service providers, and
government agencies shall collect and process only the personal data necessary to administer a claim
under this Act, and shall not use such data for advertising, profiling, or any purpose unrelated to the

claim. Nothing in this Section shall limit the jurisdiction of the National Privacy Commission.

SECTION 15. Consumer Information and Reporting. — The DTI, in coordination with
the BSP, the NTC, the Department of Education, and the Department of Social Welfare and
Development, shall inform the public of the purchase settings and refund rights under this Act, in

Filipino, English, and the major local languages.

Every merchant of record shall report to the DTI, every six (6) months, the number and
amount of refund claims received, granted, and denied under this Act. The DTI shall publish an annual
report of these data, without personal data, and shall submit it to Congress.

SECTION 16. Transitory Provision. — Merchants of record and payment service providers
shall comply with Sections 5, 6, and 9 of this Act within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
effectivity of this Act. The right to refund under Section 7 shall apply to digital purchases made after
the effectivity of this Act. Section 7(a) shall apply upon the lapse of the period for compliance under

this Section.

SECTION 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DTI, in consultation with the BSP, the NTC, the National
Privacy Commission, and the Department of Information and Communications Technology, and after
public consultation, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act, including
the forms for the claim and the statement under oath under Section 7. The implementing rules shall
prescribe only administrative and procedural matters and shall not enlarge or restrict the rights and

duties established by this Act.

The absence or delay of the implementing rules and regulations shall not suspend rights and

duties that are sufficiently defined by this Act.

SECTION 18. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of Trade and Industry.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be

included in the annua! General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 19. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared

unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and

effect.
