                              AN ACT
  PROHIBITING THE DIRECT OR INDIRECT RECOVERY OF DISTRIBUTION
      SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES, INCLUDING ANY VALUE-ADDED TAX
 ATTRIBUTABLE THERETO, FROM ELECTRICITY CONSUMERS, AMENDING
FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832, AS AMENDED, AND REPUBLIC
 ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY
           REFORM ACT OF 2001,” AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Electricity is an essential public service that powers homes, schools, hospitals,
businesses, industries, and government institutions. Reliable electricity at a just and
reasonable cost is indispensable to economic development, social welfare, and the
daily life of every Filipino.

        Electricity consumers, however, continue to be charged for “system losses” —
electricity lost before it reaches the end-user. These losses may arise from the
technical characteristics of transmission and distribution networks or from non-
technical causes such as electricity pilferage, illegal connections, defective metering,
inadequate maintenance, and operational inefficiency.

        At its core, this measure asks a simple question: Should consumers bear the
cost of electricity that never reaches them, or should that remain a business risk of the
entities that own and operate the distribution system? This bill adopts the latter policy,
consistent with the principle that financial responsibility should follow operational
control. Consumers cannot design distribution networks, replace aging facilities,
upgrade transformers, maintain meters, prevent electricity theft, or determine the
capital and operational investments of electric utilities. These matters remain within
the responsibility and control of distribution utilities.
       This concern stems from House Resolution No. 976, introduced by
Representatives Robert Nazal and Paolo Henry Marcoleta, which called for a
congressional inquiry by the Energy Regulatory Commission and the Commission on
Audit into Meralco's electricity rates, pass-through charges, and rate methodologies to
evaluate if system-loss charges are necessary and transparent.

       While House Resolution No. 976 offers oversight, this bill provides substantive
reform. It ensures that electricity lost before delivery is no longer automatically billed
to consumers who cannot prevent these losses.

       State-franchised distribution utilities and electric cooperatives are obligated to
maintain efficient systems, modernize infrastructure, and reduce technical and non-
technical losses. Passing these costs to consumers weakens utility accountability for
operational risks.

       Article XII of the 1987 Constitution subjects public utilities to State regulation in
the public interest. Congress may therefore determine which costs may lawfully form
part of regulated electricity rates and which costs must be absorbed as operating
expenses and business risks of the regulated entity.

        This measure prohibits the charging, billing, collection, or recovery of technical
and non-technical system losses from electricity end-users. It treats such losses as
operating expenses and business risks of the concerned electric power industry
participant and withdraws the authority of the Energy Regulatory Commission to
approve their direct or indirect recovery through tariffs, riders, adjustment
mechanisms, surcharges, or other rate components.

      Ang konsumer ay dapat magbayad para sa kuryenteng aktuwal na naihatid sa
kaniya — hindi para sa kuryenteng nawala dahil sa pagnanakaw, kakulangan sa
pagpapanatili, o kahinaan ng sistema. The entities that control the system must bear
responsibility for improving it.

        This measure advances consumer protection, strengthens accountability, and
creates a direct incentive for electric utilities and other industry participants to invest
in efficient, secure, and reliable power systems.

       In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

26           Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “System Loss Charge
27   Abolition Act.”
28
29          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
30   to protect electricity consumers from unreasonable and avoidable charges; promote
31   transparent, just, and reasonable electricity rates; require distribution utilities to
32   operate efficiently and accountably; encourage continuing investment in safe, modern,
33   and reliable distribution systems; and place responsibility for distribution system loss
34   upon the entity that owns, operates, manages, or controls the distribution network.
35
36          Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
37
38          (a) “Distribution system loss” means the difference between the total electrical
39   energy received by a distribution utility at its points of delivery and the total electrical
40   energy delivered and properly metered to end-users and other authorized recipients,
41   including technical and non-technical losses;
42
43          (b) “Technical system loss” means electricity lost because of the physical
44   characteristics, design, condition, operation, or maintenance of distribution lines,
45   transformers, substations, meters, and other distribution facilities;

 2           (c) “Non-technical system loss” means electricity lost because of pilferage,
 3   illegal connections, meter tampering, inaccurate metering, billing or administrative
 4   errors, unregistered consumption, fraud, or other causes not arising solely from the
 5   physical delivery of electricity;
 6
 7          (d) “Distribution utility” or “DU” means any electric cooperative, private
 8   corporation, government-owned utility, local government unit, or other entity
 9   authorized to distribute electricity to end-users;
10
11           (e) “End-user” means any person or entity requiring the supply and delivery of
12   electricity for its own use;
13
14         (f) “Direct or indirect recovery” means the billing, collection, allocation,
15   embedding, capitalization, deferral, reimbursement, reclassification, imputation, or
16   recovery of any amount from an end-user, regardless of the name, rate component,
17   account, formula, methodology, or mechanism used; and
18
19         (g) “Prohibition Date” means the date established under Sec. 15 of this Act upon
20   which the complete prohibition against the recovery of distribution system loss shall
21   commence.
22
23           Sec. 4. Allocation of Distribution-System Risk. — Technical and non-
24   technical distribution system losses shall be considered inherent operational and
25   business risks of the distribution utility that owns, operates, manages, or controls the
26   distribution system in which the losses occurred.
27
28          The physical unavoidability of a portion of technical system loss shall not
29   constitute legal or regulatory authority to recover its cost from an end-user.
30
31          Distribution system loss shall be for the sole account of the distribution utility
32   concerned and shall not constitute a prudent, reasonable, eligible, or recoverable cost
33   for purposes of determining any retail rate, distribution wheeling charge, revenue
34   requirement, rate base, regulatory asset, deferred account, true-up adjustment,
35   surcharge, rider, or other amount payable by an end-user.
36
37           Sec. 5. Prohibition Against Direct or Indirect Recovery. — Beginning on the
38   Prohibition Date, no distribution utility, electric cooperative, retail electricity supplier,
39   local retail electricity supplier, supplier of last resort, billing agent, or other person or
40   entity billing electricity end-users shall directly or indirectly charge, bill, allocate, collect,
41   or otherwise recover any distribution system loss from an end-user.

 5           Sec. 6. Prohibition Against Circumvention and Offset. — No regulated
 6   entity shall recover or offset distribution system loss by:
 7           (a) changing the name or description of the charge;
 8           (b) incorporating or embedding the cost in another rate or billing component;
 9           (c) creating or modifying a rider, surcharge, adjustment mechanism, regulatory
10   asset, deferred account, true-up mechanism, or similar device;
11           (d) increasing another regulated charge for the purpose of recovering,
12   replacing, or offsetting prohibited system-loss revenue;
13           (e) transferring the cost through an affiliate, subsidiary, contractor, supplier, or
14   related entity; or
15           (f) adopting any other arrangement that has the purpose or effect of transferring
16   distribution system loss to end-users.
17
18          In reviewing any rate application filed after the effectivity of this Act, the Energy
19   Regulatory Commission shall determine whether the requested rate or revenue
20   requirement directly or indirectly restores, replaces, or offsets any prohibited system-
21   loss recovery. Any amount determined to have such purpose or effect shall be
22   disallowed.
23
24           Sec. 7. Consequential Treatment of Value-Added Tax. — No value-added
25   tax attributable to any distribution system-loss amount prohibited under this Act shall
26   be billed, collected, allocated, or passed on to an electricity end-user.
27   This section shall not be construed as granting a tax exemption for an otherwise lawful
28   charge. It recognizes that no taxable consumer charge exists with respect to a
29   distribution system-loss amount whose recovery is prohibited by law.
30   The Bureau of Internal Revenue shall issue the necessary conforming rules on
31   invoicing, accounting, tax adjustment, credit, and refund treatment consistent with this
32   Act.

25            Sec. 12. Authorized Distribution-System Modernization Investments. —
26   Nothing in this Act shall prevent a distribution utility from applying for the recovery of
27   prudent and reasonable capital expenditures actually incurred f or the rehabilitation,
28   modernization, safety, reliability, efficiency, automation, digitalization, or expansion of
29   its distribution system.
30
31         No expenditure shall be recoverable unless the ERC determines, after notice
32   and hearing, that it:
33         (a) is necessary and directly related to the provision of distribution service;
34         (b) is technically and economically reasonable;
35         (c) has been procured through a transparent and competitive process, except
36   when another mode is permitted and justified under applicable law;
37         (d) is used and useful in providing service to consumers;
38         (e) does not arise from negligence, mismanagement, regulatory
39   noncompliance, or unjustified delay; and
40         (f) has not been recovered through another rate, subsidy, grant, insurance
41   payment, or other source.
42
43          The cost of electricity already lost within the distribution system shall not be
44   treated as a capital investment or modernization expenditure.

12           Sec. 18. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — It shall be unlawful for any person
13   or entity to:
14           (a) bill, collect, allocate, embed, or recover distribution system loss from an end-
15           user;
16           (b) conceal, rename, or misclassify distribution system loss as another rate or
17           billing component;
18           (c) increase another charge for the purpose of replacing or offsetting prohibited
19           system-loss recovery;
20           (d) bill or collect value-added tax attributable to a prohibited system-loss
21           amount;
22           (e) submit false, incomplete, or misleading system-loss, metering, energy-input,
23           sales, or billing information;
24           (f) obstruct an audit, inspection, investigation, or lawful order of the ERC; or
25           (g) knowingly authorize, assist, tolerate, or conceal any violation of this Act.
26
27           After notice and hearing, the ERC may order refunds, bill credits, disgorgement,
28   cost disallowance, corrective action, suspension or revocation proceedings, cease-
29   and-desist measures, and the fines or penalties authorized under Section 46 of
30   Republic Act No. 9136 and other applicable laws.
31   Each billing cycle containing a prohibited charge may be treated as a separate
32   violation.
