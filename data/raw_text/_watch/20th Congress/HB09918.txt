                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to mandate the installation and maintenance of
closed-circuit television (CCTV) systems in barangay posts and other locations
under the jurisdiction of local government units (LGUs). It seeks to strengthen
public safety, support law enforcement, and improve security at the community
level.

       As the primary units of local governance, barangays bear the
responsibility of maintaining peace and order, and ensuring the safety of their
constituents. Many barangays, however, continue to operate without adequate
surveillance infrastructure, thereby limiting their capacity to monitor public
spaces and respond to incidents and threats to public safety. The installation of
CCTV systems in barangay posts, public markets, and other critical areas will
provide LGUs with an effective tool for crime prevention and community
protection.

       The presence of surveillance systems in high-traffic and incident-prone
areas can deter unlawful activities and provide valuable evidence for
investigations and judicial proceedings. Furthermore, real-time surveillance
allows local disaster risk reduction units to assess situational needs instantly
during natural calamities.

      Barangays serve as the frontline units of governance and play a critical
role in maintaining peace and order within communities. However, many
barangays lack adequate technological resources to effectively monitor public
spaces, respond to emergencies, and assist law enforcement agencies in crime
prevention and investigation. The installation of CCTV systems in key areas such
as barangay posts, public markets, entrances to barangay roads, covered courts,
and evacuation centers will significantly improve surveillance capabilities and
help ensure a safer environment for residents.
      The proposed measure also establishes safeguards governing the access,
use, and disclosure of recorded footage. It limits access to authorized personnel
and requires compliance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012”, to ensure the responsible handling of personal
information and uphold the right to privacy.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT
MANDATING THE INSTALLATION OF CLOSED-CIRCUIT TELEVISION
SYSTEMS IN BARANGAY POSTS AND OTHER SPECIFIC LOCATIONS
UNDER THE JURISDICTION OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS TO
ENHANCE PUBLIC SAFETY AND SECURITY, AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Barangay CCTV
Surveillance Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect life, liberty, and property, and to maintain peace and order through
modern and proactive public safety mechanisms. To this end, the State shall
institutionalize the installation of security monitoring systems in all barangay
posts and strategic public areas to deter crime, support law enforcement, and
promote community safety.

      SEC. 3 Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

         a. Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) refers to an electronic surveillance
      system that transmits video footage to a specific set of monitors for
      security and monitoring purposes;

         b. Security Monitoring System refers to CCTV cameras, recording
      equipment, storage devices, and associated technologies designed to
      observe, record, and manage surveillance footage; and

          c. Barangay Post refers to any physical office, outpost, or satellite
      facility established by a barangay government to carry out its
      administrative and law enforcement functions.

       SEC. 4. Installation of Security Monitoring Systems. — All barangays across
the country are hereby mandated to install and maintain high-quality CCTV
systems in their respective barangay posts and other critical areas under their
jurisdiction, including, but is not limited to, public markets, entrances to
barangay roads, covered courts, and evacuation centers. CCTV units shall be
capable of recording clear, high-definition footage suitable for identification and
investigative purposes, with night vision and weather-resistant features where
necessary. Each area under surveillance must also have visible signage clearly
indicating the presence of CCTV cameras to inform the public and promote
transparency.
​
       SEC. 5. Purposes of Security Monitoring. — The use of CCTV systems under
this Act shall serve the following purposes:

         a.​ Deter criminal activity and unlawful acts;

         b.​ Provide investigative support to law enforcement agencies;

         c.​ Monitor high-traffic public areas and incident-prone zones;

         d.​ Facilitate quick and coordinated response during emergencies; and

         e.​ Strengthen    public   trust       and   accountability   in   community
      governance.
​
      SEC. 6. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts shall be prohibited:

         a.​ Tampering with, disabling, or destroying CCTV equipment;

         b.​ Unauthorized access to, or misuse of, recorded footage;

         c.​ Use of CCTV systems for political, commercial, or personal gain;
      and

         d.​ Filming of private areas such as inside homes or restrooms.

       SEC. 7. Access and Disclosure of Recordings. — Access to CCTV recordings
shall be restricted to authorized personnel and may only be disclosed for:

         a.​ Official investigations by law enforcement agencies;

         b.​ Legal proceedings upon court order; and

         c.​ Emergency response and public safety purposes.



                                            2
       SEC. 8. Data Retention and Storage. — Recorded footage shall be retained
for a minimum of sixty (60) days, unless required for ongoing investigations or
judicial processes. Storage devices must be secured and protected from
tampering, theft, or unauthorized access.

       SEC. 9. Data Protection. — All recordings shall be managed in accordance
with the provisions of Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data
Privacy Act of 2012”, and any applicable rules issued by the National Privacy
Commission.

       SEC. 10. Penalties. — Any person found guilty of violating any provision of
this Act shall be penalized with imprisonment of not less than one (1) year but
not more than three (3) years, and/or a fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos
(Php 50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos (Php
200,000.00), without prejudice to any administrative or criminal liability under
existing laws.

      SEC. 11. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged to the funds of the respective LGUs.
Thereafter, the necessary funds shall be included in the annual budget of the
LGUs. National government agencies may also provide supplemental funding
and grants.

       SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), in coordination with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), National Privacy Commission (NPC), and
other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general
circulation.
