                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The recurring loss of life and massive property destruction caused by fire
remains a critical threat to national safety. In the first four months of 2024 alone,
the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) documented 5,764 fire incidents, resulting
in 120 civilian fatalities and approximately Php 2.74 billion in property damage.

       While the BFP is the primary agency mandated to protect the public from
such disasters, the sheer scale of these losses has forced Local Government
Units (LGUs) to step in. For years, LGUs have been filling the gaps in BFP
resources—funding equipment, facilities, and personnel training—to ensure
their communities are adequately protected.

      Given that LGUs are already deeply invested in fire protection
operations, it is only logical to grant Local Chief Executives (LCEs) a formal role
in the management of these services. This bill proposes to grant LCEs joint
authority in the appointment of fire marshals and limited administrative
disciplinary power over BFP personnel within their jurisdictions.

   By empowering local leaders with these oversight functions, we foster:

      • Greater Accountability: Ensuring fire marshals are responsive to the
        specific needs of their host localities.

       • Operational Efficiency: Streamlining the implementation of local fire
         safety plans and emergency response.
       • Enhanced Coordination: Strengthening the partnership between the
         national BFP and local governments for more effective disaster
         management.

      To achieve this, the proposed measure seeks to amend relevant provisions of
Republic Act No. 6975 (the DILG Act of 1990). By devolving these specific powers,
we ensure that public safety is managed with the urgency and local expertise it requires.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

 4          SEC. 3. Administrative Disciplinary Powers over BFP Personnel. — (a)
 5   City and municipal mayors shall have the power to impose, after due notice and
 6   summary hearings, disciplinary penalties for minor offenses committed by
 7   personnel of the BFP assigned to their respective jurisdictions.
 8          For purposes of this Act, a “minor offense” shall refer to any act or omission
 9   not involving moral turpitude, but affecting the internal discipline of the BFP, and
10   shall include:
11                1. Simple misconduct or negligence;
12                2. Insubordination;
13                3. Frequent absences and tardiness;
14                4. Habitual drunkenness; and
15                5. Gambling, as prohibited by existing laws.
16          The power granted to the city and municipal mayor under this Section to
17   impose disciplinary penalties for minor offenses committed by BFP personnel under
18   their jurisdiction shall be concurrent with the authority of the BFP to discipline their
19   personnel.
20          Penalties imposed as a result of an administrative case shall be without
21   prejudice to other penalties that may be imposed after prosecution under other
22   existing laws, rules and regulations.
