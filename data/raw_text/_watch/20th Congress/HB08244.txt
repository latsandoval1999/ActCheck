13                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The Philippines remains deeply vulnerable to fluctuations in global oil markets. As a
16   country that imports the vast majority of its petroleum requirements, any disruption
17   in international oil supply—whether caused by armed conflict, geopolitical instability,
18   or disruptions in key shipping routes—immediately reverberates through the
19   domestic economy.
20
21   Recent developments in the Middle East have again underscored this vulnerability.
22   Armed hostilities involving the United States, Israel, and Iran have heightened
23   tensions in a region that sits at the heart of global oil production and transport. The
24   threat of disruption in vital maritime corridors, particularly the Strait of Hormuz
25   through which a substantial portion of the world’s petroleum supply transits daily,
26   has already begun to generate volatility in global energy markets. History has shown
27   that even the perception of instability in such regions can trigger rapid increases in
28   crude oil prices.
29
30   For the Philippines, such increases translate directly into higher pump prices for
31   gasoline, diesel, kerosene, and liquefied petroleum gas. These increases do not remain
32   confined to the fuel sector. They ripple outward—raising transportation costs,
33   pushing electricity prices upward, increasing the cost of food production and
34   distribution, and ultimately imposing a heavy burden on Filipino households.
35
36   In times of geopolitical crisis beyond our control, the government must act decisively
37   to mitigate the immediate economic consequences on the Filipino people. One of the
38   most direct tools available to the State is fiscal relief through the temporary
39   suspension of taxes that compound the price of fuel.
40
41   At present, petroleum products are subject not only to excise taxes imposed under
42   Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended by Republic Act No.
43   10963 or the TRAIN Law, but also to the twelve percent (12%) value-added tax
44   imposed on the sale and importation of such products. While these taxes are part of
45   the government’s revenue framework, they also significantly increase the retail price
46   of fuel regardless of global market conditions.
47
48   During periods of extraordinary global oil price shocks, the continued imposition of
49   both excise tax and value-added tax on petroleum products amplifies the burden on




                                               1
 1   consumers and on sectors of the economy that rely heavily on fuel, including
 2   transportation, agriculture, fisheries, and manufacturing.
 3
 4   This measure therefore seeks to establish an emergency mechanism that temporarily
 5   suspends both the excise tax and the value-added tax on petroleum products during
 6   periods of global oil supply shocks. By doing so, the government can immediately
 7   cushion the impact of rising international oil prices and help stabilize domestic fuel
 8   costs during times of international crisis.
 9
10   The bill likewise provides objective mechanisms to ensure that such intervention is
11   applied responsibly. The suspension of taxes is tied to international crude oil price
12   thresholds based on the Brent benchmark, and will automatically cease once global
13   prices stabilize. Safeguards are also introduced to prevent oil companies from
14   capturing windfall profits from the tax suspension without corresponding relief to
15   consumers.
16
17   In addition, the measure establishes the groundwork for a Strategic Petroleum
18   Reserve Program in order to enhance the country’s long-term energy security and
19   reduce the Philippines’ exposure to sudden supply disruptions in the future.
20
21   This proposal is not merely a fiscal measure. It is a recognition of the economic reality
22   faced by a nation whose prosperity and stability remain closely linked to forces
23   beyond its shores. In moments of global uncertainty and conflict, government policy
24   must stand firmly with the Filipino people.
25
26   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
27

13                             AN ACT
14   PROVIDING FOR AN EMERGENCY OIL PRICE STABILIZATION
15   MECHANISM DURING GLOBAL ENERGY SUPPLY SHOCKS,
16   ESTABLISHING AUTOMATIC PRICE-TRIGGERED SUSPENSION OF
17   EXCISE   TAXES    ON  PETROLEUM     PRODUCTS,   PROVIDING
18   SAFEGUARDS AGAINST WINDFALL PROFITS, CREATING A
19   STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE PROGRAM, AND AMENDING FOR
20   THIS PURPOSE SECTION 148 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE
21   CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED
22
23   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representative of the Philippines in
24   congress assembled:
25
26   SECTION 1. Short Title.
27
28   This Act shall be known as the “Emergency Oil Price Stabilization and Energy Security
29   Act.”
30
31   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.
32
33   It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the Filipino people and the
34   national economy from the adverse effects of sudden global oil price shocks arising
35   from geopolitical conflicts, supply disruptions, or other international crises affecting
36   petroleum supply.
37
38   The Philippines remains highly dependent on imported petroleum products. Global
39   geopolitical conflicts, particularly in the Middle East, have historically caused
40   disruptions in oil supply routes and dramatic spikes in international crude oil prices.
41   Armed conflict involving major oil-producing or oil-transit regions—including
42   conflicts involving the United States, Israel, and Iran—poses a serious threat to global
43   energy security. Such disruptions immediately translate into higher pump prices,
44   increased transportation and logistics costs, inflation in food and basic commodities,
45   and significant economic hardship for oil-import dependent countries such as the
46   Philippines.
47
48   It is therefore the policy of the State to adopt responsive fiscal and regulatory
49   mechanisms that can be immediately activated during global oil supply disruptions




                                                3
 1   in order to stabilize domestic fuel prices and protect Filipino consumers, workers,
 2   farmers, fisherfolk, and industries that rely on petroleum products.
 3
 4   SEC. 3. Temporary Suspension of Excise Taxes During Global Oil Supply
 5   Shock.
 6
 7   Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is hereby
 8   supplemented by a mechanism allowing the temporary suspension of excise taxes on
 9   petroleum products during periods of global oil supply disruption.
10
11   Upon the declaration of a Global Oil Supply Shock pursuant to this Act, the excise
12   taxes imposed on the following petroleum products shall be temporarily suspended:
13
14   (a) Diesel fuel oil
15   (b) Unleaded gasoline
16   (c) Kerosene
17   (d) Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG)
18   (e) Fuel oil
19   (f) Aviation fuel and other refined petroleum products
20
21   The suspension of excise taxes shall remain in effect for the duration of the declared
22   global oil supply disruption or until the conditions provided under the sunset clause
23   of this Act have been satisfied.
24
25   SEC. 4. Automatic Price-Trigger Mechanism.
26
27   The temporary suspension of excise taxes under this Act shall be automatically
28   triggered when the international benchmark price of crude oil, based on the Brent
29   crude oil index, reaches or exceeds Eighty United States Dollars (US$80) per barrel
30   for a continuous period of five (5) trading days.
31
32   Upon the occurrence of such trigger, the Department of Finance, in coordination with
33   the Department of Energy and the Bureau of Internal Revenue, shall immediately
34   implement the suspension of excise taxes on petroleum products.
35
36   The Department of Energy shall regularly monitor global oil market conditions and
37   publish weekly reports on Brent crude oil price movements.
38
39
40   SEC. 5. Declaration of Global Oil Supply Shock.
41
42   A Global Oil Supply Shock may be declared when any of the following conditions
43   exist:
44
45   (a) Armed conflict, blockade, or disruption affecting major global oil supply routes,
46   including but not limited to the Strait of Hormuz, the Suez Canal, or other
47   internationally recognized oil transit chokepoints;
48
49   (b) Sustained increases in global crude oil prices caused by geopolitical instability or
50   supply disruptions; or
51



                                                4
 1   (c) Determination by the Department of Energy that international events have caused
 2   a severe disruption in global petroleum supply.
 3
 4   The President of the Philippines, upon recommendation of the Department of Energy
 5   and the Department of Finance, may issue a declaration confirming the existence of
 6   such conditions.
 7
 8   SEC. 6. Sunset Clause.
 9
10   The suspension of excise taxes under this Act shall automatically cease once the
11   international benchmark price of Brent crude oil falls below Sixty-Five United States
12   Dollars (US$65) per barrel for a continuous period of thirty (30) calendar days.
13
14   Upon stabilization of global oil prices under this threshold, the Department of
15   Finance shall issue the appropriate order restoring the applicable excise taxes under
16   Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code.
17
18   Provided, That the President of the Philippines may extend the suspension if
19   geopolitical conditions continue to threaten global oil supply.
20
21   SEC. 7. Windfall Profit Safeguards on Petroleum Companies.
22
23   During the implementation of the excise tax suspension provided under this Act, the
24   Department of Energy shall monitor the retail and wholesale pricing behavior of
25   petroleum companies operating in the Philippines.
26
27   If it is determined that petroleum importers, refiners, distributors, or retailers have
28   realized extraordinary profits resulting from the excise tax suspension without
29   corresponding reductions in retail prices, the Department of Finance may
30   recommend the imposition of a temporary windfall profit tax on such excess earnings.
31
32   The windfall profit tax shall apply only to profits exceeding the historical average
33   profit margins of the affected entities over the preceding three (3) years.
34
35   Revenues collected from such windfall profit taxes shall be allocated exclusively for
36   programs that mitigate the impact of high fuel prices on vulnerable sectors, including
37   public transport operators, farmers, fisherfolk, and low-income households.
38
39   SEC. 8. Strategic Petroleum Reserve Program.
40
41   To strengthen national energy security and reduce the vulnerability of the Philippines
42   to global supply disruptions, the Department of Energy shall establish a Strategic
43   Petroleum Reserve (SPR) Program.
44
45   The Strategic Petroleum Reserve shall consist of government-maintained stockpiles
46   of crude oil and refined petroleum products sufficient to support domestic
47   consumption for a period determined by the Department of Energy but not less than
48   thirty (30) days of national fuel supply.
49
50   The Department of Energy may acquire, store, and maintain petroleum reserves
51   through:



                                               5
 1   (a) Government-owned storage facilities;
 2   (b) Leased storage facilities; or
 3   (c) Agreements with private oil companies for the maintenance of strategic reserves.
 4
 5   The Strategic Petroleum Reserve may be released during periods of severe supply
 6   disruption, national emergencies, or global oil crises affecting domestic fuel supply.
 7
 8   SEC. 9. Monitoring and Reporting.
 9
10   The Department of Energy shall continuously monitor global oil supply conditions
11   and shall submit periodic reports to Congress on:
12
13   (a) international crude oil price movements;
14   (b) supply disruptions affecting major oil transit routes;
15   (c) domestic fuel price trends; and
16   (d) the impact of the measures implemented under this Act.
17
18   SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
19
20   Within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance, in
21   coordination with the Department of Energy and the Bureau of Internal Revenue,
22   shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective
23   implementation of this Act.
24
25   SEC. 11. Congressional Oversight.
26
27   A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on Energy Price Stabilization is hereby
28   created to monitor the implementation of this Act.
29
30   The Committee shall evaluate the effectiveness of the emergency price stabilization
31   mechanisms and submit periodic reports to Congress.
32
33   SEC. 12. Separability Clause.
34
35   If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining
36   provisions shall remain in full force and effect.
37
38   SEC. 13. Repealing Clause.
39
40   All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with this Act
41   are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
42
43   SEC. 14. Effectivity.
44
45   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official Gazette or
46   in a newspaper of general circulation.
