                               AN ACT

          HOLIDAY FOR ALL KASAMBAHAYS IN THE PHILIPPINES



                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

Kasambahay literally means a household companion, our kasama sa bahay whom
generations of Filipino households have relied on for various household chores – cooking,
cleaning, laundry, gardening, and taking care of children, among others. Based on a
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)
survey conducted in October 2019, there are about 1.4 million Filipinos working as
kasambahays.1

Republic Act No. 10361 or the Domestic Workers Act, also more popularly referred to as
Batas Kasambahay, was enacted into law on January 18, 2023. This landmark law which
instituted policies for the promotion and protection of the rights and welfare of
kasambahays came in the heels of the International Labor Organization (ILO) Convention
No. 189 or the Domestic Workers Convention, which the Philippines ratified on
September 5, 2012.

In commemoration of the enactment of the Batas Kasambahay, this bill seeks to declare
a day in honor of our kasambahays and recognize their invaluable contribution to the
well-being of families and communities across the country. The National Kasambahay
Day or Araw ng Kasambahay shall be a non-working day for all kasambahays, but also a
reminder to implementing offices and agencies of the Batas Kasambahay, household
employers, and the general public, to support fair wages and provide safe and healthful
working conditions for an often-marginalized sector, in pursuit of promoting the overall
general welfare of our kasambahays.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1         Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Kasambahay
 2   Day” or “Araw ng Kasambahay”
 3
 4           Sec. 2. Declaration of Policies. – It is hereby declared that the State shall protect
 5   the workers, promote full employment and provide equal opportunities regardless of sex,
 6   race and religious belief, and regulate policies and relations between workers and
 7   employers. The State shall also guarantee the rights of the workers to self-organization,
 8   collection bargaining, security of tenure, and just and humane working conditions. As
 9   stated in the International Labor Organization (ILO) Convention No. 189 or the Decent
10   Work for Domestic Workers Convention which was ratified by the Philippine government
11   in May 2012, it is the responsibility of the State to recognize the significant contributions
12   of domestic workers to the local and global economy, which includes increasing paid job
13   opportunities for women and men with family responsibilities, greater scope for caring
14   for ageing populations, children and persons with disability and among others.
15
16          Sec. 3. Definition of Kasambahay. A kasambahay or domestic worker as defined
17   in Republic Act No. 10361 or Batas Kasambahay refers to any person engaged in domestic
18   work within an employment relationship such as, but not limited to, the following: general
19   house help, nursemaid or “yaya”, cook, gardener, or laundry person, but shall exclude any
20   person who performs domestic work only occasionally or sporadically and not on an
21   occupational basis.
22
23          Sec. 4. National Kasambahay Day or Araw ng Kasambahay. – In recognition of
24   the contribution of domestic workers to Filipino families and society in general, January
25   18 of every year shall be declared as a Special Non-Working Holiday for all kasambahays
26   and shall be known as National Kasambahay Day or Araw ng Kasambahay.

 1          In the event that this day falls on their rest day, the employer shall pay the

 6          Sec. 5. Role of Relevant Government Agencie. – The Department of Labor and
 7   Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the Philippine Information Agency (PIA) shall
 8   lead in the national observance of the National Kasambahay Day or Araw ng Kasambahay.
 9
10         The heads of all government agencies, government-owned and controlled
11   corporations, local government units, and other governmental instrumentalities shall
12   support the DOLE in its activities related to the observance of the National Kasambahay
13   Day or Araw ng Kasambahay.
14
15          Sec. 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
16   in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
