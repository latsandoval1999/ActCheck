                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Constitution mandates that "Public office is a public trust," and
emphasizes that "The State shall maintain honesty and integrity in the public service
and take positive and effective measures against graft and corruption." While Republic
Act No. 1405 or the Bank Secrecy Law, enacted in 1955, and Republic Act No. 6426
or the Foreign Currency Deposit Act of the Philippines, passed in 1974, were originally
designed to encourage bank deposits and discourage private hoarding to stimulate
economic development, the absolute mantle of bank secrecy they provide has evolved
into a significant barrier to transparency. Under the current legal framework, the
exceptions to the secrecy of local deposits are extremely narrow, while foreign
currency accounts are treated with near-absolute confidentiality, often inadvertently
shielding the accumulation of ill-gotten wealth, tax evasion, and money laundering.
As the global community moves toward greater financial transparency, leaving the
Philippines as a solitary outlier in maintaining such rigid bank secrecy, it is imperative
that our domestic laws adapt to prevent the banking system from being utilized as a
sanctuary for the proceeds of financial crimes.

        Guided by the constitutional principle of accountability, this measure seeks to
spearhead the movement toward financial transparency by mandating all government
officials and employees to waive the secrecy of their bank deposits as provided under
these laws. By requiring those in the public service to open their financial records to
scrutiny, the government can more effectively mitigate, if not entirely resolve, the
persistent challenges of graft and corruption that impede national progress. This
proposal serves as a litmus test for integrity: any public servant who operates with
honesty and has nothing to conceal should welcome this transition toward a culture
of openness. Ultimately, this bill reinforces the truth that transparency is the most

 1       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State
 2   for all public officials and employees to be open and transparent in all their financial
 3   transactions and dealings.

10       SEC. 2. Submission of Written Permission or Waiver. - All public officials and
11   employees as defined under paragraph (b) in relation to paragraph (a), Section 3 of 6
12   Republic Act No. 6713, except those who serve in an honorary capacity, shall submit to
13   the Office of the Ombudsman a written permission or waiver in favor of the Ombudsman
14   to examine, inquire and look into all their deposits, including foreign currency deposits,
15   in banks and other financial institutions, both in and outside the Philippines, including
16   investment bonds issued by the government of the Philippines, its political subdivisions
17   and instrumentalities.

20   days from the date of their assumption of office: Provided, however, That public officials

 4      Sec. 3. Failure or Refusal to Submit Written Permission or Waiver. - Any
 5   public official or employee who fails or refuses to submit such written permission or
 6   waiver as mandated by this Act shall not be allowed to enter upon, or continue exercising
 7   the functions of, his or her office.
 8
 9       Sec. 4. Limitation on Use. –The Civil Service Commission (CSC) may obtain and
10   use the written permission or waiver, or any information or data secured by virtue
11   thereof, strictly in the exercise of its constitutional mandate to enforce civil service laws
12   and to investigate administrative complaints against public officers and employees:
13   Provided, however, That such written permission, waiver, information, or data shall be
14   used exclusively for the investigation of a duly verified complaint or for the institution
15   and prosecution of the appropriate administrative case before the proper body:
16   Provided, further, That when the acts complained of fall within the jurisdiction of the
17   Office of the Ombudsman pursuant to the Constitution and existing laws, the CSC shall
18   promptly transmit the records and evidence obtained to the Office of the Ombudsman
19   for appropriate action: Provided, finally, That the written permission or waiver shall not
20   be used for any purpose other than those expressly authorized herein.
21
22      Sec. 5. Lead Agency. - The CSC shall have the primary responsibility for the
23   implementation of this Act: Provided, however, That in the formulation and drafting of
24   the written permission or waiver contemplated herein, the CSC shall consult and
25   coordinate with the Department of Justice (DOJ), the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP),
26   and other relevant agencies to ensure proper legal and regulatory compliance.
27
28      Sec. 6. Separability Clause. If any of the sections or provisions of this Act is held
29   invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid.
30
31       Sec. 7. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, letters
32   of instructions, proclamations, and administrative regulations that are inconsistent with
33   the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
34
35       Sec. 8. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect immediately following the completion
36   of its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
