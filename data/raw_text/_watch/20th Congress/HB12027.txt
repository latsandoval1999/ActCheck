AN ACT RECOGNIZING BARANGAY SERVICE POINT OFFICERS
(BSPOs) AS COMMUNITY HEALTH WORKERS (CHWs) AND
GRANTING THE SAME BENEFITS AND PRIVILEGES RECEIVED BY
BARANGAY HEALTH WORKERS (BHWs) FOR THEIR ESSENTIAL
ROLE IN POPULATION AND DEVELOPMENT

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Barangay Service Point Officers (BSPOs) play an essential role in the
implementation of government population and development programs at. the
grassroots level. As community-based volunteers, they assist in household
profiling, distribution of family planning commodities, referral of clients to
appropriate health services, and promotion of responsible parenthood. Through
these functions, they help ensure that vital health and population services reach
individuals and families, particularly in underserved communities.

Despite the importance of their contributions, BSPOs remain without the
statutory recognition and benefits accorded to accredited Barangay Health
Workers (BHWs) under Republic Act No. 7883, otherwise known as the
"Barangay Health Workers' Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995." While both
perform essential community-based health-related functions, BSPOs do not enjoy
the same statutory benefits and incentives, including hazard and subsistence
allowances, training opportunities, and other privileges granted to accredited
BHWs.

This disparity underscores the need to recognize the valuable contributions
of BSPOs and provide them with appropriate institutional support and protection.
Their responsibilities expose them to various risks and require sustained
engagement with communities in the delivery of essential population and health

AN ACT RECOGNIZING BARANGAY SERVICE POINT OFFICERS AS
COMMUNITY HEALTH WORKERS, GRANTING THEM THE SAME
BENEFITS AND INCENTIVES ACCORDED TO ACCREDITED
BARANGAY HEALTH WORKERS UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7883,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "BARANGAY HEALTH WORKERS'
BENEFITS AND INCENTIVES ACT OF 1995," AND PROVIDING
FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Service
Point Officers’ Benefits and Incentives Act of 2026.”

SEC. 2. Statement of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of every Filipino and ensure equitable
access to responsive, accessible, and quality health services, particularly at the
community and grassroots levels.

Towards this end, the State recognizes the indispensable contribution of
community-based volunteers in the effective implementation of population,
reproductive health, family planning, and responsible parenthood programs. It
likewise recognizes that Barangay Service Point Officers (BSPOs), by virtue of
their direct engagement with households and communities, perform essential
functions that complement the delivery of primary health care services.

The State shall therefore recognize BSPOs as Community Health Workers
(CHWs) and ensure that they receive appropriate recognition, benefits,
incentives, protection, training, and support commensurate with the nature and

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

(a) Barangay Service Point Officer (BSPO) refers to a community-based
volunteer duly designated or recognized by the concerned local
government unit or barangay to assist in the implementation of population
and development programs, including family planning, reproductive
health, responsible parenthood, and other related community-based
initiatives;

(b) Barangay Health Worker refers to a person who has undergone training
programs under any accredited government and non-government
organization and who voluntarily renders primary health care services in
the community after having been accredited to function as such by the local
health board in accordance with the guidelines promulgated by the
Department of Health (DOH).

(c) Community Health Worker (CHW) refers to a trained community-based
volunteer who provides or facilitates access to basic health-related
information, services, referrals, and interventions within the community,
in coordination with the appropriate local health authorities; ,

(d) Accreditation refers to the official recognition granted by the Local Health
Board to a BSPO who has satisfied the qualifications and requirements
prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the
Commission on Population and Development (CPD); and

(e) Local Health Board refers to the local health board established pursuant
to the Local Government Code of 1991 or Republic Act No. 7160,
otherwise known as the "Local Government Code of 1991."

SEC. 4. Recognition of BSPOs as Community Health Workers. — All duly
designated and accredited BSPOs shall be recognized as Community Health
Workers for purposes of implementing government population and development
programs and delivering community-based health-related services.

In recognition of their functions and contributions, accredited BSPOs shall be
accorded the same benefits, incentives, privileges, and protection granted to
accredited Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) under Republic Act No. 7883,

SEC. 5. Functions and Responsibilities of BSPOs. — Accredited BSPOs shall
perform the following functions, in coordination with the concerned barangay
officials, local health authorities, and other relevant government agencies:

(a) Assist in the implementation of government population and development
p g pop
programs, including family planning, reproductive health, and responsible
parenthood initiatives;

(b) Conduct household profiling, community mapping, and data gathering
activities relevant to population and development programs, subject to
applicable laws on data privacy and confidentiality;

(c) Disseminate accurate information and provide basic education on family
planning, reproductive health, responsible parenthood, and available
government health services;

(d) Assist in the distribution of family planning commodities and other
authorized health-related materials in accordance with the guidelines of the
DOH and CPD;

(e) Refer individuals and families to appropriate health facilities and service
providers for family planning, reproductive health, maternal health, and
other related services;

(f) Assist in monitoring and evaluating the implementation of population and
development programs at the barangay level;

(g)Promote community participation and mobilization in support of
government health and population programs;

(h) Maintain appropriate records and submit reports on activities undertaken,
subject to existing government reporting and data privacy standards; and

(i) Perform such other functions as may be prescribed by the DOH and CPD,
consistent with their training, qualifications, and the objectives of this Act.

Nothing in this Act shall authorize BSPOs to perform medical procedures,
prescribe medicines, or undertake professional health services beyond the scope

SEC. 6. Accreditation and Registration. — To qualify for the benefits and
incentives provided under this Act, a BSPO shall:

(a)Be duly designated or recognized by the concerned barangay or local
government unit;

(b) Have undergone the necessary training programs conducted or accredited
by the DOH, CPD, or other duly recognized government or non-
government organizations;

(c) Be accredited by the Local Health Board in accordance with the guidelines
promulgated by the DOH, in coordination with the CPD; and

(d) Be registered with the Local Health Board of the city or municipality where
the BSPO renders service.

The Local Health Board shall furnish the DOH and CPD with copies of the
registry of accredited BSPOs. The DOH, in coordination with the CPD, shall
maintain an updated national registry of accredited BSPOs and issue appropriate
proof of accreditation.

The accreditation of BSPOs shall be subject to periodic review and renewal in
accordance with the guidelines promulgated by the DOH and CPD.

SEC. 7. Benefits and Incentives. — All accredited BSPOs who are actively and
regularly performing their duties shall be entitled to the same benefits and
incentives granted to accredited BHWs under Section 6 of Republic Act No.
7883:

(a)Hazard Allowance.— Volunteer barangay health workers in rural and
urban areas, exposed to situations, conditions, or factors in the work
environment or place where foreseeable but unavoidable danger or risks
exist which adversely endanger their health or life and/or increase the risk
of producing adverse effect on their person in the exercise of their duties,
to be validated by the proper authorities, shall be entitled to hazard
allowance in an amount to be determined by the local health board and the
local peace and order council of the local government unit concerned.

(b)Subsistence Allowance.— Barangay health workers who rendered service

within the premises of isolated barangay health stations in order to make
their services within the premises available at any and all times, shall be
entitled to subsistence allowance equivalent to the meals they take in the

(c) Training, Education and Career Enrichment Programs.— The DOH,

in accordance with the Department of Education, Culture and Sports and
other concerned government agencies and non-government organizations,
shall provide opportunities for the following:

1) educational programs which shall recognize years of primary health
care service as credits to higher education in institutions with stepladder
curricula that will entitle barangay health workers to upgrade their skills
and knowledge for community work or to pursue further training as
midwives, pharmacists, nurses or doctors;

2) continuing education, study and exposure tours, training, grants, field’
immersion, scholarships, etc.;

3) scholarship benefits in the form of tuition fees in state colleges, to be
granted to one child of every barangay health worker who will not be
able to take advantage of the above programs; and

4) special training programs such as those on traditional medicine, disaster
preparedness and other programs that address emergent community
health problems and issues.

(d) Civil Service Eligibility A second grade eligibility shall be granted to

barangay health workers who have rendered five (5) years continuous
service as such: provided, that should the barangay health worker become
a regular employee of the government, the total number of years served as
barangay health worker shall be credited to his/her service in computing
retirement benefits.

(e) Free Legal Services.— Legal representation and consultation services for

barangay health workers shall be immediately provided by the Public
Attorneys Office in cases of coercion, interference, and in other civil and
criminal cases filed by or against barangay health workers arising out of or
in connection with the performance of their duties as such.

SEC. 8. Review and Approval of Benefits. — Every benefit or incentive granted
to accredited BSPOs that requires the expenditure of local government funds shall
be subject to review and approval by the Local Health Board to ensure that only
qualified and deserving BSPOs receive the same.

The concerned local government unit shall ensure that the benefits and incentives
are granted in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws,
rules, and regulations.

SEC. 9. Role of Government Agencies. — The DOH, in coordination with the
CPD, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other
concerned government agencies, shall oversee the implementation of this Act.
For this purpose:

(a) The DOH shall establish the accreditation standards, training requirements,
and guidelines for the recognition of BSPOs as CHWs;

(b)The CPD shall provide technical assistance and support in the
implementation of population and development programs and in the
development of training modules for BSPOs;

(c) The DILG shall assist local government units in the implementation of this
Act and ensure the integration of BSPOs into relevant local health and
development programs; and

(d)Local government units shall facilitate the accreditation, registration,
training, and provision of benefits and incentives to qualified BSPOs,
subject to applicable laws and the availability of funds.

of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and
other concerned national government agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be

SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the
CPD, DILG, Department of Budget and Management, Civil Service Commission,
Public Attorney's Office, and other concerned government agencies, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation
of this Act.

SEC. 12. Non-Creation of Employer-Employee Relationship. — The
recognition of BSPOs as Community Health Workers and the grant of benefits
and incentives under this Act shall not, by themselves, create an employer-
employee relationship between the BSPOs and the national government or any-
local government unit, nor shall such recognition be construed as an automatic
appointment to any position in the government service.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this

Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
