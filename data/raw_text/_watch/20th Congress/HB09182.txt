                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippine creative industries have emerged as a significant driver of
economic growth, cultural development, and innovation. Recognizing its strategic
importance, the State enacted Republic Act No. 11904 to establish a comprehensive
framework for the promotion and development of the country’s creative sectors
through the creation of the Philippine Creative Industries Development Council
(PCIDC).

       As the creative economy continues to evolve, there is a growing need to ensure
that the composition of the Council remains responsive, representative, and aligned
with the realities of the industry. While the existing law already provides
representation from several creative domains, certain sectors and institutional
stakeholders that play critical roles in the development, promotion, and global
competitiveness of Philippine creative industries remain underrepresented.

       This proposed measure seeks to strengthen the Philippine Creative Industries
Development Council by expanding its composition to include the Department of
Foreign Affairs (DFA) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA) as ex officio members, as well as a dedicated private sector representative
from the music industry. These additions recognize the increasingly global and skills-
driven nature of the creative economy.

      The inclusion of the DFA acknowledges the growing importance of cultural
diplomacy, international market access, and global promotion of Filipino creative
content and talent. As Philippine creative works increasingly reach international
audiences, stronger coordination with the country’s foreign service institutions
becomes essential in supporting exports, international collaborations, and cultural
exchange initiatives.

       Likewise, the inclusion of TESDA strengthens the linkage between creative
industries and technical-vocational education, particularly as demand grows for
specialized creative and digital skills across sectors such as design, animation,
audiovisual production, live events, and digital media. Expanding skills development
pathways is crucial to ensuring that the country’s creative workforce remains
competitive and responsive to industry needs.

        The addition of a dedicated music industry representative also addresses a
significant gap in sectoral representation. The music industry is a major component of
the Philippine creative economy, contributing not only to cultural expression but also
to employment, tourism, live entertainment, and digital content generation. Ensuring
its direct representation in the Council enables more responsive and inclusive
policymaking.

        By broadening representation within the PCIDC, this measure promotes more
comprehensive coordination among government agencies and industry stakeholders,
resulting in policies that are more reflective of the diverse and evolving needs of the
Philippine creative sector. It strengthens the institutional capacity of the Council to
fulfill its mandate under Republic Act No. 11904 and reinforces the State’s
commitment to the sustainable growth and global competitiveness of Filipino
creativity.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT EXPANDING THE COMPOSITION OF THE PHILIPPINE CREATIVE
    INDUSTRIES DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL, AMENDING FOR THE
    PURPOSE SECTION 4 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11904, OTHERWISE
    KNOWN    AS   THE   “PHILIPPINE  CREATIVE    INDUSTRIES
    DEVELOPMENT ACT”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Section 4 of Republic Act No. 11904 is hereby amended to read as
follows:
      “Section 4. Creation of the Philippine Creative Industries Development Council.
      — The Philippine Creative Industries Development Council, herein
      referred to as the Council, is hereby created to spearhead the development
      and promotion of the creative industries of the Philippines.
      The Council shall be composed of nineteen (19) TWENTY-TWO (22)
      members: ten (10) TWELVE (12) ex officio members and nine (9) TEN (10)
      regular members from the private sector.

          (a) The ten (10) TWELVE (12) members who shall be ex officio
              members of the Council are:
                 (1) The Secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry
                     (DTI), as Chairperson;
                 (2) The Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd);
                 (3) The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology
                     (DOST);
                 (4) The Secretary of the National Economic and Development
                     Authority (NEDA);
                 (5) The Secretary of the Department of Tourism (DOT);
                 (6) The Secretary of the Department of Information and
                     Communications Technology (DICT);
                 (7) The Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local
                     Government (DILG);
                 (8) The Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education
                     (CHED);
                 (9) The Chairman of the National Commission for Culture and
                     the Arts (NCCA); and
                 (10)       The Director General of the Intellectual Property
                     Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL);
                 (11)       THE SECRETARY OF THE DEPARTMENT OF
                     FOREIGN AFFAIRS (DFA); AND
                 (12)       THE DIRECTOR GENERAL OF THE TECHNICAL
                     EDUCATION           AND       SKILLS  DEVELOPMENT
                     AUTHORITY (TESDA).

      The ex officio members of the Council may designate their respective next-
      in-rank officials as permanent alternates whose acts are considered as the
      acts of their principals.

      (b) The nine (9) TEN (10) regular members of the Council are the
      following:
          (1) Private sector representative of the Audiovisual Media Domain;
          (2) Private sector representative of the Digital Interactive Media
              Domain;
          (3) Private sector representative of the Creative Services Domain;
          (4) Private sector representative of the Design Domain;
          (5) Private sector representative of the Publishing and Printed Media
              Domain;
          (6) Private sector representative of the Performing Arts Domain;
          (7) Private sector representative of the Visual Arts Domain;
          (8) Private sector representative of the Traditional Cultural
              Expressions Domain; and
          (9) Private sector representative of the Cultural Sites Domain; AND
          (10)       PRIVATE SECTOR REPRESENTATIVE OF THE MUSIC
              INDUSTRY.”

        SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DTI Secretary shall, in consultation with other concerned
government agencies, the industry representatives and nongovernment
organizations, shall amend or supplement the implementing rules and regulations of
Republic Act No. 11904 to carry out the provisions of this Act.

      SEC. 3. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 5. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
