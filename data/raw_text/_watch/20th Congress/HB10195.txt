                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The increasing reliance of the Department of Health (DOH) and the
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) on third-party service
providers for critical health system functions underscores the need for stronger
governance, transparency, and accountability in health service contracting.

       This bill seeks to reform and institutionalize a comprehensive framework
for health service procurement and contracting to ensure that public funds are
used efficiently, transparently, and in accordance with the principles of good
governance and universal health care. It strengthens procurement safeguards by
mandating competitive bidding, performance-based contracting, conflict-of-
interest controls, and risk-based oversight mechanisms.

       The measure establishes a Health Procurement and Contract Oversight
Committee to provide independent review of high-value contracts and introduces
a centralized digital transparency and analytics platform to ensure full public
visibility of procurement activities and contract performance.
          It further professionalizes procurement practice through mandatory
  certification and training programs and strengthens whistleblower protections and
  anti-corruption safeguards to deter fraud, collusion, and misuse of public funds.

         By aligning procurement processes with modern governance standards and
  data-driven oversight systems, this Act aims to significantly improve the
  integrity, efficiency, and effectiveness of health service delivery in the
  Philippines.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Health Service
Contracting Reform Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
ensure that all public health funds are managed with the highest standards of
integrity, transparency, efficiency, and accountability, consistent with the
principles of good governance and universal health care.


The State recognizes that contracting third-party providers is a necessary
component of modern health service delivery under the Department of Health
(DOH) and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth). However,
such contracting must be strictly regulated to prevent fraud, corruption,
overpricing, service failure, and conflicts of interest, and to ensure that all public
expenditures generate measurable health outcomes and value for money.


This Act shall be construed in harmony with Republic Act No. 9184
(Government Procurement Reform Act), Republic Act No. 11223 (Universal
Health Care Act), Republic Act No. 3019 (Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act),
Republic Act No. 9485 (Anti-Red Tape Act), and other applicable laws, rules,
and regulations.


SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all procurement, outsourcing, and
service contracting arrangements entered into by:
   a) Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) – Including IT
      systems, claims processing, actuarial services, auditing, data management,
      member services, fraud detection systems, and consulting services;
   b) Department of Health (DOH) and Attached Agencies – Including logistics,
      procurement and supply chain services, diagnostics, laboratory services,
      vaccination campaigns, telemedicine platforms, health information
      systems, facility     maintenance,     emergency response support, and
      outsourced public health programs;
   c) Government Hospitals and DOH- or LGU-Managed Health Facilities –
      Where contracting for services is allowed under applicable laws;
   d) Other Health-Related Government Programs – As may be determined by
      the DOH in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management
      (DBM).

SEC. 4. Governing Principles of Health Service Contracting. – All covered
contracts shall be governed by the following binding principles:
   a) Transparency and Open Contracting – All procurement stages shall be
      publicly accessible, except for legally protected confidential information.
   b) Competitive Neutrality – Competitive bidding shall be the default
      procurement method, with non-competitive modes strictly limited and
      fully justified under Republic Act No. 9184 and its IRR.
   c) Value-for-Money and Outcome Orientation – Contracts shall prioritize
      cost-effectiveness, efficiency, and measurable health outcomes, not merely
      service inputs.
   d) Performance-Based Payment – Payments shall be tied to verified
      deliverables, milestones, and service quality indicators.
   e) Integrity and Conflict-of-Interest Safeguards – Full disclosure of
      ownership, affiliations, and potential conflicts of interest shall be
      mandatory for all bidders and decision-makers.
   f) Risk-Based Procurement Oversight – High-value and high-risk contracts
      shall be subject to enhanced scrutiny and independent review.


SEC. 5. Procurement Integrity and Control Mechanisms. – All covered
agencies shall implement the following safeguards:
   a) Standardized Bidding Requirements – Uniform, publicly disclosed
      technical, financial, and legal eligibility standards.
   b) Performance Security Requirements – Mandatory performance bonds,
      warranties, or equivalent guarantees for all major contracts.
   c) Independent Technical Evaluation – Creation of independent technical
      evaluation panels for high-value or complex contracts.
   d) Blacklisting, Debarment, and Suspension – Entities engaged in fraud,
      collusion, gross negligence, or repeated non-performance shall be

     blacklisted for a minimum of five (5) years, without prejudice to longer
     sanctions under existing laws.
  e) Contract Lifecycle Monitoring – Continuous monitoring of procurement
     from planning to completion, including post-performance evaluation.
  f) Centralized Contract Registry – All health service contracts shall be
     recorded in a national registry for audit, oversight, and public transparency.


SEC. 6. Creation of the Health Procurement and Contract Oversight
Committee (HPCOC). – There is hereby created a Health Procurement and
Contract Oversight Committee (HPCOC), attached to the DOH, composed of:
  1. Secretary of Health – Chairperson
  2. President and CEO of PhilHealth – Co-Chairperson
  3. Representative of the Commission on Audit (COA)
  4. Representative of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM)
  5. Representative of the Civil Service Commission (CSC)
  6. Representative of the Department of Finance (DOF)
  7. Representative from a duly accredited civil society organization (CSO) in
     the health sector


Functions of the HPCOC:
  a) Approve, review, or disapprove high-value or high-risk contracts
     exceeding thresholds defined in the IRR;
  b) Conduct periodic performance, financial, and compliance audits of major
     contracts;
  c) Investigate procurement anomalies, complaints, and red flags;
  d) Recommend contract suspension, termination, blacklisting, or policy
     reforms;

   f) Recommend improvements to procurement policy and digital systems.
SEC. 7. Digital Transparency and Open Contracting System. – The DOH and
PhilHealth shall establish a fully integrated National Health Procurement
Transparency and Analytics Platform, which shall include:
   a) Publication of all procurement documents including bid notices, terms of
      reference, evaluation results, and awarded contracts;
   b) Real-time contract implementation tracking, including milestones and
      disbursements;
   c) Public procurement dashboards with analytics on pricing trends, supplier
      performance, and risk indicators;
   d) Disclosure of beneficial ownership of winning bidders;
   e) Public reporting channels for complaints, anomalies, and procurement red
      flags.


SEC. 8. Whistleblower Protection and Reporting Mechanisms. – Any person
reporting procurement irregularities in good faith shall be protected from
retaliation, harassment, dismissal, or administrative reprisal.


The DOH, PhilHealth, and COA shall establish secure and anonymous reporting
channels, including digital whistleblowing systems.


Protection shall be consistent with Republic Act No. 6713, Republic Act No.
9485, and other applicable laws.


SEC. 9. Professionalization and Capacity Building. – The Civil Service
Commission (CSC), Commission on Audit (COA), Department of Health

                                                                             5
(DOH), and Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall institutionalize
a national certification and training program for procurement officials covering:
   a) Government procurement law and ethics;
   b) Health-sector contracting risks and safeguards;
   c) Performance-based contracting design and evaluation;
   d) Fraud detection and risk management;
   e) Digital procurement systems and data transparency tools.


No procurement officer handling high-value health contracts shall serve without
completion of such certification.


SEC. 10. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are prohibited:
   a) Circumvention of competitive bidding requirements without lawful
      justification;
   b) Bid rigging, collusion, or market allocation among bidders or officials;
   c) Acceptance or solicitation of bribes, kickbacks, or any undue benefit;
   d) Falsification, manipulation, or suppression of procurement or performance
      data;
   e) Approval of substandard, non-delivered, or ghost services;
   f) Undisclosed conflict of interest in procurement participation or decision-
      making;
   g) Obstruction of audits, investigations, or whistleblower disclosures.


SEC. 11. Penalties. – Any public official, employee, or private entity found
liable under this Act shall be subject to:
   a) Administrative penalties including suspension, dismissal, and perpetual
      disqualification from public office or government contracting;
   b) Civil liability for restitution, damages, and recovery of public funds;

   c) Criminal liability under Republic Act No. 3019, the Revised Penal Code,
      and other applicable laws;
   d) Blacklisting and contract termination, without prejudice to legal action.
SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act under the
budgets of the DOH, PhilHealth, and other covered agencies.


SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, PhilHealth, DBM, CSC, COA, and DOF shall
jointly promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).


SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
