 6                                    EXPL ANATORY NOTE
 7
 8        Rabies remains one of the most fatal yet entirely preventable infectious diseases in the
 9 Philippines. Despite decades of public health interventions under Republic Act No. 9482,
10 otherwise known as the Anti-Rabies Act of 2007, and the efforts of the Department of Health's
11 National Rabies Prevention and Control Program (NRPCP), the Philippines continues to record
12 hundreds of human rabies deaths annually; the majority of which are children below fifteen (15)
13 years of age. Rabies is almost invariably fatal once clinical symptoms manifest. Timely access to
14 proper wound care, post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP), and, where indicated, rabies
15 immunoglobulin (RJG) is not merely important but life-saving and time-critical.
16
17         A persistent structural gap remains, i.e., animal Bite Centers (ABCs) are concentrated in
18 provincial and district hospitals, leaving barangay-level and municipal-level populations,
19 particularly those in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs) and 4th to 6th
20 class municipalities, without ready access to standardized rabies exposure management. The
21 consequence is delayed treatment, prohibitive out-of-pocket expenditures, and preventable
22 deaths.
23
24        Rural Health Units (RHUs), as the primary government frontline of healthcare delivery in
25 every municipality and city, are uniquely positioned to close this gap. Already embedded within
26 communities, staffed with trained health personnel, and integrated into local health systems
27 under Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code), RHUs serve as the de facto first point
28 of contact for health emergencies in underserved areas.
29
30        This measure institutionalizes RHU-based Animal Bite Centers nationwide to ensure that
31 every municipality and city, regardless of income classification, geographic accessibility, or
32 distance from a hospital, is equipped to deliver immediate and standardized rabies exposure

58          SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Rural Health Unit Animal Bite
59   Center Act of 2026.”
60
61           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
62   and promote the right to health of all Filipinos by ensuring timely, accessible, equitable, and
63   affordable post-exposure prophylaxis for individuals bitten or otherwise exposed to potentially
64   rabid animals. Consistent with Republic Act No. 9482 (Anti-Rabies Act of 2007) and Republic
65   Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care Act), the State shall close the gap in the geographic
66   distribution of Animal Bite Centers by expanding their availability through the Rural Health Unit
67   network. The State likewise upholds a One Health approach, recognizing the interdependence of
68   human health, animal health, and environmental factors in the prevention and control of rabies, in
69   coordination with the international commitment to eliminate dog-mediated human rabies by 2030.
70
71      (a) "Animal Bite Center" or "ABC" refers to a health facility or designated unit within a health
72          facility duly certified by the Department of Health to provide comprehensive rabies
73          exposure management services, including wound cleansing, post-exposure prophylaxis,
74          and rabies immunoglobulin administration, in accordance with national standards.
75      (b) "Anti-Rabies Vaccine" refers to any WHO-prequalified cell culture- or embryonated egg-
76          based rabies vaccine registered with the Food and Drug Administration and approved for
77          use in post-exposure prophylaxis.
78      (c) "Category of Exposure" refers to the World Health Organization's classification of animal
79          bite exposures into Category I (no exposure), Category II (minor exposure), and Category
80          III (severe exposure), which determines the required course of post-exposure prophylaxis.

 81      (d) "Cold Chain Management" refers to the system of handling, storing, and transporting
 82          vaccines within required temperature ranges to maintain their efficacy from manufacture
 83          to administration.
 84      (e) "Department" refers to the Department of Health (DOH).
 85      (f) "Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Area" or "GIDA" refers to a community
 86          physically and socio-economically separated from mainstream society as defined under
 87          DOH Administrative Order No. 2012-0012 and Republic Act No. 11223.
 88      (g) "Local Government Unit" or "LGU" refers to a province, city, municipality, or barangay
 89          as defined under Republic Act No. 7160.
 90      (h) "One Health" refers to the collaborative, multisectoral, and transdisciplinary approach —
 91          working at the local, regional, national, and global levels — recognizing the
 92          interconnection between humans, animals, plants, and their shared environment.
 93      (i) "Post-Exposure Prophylaxis" or "PEP" refers to the comprehensive management of a
 94          person exposed to a potentially rabid animal, which includes wound treatment, anti-rabies
 95          vaccination, and, where indicated, administration of rabies immunoglobulin.
 96      (j) "Rabies Immunoglobulin" or "RIG" refers to either human (HRIG) or equine (ERIG) rabies
 97          immunoglobulin administered as part of PEP for Category III exposures, to provide
 98          immediate passive immunity.
 99      (k) "Rural Health Unit" or "RHU" refers to the primary healthcare facility established and
100          operated by a municipality or city government under Republic Act No. 7160, staffed by a
101          municipal or city health officer.
102      (l) "Suspect Rabid Animal" refers to any animal displaying behavior consistent with rabies
103          infection or any animal that bites a person without provocation, whether or not the animal
104          is subsequently confirmed to be rabid.

109   Center shall provide the following services, in accordance with standards prescribed by the

131          SECTION 5. Phased Implementation. The establishment of Animal Bite Centers in all

137          classified as GIDA but without an existing DOH-certified Animal Bite Center within a ten
138          (10)-kilometer radius, or without accessible transport linkages to an existing ABC, shall
139          establish operational ABCs.
140      (c) Phase III; within three [3] years from effectivity: All remaining RHUs nationwide,
141          including those in municipalities and cities classified as Geographically Isolated and
142          Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs), as well as those located in provinces with a rabies
143          incidence rate above the national average, shall establish and operate compliant Animal

153            SECTION 6. Standards and Certification. All RHU-based Animal Bite Centers shall
154   comply with the standards, protocols, and certification requirements established by the Department
155   of Health under the National Rabies Prevention and Control Program. The Department shall issue
156   the necessary certifications upon finding that an RHU-based ABC satisfies minimum standards for
157   facilities, equipment, cold chain, staffing, and service delivery. Certification shall be valid for a
158   period of three (3) years, subject to periodic monitoring, unannounced inspections, and renewal.
159   The Department shall provide technical assistance, training, and capacity-building support to
160   RHUs to facilitate compliance.
161
162          SECTION 7. Licensing and Accreditation. No Animal Bite Center established under this
163   Act shall operate without a valid License to Operate (LTO) and corresponding certification issued
164   by the Department of Health. Continued operation shall be subject to periodic inspection and
165   compliance audits. Failure to renew certification, material non-compliance with applicable
166   standards, or substantiated reports of substandard care shall constitute sufficient ground for the
167   suspension or revocation of authority to operate, without prejudice to applicable administrative
168   and criminal liability under existing law.

193           SECTION 10. Supply of Vaccines, Biologics, and Equipment. The Department of Health
194   shall ensure the continuous, adequate, and equitable supply of anti-rabies vaccines, rabies
195   immunoglobulin, wound care supplies, personal protective equipment, and cold chain equipment
196   necessary for the uninterrupted operation of all RHU-based Animal Bite Centers. Procurement

198   prioritize quality-assured and internationally prequalified products. The Department shall
199   coordinate with the Department of Budget and Management to ensure timely release of

309           SECTION 18. Integrated One Health Rabies Surveillance System. The Department of

312   RHU-based Animal Bite Centers shall submit standardized exposure reports, vaccination records,
313   and adverse event notifications through the electronic Integrated Disease Surveillance and
314   Response (IDSR) system or such successor system as the Department may designate. The
