                             AN ACT
 DECLARING THE MUNICIPALITY OF DONSOL, PROVINCE OF SORSOGON,
  AS THE CAPITAL FOR BUTANDING INTERACTION AND CONSERVATION
                       IN THE PHILIPPINES

                                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Certain natural wonders inspire awe as profoundly as the butanding, the largest
fish in the world, yet among the gentlest of marine giants. Known for its immense
size, calm presence, and striking white-and-gray spotted patterns, the butanding is
one of the most majestic and recognizable species in the ocean, prized not only for its
beauty and rarity, but also for its ecological importance.1

       The Municipality of Donsol, Province of Sorsogon, has long been identified with
the butanding and has earned national and international recognition as the country’s
foremost destination for whale shark interaction. The Asia-Pacific Economic
Cooperation (APEC), in its case study on Philippine best practices in sustainable
tourism, described Donsol’s whale shark interaction program as the Philippines’ most
successful ecotourism product. Its distinction has likewise been affirmed by major
recognitions, including TIME Magazine’s “Best Place for an Animal Encounter” in 2004
and the 11th Kalakbay Award in 2003 for the eco-tourism product category.2

       This measure is necessary because Donsol is not merely a place where tourists
occasionally encounter butanding, but a locality that has built, over time, a model of
ethical and conservation-based wildlife tourism. That commitment is reflected in
Donsol’s Municipal Ordinance No. 12, series of 1998, which protected the butanding,
declared Donsol’s municipal waters a sanctuary for the species, and vested upon the
local government the authority to operate, control, and manage the community-based
ecotourism program. This legal and policy foundation is further borne out by data
reported by WWF-Philippines in 2024, showing that Donsol accounted for 40% of the

        Declaring Donsol as the Capital for Butanding Interaction and Conservation in
the Philippines is to accord formal legislative recognition to its established role as a
leading center of ethical, responsible, and sustainable ecotourism. The term
“interaction” is particularly apt, as butanding are migratory species whose seasonal
presence in Donsol reflects their natural movement consistent with the municipality’s
non-intrusive ecotourism program. Thus, the bill does more than confer a title but
provides a clear policy signal that can strengthen destination identity, enhance visitor
confidence, and encourage greater public and private investment in tourism facilities,
conservation initiatives, and local enterprise. The measure is likewise justified by its
economic and social impact. Data from the Donsol Tourism Office, as cited by the
Philippine News Agency, showed that the pre-pandemic 2019 butanding season
generated ₱448 million in gross revenues and around 13,000 tourist arrivals, with
substantial benefits flowing to the local community and the local government. These
figures demonstrate that responsible tourism, when properly managed, can protect
wildlife while creating dignified livelihoods, drawing investment, and expanding
opportunities for local residents.4

        Ultimately, this bill affirms a recognized model of development that our country
may well uphold with pride, one in which tourism safeguards wildlife, sustains
livelihoods, attracts investment, and empowers communities to become faithful
stewards of nature. Having earned various distinction through years of conservation,
responsible tourism, and community-driven progress, it is both just and timely for
Congress to accord formal and lasting recognition to the Municipality of Donsol,
Province of Sorsogon, by declaring it the Capital for Butanding Interaction and
Conservation in the Philippines.

           In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to promote and
 2   support the conservation, protection, and sustainable management of the country’s
 3   marine biodiversity and coastal resources, including threatened, vulnerable, and
 4   ecologically significant species such as whale sharks or butanding and their habitats,
 5   in order to preserve ecological balance, maintain the health and productivity of marine
 6   ecosystems, and secure for present and future generations the benefits derived
 7   therefrom.
 8
 9      Pursuant thereto, the State shall recognize the Municipality of Donsol, Province of
10   Sorsogon, as a premier center for responsible and eco-sensitive tourism, anchored on
11   the conservation, protection, and sustainable management of butanding and its
12   habitat, while advancing community-based livelihood opportunities for its residents
13   and ensuring the balanced development of tourism in a manner consistent with
14   ecological integrity, cultural preservation, and inclusive local economic growth.
15
16       SEC. 2. Donsol as the Butanding Capital of the Philippines. In recognition
17   of its status as the country’s premier and most iconic destination for butanding
18   interaction and conservation-based ecotourism, the Municipality of Donsol, Province
19   of Sorsogon, is hereby declared as the Butanding Capital of the Philippines.
