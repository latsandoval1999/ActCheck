                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to create an Independent People’s Commission (IPC) to investigate
and document anomalies involved in the completion of infrastructure projects, and to
recommend reforms that will strengthen transparency, accountability, and technical oversight
in the use of public funds.

        Corruption in public infrastructure remains a persistent issue to national development.
The Philippines ranked one hundred and twentieth (120th) out of one hundred and eighty two
(182) countries monitored in the 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) report1. This
reflects continuing concerns regarding public sector corruption and weak accountability
mechanisms. Infrastructure spending is particularly vulnerable to corruption because of the
huge amounts of public funds involved in procurement, project implementation, and contract
variation.

        The Commission on Audit (COA) has repeatedly reported systemic deficiencies in
infrastructure implementation. In 2024, COA flagged seven hundred and forty seven (747)
projects implemented by the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) that
became unusable and idle because of technical defects, improper supervision, and violations
of contract provisions2, which has resulted in government losses of approximately P6.45
Billion. These findings highlight the need for a specialized and independent oversight
mechanism that focuses specifically on infrastructure integrity. While institutions such as the
COA, the Department of Justice, and congressional oversight bodies conduct investigations
and audits, they primarily address compliance or criminal liability. The technical complexity
of modern infrastructure projects requires a multidisciplinary approach that integrates legal,
engineering, forensic accounting, and governance expertise.

       The proposed IPC will serve as an independent fact finding body composed of
experts from the judiciary, engineering, forensic accounting, civil society, and the academe.
This proposed measure strengthens the State’s capacity to detect ghost projects, overpricing
schemes, substandard construction, and other forms of corruption in infrastructure

Filipino people, rather than enriching a favored few.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
CREATING AN INDEPENDENT PEOPLE’S COMMISSION TO INVESTIGATE
ANOMALIES IN ALL GOVERNMENT INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS,
DEFINING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Independent People’s
Commission Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – As enshrined in the Philippine constitution, public
office is public trust, and that public officers and employees must at all times be accountable
to the people. It is hereby the declared policy of the State to recognize that government
infrastructure projects inherently involve public interest, as public funds are utilized.
Moreover, as a recognized policy in government auditing, all government resources must be
safeguarded against loss or wastage through illegal or improper disposition, with a view of
ensuring the economy, and the efficiency and effectiveness in government operations.
Further, the State shall ensure that public funds are utilized honestly and transparently in the
construction of all government infrastructure. Towards this end, a non-partisan and
independent commission must be established to investigate and recommend measures to
address corruption, mismanagement, and negligence in these projects.

       SEC. 3. Creation of the Independent People’s Commission. – There is hereby created
an Independent People’s Commission, herein referred to as the Commission, to document
and investigate anomalies in all government infrastructure projects, and to recommend
measures to address corruption, mismanagement, and negligence in the planning,
procurement and implementation of public infrastructure projects.​

        The Commission shall specialize in the technical, engineering, and financial audit of
such projects including, but not limited to, procurement processes, contract variations,
right-of-way acquisitions, and project implementation. It shall serve as the primary
repository of government infrastructure data.

       SEC. 4. Mandate and Scope. – The Commission shall serve as an independent,
non-partisan, and fact-finding body mandated to investigate, document, and recommend



                                               1
actions on anomalies in government infrastructure projects. In the exercise of this mandate,
the Commission shall:

           a)​ Conduct comprehensive, independent, and technical investigations of all
       reported or alleged anomalies in the planning, procurement, and implementation of
       government infrastructure projects, including but not limited to, corruption, ghost
       projects, overpricing, and the use of substandard materials. Such investigations shall
       cover all projects implemented by the National Government, its agencies and
       instrumentalities, local government units (LGUs), and government-owned and
       controlled corporations (GOCCs);

           b)​ Identify individuals and entities, both in the public and private sectors, who
       are responsible for, participated in, or benefited from such anomalies;

          c)​ In coordination with the Office of the Ombudsman, the Department of Justice
       (DOJ), or other concerned agencies, the Commission may recommend the
       prosecution or further investigation of individuals or entities; and

           d)​ Formulate and submit to the President and Congress a set of concrete and
       actionable recommendations for legislative and executive reforms to prevent future
       anomalies.

      SEC. 5. Composition and Appointment. – The members of the Commission shall be
composed of the following:

           a)​ a retired Supreme Court Justice or Court of Appeals Justice, who shall serve
       as Chairperson;

           b)​ a Certified Public Accountant with expertise in auditing or a forensic
       accounting specialist with professional certifications such as Certified Fraud
       Examiner (CFE) and Certified in Finance Forensics (CFF) and demonstrable
       experience in fraud detection in government or public institutions, with at least ten
       (10) years of experience either in public or private practice;

           c)​ a Civil Engineer or an Architect, with at least ten (10) years of experience
       either in public or private practice;

           d)​ a representative from a reputable non-governmental organization (NGO), the
       primary purpose and/or expertise of which is in good governance, anti-corruption,
       public financial management, public procurement and open contracting, or citizen
       monitoring of infrastructure projects; and

           e)​ a representative from the academe with expertise in governance reform and
       corruption prevention studies, especially in the context of public administration,
       disaster and climate risk management, natural resource management, urban planning
       and development, or application of artificial intelligence and data science.

       The members of the Commission shall be appointed by the President of the
Philippines, with a term of three (3) years, and may be once reappointed. To ensure
independence and impartiality, the chairperson shall be chosen from a list of nominees



                                             2
submitted by the Integrated Bar of the Philippines; and the other members, from a list of
nominees submitted by the Philippine Institute of Certified Public Accountants, the
Philippine Institute of Civil Engineers, United Architects of the Philippines, and a recognized
national coalition of governance and and anti-corruption NGOs. Each member shall be of
good moral character, of unquestionable integrity, or known probity and patriotism, and with
recognized competence in their respective fields.

       In case of death, resignation, or removal of any member, the vacancy shall be filled
by the appointment of a new member to complete the unexpired period of the term of the
member concerned.

       No member of the Commission shall, within one (1) year after the expiration of their
term, be eligible for appointment or designation to any office or employment in any
government agency or GOCC engaged in infrastructure, planning, procurement, or
implementation.

        SEC. 6. Powers and Functions. – The Commission is hereby granted powers related
to the investigation of all government infrastructure projects. The authority of the
Commission, which shall be concurrent with that of other agencies vested by law with
investigative powers, shall not be exercised as to diminish or encroach upon ongoing
investigations conducted by the Office of the Ombudsman or the DOJ. To the extent
permitted by the law and in accordance with delegation by the Office of the Ombudsman or
the DOJ, the Commission may assist in specific areas arising from its investigations.

       In the exercise of its mandate, the Commission shall have the following powers and
functions:

           a)​ Investigative Powers

                 i)​ Investigate government infrastructure projects, motu proprio, upon
               referral by the Commission on Audit (COA), or through a verified complaint
               from any person or from any source in whatever form concerning matters
               within the Commission’s mandate and jurisdiction;

                ii)​ To terminate within sixty (60) days therefrom an investigation opened
               within fifteen (15) days from the submission of the recommendation from
               COA or a verified complaint, regardless of the manner of its initiation;

           b)​ To issue subpoena ad testificandum and subpoena duces tecum to compel the
       attendance of witnesses and the production of documents, records, and other
       evidence;

           c)​ To administer oaths and take testimony;

           d)​ To have full and unrestricted access to all government records, including but
       not limited to, contracts, bidding documents, financial and audit reports, and official
       correspondences related to the projects under investigation;

           e)​ To file appropriate charges against government officials and employees who
       shall obstruct any inquiry and investigation of the IPC;



                                               3
           f)​ To conduct private and public hearings to gather information, with due regard
       for the rights of all persons;

           g)​ To seek the assistance of any department, bureau, office, or agency of the
       government, including the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) and the Philippine
       National Police (PNP), to ensure the integrity of the investigation and the protection
       of witnesses;

           h)​ To recommend to the Department of Justice (DOJ) the grant of witness
       immunity for any person who provides vital material information relevant to the
       investigation; and

          i)​ To promulgate its rules and regulations, and rules of procedures as it deems
       necessary to efficiently and effectively carry out its objectives, within the ambit of the
       law.

        SEC. 7. Mandatory Report. – The IPC shall submit its final report to the President
and Congress within thirty (30) working days from the termination of its investigation. The
report shall detail its findings, name individuals and entities found culpable, recommend the
filing of criminal, civil and/or administrative charges, and provide specific recommendations
for legislative and executive action. The report shall be made public immediately upon
submission.

       SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the Office of the President.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued operation shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 9. Organizational Structure and Staffing Pattern. – The IPC shall determine its
organizational structure and staffing pattern, subject to the approval of the Department of
Budget and Management, and consistent with civil service laws, rules, and regulations.

        SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is subsequently
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, rule or regulation inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national circulation.
