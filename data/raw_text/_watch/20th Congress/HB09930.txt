14                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

15   The Constitution guarantees every Filipino learner the right to quality education. This
16   guarantee, however, cannot be fully realized unless schools are safe, secure, and conducive
17   environments for learning. The increasing incidence of violence and security threats in
18   and around educational institutions underscores the urgent need to strengthen security
19   measures in public schools nationwide.

20   Recent events have exposed the vulnerabilities that continue to exist within educational
21   settings. On 22 June 2026, a shooting incident at San Jose National High School in
22   Tacloban City claimed the lives of three students and injured several others when two
23   students allegedly brought firearms onto school grounds and opened fire during class
24   hours.

25   Likewise, a separate stabbing incident in a school in General Trias City, Cavite, involved a
26   Grade 8 student who allegedly attacked several Grade 5 pupils using a kitchen knife,
27   injuring at least seven learners.

28   Together, these incidents demonstrate that violence in schools is no longer a remote
29   possibility but a real and emerging risk affecting the safety and psychological well-being of
30   learners.

31   Many public schools currently rely on limited security arrangements, including
32   contractual guards whose deployment varies depending on local resources and budget
33   availability. Numerous schools, particularly in rural and underserved areas, have no
34   dedicated security personnel at all.

35   This measure seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing a permanent school security
36   framework through the creation of plantilla positions for School Security Officers (SSOs)
37   under the Department of Education. These SSOs shall be career government employees
38   who are properly trained, adequately compensated, and fully integrated into the school
39   system. Their presence will help ensure consistent monitoring of school premises, access
40   control, and coordination with law enforcement and local government authorities.

41   A secure school environment enables learners to focus on their studies, teachers to
42   perform their duties effectively, and parents to have confidence that their children are
43   protected while in school.

44   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

65   AN ACT REQUIRING THE CREATION OF PLANTILLA POSITIONS FOR
66   SCHOOL SECURITY OFFICERS IN ALL PUBLIC ELEMENTARY AND
67   SECONDARY SCHOOLS AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF

68         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
69   Congress assembled:

70   SECTION 1. Short Title.

71          This Act shall be known as the “Public School Security Act of 2026.”

72   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.

73          Pursuant to Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution, the State shall protect
74   and promote the right of all citizens to quality education and shall take appropriate
75   measures to make such education accessible to all. The State recognizes its duty to ensure
76   that public schools remain safe and secure environments conducive to learning and
77   development. Towards this end, the State shall strengthen school security through the
78   creation of permanent plantilla positions for School Security Officers in all public basic
79   education institutions.

80   SECTION 3. Coverage.

81         This Act shall apply to all public elementary schools, junior high schools, senior
82   high schools, integrated schools, and other public basic education institutions under the
83   supervision of the Department of Education (DepEd).

84   SECTION 4. Creation of School Security Officer Plantilla Positions.

85          There are hereby created permanent plantilla positions of School Security Officer
86   (SSO) under the Department of Education. School Security Officers shall be career
87   government personnel appointed in accordance with civil service laws, rules, and
88   regulations and shall enjoy all rights, privileges, benefits, and protections accorded to
89   government employees. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM), in
90   coordination with the Department of Education and the Civil Service Commission (CSC),
91   shall determine the number, classification, salary grade, and funding requirements for
92   School Security Officer positions necessary to implement this Act.

93   SECTION 5. Deployment of School Security Officers.

94   (a) Every public school shall be assigned at least one (1) School Security Officer.

95   (b) Additional School Security Officer positions shall be allocated according to the
96   following minimum standards:
 97      1. One (1) School Security Officer for schools with up to one thousand (1,000) learners;
 98      2. Two (2) School Security Officers for schools with one thousand one (1,001) to three
 99         thousand (3,000) learners; and
100      3. One (1) additional School Security Officer for every additional two thousand (2,000)
101         learners or fraction thereof.

102   (c) The Department of Education may assign additional School Security Officers to schools
103   located in high-risk areas, geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs),
104   disaster-prone communities, or schools identified as requiring heightened security.

105   (d) The use of contractual, job-order, casual, or outsourced security personnel shall not
106   substitute for the permanent plantilla positions created under this Act. Existing contracted
107   security services may be retained only as supplemental security measures when authorized
108   by the Department of Education.

109   SECTION 6. Qualification Standards, Duties, and Responsibilities of School Security
110   Officers.

111   (a) The Civil Service Commission (CSC), in coordination with the Department of
112   Education (DepEd) and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), shall establish
113   the qualification standards, competency requirements, eligibility requirements, training
114   standards, duties, responsibilities, performance measures, and career advancement system
115   for School Security Officers created under this Act.

116   (b) Such standards shall ensure that School Security Officers possess the necessary
117   competencies to maintain school safety, protect learners and personnel, respond to
118   emergencies, and perform security-related functions within public schools.

119   SECTION 7. Coordination with Local Government Units and National Agencies.

120          The Department of Education shall coordinate with the Department of the Interior
121   and Local Government (DILG), the Philippine National Police (PNP), the Bureau of Fire
122   Protection (BFP), the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council
123   (NDRRMC), local government units (LGUs), and other relevant agencies to ensure
124   effective implementation of this Act.

125          Local School Boards may provide supplemental funding and support for personnel
126   requirements consistent with existing laws.

127   SECTION 8. Appropriations.

128          The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged
129   against the current appropriations of the Department of Education. Thereafter, such sums as
130   may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the
131   annual General Appropriations Act.

132   SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations.

133          Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education,
134   in coordination with the DBM, CSC, DILG, PNP, and BFP, shall promulgate the necessary
135   rules and regulations for its effective implementation.
