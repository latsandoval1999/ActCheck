                             AN ACT
  PROVIDING FOR FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES IN DIGITAL AND
   TRADITIONAL LENDING, STRENGTHENING REGULATION OF ONLINE
    LENDING PLATFORMS, ESTABLISHING A VERIFIED COLLECTION
           IDENTITY SYSTEM, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid expansion of online lending applications (OLAs) in the
Philippines has significantly transformed access to credit, particularly among
underserved and financially vulnerable sectors. Recent industry data indicate
that millions of Filipinos have availed themselves of digital lending services,
driven largely by ease of access, minimal documentation requirements, and
immediate fund disbursement. However, this rapid growth has also been
accompanied by a surge in complaints involving abusive, deceptive, and
predatory debt collection practices.

       Records from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the
National Privacy Commission (NPC) show a significant number of grievances
involving harassment, threats, and unauthorized disclosure of personal data by
online lending entities and their agents. In several reported cases, borrowers
have been subjected to repeated threatening messages, public shaming, and the
dissemination of personal information to third parties, in clear violation of
existing laws and regulations.

      The SEC, through SEC Memorandum Circular No. 18, Series of 2019, has
expressly prohibited unfair debt collection practices, including the use of threats,
insults, and disclosure of borrower information to unauthorized persons. Despite
these regulatory measures, enforcement remains challenging due to the
increasing use of anonymized communication channels, such as unregistered
mobile numbers, email accounts, and social media platforms, which allow
abusive actors to evade identification and accountability.

      While existing statutes such as the Lending Company Regulation Act of
2007, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012
provide mechanisms to regulate lending practices, protect personal data, and
penalize cyber-related offenses, these laws do not sufficiently address the unique
and evolving challenges posed by digital lending platforms—particularly the
traceability and accountability of collection agents operating in online
environments.

       This measure seeks to address these gaps by establishing a comprehensive
regulatory framework governing online lending and digital debt collection
practices. It introduces a Verified Collection Identity System (VCIS), which
requires all lending entities and their agents to register and utilize traceable and
verifiable communication channels in the course of collection activities. This
system aims to eliminate anonymity in debt collection and ensure that all
collection-related communications can be properly attributed and regulated.

      The bill further strengthens protections for borrowers by codifying
prohibited collection practices, enhancing transparency requirements, and
providing clear mechanisms for enforcement and redress. It likewise aligns
penalties with existing penal laws, including applicable provisions of the Revised
Penal Code, the Data Privacy Act, and the Cybercrime Prevention Act, thereby
ensuring that violations committed through digital means are met with
appropriate civil, administrative, and criminal sanctions.

      Ultimately, this proposed legislation affirms the State’s policy to protect
consumers from abusive and unethical lending practices, safeguard the right to
privacy, and promote fairness, accountability, and transparency in the digital
financial sector.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Fair Debt Collection
and Digital Lending Consumer Protection Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to protect
borrowers from abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices; promote
transparency, accountability, and ethical standards in both digital and
traditional lending; safeguard the right to privacy in accordance with the Data
Privacy Act of 2012; and strengthen regulatory enforcement consistent with the
Lending Company Regulation Act of 2007 and existing rules of the Securities
and Exchange Commission.

SECTION 3. Scope of Application. This Act shall apply to all persons and
entities engaged in lending and debt collection activities, including:

   a. Lending companies as defined under Republic Act No. 9474;
   b. Digital lending platforms or online lending applications (OLAs);
   c. Third-party service providers and collection agencies; and
   d. Any natural or juridical person engaged in the collection of debts.
SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

  a. Debtor shall refer to a borrower or person granted a loan by any digital
     lending platform
  b. Lending company shall refer to a corporation engaged in granting loans
     from its own capital funds or from funds sourced from not more than 19
     persons. It shall not be deemed to include banking institutions, investment
     houses, savings and loan associations, financing companies, pawnshops,
     insurance companies, cooperatives, and other credit institutions already
     regulated by law.
  c. Digital Lending Platform shall refer to any mobile application, website, or
     digital interface offering loans;
  d. Collection Agent refers to any person authorized and registered with the
     Security Exchange Commission (SEC);
  e. Verified Communication Channel is any communication channel, such as
     a phone number, email, or account officially declared by the lending entity
     to regulatory authorities
  f. Abusive Collection Practice is any act or omission in connection with debt
     collection that harasses, oppresses, or abuses any borrower, including
     those prohibited under SEC Memorandum Circular No. 18, Series of 2019,
     and this Act.
  g. Verified Communication Channel refers to a communication medium duly
     registered with the SSEC and capable of being authenticated by borrowers
     through a publicly accessible verification system.
  h. Consent of the borrower refers to any freely given specific, informed
     indication of will, whereby the borrower agrees to the collection and
     processing of personal information about and/or relating to him or her.
     Consent shall be evidenced by written, electronic, or recorded means.

SECTION 5. Regulatory Jurisdiction. The Securities and Exchange
Commission (SEC) shall be the primary implementing agency. The SEC shall
coordinate with the National Privacy Commission (NPC), the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas, when applicable.

SECTION 6. Verified Collection Identity System. A Verified Collection Identity
System is hereby established under the supervision of the Securities and
Exchange Commission.
   a. All covered entities shall register with the SEC:
        i. Their duly authorized collection agents; and
       ii. All communication channels used for collection activities, including
             mobile numbers, email addresses, messaging accounts, and similar
             identifiers.

   b. The SEC shall maintain a centralized, publicly accessible verification
      platform that allows borrowers to confirm the identity of collection agents
      and the authenticity of communications.

   c. All collection communications must:
        i. Originate solely from registered communication channels; and
       ii. Contain sufficient identifying information as may be prescribed by
             the SEC.

   d. The use of unregistered, spoofed, anonymous, or deceptive communication
      channels is strictly prohibited.

SECTION 7. Prohibited Acts. In addition to acts prohibited under existing laws
and regulations, including SEC Memorandum Circular No. 18, Series of 2019,
the following shall be unlawful:


   a. Harassment, intimidation, or threat of harm;
   b. Use of obscene, profane, or abusive language;
   c. Public shaming or disclosure of borrower information to third parties
      without a lawful basis, in violation of the Data Privacy Act of 2012;
   d. Communication with persons other than the borrower, except as
       permitted by law or with express consent;
   e. Excessive or unreasonable communications, including those made at
      unreasonable hours or frequency as may be defined by the SEC;
   f. Misrepresentation of the amount owed, terms of the loan, or
      consequences of non-payment;
   g. False threats of legal action or criminal prosecution;
   h. Use of unregistered communication channels; and
   i. Any deceptive, unfair, or unconscionable means in debt collection.

SECTION 8. Borrower Notification and Transparency. Prior to any collection
activities, the lender or its agent shall provide the borrower with a written or
electronic notice containing:

   a. The total amount due, with itemized breakdown;
   b. The legal basis of the obligation;
   c. Information sufficient to identify the identity of the collecting entity.
SECTION 9. Administrative Sanctions. After due notice and hearing, the SEC
may impose the following:


   a. Fines ranging from PhP 50,000 to PhP 5,000,000, depending on gravity;
   b. Suspension or revocation of Certificate of Authority;
   c. Blacklisting and permanent disqualification of the entity and responsible
   officers;
   d. Compliance and cease-and-desist orders; and
   e. Referral for criminal prosecution under applicable laws


SECTION 10. Criminal Liability. Any person who commits acts prohibited
under this Act shall, when applicable, be prosecuted under existing penal laws,
including but not limited to:
   a. Grave Threats, Light Threats, Coercion, Unjust Vexation, and Defamation
      under the Revised Penal Code;
   b. Cyber-related offenses, including but not limited to cyber libel, unlawful
      use of electronic communications, and other analogous acts under the
      Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012;
   c. Unauthorized processing, disclosure, or misuse of personal data under the
      Data Privacy Act of 2012;
   d. Other applicable special laws, where the acts constitute separate or
      additional offenses.


Where the prohibited act is committed through information and communications
technologies, the penalty shall be imposed one degree higher, in accordance with
Republic Act No. 10175.
SECTION 11. Liability of Officers and Agents. Directors, officers, and
employees who knowingly participate in or authorize violations shall be solely
liable, without prejudice to criminal prosecution under applicable laws.

SECTION 12. Complaint and Redress Mechanism. The SEC shall establish a
centralized digital complaints system that:


   a. Accepts electronic evidence (screenshots, recordings);
   b. Ensures timely investigation;
   c. Provides accessible remedies for borrowers.

SECTION 13. Relationship with Existing Laws. This Act shall supplement
existing laws, rules, and regulations governing lending, debt collection, and data
privacy. Nothing herein shall be construed to diminish rights and protections
afforded under applicable laws.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days,
the SEC, in coordination with the NPC, DICT, and BSP, shall promulgate the
IRR.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, orders, rules, and regulations, other
issuances, or any part thereof, inconsistent with any provision of this Act, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
