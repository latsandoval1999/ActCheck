                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Section 14, Article II of the 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall recognize the vital
role of women in nation-building and ensure fundamental equality before the law of women and
men. Guided by this constitutional mandate, the Philippines has progressively built a robust legal
and policy framework to advance gender equality and women's empowerment, anchored on
landmark legislation such as Republic Act No. 71921, Republic Act No. 97102, Republic Act No.
113133, alongside its commitments under the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, the
Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), and the
Beijing Platform for Action.


        The Philippine Commission on Women (PCW) serves as the government’s primary policy-
making and coordinating body on women’s rights, gender equality, and women’s empowerment.
As the national machinery for gender equality, the Commission leads national policy
development, coordinates government-wide implementation of gender equality programs, and
serves as the central institution responsible for advancing the State’s commitments to women’s
rights and empowerment.


        Yet, despite the significant expansion of its mandate over the past five decades, the
Commission’s institutional structure, powers, and resources have remained largely unchanged.
The result is a growing misalignment between the State’s expanding gender equality commitments
and the institutional capacity required to effectively implement them.


        When the Commission was established in 1975 through Presidential Decree No. 633, its
functions were primarily centered on policy advocacy, coordination, information generation,
and promoting women’s participation in development. During this period, the regulatory and
organizational framework for gender-related rights was far more restricted, characterized by
fewer systems for accountability and a more limited scope of government responsibilities.

          In 2025, the nation marked the 50th anniversary of the creation of the PCW. This milestone
presents an important opportunity not merely to commemorate the Commission’s historic
contributions, but to modernize and strengthen the country’s national gender equality machinery
so that it remains capable of responding effectively to present realities and future challenges
confronting Filipino women.


          Five decades after PCW’s creation, the institutional environment surrounding gender
equality governance has evolved significantly, requiring a stronger and more empowered
Commission.


          The enactment of Republic Act No. 9710 or the Magna Carta of Women fundamentally
transformed gender equality from a policy objective into a legally enforceable obligation of the
State. As a result, the Commission’s responsibilities expanded significantly, requiring it to ensure
that government institutions nationwide integrate women’s rights standards into laws, policies,
programs, and public institutions in ways that did not exist when the Commission’s current
structure was originally conceived.


          Equally important, the challenges confronting Filipino women have become increasingly
complex and urgent. Despite significant progress in women’s rights and participation, women
continue to face persistent discrimination arising from gender-based violence, trafficking,
economic inequality, workplace discrimination, unequal care responsibilities, digital exclusion,
online harassment, conflict-related vulnerabilities, and intersecting forms of discrimination
affecting women from marginalized sectors.


          Likewise concerning is the persistence of misogyny and gender-based discrimination
in public discourse itself. In recent years, the country has witnessed repeated instances of
misogynistic remarks, sexist rhetoric, degrading public statements, and harmful gender
stereotypes being perpetuated by public officials and other public figures. Left unaddressed, these
acts erode the State’s constitutional commitment to uphold the dignity of women and guarantee
substantive equality in both law and practice.


          The PCW, despite being the government’s lead agency for promoting and protecting
gender equality, currently lacks sufficient statutory authority to independently initiate formal
fact-finding inquiries, examine systemic or recurring acts of gender-based discrimination
affecting women in public life, gather relevant information and documentation, and issue
formal recommendations to appropriate government agencies or policymakers for responsive
action.


          As a result, the PCW is often left unable to act decisively in situations where public
conduct, institutional behavior, or emerging patterns of discrimination demand immediate
intervention, policy response, or accountability measures. This widening institutional gap can no
longer be ignored.
        This measure seeks to comprehensively strengthen and modernize the PCW by aligning
its institutional powers and organizational structure with the scale of its present-day
responsibilities.


        Specifically, this bill seeks to:


        ●       Reposition the Commission under the Office of the President, restoring its policy
        advisory and coordinating role at the highest level of government, recognizing that gender
        equality is a cross-cutting national development concern requiring whole-of-government
        coordination and stronger institutional accountability;
        ●       Strengthen the Commission’s policy leadership, oversight, research, and
        coordination functions to enable government to respond more effectively to emerging and
        increasingly complex gender equality challenges;
        ●       Authorize the Commission to undertake fact-finding and policy inquiries on
        systemic, recurring, or emerging patterns of discrimination, exclusion, or institutional
        barriers affecting women, including gaps in the implementation of laws, policies,
        programs, and services, and to issue formal findings and recommendations to appropriate
        government agencies for corrective policy, programmatic, or administrative action,
        without prejudice to the mandates of the Commission on Human Rights, the Office of the
        Ombudsman, and other bodies vested by law with investigative or adjudicatory powers;
        ●       Expand the Commission’s presence at the regional and local levels to strengthen
        direct engagement with communities, improve localization of gender-responsive
        programs, and provide more accessible technical support to local government institutions;
        ●       Institutionalize a national system for gender equality capacity development,
        research, and knowledge generation to strengthen evidence-based policymaking and
        build long-term institutional expertise on emerging gender issues; and
        ●       Strengthen government-wide institutional mechanisms for gender mainstreaming
        and accountability to ensure more effective implementation of programs and public
        investments dedicated to women’s empowerment.


        Strengthening the Philippine Commission on Women is not merely an institutional reform
— it is a strategic investment in better governance and more effective public service. This
measure reinforces whole-of-government policy coherence, strengthens accountability for the
use of public resources, improves coordination among national and local institutions, and
ensures that laws, policies and international commitments on gender equality are translated
into consistent, measurable, and responsive government action.


        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                                 ARTICLE I
                               Title, Declaration of Policy, and Objectives


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Commission on Women
Strengthening Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize and uphold women’s
rights as human rights and ensure the substantive equality of women and men before the law.
Pursuant to Article II, Section 14 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution, the State affirms the role of
women in nation-building and shall ensure their fundamental equality before the law.


Pursuant to Republic Act No. 9710, otherwise known as the Magna Carta of Women (MCW), the
State recognizes its obligation to eliminate all forms of discrimination against women by respecting,
protecting, fulfilling, and promoting the rights of Filipino women, particularly those belonging to
marginalized sectors.


The State likewise reaffirms its commitments under the United Nations Convention on the
Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (UN CEDAW), the Beijing Declaration
and Platform for Action (BPfA), the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, and other global
frameworks advancing gender equality and women empowerment (GEWE).


Accordingly, the State shall strengthen the Philippine Commission on Women (PCW) as the
country’s national women’s machinery and lead entity for the promotion of GEWE in all sectors of
society.

1
SEC. 3. The Philippine Commission on Women. — The Philippine Commission on Women
(PCW) is hereby reorganized and strengthened under the Office of the President (OP), hereinafter
referred to as the Commission.


The Commission shall serve as the primary policymaking, advisory, coordinating, and oversight
body on GEWE. It shall provide strategic direction, facilitate inter-agency coordination, and
promote the integration of gender perspectives across all levels of governance and sectors.


The Commission shall also serve as the official national focal point for regional and international
cooperation on GEWE, including engagement with multilateral and intergovernmental
mechanisms.


SEC. 4. Mandate and Core Functions. — The Commission shall exercise the following core
functions:


    a. Policy Development and Advisory. Lead the formulation, harmonization, and
         recommendation of policies, plans, and programs on GEWE;
    b. Coordination and Oversight. Ensure alignment, coherence, and convergence of national
         and local initiatives;
    c.   Monitoring, and Evaluation. Assess compliance with GEWE-related laws, policies, and
         commitments;
    d. Technical Assistance and Capacity Development. Provide support to agencies, local
         government units (LGUs), and stakeholders;
    e. International Representation and Reporting. Lead the coordination of the State’s
         participation in regional and international GEWE mechanisms and compliance reporting
         obligations.


         The Commission shall further act as the official focal point of the Philippines for the
         ASEAN Committee on Women (ACW), Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Policy
         Partnership on Women and the Economy (PPWE) - Women and the Economy Forum
         (WEF), and other regional gender equality frameworks, and shall ensure the
         mainstreaming and integration of international and regional commitments into national,
         sectoral, and local development plans, policies, and programs.


         In this capacity, it shall lead the monitoring, evaluation, and reporting of the State’s
         compliance with the UN CEDAW, the BPfA, and other international and regional human
         rights instruments on women and gender equality; and


    f.   Research, Data, and Knowledge Management. Promote evidence-based policymaking
         through data, research, and knowledge management systems.


The detailed powers and functions necessary to implement the foregoing shall be provided under
Section 6 of this Act.



2
SEC. 5. Nature and Character of the Commission. — The Commission is hereby strengthened as
the national gender equality and women’s rights mechanism, vested with policy leadership in the
formulation of GEWE policies, as well as coordination, oversight, monitoring and evaluation,
capacity-building, and international representation functions.


In the exercise of its policy leadership, the Commission shall lead in the development,
harmonization, and recommendation of national policies, frameworks, standards, and guidelines
on GEWE, for consideration and appropriate action by the OP or other competent authorities,
while concurrently serving as the primary monitoring and oversight body to ensure compliance
and effective implementation of the GFPS Policy across all agencies.


The Commission may issue policy advisories, guidelines, and standards, consistent with existing
laws and executive issuances, to ensure the effective and coherent implementation of GEWE-
related policies, plans, and programs across national government agencies (NGAs), regional line
agencies (RLAs), government-owned or –controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities and
colleges (SUCs), and LGUs.


In relation to LGUs, the exercise of such authority shall be consistent with the principles of local
autonomy and in accordance with applicable laws.


All policy issuances of the Commission shall be aligned with the Constitution, existing laws, and
the overall policy direction of the OP.

provided under existing laws, the Commission, shall exercise the following powers and functions:


    a. Policy Leadership and Standard-Setting. Formulate, adopt, and issue national policies,

        harmonized implementation of relevant laws and commitments. The Commission shall

        comments, policy recommendations, and official positions on matters affecting women’s
        rights, gender equality, and women’s empowerment.


    b. Oversight and Policy Coordination. Exercise technical oversight and policy coordination
        over NGAs, RLAs, GOCCs, SUCs, LGUs, and other government instrumentalities for
        purposes of gender mainstreaming. For this purpose, the Commission shall:


           i.   Convene and lead inter-agency and multi-sectoral coordination mechanisms on
                GEWE;
          ii.   Require the designation and maintenance of functional Gender and Development
                Focal Point Systems (GFPS) in all agencies and instrumentalities; and
         iii.   Issue standards, indicators, and guidance to ensure the functionality, effectiveness,
                and sustainability of GFPS as institutional mechanism for gender mainstreaming.

SEC 7. Nature of Investigatory, Monitoring, and Compliance Functions. — The investigatory,
monitoring, and compliance functions of the Commission shall be non-adjudicatory in nature and
shall be exercised solely for the purpose of policy oversight, assessment of compliance, and
formulation of recommendations.


In the exercise of such functions, the Commission may conduct fact-finding inquiries, request the
submission of relevant documents, data, and information, and invite concerned officials or entities
to provide clarifications, in accordance with due process and applicable laws.


The findings, reports, and issuances of the Commission shall be recommendatory and advisory,
and shall not have the force and effect of judicial or quasi-judicial determinations.

SEC. 9. Organizational Structure and Staffing. — The Chairperson of the Commission shall
determine the organizational structure and staffing pattern of the Commission in accordance with
the revised compensation and position classification system, subject to the evaluation and approval
of the DBM, and in compliance with the Civil Service laws, rules and regulations, and other
relevant laws.


The Commission shall submit the proposed revised organizational structure and staffing pattern
to the DBM within ninety (90) days from the date of effectivity of this Act. The DBM shall complete
its evaluation and act thereon within ninety (90) days from receipt of complete documentary
requirements, unless extended for justifiable reasons, in accordance with applicable laws, rules,
and regulations.


Pending the submission, evaluation, and approval by the DBM of the revised organizational
structure and staffing pattern, the Commission shall continue to operate under its existing
organizational structure and staffing complement, without prejudice to actions necessary for the
uninterrupted performance of its functions.

SECTION 12. Institutionalization of Gender and Development (GAD) Positions. All NGAs,
GOCCs, RLAs, including local water districts (LWDs), SUCs, and LGUs shall establish GAD
positions to support the effective implementation and institutionalization of gender
mainstreaming efforts.


The establishment of such positions shall be undertaken in a phased manner, taking into
consideration the size, mandate, capacity, and resource availability of the concerned agency or
LGU, and shall be subject to the guidelines issued by the Commission, in coordination with the
CSC and the DBM.


At a minimum, each covered agency or LGU shall establish at least one (1) plantilla GAD position
or its functional equivalent: Provided, That larger agencies, GOCCs, SUCs, and LGUs shall
establish additional positions commensurate to their size, budget, and scope of operations, as may
be determined by the DBM in coordination with the Commission and the CSC.


The permanent GAD positions shall be distinct from the GFPS, but may serve as members of the
GFPS Technical Working Group (TWG), as provided under the MCW.


To ensure adequate authority and effectiveness, each agency shall establish the permanent GAD
positions at a rank and salary grade determined in accordance with existing civil service and
budgetary laws, rules, and regulations. Where more than one (1) plantilla position is created for
this purpose, at least one (1) position shall be at a professional or supervisory level, with the
corresponding salary grade prescribed by the DBM, taking into account the size, mandate, and
organizational structure of the agency or LGU.


The CSC, in coordination with the DBM and the Commission, shall prescribe the qualification
standards, competency framework, and minimum training requirements for GAD positions,
including mandatory training on GEWE, gender mainstreaming, gender-responsive planning and
budgeting, and relevant laws, policies, and international commitments.


SECTION 13. Compliance, Administrative Accountability, and Referral Mechanism. —


     a. Prima Facie Evidence of Non-Compliance. The findings, reports, and recommendations
        of the Commission shall constitute prima facie evidence of non-compliance or violation of
        GEWE laws, policies, directives, or standards. Such findings may serve as the basis for
        referral or endorsement to the appropriate administrative, disciplinary, or prosecutorial
        authority having jurisdiction over the concerned agency, official, or staff.



12
     b. Non-compliance with PCW Directives. Any public officer, employee, or responsible
          person who, without lawful justification, fails or refuses to comply with policies, orders,
          directives, or corrective measures duly issued by the PCW, in the exercise of its mandate,
          shall be subject to appropriate administrative proceedings in accordance with existing
          laws, rules, and regulations. Such non-compliance shall include, but shall not be limited to,
          the following acts or omissions:


             i.   Failure to prepare and submit GAD Plans and Budgets, GAD Accomplishment
                  Reports, gender audit results, or other reports required under law, rule, or
                  regulation;
            ii.   Failure to establish, fill, or maintain the required permanent GAD Focal Person
                  plantilla positions;
           iii.   Refusal to allocate, or the misuse, diversion, or improper utilization of GAD budget
                  funds, in violation of existing budgeting, accounting, or auditing laws and rules;
           iv.    Disregard of PCW gender audit findings, compliance directives, or corrective
                  measures;
            v.    Failure or refusal to grant access to records, documents, facilities, premises, or data
                  lawfully required for monitoring, audit, evaluation, or compliance verification; and
           vi.    Any other act or omission which materially delays, obstructs, or defeats the
                  implementation of laws, policies, or programs on GEWE.


     c.   Obstruction of Investigations. Any act that directly or indirectly obstructs, impedes, or
          frustrates PCW investigations, monitoring, or compliance proceedings shall constitute
          obstruction, including but not limited to:


             i.   Refusal or unjustified failure to cooperate in lawful investigation or compliance
                  processes;
            ii.   Deliberate withholding, destruction, alteration, falsification, concealment, or
                  tampering of documents, records, or electronic data relevant to an inquiry;
           iii.   Intimidation, coercion, harassment, reprisal, retaliation, or threats against
                  complainants, witnesses, whistleblowers, or investigators;
           iv.    Knowingly making false statements or submitting false, incomplete, or misleading
                  information;
            v.    Preventing or restricting lawful access to premises, facilities, information systems,
                  or evidence; and
           vi.    Any act intended to compromise the integrity of evidence, fact-finding, or case
                  documentation.


     d. Referral to Proper Authorities. The Commission shall not exercise disciplinary or
          adjudicatory powers. Where non-compliance or violations are established based on its
          monitoring, fact-finding, or assessment functions, the Commission shall formally refer or
          endorse the matter, together with its findings and recommendations, to the appropriate
          authority, including but not limited to the CHR, CSC, the DILG, the COA, the Office of the



13
          Ombudsman, or other competent bodies, for investigation and disposition in accordance
          with their respective mandates.


     e. Presumption of Responsibility of Agency Heads. In cases of institutional or systemic non-
          compliance, the head of the concerned agency or local government unit may be held prima
          facie responsible, subject to due process and based on substantial evidence, in accordance
          with applicable civil service and administrative laws. This presumption may be rebutted
          by demonstrating that the head exercised due diligence, including issuance of directives,
          allocation of resources, and institution of reasonable monitoring and control mechanisms.


     f.   Administrative and Criminal Liability. Acts or omissions constituting non-compliance or
          obstruction under this Section may give rise to administrative, civil, or criminal liability, as
          may be appropriate, in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations. The
          Commission’s findings, reports, and recommendations shall constitute prima facie
          evidence for purposes of initiating or supporting such proceedings before the appropriate
          authority.


          Criminal liability may attach where non-compliance or obstruction results in, contributes
          to, or facilitates, as defined and penalized under existing laws, any of the following:


             i.   Violations of women’s rights under the Magna Carta of Women and related
                  statutes;
            ii.   Gender-based violence, including digital or technology-facilitated violence;
           iii.   Discrimination prohibited under national or local gender equality and non-
                  discrimination laws;
           iv.    Trafficking in persons, exploitation, sexual abuse, or related offenses;
            v.    Violations of data privacy and confidentiality rights affecting women and girls;
           vi.    Gross negligence or willful omission resulting in harm to the life, safety, dignity,
                  liberty, employment, or autonomy of women or girls; and
          vii.    Retaliation, reprisal, or intimidation against whistleblowers, complainants,
                  witnesses, or individuals assisting investigations or enforcement proceedings.


     g. Referral for Prosecution. Where the facts disclosed indicate the probable commission of
          an offense under the Revised Penal Code or special laws, the Commission shall refer or
          endorse the case, together with its findings, to the appropriate prosecutorial or law
          enforcement authority for investigation and prosecution.


     h. Coverage of Liability. Administrative and criminal liability under this Section shall attach
          notwithstanding any change in leadership or organizational structure, and may attach to
          any elective or appointive public official, employee, or responsible person, including
          uniformed staff, contractual staff, consultants, or service providers engaged by
          government entities, without prejudice to the liability of the public office or institution
          concerned under existing laws.



14
     i.   Due Process and Safeguards. All proceedings arising from referrals or endorsements
          made by the Commission shall observe the requirements of due process, including the right
          to notice, explanation, and opportunity to be heard, in accordance with applicable laws,
          rules, and regulations.


          The Commission’s findings and recommendations shall not be considered final or binding
          determinations of liability, but shall form part of the evidentiary record in the appropriate
          administrative or judicial proceedings.


SECTION 14. Staff Transition. — No officer or employee of the Commission, or of any office, unit,
or program affected by the reorganization under this Act, shall be removed from service, separated,
demoted in rank, reassigned to a position with lower responsibilities, or subjected to any
diminution of salary, allowances, benefits, privileges, or security of tenure, solely by reason of the
structural changes, transfer of supervision, expansion of functions, or creation of new positions
and field offices mandated by this Act.


All staff shall retain their current rank, status, compensation, and tenure, and shall be absorbed,
transferred, or reassigned in a manner consistent with Civil Service laws, ensuring the full
protection of their rights as government workers.


Officers or employees appointed to co-terminus positions shall continue to serve only in a hold-
over capacity until such time that a new appointment has been made in accordance with applicable
laws. During this period, they shall continue to perform duties necessary to ensure continuity of
operations and programs, without prejudice to the authority of the incoming appointee.


Any movement, reassignment, or reclassification shall not prejudice the employee and shall be
undertaken only to achieve organizational efficiency, with the employee’s consent whenever
practicable.


SECTION 15. Appropriations, Transfer of Assets, and Records.


     a. Appropriations. The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act shall be
          charged against the current year's appropriations of the Commission. Thereafter, such
          sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
          General Appropriations Act (GAA).


          The Commission shall be accorded priority funding status to enable the effective discharge
          of its mandate as the national gender equality and women’s rights machinery. The
          preparation, review, and approval of its annual budget shall take into consideration the
          scope, scale, and cross-sectoral nature of its functions.


          The approved appropriations of the Commission shall be released in a timely and regular
          manner, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and
          regulations.


15
        No reduction, realignment, or modification of the approved budget of the Commission
        shall be made in a manner that would materially impair the implementation of its core
        mandates, except in accordance with law.


        Nothing in this Act shall be construed as granting the Commission fiscal autonomy as
        reserved for constitutional bodies.


     b. Custody, Control, and Protection of Assets and Records.


           i.   Custody and Management of Assets. All assets, equipment, property, facilities,
                digital infrastructure, and resources of the PCW shall remain under the exclusive
                management, control, and administration of the Commission, notwithstanding its
                attachment to the OP.


                No asset of the Commission may be realigned, reassigned, disposed of, or utilized
                by any other office without the express written approval of the PCW Chairperson.


          ii.   Protection and Confidentiality of Records. All records, documents, case files,
                digital databases, archives, evidence gathered during investigations, gender
                compliance reports, and all other information produced or acquired by the
                Commission shall remain under its exclusive custody, and shall be protected by
                strict confidentiality and data protection protocols.


                Such records shall not be accessed, altered, transferred, or disclosed by any person
                or office, including those within the OP except in accordance with law or with the
                written authorization of the PCW Chairperson.


         iii.   Independence of Investigatory and Oversight Records. Records relating to
                investigations, audits, monitoring, or enforcement actions shall be immune from
                external interference. Destruction, alteration, withholding, or unauthorized access
                to such records shall constitute:


                    1. an administrative offense (grave misconduct), and
                    2. when applicable, a criminal offense (obstruction of justice, violation of
                        women’s rights, or breach of data privacy).


         iv.    Digital and Data Assets. All digital platforms, gender databases, AI systems, and
                data infrastructures developed or used by the Commission shall remain under
                PCW control. The Commission shall maintain independent servers or secured
                cloud storage to guarantee integrity, confidentiality, and protection from external
                interference.




16
            v.   Continuity and Institutional Memory. During reorganizations or transitions, no
                 asset, record, or digital file of the Commission shall be transferred, archived,
                 modified, or disposed of without:


                     1. a written directive from the PCW Chairperson;
                     2. audit documentation by COA; and
                     3. secure custody procedures consistent with government records laws.


                 This guarantees preservation of institutional memory and continuity of
                 governance.


SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days from
the approval of the revised organizational structure and staffing pattern under Section 8 of this
Act, the PCW, in coordination with the DBM, DILG, CHR, PSA, DEPDev, CSC, COA, and DOJ,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.


SEC. 17. Transitory Provision. — All references to the Commission in existing laws, executive
orders, administrative issuances, rules and regulations, contracts, memoranda of agreement, and
other official documents shall hereafter be deemed references to the Philippine Commission on
Women (PCW). Such references shall not affect the validity, continuity, or enforceability of any
rights, obligations, powers, functions, or authorities previously vested in or exercised by the PCW.


SEC. 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional,
the remaining provisions shall not be affected thereby.


SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees or issuances, executive orders, letters
of instruction, rules or regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or amended accordingly.


SEC. 20. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
