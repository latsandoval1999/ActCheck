    POWERS AND FUNCTIONS AS THE CENTRAL DIGITAL ENGINEERING,

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The government has a fundamental responsibility to deliver efficient, accessible,
secure, and citizen-centric public services. In our increasingly digital society, it is essential for
the government to design, build, operate, and continuously improve digital products,
applications, and platforms that meet citizens' needs and improve the delivery of government
services.

       Digital transformation is no longer a peripheral function of governance but a core
mechanism for delivering public service, ensuring transparency, enhancing economic
competitiveness, and strengthening public trust. In an increasingly data-driven and technology-
enabled world, governments must adapt institutional structures that are agile, innovative, and
capable of responding to rapidly evolving technological demands.

         Global experience shows that effective digital governments rely on empowered, agile
institutions. The Government Technology Agency of Singapore exemplifies this with its
flexible structure, technical independence, and product-focused strategy for managing
government digital platforms.

        Digital initiatives across government agencies are currently fragmented, inconsistent,
and redundant. Each agency develops and procures its own systems independently, leading to
duplication of efforts, inconsistent standards, cybersecurity risks, vendor dependence, and
ineffective use of public funds. Observations indicate that there is no single entity designated
as the central authority for digital engineering and product development within the government.

        The government cannot rely on private companies indefinitely for the development,
maintenance, operation, and evolution of essential platforms and infrastructure. Government
digital systems carry out sovereign functions, process sensitive personal and state data, and
support the ongoing delivery of critical public services. Relying on private vendors for these
core functions exposes the government to several risks, including vendor lock-in, rising long-
term costs, loss of institutional knowledge, continuity failures, cybersecurity vulnerabilities,
and reduced accountability. Without an internal, permanent digital engineering and product
development capability, the government struggles to effectively manage, secure, and enhance
its own digital assets.

        The Philippines is currently falling behind its regional and global peers in digital
government performance. According to the United Nations E-Government Development Index
(EGDI), the Philippines ranks 73rd globally, significantly trailing behind leading countries
such as Estonia (2nd), Singapore (3rd), and South Korea (4th). This disparity highlights a
structural weakness in the country’s digital governance framework, particularly in the
execution and integration of digital government services.

        International experience shows that successful digital states maintain a clear separation
between ICT policy and regulation, on one hand, and the delivery of digital government
services, on the other. Although the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) effectively manages ICT infrastructure, cybersecurity standards, and
sector regulation under Republic Act No. 10844, the rapidly evolving digital landscape poses
challenges. The DICT was not specifically designed to serve as a comprehensive digital service
delivery agency for the entire government and as a sectoral department for ICT policy and
planning, infrastructure, cybersecurity, and industry regulation and not as a tool for
restructuring public administration.

        E-government is not primarily a technology or infrastructure issue but rather a
governance and administrative reform challenge that requires redesigning processes,
integrating agencies, harmonizing legal mandates, and improving service delivery across the
entire bureaucracy. These functions fall outside the DICT’s core statutory design.

        Moreover, DICT operates as a line department with a vertical structure, while effective
e-government requires horizontal, whole-of-government authority. DICT cannot compel peer
departments, constitutional bodies, or local government units to reengineer workflows or
integrate systems without risking institutional overreach.

        While the recent passage of the E-Governance Act established a national framework for
electronic governance and designated the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) as the lead policy agency, this law did not create a permanent institution
responsible for the actual creation, development, operation, and maintenance of government
digital products, platforms, and shared infrastructure.

      Under the current framework, the Department of Information and Communication
Technology is tasked simultaneously with policy formulation, regulation, and, through the E-
Government Unified Project Management (Egov-UPMO), the implementation of digital
systems.

        The E-Government Unified Project Management Office (Egov-UPMO), established
under the E-Governance Act, remains project-based and transitional, leading to fragmented
portals, duplicated systems, and weak platform ownership. Its role is best understood as a
coordination and transition mechanism and not as a permanent digital government institution.
Hence the need to abolish and elevate it to a Government-owned or Controlled Corporation to
ensure strategic independence, enhance specialization and fiscal stability.
       A critical feature of this proposed bill is the institutional separation of policy-making
and regulatory functions from operation and technical execution.

        The digital governance model of Singapore clearly delineates these functions: the
Ministry of Communications and its successor policy structures is responsible for policy
direction and regulatory oversight, while the Government Technology Agency of Singapore
serves as the dedicated implementation and engineering arm, focused exclusively on building,
operating, and continuously improving government digital platforms.

       This separation enables policymaking and strategic governance to remain within the
department while GovTech operates with a technical focus, speed, and flexibility.

       Adopting a similar delineation in the Philippines, where DICT will retain its role as
policy maker and regulator, and GovTech Philippines functions as the centralized digital
delivery arm, will eliminate role conflicts, enhance specialization, improve accountability, and
ensure the government's digital transformation is both both strategicall guided and operational.

        GovTech Philippines will ensure to create, design, build, operate, maintain, and
continually improve government digital products, platforms, and infrastructure, including
national digital platforms like eGov.ph. functioning strictly as an operational and technical
entity, without regulatory or quasi-judicial powers.

        This legislation adopts the Government-Owned and Controlled Corporation (GOCC)
model to balance operational flexibility with accountability. GovTech Philippines will be
governed by Republic Act No. 10149, under the oversight of the Governance Commission for
GOCCs (GCG) and audited by the Commission on Audit (COA). Additionally, to ensure strong
data protection and uphold privacy-by-design principles, the National Privacy Commission
will serve as an ex officio member of the Board.

       It is imperative for the Philippines to close the digital governance gap to ensure the
delivery of efficient, integrated, and trustworthy public services.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “GovTech Philippines and E-
Governance Integration Act of 2025.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

       (a) Implement and empower a whole-of-government (WOG) mandate to digital
       governance, consistent with the E-Governance Act, ensuring that government digital
       systems and services are interoperable, secure, reliable, accessible, and citizen-centric
       and digital transformation is treated as a governance and administrative reform
       challenge, rather than a mere technical upgrade.

       (b) Institutionalize a “Government-as-a-Platform” (GaaP) model with centralized
       digital engineering, product development, and service delivery capability within
       government to create, build, operate, maintain, and continuously improve digital
       products, platforms, and infrastructure including integrated digital systems, allowing
       the shift from siloed agency systems to a unified national digital ecosystem where
       common services are built once and used by all.

       (c) Address fragmentation, duplication, inefficiency, and vendor dependence in
       government digital systems by adopting shared platforms, a common infrastructure, and
       standardized digital products and services, at the same adopting the "Once-Only" Data
       Principle through an auditable framework where citizens and businesses provide data
       to the government only once, with the State ensuring seamless and lawful sharing
       among authorized entities.
       (d) Ensure transparency, accountability, fiscal responsibility, and performance-based
       management in the delivery of digital government services, consistent with laws
       governing government-owned or controlled corporations;

      (e) Strengthen executive coordination and national oversight of digital government
      initiatives by attaching the GovTech Philippines Corporation to the Office of the
      President;

      (f) Support the mandates of the Department of Information and Communications
      Technology and other relevant agencies under existing laws through a dedicated
      operational, engineering, and product delivery arm; and

      (g) Advance inclusive, secure, and technology-enabled public services aligned with
      national development goals and the public interest.

      (h) In line with Section 6(h) of Republic Act 12254, also known as the E-Governance
      Act of 2025, the government shall aim to attract, develop, and retain skilled
      professionals in digital and technology fields. To achieve this, it will implement a
      compensation and benefits framework that is competitive with the prevailing rates and
      benefits offered in the private sector. This framework will adhere to principles of merit,
      transparency, and public accountability.

      (i) To harness artificial intelligence or AI, data-driven systems, and smart digital
      technologies as strategic enablers of efficient, transparent and responsive governance,
      while ensuring that such technologies are human-centered, accountable, secure, and
      consistent with law and public interest.

      (j) Strengthen public trust in government through efficiency, interoperability and
      innovation.

      (k) Prioritize “Sovereign Digital Capability” by reducing long-term costs and security
      risks by developing internal, permanent digital engineering expertise, ending indefinite
      reliance on private vendors for core sovereign functions.

      (l) Adopt agile and human-centered designs by mandating that all government digital
      products be built using iterative, user-centric methodologies that prioritize the citizen's
      experience over bureaucratic convenience.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:

       (a) Application Programming Interface (API) – a set of protocols and technical
       standards that enable different software applications or systems to securely
       communicate and exchange data;

       (b) Central Digital Engineering, Product Development, and Service Delivery Authority
       – the institutional role of the GovTech Philippines Corporation as the government entity
 responsible for the creation, design, development, operation, maintenance, and
 continuous improvement of shared digital products, platforms, and infrastructure;

(c) Data Exchange Mechanism – a secure, standardized, and auditable framework that
enables lawful sharing of data among authorized entities, consistent with applicable
laws on data privacy, cybersecurity, and access to information;

(d) Digital Platform – an integrated set of digital systems, applications, and services
that enable electronic transactions, service delivery, data exchange, and interaction
among government agencies, citizens, businesses, and other stakeholders;

(e) E-Governance- refers to the use of ICT by the government to provide public services
in a more friendly, convenient, affordable, efficient and transparent manner. Further, it
is the application of ICT for delivering govenrment services through integration of
various stand-alone systems, platforms, and applications between Government-to-
Citizens (G2C), Government-to-Business (G2B), and Government-to-Government
(G2G) services. It is often linked to back-office processes and interactions within the
entire government framework;

(f) E-Government- refers to the use of ICT by the government to enhance access to and
delivery of government services for an efficient, responsive, ethical, accountable, and
transparent government

(g) eGov.ph – the official national government digital platform or portal designated for
integrated access to government digital services and applications;

(h) Government Application – any software application, system, or digital service
developed, owned, managed, operated, or used by a government agency or government-
owned or controlled corporation in the performance of its functions;

(i) Government-as-a-Platform (GaaP) – refers to a service-oriented architectural model
where the Corporation provides a common core of shared digital infrastructure,
standards, and reusable software components. This model enables government agencies
to "plug into" a unified ecosystem to deliver integrated services, rather than building
independent, siloed systems. It is characterized by the use of shared digital identity,
unified payment gateways, secure data exchange, and common cloud hosting;

(j) Government Cloud or GovCloud – the shared cloud computing infrastructure and
services used by government agencies for hosting applications, systems, and data in a
secure, scalable, and resilient environment;

(k) GovTech Venture Sandbox. – refers to a controlled and secure operational
environment managed by the Corporation that allows for the testing and validation of
innovative technologies, business models, or software solutions from the private sector
or academe. It provides a "safe harbor" for experimentation with emerging technologies
(such as Artificial Intelligence or Blockchain) under technical supervision before they
are considered for government-wide procurement or deployment;
      (l) Interoperability – the ability of different government information systems,
      applications, and platforms to communicate, exchange data, and use information
      effectively, securely, and lawfully;

      (m) Legacy System – an existing government information system or application that is
      outdated, inefficient, costly to maintain, or incompatible with current digital standards
      or interoperability requirements;

      (n) Once-Only Data Principle – refers to the digital governance strategy where citizens
      and businesses provide standard information to the government only once. The
      Corporation provides the technical framework to ensure this data is securely and lawfully
      shared among authorized agencies, eliminating the need for redundant data submissions;

      (o) Philippine Government Tech Stack (PGTS)– refers to a modular suite of central
      software components, including but not limited to, national digital identity integration,
      secure payment gateways, and standardized API layers, which are developed and
      maintained by the Corporation as mandatory shared services for all government entities.

     (p) Private Sector Entity – any natural or juridical person not forming part of the national
     government, local government units, or government-owned or controlled corporations;

     (q) Service-Fee or Cost-Recovery Basis – a pricing arrangement whereby fees are
     charged to recover reasonable costs of developing, operating, maintaining, and improving
     digital platforms or services, subject to applicable laws;

     (r) Step-in-Rights. refers to the extraordinary technical and managerial authority of the
     Corporation to intervene in the development, operation, or rehabilitation of a government
     digital project or system. These rights are triggered when a project is deemed critical to
     national interest, security, or essential public services and is found to be failing,
     compromised by a cybersecurity breach, or suffering from significant contractual or
     technical default that threatens service continuity; and

     (n) Whole-of-Government (WOG) Approach – a coordinated method of governance
     whereby government agencies work collaboratively across institutional boundaries to
     deliver integrated, efficient, and citizen-centric digital services.

SEC. 4. Rules of Construction. – This Act shall be liberally construed to promote efficient,
secure, interoperable, and citizen-centric digital government services. In case of doubt, the
provisions of this Act shall be interpreted in a manner consistent with the Constitution, the E-
Governance Act, Republic Act No. 10149, and other applicable laws.

SEC. 5. Technology-Neutrality. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to mandate or limit
the use of any specific technology, platform, software, architecture, or vendor. The GovTech
Philippines Corporation shall adopt technology-neutral, standards-based, and future-ready
solutions, subject to applicable laws and governance rules.

SEC. 6. Creation of the GovTech Philippines Corporation. – There is hereby created a
government-owned or controlled corporation to be known as the GovTech Philippines
Corporation (GTPC), hereinafter referred to as the Corporation, which shall be attached to the
Office of the President for policy coordination and strategic oversight. The Corporation shall
possess corporate personality, perpetual succession, and all powers necessary to carry out its
mandate in accordance with this Act and existing laws.

SEC. 7. General Mandate and Functions. – The Corporation shall have the mandate to
conceive, design, build, develop, deploy, operate, maintain, and continuously improve
government digital products, platforms, and infrastructure, and shall:

       (a) Create, design, and build digital products and applications for government use;

       (b) Manage, operate, integrate, maintain, and enhance government digital platforms,
       including eGov.ph;

       (c) Build, own, and operate shared digital infrastructure, including government cloud
       platforms, middleware, APIs, authentication, and interoperability services;

       (d) Develop and operate secure and lawful data exchange mechanisms, digital identity,
       authentication and authorization systems subject to law.

       (e) Ensure reliability, cybersecurity, accessibility, resilience, and continuity of digital
       products and infrastructure;

      (f) Provide end-to-end digital engineering and product services to government entities
      and, subject to safeguards under this Act, to private sector entities;

      (g) Offer digital products and infrastructure services on a service-fee or cost-recovery
      basis;

      (h) Rationalize, modernize, migrate, consolidate, or retire legacy systems;

      (i) Own and manage intellectual property arising from government digital products,
      subject to applicable laws; and

      (j) Adopt modern, user-centered, and iterative digital product development and
      operations practices and continuously improve smart government capabilities through
      innovation, system modernization and integration of emerging technologies, subject to
      limitation imposed by law.

      (k) Develop and operate government -owned artificial intelligence systems, including
      decision support tools, automation platforms, predictive analytics, and intelligent
      service systems, for use by government agencies in the performance of their official
      functions.

      (l) Design, embed and integrate artificial and data-driven technologies in government
      digital platforms and infrastructure to improve efficiency, responsiveness, accessibility,
      and evidence-based decision making.

      (m) Serve as technical integrator of PhilSys for secure, consent- based digital
      authentication for government services, without creating new biometric databases; and
      (n) Perform such other acts and functions as may be necessary to carry out the purposes
      of this Act.

SEC. 8. Specific Operational Powers. – In addition to the powers and mandate as provided in
the preceding provisions, GovTech Philippines Corporation shall have the following
operational authority:

       (a) Serve as the Primary Technical Evaluator for all high-value government ICT
       procurements to ensure alignment with the Philippine Government Tech Stack;

       (b) Operate a 'GovTech Venture' Sandbox to facilitate the adoption of emerging
       technologies from the private sector;

       (c) Implement 'Step-In Rights' to provide technical rescue for mission-critical systems
       at risk of failure; and

       (d) Act as the National Repository for Government Source Code, ensuring that all
       software built with public funds remains the property of the State and is reusable across
       agencies.

SEC. 9. Technical Step-In Rights. – Upon the recommendation of the Board and approval by
the Office of the President, the Corporation shall have the authority to intervene in the
management and operation of any government digital project or system that is deemed critical
to national security, public health, or essential public services, and is found to be failing or at
high risk of failure. The Corporation shall provide the necessary technical expertise to
rehabilitate, migrate, or integrate said system into the National Tech Stack."

SEC. 10. Smart Nation Infrastructure Program. – As the primary authority for digital
transformation and to ensure digital resilience and fiscal efficiency, GovTech Philippines
Corporation envisions a Smart Nation Program that will:

       (a) Establish the Philippine Government Tech Stack (PGTS) to develop and maintain a
           mandatory, modular suite of shared software components, including national digital
           identity, secure payments, and a unified communications gateway, to enable the
           "Once-Only" data principle.

       (b) Set and enforce government-wide technical architecture and interoperability
           standards to ensure all agency systems can communicate seamlessly through secure
           Application Programming Interfaces (APIs).

       (c) Develop and proposed agile digital procurement and specialized, iterative
           procurement frameworks for digital services to replace traditional, rigid software
           acquisition models subject to existing laws and regulations.

       (d) Serve as the active cybersecurity defense and threat-hunting arm for all government
           digital platforms, complementing the policy functions of the DICT.
       (e) Lead the deployment and management of a common sensor and Internet of Things
           (IoT) platform to provide data-driven insights for public safety, transport, and
           environmental monitoring.

SEC. 11. The Board of Directors. – The powers and functions of the Corporation shall be
vested in a Board of Directors composed of seven (7) members, all of whom shall be appointed
by the President of the Philippines.

The Board shall be composed of:

       (a) the Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications Technology
       (DICT) or his or her duly authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

       (b) The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) or his or her
       duly authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

       (c) The Commissioner of the National Privacy Commission (NPC) or his or her duly
       authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

       (d) One (1) appointive member who is a member of the Philippine Bar, with at least ten
       (10) years of practice of law, preferably in the filed of technology law, digital law, data
       privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual property, or corporate governance, who shall
       likewise serve as the Corporate Secretary of the Corporation; and

      (e) Three (3) appointive members from the private sector or academe with recognized
      competence and experience in information technology, digital systems, cybersecurity,
      data governance, digital infrastructure, or public sector innovation.

The appointive members shall serve for a term of five (5) years, with staggered initial terms
and hold-over capacity, and shall comply with the fit-and-proper standards of the Governance
Commission for Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG) pursuant to Republic
Act No. 10149.

SEC. 12. President and Chief Executive Officer (CEO). – The Corporation shall have a
President and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) who shall be appointed by the President of the
Philippines. No person shall be appointed President and CEO unless he or she:

       (a) Must be a citizen of the Philippines;

       (b) Requires at least twenty (20) years of progressively responsible experience in
       information and communication technology (ICT), digital transformation, and e-
       governance;

       (c) Must hold a bachelor’s degree in information technology, preferably in computer
       science, with a proven track record of at least ten (10) years in a senior leadership or
       executive position within government or technology-driven organizations;

       (d) Should have demonstrated hands-on experience in software development and in
       designing, implementing, or overseeing large-scale government digitalization
       programs. This includes, but is not limited to, e-Government platforms, interoperability
       frameworks, and data-sharing systems, as well as previous involvement in the design,
       development, or deployment of digital platforms, applications, or information systems
       in either the government or private sector;

       (e) Must have a proven track record of leadership and performance in managing
       significant digital systems, platforms, or technology-driven organizations;

       (f) Must meet the fit-and-proper standards prescribed by the Governance Commission
       for Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG).(a) Must be a citizen of the
       Philippines;

The President and CEO shall serve for a term of six (6) years, and may be reappointed for the
same term, unless earlier removed for cause in accordance with law.

SEC. 13. Capitalization and Sources of Funds. – The Corporation shall have an authorized
capital stock of Fifty Billion Pesos (₱50,000,000,000.00), fully subscribed by the National
Government, subject to appropriation by Congress. The funds of the Corporation shall be
sourced from: (a) appropriations authorized in the General Appropriations Act; (b) lawfully
realigned funds, subject to applicable laws and regulations; and (c) income and revenues
generated from services provided to government agencies and, subject to safeguards, to private
sector entities. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as an automatic appropriation of public
funds. All funds of the Corporation shall be subject to audit by the Commission on Audit (COA)
in accordance with existing auditing rules.

SEC. 14. Authority to Provide Services to the Private Sector. – The Corporation may provide
digital products, platforms, infrastructure, engineering, and related technology services to
private sector entities, provided that:

       (a) Such services are ancillary to and supportive of its public-sector mandate;

       (b) Public service delivery is not compromised;

       (c) Terms and conditions are transparent, non-discriminatory, and compliant with
       competition laws; and

       (d) Revenues derived therefrom are used exclusively to support and sustain government
       digital products, platforms, and services.

SEC. 15. Revenue Generation and Financial Sustainability. – The Corporation is authorized
by this Act to operate on a service-fee or cost-recovery basis. This ensures the long-term
stewardship, continuity, and evolution of national digital platforms through various revenue
generation schemes, including but not limited to the following:

       (a) Service Fees for Government Entities: The Corporation is permitted to charge
           reasonable fees to government agencies, local government units, and other
           government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs) for the use of shared
          digital infrastructure, including GovCloud, managed APIs, and the national
          payment gateway.
       (b) Private Sector Integration Fees: Subject to strict data privacy safeguards and
           oversight from the National Privacy Commission (NPC) and the provisions of the
           Data Privacy Act, the Corporation may charge private sector entities for access to
           government-owned digital products or infrastructure services, such as secure
           authentication and data exchange interfaces.
       (c) Ancillary Revenue: Additional revenues may be generated from technical
           consultancy, digital engineering services, and the management of intellectual
           property arising from government digital products.
       (d) Reinvestment of Earnings: All income and revenues generated shall be used
           exclusively to support, maintain, and continuously improve government digital
           products and shared infrastructure.
       (e) Fiscal Transparency: All fees and pricing models shall be transparent, non-
           discriminatory, and subject to the review of the Governance Commission for
           GOCCs (GCG) to ensure they remain in the public interest.
SEC. 16. Investment of Funds. – The Corporation shall have the authority to invest its
generated income and such portions of the Corporation's funds that are not needed to meet
current operational requirements. To ensure the liquidity and safety of the funds, the
Corporation shall invest with the care, foresight, and prudence that a "prudent man" would
exercise in the management of his own affairs.

The Corporation may invest in the following:

       (a) Government Securities: In bonds, securities, promissory notes, or other evidence of
       indebtedness of the Government of the Philippines, or in bonds and securities where
       the payment of principal and interest is guaranteed by the National Government;

       (b) High-Grade Deposits: In interest-bearing deposits and self-liquidating certificates
       of deposit with any government-owned bank or any domestic bank designated as a
       systemically important financial institution;

       (c) Strategic Digital Assets: In the acquisition or development of intellectual property,
       proprietary software, and critical digital infrastructure that provides a long-term
       technical return on investment for the State;

       (d) Digital Transformation Projects: In joint-venture agreements or public-private
       partnerships specifically aimed at scaling the National Tech Stack, provided that the
       Corporation retains control over the sovereign data and core architecture;

       (e) Corporate Bonds: In bonds or other evidence of indebtedness of any stable
       corporation with a track record of profitability for at least the last five (5) years and
       with a high credit rating from a reputable agency.
All income, dividends, and interests earned from these investments shall be credited to the
Corporation's Digital Sustainability Fund and shall be used exclusively for the maintenance,
upgrading, and continuous innovation of government digital services and infrastructure.

SEC. 17. Abolition of the E-Government Unified Project Management Office (Egov-
UPMO) under Section 7 of the E-Governance Act. – The abolition of the Egov-UPMO and
its transition and absorption to the GovTech Philippines Corporation shall be governed by the
following provisions:

       (a) The functions, mandate, powers, responsibilities, programs, projects, personnel,
           and records of the E-Government Unified Management Project Office (Egov-
           UPMO), created under the E-Governance Act, are hereby transferred to and
           absorbed by the GovTech Philippines Corporation;

       (b) GovTech Philippines Corporation shall succeed to and assume all operational,
           technical, and project management functions of Egov-UPMO, including but not
           limited to the planning, development, integration, operation, maintenance, and
           lifecycle management of government digital platforms, shared services, and e-
           government systems;

       (c) All ongoing projects, systems, platforms, and initiatives previously undertaken,
           coordinated, or supervised by Egov-UPMO shall continue without interruption and
           shall be deemed absorbed as projects of GovTech Philippines Corporation, ensuring
           permanent ownership and continuity of national platforms like eGov.ph;

       (d) All references to Egov-UPMO in existing laws, executive orders, rules and
           regulations, administrative issuances, and government instruments shall be deemed
           references to GovTech Philippines Corporation; and

       (e) The transfer and absorption provided under this section shall be without prejudice
           to the policy-setting, standard-issuing, and oversight functions of the E-Governance
           Authority created under the E-Governance Act, which shall remain distinct from
           the execution and operational role of GovTech Philippines Corporation.

SEC. 18. GOCC Governance and Oversight. – The GovTech Philippines Corporation shall be
subject to Republic Act No. 10149, otherwise known as the “GOCC Governance Act of 2011,”
and all applicable rules, regulations, and circulars issued by the Governance Commission for
Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG). The Corporation shall likewise be
subject to audit by the Commission on Audit (COA) and such other accountability mechanisms
as may be provided by law.

SEC. 19. Coordination and Non-Duplication. – The Corporation shall coordinate with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of
Science and Technology (DOST), the National Privacy Commission (NPC), and other
concerned agencies to ensure functional complementarity, avoid duplication, and promote the
efficient delivery of digital government services.
The Corporation, upon approval of the Board, may designate specific digital services such as
Cloud and Identity, Payments as "Mandatory Shared Services," which all government agencies
must adopt to prevent redundant spending

SEC. 20. Market Competitiveness and Benefits. – Notwithstanding Republic Act No. 6758,
as amended, and all laws, rules, and regulations regarding compensation standards—including
the Salary Standardization Law and related issuances—this section covers the creation,
reclassification, and rationalization of both plantilla and non-plantilla positions, including
project-based, fixed-term, and contract engagements for highly specialized expertise. All
actions will be in accordance with applicable civil service, procurement, and auditing laws.

SEC. 21. Technical Specialist Track. – To attract top-tier engineering talent essential for a
"GovTech" model, the GovTech Philippine Corporation will create a dual-career path,
ensuring technical experts, including software architects, cybersecurity engineers, data
scientists, and other identified specialists, to receive compensation and benefits that are
competitive with the private sector, regardless of their administrative or managerial status. As
such, the Corporation shall implement and establish a:

       (a) Performance-Based Incentives based on technical milestones and service delivery
           performance, rather than seniority.

       (b) National Digital Academy that operates as a training arm to continuously upskill
           government employees in agile methodologies, data science, and user-experience
           (UX) design.
SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Office of the President, in coordination with the Governance Commission for
Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG), the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the
Commission on Audit (COA), and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the Implementing
Rules and Regulations (IRR) necessary for the effective implementation of this Act. No
provision of the IRR shall expand, diminish, or contradict the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 23. Annual Report. – The Corporation shall submit an annual report to the President of
the Philippines and to Congress, detailing its financial condition, operations, performance, and
the status of government digital products, platforms, and infrastructure under its management.

SEC. 24. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
