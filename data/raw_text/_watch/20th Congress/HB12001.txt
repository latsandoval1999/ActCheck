                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 23, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides that the State shall
encourage non-governmental, community-based, or sectoral organizations that promote the welfare
of the nation. Furthermore, Section 16, Article XIII mandates the right of the people and their
organizations to effective and reasonable participation at all levels of social, political, and economic
decision-making and directs the State to facilitate the establishment of adequate consultation
mechanisms.

       These constitutional provisions recognize that meaningful public participation is essential to
responsive governance, particularly in the allocation and utilization of public resources.

        The national budget is one of the most important instruments through which the government
translates its development priorities into actual programs, activities, and projects. However, despite
the existence of local development planning and consultation mechanisms, community-identified
needs are not always systematically incorporated into the budget proposals of national government
agencies.

       The absence of a permanent and coordinated mechanism connecting grassroots consultations
with the preparation of the Annual National Budget may result in development priorities being
overlooked, projects being inadequately funded, and public expenditures becoming disconnected
from the actual needs of communities.

        This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize Grassroots Participatory Budgeting (GPB),
formerly known as Bottom-Up Budgeting, as a regular and continuing mechanism in the preparation
of the Annual National Budget.

       Through this measure, local government units shall conduct participatory consultation
assemblies at least twice a year, enabling accredited Civil Society Organizations (CSOs), People's
Organizations (POs), and basic sector representatives to identify and prioritize poverty reduction and
community development projects.
       The priorities identified through these consultations shall be consolidated into Local Poverty
Reduction Action Plans and submitted to the appropriate national government agencies for technical
evaluation and consideration in their respective annual budget proposals. Qualified projects shall be
considered for integration into the National Expenditure Program and, ultimately, the General
Appropriations Act, subject to applicable budgeting requirements and available fiscal resources.

        The measure likewise establishes mechanisms to align community-identified projects with the
annual cash-based budgeting and procurement system, ensuring that proposed projects are
sufficiently prepared for timely implementation. It further strengthens transparency and
accountability by requiring the public disclosure of project proposals, approved project lists,
procurement documents, implementation progress reports, and other relevant information.

       Moreover, the participation of CSOs and POs in third-party monitoring and evaluation shall
provide communities with an opportunity to verify whether public funds are properly utilized and
whether government-funded projects deliver their intended benefits.

        By institutionalizing a bottom-up approach in national budget preparation, this measure seeks
to ensure that government spending is more responsive to the needs of ordinary Filipinos, particularly
poor, marginalized, and underserved communities.

       Ultimately, public participation must not end with consultation. It must translate into
meaningful consideration of community priorities in the national budget, transparent implementation
of approved projects, and accountability in the use of public funds.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
 STRENGTHENING GRASSROOTS PARTICIPATORY BUDGETING, EMPOWERING
 CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS (CSOs), INSTITUTIONALIZING A BOTTOM-UP
 BUDGETING MECHANISM IN DEVELOPMENT PLANNING AND BUDGETING, AND
                  APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Grassroots Participatory
Budgeting Act."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to recognize, protect, and enhance
the right of the people and their organizations to meaningful participation at all levels of social,
political, and economic decision-making. Further, the State shall promote a bottom-to-top approach in
development planning, policy formulation, and governance by institutionalizing direct collaboration
between government institutions and accredited Civil Society Organizations (CSOs).

       SEC. 3. Grassroots Participatory Budgeting (GPB). – Grassroots Participatory Budgeting
(GPB), formerly known as Bottom-Up Budgeting (BUB), is hereby institutionalized as a fundamental
approach in the preparation of the National Budget.

        Under this process, community-identified priority poverty reduction projects shall form part of
the annual agency budget proposals submitted to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM)
for inclusion in the General Appropriations Act (GAA).

         The GPB mechanism shall establish a systematic process through which community-identified
priorities are translated into project proposals, evaluated by appropriate national government agencies,
and considered in the preparation of their respective annual budget proposals, consistent with national
development priorities and applicable budgeting requirements.

       SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act:

(a) Grassroots Participatory Budgeting System – refers to an institutionalized planning and
budgeting approach wherein local development needs and priority projects identified by cities and
municipalities, through genuine community consultations led by basic sector organizations and Civil
Society Organizations, are systematically integrated into local and national expenditure programs;
(b) Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) – refers to any non-government, non-profit, voluntary group
or organized citizens' association operating independently from the State to promote public interest,
local development, and social welfare. This includes Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs),
People's Organizations (POs), cooperatives, faith-based groups, and academic or sectoral associations;

(c) Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) – refers to a non-stock, non-profit voluntary
organization, duly registered with appropriate government agencies, formed to provide service,
advocacy, or development interventions;

(d) People's Organization (PO) – refers to a bona fide, community-based, or member-governed
association of citizens representing basic sectors (e.g. farmers, fisherfolk, urban poor, workers, women,
youth, persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, senior citizens) established to advance the direct
rights and collective welfare of its members;

(e) Local Poverty Reduction Action Plan (LPRAP) or Local Development Action Plan (LDAP) –
refers to the official local government plan containing programs and projects collectively drawn
through a mandatory participatory process with CSOs, directly addressing the needs of marginalized
and vulnerable communities; and

(f) Annual National Budget – refers to the annual national government expenditure program,
including the proposed National Expenditure Program and the General Appropriations Act, through
which government resources are allocated to national government agencies and programs.

        SEC. 5. Institutionalization of Grassroots Consultations. – All Barangays, Municipalities,
Cities, and Provinces shall conduct structured participatory consultation assemblies at least twice a
year with accredited Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) operating within their territorial jurisdictions.

       Priorities identified during these grassroots assemblies shall form the core component of Local
Poverty Reduction Action Plans, Local Development Investment Programs, and annual local budget
proposals.

        The concerned local government units shall consolidate and endorse the identified priority
projects to the appropriate national government agencies for evaluation and possible inclusion in their
respective annual budget proposals.

       The consultation process shall ensure the meaningful participation of basic sector organizations
and representatives of marginalized and vulnerable communities, including farmers, fisherfolk,
workers, women, youth, senior citizens, persons with disabilities, Indigenous Peoples, and the urban
and rural poor.

        The results of the consultations, including the identified priority projects, estimated project
costs, intended beneficiaries, and proposed implementing agencies, shall be properly documented and
made accessible to participating CSOs and the public.

        SEC. 6. Accreditation and Mandatory Representation. – Local Sanggunians shall establish
a simplified, transparent accreditation process for local CSOs. Accredited CSOs shall constitute not
less than one-fourth (1/4) of the total membership of fully constituted Local Development Councils
(LDCs) at the provincial, city, municipal, and barangay levels.

       Furthermore, Local Poverty Reduction Action Teams or local budgeting bodies shall ensure
equal representation between local government officials and accredited basic sector Civil Society
Organization and People's Organization representatives.
        National government agencies shall establish appropriate mechanisms for the participation of
accredited CSOs and POs in the evaluation, validation, and monitoring of community-identified
projects proposed for national government funding.

        The accreditation process shall be transparent, accessible, and non-discriminatory, subject to
existing laws, rules, and regulations.

        SEC. 7. Alignment with Cash-Based Budgeting and Procurement. – To ensure compliance
with the Annual Cash-Based Budgeting System, grassroots community plans and Local Poverty
Reduction Action Plans shall be finalized not later than July thirty-first (31st) of the preceding fiscal
year for inclusion in local and national budget preparation.

       The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the Department of Budget
and Management (DBM), in coordination with the Department of Economy, Planning, and
Development (DEPDev), shall provide technical templates and advance engineering guidance to
prevent procurement and implementation delays.

        The appropriate national government agencies shall evaluate and prioritize community-
identified projects based on their consistency with national development priorities, technical and
financial feasibility, project readiness, cost-effectiveness, and potential benefits to the intended
communities.

       Projects that meet the applicable requirements shall be considered for integration into the
annual agency budget proposals submitted to the DBM for possible inclusion in the National
Expenditure Program and the General Appropriations Act.

        The DBM shall establish appropriate guidelines for tracking the submission, evaluation, and
status of GPB project proposals during the annual budget preparation process.

        The inclusion of a project in an LPRAP or GPB priority list shall not automatically guarantee
its funding or inclusion in the GAA. All projects shall remain subject to applicable budget preparation,
evaluation, approval, procurement, accounting, and auditing requirements.

       SEC. 8. Public Transparency and Monitoring. – All LPRAPs, approved project lists,
procurement documents, and implementation progress reports shall be published on the official
websites of the concerned Local Government Units, DILG, and DBM.

       The concerned national government agencies shall likewise make publicly accessible the status
of community-identified projects submitted for national government funding, including their
evaluation, approval, inclusion in agency budget proposals, and implementation.

         Accredited CSOs and POs shall be encouraged to participate in third-party monitoring and
evaluation of projects funded under the GPB process to ensure that public resources are utilized for
their intended purposes.

       The concerned implementing agencies shall provide reasonable access to relevant project
information and documentation, subject to existing laws and regulations.

        The concerned national government agencies shall also provide appropriate feedback to the
submitting local government units and participating CSOs regarding the status of their proposed
projects. In cases where projects are not considered for inclusion in the agency budget proposal, the
reasons for such non-inclusion shall be communicated to the concerned proponents.
        The monitoring and evaluation process shall complement, and shall not diminish, the
constitutional and statutory functions of the Commission on Audit and other duly authorized
government oversight institutions.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The funding required for the implementation of projects identified under
the GPB process shall be incorporated in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA), through the
respective annual budget proposals of the concerned national government agencies, subject to existing
government budgeting, evaluation, and approval procedures.

The amounts necessary for the administrative, technical, and capacity-building requirements of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned implementing agencies.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Department
of Budget and Management (DBM), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and the National Anti-Poverty
Commission (NAPC), in joint consultation with the leagues of local government units and accredited
national coalitions of CSOs, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

        The implementing rules and regulations shall include the procedures for the conduct of
consultations, consolidation and submission of community-identified projects, evaluation by national
government agencies, integration into annual agency budget proposals, and monitoring of funded
projects.

        SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act shall be declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

        SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and
other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved.
