                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Private establishments have long been partners of the government in
implementing the mandatory discounts granted to persons with disabilities. While these
benefits are essential and must continue to be protected, the financial burden of
providing them has largely been borne by businesses.

       Under the current law, establishments may only claim the mandatory discounts
as tax deductions, allowing them to recover only a portion of the amount they are
required by law to provide. Today, many businesses—especially micro, small, and
medium enterprises (MSMEs)—continue to faithfully honor these discounts despite the
growing financial burden of doing so. As partners of the government in advancing social
welfare, they should not be expected to finance public welfare programs from their own
limited resources.

       This bill seeks to replace the existing tax deduction with a tax credit equivalent
to one hundred percent (100%) of the mandatory discounts granted to qualified persons
with disabilities. In doing so, it ensures that private establishments are fully reimbursed
for complying with the law, while preserving all the benefits and privileges currently
enjoyed by persons with disabilities.

       This measure does not seek to diminish or remove the benefits. On the
contrary, it seeks to preserve these benefits while restoring the original principle that
government-funded social welfare programs should ultimately be financed by the
government itself.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT GRANTING A TAX CREDIT FOR MANDATORY PWD DISCOUNTS,
THEREBY AMENDING SECTION 32 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7277, OTHERWISE
KNOWN AS THE MAGNA CARTA FOR DISABLED PERSONS, AS AMENDED,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        Section 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the rights and welfare of persons with disabilities and to ensure their full enjoyment of the
benefits and privileges granted by law.

        The State likewise recognizes that private enterprises are essential partners in nation-
building, economic development, and employment generation. Accordingly, while private
establishments shall continue to implement the mandatory discounts provided under existing
laws, the State shall ensure that the cost of such government-mandated social welfare measures
is equitably borne through an appropriate tax credit mechanism.

       Section 2. Amendment. The last paragraph of Section 32 of Republic Act No. 7277,
otherwise known as the "Magna Carta for Persons with Disability," as amended, is hereby
amended to read as follows:

       "The establishments may claim the discounts granted under subsection (a),
       paragraphs (1), (2), (3), (5), (6), (7), and (8) as a tax credit equivalent to one
       hundred percent (100%) of the total mandatory discounts actually granted to
       qualified persons with disabilities: Provided, That such tax credit shall be
       creditable against any national internal revenue tax liability of the establishment
       and may be carried over to succeeding taxable years until fully utilized, but shall
       not be refundable or transferable: Provided, further, That the amount of the tax
       credit claimed shall be supported by proper documentation and shall be subject to
       the provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended, and
       the pertinent rules and regulations issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue."

        Section 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance (DOF), the Bureau of Internal Revenue
(BIR), the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), the Department of Trade and
Industry and other concerned agencies shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
the effective implementation of this Act.

       In the formulation of the Implementing Rules and Regulations, the DOF and BIR shall
consult with at least two (2) representatives from duly recognized national business
organizations representing micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and the broader
business community.

       The implementing rules shall provide for a simplified and digitalized procedure for
claiming tax credits, particularly for micro, small, and medium enterprises.

        Section 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

        Section 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

        Section 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
