                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to clean, safe, and properly maintained toilets is a basic public health, hygiene, dignity,
environmental, and mobility concern. Commuters, market vendors, persons with disabilities, children,
pregnant women, senior citizens, and persons with medical conditions are especially affected when
public sanitation facilities are absent, unsafe, inaccessible, or poorly maintained.

Philippine data and studies show that sanitation and hygiene remain continuing public concerns. The
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that in 2024, while 84.7% of Filipino families had access
to basic sanitation service, 10.1% still relied on shared sanitation facilities, 2.7% used unimproved
sanitation facilities, and 2.6% practiced open defecation due to lack of toilet facilities. The same report
also noted gaps in handwashing access and availability of soap, showing the need to strengthen
hygiene infrastructure and practices.

In schools, the Department of Education’s WASH in Schools program recognizes that insufficient
toilets, inadequate handwashing facilities, and poor hygiene practices contribute to sanitation
challenges, illness, absenteeism, and other barriers to learning. A Philippine study on public school
toilets further showed that improving toilet usability and cleanliness requires not only infrastructure,
but also proper operation, maintenance, monitoring, and hygiene promotion. In public markets, a 2025
study on handwashing nudges in urban public markets in the Philippines found that the introduction
of visible handwashing facilities increased handwashing with soap behavior over time, while lack of
consistent water and soap supply affected sustained practice.

This bill mandates minimum standards for public toilets and sanitation facilities in key public places
and requires LGUs and facility operators to plan, maintain, and improve access. It encourages healthy
and hygienic practices among the public by ensuring that sanitation facilities are clean, accessible,
properly supplied, and suitable for regular use.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF ACCESSIBLE, SAFE, CLEAN,
    GENDER-RESPONSIVE, AND WELL-MAINTAINED PUBLIC TOILETS AND
SANITATION FACILITIES IN PUBLIC PLACES, TRANSPORT TERMINALS, TOURIST
  AREAS, MARKETS, PARKS, AND GOVERNMENT CENTERS, AND FOR OTHER
                              PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title.—This Act shall be known as the "Public Toilet and Sanitation Access Act".


SEC 2. Declaration of State Policy— It is the policy of the State to ensure dignified access to safe,
clean, accessible, and inclusive public sanitation facilities as part of public health and local
development.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

1. Public toilet refers to a toilet, restroom, comfort room, urinal, handwashing facility, diaper-
   changing area where applicable, or related sanitation facility intended for public use.

2. Covered public place includes public markets, transport terminals, parks, plazas, tourist areas,
   government centers, public hospitals, schools where open for public events, and other areas
   identified by implementing rules.

3. Accessible facility refers to a sanitation facility designed for use by persons with disabilities, senior
   citizens, pregnant persons, and persons with mobility limitations.


SEC. 4. Mandatory Public Toilet Access Program and Minimum Sanitation Standards. — The
following minimum standards and requirements are hereby established:

1. LGUs and operators of covered public places shall provide or ensure reasonable access to public
   toilets meeting minimum standards on cleanliness, water, lighting, ventilation, safety, privacy,
   menstrual hygiene disposal, handwashing, and accessibility.

2. Each covered LGU shall prepare a Public Sanitation Access Plan mapping existing facilities, gaps,
   maintenance responsibilities, financing options, and priority locations.
SEC. 5. Implementing Agency. - The Department of Health, Department of the Interior and Local
Government, Department of Public Works and Highways, Department of Tourism, Department of
Transportation, and LGUs shall jointly implement this Act.

SEC. 6. Penalties and Remedies. - Any violation of this Act, its implementing rules and regulations,
or any lawful order issued pursuant thereto shall be punishable in accordance with applicable laws,
rules, regulations, and ordinances, without prejudice to other appropriate legal actions.

SEC. 7. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be
charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may
be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA).

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
the lead implementing agency, in coordination with concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the
remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and other
issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
