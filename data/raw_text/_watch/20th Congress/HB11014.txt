                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Artificial intelligence is rapidly evolving from a technology that merely generates
recommendations or content into one capable of acting on behalf of people and businesses.
AI agents can increasingly negotiate terms, communicate with counterparties, execute
electronic documents, initiate payments, and interact with automated systems without
requiring a human to approve every individual step.

        Philippine law already recognizes electronic transactions and automated information
systems. The next policy challenge is to provide greater legal certainty when increasingly
autonomous AI agents perform legally consequential acts: when should an act be attributed
to the person who authorized the agent, when may a third party reasonably rely upon that
authority, and how should accountability be preserved without granting legal personhood to
software?

        This measure adopts an enabling rather than prohibitory approach. It recognizes AI
agents as technological instruments through which natural and juridical persons may act, while
ensuring that an identifiable Principal remains accountable. It establishes a primarily voluntary
Digital Authority Credential framework so that persons and businesses that need greater
certainty may make delegated authority verifiable to counterparties. Mandatory registration is
reserved for narrowly designated high-risk autonomous transactions where existing regulation
is inadequate.

         The measure is also founded on technological neutrality. AI agents may interact with
conventional payment systems, application programming interfaces, blockchain networks,
smart contracts, digital assets, self-custodial wallets, and future technologies, subject to laws
independently applicable to the person or transaction. The Act does not create a new license
for digital assets, does not require the registration of wallets or blockchain addresses, does
not require disclosure of private keys or seed phrases, and does not prohibit lawful self-
custody or peer-to-peer transactions.

       The bill likewise protects open-source and non-custodial innovation by providing that
developers and infrastructure participants are not treated as Principals or financial
intermediaries merely because they publish or maintain software without controlling users'
assets or transactions.

        The objective is simple: to give Filipinos and Philippine businesses a clear legal
framework for using AI agents to transact, while preserving accountability, consumer and third-
party protection, privacy, cybersecurity, innovation, competition, and technological freedom.

  AN ACT PROVIDING A LEGAL FRAMEWORK FOR TRANSACTIONS AND OTHER
 LEGALLY RELEVANT ACTS PERFORMED THROUGH ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
   AGENTS, ESTABLISHING A VOLUNTARY DIGITAL AUTHORITY CREDENTIAL
  FRAMEWORK, PRESERVING ACCOUNTABILITY AND GOOD-FAITH RELIANCE,
 PROTECTING LAWFUL DIGITAL ASSET OWNERSHIP, SELF-CUSTODY, PEER-TO-
    PEER AND OPEN-SOURCE TECHNOLOGIES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "AI Agent Transactions and
Accountability Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the growing use of artificial intelligence
agents capable of negotiating, communicating, executing electronic documents, initiating
payments, interacting with digital platforms, smart contracts, distributed ledgers, and other
systems, and otherwise performing acts capable of producing legal or commercial effects on
behalf of natural and juridical persons.

         It is the policy of the State to foster the responsible adoption of artificial intelligence
and emerging technologies by providing legal certainty as to when acts performed through an
Artificial Intelligence Agent are attributable to the person that authorized such Agent.

       Towards this end, the State shall:

   (a) recognize the use of Artificial Intelligence Agents as technological instruments through
       which natural and juridical persons may perform legally relevant electronic acts;
   (b) ensure that the use of an Artificial Intelligence Agent does not create an artificial shield
       from accountability or confer independent legal personality upon software;
   (c) protect persons who reasonably and in good faith rely upon verifiable manifestations
       of authority given to an Artificial Intelligence Agent;




                                                 3
   (d) encourage innovation, competition, financial inclusion, and the development and lawful
       use of artificial intelligence, blockchain, distributed ledger, cryptographic, open-source,
       and decentralized technologies;
   (e) preserve technological neutrality and avoid imposing regulatory obligations merely
       because a person chooses one lawful technological architecture over another;
   (f) recognize and protect, subject to existing law, the lawful use of self-hosted and self-
       custodial digital asset technologies and lawful peer-to-peer transactions; and
   (g) ensure that any registration or credentialing mechanism established under this Act
       serves primarily as enabling infrastructure for legal certainty and shall not be construed
       as a general license to develop, deploy, own, or use artificial intelligence or digital
       assets.

         Nothing in this Act shall be construed as granting an Artificial Intelligence Agent natural
or juridical personality, constitutional rights, independent ownership rights, or standing to sue
or be sued.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. –

       For purposes of this Act:

   (a) "Artificial Intelligence Agent" or "AI Agent" means a software system that uses artificial
       intelligence to plan, decide, communicate, or execute one or more acts or transactions
       with limited or no step-by-step human confirmation pursuant to authority granted by a
       Principal.
   (b) "Principal" means a natural person of legal age or a juridical person recognized under
       Philippine law that authorizes an AI Agent to act on its behalf.
   (c) "Scope of Authority" means the acts, transactions, limits, conditions, platforms,
       networks, accounts, wallets, assets, or counterparties in relation to which a Principal
       has authorized an AI Agent to act.
   (d) "Registered AI Agent" means an AI Agent voluntarily registered under Section 7, or
       required to be registered pursuant to Section 8, for purposes of obtaining or complying
       with the verification and reliance mechanisms provided by this Act.
   (e) "Digital Authority Credential" means a cryptographically verifiable or otherwise secure
       electronic credential through which a third party may verify the identity of the Principal
       and the relevant Scope of Authority of a Registered AI Agent without requiring
       disclosure of secret authentication information.
   (f) "Authorized Account or Wallet" means a payment, deposit, securities, digital asset,
       blockchain, custodial, self-hosted, self-custodial, or other account or wallet over which
       the Principal has lawful ownership, control, or authority and through which the Principal
       authorizes an AI Agent to initiate or settle transactions.
   (g) "Self-Custodial Wallet" means a digital asset wallet or technological arrangement
       through which a person directly controls the cryptographic credentials necessary to
       authorize transactions without requiring a third-party custodian.
   (h) "Digital Asset" means a digitally represented asset, token, unit of value, or
       cryptographically secured asset capable of being held or transferred electronically,
       without prejudice to classifications established under other applicable laws.
   (i) "Transaction Record" means a secure, time-stamped, tamper-evident, or otherwise
       independently verifiable record sufficient to establish material facts relating to a legally
       consequential act performed through an AI Agent.
   (j) "Material Excess of Authority" means an act undertaken through an AI Agent that
       materially exceeds the Scope of Authority granted by the Principal.

SEC. 4. Legal Recognition and Attribution of Acts Performed Through AI Agents. – An
electronic act, instruction, communication, signature, transaction, or other legally relevant act
performed through an AI Agent shall be attributable to the Principal when:



                                                 4
   (a) the act was performed pursuant to the Principal's actual authority;
   (b) the Principal programmed, configured, instructed, authorized, or knowingly permitted
       the AI Agent to perform acts of that nature; or
   (c) under applicable law, the circumstances otherwise justify attribution to the Principal.

       An act attributable to the Principal under this Section shall be treated as an act of the
Principal and not as the independent act of the AI Agent.

       The use of an AI Agent shall not, by itself, invalidate an electronic contract, electronic
signature, payment instruction, or other electronic act that is otherwise valid under Republic
Act No. 8792, otherwise known as the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000, the Civil Code, or
other applicable law.

      Nothing in this Section shall confer independent contractual capacity or legal
personality upon an AI Agent.

SEC. 5. Transactions Through AI Agents. – Subject to the authority granted by the Principal
and applicable law, an AI Agent may be used to:

   (a) negotiate or communicate proposed terms;
   (b) execute electronic signatures, contracts, instructions, and documents attributable to
       the Principal;
   (c) initiate, authorize, or settle transactions using an Authorized Account or Wallet;
   (d) interact with application programming interfaces, automated systems, smart contracts,
       distributed ledgers, blockchain networks, and other technological systems; and
   (e) perform other electronic acts capable of producing legal or commercial effects.

       Nothing in this Act shall require a human to separately confirm each individual step or
transaction where the Principal has lawfully granted the AI Agent authority to act without such
step-by-step confirmation.

SEC. 6. Principal Accountability. – A Principal shall remain accountable for acts properly
attributable to the Principal under Section 4 and may not avoid such accountability solely by
asserting that the AI Agent acted autonomously, probabilistically, or without step-by-step
human instruction.

        Nothing in this Act shall prevent a Principal from proving that a disputed act was not
attributable to the Principal because it resulted from:

   (a) theft, forgery, or compromise of credentials;
   (b) unauthorized access or a cybersecurity attack;
   (c) unauthorized material modification of the AI Agent;
   (d) fraud or malicious interference by a third party;
   (e) a material failure of a provider, developer, custodian, platform, or other person outside
       the Principal's reasonable control; or
   (f) another circumstance recognized under applicable law as negating authority or
       attribution.

       Nothing herein shall prevent contractual indemnification or allocation of liability among
Principals, developers, providers, platforms, custodians, and other persons, provided that
such arrangements shall not prejudice rights granted to third parties under law.

SEC. 7. Voluntary AI Agent Registration and Digital Authority Credentials. – The
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall establish a



                                               5
voluntary registration and Digital Authority Credential framework for Principals who wish to
provide third parties with a government-recognized means of verifying the delegated authority
of an AI Agent.

      Registration under this Section shall not be a prerequisite to the lawful development,
deployment, possession, or use of an AI Agent, except as expressly provided in Section 8.

        The framework shall permit a Principal to register:

  (a)   the identity of the Principal;
  (b)   sufficient information to identify the AI Agent or class of AI Agents;
  (c)   the Scope of Authority;
  (d)   relevant transaction limits or conditions, if any; and
  (e)   such other information as is reasonably necessary to enable verification of authority.

       The DICT shall provide a secure means for a third party to verify whether a Digital
Authority Credential is valid, suspended, revoked, or expired and whether the contemplated
transaction falls within the relevant registered Scope of Authority.

        The framework shall be technologically neutral and shall, to the greatest extent
practicable,   support      interoperable, machine-readable,  privacy-preserving,   and
cryptographically verifiable credentials.

SEC. 8. Limited Mandatory Registration for High-Risk Autonomous Transactions. –
Registration shall not be mandatory except for specifically designated categories of AI Agents
that autonomously undertake high-value, high-risk, or systemically significant transactions and
for which existing sectoral regulation does not provide an adequate equivalent mechanism for
identity, authority, and accountability.

       Any category subject to mandatory registration shall be designated by the DICT only
through rules promulgated in coordination with the appropriate sectoral regulator, after public
consultation.

       In determining whether mandatory registration is necessary, the regulators shall
consider:

  (a) transaction value and volume;
  (b) degree of autonomous authority;
  (c) potential financial, safety, or legal harm to third parties;
  (d) systemic or sectoral risk;
  (e) sensitivity of the assets or information involved;
  (f) whether an existing regulatory framework already addresses substantially the same
      risk; and
  (g) the burden that registration would impose on innovation, competition, small
      enterprises, and individual users.

       No AI Agent shall be subjected to mandatory registration solely because it interacts
with a blockchain, distributed ledger, smart contract, Digital Asset, Self-Custodial Wallet, or
open-source protocol.

       For AI Agents subject to mandatory registration under this Section, the Principal shall
comply with additional safeguards proportionate to the nature and risk of the autonomous
transactions, which may include technical and security assessment, transaction logging and
recordkeeping, human oversight, reasonable means to review, override, or halt transactions




                                               6
where technically feasible, and other appropriate measures prescribed in the implementing
rules and regulations.

SEC. 9. Good-Faith Reliance on Registered Authority. – A person who reasonably and in
good faith relies upon a valid Digital Authority Credential and a registered Scope of Authority
may rely upon the authority represented therein, subject to applicable law.

Where an AI Agent materially exceeds its registered Scope of Authority:

   (a) a person who knew or reasonably should have known of the lack of authority shall not
       be entitled to bind the Principal unless the Principal subsequently ratifies the act;
   (b) a person who reasonably and in good faith relied upon a valid Digital Authority
       Credential shall be protected in accordance with applicable principles of agency,
       apparent authority, electronic transactions, and other applicable law; and
   (c) the Principal shall retain any right of action or indemnification against any developer,
       provider, operator, malicious actor, or other responsible person whose breach,
       negligence, fraud, cybersecurity failure, or wrongful conduct caused or contributed to
       the excess of authority.

        The DICT shall establish procedures for the prompt suspension, revocation, and
verification of compromised or superseded Digital Authority Credentials.

SEC. 10. Transaction Records and Auditability. – A Registered AI Agent undertaking
legally consequential autonomous transactions shall generate or permit the generation of
Transaction Records proportionate to the nature and risk of the activity. Such records shall be
securely retained, tamper-evident or otherwise independently verifiable, and sufficient to
establish material facts concerning authorization and the transaction.

        A public blockchain record, cryptographic proof, digital signature, distributed-ledger
record, or equivalent verifiable technological record may satisfy all or part of a recordkeeping
or audit requirement under this Act where it reliably establishes the required information.

        The DICT and appropriate sectoral regulators may prescribe proportionate retention
requirements. No regulation under this Section shall require the creation or centralized
retention of information that is unnecessary to establish attribution, authority, or compliance.

SEC. 11. Protection of Digital Asset Ownership, Self-Custody, and Peer-to-Peer
Technology. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to:

   (a) prohibit or restrict the lawful ownership, possession, acquisition, transfer, or use of
       Digital Assets;
   (b) prohibit or restrict the use of Self-Custodial Wallets or other technologies through which
       a person directly controls the person's own assets;
   (c) require a person to use a bank, exchange, virtual asset service provider, crypto asset
       service provider, custodian, or other intermediary solely because an AI Agent interacts
       with a Digital Asset;
   (d) require the registration, licensing, or government approval of a Digital Asset,
       blockchain address, wallet, private key, smart contract, decentralized protocol,
       distributed ledger, or open-source software merely because an AI Agent interacts with
       such technology;
   (e) require disclosure to the DICT or any registry established under this Act of private keys,
       seed phrases, passwords, or equivalent secret cryptographic credentials;
   (f) prohibit lawful peer-to-peer transactions conducted without a financial intermediary;




                                               7
   (g) impose an obligation upon a person solely for developing, publishing, maintaining,
       validating, or providing open-source, decentralized, or non-custodial software where
       such person does not exercise control over the user's assets or transactions; or
   (h) expand the jurisdiction of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), Securities and
       Exchange Commission (SEC), Anti–Money Laundering Council (AMLC), DICT, or any
       other agency beyond that granted under existing law.

       Registration of an AI Agent or issuance of a Digital Authority Credential pertains only
to the authority delegated by the Principal. It shall not constitute registration, licensing,
approval, endorsement, or certification of the underlying Digital Asset, wallet, blockchain,
smart contract, protocol, or technology.

       Nothing in this Section shall exempt any person or transaction from obligations
independently applicable under existing law.

SEC. 12. Protection of Cryptographic Secrets and Financial Privacy. – No person shall
be required under this Act to surrender or disclose a private key, seed phrase, password, or
equivalent secret cryptographic credential as a condition for registration, verification, or lawful
use of an AI Agent.

        The publicly accessible portion of any registry or verification system established under
this Act shall disclose only information reasonably necessary to verify identity, credential
status, and relevant authority.

        Wallet balances, complete transaction histories, private wallet addresses not
necessary for verification, trade secrets, proprietary technical information, and other sensitive
financial or cybersecurity information shall not be publicly disclosed under this Act except
where disclosure is otherwise required by law or lawful court order.

SEC. 13. Protection of Developers, Open-Source Software, and Decentralized
Protocols. – A developer, publisher, contributor, validator, node operator, protocol participant,
infrastructure provider, or provider of open-source or non-custodial software shall not be
deemed the Principal of an AI Agent, a financial intermediary, or a custodian solely by reason
of developing, publishing, maintaining, validating, hosting, transmitting, or making software or
a protocol available, where such person does not exercise the relevant control over the AI
Agent, user assets, or the transaction at issue.

        Nothing in this Section shall exempt a person from liability arising from that person's
own fraud, intentional misconduct, contractual undertaking, or other liability independently
established under applicable law.

SEC. 14. Responsible Configuration and Conflicts of Interest. – A Principal and, where
applicable, a provider responsible for configuring or operating a Registered AI Agent shall take
reasonable and proportionate measures to ensure that the AI Agent operates consistently with
the authority granted to it.

       A provider shall disclose to the Principal any material arrangement known to the
provider, including referral fees, preferential routing, commissions, or revenue-sharing
arrangements, that could reasonably influence the AI Agent's recommendations or
transactions in a manner contrary to the Principal's stated instructions or interests.

SEC. 15. Risk-Based Financial Security. – The DICT, in coordination with the appropriate
sectoral regulator, may require insurance, a bond, reserve, or other reasonable financial
security only for categories of Registered AI Agents presenting a substantial risk of financial
harm to third parties.



                                                8
       Any such requirement shall be proportionate to transaction volume, maximum
transaction value, nature of assets involved, degree of autonomous authority, existing
safeguards, and potential third-party harm.

       Low-risk and low-value AI Agents shall be exempt from such requirement unless
otherwise required under an independently applicable law or sectoral regulation.

SEC. 16. Suspension and Revocation of Digital Authority Credentials. – The DICT may,
after due notice and opportunity to be heard, suspend or revoke a Digital Authority Credential
for material misrepresentation, repeated material excesses of registered authority resulting
from inadequate safeguards, knowing misuse of the credential, or material failure to comply
with requirements applicable to Registered AI Agents under this Act.

         The DICT may temporarily suspend a credential without prior hearing where
reasonably necessary to prevent imminent fraud, theft, cybersecurity harm, or substantial
injury, provided that the Principal shall be afforded prompt post-suspension review.

       A Principal may revoke or terminate a voluntary registration or credential at any time,
without prejudice to obligations arising from transactions validly initiated before such
revocation or termination became effective and reasonably discoverable by third parties.

SEC. 17. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. – It shall be unlawful for any person to:

   (a) knowingly and materially falsify information in an application for a Digital Authority
       Credential;
   (b) falsify, forge, steal, or knowingly misuse a Digital Authority Credential;
   (c) knowingly operate an AI Agent without registration where registration has been
       expressly and validly required under Section 8;
   (d) knowingly use a Registered AI Agent or Digital Authority Credential to commit fraud or
       deliberately circumvent a law independently applicable to the person or transaction; or
   (e) fraudulently represent registration as conferring legal personality upon an AI Agent or
       as constituting government endorsement of an AI Agent, Digital Asset, wallet,
       blockchain, protocol, or commercial product.

       Minor registration, reporting, or recordkeeping violations shall be subject to
proportionate administrative sanctions.

        Criminal penalties shall apply only to knowing or willful conduct involving fraud,
intentional falsification, deliberate circumvention of applicable law, theft or misuse of
credentials, or comparable serious misconduct.

         Any person who knowingly or willfully commits an offense under paragraphs (a), (b),
or (d) involving fraud or deliberate circumvention of law shall be punished by a fine of not less
than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00) but not more than Two Million Pesos
(P2,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more than six (6)
years, or both, at the discretion of the court, without prejudice to liability under other applicable
laws.

SEC. 18. Relationship with Existing Financial, Digital Asset, and Sectoral Regulation. –
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to expand, diminish, or alter the jurisdiction granted by
existing law to the BSP, SEC, AMLC, NPC, or other sectoral regulators.




                                                 9
        Customer identification, anti-money laundering, travel rule, licensing, registration,
consumer protection, securities, banking, payment system, and other sectoral requirements
shall apply only to the extent that they are independently applicable under existing law.

       Compliance with this Act shall not, by itself, cause a person who is not otherwise a
regulated financial intermediary, virtual asset service provider, crypto asset service provider,
custodian, or covered person to become one solely because the person uses or provides an
AI Agent.

        Where an existing sectoral framework provides a substantially equivalent mechanism
for identity, authority, accountability, auditability, or transaction verification, the implementing
agencies shall recognize such mechanism to avoid duplicative regulation.

SEC. 19. Interface with Subsequent Artificial Intelligence Legislation. – Nothing in this
Act shall independently establish a comprehensive system for classifying the general risk level
or permissible uses of artificial intelligence.

       Upon the effectivity of a subsequent law establishing a dedicated artificial intelligence
regulatory authority, functions under this Act may be transferred to such authority only to the
extent expressly provided by law.

        Any subsequent implementation of this Act shall preserve its principles of technological
neutrality, proportionate regulation, legal attribution, protection of lawful self-custody, and
avoidance of duplicative regulation unless otherwise expressly provided by Congress.

SEC. 20. Regulatory Sandbox and Interoperability. – The DICT, in coordination with
appropriate sectoral regulators, may establish a regulatory sandbox or controlled testing
environment for voluntary Digital Authority Credentials, machine-to-machine transactions, AI
Agent authentication, privacy-preserving verification, and interoperable transaction standards.
Participation in such sandbox shall not be construed as a prerequisite to lawful innovation
outside the sandbox where the activity is otherwise lawful.

        The government shall, where practicable, favor open standards, interoperability,
portability, and competition and shall avoid technical requirements that unnecessarily lock
users or businesses into a single vendor, platform, custodian, or technological architecture.

SEC. 21. Mandatory Congressional Review. – Congress shall review the implementation of
this Act three (3) years after its effectivity. The DICT shall submit a report covering:

   (a) adoption of voluntary Digital Authority Credentials;
   (b) categories, volume, and value of transactions using Registered AI Agents, using
       aggregated information where appropriate;
   (c) disputes involving attribution or excess of authority;
   (d) cybersecurity incidents involving Registered AI Agents;
   (e) enforcement actions;
   (f) impact upon innovation, competition, financial inclusion, small enterprises, and
       adoption of emerging technologies;
   (g) whether mandatory registration under Section 8 remains necessary for any designated
       category; and
   (h) whether any provision or implementing rule has unnecessarily restricted self-custody,
       lawful peer-to-peer activity, open-source development, decentralized technologies, or
       lawful Digital Asset activity.

       The report shall include recommendations for amendment, simplification, deregulation,
or repeal of requirements that have become unnecessary or disproportionate.



                                                 10
SEC. 22. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DICT. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred twenty (120) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in coordination with the SEC, BSP, NPC, AMLC,
Department of Trade and Industry, and other appropriate agencies, and after public
consultation with industry, consumer, technology, open-source, academic, financial, and
digital asset stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

The implementing rules shall:
  (a) be technologically neutral;
  (b) employ risk-based and proportionate regulation;
  (c) minimize unnecessary barriers to innovation, competition, and market entry;
  (d) avoid duplicative regulation and recognize substantially equivalent sectoral
      safeguards;
  (e) preserve cybersecurity, privacy, trade secrets, and commercially sensitive information;
  (f) not require disclosure of private keys, seed phrases, or equivalent cryptographic
      secrets;
  (g) not expand the jurisdiction of any participating agency beyond that granted by law; and
  (h) provide reasonable compliance pathways for individuals, startups, small and medium
      enterprises, open-source projects, and other innovators.

SEC. 24. Separability Clause. – If any section or part of this Act is held unconstitutional or
invalid, the other sections or provisions not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
issuances or any part thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SEC. 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
