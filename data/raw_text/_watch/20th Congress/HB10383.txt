                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Elections, as the cornerstone of the democratic process, serve as the
fundamental mechanism through which citizens exercise their sovereignty and hold
leaders accountable. It relies on the informed choices of the electorate, ensuring that
government power derives solely from the genuine consent of the people. However
the integrity and sanctity of the election process is often threatened by nuisance
candidates.


       Section 69 of the Omnibus Election Code defines Nuisance Candidate as an
individual who files his or her certificate of candidacy (COC) to put the election
process in mockery or disrepute or to cause confusion among voters by the similarity
of the names of the registered candidates or by other circumstances or acts which
clearly demonstrates that the candidate has no bona fide intention to run for the office
for which the COC has been filed.


       Notwithstanding this safeguard, nuisance candidates lacking bona fide intent
persist in exploiting the electoral process by adopting names similar or substantially
identical to those of registered officials and incumbents to cause voter confusion.
Additionally, some political actors resort to coercion, threats, and inducement to
secure the filing of nuisance candidates, thereby subverting the democratic process.
Furthermore, bad faith actors leverage their own names or resources to monetize and
abuse the system. Collectively, these practices distort election results, causes voter
confusion, and undermine public confidence in the integrity of democratic institutions.

       This bill aims to expand the definition of a nuisance candidate and penalize
both the malicious filing itself and the individuals who coerce, threaten, or induce
such actions. These measures are essential to uphold the people's right to suffrage and
ensure the integrity of our democracy.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Section 1. Section 69 of Batas Pambansa Blg. 881, as amended is hereby further
amended to read as follows:
       “Section 69. Nuisance Candidates.- the Commission may, motu proprio or
       upon a verified petition of an interested party, AFTER DUE NOTICE AND
       HEARING, refuse to give due course to or cancel a certificate of candidacy,
       if it is shown that said certificate has been filed UNDER ANY OF THESE
       CIRCUMSTANCES:
            1.    To put the election process in mockery or disrepute; [or]


            2.    To DELIBERATELY cause confusion among the voters by the use
                  of   IDENTICAL OR SIMILAR NAMES OF REGISTERED
                  CANDIDATES         OR     ESTABLISHED          OR    INCUMBENT
                  PUBLIC OFFICIALS;


            3.    TO    OBTAIN       MONEY,       PROFIT,      OR     ANY     OTHER
                  CONSIDERATION; OR

Section 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within sixty (60) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Elections Shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.


Section 7. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.


Section 8. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees or
issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.
