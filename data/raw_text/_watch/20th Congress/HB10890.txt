                                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Food does not become less necessary when concrete replaces soil. But as
communities urbanize, the places where people live and the places where much of their
food is produced grow farther apart. That distance makes cities increasingly dependent
on transport, logistics, functioning markets, and uninterrupted supply chains for
something no household can postpone buying.

       In 2024, 62.22 million Filipinos, or 55.2% of the population, lived in urban
barangays, up from 58.93 million in 2020.1 At the same time, food remains beyond the
comfortable reach of many households with 4.84 million Filipinos lived below the food
threshold in 2023,2 meaning their incomes were insufficient to meet even their basic food
needs.

       Urbanization changes the geography of food security. It does not diminish the
importance of rural agriculture, but it makes the resilience of urban food systems
increasingly important.

       Urban and peri-urban agriculture can be a part of that resilience. The Food and
Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) recognizes its potential to improve
access to fresh food, support livelihoods, and strengthen food security and nutrition,
particularly when integrated into broader urban and territorial planning.3

       Fortunately, we are not starting from an empty garden bed. The Department of
Agriculture (DA), through Administrative Order No. 03, Series of 2022, established the
National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture Program (NUPAP) to promote food
production in urban and peri-urban areas, improve access to nutritious food, reduce food
miles and logistics costs, and create livelihood opportunities.

another bureaucracy beside it. It supports household and community gardens, rooftop
and container gardening, hydroponics, aquaponics, vertical farming, controlled-
environment agriculture, and other appropriate production systems. It likewise integrates
food safety, site suitability, environmental protection, structural safety, and responsible
resource use into the national framework.

       As our towns and cities continue to build upward and outward, their capacity to
nourish the people who live in them should not be left behind. Progress may change the
landscape, but it should never put the most basic necessities farther out of reach.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is respectfully urged to ensure that,
where food can be grown safely, sensibly, and sustainably, government can help make
room for it.

                                  AN ACT
     INSTITUTIONALIZING AND STRENGTHENING THE NATIONAL URBAN AND
      PERI-URBAN AGRICULTURE PROGRAM, PROMOTING VERTICAL FARMING
           AND OTHER SPACE-EFFICIENT AND CLIMATE-RESILIENT FOOD
         PRODUCTION SYSTEMS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

            Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1         Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Urban and Peri-Urban
 2   Agriculture Act.”
 3
 4          Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   promote food security and nutrition by strengthening resilient, safe, sustainable, and
 6   inclusive local food systems and expanding opportunities for food production in urban
 7   and peri-urban communities.
 8
 9           Towards this end, the State shall promote appropriate urban and peri-urban
10   agriculture technologies and practices; support household, community-based,
11   institutional, and commercially-oriented food production; encourage the productive and
12   environmentally sound use of suitable spaces; strengthen research, extension, skills
13   development, enterprise development, and market linkages; and integrate urban and
14   peri-urban agriculture, where appropriate, into local development, land use, climate
15   resilience, and food security planning.
16
17           Urban and peri-urban agriculture shall complement, and shall not be used to
18   justify the displacement of existing productive agricultural activities or the unlawful
19   conversion or reclassification of agricultural lands.
20
21          Section 3. Objectives. – This Act shall:
22          (a) Increase access to safe, nutritious, and locally produced food in urban and peri-
23              urban communities;

24   Section 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
25
26   (a) Controlled-environment agriculture refers to the production of crops within a
27       partially or fully controlled growing environment in which one or more
28       conditions, including temperature, humidity, light, nutrients, water, or air
29       circulation, are managed to improve production efficiency, productivity, or
30       crop quality;
31
32   (b) Peri-urban area refers to an area adjacent to or functionally connected with an
33       urban area and characterized by a mixture of urban and agricultural land uses,
34       subject to such operational criteria as may be prescribed in the implementing
35       rules and regulations;
36
37   (c) Peri-urban agriculture refers the production, processing, and related activities
38       involving food and agricultural products in peri-urban areas, using production
39       systems and practices appropriate to the area’s transition between urban and
40       rural land uses, as may be further specified in the implementing rules and
41       regulations;
42
43   (d) Urban area refers to an area classified as urban by the Philippine Statistics
44       Authority (PSA) under its prevailing official urban-rural classification;

 2          (e) Urban agriculture refers to the production of food and other agricultural
 3              products within urban areas using available land, buildings, rooftops,
 4              containers, community spaces, and other suitable areas. It may include crop
 5              production, horticulture, nursery operations, agroforestry, aquaculture, and,
 6              where permitted under applicable laws and local regulations, small-scale
 7              animal production, as well as related postharvest, processing, distribution,
 8              composting, and enterprise activities; and

11              vertically integrated growing layers, whether in indoor facilities, greenhouses,
12              rooftops, or other suitable structures, using soil-based, substrate-based,
13              hydroponic, aeroponic, aquaponic, or other appropriate production systems.

15           Section 5. Institutionalization of the National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture
16   Program. – The existing National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture Program (NUPAP)
17   of the Department of Agriculture (DA) is hereby institutionalized and strengthened as the
18   principal national government program for the promotion and development of urban and
19   peri-urban agriculture.
20
21          The DA shall be the lead implementing agency of this Act. It shall implement the
22   NUPAP through its existing offices, bureaus, attached agencies, regional field offices, and
23   other appropriate instrumentalities, in coordination with concerned national government
24   agencies, local government units (LGUs), state universities and colleges (SUCs), academic
25   and research institutions, and other relevant public and private entities.
26
27          The implementation of this Act shall, to the extent practicable, utilize existing
28   government programs, personnel, facilities, appropriations, and administrative
29   structures.

31          Section 6. National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture Development Framework.

33   concerned government agencies, LGUs, SUCs, farmers and growers, cooperatives and
34   associations, community organizations, academic and research institutions, technical
35   experts, civil society organizations, and the private sector, shall formulate a National
36   Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture Development Framework, hereinafter referred to as
37   the Framework.
38
39          The Framework shall provide national strategies, standards, and guidelines for:
40          (a) Appropriate urban and peri-urban production systems and technologies;
41          (b) Research, extension, education, skills development, and technology
42              assessment;
43          (c) Access to production support, infrastructure, and technical assistance;
44          (d) Food safety, environmental sustainability, climate resilience, and efficient use

 7          The Framework shall recognize differences in land availability, population

20              and

23         Public assistance shall be allocated pursuant to transparent criteria that take into
24   account the nature and scale of the project, its expected public benefit, its viability, and its
25   reasonable prospect of continuity.

27          Household, community-based, educational, demonstration, nutrition-oriented,
28   food-security, and other public interest projects may receive grants or higher levels of

35   comparable public benefits that justify direct public assistance, subject to applicable
36   accountability requirements.
37
38          In determining whether a grant is warranted, consideration shall be given to
39   whether the intended public benefits may reasonably be achieved through cost-sharing,

23          Section 10. Use of Government Lands, Buildings, and Facilities. – National

26   other facilities under their administration that may be suitable for urban and peri-urban
27   agriculture.

34          No government property shall be made available under this Act unless:

36              property is held;

38              government project;
39          (c) the proposed use complies with applicable land-use, environmental, building,
40              safety, and other regulatory requirements and does not materially impair
41              public access or the delivery of government services; and
