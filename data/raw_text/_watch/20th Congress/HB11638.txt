                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The rapid growth of social media has fundamentally changed the way children
communicate, learn, obtain information, and interact with others. While social media
platforms provide valuable opportunities for education, expression, creativity, and social
connection, children are increasingly exposed to online risks, including cyberbullying,
exploitation, grooming, harmful or age-inappropriate content, privacy violations,
excessive and compulsive use, manipulation through algorithms, and artificially
generated or altered content.

        Children, by reason of their age and evolving capacities, require a level of
protection that is different from that afforded to adults in the digital environment. The
State has the responsibility to promote and protect the physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, emotional, and social well-being of children and to ensure that their best
interests remain paramount in all actions concerning them.

       For this reason, there is a need to establish a clear and comprehensive age-based
regulatory framework for children's access to social media. This measure recognizes that
children of different ages possess different levels of maturity, understanding, and capacity
to identify and respond to online risks. Accordingly, the proposed Act adopts three
distinct levels of protection.

       Beyond restriction and enforcement, the bill promotes digital literacy and
responsible digital engagement among children, parents, guardians, and educators. It
seeks to equip children with the knowledge and skills necessary to critically evaluate
online information, recognize algorithmic influence, protect personal information, report
harmful conduct, manage screen time, and engage responsibly in digital spaces. It
likewise encourages local government units to provide community-based recreational,
educational, cultural, civic, and device-free activities that support children's healthy
development.
       Ultimately, this measure seeks to strike a careful balance between protecting
children from the risks of the digital environment and respecting their rights to privacy,
access to information, education, communication, and freedom of expression consistent
with their age and evolving capacities. It does not seek to prevent children from
participating in the digital world; rather, it seeks to ensure that such participation takes
place in an environment that is safer, more responsible, age-appropriate, and respectful
of their rights.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                        AN ACT

ESTABLISHING A MINIMUM AGE FOR SOCIAL MEDIA USE, REGULATING
CHILDREN'S ACCESS TO SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS, PRESCRIBING
SAFETY, PRIVACY, AGE-ASSURANCE AND PARENTAL-SUPERVISION
MEASURES, PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Children's Social Media
Safety and Responsible Digital Engagement Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote and protect
the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, emotional, and social well-being of children
and to ensure that their best interests are given paramount consideration in all actions
concerning them.

The State recognizes the growing role of social media in communication, education,
information, entertainment, and social interaction, as well as the risks that children may
encounter online, including exposure to harmful or prohibited content, cyberbullying,
exploitation, unsafe interactions, excessive or compulsive use, privacy violations,
manipulation through algorithms, and artificially generated or altered content.

Accordingly, the State shall establish an age-based regulatory framework for children's
use of social media platforms consisting of:
(a) an absolute prohibition on the creation, registration, maintenance, or use of social
media accounts by children below thirteen (13) years of age; and

(b) regulated and conditional access for children thirteen (13) years of age but below
eighteen (18) years of age, subject to verifiable parental or guardian consent, active
supervision, age assurance, privacy protections, and child-safety safeguards.

The State shall likewise require social media platforms to adopt reasonable, proportionate,
transparent, and effective measures to prevent children's exposure to online harm while
respecting their rights to privacy, access to information, education, and freedom of
expression consistent with their age and evolving capacities.



SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Age Assurance refers to any process, measure, technology, system, or combination
thereof used to determine, estimate, verify, confirm, or infer whether an individual falls
within a specified age or age range for purposes of compliance with this Act.

It may include age verification, age estimation, parental or guardian authentication,
identity authentication, behavioral or contextual age inference, or other reasonable and
proportionate methods that provide an appropriate level of confidence regarding a
person's age.

(b) Age Verification refers to a process by which a person's claimed or asserted age is
confirmed through reliable and privacy-preserving means.

(c) Child refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age.

(d) Child Below Thirteen refers to a child who has not yet attained thirteen (13) years of
age.

(e) Adolescent User refers to a child who is at least thirteen (13) years of age but below
eighteen (18) years of age and who is authorized to use a social media platform pursuant
to this Act.

(f) Social Media Platform (SMP) refers to an internet-based service, application, website,
network, or computer program that enables users to create, publish, transmit, share, or
interact with user-generated content or communicate with other users.

The term shall not include services primarily intended for electronic mail, private
messaging incidental to another service, or closed educational, governmental, or
institutional systems that do not constitute social media platforms as determined under
the implementing rules and regulations.

(g) Parent or Guardian refers to a parent, legal guardian, or person exercising parental
or substitute parental authority in accordance with existing laws.
(h) Parental or Guardian Consent refers to the freely given, specific, informed, and
verifiable authorization of a parent or guardian allowing an adolescent user to create,
maintain, or continue using an account on a social media platform. Such consent may be
withdrawn at any time.

(i) Parental or Guardian Supervision refers to the continuing ability and authority of a
parent or guardian to monitor, manage, and control the social media use of an adolescent
user, including account settings, privacy controls, content exposure, interactions, and
time limits.

(j) Prohibited Content refers to content that poses a material risk to the physical, mental,
emotional, or social well-being of a child, including content involving child sexual
exploitation, sexual abuse, self-harm, suicide promotion, extreme violence, exploitation,
or materially harmful or misleading information, as further defined in the implementing
rules and regulations.

(k) Algorithm refers to any computational process, automated process, or set of rules,
including machine-learning systems, used to select, rank, prioritize, recommend, or
display content, advertisements, or other information.

(l) Artificially Generated or Altered Content refers to content created, modified, or
manipulated through automated or digital means that does not accurately represent
actual events, persons, statements, or conduct and which may mislead or deceive users
regarding its authenticity.

(m) Personal Data shall have the same meaning as provided under Republic Act No.
10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012.”



SEC. 4. Age-Based Access Framework. — Access to social media platforms by
children shall be governed by the following age-based framework:

(a) Children Below Thirteen (13) Years of Age — Absolute Prohibition. — A
child below thirteen (13) years of age shall be absolutely prohibited from creating,
registering, maintaining, or using a personal account on any social media platform.

No parental or guardian consent, supervision, educational purpose, or other
authorization shall permit a child below thirteen (13) years of age to maintain or use a
personal social media account.

Social media platforms shall implement reasonable and effective age-assurance
mechanisms to prevent the creation and continued operation of accounts belonging to
children below thirteen (13) years of age.

Upon determining that an account belongs to a child below thirteen (13) years of age, the
platform shall promptly suspend or terminate the account and implement reasonable
measures to prevent circumvention of the prohibition.
(b) Children Thirteen (13) to Below Sixteen (16) Years of Age — Regulated
Access. — A child who is at least thirteen (13) years of age but below sixteen (16) years
of age may access and use a social media platform only under a regulated-access
framework requiring:

   1. verified parental or guardian consent prior to the creation or activation of an
      account;
   2. active and continuing parental or guardian supervision;
   3. successful age assurance and parental or guardian authentication;
   4. the application of the platform's highest available privacy and safety settings by
      default;
   5. reasonable restrictions on direct messaging, interaction with unknown or adult
      users, geolocation sharing, targeted advertising, financial transactions, and other
      functionalities that may pose heightened risks to children;
   6. reasonable daily screen-time or usage-management tools;
   7. accessible mechanisms through which the parent or guardian may monitor and
      manage the child's account and safety settings; and
   8. immediate restriction or suspension of the account upon withdrawal or
      invalidation of parental or guardian consent.

The parental or guardian consent required under this subsection shall be periodically
revalidated in accordance with standards prescribed by the Department of Information
and Communications Technology (DICT).

(c) Children Sixteen (16) to Below Eighteen (18) Years of Age — Conditional
Access. — A child who is at least sixteen (16) years of age but below eighteen (18) years
of age may access and use a social media platform subject to the following conditions:

   1. successful age assurance or age verification;
   2. compliance with the platform's enhanced child and adolescent safety
      requirements;
   3. application of enhanced privacy and safety settings by default;
   4. reasonable restrictions on access to prohibited or age-inappropriate content;
   5. safeguards against unsolicited contact, exploitation, grooming, harassment, and
      other harmful interactions;
   6. restrictions on the collection, processing, profiling, sale, or disclosure of the child's
      personal and sensitive personal data, except as authorized by law;
   7. reasonable controls over targeted advertising, algorithmic recommendations, and
      other automated systems that may materially affect the child's online experience;
      and
   8. access to readily available mechanisms for reporting harmful content, abuse,
      exploitation, cyberbullying, and other violations.

For purposes of this subsection, parental or guardian consent shall not be a mandatory
condition for ordinary access solely by reason of the user's age, without prejudice to the
rights of parents or guardians to provide guidance, supervision, and reasonable
restrictions consistent with law and the child's evolving capacity.
Nothing in this subsection shall prevent a social media platform from imposing additional
age-appropriate safeguards that are reasonable, proportionate, transparent, and
consistent with this Act.

(d) Persons Eighteen (18) Years of Age and Above. — Persons eighteen (18) years
of age and above shall not be subject to the child-specific access restrictions under this
Section, without prejudice to generally applicable laws governing social media platforms,
data privacy, online safety, and unlawful content.



SEC. 5. Absolute Prohibition on Accounts of Children Below Thirteen (13). —
No social media platform shall knowingly permit a child below thirteen (13) years of age
to create, register, maintain, or operate a personal social media account.

No platform shall circumvent the prohibition under this Section by allowing a child below
thirteen (13) years of age to operate an account through another person's account, except
where such access is merely incidental to an adult's lawful use of the platform and does
not permit the child to independently maintain or operate a personal account.

Upon actual knowledge or reasonable determination that an account is operated by a
child below thirteen (13) years of age, the platform shall:

      (a) suspend or terminate the account;

      (b) prevent the continued collection and processing of the child's personal data,
      subject to applicable law;

      (c) delete personal data collected in violation of this Act, subject to lawful retention
      requirements; and

      (d) take reasonable measures to prevent the immediate re-creation of the
      prohibited account.



SEC. 6. Regulated Access for Children Thirteen (13) to Below Sixteen (16). —
Social media platforms shall establish a distinct regulated child account for users
thirteen (13) to below sixteen (16) years of age. Such accounts shall, by default:

      (a) have the highest available privacy and safety settings;

      (b) limit contact from unknown persons and adult users;

      (c) restrict direct messaging and other interactive functions that present
      heightened risks;

      (d) restrict or disable precise geolocation sharing;
      (e) prohibit or substantially restrict targeted advertising based on the child's
      personal data;

      (f) restrict financial transactions and in-platform purchases;

      (g) provide parents or guardians with accessible supervision and account-
      management tools;

      (h) provide mechanisms for setting appropriate screen-time limits;

      (i) provide clear mechanisms for reporting harmful content or interactions; and

      (j) provide parents or guardians with the ability to withdraw consent and request
      suspension of the account.

A platform shall not design or configure a regulated child account in a manner intended
to encourage excessive, compulsive, or prolonged use.



SEC. 7. Conditional Access for Children Sixteen (16) to Below Eighteen (18).
— Social media platforms shall establish an adolescent account framework for users
sixteen (16) to below eighteen (18) years of age. Adolescent accounts shall be subject to
enhanced safety protections, including:

      (a) age assurance;

      (b) default privacy and safety settings appropriate to the user's age;

      (c) restrictions against unsolicited adult-to-minor contact;

      (d) safeguards against grooming, exploitation, harassment, and cyberbullying;

      (e) restrictions on exposure to prohibited or age-inappropriate content;

      (f) safeguards concerning targeted advertising and profiling;

      (g) restrictions on the collection and processing of sensitive personal data;

      (h) mechanisms to control or limit algorithmic recommendations that may expose
      the user to harmful content;

      (i) accessible reporting, blocking, and complaint mechanisms; and

      (j) reasonable tools allowing the adolescent user to manage privacy, safety,
      notifications, interactions, and screen time.
A platform may require additional safeguards as a condition of access where such
safeguards are reasonably necessary to protect adolescent users and are proportionate to
the risks presented by the service.



SEC. 8. Parental and Guardian Rights and Responsibilities. — For users thirteen
(13) to below sixteen (16) years of age, parents or guardians shall have the right and
responsibility to provide, withhold, or withdraw verified consent for the child's use of a
social media platform.

For users sixteen (16) to below eighteen (18) years of age, parents and guardians shall
retain the right and responsibility to provide appropriate guidance and supervision
consistent with the child's age, maturity, evolving capacity, privacy, and other rights.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require a parent or guardian to obtain access to
the private communications of an adolescent user except when permitted by law or
voluntarily authorized by the adolescent user.



SEC. 9. Age Assurance. — All covered social media platforms shall implement
reasonable, proportionate, reliable, and privacy-preserving age-assurance mechanisms
appropriate to the applicable age category.

The age-assurance system shall be capable of distinguishing, at a minimum, among:

      (a) users below thirteen (13) years of age;

      (b) users thirteen (13) to below sixteen (16) years of age; and

      (c) users sixteen (16) to below eighteen (18) years of age.

Age-assurance mechanisms shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known
as the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and shall observe the principles of necessity,
proportionality, transparency, data minimization, security, and purpose limitation.
SEC. 10. Prohibition Against Circumvention of Age-Based Restrictions. — No
person shall knowingly misrepresent, falsify, or manipulate the age of a child for the
purpose of circumventing the age-based restrictions under this Act.

Social media platforms shall take reasonable measures to detect and prevent attempts to
circumvent age-assurance systems, including through fraudulent account creation,
subject to data-privacy and constitutional safeguards.

Platforms shall regularly update their systems to address reasonably foreseeable
circumvention methods. The implementation of such measures shall not authorize
indiscriminate surveillance or unnecessary collection of personal data.



SEC. 11. Reporting and Complaint Mechanism. — Every covered social media
platform shall maintain a readily accessible, user-friendly, and child-sensitive reporting
mechanism through which children, parents, guardians, schools, and other concerned
persons may report:

      (a) violations of this Act;

      (b) harmful or prohibited content;

      (c) unsafe interactions;

      (d) improper collection or processing of children's personal data; and

      (e) violations of parental controls or consent requirements.

The platform shall take appropriate action within the period prescribed by the DICT.



SEC. 12. Foreign-Based Social Media Platforms. —

Social media platforms operating outside the Philippines but providing services to users
located in the Philippines shall comply with this Act as a condition for providing covered
services in the country. Such platforms shall:

      (a) designate a legal representative, agent, or point of contact in the Philippines
      authorized to receive notices and legal processes;

      (b) cooperate with Philippine authorities in lawful investigations and enforcement
      proceedings; and

      (c) comply with lawful orders issued pursuant to this Act.
SEC. 13. Role of the Department of Information and Communications
Technology. — The Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT) shall be the primary implementing agency of this Act. To this end, the DICT shall:

      (a) monitor compliance by social media platforms and age-assurance providers;

      (b) establish complaint, reporting, and referral mechanisms;

      (c) investigate alleged violations, motu proprio or upon complaint;

      (d) issue notices of violation and cease-and-desist orders in accordance with due
      process;

      (e) require social media platforms to take corrective measures;

      (f) impose administrative penalties as authorized under this Act;

      (g) issue rules concerning age assurance and child-safety standards;

      (h) coordinate with the NTC, NPC, DOJ, DepEd, DSWD, CHED, TESDA, National
      Youth Commission, and other relevant agencies; and

      (i) submit an annual report to Congress concerning implementation of this Act.

For purposes of coordinated reinforcement, the DICT shall coordinate with: the National
Privacy Commission (NPC) on the protection and lawful processing of children's personal
data; the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) on telecommunications and
technical enforcement; the Department of Justice (DOJ), including its Office of
Cybercrime, on appropriate legal and criminal enforcement; the Department of
Education (DepEd) on digital literacy and child protection; the Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD) on child protection and welfare; the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) and Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA) on digital-safety education; and other appropriate government agencies and
recognized child-rights organizations.



SEC. 14. Disabling Orders. — Upon recommendation of the DICT, the Department of
Justice, through its Office of Cybercrime, may, upon due notice and hearing, issue a
Disabling Order directing a covered social media platform to suspend, restrict, disable,
remove, or otherwise deny access to an account, page, profile, channel, or similar digital
presence found to be in violation of this Act. A Disabling Order may be issued when:

      (a) there is prima facie evidence that an account is being maintained or operated
      by, for, or on behalf of a child below thirteen (13) years of age;

      (b) continued access presents a substantial risk to the safety, privacy, or well-being
      of a child; and
      (c) the concerned platform has been given written notice and a reasonable
      opportunity to comply voluntarily.

The voluntary compliance period shall generally be seventy-two (72) hours, except when
immediate action is necessary to prevent imminent harm to a child.

A Disabling Order shall be temporary and shall not exceed thirty (30) days, unless
extended by a competent court upon proper application.

All disabling orders shall observe due process, necessity, proportionality, transparency,
privacy, freedom of expression, and access to information.



SEC. 15. Cease-and-Desist Orders and Technical Measures. — The DICT may
issue a cease-and-desist order against a social media platform that fails or refuses to
comply with the requirements of this Act, subject to notice and opportunity to be heard.
A platform subject to a lawful order shall implement appropriate technical measures,
which may include:

      (a) disabling accounts of children below thirteen (13) years of age;

      (b) preventing repeated fraudulent account creation;

      (c) restricting access to prohibited accounts or pages;

      (d) blocking URLs or domain names directly used to facilitate prohibited access,
      when authorized by law;

      (e) other reasonable and proportionate technical measures necessary to enforce
      this Act.

No technical measure shall be implemented in a manner that unnecessarily restricts
lawful access to unrelated content or services.



SEC. 16. Digital Literacy and Responsible Use Program. — The DICT, in
coordination with the DepEd, CHED, TESDA, DSWD, National Youth Commission, and
other appropriate agencies, shall develop and implement a Digital Literacy and
Responsible Use of Social Media Program. The program shall include:

      (a) critical evaluation of online information;

      (b) awareness of algorithmic influence;

      (c) recognition and reporting of cyberbullying, exploitation, and harmful content;

      (d) responsible digital citizenship;
       (e) ethical online interaction;

       (f) protection of personal information;

       (g) healthy screen-time management; and

       (h) responsible and safe use of digital technologies.

The DICT shall conduct periodic awareness programs for parents, guardians, educators,
and children.



SEC. 17. Community-Based Recreational Activities. — Local government units,
through their Local Councils for the Protection of Children, shall develop age-appropriate
community-based recreational programs, particularly for children below thirteen (13)
years of age, including (a) device-free community spaces; (b) sports, arts, cultural,
educational, and civic activities; and (c) other programs promoting physical activity,
social interaction, creativity, and child development.



SEC. 18. Administrative Penalties. — Any social media platform or third-party age-
assurance provider that violates this Act shall, after notice and opportunity to be heard,
be subject to the following administrative fines:

(a) For the first violation — a fine of Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00);

(b) For the second violation — a fine of Ten Million Pesos (₱10,000,000.00); and

(c) For the third and subsequent violations — a fine of Twenty Million Pesos
(₱20,000,000.00).

Where the violation involves a systemic or serious failure to comply with the absolute
prohibition under Section 4, the age-assurance requirements under Section 6, or the
enforcement obligations under Sections 13 to 17, the platform may, depending on the
nature, gravity, duration, frequency, and impact of the violation, be assessed an
administrative fine of not less than Twenty Million Pesos (₱20,000,000.00) but not more
than Fifty Million Pesos (₱50,000,000.00) for each violation.

In determining the amount of the fine, the DICT shall consider: (1) the number of affected
children; (2) the duration of the violation; (3) whether the violation was intentional or
resulted from gross negligence; (4) the platform's history of violations; (5) the platform's
cooperation with authorities; and (6) the measures undertaken to remedy the violation.
SEC. 19. Other Sanctions Against Seriously Non-Compliant Platforms. — In
addition to administrative fines, a social media platform that repeatedly and seriously
violates this Act may, upon recommendation of the DICT and after due process, be subject
to (a) temporary restriction of access to the platform within the Philippines until
compliance is achieved; or (b) in cases of repeated and serious violations and pursuant to
applicable law, suspension or prohibition of its operation in the Philippines.

The sanction imposed shall be necessary and proportionate to the violation.



SEC. 20. Liability Under Other Laws. — The penalties provided under this Act shall
be without prejudice to civil, administrative, or criminal liability under other applicable
laws, including Republic Act No. 10173, Republic Act No. 7610, Republic Act No. 10175,
and other laws protecting children and personal data.



SEC. 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in coordination with the NPC, DOJ, NTC, DSWD,
DepEd, CHED, TESDA, National Youth Commission, Council for the Welfare of Children,
and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
the effective implementation of this Act.

The IRR shall particularly establish: standards for age assurance; standards for parental
or guardian authentication; procedures for account suspension and removal; minimum
parental-control requirements; standards for algorithmic safeguards; procedures for
complaints and enforcement; standards for transparency reports; procedures for
disabling and cease-and-desist orders; and other measures necessary to implement the
age-based regulatory framework.



SEC. 22. Periodic Review. — The DICT, in coordination with the NPC and other
appropriate agencies, shall review the implementation of this Act at least once every three
(3) years and submit to Congress a report containing recommendations concerning
technological developments, emerging risks, age-assurance mechanisms, and the
effectiveness of child-safety measures.



SEC. 23. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.
SEC. 24. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.



SEC. 25. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved.
