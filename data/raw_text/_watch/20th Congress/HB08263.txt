                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “Where there is no vision, the people perish.” – Proverbs 29:18

      The digital transformation of governance is no longer optional. It is a national
imperative. At the heart of this transformation are Information and Communications
Technology (ICT) professionals who design, secure, maintain, and innovate the digital
infrastructure that powers public services, national security systems, financial
platforms, data governance, and citizen engagement.

      Despite their indispensable role in sustaining government operations and
advancing national competitiveness, ICT professionals in government service
continue to face structural disadvantages. These include non-competitive
compensation compared to the private sector, limited career progression pathways,
insufficient continuing professional development support, heavy workloads without
commensurate recognition, and increasing cybersecurity and infrastructure
responsibilities without institutional reinforcement.

      Republic Act No. 8439, or the Magna Carta for Scientists, Engineers,
Researchers, and Other Science and Technology Personnel in Government,
recognizes the strategic importance of science and technology professionals.
However, the rapid evolution of digital systems and the growing reliance on ICT
infrastructure require a more tailored and comprehensive framework specifically
addressing the unique conditions, competencies, and demands placed upon ICT
professionals.

     This proposed “Magna Carta for Information and Communications Technology
(ICT) Professionals in Government” seeks to institutionalize a structured and
sustainable support system for government ICT personnel. It provides for clear
classification standards, competency frameworks, structured career progression,
mandatory continuing professional development, scholarship and training programs,
mental health support, fair compensation mechanisms, retirement benefits, and
competitive remuneration aligned with industry benchmarks.


                                          1
      The measure further strengthens the role of the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) as the lead agency in professionalizing and
modernizing the ICT workforce in government, while establishing a Monitoring and
Evaluation Committee to ensure transparency, accountability, and periodic review of
the law’s implementation.

      Recognizing the global competition for highly skilled ICT talent, the bill introduces
an alternative compensation framework that allows exemptions from rigid salary
structures where necessary to protect critical digital infrastructure and national security
systems. It likewise affirms the importance of research, innovation, intellectual
property development, and youth participation in shaping the country’s digital future.

      By institutionalizing protections and incentives for government ICT professionals,
this measure does not merely grant benefits—it safeguards the backbone of the
nation’s digital governance. A modern state cannot function without secure networks,
resilient systems, and competent digital leadership. Investing in ICT professionals is
therefore an investment in national stability, economic growth, and global
competitiveness.

      Where there is vision, there must also be infrastructure. And where there is digital
infrastructure, there must be people equipped, protected, and empowered to sustain
it.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     PROVIDING  FOR   A  MAGNA    CARTA   FOR   INFORMATION   AND
     COMMUNICATIONS TECHNOLOGY (ICT) PROFESSIONALS IN GOVERNMENT

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta for
 2   Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Professionals in Government."
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The foundational contribution of ICT
 5   professionals is indispensable to national progress. To this end, the State aims to
 6   prioritize research, development, and science and technology (S&T) education,
 7   promote Filipino labor and domestic industry competitiveness, and affirm the vital role
 8   of youth in nation-building.
 9
10        The State is committed to actively supporting ICT professionals' welfare and
11   development and seeks to guarantee their rights, provide incentives to boost
12   productivity and innovation, and foster an environment that attracts, retains, and
13   empowers them.
14
15        The State aims to enhance the country's progress and competitiveness in the
16   global digital arena, directly aligning with the constitutional principles of promoting
17   national development and empowering its citizens.
18
19         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For the purpose of this Act, the following terms
20   shall mean as follows:
21         a) Continuing Professional Development (CPD) - refers to the ongoing, lifelong
22            process of actively maintaining and progressively enhancing the knowledge,
23            technical skills, professional competencies, and ethical standards of ICT
24            professionals.
25         b) Government ICT Professional - refers to an ICT professional employed by
26            any branch, department, bureau, office, agency, or instrumentality of the
27            national government, government-owned or controlled corporations

                                               3
 1           (GOCCs), state universities and colleges (SUCs), and local government units
 2           (LGUS).
 3        c) ICT Professional - refers to an individual who, through formal education,
 4           specialized training, relevant experience, or recognized professional
 5           certification, is engaged in activities intrinsically related to and in support of
 6           ICT. This includes roles in software development, network administration,
 7           cybersecurity, data science, artificial intelligence, systems analysis and
 8           design, IT project management, technical support, ICT education and training,
 9           and emerging ICT specializations.
10        d) ICT Professional Organization - refers to any association, society, or institute
11           of ICT professionals duly recognized and accredited by the appropriate
12           government agency.
13
14         SEC. 4. Classification of ICT Professionals. – For the effective implementation
15   of the benefits and incentives provided under this Act, ICT professionals shall be
16   classified analogous to those defined in Republic Act No. 8439, or the "Magna Carta
17   for Scientists, Engineers, Researchers and Other Science and Technology Personnel
18   in Government," as follows:
19
20        a) ICT Specialists - individuals who possess a high degree of expertise and
21           specialization in a specific field of ICT, demonstrated by advanced education,
22           professional certifications, significant experience, and recognized
23           contributions to their area of specialization. This may include cybersecurity
24           experts, data scientists, AI/Machine Learning engineers, cloud architects, and
25           senior software architects.
26        b) ICT Engineers - individuals who apply engineering principles and
27           methodologies to the design, development, implementation, and
28           maintenance of ICT systems, infrastructure, and solutions. This may include
29           network engineers, systems engineers, software engineers, and hardware
30           engineers.
31        c) ICT Researchers - individuals who are primarily engaged in the conduct of
32           research and development activities aimed at advancing knowledge and
33           innovation in the field of ICT. This includes those involved in basic and applied
34           research in areas such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, emerging
35           technologies, and software engineering.
36        d) ICT Technologists - individuals who possess the technical skills and
37           knowledge to implement, operate, and maintain ICT systems and equipment.
38           This may include network administrators, systems administrators, database
39           administrators, and IT support specialists.
40        e) ICT Educators - individuals who are primarily engaged in the teaching and
41           training of ICT-related subjects in academic institutions, technical-vocational
42           schools, and training centers. This includes professors, instructors, and
43           trainers in various ICT fields.
44        f) Other ICT Professionals - individuals providing support services to the ICT
45           Personnel enumerated in the preceding subsections as certified by the
46           Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications Technology
47           (DICT), and as may be further defined in the Implementing Rules and
48           Regulations of this Act.

 1          SEC. 5. Criteria for Classification. – The DICT shall, in consultation with the
 2   Civil Service Commission (CS) and the Department of Budget and Management
 3   (DBM), establish specific criteria and guidelines for the classification of ICT
 4   professionals under the categories defined under Section 4 of this Act. These criteria
 5   shall take into account factors such as educational attainment, professional
 6   certifications, relevant experience, level of responsibility, and contributions to the ICT
 7   sector. The DICT shall publish a Competency Framework for ICT Roles and require a
 8   National Registry of Government ICT Professionals aligned with ICT industry
 9   standards, local and international competency frameworks, and mutual recognition
10   agreements.
11
12          SEC. 6. Structured Career Progression and Opportunities for Specialization.
13   – Government agencies and institutions shall establish transparent and well-defined
14   career progression pathways for ICT professionals, providing clear opportunities for
15   professional advancement based on demonstrated performance, accumulated
16   relevant experience, and commitment to continuous professional development. They
17   shall also actively support specialization in emerging and critical ICT fields through the
18   provision of relevant training programs, mentorship opportunities, and professional
19   certification support.
20
21       SEC. 7. Mandatory and Accessible Continuing Professional Development
22   (CPD). —
23       a) Establishment of a Mandatory CPD Program. - The DICT shall, in close
24           collaboration with relevant professional organizations, duly recognized locally
25           or internationally, establish and implement a robust and mandatory CPD
26           program for all ICT professionals to ensure the continuous updating and
27           enhancement of their knowledge, technical skills, professional competencies,
28           and ethical awareness.
29       b) CPD Credit Units and Recognition of Diverse Learning Activities. - ICT
30           professionals shall be required to earn a specified number of CPD credit units
31           within a specified period for the maintenance and renewal of their
32           professional licenses or certifications, where such are applicable. The CPD
33           program shall recognize a diverse range of learning activities, including
34           formal training, seminars, workshops, online courses, research, publications,
35           and professional practice such as but not limited to the following:
36               i.     CompTIA, Cisco, AWS, Google, ISC2, MI certifications; and
37               ii.    Participation in hackathons, innovation bootcamps, and coding
38                      academies.
39       c) Ensuring Accessible and Quality CPD Programs. - The government shall
40           actively promote and provide substantial support for the development and
41           widespread accessibility of affordable and high-quality CPD programs for ICT
42           professionals throughout the nation, including the strategic utilization of online
43           and blended learning methodologies to reach a wider audience.
44
45        SEC. 8. Strategic Training and Scholarship Programs in ICT. - The
46   government shall establish and substantially support comprehensive training and
47   scholarship programs across diverse and specialized fields of ICT, with a particular
48   focus on emerging technologies and critical skills identified as essential for national
49   development and global competitiveness. These programs shall be made accessible
50   to deserving individuals from all socioeconomic backgrounds, including those from

                                                 5
 1   marginalized communities, to ensure equitable access to opportunities in the ICT
 2   sector.

 4         SEC. 9. Compensation for ICT Professionals in Government. – To recognize
 5   the unique expertise and evolving demands of ICT professionals in government
 6   service, an alternative compensation framework shall be established, notwithstanding
 7   the provisions of Republic Act No. 6758, or the "Compensation and Position
 8   Classification Act of 1989."
 9
10         The CSC and the DBM shall jointly issue regulations governing the classification,
11   compensation, and benefits of ICT professionals, ensuring competitiveness with
12   private sector standards while maintaining fiscal responsibility.
13
14        ICT positions identified as critical to national security, digital infrastructure, and
15   innovation shall be exempt from standard salary grades under R.A. 6758, subject to
16   CSC and DBM approval. Such exemptions shall be based on industry benchmarks,
17   specialized skill requirements, and market conditions.
18
19         The CSC and the DBM shall periodically review and adjust the compensation
20   framework to reflect technological advancements and labor market trends, ensuring
21   the retention and motivation of highly skilled ICT personnel in government service.
22
23         SEC. 10. Standard Working Hours and Fair Overtime Compensation. – The
24   standard working hours for ICT professionals in government shall be governed by
25   Executive Order No. 292, or the Administrative Code of 1987, and by CSC rules and
26   regulations. Any work rendered beyond these standard hours shall be compensated
27   fairly and promptly in accordance with applicable overtime pay regulations and
28   industry standards.
29
30        SEC. 11. Comprehensive Leave Benefits and Entitlements. – ICT
31   professionals shall be entitled to a comprehensive range of leave benefits, including,
32   but not limited to, ample sick leave, generous vacation leave, full maternity leave,
33   supportive paternity leave, and other special leaves as mandated by existing labor
34   laws and the specific policies of their respective employers.
35
36         To ensure fair and equitable compensation, all Job Order (JO) and similarly
37   situated ICT workers who are engaged to perform, and do perform, work beyond the
38   standard working hours shall be entitled to overtime pay in accordance with applicable
39   pay regulations and industry standards.
40
41         SEC. 12. Guarantee of a Safe and Healthy Working Environment. –
42   Employers shall have a fundamental responsibility to ensure and maintain a safe,
43   healthy, and ergonomically sound working environment for all ICT professionals. This
44   includes the provision of necessary ergonomic equipment, adequate facilities, and
45   comprehensive training on occupational safety and health protocols to mitigate
46   workplace hazards.
47
48        SEC. 13. Robust Social Security and Comprehensive Welfare Benefits. –
49   ICT professionals shall be entitled to full coverage under the Government Service



                                                 6
 1   Insurance System (SIS) for government employees, as well as all other relevant social
 2   welfare benefits as mandated and provided by existing laws, rules, and regulations.
 3
 4         SEC. 14. Facilitation of Housing and Transportation Assistance. –
 5   Government agencies and institutions shall facilitate access to affordable housing
 6   options and provide adequate transportation assistance to ICT professionals,
 7   particularly those working in geographically remote or economically disadvantaged
 8   areas, to enhance their quality of life and reduce commuting burdens.
 9
10         SEC. 15. Access to Psychological Support Services. – Government agencies
11   shall ensure that their ICT Professionals have access to the following:
12         a) On-site or partner mental health professionals for counseling and
13            psychological first aid;
14         b) Tele-mental health services for remote or field-based personnel; and
15         c) Peer support programs and mental health champions within the workplace.
16
17        SEC. 16. Highest Basic Salary Upon Retirement. – Upon retirement, the ICT
18   personnel concerned shall automatically be granted one (1) salary grade higher than
19   his/her basic salary, and his/her retirement benefits shall be computed on the basis of
20   his/her highest salary received.
21
22         SEC. 17. Enhanced Role and Responsibilities of the Department of
23   Information and Communications Technology (DICT). – The DICT shall serve as
24   the primary government agency vested with the comprehensive responsibility for the
25   effective implementation and rigorous enforcement of this Act. It shall:
26
27        a)   Proactively formulate and strategically implement policies and
28             comprehensive programs designed to promote the overall welfare and
29             continuous professional development of all ICT professionals in the
30             Philippines;
31        b)   Establish, regularly review, and ensure the enforcement of equitable
32             minimum compensation standards and comprehensive guidelines for ICT
33             professionals across all sectors, in close collaboration with the CSC, and
34             duly recognized relevant professional organizations;
35        c)   Develop, implement, and continuously improve a robust and accessible
36             national CPD program that caters to the diverse needs and specializations
37             within the ICT profession;
38        d)   Actively promote and provide substantial support for the establishment and
39             expansion of high-quality training and scholarship programs in critical and
40             emerging fields of ICT;
41        e)   Facilitate the meaningful and substantive participation of ICT professionals,
42             through their accredited professional organizations, in the critical processes
43             of policy formulation, legislative review, and strategic decision-making that
44             directly impact the ICT sector;
45        f)   Diligently monitor and rigorously evaluate the effective implementation of
46             this Act, identify areas for improvement, and recommend necessary
47             legislative or regulatory amendments to ensure its continued relevance and
48             impact; and

 5        SEC. 18. Vital Role of Accredited Professional Organizations. – Duly
 6   accredited professional organizations of ICT professionals shall play a vital and
 7   recognized role in:
 8      a) Effectively representing the collective interests of their members and actively
 9         advocating for the comprehensive protection and advancement of their rights,
10         welfare, and professional standing;
11      b) Collaborating closely and constructively with the DICT and other relevant
12         government agencies in the strategic development, thorough review, and
13         effective implementation of policies and programs that directly affect ICT
14         professionals;

16         and comprehensive codes of professional conduct for their members, ensuring
17         accountability and responsible practice;
18      d) Proactively developing and delivering relevant and high-quality CPD programs
19         and diverse professional development opportunities that cater to the evolving
20         needs of their membership; and
21      e) Actively participating in the establishment and implementation of fair and
22         transparent accreditation and certification processes for ICT professionals,
23         where such mechanisms are deemed necessary and beneficial for the
24         profession.

26         SEC. 19. Establishment of National ICT Awards. – The government shall
27   establish a National ICT Awards program to recognize and honor outstanding ICT
28   professionals, groundbreaking innovations, and significant contributions to the ICT
29   sector and national development. These awards shall be conferred annually and shall
30   carry appropriate prestige and incentives.
31
32         SEC. 20. Incentives for Innovation, Research, and Development. – The
33   government shall provide meaningful incentives and substantial support for ICT
34   professionals actively engaged in research and development, fostering innovation,
35   and creating valuable intellectual property within the field of ICT. These incentives may
36   include research grants, tax exemptions or credits, access to specialized facilities, and
37   assistance in the commercialization of their innovations.
38
39         SEC. 21. Non-DICT ICT Professional. – ICT personnel not employed by the
40   DICT, but is an employee of any executive, legislative, judicial and constitutional
41   offices, including local government units (LGUs), state universities and colleges
42   (SUCs), government-owned or -controlled corporations (GOCCs) and other
43   instrumentalities, whether located in the Philippines or abroad may also avail benefits
44   outlined in this Act: Provided, That they are involved in ICT functions as provided in
45   Section 4 of this Act and have secured certification from the DICT.
46
47        SEC. 22. Establishment of ICT Scholarship and Grant Programs. – The
48   government shall, through the DICT, in collaboration with the Department of Science
49   and Technology (DOST), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Technical
50   Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and other relevant government

 2   support the education, training, and professional development of ICT professionals
 3   and individuals aspiring to enter the ICT sector.
 4
 5        SEC. 23. Coverage of Scholarships and Grants. – Scholarship and grant
 6   programs shall cover various aspects of ICT education and training, such as:
 7
 8        a) Undergraduate and Graduate Degree Programs. - Scholarships for
 9           deserving students pursuing bachelor's and postgraduate degrees in ICT-
10           related fields in accredited higher education institutions, both local and
11           international, where deemed strategic.
12        b) Technical and Vocational Training. - Grants and financial assistance for
13           individuals undertaking technical and vocational courses in ICT skills
14           identified as critical for national development and industry needs, provided
15           by TESDA-accredited institutions.
16
17        c) Specialized Skills Development. - Grants for ICT professionals seeking to
18           acquire advanced or specialized skills in emerging technologies and
19           strategic ICT domains through certificate programs, short courses, and
20           industry-recognized certifications.
21
22        d) Research and Development Grants. - Grants to support ICT professionals
23           and researchers engaged in innovative research and development projects
24           that contribute to the advancement of the ICT sector in the Philippines.
25
26        e) Faculty Development Grants. - Grants to support the professional
27           development of ICT educators, including opportunities for further studies,
28           research, and training in emerging ICT fields.
29
30         SEC. 24. Funding and Administration. – The funds necessary for the
31   implementation of these scholarship and grant programs shall be sourced from the
32   national budget and may be augmented through partnerships with private sector
33   entities, international organizations, and other funding sources. The DICT shall be
34   responsible for the overall administration and monitoring of these programs, ensuring
35   their effective and equitable distribution.
36
37         SEC. 25. Provision Against Double Benefits. – ICT personnel receiving the
38   same benefits under any other law shall not be allowed to avail of the benefits under
39   this Act unless they submit in writing their intention to withdraw the benefits already
40   being received and opt for those provided hereunder.
