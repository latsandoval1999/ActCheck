                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) stands as a critical pillar in
safeguarding the lives and property of the Filipino people against the
ever-present threats of fire and related emergencies. Over the years, the
BFP’s operational landscape has evolved, with increasing demands for
rapid response, specialized expertise, and effective command
structures. However, the current rank classification and organizational
framework, as provided under Republic Act No. 6975 and its subsequent
amendments, have not kept pace with the Bureau’s expanding mandate
and the complexities of modern fire service.

      This bill seeks to address these gaps by instituting a clear and
responsive rank classification system for the BFP. The proposed
amendments to Sections 58 and 59 of Republic Act No. 6975 are
designed to clarify the chain of command, delineate responsibilities, and
provide a robust structure for career progression and leadership
development within the Bureau.

      The bill introduces a comprehensive rank structure, from Fire
Director General down to Fire Officer I, ensuring that each level of
command is well-defined and aligned with the operational needs of the
BFP. This structure is intended to foster professionalism, merit-based
advancement, and accountability, while also supporting the Bureau’s
capacity to respond effectively to emergencies and disasters across the
country. Furthermore, the bill rationalizes the key positions within the
BFP, from the national headquarters down to the regional, provincial,
and local levels. The bill also provides for the establishment of regional
and district headquarters, in line with the principles of decentralization
and local autonomy.

      The proposed measure is consistent with the State’s policy to
professionalize and modernize the country’s fire service, as originally
enshrined in Republic Act No. 6975 and further supported by
subsequent legislation. It draws from the lessons of recent
jurisprudence, which underscore the importance of proper rank
classification and eligibility in ensuring the effective administration of
the BFP, as seen in Claveria vs. Civil Service Commission, G.R. No.
245457, December 9, 2020.

      In sum, the passage of this bill will empower the BFP to fulfill its
mandate with greater efficiency, professionalism, and responsiveness,
ultimately enhancing public safety and disaster resilience in the
Philippines.

                            AN ACT
     STRENGTHENING THE ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE AND RANK
 CLASSIFICATION OF THE BUREAU OF FIRE PROTECTION, AMENDING FOR
    THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 58 AND 59 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6975,
 OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR AND LOCAL
       GOVERNMENT ACT OF 1990”, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Title- This Act shall be known as the “BFP Organizational
Strengthening and Rank Classification Act of 2025.”

SECTION 2. Section 58 of Republic Act No. 6975 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

"Section 58. Rank Classification.

For purposes of clarity of command and responsibility, and for the effective and
efficient administration, supervision, and control of the Bureau of Fire Protection, the
rank classification of the members of the Bureau shall be as follows:

   1. Fire Director General
   2. Fire Deputy Director General
   3. Fire Director
   4. Fire Chief Superintendent
   5. Fire Senior Superintendent
   6. Fire Superintendent
   7. Fire Chief Inspector
   8. Fire Senior Inspector
   9. Fire Inspector
   10. Senior Fire Officer IV
   11. Senior Fire Officer III
   12. Senior Fire Officer II
   13. Senior Fire Officer I
   14. Fire Officer III
   15. Fire Officer II
   16. Fire Officer I
The rank classifications herein provided shall be used for all purposes related to
command, promotion, and other career-related advancements of personnel in the
Bureau of Fire Protection, as well as for the general supervision and effective response
to emergencies and disasters. The rank structure shall be reviewed periodically to
ensure alignment with the operational needs of the Bureau."

SECTION 3. Section 59 of Republic Act No. 6975 is hereby amended to read as
follows:

"Section 59. Key Positions.

The head of the Fire Bureau with the rank of Fire Director General (FDG) shall have
the position title of Chief, Bureau of Fire Protection. He/she shall be assisted by 2
deputy chiefs, one (1) for Administration and one (1) for Operations, both with the rank
of Fire Deputy Director General (FDDG). All of whom shall be appointed by the
President upon recommendation of the Secretary of the DILG from among the
qualified officers with at least the rank of Fire Chief Superintendent (FCSUPT) in the
service: Provided, that in no case shall any officer who has retired or is retirable within
six (6) months from his/her compulsory retirement age be appointed as Chief of the
Fire Bureau, as the case may be, Provided, further, that the Chief of the Fire Bureau
shall serve a tour of duty not to exceed three (3) years: Provided, however, that in
times of war or other national emergency declared by Congress, the President may
extend such tour of duty.

At the national headquarters, with the rank of Fire Deputy Director General
(FDDG), shall have the position title of Chief Directorial Staff (CDS), who shall
be assisted by the Directors of the various Directorates in the National
Headquarters (NHQ), each with the rank of Fire Director.

The Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) shall construct, establish, operate, and maintain
Regional Headquarters in each administrative region of the country. Each Regional
Headquarters shall be headed by a Regional Director, who shall be assisted by the
following officers: The Deputy Regional Director for Administration, the Deputy
Regional Director for Operations, and the Regional Chief of Staff.

National Capital Region (NCR) shall be headed by a Regional Director with the
rank of Fire Director, the BFP shall establish District Headquarters, each headed
by a District Director with the rank of Fire Chief Superintendent. Each district shall
have subordinate Fire Stations that shall be headed by a City Director with the
rank of SSUPT, City Fire Chief with the rank of SUPT, or a Station Commander
with the rank of Fire Chief Inspector depending on the local government unit's
classification.

At the provincial level, the BFP shall establish a Provincial Headquarters headed
by a Provincial Director. The Provincial Director shall be responsible for
implementing the Bureau’s policies, plans, and programs, as well as monitoring,
evaluating, and coordinating fire service operations across all cities and municipalities
within the province
Fire Stations in Highly Urbanized Cities (HUCs) shall be headed by a City Director with
the rank of Fire Senior Superintendent (FSSUPT).

In other cities and municipalities, fire stations shall be headed by a City Fire Chief
with the rank of Fire Superintendent (FSUPT) or a Municipal Fire Chief with the
rank of Fire Chief Inspector (FCINSP) respectively, as appropriate based on the
local government unit’s classification.

Section 4. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of Republic Act No. 6975, as amended
by Republic Act No. 9263 otherwise known as the “Bureau of Fire Protection and
Bureau of Jail Management and Penology Professionalization Act of 2004”, shall
remain in full force and effect, except those specifically pertaining to rank
reclassification and organizational structure of the Bureau of Fire Protection, which are
hereby amended or modified accordingly by this Act. Any other laws, decrees, orders,
rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 5. Transitory Provisions. In view of the amendments to Sections 58 and
59 of Republic Act No. 6975, the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG) shall implement necessary transitions and adjustments within the Bureau of
Fire Protection, including the reclassification of positions and ranks within one (1) year
from the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in consultation with the
Bureau of Fire Protection and Civil Service Commission, shall promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations within sixty (60) days after the effectivity
of this Act to ensure the proper implementation of the provisions herein

SECTION 7. Separability Clause.
If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 9. Effectivity Clause.
This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or
in a newspaper of general circulation.
