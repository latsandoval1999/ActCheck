EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to basic menstrual hygiene and reproductive health
commodities is not a privilege, but a fundamental human right intertwined
with dignity, health, and substantive equality. Despite this, millions of
Filipinos continue to suffer from “period poverty”—the inability to afford or
access safe period products, clean water, adequate sanitation, and pain
management. This systemic barrier leads to profound physical, educational,
and economic consequences, frequently causing students to miss school and
workers to lose productivity, disproportionately impacting marginalized
women, girls, and gender-nonconforming individuals.

Furthermore, while the Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive
Health Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10354) laid the groundwork for
reproductive health access, significant gaps remain. Gatekeeping practices,
supply chain stock-outs, and a lack of emergency preparedness consistently
hinder the delivery of critical reproductive health items, especially to the
youth and indigent sectors.

This bill seeks to decisively eliminate period poverty and strengthen the
country’s reproductive health framework, serving as a critical legislative
vehicle for fulfilling the Philippines’ commitments to the United Nations
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It advances SDG 3 (Good Health and
Well-being) through universal access to reproductive health-care services and
commodities; SDG 4 (Quality Education) through free period products in
schools that curb menstruation-related absenteeism and dropouts; SDG 5
(Gender Equality) by dismantling barriers that disproportionately affect
women and girls and promoting bodily autonomy; SDG 6 (Clean Water and
Sanitation) by ensuring adequate sanitation and hygiene for menstruators;
and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) by promoting

To achieve these goals, this measure establishes the following core
interventions:

1. Universal, Gatekeeper-Free Access: Mandates the free provision of
period products and reproductive health items in covered public
institutions (schools, health centers, workplaces, transport terminals,
and jails) without requiring identification, proof of income, or parental
consent.

2. Zero-Stock-Out Policy: Requires accredited public health facilities to
maintain a minimum 90-day buffer of period products and reproductive
health items to ensure uninterrupted access.

3. Emergency Preparedness: Directs local government units and disaster
risk reduction agencies to maintain emergency stockpiles, ensuring
that standard dignity kits for evacuees include period products for at
least three menstrual cycles.

4, Sustainability: Promotes the use of eco-friendly menstrual products
alongside proper waste management protocols.

5. Holistic Literacy and Destigmatization: Integrates comprehensive
menstrual and reproductive health education into the national
curriculum for all genders to dismantle long-standing stigmas.

By leveraging existing national budget appropriations and the
mandated 5% Gender and Development (GAD) funds of government agencies,
this measure is both fiscally viable and structurally sustainable. Passing this
bill is a necessary step toward ensuring that biological functions do not dictate
a person’s ability to participate fully in the economic, social, civic, and political
life of the nation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly

sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Free Menstrual
and Reproductive Health Products Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to hold the inherent dignity of every person and to guarantee, without
discrimination, the rights to health, education, information, privacy, and
equal protection of the laws. Pursuant to Sections 11, 12, 13, 14, and 15 of
Article II, Section 1 of Article III, and Sections 11 and 14 of Article XIII of the
1987 Constitution, the State recognizes that access to menstrual hygiene and
reproductive health commodities is a component of the fundamental right to
health and a precondition to women’s substantive equality, the full realization
of the rights of the youth, and the participation of all persons, regardless of
sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, and sex characteristics,
in the economic, social, civic, and political life of the nation.

The State shall therefore ensure that every Filipino who menstruates, or who
requires reproductive health commodities, has universal, free, confidential,
and dignified access to safe, effective, culturally appropriate, age-appropriate,
and environmentally responsible period products and reproductive health
items, especially in times of ordinary life, in public institutions, and during
emergencies, natural disasters, and armed conflict.

SEC. 4. Universal Right to Period Products and Reproductive Health
Items; Non-Discrimination. — Every person in the Philippines shall have the
right to receive, without cost, period products and reproductive health items
at any Covered Institution. Such right shall be enjoyed without discrimination
on account of sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, sex
characteristics, age, civil status, religion, political belief, disability, ethnicity,
indigenous identity, socioeconomic status, HIV status, nationality,
immigration status, or nature of work.

No identification document, proof of income, parental or spousal
consent, prescription, registration, or other form of gatekeeping shall be
required as a pre-condition for obtaining period products. Reproductive
health items shall be dispensed in accordance with existing laws and medical
protocols: Provided, That no minor shall be denied access to information or
counseling on reproductive health and that no person shall be deprived of
emergency reproductive health care on account of age, marital status, or
absence of parental or spousal consent.

SEC. 5. Free Period Products in Covered Institutions. - All Covered
Institutions shall make period products available, free of charge, in accessible,
clearly marked, and adequately private locations, including but not limited to
restrooms, clinic rooms, guidance offices, and similar spaces. Period products
shall be available at all times during the normal operating hours of the
Covered Institution, and in sufficient variety (disposable, reusable, and
sustainable options) to allow the user to choose a product that meets her
health, comfort, and religious or cultural needs. In public elementary and
secondary schools, a designated menstrual-hygiene focal point, which may be
the school nurse, a female guidance counselor, or a barangay health worker,
shall be available to assist learners in accessing period products with privacy
and without embarrassment. No learner shall be required to seek permission
from, or make a written request to, male personnel to obtain period products.

SEC. 6. Free Reproductive Health Items. - All Covered Institutions with a
health-service function, including barangay health stations, rural health
units, city and municipal health offices, DOH-retained hospitals, district
hospitals, provincial hospitals, and public-sector employee clinics, shall
dispense, free of charge and in accordance with medical protocols, the full
range of Reproductive Health Items defined in Section 3(c) of this Act. All
accredited public health facilities shall observe the Zero-Stock-Out Policy.
Private health facilities receiving accreditation from the Philippine Health

SEC. 8. Emergency Stockpiles. - In recognition of the fact that natural
disasters, public-health emergencies, and armed conflict disproportionately
affect women, girls, and other menstruators, all LGUs, in coordination with
the Office of Civil Defense and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Council (NDRRMC}, shall maintain menstrual-hygiene and
reproductive-health emergency stockpiles in every evacuation center, relief-
operations hub, and pre-positioned warehouse. Standard dignity-kit contents
shall include, at a minimum, period products for three (3) menstrual cycles,
pain-relief medication, wean undergarments, soap, and information materials
in the local dialect.

SEC. 9. Sustainability and Environmental Considerations. - The DOH,
in coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
(DENR) and the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), shall promote
the use of reusable, biodegradable, and environmentally responsible
menstrual products, such as reusable pads, period underwear, and
menstrual cups, and shall support research and development of locally
appropriate sustainable alternatives. Covered Institutions shall ensure proper
disposal of single-use period products, the availability of menstrual-waste
receptacles in all gender-inclusive restrooms, and menstruation-sensitive
solid-waste management consistent with Republic Act No. 9003 (the
Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000).

SEC. 10. Information, Education, and Communication; Menstrual and
Reproductive-Health Literacy. - DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and_ the
Commission on Higher Education shall integrate menstrual health and
reproductive-health literacy, including the physiology of menstruation, pain
management, proper product use, the right to freedom from menstrual
stigma, consent and bodily STI prevention, and responsible parenthood, into
the age- development-appropriate reproductive health education mandated
by Republic Act No. 10354. The DOH and the Philippine Information Agency
shall implement a sustained nationwide information campaign, including in
Filipino sign language and in at least the eight (8) major Philippine languages.

Menstrual and reproductive-health education shall be delivered to all
learners, regardless of sex or gender, and shall address the role of boys, men
and gender-nonconforming persons in dismantling stigma.

SEC. 11. Confidentiality and Date Privacy. — All personal data collected in
connection with the receipt of period products or reproductive health items
shall be treated, with the highest level of confidentiality, consistent with
Republic Act No. 10173 (the Data Privacy Act of 2012). The receipt of such
items shall not be recorded in any manner that identifies the recipient’s free
and informed consent; in the case of minors, with appropriate age-sensitive
protocols.

SEC. 12. Integration with Republic Act No. 10354 and other Laws. —This
Act shall read in pari materia with, and is hereby integrated into, the
framework of Republic Act No. 10354. The rights and services under this Act
are in addition to , and not in derogation of, those provided under Republic
Act No. 9710 (Magna Carta of Women), Republic Act No. 11148 (Kalusugan at
Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act), Republic Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care
Act}, Republic Act No. 11210 (105-Day Expanded Maternity Leave Law),
Republic Act No. 11166 (Philippine HIV and AIDS Policy Act), and all other
laws enhancing the right to health and gender equality.

SEC. 13. Reporting, Monitoring, and Citizen Oversight. —The DOH shall
submit annually to the President of the Philippines and to both Houses of
Congress a consolidated annual report on the implementation of this Act,
which shall be publicly available online.

SEC. 14. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charges against the current year’s
appropriations of the DOH, DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and the Philippine
Commission on Women. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriation Act under the budgets of the said agencies. The Gender and
Development (GAD) funds of all national government agencies and LGUs,
equivalent to at least five percent (5%) of their total budgets under the Magna
Carta of Women, may be used as a source of funding for this Act.

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, as lead agency, in consultation with
DepEd, CHED, TESDA, DBM, DILG, DSWD, DENR, DOST, the Department of
Labor and Employment, the Bureau of Internal Revenue, the Philippine
Commission on Women, the National Youth Commission, the National Anti-
Poverty Commission, LGU leagues, and accredited civil-society organizations
representing women, youth, LGBTQI+ persons, persons with disabilities, and
indigenous peoples, shall issue the implementing rules and regulations for
this Act.

SEC. 16. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules, regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, modified, or amended accordingly.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or any two (2) newspapers of general
circulation in the Philippines.
