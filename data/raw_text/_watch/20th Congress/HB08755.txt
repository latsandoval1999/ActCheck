                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid expansion of e-commerce has significantly improved access to
  goods and services, including health products. However, it has also facilitated the
  proliferation of counterfeit, unregistered, adulterated, and otherwise unsafe
  health products sold through online platforms, posing serious risks to public
  health and safety.

      While Republic Act No. 9711 (Food and Drug Administration Act of 2009) and
  Republic Act No. 11967 (Internet Transactions Act) provide important regulatory
  frameworks, gaps remain in addressing the unique challenges of online health
  product transactions. In particular, there is a need for clearer regulatory
  authority, stronger enforcement mechanisms, and defined accountability for
  digital platforms that enable such transactions.

      This bill seeks to strengthen the regulatory framework by reinforcing the
  authority of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) over online health product
  transactions, in coordination with relevant agencies. It imposes enhanced
  obligations on digital platforms, including verification of FDA authorizations,
  monitoring of listings, traceability, and timely takedown of violative products.

      The measure likewise establishes platform accountability by providing for
  solidary liability with sellers upon a showing of fault or negligence, ensuring that
  platforms that allow or fail to act against the sale of illicit health products may be
  held accountable.

     By strengthening and harmonizing Republic Act No. 9711 and Republic Act
  No. 11967, this measure promotes a safer and more accountable digital
  marketplace for health products and upholds the State’s duty to protect public
  health.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           “AN ACT
   STRENGTHENING THE REGULATION OF ONLINE TRANSACTIONS
   INVOLVING HEALTH PRODUCTS, EXPANDING THE POWERS OF THE
   FOOD AND DRUG ADMINISTRATION UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO.
   9711, AND ENHANCING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11967 (INTERNET
   TRANSACTIONS   ACT),  IMPOSING  LIABILITY  ON  DIGITAL
   PLATFORMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”


SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Online Health
Products Safety and Accountability Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to:

   a. Protect the public from unsafe, counterfeit, adulterated, falsified,
      unregistered, and violative health products sold through digital platforms;
   b. Ensure that online commerce involving health products is subject to the
      same or higher level of regulatory control as physical distribution;
   c. Strengthen the authority of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as
      the primary regulatory authority over health products in online
      transactions;
   d. Enhance Republic Act No. 11967 (Internet Transactions Act) by
      establishing heightened obligations and liabilities for digital platforms
      engaged in health product commerce.

SECTION 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all health products
regulated under Republic Act No. 9711, including:

   a. Food
   b. Drugs and medicines
   c. Biological products and vaccines
   d. Medical devices
   e. In vitro diagnostic products
   f. Cosmetics
   g. Veterinary drugs and products
   h. Household/urban hazardous substances regulated by the FDA

      This Act shall not apply to non-FDA-regulated consumer goods.


SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

   a. Online Health Product Transaction – Any act of offering, listing,
      advertising, selling, distributing, or facilitating the sale of FDA-regulated
      health products through electronic, digital, or online means.

   b. Digital Platform – Any online marketplace, e-commerce platform, website,
      application, social media platform, or intermediary service that enables or
      facilitates online health product transactions.

   c. Illicit Health Products – Health products that are counterfeit, falsified,
      adulterated, misbranded, unregistered, expired, recalled, prohibited, or
      otherwise violative of FDA laws and regulations.

   d. Platform Operator – Any natural or juridical person that owns, controls,
      manages, or operates a digital platform accessible to Philippine
      consumers.

SECTION 5. Primary Regulatory Authority. - The Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) shall have primary jurisdiction over the regulation of
health products in online transactions and shall exercise such authority in
coordination with relevant government agencies, including but not limited to the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), and the National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC).

      The FDA shall have the authority to:

   a. Regulate online health product transactions;
   b. Supervise, inspect, investigate, and monitor digital platforms selling
      health products;
   c. Issue binding compliance orders, takedown orders, cease-and-desist
      orders, and platform sanctions;
   d. Impose administrative penalties and recommend criminal prosecution.

      All powers granted under this Act shall be in addition to those already
provided under Republic Act No. 9711.
SECTION 6. Enhanced Obligations of Digital Platforms. - In addition to
obligations under Republic Act No. 11967, digital platforms that allow the sale
of FDA-regulated health products shall be required to:

   a. Seller and Product Verification- Verify FDA authorizations (CPR, LTO,
      CMDR,      and     other    relevant  certifications) prior   to listing;
      Prohibit listings of health products without valid FDA approval.

   b. Continuous Compliance Monitoring- Implement automated and manual
      monitoring      systems     to     detect    illegal   health     products;
      Prevent relisting of previously removed violative products or sellers.

   c. Traceability and Recordkeeping- Maintain transaction, seller, and
      product records for at least five (5) years, accessible to the FDA upon lawful
      request.

   d. Immediate Takedown Obligation- Remove or disable access to violative
      listings within a reasonable period not exceeding forty-eight (48) hours,
      upon FDA notice, unless a shorter period is required due to imminent
      public health risk, and without the need for a prior court order as may be
      allowed by law.

   e. Consumer          Notification-    Notify     affected     consumers       of:

                   i.    FDA advisories
                  ii.    Product recalls
                 iii.    Safety alerts
                 iv.     Enforcement actions

SECTION 7. FDA Emergency Powers Over Online Platforms - Where public
health is at imminent risk, the FDA may:

 a.   Order immediate takedown of listings or specific platform sections;
 b.   Recommend to appropriate government agencies the restriction of access
      to non-compliant platforms;
 c.   Freeze digital listings and online advertisements pending investigation.

      All actions under this section shall be exercised subject to due process
and in coordination with appropriate government agencies.

SECTION 8. Platform Liability - Digital platforms may be held solidarily liable
with sellers upon clear showing of fault or negligence, including when they:

   a. Knowingly allow the sale of illicit health products;
   b. Fail to act after receiving FDA notice;
   c. Profit from the continued availability of violative listings;
   d. Systematically fail to implement reasonable compliance safeguards.

        This Section shall be deemed a supplement to, and shall strengthen, the
liability framework under Republic Act No. 11967 with respect to online
transactions involving health products.

SECTION 9. Prohibited Acts. - It shall be unlawful to:

   a. Offer or sell FDA-regulated health products online without valid
      authorization from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), including the
      appropriate License to Operate (LTO), Certificate of Product Registration
      (CPR), Certificate of Medical Device Registration (CMDR), or such other
      authorization as may be required under existing laws, rules, and
      regulations;
   b. Advertise health products with false, misleading, or unapproved claims;
   c. Circumvent FDA monitoring or takedown orders;
   d. Facilitate anonymous or untraceable sales of health products;
   e. Obstruct FDA inspections or investigations.

SECTION 10. Penalties. -

   A. Digital Platforms. -

        i.   Fine of ₱500,000 to ₱10,000,000 per violation depending on gravity,
             frequency, and public health impact;
       ii.   Suspension or restriction of platform operations involving health
             products;
      iii.   Recommendation for ISP or app store delisting, subject to applicable
             laws.

   B. Sellers. –

        i.   Fine of ₱100,000 to ₱5,000,000, per violation depending on gravity,
             frequency, and public health impact;
       ii.   License revocation;
      iii.   Criminal prosecution under Republic Act No. 9711 and related laws.

      Each violative listing may be treated as a separate offense, subject to the
discretion of the FDA based on the circumstances of the case.

SECTION 11. Relationship with Republic Act No. 11967 (Internet
Transactions Act). - This Act shall be construed as a special law that
supplements and strengthens Republic Act No. 11967 with respect to online
transactions involving FDA-regulated health products.
       In case of conflict, the provisions of this Act and Republic Act No. 9711
shall prevail with respect to online transactions involving health products.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the FDA, in coordination with the DTI, DICT, NTC,
and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall not be affected
thereby.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
