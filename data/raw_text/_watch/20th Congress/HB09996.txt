                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
The integrity of the Philippine capital market relies entirely on public trust and investor
confidence. For the market to thrive, investors must believe that everyone is playing by
the same rules. At present, Sections 3.8 and 27 of Republic Act No. 8799, otherwise
known as the Securities Regulation Code (SRC), define an "insider" to include
individuals with a direct relationship to the company such as directors, officers,
controlling shareholders, or individuals who obtain material non-public information
directly from these sources.

However, this narrow definition leaves a dangerous regulatory blind spot. It cannot
punish third-party bad actors who steal confidential, market-moving data from their own
employers or clients to trade stocks, simply because they have no direct relationship with
the company being traded. This loophole undermines market integrity and is insufficient
to protect the investing public—a legal deficiency that international jurisdictions
successfully addressed through the Misappropriation Theory.

This Act adopts the Misappropriation Theory which anchors liability on the breach of a
duty of loyalty and confidentiality owed to the source of the information, rather than to
the corporation whose shares are being traded.

By expanding the definition of an insider to include anyone who misappropriates material
non-public information in breach of a duty of trust or confidence, this Act ensures that no
one can legally profit from stolen secrets, and more importantly, safeguards the
Philippine capital market for all investors.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT EXPANDING THE SCOPE OF INSIDER TRADING LIABILITY,
AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE SECTIONS 3.8 AND 27 OF REPUBLIC ACT
NO. 8799, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE SECURITIES REGULATION CODE,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as "The Capital Market
Misappropriation and Insider Trading Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to establish a socially
conscious and free market system that regulates itself, encourages the widest participation
of ownership in enterprises, and protects the investing public. To this end, the State shall
eliminate fraudulent, deceptive, and manipulative practices in the capital markets,
specifically ensuring that no person shall unfairly profit from the deceptive use of
material non-public information obtained in breach of any duty of trust, confidence, or
loyalty.

SECTION 3. Amendment of Section 3.8 of Republic Act No. 8799. – Section 3.8 of
Republic Act No. 8799 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 3. Definition of Terms Used in Rules and Regulations. – As used in
      this Code, unless the context otherwise requires:

      xxx xxx xxx

      3.8. 'Insider' means: (a) the issuer; (b) a director or officer (or person
      performing similar functions) of, or a person controlling the issuer; (c) a
      person whose relationship or former relationship to the issuer gives or gave
      him access to material information about the issuer or the security that is not
      generally available to the public; (d) a government employee, or a director,
      or officer of an exchange, clearing agency and/or self-regulatory
      organization who has access to material information about an issuer or a
      security that is not generally available to the public; [or] (e) a person who
      learns such information by a communication from any of the foregoing
      insiders[.]; OR (F) A PERSON WHO OBTAINS, POSSESSES, OR
      LEARNS OF MATERIAL NON-PUBLIC INFORMATION AND
      MISAPPROPRIATES THE SAME FOR TRADING PURPOSES, OR
      COMMUNICATES THE SAME IN BREACH OF A DUTY OF TRUST,
      CONFIDENCE, OR LOYALTY, OWED TO THE SOURCE OF THE
      INFORMATION, REGARDLESS OF WHETHER SUCH SOURCE HAS A
      FIDUCIARY RELATIONSHIP WITH THE ISSUER OF THE SECURITY."

SECTION 4. Amendment of Section 27 of Republic Act No. 8799. – Section 27 of
Republic Act No. 8799 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 27. Insider's Duty to Disclose When Trading. –

      27.1. It shall be unlawful for an insider to sell or buy a security of the issuer,
      while in possession of material information with respect to the issuer or the
      security that is not generally available to the public, unless: (a) The insider
      proves that the information was not gained from such relationship; or (b) If
      the other party to the transaction is identified, the insider proves that he
      disclosed the information to the other party, or that the other party had
      known or reasonably could be expected to know the information from the
      same source.

      27.2. IT SHALL LIKEWISE BE UNLAWFUL FOR ANY PERSON TO
      PURCHASE OR SELL ANY SECURITY, OR COMMUNICATE
      MATERIAL NON-PUBLIC INFORMATION TO OTHERS FOR THE
      PURPOSE OF TRADING, IN BREACH OF A DUTY OF TRUST,
      CONFIDENCE, OR LOYALTY, OWED TO THE SOURCE OF SUCH
      INFORMATION. LIABILITY UNDER THIS SUBSECTION SHALL
      ATTACH EVEN IF THE DEFENDANT OWES NO FIDUCIARY
      DUTY TO THE ISSUER CORPORATION, ITS SHAREHOLDERS,
      OR THE COUNTERPARTY TO THE TRADE. THE DECEPTIVE
      MISAPPROPRIATION      OF SUCH INFORMATION SHALL
      CONSTITUTE A FRAUD CONCURRENT WITH THE PURCHASE
      OR SALE OF A SECURITY.

      27.3.[27.2.] For purposes of this Section, information is 'material non-public'
      if: (a) It has not been generally disclosed to the public and would likely
      affect the market price of the security after being disseminated to the public
      and the lapse of a reasonable time for the market to absorb the information;
      or (b) A reasonable investor would consider it important under the
      circumstances in determining whether to buy, sell or hold the securities.

      27.4.[27.3.] xxx (Renumbering of subsequent subsections accordingly)"

SECTION 5. Presumption of Duty of Trust or Confidence. – For purposes of
implementing Section 27.2 of the SRC, a duty of trust or confidence shall be presumed to
exist in any of the following circumstances:

   1.​ Whenever a person agrees to maintain information in confidence;
   2.​ Whenever the person providing the material non-public information and the
       person receiving it have a history, pattern, or practice of sharing confidences, such
       that the recipient of the information knows or reasonably should know that the
       speaker expects that the recipient will maintain its confidentiality; or
   3.​ Whenever a person receives or obtains material non-public information from his
       or her spouse, parent, child, sibling, professional employer, client, or legal partner,
       unless it is affirmatively demonstrated that no duty of confidentiality existed
       under the circumstances.

SECTION 6. Penalties. – Any person found guilty of violating the provisions of this Act
shall suffer the penalties prescribed under Section 73 of Republic Act No. 8799. If the
offender is a juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed upon the officers of the
corporation responsible for the violation.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this
Act.

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full
force and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. – Sections 3.8 and 27 of Republic Act No. 8799 are
hereby amended. All other laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
