                            AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE ESTABLISHMENT OF COMPOSTING FACILITIES IN ALL
BARANGAYS, PROMOTING RESIDENTIAL COMPOSTING, AMENDING FOR THE
PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9003, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“ECOLOGICAL SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT ACT OF 2000,” AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Global waste generated in 2022 reached 2.56 billion tons, an amount
originally projected for 2030. Without intervention, global waste is estimated to grow
by fifty percent (50%) by 2050, or a total of 3.86 billion tons (Cook et al., 2026).
Driven by urbanization, population growth, and rising consumption patterns, the
Philippines ranked 27th globally in municipal solid waste production, at 15 million
tons annually (Dela Peña, 2025). The Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR) reported that 52 to 58 percent of the country’s waste is
biodegradable. With proper segregation, biodegradable waste is recovered and
composted, diverting usable trash away from our dumpsites (Teves, 2021). Without
segregation, a significant portion of biodegradable waste is disposed of in landfills or
open dumpsites, contributing to environmental degradation, methane emissions,
and public health risks. Despite the enactment of Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise
known as the “Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000,” full compliance at
the local level remains uneven and inconsistent, particularly in the segregation and
processing of organic waste.

         A major gap in the solid waste management system is the limited support
mechanisms for solid waste disposal, recycling, and recovery, including technologies,
facilities, and systems at the barangay level (Rebullida & Taguibao, 2020). While
Materials Recovery Facilities (MRFs) are mandated under existing law, many lack the
capacity, coverage, and functionality for composting, resulting in the continued
disposal of organic waste in landfills (Andaya, 2025). Decomposing biodegradable
waste in landfills generates methane, a greenhouse gas that contributes to global
warming and climate change. Conversely, composting transforms organic waste into
soil amendments that support agriculture, landscaping, and ecological restoration.

       In recognition of this challenge, grounded on Article II, Section 16 of the 1987
Constitution guaranteeing “the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology
in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature,” there is a need to strengthen the
implementation of ecological solid waste management by shifting toward localized,
decentralized, and resource-oriented approaches. This includes
empowering barangays as primary units of implementation, institutionalizing
composting infrastructure, and promoting household and community participation
in organic waste diversion.

       To address these gaps, this bill seeks to amend the Ecological Solid Waste
Management Act of 2000 by providing for the establishment, operationalization, and
integration of Barangay Composting Facilities (BCFs) within existing solid waste
management systems. By requiring all barangays to develop composting capacity,
the measure ensures that biodegradable waste is processed at or near the source,
thereby reducing dependence on landfills. Further, this measure strengthens
community-level participation by institutionalizing the Residential Composting
Support Desk (RCSD) in every barangay. The RCSD is designed to provide material
and technical assistance to households, homeowners’ associations (HOAs), and
condominium corporations, including the distribution of composting starter kits,
training programs, and access to scaled composting equipment for larger residential
developments. This approach lowers the barriers to entry for composting and
promotes sustained behavioral change at the household level. To ensure coverage,
subdivisions and condominium projects are required to divert organic waste either
through on-site composting facilities or by utilizing their barangay’s BCF. Registered
participants retain the right to the finished compost they produce, including personal
use, public greening programs, agricultural use, and community distribution, with
provisions allowing surplus compost to be sold commercially or directly to the
government. This mechanism transforms organic waste from a disposal burden into
an economic opportunity and environmental resource.

       Furthermore, while the Barangays are primarily responsible in organic waste
diversion, this bill mobilizes key national agencies, including the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Agriculture (DA),
Department of Science and Technology (DOST), Department of Human Settlements
and Urban Development (DHSUD), and the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), to ensure coordinated implementation, technical standard-
setting, and regulatory oversight.

       Ultimately, this measure advances a circular approach to solid waste
management by institutionalizing composting as a core component of
environmental governance. By reducing landfill dependence, lowering greenhouse
gas emissions, promoting resource recovery, and strengthening community
participation, the bill contributes to building sustainable, climate-resilient, and
environmentally responsible communities.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Community and
Home Composting Act.”
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to adopt a systematic, comprehensive, and ecological solid waste management
program. Recognizing that biodegradable matter constitutes more than fifty percent
(50%) of municipal solid waste, the State shall pursue environmentally-sound solid
waste management in accordance with ecologically sustainable development
principles. To this end, the State shall mandate waste volume reduction through
localized community composting and institutionalize comprehensive material and
technical support for household-level composting, thereby promoting waste diversion
and resource recovery.
      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
      (a)     Barangay Composting Facility (BCF) refers to a localized facility
      established and operated by a barangay or cluster of barangays for the
      biological processing of biodegradable waste into compost;
      (b)     Biodegradable Waste refers to waste capable of undergoing biological
      decomposition by micro-organisms, mainly bacteria and fungi;
      (c)     Organic Waste Diversion refers to the segregation and processing of
      biodegradable waste away from landfill disposal;
       (d)    Registered Participants refer to households, homeowners’ associations
       (HOAs), or condominium corporations formally enrolled in barangay
       composting programs; and
       (e)    Residential Composting Support Desk (RCSD) refers to a barangay-level
       unit tasked to provide technical and material assistance to households and
       residential communities for composting.

      through the Environment Management Bureau (EMB), formulate the
      engineering, environmental, and safety standards for BCF, subdivision, and

      shall not diminish or impair the regulatory and enforcement powers of the

      (c)    Department of Agriculture (DA) shall, through the Bureau of Soils and

      develop a program to procure community or LGU produced compost for

      individual and institutional starter kits for distribution to registered
      composting participants.
      (d)    Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD)
      shall integrate the mandatory compliance frameworks under Section 4 of this
      Act into the Implementing Rules and Regulations of Presidential Decree (PD)
      No. 957, as amended by PD No. 1216, otherwise known as the “Subdivision
      and Condominium Buyer’s Protective Decree,” as amended, and RA No. 9904,

      monitor compliance among real estate developers, HOAs, and condominium

      SEC. 7. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against available appropriations and
savings of the DENR, DA, and DOST, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and
auditing rules and regulations. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).
      Furthermore, LGUs may utilize their Development Fund under the National
Tax Allotment (NTA) for the construction, upkeep, personnel compensation, and
household starter kit procurement of their respective composting programs, subject
to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules and regulations.
      SEC. 8. Penalties. – Any local government official, whether elected or
appointed, who fails to comply with the institutionalization of RCSD and the
mandatory establishment, operation, and reporting of the BCF within the prescribed
period under this Act, shall, upon due notice and hearing, be subject to
administrative sanctions, including written warning, suspension, or other penalties
provided under RA No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of
1991” and applicable civil service laws, without prejudice to criminal prosecution
under existing environmental laws.
      Real estate developers, HOAs, or condominium corporations who fail to
implement mandatory composting provided under Section 5 of this Act shall, upon
due notice and hearing, be imposed a fine of not less than Five Thousand Pesos
(P5,000.00) but not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00), without prejudice
to other sanctions as may be imposed by the DHSUD under the Subdivision and
Condominium Buyer’s Decree and the Magna Carta for Homeowners and
Homeowners Associations.
       SEC. 9. Monitoring and Reporting. – Every barangay shall submit an
annual report on its local composting operations and biodegradable waste diversion
to their respective city or provincial LGUs not later than March 31 of the following
year. The city or provincial LGU shall consolidate these records and forward the
consolidated report to the National Solid Waste Management Commission (NSWMC)
within thirty (30) days thereafter.
       SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the NSWMC shall, in coordination with the DA, DOST,
DHSUD, and the DILG, and in consultation with other agencies and stakeholders,
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.
       SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts of this Act which are not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof, which
are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.
       SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
