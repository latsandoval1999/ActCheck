                        AN ACT
PROVIDING A COMPREHENSIVE STATUTORY FRAMEWORK FOR
THE   OBJECTIVES,    ORGANIZATION,  ADMINISTRATION,
DEVELOPMENT, AND OPERATIONS OF THE ARMED FORCES OF
THE PHILIPPINES, REPEALING COMMONWEALTH ACT NO. 1,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE NATIONAL DEFENSE ACT, AS
AMENDED, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Armed Forces of the Philippines is constitutionally mandated to
protect the people and the State, secure the sovereignty of the Republic, and
preserve the integrity of its national territory. The discharge of this mandate
requires a comprehensive statutory framework defining the organization,
authority, responsibilities, and operations of the country’s military establishment.
      Commonwealth Act No. 1, otherwise known as the National Defense Act,
was enacted in 1935 to establish the defense organization of the Philippine
Commonwealth. Although subsequent legislation has addressed particular
aspects of military administration and national defense, the principal institutional
framework continues to rest on a statute enacted under substantially different
constitutional, organizational, and strategic circumstances.

      The development of the Philippine Army, Philippine Navy, Philippine Air
Force, joint commands, reserve components, and specialized military capabilities
calls for a statutory framework suited to the present requirements of national
defense. Such a framework must provide clear lines of authority, effective civilian
supervision, coherent force development, and enforceable standards of
institutional accountability.

      This bill seeks to enact the Armed Forces of the Philippines Act as the
foundational statute governing the objectives, organization, administration,
development, and operations of the AFP. It places the AFP under the command
of the President of the Philippines as Commander-in-Chief, with the Secretary of
National Defense exercising direction, supervision, and control for and by
authority of the President.

      The measure defines the respective responsibilities of the Chief of Staff,
the major services, and joint commands. It establishes common requirements for
military strategy, capability development, operational readiness, professional
military education, personnel administration, reserve integration, and logistical
support. It likewise prescribes general standards governing military operations,
assistance to civilian authorities, international defense cooperation, and the
exercise of military authority.




                                                                                   2
      The principal legislative purpose is to establish an overarching institutional
framework within which specialized defense legislation may operate. Existing
laws governing military personnel, retirement and pensions, reserve service,
modernization, procurement, military justice, and domestic defense industry
development shall continue to govern their respective subjects, subject to the
express provisions of this Act.

      The bill further establishes a Warrant Officer Corps for specialized military
expertise, integrates the Women’s Auxiliary Corps into the major services, and
prescribes additional standards for enlistment, senior appointments, military
education, recall of retirees, and administrative discipline. Legal assistance,
reserve administration, and retirement entitlements are coordinated with their
governing special laws.

      The bill repeals Commonwealth Act No. 1 while preserving the lawful
continuity of the AFP, its institutions, existing appointments, accrued rights,
contracts, and obligations. It further requires the consolidation of implementing
issuances and the submission of proposed consequential amendments to
Congress.

      Through this measure, the AFP shall be provided with a coherent legal
foundation that supports military effectiveness, affirms civilian supremacy, and
ensures that the exercise of military power remains faithful to the Constitution
and the Republic.


      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                                   TITLE I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Armed Forces of
the Philippines Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
maintain a professional, competent, adequately equipped, accountable, and
nonpartisan Armed Forces of the Philippines, hereinafter referred to as the AFP,
for the protection of the people and the State, the preservation of national
sovereignty, and the defense of the integrity of the national territory.

      Civilian authority shall, at all times, be supreme over the military. The AFP
shall uphold the Constitution, respect democratic institutions, observe the rule of
law, and discharge its responsibilities in accordance with applicable domestic and
international law.

      The State shall develop and maintain defense capabilities commensurate
with the country’s archipelagic character, strategic requirements, international
obligations, and available resources. For this purpose, it shall maintain a Regular
Force supported by an organized and trained Reserve Force.

      The State shall promote military professionalism through merit-based
personnel administration, adequate remuneration and benefits, continuing
education and training, and appropriate measures for the welfare of military
personnel and their dependents.

      Military organization and resource allocation shall be directed toward the
accomplishment of defense objectives. The AFP shall maintain the capability to
operate across the national territory, reinforce threatened areas, protect essential
military functions, and sustain operations for the periods required by approved
plans. Reforms shall preserve unity of command, promote joint action, and
strengthen the capacity of the State to respond to military requirements.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall:



                                                                                   2
   (a) Establish the institutional mandate and general legal framework of the
      AFP;

   (b) Define the exercise of civilian direction and supervision and the allocation
      of military command responsibilities;

   (c) Provide     common       standards   for   force   development,   personnel
      administration, military education, readiness, operations, and sustainment;

   (d) Prescribe the general conditions governing the employment of military
      forces and the provision of assistance to civilian authorities;

   (e) Establish    planning,     coordination,   reporting,   and   accountability
      mechanisms applicable to AFP programs; and

   (f) Ensure the continuity of lawful military institutions, functions, rights, and
      obligations upon the repeal of Commonwealth Act No. 1, as amended.

   The provisions of this Act shall establish common institutional requirements
applicable to programs subsequently authorized by Congress. Implementing
measures shall identify the responsible authority, required resources, reporting
obligations, and relationship with existing programs. No implementing measure
shall be construed as granting a power, privilege, exemption, or entitlement for
which a separate statutory basis is required.

SEC. 4. Scope and Application. — This Act shall apply to the AFP, its
commands, major services, offices, units, military educational institutions,
military personnel, and civilian personnel insofar as their respective duties and
functions are concerned.




                                                                                   3
       Special laws governing particular aspects of military administration and
national defense shall continue in force unless expressly amended or repealed
herein. The provisions of this Act and such special laws shall, whenever
practicable, be construed together to give effect to their respective purposes.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to amend, by implication alone,
statutory provisions governing retirement ages, tours of duty, pensions,
compensation, reserve service obligations, criminal liability, judicial jurisdiction,
procurement exceptions, tax privileges, or the mandates of other government
institutions.

       Where implementation involves more than one statutory regime, the
responsible office shall identify the applicable provisions and obtain the required
approvals before proceeding. An administrative interpretation resolving an
apparent inconsistency shall state its legal basis and shall not operate as an
amendment of law. Unresolved matters requiring legislative action shall be
included in the legislative harmonization report prescribed herein.

SEC. 5. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) Administrative control refers to authority over the organization, personnel
       administration, training, equipment, logistics, and institutional support of
       military forces, subject to law and the terms of the applicable order;

   (b) Combined operations refer to military operations conducted by Philippine
       forces in conjunction with the military forces of one or more foreign states
       pursuant to lawful authority;

   (c) Command refers to the lawful authority vested in a military commander to
       direct subordinate personnel and units, together with the corresponding
       responsibility for their employment, discipline, welfare, and performance;

                                                                                    4
(d) Defense capability refers to the integrated capacity of personnel,
   organizations, equipment, doctrine, training, infrastructure, and support
   systems to perform an assigned defense function;

(e) Force development refers to the assessment, design, generation,
   improvement, and sustainment of military capabilities;

(f) Force employment refers to the assignment and operational use of military
   forces for the accomplishment of authorized missions;

(g) Joint operations refer to military operations involving elements of two or
   more major services under an integrated command arrangement;

(h) Major services refer to the Philippine Army, Philippine Navy, and
   Philippine Air Force;

(i) Mobilization refers to the authorized assembly, activation, preparation,
   and employment of personnel, units, and resources for national defense or
   other purposes prescribed by law;

(j) Operational control refers to delegated authority to organize and employ
   assigned or attached forces, assign operational tasks, and direct their
   execution, subject to the limitations prescribed in the applicable order;

(k) Readiness refers to the capacity of a military organization to accomplish
   its assigned mission within the prescribed period and under specified
   conditions;

(l) Regular Force refers to the standing military establishment constituted in
   accordance with law; and

(m) Reserve Force refers to the citizen armed force organized and maintained
   pursuant to Republic Act No. 7077 and other applicable laws;
                                                                               5
   (n) Active military service refers to service performed in a military status
       pursuant to a valid appointment, enlistment, activation, recall, or other
       lawful order: Provided, That creditable service for retirement and other
       benefits shall be determined under the applicable special law;

   (o) Force provider refers to a major service or other designated organization
       responsible for generating and sustaining forces for operational
       assignment;

   (p) Force employer refers to a commander vested with operational authority
       over forces assigned or attached for an authorized mission;

   (q) Warrant officer refers to a member appointed to the Warrant Officer Corps
       established under this Act to perform specialized military duties; and

   (r) Military educational institution refers to an AFP institution authorized to
       conduct military academic, professional, technical, or leadership
       instruction.

   The use of a term herein shall not modify a definition specifically prescribed
by another law for the computation of an entitlement, determination of
jurisdiction, or imposition of liability.

   The delegation of operational control shall not, by itself, confer authority over
appointments, promotions, permanent assignments, or other administrative
matters reserved by law or regulation to another authority.

SEC. 6. Constitutional Allegiance and Political Neutrality. — The AFP shall
owe allegiance to the Constitution and the Republic. No military authority shall
be exercised for the purpose of securing personal, partisan, or factional
allegiance.


                                                                                   6
      No member of the AFP in active military service shall engage, directly or
indirectly, in partisan political activity, except to exercise the right of suffrage.

      Military personnel, funds, facilities, equipment, official information, and
communications systems shall not be used to promote or oppose any political
party or candidate.

      No adverse personnel action shall be taken solely on account of the lawful
political opinions or affiliations of a member’s relatives.

      Official communications, ceremonies, and personnel instructions shall
reflect institutional allegiance and political neutrality. Commanders shall prohibit
the solicitation of political contributions within military units and the use of
subordinate personnel for partisan purposes. Complaints concerning prohibited
political activity shall be investigated through the appropriate administrative
process, without prejudice to prosecution under applicable law and the lawful
exercise of individual civil rights.

SEC. 7. Citizen Armed Force. — The AFP shall be composed of a citizen armed
force trained and organized in accordance with law.

      Personal military or civil service may be required only under conditions
prescribed by law. No rule or regulation issued pursuant to this Act shall, by itself,
impose compulsory military service, compulsory military training, or an
additional reserve service obligation.

      Participation in national defense shall be promoted through lawful reserve
programs, public education, scientific and industrial cooperation, and other
activities authorized by law.




                                                                                        7
        Reserve participation shall be organized according to actual defense
requirements and the capacity to train, equip, and sustain the units concerned.
Public information shall distinguish military service, reserve affiliation, disaster
volunteering, and other forms of civic participation. No civilian volunteer shall
acquire military rank, authority, or compulsory service obligations solely by
participating in an AFP-supported activity.




                                     TITLE II

                  MANDATE AND CIVILIAN DIRECTION

SEC. 8. Institutional Continuance of the AFP. — The AFP is hereby continued
as the unified military institution of the Republic under the command of the
President of the Philippines as Commander-in-Chief, with the Secretary of
National Defense exercising direction, supervision, and control for and by
authority of the President.

        The AFP shall form part of the Department of National Defense,
hereinafter referred to as the Department. Its existing lawful commands, major
services, offices, units, institutions, appointments, commissions, property
interests, and obligations shall continue, subject to this Act and other applicable
laws.

        The AFP shall act through the Republic and its duly authorized officials. It
shall not possess a corporate personality separate from the Republic.

SEC. 9. Primary Mandate. — The AFP shall protect the people and the State,
secure the sovereignty of the Republic, and defend the integrity of the national
territory against armed aggression and other military threats.


                                                                                   8
      In the protection of Philippine maritime interests, the AFP shall observe
the distinction between sovereignty over territory and territorial waters and
sovereign rights and jurisdiction in other maritime zones, in accordance with the
Constitution, applicable laws, and international law.

      The AFP shall give priority to territorial defense, deterrence, the protection
of military forces and installations, and the maintenance of capabilities necessary
to respond to armed threats.

      Defense missions shall be supported by contingency plans for the
protection of population centers, access to threatened territory, reinforcement of
dispersed forces, and sustainment of military operations. The exercise of military
functions shall respect the authority of civilian institutions. Military action
intended to protect sovereign rights shall be undertaken under competent
direction and shall not be construed as an assertion of territorial sovereignty
beyond that recognized by law.

SEC. 10. Powers and Functions. — Subject to the Constitution and applicable
laws, the AFP shall:

   (a) Assess military threats and advise the President and the Secretary of
      National Defense on military requirements;

   (b) Formulate and implement the national military strategy;

   (c) Organize, recruit, educate, train, equip, maintain, and sustain the Regular
      and Reserve Forces;

   (d) Conduct military operations pursuant to lawful authority;

   (e) Develop joint doctrine and interoperable capabilities;

   (f) Prepare and implement military mobilization and contingency plans;
                                                                                   9
   (g) Undertake military intelligence activities within its lawful mandate;

   (h) Protect military installations, networks, systems, information, and
      personnel;

   (i) Provide assistance to civilian authorities under conditions prescribed by
      law;

   (j) Participate in authorized international defense cooperation and peace
      support operations; and

   (k) Perform such other functions as may be prescribed by law or lawfully
      directed by the President.

   The performance of the foregoing functions shall be governed by approved
plans and assigned responsibilities. The AFP shall maintain the capacity to
identify capability deficiencies, recommend corrective measures, and provide
timely military advice. Activities involving another government agency shall be
coordinated with that agency, and no general assignment of a defense function
shall displace a statutory responsibility vested elsewhere.

SEC. 11. President as Commander-in-Chief. — The President of the
Philippines, as Commander-in-Chief of all armed forces of the Philippines, shall
exercise supreme command over the AFP in accordance with the Constitution.

      The President shall determine national military objectives, approve major
military policies and force structure decisions, and direct the employment of
military forces.




                                                                               10
      The President may exercise authority directly or through the Secretary of
National Defense, acting for and by authority of the President: Provided, That
powers which the Constitution or law requires the President to exercise personally
shall remain reserved to the President.

      Nothing herein shall enlarge or diminish the constitutional grounds,
procedures, limitations, or review mechanisms governing the calling out of the
armed forces, the suspension of the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus, or the
declaration of martial law.

      Presidential orders may be transmitted through secure communications and
authenticated by procedures prescribed for that purpose. Where an order is issued
orally because of operational urgency, the receiving authority shall cause its
substance, time of receipt, and source to be recorded and shall seek written
confirmation as soon as practicable. Such procedures shall preserve the
President’s direct command authority and shall not condition its exercise upon
subordinate approval.

SEC. 12. Authority of the Secretary of National Defense. — The Secretary of
National Defense, hereinafter referred to as the Secretary, shall, acting for and by
authority of the President, exercise direction, supervision, and control over the
defense establishment and transmit and implement presidential military policies,
orders, and directives.

      The Secretary shall:

   (a) Implement presidential defense policy through appropriate departmental
       directives;

   (b) Issue lawful directives to the AFP through the Chief of Staff;



                                                                                 11
   (c) Review and approve military plans, programs, and organizational
       proposals within the Secretary’s authority;

   (d) Supervise defense resource allocation and capability development;

   (e) Ensure compliance with law in military administration and operations;

   (f) Evaluate institutional performance and direct appropriate corrective
       measures; and

   (g) Submit the reports and recommendations required under this Act.

   The authority of the Secretary shall be exercised subject to the direction and
control of the President and the limitations prescribed by the Constitution and
applicable laws.

   Directions to the AFP shall specify the policy or administrative objective and
the authority under which they are issued. Professional military recommendations
requiring presidential determination shall be submitted without material omission
or alteration of their technical basis. The Secretary may require supplementary
analysis and shall communicate presidential decisions promptly through the
lawful command structure, together with applicable limitations and reporting
requirements.

SEC. 13. Chain of Command. — Command authority over the AFP shall
originate from the President as Commander-in-Chief. The ordinary transmission
of military orders and directives shall proceed from the President, through the
Secretary acting for and by authority of the President, to the Chief of Staff, and
thereafter to the appropriate subordinate commanders.




                                                                               12
       The President may issue orders directly to the Chief of Staff or any military
commander. Such orders shall be communicated promptly to the Secretary and
other affected commanders, subject to operational necessity and lawful security
requirements.

       The Chief of Staff and subordinate commanders shall exercise military
command within their respective responsibilities pursuant to lawful orders and
directives.

       Every operational assignment shall specify the mission, responsible
commander, assigned forces, command relationships, scope of authority, and
reporting requirements.

       No person outside the lawful command structure shall assume command
of AFP personnel or direct military operations, except as otherwise authorized by
law.

       Delegations shall be recorded in writing and shall specify their scope,
duration, and any limitation upon further delegation. Commanders shall ensure
that operational orders are authenticated and understood by subordinate units.
Conflicting orders shall be referred promptly to the competent superior authority
for clarification. Pending clarification, commanders shall act within lawful
standing instructions and any authority necessary to address an immediate
operational threat.

SEC. 14. Interagency Coordination. — The Department shall coordinate AFP
requirements and activities with the appropriate national security, foreign affairs,
economic, disaster management, and other civilian institutions.

       Existing national security advisory bodies shall continue to exercise their
functions under their respective legal authorities.

                                                                                  13
      Interagency coordination shall not transfer to the AFP any power vested by
law in a civilian agency or place such agency under military command, except as
expressly authorized by law.

      Coordination arrangements shall identify lead and supporting agencies,
authorized   liaison   officers,   information-sharing   procedures,   resource
commitments, and the means for resolving operational disagreements.
Agreements shall be reviewed periodically and following material changes in
agency responsibilities. No liaison arrangement shall authorize an AFP
representative to commit the funds or personnel of another agency without that
agency’s lawful approval.




                                   TITLE III

       ORGANIZATION AND COMMAND RESPONSIBILITIES

SEC. 15. Composition of the AFP. — The AFP shall consist of:

   (a) The General Headquarters and Joint Staff;

   (b) The Philippine Army;

   (c) The Philippine Navy, including the Philippine Marine Corps;

   (d) The Philippine Air Force;

   (e) Joint operational and functional commands;

   (f) The Reserve Force; and

   (g) Military educational institutions, technical and administrative services,
      support organizations, and other units established in accordance with law.

                                                                              14
   The Philippine Coast Guard and Philippine National Police shall remain
governed by their respective laws. Their relationship with the AFP during war or
other circumstances shall be determined in accordance with such laws.

   Each constituent organization shall possess a prescribed mission and an
approved organizational instrument identifying its command or administrative
relationships. The establishment of a task force shall identify its parent
organizations and the arrangements for its dissolution. The creation of temporary
organizations shall not constitute authority to establish permanent positions or
retain resources beyond the period and purpose approved.

SEC. 16. Regular and Reserve Establishments. — The AFP shall maintain
distinct and coordinated Regular and Reserve establishments.

      A reservist called to active duty shall retain reserve status unless appointed
into the Regular Force in accordance with law.

      Cadets, officer candidates, trainees, and other persons undergoing
authorized military preparation shall possess the status and receive the
entitlements prescribed by law. Admission to a military educational or training
institution shall not, by itself, constitute a permanent commission or enlistment.

      The Regular Force shall include commissioned officers, warrant officers,
and enlisted personnel appointed or enlisted under law. Reservists, recalled
retirees, draftees, and other persons lawfully placed on active duty may serve with
regular units without conversion of their underlying status. Their orders shall state
the applicable status, duties, duration, and conditions of release. Government-
sponsored Filipino cadets in foreign military institutions shall remain subject to
approved service and educational arrangements.



                                                                                  15
SEC. 17. Authorized Strength and Organizational Tables. — The President,
upon the recommendation of the Secretary and the Chief of Staff, shall approve
the authorized strength and general force structure of the AFP, subject to statutory
limitations and available appropriations.

      The Secretary shall approve tables of organization, personnel, and
equipment consistent with the approved force structure.

      Organizational proposals shall state the mission requirement, personnel
and operating costs, support requirements, and effects on existing capabilities.

      No organizational table shall modify a statutory grade limitation or confer
an entitlement to appointment or promotion.

      Approved tables shall state the number, classification, and authorized
grade of positions, together with essential equipment requirements. Changes
affecting personnel expenditure shall be coordinated with the Department of
Budget and Management. Vacancies, personnel strength, and actual equipment
holdings shall be reconciled periodically against approved tables. The existence
of a vacant position shall not dispense with selection, appointment, confirmation,
or budget requirements.

SEC. 18. Organizational Changes. — The President, upon the recommendation
of the Secretary, may create, consolidate, transfer, redesignate, or deactivate AFP
commands, offices, and units necessary for the performance of functions
authorized by this Act.

      The Secretary may approve subordinate organizational changes pursuant
to a written delegation of authority from the President.

      Such authority shall not include the power to:


                                                                                   16
   (a) Abolish a major service or an institution expressly established by law;

   (b) Establish an additional major service;

   (c) Transfer to the AFP a function vested by law in another department or
       agency;

   (d) Modify statutory qualifications, compensation, tenure, or retirement
       entitlements; or

   (e) Incur expenditures without the required appropriation and budget
       authority.

   Organizational changes shall provide for the orderly disposition of affected
personnel, records, property, and obligations and shall preserve essential defense
capabilities.

       Every material organizational change shall be supported by an
implementation plan identifying the functions transferred, the authority receiving
them, the disposition of personnel and property, and the implementation
timetable. The plan shall address continuity of command, statutory obligations,
and foreseeable expenditure. A unit shall not be deactivated until responsibility
for its outstanding obligations and essential functions has been assigned to a
competent successor.

SEC. 19. General Headquarters and Joint Staff. — The General Headquarters
shall serve as the central military headquarters of the AFP.

       The Joint Staff shall assist the Chief of Staff in integrated planning,
operations, personnel administration, intelligence, logistics, communications,
force development, reserve affairs, education, and other functions prescribed by
the Secretary.

                                                                                 17
      Its organization shall provide for appropriate representation of the major
services, clearly defined responsibilities, and the avoidance of unnecessary
duplication.

      A staff appointment shall not, by itself, confer command authority over
military units.

      The General Headquarters may be designated as the Joint Staff
Headquarters by presidential order upon the recommendation of the Secretary and
the Chief of Staff. Such designation shall not interrupt institutional succession or
alter statutory offices. Staff organization shall provide for coordinated advice and
completed staff work, with recommendations identifying operational, personnel,
financial, logistical, and legal implications requiring command decision.

SEC. 20. Joint, Personal, and Special Staff. — The Joint Staff shall constitute
the coordinating staff of the Chief of Staff. Personal staff shall provide immediate
assistance to the Chief of Staff, and special staff shall provide professional,
technical, or specialized advice and support.

      The Chief of Staff, with the approval of the Secretary and within approved
organizational and budgetary limits, shall prescribe their respective functions and
working relationships. Such authority shall include the consolidation or transfer
of staff functions not separately vested by statute.

      Staff recommendations requiring action shall state the issue, relevant facts,
available alternatives, legal and resource implications, and proposed disposition.
Staff officers may communicate instructions only within a recorded delegation
and shall identify the commander whose authority is exercised. No staff office
shall establish a parallel operational chain of command.



                                                                                  18
SEC. 21. Chief of Staff. — The Chief of Staff shall be the senior military officer
of the AFP and the principal military adviser to the President and the Secretary.

      Subject to presidential authority and the lawful direction of the Secretary,
the Chief of Staff shall:

   (a) Exercise military command over the AFP;

   (b) Formulate and recommend military strategy and force requirements;

   (c) Direct joint planning and authorized military operations;

   (d) Ensure the readiness and sustainment of military forces;

   (e) Supervise military administration and discipline;

   (f) Integrate the capabilities and activities of the major services;

   (g) Prescribe common military standards within the Chief of Staff’s authority;

   (h) Recommend appointments, assignments, and organizational changes; and

   (i) Report material military risks, capability deficiencies, and institutional
       performance.

   Military advice shall include feasible courses of action, resource requirements,
applicable legal considerations, and material operational risks.

   The Chief of Staff shall be appointed by the President from qualified officers
in active service in accordance with applicable personnel laws. The office shall
carry the grade of General or Admiral, subject to constitutional confirmation
requirements. The Chief of Staff shall require subordinate commanders to
account for assigned responsibilities and shall ensure that significant institutional
deficiencies are reported to civilian authorities with proposed remedial action.

                                                                                   19
SEC. 22. Vice Chief of Staff and Deputy Chief of Staff. — The Vice Chief of
Staff shall assist the Chief of Staff and perform such duties as may be prescribed
by competent authority.

       The Deputy Chief of Staff shall coordinate and integrate the functions of
the Joint Staff.

       The exercise of duties in an acting capacity during absence, incapacity, or
vacancy shall be governed by orders of the President or other competent
authority. An acting designation shall not confer permanent rank, extend statutory
tenure, or constitute a permanent appointment.

       The Vice Chief of Staff shall serve as principal assistant to the Chief of
Staff and may perform the latter’s duties during temporary absence or incapacity
under a valid designation. The Deputy Chief of Staff shall ensure the timely
integration of staff recommendations and monitor the execution of approved
decisions. Appointment, relief, retirement, and eligibility shall remain subject to
applicable personnel laws.

SEC. 23. Responsibilities of the Major Services. — The major services shall
organize, train, equip, maintain, and sustain military forces for assignment to
authorized missions.

       Each major service shall administer its personnel, maintain its equipment
and infrastructure, develop its reserve component, and formulate service doctrine
consistent with joint doctrine.

       The assignment or attachment of forces to a joint command shall be made
by written order specifying operational and administrative responsibilities.




                                                                                 20
      Major service chiefs shall be appointed by the President and shall exercise
command over their services subject to the authority of the Chief of Staff. The
major services shall maintain combat, combat support, combat service support,
and technical capabilities appropriate to their missions. They shall furnish
assigned forces with the training, equipment, personnel administration, and
sustainment required by the governing operational assignment.

SEC. 24. Principles of Force Generation and Employment. — The AFP shall
observe unity of command, integrated planning, and decentralized execution
under clearly defined operational authority.

      The General Headquarters shall integrate military direction and
coordination. Major services shall serve principally as force providers, while joint
operational commands shall serve as force employers within assigned missions.
Functional commands shall provide capabilities and support under their
constituting orders. The Reserve Force shall provide an organized basis for
expansion and reinforcement.

      Orders assigning forces shall prescribe their operational availability,
administrative responsibilities, support commitments, and conditions for
reassignment. A force provider shall not issue operational instructions
inconsistent with the governing assignment, and a force employer shall not alter
permanent personnel status or administrative arrangements beyond delegated
authority. Questions concerning competing requirements shall be resolved by the
Chief of Staff, subject to civilian direction.

SEC. 25. Philippine Army. — The Philippine Army shall provide land forces for
territorial defense and other authorized military operations.




                                                                                 21
      Its functions shall include land maneuver, protection of assigned areas and
installations, military engineering, land-based combat support, and the
development of capabilities for joint operations.

      The organization and development of Army forces shall take into account
the country’s terrain, geographic conditions, and defense requirements.

      The Philippine Army shall maintain its headquarters, maneuver
formations, combat support units, and sustainment organizations. It shall prepare
forces for prompt and sustained joint or combined ground operations, develop
land warfare doctrine and procedures, and implement authorized mobilization of
the Army Reserve. Army training shall include the movement and support of
forces across islands and coordination with naval and air transport capabilities.

SEC. 26. Philippine Navy and Philippine Marine Corps. — The Philippine
Navy shall provide naval forces for maritime defense, naval operations, maritime
domain awareness, sealift, and other authorized missions.

      The Philippine Marine Corps shall continue as a component of the
Philippine Navy and shall provide forces for amphibious, littoral, expeditionary,
and related land operations.

      Naval and marine force development shall be coordinated with joint
military requirements and the statutory responsibilities of civilian maritime
agencies.




                                                                                    22
      The Philippine Navy shall maintain its headquarters, naval forces, marine
formations, service support units, and sustainment organizations. It shall develop
naval and amphibious doctrine and implement authorized mobilization of the
Navy Reserve. Force development shall address coastal and offshore operations,
maritime surveillance, protection of naval facilities, and support to dispersed
forces. Marine capabilities shall be integrated into naval and joint operational
planning.

SEC. 27. Philippine Air Force. — The Philippine Air Force shall provide air
forces for air defense, surveillance, air mobility, air operations, and related
military support.

      It shall maintain capabilities for the defense of Philippine airspace and the
support of joint military operations.

      Activities affecting civil aviation shall be coordinated with the competent
civilian authorities without prejudice to their statutory powers.

      The Philippine Air Force shall maintain its headquarters, air combat,
combat support, and sustainment organizations. It shall develop air warfare
doctrine, prepare forces for prompt and sustained air operations, and implement
authorized mobilization of the Air Force Reserve. Planning shall address aircraft
availability, airfield support, maintenance capacity, crew proficiency, airspace
coordination, and the survivability of air operations and supporting installations.

SEC. 28. Joint Operational Commands. — Joint operational commands may be
constituted on a geographic or mission basis for the employment of forces
assigned by competent authority.




                                                                                 23
      Their commanders shall be responsible for mission accomplishment,
operational readiness, coordination, force protection, compliance with law, and
the submission of required reports.

      The designation of an operational area shall not establish military
government, alter political boundaries, or confer authority over civilian officials.

      Joint force commanders shall be appointed or designated by the President
upon the recommendation of the Chief of Staff through the Secretary. Their
operational authority shall extend only to forces assigned or attached under the
governing order. Geographic presence alone shall not transfer operational control
of a unit. Orders shall specify responsibilities for joint planning, coordination,
intelligence, logistics, reinforcement, and the return of forces to their parent
services.

SEC. 29. Functional and Specialized Commands. — Functional or specialized
commands may be constituted for integrated military capabilities, including
logistics, special operations, cyber defense, communications, education, health
services, and reserve development.

      Their constituting orders shall specify their functions, command
relationships, and administrative responsibilities.

      The establishment of a functional or specialized command shall not confer
powers beyond those authorized by law.




                                                                                  24
      A functional command shall maintain standards and technical procedures
applicable to its assigned capability, without displacing the command authority
of an operational commander. Arrangements involving shared assets shall specify
priorities, support commitments, and dispute resolution procedures. The Chief of
Staff shall review overlapping functions and recommend consolidation where
necessary to improve effectiveness and reduce unnecessary organizational
expenditure.

SEC. 30. Technical, Administrative, and Support Services. — The AFP shall
maintain legal, medical,      dental, nursing, engineering, communications,
chaplaincy, educational, financial, and other technical or support services
necessary for the performance of its mandate.

      Personnel engaged in regulated professions shall comply with applicable
professional standards.

      Military legal advisers shall have direct access to commanders on matters
requiring legal advice. Health professionals shall exercise clinical judgment in
accordance with their professional obligations. Chaplaincy services shall respect
religious freedom, and participation in religious observances shall not be
compulsory.

      Technical and administrative services may be maintained within major
services or on an AFP-wide basis according to operational and professional
requirements. They may include the Judge Advocate General Service, Medical
Corps, Veterinary Corps, Medical Administrative Corps, Nurse Corps, Dental
Corps, Chaplain Service, and Corps of Professors. Their organization shall
preserve professional supervision, equitable assignment of specialists, and access
to essential support by deployed units.



                                                                                25
SEC. 31. Integration of Women in the Major Services. — The Women’s
Auxiliary Corps established under Republic Act No. 3835, as amended, is hereby
integrated into the respective major services. Republic Act No. 3835, as amended,
is hereby repealed, subject to the preservation of valid appointments, service
credits, accrued benefits, and other existing rights.

      Women shall be eligible for military occupations, assignments, education,
and advancement according to lawful qualifications and objective requirements
of the position. No separate numerical ceiling shall be imposed solely on account
of sex. Existing personnel shall be placed in appropriate service and occupational
classifications without reduction of rank or interruption of service.

      The transition plan shall address personnel records, seniority, training,
equipment, facilities, and career progression. Marriage or parenthood shall not,
by itself, constitute a ground for separation. Statutory maternity and other
applicable benefits shall be preserved. Integration shall be completed within two
(2) years from effectivity and shall be reported to Congress.

SEC. 32. Civilian Personnel. — Civilian personnel of the AFP shall be governed
by civil service, compensation, and other applicable laws.

      Military supervision shall not convert civilian employment into military
service or subject civilian personnel to military disciplinary jurisdiction except as
expressly provided by law.

      Positions requiring civilian professional or technical expertise may be
filled by qualified civilian personnel, subject to applicable security and staffing
requirements.




                                                                                   26
      Personnel restructuring affecting civilian employees shall comply with
civil service requirements and applicable separation or retirement laws. An
employee shall be informed of a proposed reassignment or abolition of position
and afforded the remedies provided by law. Security requirements shall be
applied through documented procedures, and access restrictions shall not be used
to impose a disciplinary penalty without the required administrative process.




                                   TITLE IV

MILITARY STRATEGY, FORCE DEVELOPMENT, AND READINESS

SEC. 33. National Military Strategy. — The Chief of Staff shall prepare a
national military strategy for review by the Secretary and approval by the
President.

      The strategy shall specify military objectives, threat assessments,
operational concepts, force requirements, defense cooperation considerations,
and principal military risks.

      It shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years or whenever a
material change in the security environment so requires.

      An unclassified statement of its general objectives shall be furnished to
Congress and made available to the public.




                                                                                27
      The strategy shall distinguish military objectives from the broader
responsibilities of civilian government. It shall identify assumptions, force
employment priorities, and the relationship between available capabilities and
assigned missions. Reviews shall consider changes in threat assessments,
technology, partnerships, and resources. Material departures from an approved
strategy shall be submitted to the President through the Secretary with an
assessment of their consequences.

SEC. 34. Defense Capability Development Plan. — The Department and the
AFP shall maintain a rolling ten-year defense capability development plan, which
shall be reviewed annually.

      The plan shall integrate personnel, organization, doctrine, education,
equipment, infrastructure, logistics, reserves, and domestic support requirements.

      Each major capability proposal shall identify:

   (a) The operational requirement and capability deficiency;

   (b) Available alternatives;

   (c) Acquisition and lifecycle costs;

   (d) Personnel, training, and infrastructure requirements;

   (e) Implementation milestones;

   (f) Sustainment and supply arrangements; and

   (g) Measures of operational effectiveness.

   The plan shall guide the preparation of programs under applicable
modernization laws. It shall not extend a statutory program period or authorize
expenditure independently of an appropriation.
                                                                                28
   The plan shall distinguish projects ready for implementation from proposals
requiring further study. It shall identify dependencies among equipment,
personnel, facilities, and training and the effect of delayed funding upon
operational capability. Annual revisions shall record material changes in cost,
schedule, and expected capability. Projects shall not proceed solely by reason of
inclusion in the plan without the approvals otherwise required by law.

SEC. 35. Joint Requirements and Prioritization. — The AFP shall establish a
joint requirements process for the assessment and prioritization of capability
proposals.

      Assessment shall consider strategic relevance, interoperability, readiness,
affordability, sustainment, and the maintenance of existing capabilities.

      The Secretary shall approve priorities within departmental authority and
submit matters requiring presidential approval or legislative action to the
appropriate authority.

      The joint requirements process shall include operational user assessment,
technical evaluation, legal review where appropriate, and consideration of
sustainment alternatives. Recommendations shall identify the consequences of
accepting or deferring a capability requirement. Requirements shall be stated in
terms of operational performance and shall not be designed to favor a supplier
without a lawful and documented justification.

SEC. 36. Readiness Standards. — The Chief of Staff shall prescribe measurable
readiness standards covering personnel availability, training proficiency,
equipment      serviceability,   deployability,   supply, medical   support, and
sustainment.



                                                                               29
         Commanders shall submit periodic readiness reports and corrective action
plans.

         Material readiness deficiencies shall be reported to the Secretary, together
with their operational consequences and proposed corrective measures.

         The falsification of a readiness report shall be subject to the administrative
or criminal liability prescribed by applicable law.

         Readiness reports shall distinguish authorized strength from deployable
personnel and equipment holdings from serviceable assets. Temporary
improvements achieved by transferring resources among units shall be disclosed
where they materially affect reported readiness. Corrective plans shall identify
responsible offices, required resources, and completion dates. Inspection findings
shall be reconciled with command reports, and unresolved discrepancies shall be
referred to the appropriate reviewing authority.

SEC. 37. Doctrine, Exercises, and Operational Assessment. — The AFP shall
maintain procedures for the development, validation, and periodic review of joint
and service doctrine.

         Military exercises shall assess operational plans, command relationships,
reserve integration, logistics, and interagency coordination.

         After-action assessments shall identify deficiencies, prescribe corrective
measures, and assign responsibility for their implementation. Findings shall be
incorporated, as appropriate, into doctrine, training, capability development, and
resource planning.




                                                                                    30
       Exercises involving civilian authorities shall establish agreed objectives,
participation, safety measures, and responsibility for costs. Evaluation teams shall
assess performance against prescribed standards and document operational
limitations. Material lessons shall be assigned to designated offices for
implementation and subsequent verification. Exercise outcomes shall not be
represented as evidence of full operational capability where material deficiencies
remain unresolved.

SEC. 38. Continuity of Military Functions. — The AFP shall maintain plans for
the continuity of essential military functions in the event of disruption of
command facilities, communications, transportation, energy, supply systems, or
other critical infrastructure.

       Such plans shall provide for lawful succession of command, alternate
facilities, protected records, redundant communications, and support to dispersed
forces.

       Environmental, climatic, technological, and infrastructure risks shall be
considered in military basing and sustainment planning.

       Continuity arrangements shall identify alternate command authorities and
conditions for their assumption of duties. Records necessary to personnel
administration, benefits, logistics, and financial accountability shall be backed up
securely. Essential functions shall be tested periodically through exercises.
Recovery plans shall provide for the restoration of ordinary command
arrangements and the reconciliation of decisions and transactions undertaken
during disruption.




                                                                                 31
SEC. 39. Military Defense Conditions. — The President may prescribe levels of
military defense readiness upon the recommendation of the Secretary and the
Chief of Staff. Such levels shall provide a common basis for military preparation
in response to armed threats, invasion, internal security emergencies, disasters,
and other circumstances within the AFP’s lawful mandate.

      Each level shall specify readiness measures, alert requirements, reporting,
personnel availability, logistics preparation, and the authority necessary for
implementation. Measures requiring separate statutory or constitutional authority
shall not become lawful solely by reason of a readiness designation.

      A declaration of military defense readiness shall not constitute martial law,
suspend any constitutional right, authorize compulsory service or requisition, or
transfer civilian powers to military authorities. Readiness levels shall be reviewed
as circumstances change and withdrawn or reduced when their operational basis
ceases. Significant changes shall be reported to the President with their personnel
and resource implications.

                                    TITLE V

  MILITARY PERSONNEL AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

SEC. 40. Personnel Administration. — Recruitment, appointment, assignment,
promotion, retention, and separation shall be governed by law, merit,
competence, integrity, fitness, and the requirements of the service.

      Personnel actions shall be documented and subject to the applicable review
procedures.

      Qualification standards shall bear a reasonable relationship to the duties of
the position and shall be applied consistently with statutory requirements.


                                                                                  32
       Personnel policies shall provide for occupational classification, verified
qualification records, fair performance assessment, and orderly career
progression. The responsible personnel office shall maintain an audit trail of
material decisions. Command influence shall not be used to conceal adverse
findings, fabricate qualifications, or bypass a required selection process. Access
to personal records shall be limited to authorized purposes.

SEC. 41. Recruitment and Entry into Service. — Recruitment shall be
conducted through prescribed procedures containing safeguards against fraud,
favoritism, exploitation, and unauthorized charges.

       Applicants shall be informed of applicable service obligations, training
requirements, compensation, benefits, and conditions of separation.

       No person below eighteen (18) years of age shall be enlisted or
commissioned into military service. Participation in a preparatory educational
activity shall not authorize the involvement of a minor in hostilities.

       Medical and psychological examinations shall be conducted according to
appropriate and validated standards.

       Recruiting offices shall maintain records of applicant assessments,
selection results, and reasons for disqualification. Qualified applicants shall be
informed of available review procedures for factual or procedural errors.
Recruitment shall, as far as practicable, provide access across the provinces while
maintaining common competence standards. Educational equivalencies and
recognition of relevant technical skills may be considered where permitted by
law.




                                                                                33
SEC. 42. Commissioned Officers and Grades. — Commissioned officers shall
be appointed by the President in accordance with the Constitution and applicable
laws. Appointments requiring confirmation shall be submitted to the Commission
on Appointments.

        The AFP shall maintain the commissioned grades of Second Lieutenant or
Ensign, First Lieutenant or Lieutenant Junior Grade, Captain or Lieutenant, Major
or Lieutenant Commander, Lieutenant Colonel or Commander, Colonel or
Captain, Brigadier General or Commodore, Major General or Rear Admiral,
Lieutenant General or Vice Admiral, and General or Admiral, as applicable to the
respective services.

        Grade distribution, seniority, promotion, and tenure-in-grade shall be
governed by applicable personnel laws.

        The establishment of statutory military grades other than those expressly
provided in this Act shall require legislation.

        Unless otherwise provided by law, the date of rank shall be the effective
date stated in the lawful appointment or promotion instrument. The AFP shall
maintain authenticated appointment records and separate regular, reserve, and
other    authorized    personnel   lists.   Temporary   assignments,   ceremonial
designations, and performance of higher duties shall not, by themselves, confer a
permanent commission, seniority, or entitlement to the pay of a higher grade.

SEC. 43. Qualifications for Commission and Revocation. — An applicant for
original commission in the Regular Force shall be a natural-born citizen of the
Philippines, at least eighteen (18) years of age, of good moral character,
physically and mentally qualified for the duties of military service, and possessed
of the education and professional qualifications prescribed by law and lawful
regulation.
                                                                                34
       Specialized entry shall require verified qualifications and approved
military preparation. Existing valid commissions shall not be invalidated solely
by the enactment of this section.

       A commission obtained through fraud or issued to a person lacking a
required qualification may be revoked by the President upon the recommendation
of the Secretary and after the proceedings required by law. The affected officer
shall be notified of the grounds and afforded an opportunity to respond.
Revocation shall identify its effective date and applicable legal consequences and
shall not automatically extinguish benefits otherwise protected by law.

SEC. 44. Enlisted Personnel and Noncommissioned Officers. — The AFP shall
maintain a professional enlisted and noncommissioned officer corps.

       The Secretary shall prescribe enlisted grades, occupational classifications,
qualification standards, and career development arrangements consistent with
law.

       Noncommissioned officers shall receive appropriate leadership training
and shall exercise duties in unit training, discipline, technical supervision, and
administration as prescribed by competent authority.

       Reenlistment shall be governed by performance, qualifications, fitness, and
service requirements under prescribed procedures.

       Enlisted occupational requirements shall include technical proficiency,
field competence, leadership development, and periodic qualification assessment.
The Chief of Staff shall allocate authorized enlisted strength among the services
and prescribe promotion and demotion authority consistent with law. Personnel
shall receive notice of reenlistment requirements sufficiently in advance of
expiration of their term to permit orderly processing and unit succession.

                                                                                 35
SEC. 45. Establishment of the Warrant Officer Corps. — There is hereby
established in the Regular Force a Warrant Officer Corps composed of military
specialists whose duties require continuing technical expertise, advanced
occupational proficiency, or specialized training and advisory competence.

      The Corps shall consist of Warrant Officer One, Warrant Officer Two,
Warrant Officer Three, Warrant Officer Four, and Warrant Officer Five. Warrant
officers shall be appointed by the Secretary upon the recommendation of the
Chief of Staff, pursuant to merit selection and approved personnel ceilings. They
shall not hold commissioned officer status by reason of such appointment.

      Applicants shall be natural-born citizens of the Philippines, at least
eighteen (18) years of age, of good moral character, medically qualified for the
position, and possessed of the prescribed technical qualifications. Qualified
enlisted personnel and civilian technical specialists may apply through
competitive selection and shall complete approved military and leadership
instruction.

      Warrant officers may perform technical leadership, training, maintenance,
systems management, and advisory duties. Command authority shall arise only
from a lawful assignment and shall remain subject to prescribed limits.
Establishment of the Corps shall not create an additional major service or permit
displacement of personnel solely to fill warrant officer positions.

SEC. 46. Warrant Officer Administration and Entitlements. — The Secretary
shall prescribe warrant officer occupational fields, selection boards, career
progression, assignments, promotion standards, and authorized strength within
approved organizational and budgetary limits. Promotion shall require a vacancy,
demonstrated proficiency, prescribed education, and favorable professional
assessment.

                                                                              36
      The President shall approve a warrant officer compensation schedule upon
the recommendation of the Secretary and the Department of Budget and
Management, subject to applicable compensation laws and appropriations. No
appointment shall be issued before its compensation and staffing authority is
approved.

      Warrant officers shall be treated as military personnel for discipline,
authorized welfare services, and retirement and separation under Presidential
Decree No. 1638, as amended, insofar as applicable. Their compulsory retirement
shall occur at age fifty-seven (57) or upon completion of thirty (30) years of
satisfactory active service, whichever comes later. Benefits shall be computed
using the applicable statutory formula and the corresponding approved warrant
officer pay, without an assumed promotion to a higher grade. Creditable prior
service shall not be counted twice.

      A warrant appointment obtained through fraud or issued without a required
qualification may be revoked by the Secretary, upon the recommendation of the
Chief of Staff, after notice and opportunity to be heard. The affected person shall
receive a written decision and access to applicable review. Revocation shall not
extinguish an accrued benefit except upon grounds authorized by law.

SEC. 47. Terms of Enlistment and Reenlistment. — An applicant for original
enlistment shall be a natural-born citizen of the Philippines, at least eighteen (18)
years of age, of good moral character, medically qualified for the duties
concerned, and possessed of prescribed educational or technical qualifications.

      Each enlistment shall be for five (5) years, unless a special law provides
otherwise. Existing enlistments shall continue under their lawful terms until
expiration, after which this section shall apply to reenlistment.



                                                                                  37
      In time of war or grave national emergency, the President may, by written
order, extend unexpired or wartime enlistments until not later than six (6) months
after termination of the circumstance stated in the order, subject to lawful
separation and medical retirement. Such extension shall not arise automatically
from a readiness designation.

      Reenlistment     shall    require    evaluation   of   performance,   fitness,
qualifications, and service needs. The enlistment instrument shall specify the
term, service obligations, and grounds for discharge. Regional recruitment shall
be encouraged without reducing competence standards.

SEC. 48. Selection and Promotion. — Selection and promotion shall be
conducted through duly constituted boards applying written criteria and verified
personnel records.

      The applicable rules shall provide safeguards against conflicts of interest
and shall consider competence, leadership, integrity, performance, required
education, and the needs of the service.

      Political endorsement shall not substitute for a qualification prescribed by
law or a required professional assessment.

      Procedures shall be provided for the correction of factual errors and review
of material procedural irregularities, without prejudice to the lawful authority of
the appointing official.




                                                                                 38
      Selection boards shall be composed of qualified personnel and shall record
their proceedings and recommendations. Board members shall disclose
relationships or interests that may impair impartiality. Review shall address
material errors or procedural violations without substituting personal preference
for professional evaluation. Seniority lists shall be updated regularly, and
correction of an erroneous entry shall be supported by the appropriate
appointment or personnel record.

SEC. 49. Senior Officer Positions and Promotion. — The Secretary, upon the
recommendation of the Chief of Staff, shall allocate senior officer positions
among the headquarters, major services, and commands according to mission,
technical requirements, responsibility, equipment, and approved force structure.

      Promotion to General or Flag Officer grade shall require an authorized
vacancy and an assignment appropriate to the grade, except where another law
expressly provides otherwise. The applicable statutory officer distribution limits
and remaining-service requirements shall be observed.

      A recommendation shall identify the position, authorized grade, vacancy,
qualifications, professional assessment, and compliance with applicable
eligibility requirements. No position shall be upgraded solely to accommodate an
individual promotion. Temporary assumption of higher duties shall not confer
entitlement to permanent promotion. Appointments and promotions requiring
confirmation shall remain subject to the Constitution and the authority of the
Commission on Appointments.

SEC. 50. Key Positions, Tours of Duty, Retirement, and Attrition. —
Appointment to key positions, tours of duty, compulsory retirement, tenure-in-
grade, attrition, and officer grade distribution shall be governed by Republic Act
No. 11709, as amended by Republic Act No. 11939, and other applicable laws.

                                                                                39
      No rule or regulation issued under this Act shall extend a statutory tour of
duty, modify a retirement condition, waive a statutory eligibility requirement, or
alter a statutory grade limitation.

      Succession planning shall provide for institutional continuity consistent
with the President’s constitutional and statutory appointment powers.

      The AFP shall maintain a consolidated personnel reference stating the
current statutory rules on tours of duty, retirement, attrition, and grade
distribution. Any amendment to those rules shall be incorporated promptly and
communicated to affected offices. Personnel planning shall consider forecast
vacancies and the availability of qualified successors. Regulations shall not
reinstate a statutory tenure or eligibility rule that has been expressly repealed.

SEC. 51. Professional Military Education. — The AFP shall maintain a
coordinated system of professional military education appropriate to the duties
and career stages of military personnel.

      The system shall include instruction in military operations, leadership,
constitutional government, professional ethics, technology, joint operations, and
coordination with civilian institutions.

      Instruction shall include international humanitarian law, human rights,
lawful use of force, military justice, public resource accountability, and the
obligations of personnel concerning unlawful orders.

      Educational requirements shall be relevant to the duties and responsibilities
for which they are prescribed.




                                                                                     40
      Education plans shall distinguish initial military preparation, occupational
training, intermediate leadership development, command and staff education, and
senior strategic instruction. Course completion shall be evaluated by prescribed
standards. Equivalency decisions shall be documented and based on
demonstrated learning outcomes. Personnel unable to attend a required course
because of operational assignment shall be considered under lawful alternative
arrangements without automatic waiver of substantive competence requirements.

SEC. 52. Philippine Military Academy. — The Philippine Military Academy is
hereby continued as a military educational institution of the AFP for the education
and professional formation of officers.

      The Academy shall be under the Department through the AFP and shall be
headed by a Superintendent appointed in accordance with law.

      The Academy shall:

   (a) Conduct approved academic and military programs;

   (b) Confer baccalaureate degrees upon cadets who satisfactorily complete its
      authorized degree programs;

   (c) Maintain an Academic Board responsible for academic standards and
      recommendations for the conferment of degrees;

   (d) Develop its faculty, research, facilities, and educational resources; and

   (e) Provide for periodic external assessment of educational quality and cadet
      welfare.

   Academic degree programs shall comply with applicable higher education
standards in coordination with the Commission on Higher Education, subject to
lawful military requirements.
                                                                                   41
   Cadets shall receive the training, subsistence, medical care, allowances, and
other benefits authorized by law and appropriations. Service obligations shall be
disclosed upon admission.

   The student body shall constitute the Cadet Corps, with authorized strength
determined under approved personnel and budget plans. The Academic Board
shall recommend curricula, academic policies, and degree conferment. Cadet
assessment shall distinguish academic performance, military proficiency, and
disciplinary matters. The Superintendent shall maintain procedures for academic
review, safety, welfare, and reporting of abuse. Institutional research and faculty
development shall support the Academy’s educational mandate.

SEC. 53. Board of Visitors of the Philippine Military Academy. — The
Philippine Military Academy shall maintain a Board of Visitors composed of not
fewer than five (5) nor more than seven (7) persons appointed by the Secretary
upon the recommendation of the Superintendent.

      Members shall collectively possess experience in education, military
leadership, public administration, or professional practice. The Board shall meet
at least annually and assess academic quality, faculty development, institutional
resources, and cadet welfare.

      It shall submit recommendations to the Superintendent, Chief of Staff, and
Secretary. Its functions shall be advisory and shall not include military command,
appointment, or disciplinary adjudication. Access to records shall be subject to
lawful security and privacy requirements. Members shall disclose interests
affecting impartiality and shall receive only allowances or reimbursement
authorized by law. The Superintendent shall report the action taken on material
recommendations.



                                                                                 42
SEC. 54. Other Military Educational Institutions. — Existing AFP officer
candidate schools, command and staff institutions, noncommissioned officer
schools, technical schools, and service training institutions shall continue to
perform their authorized functions.

      Additional military training institutions may be established within
approved organizational and budgetary limits.

      Authority to conduct military training shall not, by itself, confer authority
to grant academic degrees.

      The National Defense College of the Philippines shall retain its existing
legal status. Its conversion into a university shall require separate legislation.

      Each institution shall be headed by a Superintendent, Commandant, or
other authorized officer responsible for instruction, discipline, resources, and
student welfare. Courses shall have approved objectives, admission requirements,
instructional standards, and assessment methods. Schools shall coordinate to
avoid unnecessary duplication and facilitate progression among professional
courses. Partnerships with civilian         institutions   shall specify academic
responsibility and the recognition of completed instruction.

SEC. 55. Senior Military Education and Equivalency. — Appointment or
designation to senior command positions and promotion to Brigadier General or
Commodore shall require completion of the prescribed senior military career
development course, the Master in National Security Administration, or an
equivalent program approved by the Secretary upon the recommendation of the
Chief of Staff, in addition to statutory eligibility requirements.




                                                                                     43
      Equivalency shall be based on strategic studies, leadership, joint
operations, public administration, and other relevant learning outcomes. The AFP
shall maintain documented assessment standards and provide reasonable access
to qualifying programs.

      Existing incumbents shall not be removed solely for noncompliance with
a newly prescribed educational requirement. Prospective implementation shall
commence after an appropriate transition period, not exceeding three (3) years.
Completion of a civilian degree shall not automatically confer military rank;
reserve commissions shall remain subject to applicable reserve laws and
regulations.

SEC. 56. Technical Development and Institutional Partnerships. — The AFP
may establish technical career development programs and enter into authorized
education, research, and training arrangements with academic institutions,
industry, and professional organizations.

      Such arrangements shall specify service obligations, intellectual property
rights, confidentiality requirements, funding responsibilities, and the public
purposes to be served.

      Recruitment and retention incentives shall be granted only pursuant to law
and available appropriations.

      Partnerships shall provide for the security and lawful use of research
outputs, access to facilities, and responsibility for testing and evaluation.
Personnel selected for publicly funded education shall be governed by written
service commitments and lawful repayment conditions, where applicable.
Selection shall be based on institutional requirements and relevant qualifications.
No partner shall acquire authority over military appointments or operational
decisions by reason of an educational arrangement.
                                                                                 44
SEC. 57. Personnel Welfare. — The AFP shall maintain programs for military
health, mental health, occupational safety, family assistance, casualty support,
rehabilitation, and transition to civilian life.

       Commanders shall facilitate access to authorized services and take
appropriate measures to address foreseeable risks arising from duty conditions.

       Personnel affected by illness or disability shall be provided treatment and
assessed fairly for continued service, rehabilitation, and applicable benefits.

       Medical information shall be protected in accordance with law, subject to
disclosure necessary for lawful military purposes.

       Welfare programs shall include preventive care, referral pathways,
rehabilitation, and assistance to families during deployment, injury, or death.
Units shall identify personnel responsible for casualty notification and benefits
coordination. Mental health consultations shall be treated confidentially subject
to lawful requirements, and the seeking of treatment shall not, by itself, constitute
misconduct. Commanders shall ensure that welfare concerns are addressed
without interference in clinical decisions.

SEC. 58. Compensation and Benefits. — Pay, allowances, pensions, gratuities,
insurance, disability benefits, survivor benefits, and other monetary entitlements
shall be governed by applicable laws.

       The AFP shall maintain accurate service and beneficiary records and
prescribe accessible procedures for claims and correction of errors.

       No accrued entitlement shall be reduced, and no unauthorized deduction
shall be imposed, by a rule or regulation issued under this Act.



                                                                                  45
          Proposals for additional benefits shall be supported by the appropriate
fiscal or actuarial assessment and submitted for the required legislative and
budgetary authorization.

          Payroll and benefit records shall be reconciled regularly with verified
personnel status. Claims involving missing or conflicting records shall be
processed through documented evidentiary            procedures. An unresolved
administrative discrepancy shall be referred promptly for correction and shall not
justify indefinite withholding without lawful grounds. Retirement and separation
budget proposals shall identify projected beneficiaries and obligations under the
governing laws.

SEC. 59. Citizenship and Retirement Entitlements. — The effect of loss,
retention, or reacquisition of Philippine citizenship upon military retirement and
survivor benefits shall be determined under applicable retirement and citizenship
laws, including Presidential Decree No. 1638, as amended, and Republic Act No.
9225.

          Before terminating benefits on citizenship grounds, the administering
authority shall verify the relevant facts, identify the controlling legal provision,
give notice, and afford the affected person an opportunity to submit proof of
retention or reacquisition. Residence abroad or possession of a foreign travel
document shall not, by itself, substitute for a legal determination of citizenship
status.

          Survivor claims shall be assessed under the law governing the particular
entitlement. No derivative forfeiture shall be imposed solely by administrative
rule. A decision shall state its effective date and available remedies. Nothing
herein shall create an entitlement prohibited by law or authorize double payment
for the same period.

                                                                                 46
SEC. 60. Separation and Personnel Remedies. — Separation, discharge,
revocation of commission, and attrition shall be effected only upon grounds and
through procedures authorized by law.

      Subject to applicable constitutional and statutory exceptions, the affected
person shall be furnished notice, afforded an opportunity to be heard, and
provided a written disposition.

      Administrative grievance and review procedures shall not impair access to
judicial remedies or independent oversight bodies as provided by law.

      Proceedings shall distinguish disciplinary separation, performance-based
attrition, medical separation, resignation, and expiration of enlistment. The
disposition shall identify the competent deciding authority, effective date, service
characterization, and available review procedure. Exit processing shall include
clearance of government property, completion of service records, and referral of
benefit claims. Administrative delay shall not change a statutory retirement date
or create an unauthorized extension of service.

SEC. 61. Recall of Retired Military Personnel. — The President may recall
qualified retired military personnel to active duty in accordance with applicable
laws and defense requirements. Recall orders shall identify the legal basis, duties,
service period, permanent grade, reporting authority, and conditions of release.

      Medical fitness and professional suitability shall be assessed before
assignment. Recalled personnel shall serve in the last permanent grade held
before retirement, unless a different status or promotion is authorized by law.
Recall shall not convert a retirement grade granted solely for benefit computation
into an active command rank.



                                                                                  47
         Pay, allowances, continued pension status, and adjustment of benefits shall
be determined under the governing retirement law. The AFP shall prevent
duplicate payment where prohibited and maintain a complete service record.
Military jurisdiction shall extend only as authorized by law. Upon release, the
personnel concerned shall receive orders documenting the period served and the
lawful disposition of any resulting claims.



                                     TITLE VI
                   RESERVE FORCE AND MOBILIZATION

SEC. 62. Reserve Force. — The organization, development, administration,
training, maintenance, and utilization of the Reserve Force shall be governed by
Republic Act No. 7077 and other applicable laws.

         Reserve development shall form part of military strategy, capability
planning, and readiness assessment.

         Reserve units shall have prescribed missions, personnel requirements,
equipment arrangements, training programs, and relationships with the Regular
Force.

         Reserve organization shall conform to projected and actual force
requirements and approved tables of organization and equipment. Planning shall
identify mobilization assignments and the regular units or commands supported.
Reserve personnel with technical qualifications shall be assigned to duties
appropriate to their competence. Regular and reserve training shall develop
compatible procedures and shall preserve the civilian status of inactive reservists.




                                                                                  48
SEC. 63. Reserve Components and Classification. — The Reserve Force shall
consist of the Army Reserve Component, Air Force Reserve Component, Navy
Reserve Component, AFP-Wide Technical Reserve Component, and Affiliated
Reserves, in accordance with Republic Act No. 7077.

      Its categorization and classification into Ready Reserve, Standby Reserve,
and Retired Reserve shall remain governed by that law. The AFP shall not reduce
these components or alter their activation conditions solely through an
organizational order.

      The Secretary shall approve reserve organizational tables and manpower
requirements within lawful authority. Reserve units shall have designated
mobilization centers, contact arrangements, and responsible regular-force
counterparts. Their organization shall take account of geographic distribution,
civilian expertise, equipment availability, and actual operational needs. Affiliated
units shall retain their distinctive functions while being coordinated with the
appropriate service or joint requirement.

SEC. 64. Reserve Registry and Readiness. — The AFP shall maintain an
accurate and secure reserve registry containing the qualifications, assignments,
training, availability, and service status of reservists.

      Registry administration shall be coordinated with civilian authorities
designated by law and shall comply with applicable data protection requirements.

      Reserve readiness shall be assessed through authorized training,
verification of availability, and evaluation of unit requirements.

      Registration shall not constitute authority to require active service beyond
the conditions prescribed by law.



                                                                                  49
        Registry entries shall include verified contact information, relevant civilian
expertise, medical status where lawfully required, and unit assignments.
Reservists shall be provided procedures to correct erroneous information and
notify changes in availability. Local coordination shall comply with statutory
responsibilities and shall not authorize unrestricted access to personal data.
Registry security shall include access controls, retention rules, and procedures for
responding to unauthorized disclosure.

SEC. 65. Military Training and Student Programs. — Reserve Officers’
Training Corps and other military training programs shall be administered
pursuant to Republic Act No. 7077, Republic Act No. 9163, and other applicable
laws.

        Nothing herein shall render compulsory a program made optional by law
or remove an obligation separately imposed by law.

        Training arrangements shall prescribe institutional responsibilities,
instructor qualifications, safety requirements, funding, complaint procedures, and
safeguards against abuse.

        The AFP shall coordinate with participating schools concerning instructor
conduct, approved training schedules, student safety, and referral of complaints.
Training shall not interfere with academic requirements beyond arrangements
authorized by law. Students shall be informed of program status, authorized
charges, and the conditions for reserve incorporation. Hazing, unauthorized
punishment, and the solicitation of unofficial payments shall be reported to the
competent authorities.

SEC. 66. Activation and Mobilization. — Reservists may be called to active
duty, reserve units may be mobilized, and military service may be required only
by the competent authority, upon grounds and for periods prescribed by law.
                                                                                    50
         The AFP shall maintain mobilization plans providing for personnel
reception, medical screening, training, equipment, transportation, command
arrangements, and sustainment.

         Activation orders shall state the legal basis, mission, reporting instructions,
applicable entitlements, and period of service whenever determinable.

         Affected personnel shall be notified promptly, and applicable employment
protections shall be implemented in coordination with the competent civilian
authorities.

         Orders shall distinguish voluntary training, compulsory service authorized
by law, unit mobilization, and individual deployment. Medical fitness and
occupational qualifications shall be verified before assignment. Released
reservists shall receive records of service and instructions concerning outstanding
claims. Mobilization reports shall identify activated strength, duration,
expenditure, casualties, and capability limitations, subject to lawful security
restrictions.

SEC. 67. Affiliated Reserve Units. — Affiliated reserve arrangements shall be
established and implemented in accordance with law for defense preparedness
and the continuity of essential services.

         Such arrangements shall specify personnel status, training, activation
conditions, equipment access, command relationships, and responsibility for
costs.

         Affiliation shall not, by itself, transfer ownership or general management
of an enterprise to the AFP.




                                                                                      51
      Affiliated units shall maintain rosters, continuity plans, and arrangements
for training without unnecessary interruption of essential services. Agreements
shall identify the equipment and facilities available upon lawful activation and
the conditions governing their use. Civilian employment shall not confer a
military commission automatically. The functions of affiliated units shall remain
consistent with the legal status and responsibilities of participating organizations.

SEC. 68. Resource Mobilization and Demobilization. — Defense resource
planning shall identify requirements for transportation, communications, energy,
medical support, production, and other capabilities necessary for authorized
military operations.

      Compulsory acquisition, requisition, temporary takeover, or use of private
property or services shall require a sufficient constitutional or statutory basis and
compliance with due process and applicable compensation requirements.

      Mobilization plans shall provide for release from duty, return or lawful
disposition of property, settlement of obligations, rehabilitation, and restoration
of ordinary civilian arrangements.

      Resource estimates shall distinguish government-owned assets, assets
available by agreement, and resources requiring separate legal authority. Civilian
departments shall retain responsibility for their respective sectoral plans.
Demobilization shall include verification of returned property, documentation of
damage or loss, and referral of compensation claims. Records shall permit audit
of emergency transactions and identification of obligations remaining after
release of personnel and resources.




                                                                                   52
                                    TITLE VII
                          MILITARY OPERATIONS

SEC. 69. Authorization and Planning. — Military operations shall be
undertaken for a lawful purpose, pursuant to competent authority, and under
defined command responsibility.

      Operational plans shall state the mission, legal basis, assigned forces,
command arrangements, resource requirements, civilian risks, interagency
responsibilities, and conditions for termination or transition.

      Action taken under standing authority in urgent circumstances shall be
documented and reported as soon as practicable.

      Operational directives shall prescribe mission limits, coordination
requirements, communications procedures, and the authority for material
changes. Commanders shall review whether assigned forces and support are
sufficient for the mission and report material deficiencies. The continuation of an
operation shall be reassessed when its legal basis, objectives, or operating
circumstances materially change. Termination instructions shall provide for force
recovery and reconciliation of personnel and property records.

SEC. 70. Rules Governing the Use of Force. — The AFP shall prescribe written
rules governing the use of force appropriate to the legal character and
circumstances of each mission.

      Such rules shall distinguish armed conflict, law enforcement assistance,
protection of military installations, and other authorized activities and shall
prescribe authorization levels, identification requirements, restrictions, reporting,
medical response, and investigation procedures.



                                                                                  53
      No operational instruction shall authorize an act prohibited by the
Constitution, statute, or applicable international law.

      The legal classification of an operation or situation shall be determined by
the applicable law and facts, irrespective of its administrative designation.

      Rules shall be explained to personnel before deployment and reviewed
when the mission or legal circumstances change. Training shall include reporting
of incidents and the duty to provide medical assistance when required.
Operational necessity shall not dispense with applicable legal restrictions.
Commanders shall preserve records of significant use-of-force decisions and refer
credible allegations of unlawful conduct for investigation.

SEC. 71. Territorial and Maritime Defense. — Territorial defense shall be
conducted through coordinated land, naval, air, intelligence, communications,
logistics, and reserve capabilities.

      Operations in maritime zones shall conform to applicable Philippine laws
and international law, including the distinctions among sovereignty, sovereign
rights, and jurisdiction.

      Coordination with civilian maritime authorities shall prescribe lead
responsibilities, information sharing, communications, and incident response
procedures.




                                                                                54
      Maritime and territorial plans shall provide for surveillance, identification,
communications, reinforcement, protection of forces, and logistical support.
Where civilian agencies exercise enforcement jurisdiction, military support shall
be defined by the applicable coordination arrangement. Incident reports shall
distinguish verified facts from assessment and shall record the authority for
significant operational decisions. Escalation measures shall remain subject to
competent direction and applicable legal restrictions.

SEC. 72. Internal Security and Law Enforcement Assistance. — AFP
participation in internal security shall be undertaken pursuant to constitutional
and statutory authority and lawful presidential direction.

      Routine policing and criminal investigation shall remain the responsibility
of the competent civilian agencies.

      An authorized military assistance arrangement shall specify the mission,
participating agencies, command relationships, permitted activities, applicable
use-of-force rules, and termination or transition arrangements.

      Nothing herein shall independently confer general police powers upon
AFP personnel.

      Military assistance shall be proportionate to the authorized requirement
and reviewed periodically with the responsible civilian authority. Orders shall
distinguish   military   operational   command      from     civilian   enforcement
responsibility. Personnel assigned to support duties shall receive appropriate
instruction in applicable legal procedures. The transfer of responsibility to
civilian authorities shall be planned from the commencement of assistance
whenever operational circumstances permit.



                                                                                  55
SEC. 73. Arrest, Search, Seizure, and Custody. — Any arrest, search, seizure,
interception, or deprivation of liberty undertaken by military personnel shall have
a lawful basis and comply with constitutional and statutory safeguards.

      Persons taken into custody shall be registered, treated humanely, informed
of applicable rights, afforded necessary medical assistance, and transferred or
presented to the proper authority within the period prescribed by law.

      Custody arising from armed conflict shall comply with applicable domestic
law and international humanitarian law.

      Secret or unofficial detention facilities and unacknowledged custody are
prohibited.

      Custody records shall identify the person, time and place of custody, legal
grounds, receiving unit, medical condition, and transfer history. Access by
counsel, family, courts, and authorized oversight bodies shall be afforded as
required by law. Seized property shall be inventoried and transferred under
documented procedures. No military order shall excuse concealment of a person’s
whereabouts or obstruction of lawful judicial process.

SEC. 74. Disaster Response and Humanitarian Assistance. — The AFP may
provide transportation, engineering, communications, medical, rescue, logistics,
and other assistance pursuant to applicable disaster management laws.

      Civilian disaster authorities shall retain their statutory responsibilities.

      AFP units may provide immediate assistance to protect life within their
lawful authority and available capacity, subject to prompt coordination and
reporting.



                                                                                     56
       Assistance shall be provided impartially and shall not be conditioned upon
political support, religious affiliation, or participation in military activities.

       Requests for assistance shall identify the support required and the
responsible civilian coordinating authority. Deployed units shall account for
personnel, equipment, relief goods, and expenditures. Relief distribution shall be
documented to preserve accountability and prevent diversion. Operations shall
include arrangements for withdrawal or transition when civilian capacity is
restored, without prejudice to continuing military assistance lawfully requested
and authorized.

SEC. 75. Critical Infrastructure Protection. — The AFP may protect designated
installations or assist in securing critical infrastructure pursuant to law or lawful
presidential direction.

       The competent civilian agency or lawful operator shall retain its regulatory
and operational responsibilities unless otherwise provided by law.

       Security    arrangements       shall    specify    military     tasks,    access,
communications, duration, resource responsibilities, and safeguards for affected
persons.

       Protection plans shall assess threats, access requirements, continuity needs,
and interaction with civilian security arrangements. Military personnel shall not
assume regulatory or commercial management functions solely by reason of
security deployment. The duration and scope of support shall be reviewed
periodically. Records shall identify the authority for deployment and any material
incident affecting the installation or surrounding communities.




                                                                                      57
SEC. 76. Military Intelligence. — Military intelligence activities shall be
undertaken for defense assessment, force protection, and authorized military
operations.

      The collection, retention, analysis, and dissemination of information shall
be limited to lawful purposes and subject to appropriate authorization, security,
and review.

      Nothing herein shall confer authority for surveillance, interception, access
to protected communications, or compulsory disclosure beyond that separately
established by law.

      Lawful political activity, journalism, religious practice, labor organizing,
academic activity, or peaceful advocacy shall not, by itself, constitute a military
intelligence threat.

      Intelligence requirements shall be approved through designated channels
and linked to an authorized military purpose. Information shall be assessed for
reliability and disseminated only to persons with lawful need for access.
Retention and disposal schedules shall be prescribed. An allegation affecting a
person shall distinguish verified information from uncorroborated reporting, and
erroneous records shall be corrected through authorized procedures.

SEC. 77. Cyber Defense and Emerging Technologies. — The AFP shall develop
capabilities for the protection of military networks, systems, data, and authorized
operations.

      Military cyber activities affecting systems outside AFP control shall
require a sufficient legal basis and the appropriate authorization.

      Weapons and operational technologies shall undergo legal, safety,
cybersecurity, and operational review prior to deployment.
                                                                                 58
      The employment of artificial intelligence or autonomous functions shall
preserve identifiable human responsibility and appropriate human judgment over
decisions involving the use of force.

      Cybersecurity     programs shall include incident          reporting, access
management, continuity arrangements, technical training, and assessment of
third-party risks. Operational systems shall maintain records sufficient to identify
authorized users and significant decisions. Technology review shall consider
reliability, foreseeable failure, data integrity, and the ability to terminate unsafe
operation. Procurement and deployment approvals shall identify responsibility
for maintenance and continuing security assessment.

SEC. 78. International Defense Cooperation and Deployment. — Military
exchanges, exercises, training, peace support operations, and other foreign
deployments shall be undertaken pursuant to competent authority and applicable
laws and agreements.

      Deployment authority shall specify the mission, command arrangements,
funding, jurisdictional provisions, use-of-force rules, personnel protections, and
reporting requirements.

      Nothing herein shall independently authorize foreign military bases,
troops, or facilities in the Philippines or dispense with any constitutional
requirement governing treaties or international agreements.




                                                                                  59
      Participation shall be supported by an assessment of military benefit,
personnel requirements, costs, applicable jurisdiction, and operational risks.
Personnel shall receive instruction on the governing agreement and rules before
deployment. Activities shall be evaluated upon completion, and material
incidents shall be reported through diplomatic and military channels as
appropriate. No subordinate arrangement shall amend a treaty obligation or
confer immunity beyond applicable law.

SEC. 79. Operational Incidents and Civilian Harm. — Commanders shall cause
the timely review of significant operational incidents, including death, serious
injury, alleged violations of law, and material damage to civilian property.

      The AFP shall preserve evidence, cooperate with lawful investigations, and
maintain accessible procedures for reports and claims by affected persons.

      Assistance authorized by law and supported by appropriations may be
extended to affected civilians without requiring the waiver of legal remedies.

      An operational review shall not substitute for a criminal investigation or
impair the jurisdiction of a competent authority.

      Initial reports shall identify known facts, immediate protective measures,
and the preservation of relevant records. Review officers shall avoid conflicts of
interest and shall distinguish operational assessment from determination of
criminal liability. Findings requiring action by another agency shall be referred
promptly. Information supplied to affected persons shall be accurate and
consistent with lawful confidentiality and the protection of witnesses.




                                                                                 60
                                   TITLE VIII

     MILITARY DISCIPLINE, JUSTICE, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

SEC. 80. Lawful Orders and Command Responsibility. — Military personnel
shall obey lawful orders and faithfully discharge their duties.

       A manifestly unlawful order shall not be obeyed. The AFP shall prescribe
procedures for seeking clarification and reporting suspected illegality consistent
with operational requirements.

       Commanders shall take lawful measures to prevent violations, act upon
credible reports, preserve evidence, and refer matters to competent authorities.

       Criminal liability arising from command responsibility shall be determined
in accordance with applicable law. Rank or command position alone shall not
establish criminal liability.

       Orders shall be sufficiently clear to permit lawful execution and shall
identify the issuing authority. A request for clarification shall be recorded when
practicable. Commanders shall establish reporting channels for suspected
violations and shall prevent intimidation of witnesses. The lawful refusal of a
manifestly unlawful order shall be assessed according to the circumstances and
applicable law, without treating obedience as an automatic defense to prohibited
conduct.

SEC. 81. Military Justice and Civilian Jurisdiction. — Military justice shall be
administered pursuant to Commonwealth Act No. 408, as amended, Republic Act
No. 7055, and other applicable laws.



                                                                                   61
      The jurisdiction of civil courts and courts-martial shall not be modified by
a rule or regulation issued under this Act.

      Military administrative or disciplinary proceedings shall not obstruct
criminal investigation, prosecution, judicial process, or the lawful exercise of
oversight authority.

      Persons subject to proceedings shall be afforded the protections guaranteed
by the Constitution and applicable laws.

      The AFP shall provide trained personnel and appropriate administrative
support for the lawful administration of military justice. Charging, investigation,
adjudication, and review functions shall be allocated consistently with governing
law. Case records shall be maintained securely, and lawful orders of civil courts
shall be complied with. Administrative settlement shall not extinguish an offense
or defeat prosecution where the law requires criminal proceedings.

SEC. 82. Administrative Disciplinary Measures. — Administrative disciplinary
measures shall be imposed only by competent authority, upon lawful grounds,
and after the proceedings required by law. Authorized measures may include
reprimand, suspension, reduction in grade, demotion, or forfeiture or withholding
of pay where expressly permitted by the governing law.

      Regulations      shall   prescribe      the    authority,    limits,   procedure,
documentation, and review applicable to each measure. A regulatory provision
shall not authorize a penalty absent a statutory basis or alter a presidential
appointment beyond lawful authority.                Confirmation    requirements   and
constitutional protections shall be observed.




                                                                                     62
       Interim relief from duty for operational or investigative purposes shall be
distinguished from a final disciplinary penalty. Pay shall not be withheld solely
by reason of an unresolved allegation without legal authority. The final
disposition shall state its factual and legal basis, effective date, and available
remedies. Administrative action shall remain without prejudice to proceedings
before the competent court or tribunal.

SEC. 83. Ethical Standards and Conflicts of Interest. — AFP personnel shall
comply with applicable laws governing public office, financial disclosure, gifts,
outside employment, confidential information, and conflicts of interest.

       Personnel participating in procurement, selection, inspection, acceptance,
or contract administration shall disclose relevant interests and inhibit themselves
when required by law.

       Military office, rank, insignia, official information, or influence shall not
be used for unauthorized private gain.

       Officials shall not solicit personal benefits from suppliers, applicants, or
subordinate personnel. Confidential information obtained by reason of office
shall be used only for authorized purposes. Financial or other interests affecting
impartial performance shall be disclosed to the proper authority. Commanders
shall ensure that integrity training and compliance procedures are included in
institutional   administration   without   replacing   independent enforcement
mechanisms.

SEC. 84. Inspector General. — The AFP shall maintain an Inspector General
function for inspection, investigation,       assessment of compliance, and
recommendation of corrective measures.



                                                                                 63
       The Inspector General shall have access to personnel, records, and
installations necessary for the lawful performance of such functions.

       Where a matter concerns the Chief of Staff or presents a material conflict
of interest, the Inspector General may report directly to the Secretary.

       Nothing herein shall diminish the powers of the Commission on Audit,
Ombudsman, Commission on Human Rights, courts, or other competent bodies.

SEC. 85. Complaints and Protection Against Retaliation. — The AFP shall
maintain accessible procedures for complaints by personnel and members of the
public.

       Retaliation against a person for making a good-faith complaint or lawful
disclosure, cooperating with an investigation, or refusing a manifestly unlawful
order is prohibited.

       Secure reporting procedures shall be provided for information subject to
lawful security restrictions. Such procedures shall not authorize public disclosure
contrary to law.

       The failure to substantiate a complaint shall not, by itself, establish that the
complaint was malicious.

       Complaint procedures shall provide acknowledgment, referral, status
information, and secure handling of evidence. Anonymous information may be
evaluated according to its specificity and reliability, without presuming its
accuracy. Persons accused shall be afforded applicable procedural rights. The
protection of complainants and witnesses shall be coordinated with competent
authorities, and disciplinary or criminal remedies shall be pursued under existing
law.


                                                                                    64
SEC. 86. Legal Assistance for Service-Related Cases. — The AFP shall
administer legal assistance for qualified military personnel pursuant to Republic
Act No. 12177. Coverage shall include eligible retired, honorably discharged, or
separated personnel facing proceedings arising from incidents during their active
service.

      Applications and referrals shall be processed within the periods prescribed
by law. The AFP legal office shall determine service connection and statutory
eligibility from the evidence submitted and shall record its decision. Conflict-of-
interest limitations shall be applied according to the governing law and shall not
be disregarded by command approval.

      Assistance may include representation, advice, preparation of pleadings,
and authorized related expenses. The engagement of private counsel and
reimbursement of fees shall require lawful funding and procurement authority.
Legal offices shall maintain appropriate staffing and case records. The grant of
assistance shall not constitute an institutional finding of innocence or obstruct
independent investigation or prosecution.

                                       TITLE IX

                     DEFENSE RESOURCES AND SUPPORT

      SEC. 87. Resource Management. — AFP budget proposals shall be based
on approved missions, force requirements, readiness standards, and capability
development plans.

      Resource proposals shall identify personnel, operations, maintenance,
infrastructure, acquisition, and sustainment costs.

      The Secretary shall ensure that proposed acquisitions are supported by
adequate arrangements for operation and sustainment.
                                                                                65
      Budget execution shall comply with appropriation, accounting, auditing,
and other applicable laws.

      Budget preparation shall distinguish recurring expenditure from temporary
program costs and identify the resources needed to operate acquired capabilities.
Financial responsibilities shall be separated among requesting, approving,
receiving, and accounting offices as required by law. Material deviations from
approved plans shall be reported with their causes and operational effects.
Savings and realignments shall be undertaken only through lawful budget
authority.

SEC. 88. Modernization and Procurement. — AFP modernization shall be
undertaken pursuant to Republic Act No. 7898, as amended by Republic Act No.
10349, and other applicable laws.

      Procurement shall be governed by Republic Act No. 12009 and other
applicable laws, including authorized special arrangements and transition
provisions.

      No general exemption from procurement law is granted under this Act.

      Major acquisitions shall be supported by validated requirements, lifecycle
costing,     interoperability   assessment,   training   and   infrastructure   plans,
sustainment arrangements, acceptance standards, and contract risk assessment.

      Multi-year obligations shall require the applicable budgetary and
contractual authority.




                                                                                   66
      Acceptance shall be based on verified compliance with contract
specifications and prescribed testing. Contracts shall allocate responsibilities for
defects, training, maintenance, supply, documentation, and intellectual property
as appropriate. Material changes shall undergo authorized review. Delivery of
equipment shall not be treated as operational capability until personnel, facilities,
training, and sustainment requirements have been satisfied or the remaining
limitations have been expressly recorded.

SEC. 89. Self-Reliant Defense and Research. — The AFP shall coordinate its
capability requirements with programs established under Republic Act No. 12024
and other applicable laws supporting domestic defense capability.

      For this purpose, it may coordinate with the Department, Government
Arsenal, scientific institutions, industry, and other authorized partners in research,
testing, technology transfer, maintenance, and domestic production or support.

      Specifications shall consider operational suitability, quality, security, cost,
and the development of domestic capability in accordance with law.

      Nothing herein shall independently establish a tax incentive, import
exemption, industrial privilege, or exclusive supply right.

      Research projects shall specify objectives, milestones, technical risks,
funding, ownership of outputs, and arrangements for testing. Cooperation with
domestic enterprises      shall comply with procurement and competition
requirements. Military end-user assessments shall identify actual operational
needs. Technology transfer arrangements shall include measures for retaining
acquired knowledge and sustaining relevant skills, without conferring
procurement preference beyond that permitted by law.



                                                                                   67
SEC. 90. Logistics and Property Administration. — The AFP shall maintain
accountable    systems    for supply, inventory, maintenance,        ammunition,
transportation, medical logistics, and property disposition.

      Defense real property shall be administered for lawful defense purposes
and protected against unauthorized occupation, encumbrance, or disposal.

      The sale, lease, exchange, joint development, conversion, or disposition of
military property shall be governed by applicable laws, including Republic Act
No. 7227, as amended, where applicable.

      Proceeds shall be deposited, allocated, and expended as prescribed by law.
No off-budget expenditure or modification of a statutory revenue allocation is
authorized herein.

      Property records shall distinguish custodial responsibility, ownership,
restrictions, and authorized use. Inventories shall be reconciled periodically, and
loss or damage shall be reported through prescribed procedures. Military
reservations shall have documented boundaries and land records. Before any
lawful disposition, competent authorities shall assess continuing military
requirements and relocation costs. Environmental, safety, and land-use
requirements shall be observed in development and disposal activities.

SEC. 91. Contracts, Grants, and Donations. — Duly authorized officials may
enter into contracts and accept grants, assistance, and donations for AFP
purposes, subject to applicable procurement, budget, audit, foreign relations, and
other laws.

      Acceptance shall take into account imposed conditions, security
implications, lifecycle costs, and operational suitability.

      All transactions shall be duly recorded and reported.
                                                                                 68
      No agreement shall confer command authority upon an unauthorized
person, require disclosure prohibited by law, or create an obligation of public
funds without lawful authority.

      Delegations to sign contracts or accept assistance shall specify their scope
and financial limits. Donations requiring recurring expenditure shall be assessed
before acceptance. Foreign assistance shall be coordinated with the competent
civilian authorities. Equipment received shall undergo inspection and be entered
in accountable property records. Acceptance shall not dispense with licensing,
safety, import, or other requirements imposed by law.




                                   TITLE X

    OVERSIGHT, IMPLEMENTATION, AND FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 92. Annual Report. — Within ninety (90) days after the end of each
calendar year, the Secretary shall submit to the President and Congress an annual
report on the AFP covering:

   (a) Mission performance and strategic risks;

   (b) Authorized and actual personnel strength;

   (c) Readiness and capability development;

   (d) Reserve preparedness;

   (e) Personnel welfare and professional military education;

   (f) Budget execution and significant procurement matters;

   (g) Material audit findings and corrective measures;

                                                                               69
   (h) Operational accountability and institutional complaints; and

   (i) Recommended legislative measures.

   An unclassified version shall be published. Information subject to lawful
security restrictions may be submitted in a classified annex.

   Classification shall not extinguish the constitutional authority of Congress, the
courts, or oversight bodies. Claims of privilege and disputes concerning access
shall be resolved in accordance with the Constitution and applicable law.

   The report shall distinguish verified accomplishments from targets and
describe unresolved material deficiencies. Classified annexes shall identify the
responsible reporting offices and the basis for restricted disclosure. Congress may
request supplementary information in accordance with its constitutional powers.
Reporting shall be coordinated with existing statutory obligations to reduce
duplication while preserving the information required for effective oversight.

SEC. 93. Consolidation of Implementing              Issuances and Legislative
Harmonization. — Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the
Department shall prepare a consolidated code of AFP implementing rules,
regulations, and administrative issuances concerning matters governed herein.

      The consolidated code shall identify the statutory basis of its provisions,
distinguish rules of general application from classified operational instructions,
and specify the prior issuances superseded.

      It shall not amend a statute, create a criminal offense, alter judicial
jurisdiction, establish an unauthorized benefit, or enlarge a coercive power.

      Within the same period, the Department shall submit to Congress a
legislative harmonization report containing:

                                                                                 70
   (a) An inventory of relevant laws and issuances;

   (b) A schedule of provisions and references affected by the repeal of
      Commonwealth Act No. 1;

   (c) Identified statutory gaps, overlaps, and inconsistencies;

   (d) Proposed consequential amendments; and

   (e) Recommendations for specialized legislation.

   The report shall address, among other matters, military personnel and
pensions, reserves, military justice, modernization and procurement, military
education, auxiliaries, and emerging defense capabilities.

   The Department shall prepare the harmonization report with the participation
of AFP legal and personnel offices and consultation with relevant civilian
agencies. Proposed amendments shall identify the law affected and the specific
inconsistency or gap addressed. The consolidated code shall contain a public
index of issuances, their authority, and status. Classified instruments shall be
indexed without disclosing protected operational content.

SEC. 94. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary, in coordination with the
AFP and relevant government agencies, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation.

      The Department shall consult affected personnel, reserve organizations,
relevant civilian institutions, and appropriate professional and academic bodies.

      Rules of general application shall comply with applicable publication and
administrative rule-making requirements. Classified instructions shall not impose
unpublished obligations or penalties upon the public.
                                                                                  71
        The absence of implementing rules shall not suspend the operation of
provisions capable of direct application.

SEC. 95. Transitory and Savings Provisions. — Implementation of this Act shall
preserve defense readiness and the continuity of lawful military functions.

     Unless altered pursuant to lawful authority, the following shall remain valid
and effective:

     (a) Existing commissions, enlistments, appointments, assignments, and
        service status;

     (b) Existing commands, major services, units, offices, and military
        institutions;

     (c) Accrued compensation, benefits, pension rights, and valid claims;

     (d) Contracts, property rights, appropriations, records, and obligations;

     (e) Pending judicial, disciplinary, administrative, and audit proceedings; and

     (f) Existing rules, regulations, and orders consistent with the Constitution, this
        Act, and other applicable laws.

     Existing military educational institutions and their lawful authority to confer
degrees shall continue.

        No person shall be separated from service, reduced in rank, or deprived of

SEC. 96. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against available appropriations of the Department
and the AFP, subject to applicable budget laws.

      Thereafter, the amounts necessary for its continued implementation shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      New organizational and program expenditures shall be supported by cost
estimates and the required budgetary approvals.

      Nothing herein shall constitute an automatic appropriation or authorize
expenditure in excess of available appropriations.

SEC. 97. Periodic Review. — Five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act, and
every five (5) years thereafter, the Department shall submit to Congress an
assessment of its implementation.

      The assessment shall cover organizational effectiveness, civilian oversight,
readiness, personnel administration, operational compliance with law, resource
management, and the operation of this Act in relation to specialized legislation.

      The Department shall recommend amendments as may be necessary. The
conduct or pendency of a review shall not suspend the operation of this Act.

SEC. 98. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 99. Repealing Clause. — Commonwealth Act No. 1, otherwise known as
the National Defense Act, as amended, is hereby repealed, subject to Section 95
of this Act. Republic Act No. 3835, as amended, is likewise repealed pursuant to
the section on integration of women in the major services.

       Commonwealth Act No. 408, Republic Act No. 7055, Republic Act No.
7077, Republic Act No. 7898 as amended by Republic Act No. 10349, Republic
Act No. 9163, Republic Act No. 11709 as amended by Republic Act No. 11939,
Republic Act No. 12009, Republic Act No. 12024, Republic Act No. 12177,
Presidential Decree No. 1638 as amended, and other special laws shall remain in
force, except to the extent expressly amended by this Act.

       The Administrative Code of 1987 and laws governing other defense and
civilian institutions shall remain operative, except for provisions directly
inconsistent with an express provision herein.

       All other laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative orders, rules,
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly: Provided, That no provision of a special
law shall be deemed repealed solely because its subject is also addressed in
general terms by this Act.

      No law or provision previously repealed shall be revived by the enactment
of this Act.

SEC. 100. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
