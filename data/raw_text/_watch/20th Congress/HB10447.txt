                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
       Local farmers and agricultural producers are often at the mercy of seasonal
calamities, economic crises, pressure from middlemen, and other pain points in the
agricultural supply and value chain. As such, these important producers are often
confronted with having to sell their harvest at below-market prices or to throw it away
altogether, resulting in large-scale food loss. Most of such losses happen post-harvest,
where the lack of post-harvesting facilities and handling result in losses up to fifty
percent (50%) for crops such as rice, wheat, and cereal grains which makes up a
staggering seventy percent (70%) of all calories consumed in the country1.
        These figures indicate that roughly forty percent (40%) of all food being
produced are going to waste, with around twenty percent (20%) of all carrots,
bananas, cabbage, calamansi, eggplant, mangoes, sweet potatoes, rice and tomatoes
going to waste2. With such a pressing need to divert food loss, support farmers and
producers, and strengthen food security, a clear mandate is needed to allow
government institutions and food service establishments to prioritize the purchase of
locally grown harvest and produce.
       The Philippines, with its growing population and aging harvest and food chain
infrastructure, is especially vulnerable to worsening climate impacts and
unpredictable economic swings. By prioritizing the purchase of food from local
suppliers and growers, the country is not only empowering its own agricultural
workers; helping food security; and contribute towards our Nationally Determined
Contributions (NDCs)3, an important action point under the Paris Agreement and an
active agenda of the Climate Change Commission (CCC), the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), and the country’s National Climate
Change Action Plan (NCCAP).
        It is for these pressing needs that the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
      MANDATING GOVERNMENT INSTITUTIONS AND FOOD SERVICE
 ESTABLISHMENTS TO PURCHASE THE AGRICULTURAL PRODUCE OF LOCAL
FARMERS OR PRODUCERS FOR THEIR FOOD PREPARATION AND PRODUCTION
      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Pagkaing Pilipino Act
of 2026.”
       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State to
ensure local food security and spur the development of the Filipino agriculture
industry. As such, the government must give priority to the produce of local farmers
and producers in terms of the purchases of government institutions and food service
establishments intended for food preparation and production.
       SECTION 3. Mandatory Purchase. - Government Institution and Food Service
Establishments, such as hotels, restaurants, and catering services, shall prioritize the
purchase of local agricultural produce of local farmers for their food preparation and
production.
       Moreover, the produce of local farmers within the territorial jurisdiction of the
local government unit (LGU) wherein the government institution and food service
establishment is located shall take precedence over produce outside their respective
LGUs to lower transportation or other hauling costs in the delivery of such produce.
       Nothing in this Act shall be construed as prohibiting the government institution
and food service establishment to purchase local produce in different political
subdivisions, provided that the requirement herein set forth is complied by the food
service establishment.
        SECTION 4. Exceptions. - Section 3 of this Act shall not be applicable in any of
the following cases where the local produce is:
          a) No longer fit for human consumption;
          b)​ Insufficient in quantity;
          c)​ More costly and impractical compared to imported or foreign sources of
              produce; and
          d)​ Inferior in quality.
       SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Interior
and Local Government (DILG), in coordination with Department of Agriculture (DA),
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR), the
Department of Health (DOH), and other relevant government agencies and
non-government organizations, shall formulate and promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations and other issuance as may be necessary to ensure the effective
implementation of the provisions of this Act within sixty (60) days from the approval
thereof. The implementing rules and regulations issued pursuant to this section shall
take effect (30) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or any newspaper of
general circulation.
       SECTION 6. Appropriations. – Such amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation
for the concerned agencies. Thereafter, the funds necessary for this Act’s continuous
and effective implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.
      SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
proclamations, rules, and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act, are
hereby amended or modified accordingly.
       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.
      SECTION 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
