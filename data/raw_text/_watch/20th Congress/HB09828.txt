                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to penalize fraudulent acts committed against Overseas
Filipino Workers (OFWs). This includes investment scams, false sales, hidden
charges, coercive financial practices, and financial account scamming.

       OFWs sustain families, communities, and the national economy through
their labor and remittances. However, their distance from the country, reliance on
digital transactions, and limited access to immediate legal remedies expose them
to fraudulent aches that target their savings, income, and financial accounts.
These factors not only cause personal financial loss, but also undermine the
welfare of families who depend on OFW remittances for education, health care,
housing, and daily subsistence.

      Fraud committed against OFWs require a more specific legal response
because perpetrators often exploit their distinct circumstances as migrant
workers. This bill strengthens protection for overseas workers and their
dependents by expressly defining prohibited fraudulent acts, imposing higher
penalties, and allowing restitution for financial losses suffered by the victims.

      The proposed measure affirms the State’s duty to protect migrant workers
not only from labor-related abuse, but also from financial exploitation that
deprives them of the fruits of their work abroad. It establishes a clear deterrent
against persons and entities that prey on OFWs through deceit,
misrepresentation, coercion, or abuse of financial accounts.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PENALIZING FRAUD AGAINST OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS AND
PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATION THEREOF

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Overseas Filipino
Workers’ (OFW) Fraud Protection Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vulnerability of
Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) and their dependents to fraudulent schemes
and predatory financial practices. It is hereby the declared policy of the State to
safeguard the financial security of OFWs by penalizing deceitful practices,
investment scams, and financial crimes that undermine their economic welfare.

       To this end, the State shall provide mechanisms to deter exploitation and
ensure that the hard-earned savings of migrant workers are protected from bad
faith, thereby preserving the fruits of their labor for the well-being of their
families.

      SEC. 3. Definitions. – As used in this Act:

          a. Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a Filipino who is to be
      engaged, is engaged, or has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a
      country in which he or she is not an immigrant, citizen, or permanent
      resident or is not awaiting naturalization, recognition, or admission,
      whether land-based or sea-based regardless of status; excluding a Filipino
      engaged under a government-recognized exchange visitor program for
      cultural and educational purposes. For purposes of this provision, a
      person engaged in remunerated activity covers a person who has been
      contracted for overseas employment but has yet to leave the Philippines,
      regardless of status, and includes “Overseas Contract Workers”. The term

         b.​ Dependents refer to:

                1.​ A legal spouse who is not an active member of the workforce;

                 2.​ Unmarried     and    unemployed      children    (legitimate,
             illegitimate, adopted, or stepchildren) below the age of twenty-one
             (21); and

                3.​ Parents aged sixty (60) years or older.

          c. Fraudulent activity refers to any act involving deceit,
      misrepresentation, or omission of material facts, resulting in financial loss
      or harm to an OFW and their dependents. This includes, but is not limited
      to, false promises regarding investments, selling non-existent products, or
      charging excessive fees; and

         d. Investment scam refers to a fraudulent scheme that misleads an
      OFW into investing money based on false claims of high earnings,
      guaranteed profits, or low-risk ventures.

       SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are considered fraudulent and
are hereby prohibited:

         a.​ Investment Fraud – Enticing an OFW to invest in schemes
      misrepresented as legitimate, promising unreasonable or unrealistically
      high returns.

         b.​ False Sales – Selling products or services to OFWs under false
      pretenses, including but not limited to defective, non-existent, or grossly
      overpriced items.

         c.​ Hidden Fees and Unnecessary Purchases – Charging OFWs for
      hidden fees or misrepresenting the necessity of products or services that
      result in unnecessary financial expenditure.

         d.​ Coercion and Deceptive Practices – Using misleading or coercive
      language to pressure or induce an OFW into making investments or
      purchases.

         e.​ Financial Account Scamming – The following actions shall
      constitute Financial Account Scamming as defined in RA No. 12010,
      otherwise known as the “Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA)”:

      SEC. 5. Penalties. – Any person or entity found guilty of committing fraud
against OFWs shall be penalized as follows:

         a.​ For Individuals: A fine of not less than Ten million pesos
      (P10,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than twenty (20) years to life
      imprisonment, or both at the discretion of the court.

          b.​ For Corporations or Entities: A fine of not less than Twenty million
      pesos (P20,000,000.00) for the first offense, and Thirty million pesos
      (P30,000,000.00) for subsequent offenses. In addition, responsible officers
      of the corporation may face imprisonment as outlined above.

         c.​ Restitution: The courts may also order the perpetrator to make
      restitution to the OFW for any financial losses incurred.

       SEC. 6. Criminal Liability Under Other Laws. – Prosecution under this Act
shall be without prejudice to prosecution for any violation of the Revised Penal
Code, as amended, or special laws such as RA No. 10175, otherwise known as the
“Cyber-crime Prevention Act of 2012”; RA No. 11967, otherwise known as the
“Internet Transactions Act of 2023”; and RA No. 12010, otherwise known as the
”Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA)”.
