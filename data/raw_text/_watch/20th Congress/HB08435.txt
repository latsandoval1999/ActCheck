                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        On June 25, 2021, the Philippines was included by the Financial Action Task Force
(FATF)1 in its list of “Jurisdictions Under Increased Monitoring,” commonly referred to as
the FATF Greylist. The country was given eighteen (18) action plan items to address iden-
tified gaps in its anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing framework. On
January 2, 2024, the national government directed relevant agencies to complete the re-
maining action plan items within 2024.


        Through coordinated action across multiple government institutions, the Philip-
pines successfully completed all eighteen (18) Action Plan Items by the October 2024
FATF Plenary. In its February 2025 statement, the FATF removed the Philippines from the
greylist.


        This development is significant for the Philippine economy. Greylisting affects the
confidence of foreign investors, increases the cost of financial transactions, and compli-
cates cross-border remittances for millions of overseas Filipino workers. Maintaining a
strong and credible anti-money laundering framework is therefore essential to protecting
the country’s financial stability and economic growth.




1 The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is an Inter-governmental organization that sets the in-

ternational standards on anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism, known as
the FATF 40 Recommendations. All countries are assessed as to the technical compliance of their laws
and regulations with the FATF Recommendations.
        At the same time, strengthening the country’s anti-money laundering regime is
not merely a matter of international compliance. It is fundamentally about protecting the
Filipino people from financial crimes, corruption, and the misuse of public and private
resources.


        Recent incidents involving complex financial crimes, corruption schemes, cyber-
enabled fraud, and illicit financial flows demonstrate the need to equip Philippine institu-
tions with the tools necessary to detect, disrupt, and prosecute such activities effectively.
Criminal organizations increasingly exploit emerging financial technologies, complex
corporate structures, and cross-border transactions to conceal the proceeds of unlawful
activity.


        In this context, the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) plays a critical role in
safeguarding the integrity of the Philippine financial system. As the country’s financial
intelligence unit and coordinating authority for anti-money laundering and counter-ter-
rorism financing efforts, the AMLC must have the institutional capacity, operational in-
dependence, and legal authority necessary to respond to evolving threats.


        The FATF is currently conducting its Fourth Round of Mutual Evaluations2 among
its member jurisdictions based on the FATF Recommendations and the FATF Methodolo-
gy. The evaluation process examines two interrelated components: technical compliance3
and effectiveness.4 The Philippines is scheduled to undergo its next evaluation in 2027.


        To ensure that the country maintains a resilient financial system and remains
aligned with evolving international standards, it is necessary to update and strengthen
key provisions of Republic Act No. 9160, otherwise known as the Anti-Money Launder-
ing Act of 2001 (AMLA), as amended.




2 Mutual Evaluation is a peer assessment of the technical compliance of our laws and regulations with

the FATF Recommendations, and the effective implementation of these laws and regulations. The ME
looks at the totality of the AML/CFT system including supervision, beneficial ownership, criminal
justice system (intelligence, investigation, prosecution, confiscation), non-profit organizations, target-
ed financial sanctions and proliferation financing of weapons of mass destruction.

3 The technical compliance component will assess whether the necessary laws, regulations or other

required measures are in force and effect, and whether the supporting anti-money laundering/
counter-terrorism financing (AML/CTF) institutional framework is in place.

4 The effectiveness component will assess whether the AML/CTF systems are working, and the extent

to which the country is achieving the defined set of outcomes.
       This bill seeks to enhance the country’s ability to prevent, detect, and prosecute
financial crimes while ensuring that the exercise of regulatory authority remains consis-
tent with the Constitution, the rule of law, and the protection of civil liberties.


       Among others, the bill introduces several key reforms.


       First, it strengthens the institutional framework of the AMLC by affirming its role
as the country’s central anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing authority.
The bill enhances the AMLC’s operational capacity, supervisory authority, and enforce-
ment powers, while ensuring that these powers remain subject to judicial oversight and
due process protections.


       Second, the measure modernizes the scope of covered persons under the AMLA to
reflect developments in the financial sector and emerging areas of risk. The bill recognizes
the growing role of virtual asset service providers, online gambling operators, trust and
company service providers, and certain financial or business transactions conducted by
lawyers and accountants, which may be vulnerable to abuse for illicit financial activities.


       Third, the bill provides mechanisms to enable earlier disruption of suspicious fi-
nancial activity. It authorizes the AMLC to issue Transaction Suspension Orders upon rea-
sonable suspicion that a transaction or fund may be related to unlawful activity or money
laundering. This preventive tool allows authorities to temporarily halt suspicious transac-
tions while appropriate investigations are conducted.


       Fourth, the bill introduces Administrative Freeze Orders, allowing the temporary
preservation of assets that are suspected to be connected to unlawful activity, subject to
review by the Court of Appeals. These measures are designed to prevent the rapid dissi-
pation or transfer of illicit assets while ensuring that affected parties retain access to judi-
cial remedies.


       Fifth, the bill strengthens the AMLC’s supervisory and enforcement framework,
including expanded inspection powers, improved administrative sanctions, and en-
hanced coordination with financial regulators such as the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and
the Securities and Exchange Commission.
         Finally, the bill introduces mechanisms to support transparency, accountability,
and effective implementation, including annual reporting to Congress, safeguards on in-
formation security and confidentiality, and funding mechanisms to sustain AMLC opera-
tions.


         By strengthening the country’s legal and institutional framework against money
laundering and related financial crimes, this measure contributes to three important na-
tional objectives:

         •   protecting the integrity and stability of the Philippine financial system;
         •   strengthening the country’s fight against corruption and illicit financial activi-
             ty; and
         •   safeguarding national security and public resources from criminal exploitation.


         Ultimately, the proposed measure affirms the Philippines’ commitment to main-
taining a credible, resilient, and globally trusted financial system, while ensuring that reg-
ulatory powers are exercised in accordance with Philippine law, constitutional safeguards,
and democratic accountability.


         Immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The AMLC shall, within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, issue the necessary rules and regulations. Pro-
vided, That the non-issuance of the implementing rules and regulations shall not
prevent this Act from coming into force and effect.

SEC. 21. Separability Clause. – If any provision or section of this Act is held to be
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions or sections hereof, which are not af-
fected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 22. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of existing laws, rules and regulations,
orders, or parts thereof which are in con ict or inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ed accordingly: Provided, That all provisions
of Republic Act No. 9160, as amended, which are not inconsistent with this Act are
hereby adopted.

SEC. 23. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect immediately after the completion of
its publication in the Of cial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
