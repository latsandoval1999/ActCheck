                               EXPLANATORY NOTE
       The Constitution recognizes that science and technology are essential to national
development and progress. It directs the State to give priority to research and
development, invention, innovation, and their utilization, as well as to science and
technology education, training, and services. It further encourages the widest
participation of private groups, local governments, and community-based organizations
in the generation and utilization of science and technology.
       The Department of Science and Technology (DOST), as the country's principal
government agency for science and technology, is mandated to provide central direction,
leadership, and coordination of scientific and technological efforts and to ensure that
their results are utilized for the maximum economic and social benefit of the Filipino
people. Its Regional Offices are at the forefront of translating this national mandate into
programs, services, technologies, and interventions that directly respond to the needs of
communities, enterprises, local governments, and institutions throughout the country.
       However, the rapidly changing economic, technological, environmental, and social
conditions confronting the country demand a stronger and more responsive regional
science, technology, and innovation (STI) system. Regional economies differ in their
industries, natural resources, technological capabilities, infrastructure, human resources,
and development challenges. A uniform and highly centralized approach to STI
development may therefore fail to adequately address the distinct needs and
opportunities of every region.
      There is a compelling need to strengthen the DOST Regional Offices so that they
can serve not merely as implementing units, but as dynamic regional engines of
innovation, technological development, research utilization, enterprise upgrading, and
community empowerment.
        This measure seeks to provide a stronger institutional framework for the DOST
Regional Offices by enhancing their organizational structure, operational capacity,
personnel, technical capabilities, and coordination mechanisms. It recognizes that
effective regional STI development requires adequate leadership, competent personnel,
accessible scientific facilities, sustained funding, and strong partnerships among
government, industry, academe, research institutions, and local communities.
       The proposed measure will strengthen the ability of DOST Regional Offices to
identify regional STI priorities and formulate programs responsive to local development
needs. It will likewise enhance the delivery of technology transfer, research and
development, technical assistance, training, testing, calibration, innovation support, and
other scientific services. These functions are consistent with the existing role of DOST
Regional Offices in providing STI services, supporting technology-based enterprises,
coordinating DOST programs, and developing institutional linkages with LGUs and other
stakeholders.
       Particular attention is given to Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs),
which are vital contributors to employment, livelihood, and local economic activity. By
strengthening regional access to technology upgrading, laboratory services, product
development, testing, calibration, technical assistance, and innovation support, the
measure will enable local enterprises to improve productivity, quality, competitiveness,
and access to domestic and international markets.
       The bill further seeks to strengthen the OneLab system by bringing laboratory,
testing, calibration, and related scientific services closer to MSMEs, local industries,
government institutions, academic institutions, and communities. Accessible and
reasonably priced scientific services can reduce technological barriers faced by
enterprises and innovators, particularly those located outside major urban centers.
        The measure likewise strengthens the Community Empowerment Through
Science and Technology (CEST) Program by expanding science-based interventions in
communities that need assistance in agriculture, health and nutrition, education,
livelihood and entrepreneurship, environmental sustainability, and other areas of local
development. Through stronger regional implementation, communities will be given
greater opportunities to identify their own challenges and receive appropriate, practical,
and sustainable technological solutions.
       Equally important is the institutionalization and strengthening of the Grassroots
Innovation for Inclusive Development (GRIND) Program. Filipino communities possess
considerable ingenuity and practical knowledge, but many grassroots innovators lack
access to research facilities, technical expertise, financing, mentorship, intellectual
property assistance, and markets. The measure seeks to bridge this gap by providing
grassroots innovators with opportunities for technology and product development,
capacity building, market access, commercialization, and scaling.
        The bill also provides for the establishment of Regional Science and Technology
Innovation Hubs that will serve as collaborative platforms connecting researchers,
students, entrepreneurs, industries, LGUs, and communities. These hubs will encourage
the development and application of technologies addressing regional concerns, including
food and agricultural productivity, public health, environmental protection, disaster
resilience, sustainable energy, digital transformation, and local enterprise development.
       The proposed strengthening of the Regional Offices is likewise intended to improve
coordination between the national government and LGUs. Local governments are closest
to the communities and are therefore in a unique position to identify local problems and
development opportunities. By establishing stronger institutional mechanisms for
collaboration between DOST and LGUs, science and technology interventions can be
better integrated into local development priorities and translated into concrete
improvements in the lives and livelihoods of Filipinos.
       The measure also recognizes that organizational capacity must accompany
expanded responsibilities. Accordingly, it provides for an appropriate organizational
structure for the DOST Regional Offices, including additional Assistant Regional
Directors, subject to existing civil service, compensation, position classification, and
budgeting laws, rules, and regulations. This will strengthen regional leadership, program
management, partnership development, monitoring, evaluation, and technical
assistance.
      The strengthening of the DOST Regional Offices is not simply an administrative
reform. It is an investment in the country's capacity to compete, innovate, adapt, and
prosper in an increasingly technology-driven global economy.
       A stronger regional STI system means more productive farmers, more competitive
MSMEs, better scientific services, stronger local industries, more capable communities,
greater opportunities for Filipino innovators, and more responsive local governments. It
also means that the benefits of scientific advancement will not remain concentrated in
major metropolitan centers but will reach provinces, municipalities, cities, rural
communities, and underserved areas across the country.
      Science and technology must serve the Filipino people where they live, work, and
build their communities. Strengthening the DOST Regional Offices will bring the
capabilities of the national science and technology system closer to the people and enable
regions to develop solutions based on their own needs, resources, and opportunities.
      This measure therefore seeks to transform the DOST Regional Offices into
stronger, better-equipped, and more responsive institutions capable of driving inclusive,
innovation-led, and sustainable regional development.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
         FURTHER STRENGTHENING THE REGIONAL OFFICES OF THE
      DEPARTMENT OF SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY, AND APPROPRIATING
                          FUNDS THEREFOR

          Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:


 1   SECTION 1. Title. - This Act shall be known as the “DOST Regional Office Act.”
 2

 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared that the policy of the State to
 4   take account regional and sectoral needs and conditions to strengthen Local Government
 5   Units (LGUs) therein and to accelerate the economic and social growth and development
 6   in every part of the country. The State also recognizes the role of science, technology and
 7   innovation (STI) for national benefit as such there is a need to modernize the existing
 8   Regional Offices of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) in the generation,
 9   transfer, promotion and regulation of STI-based knowledge and application; and to
10   encourage the widest participation of all Filipinos in these endeavors.
11

12   SEC. 3. Objectives. The following are the objectives of this Act:
13      (a)    To strengthen the operational capacity of the DOST Regional Offices.
14      (b)    To enhance the implementation of science and technology programs at the
15             regional level.
16      (c)    To integrate the OneLab Program into regional operations to enhance local
17             access to laboratory services.
18      (d)    To improve the delivery of the Community Empowerment Through Science and
19             Technology (CEST) Program to empower local communities through scientific
20             innovation.
21      (e)    To establish a unified network that fosters collaboration between local
22             governments, businesses, academic institutions, and DOST.
23

24   SEC. 4. Functions of the Regional Office. – The Regional Offices shall have the
25   following primary functions being the focal points for the planning and implementation
26   of Science, Technology and Innovations (STI) programs and projects in the regions.
27   Specifically, each regional office shall assume the following functions:
28             a)     Identify the needs and opportunities in STI in the region;
 1               b)    Formulate and implement a regional STI plan consistent with and
 2                     supportive of regional development thrusts;
 3               c)    Provide STI interventions to Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises
 4                     (MSME’s) in the regions to improve their productivity and
 5                     competitiveness in both local and international markets through the
 6                     Small Enterprises Technology Upgrading Program (SET-UP), the
 7                     banner program of DOST for technology transfer and
 8                     commercialization, CEST, GRIND, iHub and OneLab.
 9               d)    Plan and implement STI human resource development programs in
10                     coordination with relevant DOST institutes, such as STI scholarships,
11                     trainings and similar activities aimed at creating a critical pool of S&T
12                     manpower.
13               e)    Develop institutional linkages and other mechanisms with ROs and
14                     other agencies, local governments, and private organizations or entities
15                     for the effective planning and implementation of STI programs in the
16                     region.
17               f)    Monitor and coordinate the various programs, projects, and activities of
18                     DOST Councils, Institutes and other attached agencies in the region.
19

20   SEC. 5. Organizational Restructure. - The Department of Science and Technology
21   Regional Offices shall be restructured to enhance operational effectiveness and improve
22   coordination with local government units (LGUs), academic institutions, and businesses.
23   The Regional Office shall be headed by a Regional Director, who shall be assisted by at
24   least two (2) Assistant Regional Directors. The designated Chief Science Research
25   Specialist shall be reclassified as Director III to serve as ARD of the RO.
26   The DOST shall likewise determine the organization structure and staffing pattern of the
27   Regional Offices including its Provincial Science and Technology Offices under RA 11914,
28   in accordance with civil service laws, rules and regulations, subject to the review and
29   approval of the DBM. In line with this, the existing position of Chief Science Research
30   Specialist which is currently held by the head of the PSTOs shall be reclassified as Director
31   III.
32

38               technology programs in the region.

22   SEC. 7. Qualifications of Assistant Regional Directors. – The Assistant Regional
23   Director/s shall be appointed by the Secretary of the Department of Science and
24   Technology, based on the following minimum qualifications:
25        a)     A bachelor’s degree in science, engineering, technology management, or a
26               related field; a master’s degree in a relevant area is preferred;
27        b)     At least five (5) years of experience in program management, preferably in the
28               science and technology sector, with significant exposure to regional
29               development initiatives;
30        c)     Strong leadership, management, communication, and coordination skills;
31               demonstrated ability to work with diverse stakeholders at the local and national
32               levels.
33

34   SEC. 8. Establishment of OneLab Services in Regional Offices. - The DOST
35   Regional Offices (ROs) shall establish or upgrade the existing regional OneLab centers to
36   provide Micro Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), local government units, and the
37   general public with easier access to laboratory services, including testing, calibration, and
38   product certification.
39   The DOST ROs shall likewise partner with local laboratories, universities, and other
40   entities to extend laboratory services and testing capabilities to underserved areas.
41   Services provided through OneLab shall be priced reasonably to ensure accessibility to all
42   sectors, especially MSMEs, start-ups, and local entrepreneurs.
43

44   SEC. 9. Strengthening the Community Empowerment Through Science and
45   Technology (CEST) Program. - The CEST Program shall be expanded to include
 1   additional communities across all regions, focusing on areas in need of scientific
 2   assistance to address local challenges.
 3   The CEST Program shall focus on providing scientific solutions but not limited to the
 4   following sectors: a) Agriculture; b) Health and nutrition; c) Education; d) Livelihood and
 5   entrepreneurship; and e) Environmental sustainability
 6   The program shall likewise facilitate the transfer of appropriate technologies to
 7   communities, enhancing productivity and livelihoods.
 8   Each DOST Regional Office shall engage local communities to assess needs, provide
 9   training, and deliver science-based solutions.
10   In line with this, an efficient monitoring and evaluation system will be developed to
11   measure the effectiveness of the CEST Program and ensure its sustainability.
12

13   SEC. 10. Establishment of the Grassroots Innovation for Inclusive
14   Development Program (GRIND). - The Department of Science and Technology
15   (DOST) shall establish and implement, through the Regional Offices, the Grassroots
16   Innovation for Inclusive Development (GRIND) Program to support, promote, and scale
17   grassroots innovations. The program shall be inclusive and accessible to all Filipino
18   citizens, with particular focus on individuals and communities in marginalized, rural, or
19   underserved areas. It shall address the following key areas:
20      a)     Technology and Product Development. – Providing support for the
21             development of locally relevant and culturally appropriate innovations.
22      b)     Market Access. – Helping grassroots innovators access markets, networks,
23             and commercial opportunities.
24      c)     Capacity Building. – Providing training, mentorship, and resources to
25             enable innovators to refine their products, services, and business models.
26      d)     Sustainability and Scalability. – Supporting efforts to scale successful
27             innovations and integrate them into local economies in a sustainable manner.
28

29   SEC. 11. Creation of the Regional Science and Technology Innovation Hub.
30   Each DOST Regional Office shall create a Regional Science and Technology Innovation
31   Hub, which will serve as the focal point for the following activities:
32      a)     Research and Development – Support research and development
33             initiatives to address regional issues, such as agricultural productivity, public
34             health, environmental protection, and sustainable energy.
35      b)     Capacity Building – Offer programs that equip local communities, students,
36             and professionals with the necessary skills to innovate and adopt new
37             technologies.
38      c)     Partnerships – Foster partnerships between local government units,
39             businesses, and academic institutions to facilitate the application of science and
40             technology for regional development.
41

42   SEC. 12. Role of Local Government Units. – Local government units (LGUs) shall
43   play an active role in the implementation of the CEST Program within their respective
44   areas.
 1   In cooperation with the DOST, the LGUs shall develop and implement a Community
 2   Science and Technology Development Plan (CSTDP), which shall be integrated into their
 3   local development plans.
 4   Further, this Act empowers LGUs to:
 5             (a)      Establish a City/Municipality Science and Technology Office (CMSTO)
 6                      responsible for coordinating and implementing science and technology-
 7                      related programs, projects, and activities within their respective
 8                      jurisdictions to enhance the local implementation of science and
 9                      technology initiatives.
10             (b)      Collaborate with the DOST to identify the needs of their communities
11                      and prioritize technology interventions.
12             (c)      Provide logistical support, facilitate the integration of S&T solutions into
13                      local policies, and allocate funds to complement the national
14                      government’s efforts.
15             (d)      Support local S&T innovations by facilitating market access,
16                      distribution, and sustainability.
17

18   SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). The DOST through the
19   Office of the Undersecretary for Regional Operations in consultation with relevant
20   stakeholders shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within
21   ninety (90) days after the effectivity thereof.
22

23   SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
24   unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force
25   and effect.
26

27   SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules
28   and regulations, and issuances, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the
29   provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

31   SEC. 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
