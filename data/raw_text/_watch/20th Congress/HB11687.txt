EXPLANATORY NOTE

Cleft lip and palate are among the most common congenital conditions in the
Philippines, yet access to comprehensive, multidisciplinary care remains severely
limited, especially outside Metro Manila. Families face insurmountable barriers,
including high travel costs, a lack of specialized surgeons and speech therapists,
and prohibitive out-of-pocket expenses that push vulnerable households further
into poverty.

To address these systemic gaps, this bill establishes a phased, evidence-
based, and sustainable framework for integrated cleft and craniofacial care. The
implementation begins with a mandatory feasibility and costing study to accurately
determine the resource allocation required for long-term scalability before initiating
nationwide expansion. To ensure immediate regional access, the bill establishes
at least three pilot Regional Cleft and Craniofacial Centers within existing public
hospitals, leveraging strategic co-location with established non-governmental
organization partners.

Patients at these pilot centers are guaranteed comprehensive, cost-free care
covering surgery, hospitalization, essential medicines, speech therapy, and
necessary transport and accommodation vouchers—backed by a strict, absolute
prohibition on balance billing. To solve the critical shortage of specialists, the bill
institutionalizes a workforce development plan featuring fellowships for surgeons
and anesthesiologists, scholarships for speech-language pathologists, and a tiered
telehealth framework that authorizes task-sharing with nurses and community
health workers. Furthermore, a "No Wrong Door" referral policy is mandated across
all public health facilities to ensure rapid identification, referral, and navigation of
patients to specialized care.

Systemic accountability and transparency are embedded through the creation
of a National Cleft Oversight Committee, local Family Advisory Councils, and a

National Cleft Registry designed to track clinical outcomes, complications, and
strict Zero-out-of-pocket compliance. Finally, to prevent premature or unfunded
mandates, expansion beyond the initial pilot phase requires objective certification
of readiness metrics, with full nationwide rollout strictly contingent upon the
passage of subsequent legislation.

By prioritizing feasibility, workforce development, and absolute patient
protection, this bill transforms cleft care from sporadic surgical missions into an
integrated, institutionalized, and just public health system.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING REGIONAL CLEFT PALATE AND CRANIOFACIAL
CENTERS AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines

SECTION 1. Short Title. —- This Act shall be known as the “Cleft Care Act.”
SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is the policy of the State to achieve
universal access to comprehensive cleft lip and palate care. To ensure feasibility

and sustainability, the State shall adopt a phased strategy consisting of:

a) Amandatory feasibility and costing study to be completed within eighteen
(18) months;

b) The establishment of at least three (3) pilot Regional Cleft and
Craniofacial Centers in existing public hospitals, prioritizing integration
with existing non-governmental organization (NGO) partner hospitals;

c) A workforce development plan for cleft surgeons, anesthesiologists, and
speech-language pathologists, including visiting surgeon clusters and a
government indemnity clause;

d) Free surgery, essential medications, basic speech therapy, and
transport/accommodation support at pilot centers;

e) Integration with existing NGO cleft networks;

f) A"“No Wrong Door" referral policy to ensure early diagnosis and referral;
and

g) Legal guarantees against out-of-pocket payments.

SECTION 10. National Cleft Registry and Complication Tracking. —

a) The DOH shall establish a phased National Cleft Registry. Phase 1 (first
two years of pilot operation) shall use a Cleft Care Passport, a paper-
based or QR-coded booklet carried by the patient to track surgery date,
follow-up visits, speech therapy attendance, transport voucher use, and
out-of-pocket expenses. Phase 2 (after two years) shall transition to a fully
electronic national registry.

b) The registry shall track for each patient: age, sex, cleft type, surgery date,
complications, speech therapy attendance, transport voucher usage, and
out-of-pocket expenses to verify zero cost.

c) The DOH shall publish a quarterly pilot progress report containing:
number of surgeries performed per pilot center, median waiting time from
diagnosis to surgery, complication rates, workforce vacancy rates, stock-
out incidents, and patient satisfaction feedback from family advisory
councils. The report shall be submitted to Congress and made publicly
available online.

d) Complication Tracking and Rapid Response. — The registry shall include
mandatory reporting of all major postoperative complications (palatal
fistula, surgical site infection, bleeding requiring transfusion, readmission)
occurring within thirty (30) days of surgery. Any pilot center with a fistula
rate exceeding fifteen percent (15%) in a given calendar year shall
undergo a DOH quality improvement review, and the center shall
implement corrective measures within six (6) months. The registry system
shall automatically trigger a tele-consult alert to the national reference
center (e.g., Philippine General Hospital Cleft Unit) within forty-eight (48)
hours for any reported fistula, dehiscence, or post-discharge bleeding.

e) Cleft Birth Surveillance Module. — Within six (6) months after the effective
date of this Act, the DOH shall integrate cleft surveillance into the Field
Health Services Information System (FHSIS) for all live births occurring in
government facilities. The birth reporting form shall include the following
fields:

i. "Cleft lip and/or palate observed at birth: Yes / No / Not examined";
and

ii. "If yes, referral made to [facility name] and date of referral."

Data from this module shall be used to estimate true prevalence and to
monitor referral timeliness.

SECTION 12. "No Wrong Door" Referral Policy. —

a) The DOH shall establish a nationwide "No Wrong Door" referral protocol
for cleft conditions. Every government lying-in clinic, rural health unit,
district hospital, and provincial hospital shall:

i. Display a standard Cleft Referral Pathway poster in a visible area;

ii. Train all midwives, nurses, and physicians on the recognition of cleft
lip/palate at birth;

iii. Immediately upon identification of a newborn with suspected cleft,
register the child in the existing Community Health Information
Tracking System (CHITS) or e-Buntis registry, which shall be modified
to include a cleft referral field; and

SECTION 13. Transitional Access for Patients Outside Pilot Catchments. —

During the pilot phase, patients who reside outside the catchment areas of pilot
centers shall continue to receive cleft surgery through:

a) PhilHealth coverage for cleft surgery in any accredited hospital:

b) Referral to NGO partner hospitals under existing programs; and

c) DOH medical assistance programs for indigent patients.

The DOH shall issue a referral guideline to help provincial health offices

connect cleft patients to the nearest available provider within ninety (90) days of
diagnosis.

hospital with a functional OR, and a signed NGO technical assistance

b) Congress is notified at least sixty (60) days in advance and does not

disapprove by resolution; and
