   AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE SOLAR HOME SYSTEMS FINANCING FOR
    SOCIAL HOUSING PROGRAM, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR


The proposed measure seeks to promote sustainable, affordable, and reliable access
to electricity for beneficiaries of social housing programs through the integration of
solar home systems (SHS) financing. Despite ongoing government housing initiatives,
many resettlement communities continue to face high electricity costs and limited
access to stable power services, particularly in off-grid and disaster-prone areas.


By institutionalizing a Solar Home Systems Financing for Social Housing Program, the
financial burden of electricity expenses on low-income households will be reduced
while advancing the country’s renewable energy and climate resilience goals. The
measure further ensures that socialized housing projects are equipped with cleaner
and more sustainable energy solutions, contributing to improved living conditions and
long-term energy security for informal settler families and other marginalized
communities.


The bill also strengthens inter-agency coordination among housing and energy
institutions to support the adoption of renewable energy technologies in government
housing programs. In doing so, it aligns housing development with the State’s
commitment to sustainable development, environmental protection, and inclusive
social welfare.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Solar Home System
 2   Financing for Social Housing Program Act."
 3
 4   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The Constitution mandates that the State shall
 5   protect and promote the right of people to decent housing with basic services at
 6   affordable cost. The informal settler families (ISFS), particularly those located in
 7   danger zones and those displaced by government infrastructure projects and
 8   calamities deserve no less than habitable resettlement areas as well as climate resilient
 9   communities. Recognizing the right to have quality, reliable, affordable, safe and
10   regular supply of electric power vis-a-vis the vulnerability of the Philippine archipelago
11   and its local communities, particularly the poor, women, and children, to potential
12   dangerous consequences of climate change, the State shall strengthen, integrate,
13   consolidate and institutionalize initiatives to address climate change in the context of
14   sustainable development, such as the prioritization of renewable sources in energy
15   generation to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
16
17   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. -
18     a) Solar Energy. - refers to the energy derived from solar radiation that can be
19         converted into useful thermal or electricity energy.
20     b) Solar Energy Systems. - refers to energy systems which convert solar energy
21         into thermal or electrical energy.
 1      c) Solar Home System (SHS). - refers to a stand-alone photovoltaic system that
 2         offers a cost-effective mode of supplying amenity power for lighting and
 3         appliances to remote off-grid households. Depending on viability, SHS may be
 4         grid-tie, off-grid, or hybrid solar PV.
 5      d) Net Metering. - refers to a system, appropriate for distributed generation, in
 6         which a distribution grid user has a two-way connection to the grid and only
 7         charged for his/her net electricity consumption and is credited for any overall
 8         contribution to the electricity grid.
 9      e) Off-grid Systems. - refer to electrical systems not connected to the wires and
10         related facilities of the On-Grid Systems of the Philippines.
11      f) On-grid Systems. - refers to electrical systems composed of interconnected
12         transmission lines, distribution lines, substations, and related facilities for the
13         purpose of conveyance of the bilk power on the grid of the Philippines.
14      g) Renewable Energy Systems. - refer to energy systems which convert RE
15         resources into useful energy forms, like electrical, mechanical, etc.
16      h) Rural Electrification. - refers to the delivery of basic electricity services,
17         consisting of power generation, sub-transmission, and/or extension of
18         associated power delivery system that would bring about important social and
19         economic benefits to the countryside.
20
21   SEC. 4. Creating the solar home systems financing for social housing
22   program. - All current and future social housing financing programs shall require the
23   integration of the SHS financing program to defray up-front Investment of poor
24   families and communities on solar PV installations to provide secure, clean, and
25   environment-friendly power services in both in-city and off-city resettlement sites. The
26   annual target for solar housing units shall be based on the combined annual social
27   housing targets of the LGUs, NHA and SHFC. Per housing unit, a corresponding budget
28   will be allocated for SHS financing based on the prevailing cost of solar home systems
29   in the market on top of the cost for the production of the housing units. The terms of
30   the financing program, which may be in the form of loans, grants and equity
31   contributions from the shelter agencies shall be designed so that electricity-related
32   expenditures of households over the medium-term will not be greater than
33   expenditures they would incur if they were connected to the electricity grid.
34
35   The annual target for solar housing units shall be based on the combined annual social
36   housing targets of the NHA and SHFC. Per housing unit, a corresponding budget will
37   be allocated for SHS financing based on the prevailing cost of solar home systems in
38   the market on top of the cost for the production of the housing units.
39
40   SEC. 5. Promotion of solar home systems as an affordable, viable,
41   accessible, clean and cheaper energy source for social housing programs. -
42   The NHA and SHFC shall incorporate education and information dissemination
 1   immediately in the first phase of the implementation of resettlement projects to orient
 2   ISFs about the SHS financing program.
