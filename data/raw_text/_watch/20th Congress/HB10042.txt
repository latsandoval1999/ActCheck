                                AN ACT
           PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF YOUNG FARMERS

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The country’s agriculture sector plays a significant role in the nation’s food
security. However, in recent years, the country’s dependence on agricultural exports
has been steadily increasing, from $17.91 billion in 2023 and $19.46 billion in 2024, to
$20.37 billion in 2025. 1 This increasing rate of imports is brought about by several
factors, including the inability of the domestic agricultural market to sufficiently meet
the food demands of a growing population.

       In light of the country’s experiences with global issues, such as the crisis in
West Asia, the Ukraine-Russia war, and COVID-19, which have heavily affected global
trade restrictions and prices, the country’s dependence on importation is concerning.
Thus, there is a need to continue addressing the problems facing the agricultural
sector to reduce importation dependence and work towards food self-sufficiency.

        In 2022, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported a drop from 9.73
million agriculture and forestry workers in July 2022 to 9.22 million in October 2022,
marking a loss of over 511,000 workers.2 These declines reflect not just seasonal
factors but are largely due to workers leaving the agriculture industry. 3 The Philippine
Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) found that population growth, diminishing
farm size, and declining incomes in agriculture incentivize the “shift out of agriculture.” 4

         A survey has found that 65% of Filipino farmers believe their kids would not
have a bright future if they became farmers.5 Young Filipinos opt out of farming,
preferring careers in medicine, IT, or business, because they associate farming with
“hard work and poverty.”6 A 2021 science journal study found that the average Filipino
farmer is 53 years old, while a Department of Agriculture (DA) report stated 56 years
old.7 If these trends persist, the country may face a bigger problem in the agricultural
sector and food security in the coming years.

        This bill seeks to create the Magna Carta of Young Farmers to institutionalize
mechanisms and policies that directly address the challenges faced by young farmers
and uphold their rights. The bill creates the Council and the National Young Farmers
Roadmap to ensure that young farmers are adequately prioritized in the formulation
and implementation of national policies and programs that empower them. The bill
also guarantees equal access to technology, credit, and capital among young farmers.
Furthermore, the bill focuses on the land rights of young farmers by guaranteeing
improved access to low-cost farmland. To make young farmers more competitive, the
bill also provides them with assistance in farm planning, management, and access to
market spaces. Finally, to uphold young farmers’ rights against discrimination and
violence, the bill ensures their access to free legal services.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representative Nathaniel
Oducado in the House of Representatives, and Senator Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan and
Senator Risa Hontiveros in the Senate. This bill fully adopts Senator Pangilinan’s
Senate Bill No. 719.

        Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 4        SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “The Magna Carta of
 5   Young Farmers.”

 7           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the economic, political,
 8   and sociocultural realities affecting young farmers' current condition and affirms the
 9   role of young farmers in nation-building and attaining environmental sustainability and
10   food sufficiency. It shall endeavor to develop plans, policies, programs, measures, and
11   mechanisms to ensure young farmers' empowerment, productivity, and
12   competitiveness in local and international trade;
13
14           The State recognizes young farmers as supplemental income earners on the
15   family farms, playing a major role in augmenting their family's income. Doing so entails
16   working odd jobs to sustain the family's source of income, most especially in non-
17   harvesting seasons;
18
19           The State acknowledges the invaluable role of young farmers who are present
20   in the value chain process, starting from production, to processing, to value-adding up
21   to marketing;
22
23         The State recognizes the role of young farmers as eco-warriors and staunch
24   advocates of environmental protection; and,

25          The State recognizes the indispensable contribution of young farmers to the
26   overall food security and national economy.
27
28         Towards this end, the State reaffirms the right of young farmers as a separate
29   basic sector with its own distinct rights and responsibilities to participate in policy
30   formulation, planning, organization, implementation, management, monitoring, and
31   evaluation of all programs, projects, and services targeting young farmers as
32   stakeholders.
33
34         Further, it shall establish mechanisms for the promotion and protection of young
35   farmers' rights.

38                                        Definition of Terms

40         SEC. 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:
41
42             a) Agriculture refers to the prudent endeavor of cultivating plants and
43                animals which includes the tillage of the soil, dairy, production,
44                cultivation, growing, and harvesting of any agricultural, aquacultural,
45                floricultural, or horticultural commodities, the growing and harvesting of
46                forest products upon forest lands, the raising of livestock including
47                horses, the keeping of horses as a commercial enterprise, the keeping
48                and raising of poultry, swine, cattle, and other domesticated animals
49                used for food purposes and any forestry or lumbering operations,
50                performed by a farmer;
51
52             b) Farm work refers to the land cultivation and tillage, dairying, production,
53                growing, harvesting, plowing, planting, application of fertilizers, and farm
54                supervision performed by an individual or a group of people;
55
56             c) Discrimination refers to any distinction, exclusion, or restriction which
57                has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition,
58                enjoyment, or exercise by young farmers, irrespective of their age,
59                status, or income, on a basis of equality, human rights, and fundamental
60                freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil, or any other
61                field. It includes any act or omission, including by law, policy,
62                administrative measure, or practice, that directly or indirectly excludes or
63                restricts young farmers in the recognition and promotion of their rights
64                and their access to and enjoyment of opportunities, benefits, or
65                privileges. A measure or practice of general application is discrimination
66                against young farmers if it fails to provide for a mechanism to offset or
67                address the disadvantages or limitations of young farmers, as a result of
68                which they are denied or restricted in the recognition and protection of

                                                2
 69      their rights and in their access to and enjoyment of opportunities,
 70      benefits, or privileges: Provided, That discrimination compounded by or
 71      intersecting with other grounds, status, or condition, such as ethnicity,
 72      age, poverty, or religion shall be considered discrimination against
 73      young farmers under this Act;
 74
 75   d) Eco-warrior refers to the staunch advocates of environmental protection
 76      and practitioners of sustainable farming;
 77
 78   e) Food Security refers to the condition in which all people, at all times,
 79      have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and
 80      nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for
 81      an active and healthy life;
 82
 83   f) Family Farming is a means of organizing agricultural, forestry, fisheries,
 84      pastoral, and aquaculture production which is managed and operated by
 85      a family and predominantly reliant on family labor, including both women
 86      and men;
 87
 88   g) Lessee refers to a person who, by themselves, or with the aid available
 89      from within their immediate farm household, cultivates the land,
 90      belonging to or lawfully possessed by another, with the latter's consent,
 91      for purposes of agricultural production, for a price certain in money or in
 92      produce or both. The term is distinguished from a civil lessee as
 93      understood in the New Civil Code of the Philippines;
 94
 95   h) Organic refers to the particular farming and processing system,
 96      described in the standards and not in the classical chemical sense. The
 97      term "organic" is synonymous in other languages with "biological" or
 98      "ecological". It is also a labeling term that denotes products considered
 99      organic based on the Philippine National Standards for organic
100      agriculture;
101
102   i) Organic Agriculture refers to all agricultural systems that promote the
103      ecologically sound, socially acceptable, economically viable, and
104      technically feasible production of food and fibers. Organic agriculture
105      dramatically reduces external inputs by refraining from the use of
106      chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and pharmaceuticals. It also covers
107      areas such as, but is not limited to, soil fertility management, varietal
108      breeding and selection under chemical and pesticide-free conditions, the
109      use of biotechnology, and other cultural practices that are consistent with
110      the principles and policies of this Act, and enhance productivity without
111      destroying the soil and harming fanners, consumers and the
112      environment as defined by the International Federation of Organic

                                       3
113                  Agricultural Movement (IFOAM): Provided, That the biotechnology
114                  herein referred to shall not include genetically modified organisms or
115                  GMOs;
116
117               j) Sustainability refers to meeting the needs of the present without
118                  compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs,
119                  as defined by the United Nations World Commission on Environment
120                  and Development;
121
122               k) Tenant refers to a person who, themselves, or with the aid available from
123                  within their immediate farm household, cultivates the land belonging to,
124                  or possessed by another, with the latter's consent for purposes of
125                  production, sharing the produce with the landholder under the share
126                  tenancy system, or paying the landholder a price certain or ascertainable
127                  in produce or in money or both, under the leasehold tenancy system;
128
129               l) Young Farmer refers to an individual whose primary source of income
130                  comes from agriculture, with an age range of 15-35 years old, who is an
131                  owner, tenant, lessee, or worker of the land in which they personally
132                  cultivate and till;
133
134               m) Red-tagging refers to the practice of the State branding or maliciously
135                  blacklisting individuals or organizations, whether critical or not fully
136                  supportive of the government. They could be branded as anti-
137                  government, insurgents, and members of leftist groups.

140                           Duties Related to the Rights of Young Farmers
141
142            SEC. 4. State as Primary Duty-Bearer. – The State, as the primary duty bearer,
143   shall:
144               a) Recognize, promote, and protect the rights of young farmers defined and
145                  guaranteed under this Act;
146               b) Lead initiatives to eliminate all forms of discrimination, coercion, and
147                  violence, including physical, verbal, sexual, emotional, psychological,
148                  spiritual, and cultural, affecting young farmers;
149               c) Endorse farm products produced by young farmers for economic
150                  development nationwide;
151               d) Promote and fulfill the rights of young farmers in all aspects, including
152                  but not limited to their rights to food security, education and training,
153                  access to land, social justice, employment, health services, and
154                  participation.
155

157   coherent and integrated implementation and enforcement of this Act and related laws,

159   sector, stop discrimination against young farmers and farming in general, and advance
160   their rights and responsibilities.

182          SEC. 8. Education and Training. – The State shall ensure equal access to
183   quality education and training of young farmers and encourage partnership and
184   linkage with state universities and colleges (SUCs) on information and technology
185   transfer to young farmers and organizations.
186
187             a) Curriculum. – To ensure better quality education and training for young
188                farmers and aspiring farmers, the following will be incorporated in
189                curricula:
190
191                    1. K to 12 High Schools offering Technology and Livelihood
192                       Education (TLE) Track with specialization in Agriculture-Fisheries
193                       Arts shall be required to create laboratories for agricultural
194                       science and technology research.
195                    2. Climate Smart and Resilient Agriculture and Agricultural Laws
196                       and Farmers’ Rights shall be incorporated in both K to 12 and
197                       Tertiary Education Curriculum.
198

199             b) Scholarship. – K to 12 students under the TLE Track with specialization
200                in Agriculture-Fisheries Arts shall be entitled to scholarship grants and
201                incentives through the Department of Agriculture (DA) upon compliance
202                with the conditions set by the department.
203
204             c) Agricultural Training Service Extension. – Agricultural SUCs shall serve
205                as an extension service provider for practical application, learning
206                seminars, hands-on training, and knowledge on technological
207                advancements of young farmers' organizations and individuals through
208                the proper facilitation of the DA.
209
210          SEC. 9. Right to Resources for Food Production. – The State shall guarantee
211   young farmers' vital role in food production and food security by prioritizing their rights
212   to access technology, credit, and capital. It shall ensure that young farmers are
213   provided with the following:
214
215             a) Equal access to support for farm inputs and agricultural tools;
216             b) Equal access to farm technologies and mechanization;
217             c) Ensure innovation, development, and proliferation of young farmer-
218                friendly technologies and mechanization of agricultural production and
219                processing;
220             d) Equal access to formal sources of credit and capital, including forms of
221                clean loans and Mortgage, Redemption and Insurance (MRI); and
222             e) Equitable share of the produce of farms and aquatic resources.
223
224          SEC. 10. Access to Information. – Young farmers' access to information
225   regarding policies, programs, projects, subsidies, and funding outlays that affect them
226   shall be ensured. The DA-Regional Field Offices (RFOs), Provincial Agricultural
227   Offices (PAOs), and Municipal Agricultural Offices (MAOs) shall endeavor to
228   strengthen information dissemination of agricultural projects benefiting young farmers.
229   Moreover, these offices shall offer assistance to farmers to access agricultural
230   services offered, whether offline or online.
231
232            SEC. 11. Right to Farmland. – The land rights of young farmers shall be
233   prioritized. In doing so, the State shall guarantee the following:
234
235             a) Improve access to low-cost farmland. – Create more flexibility within
236                CARP/CARPER to allow land trusts to protect farmland quickly and
237                facilitate a new farmer. The DAR shall also ensure to protect the
238                affordability of conserved lands and ensure that it remains in farming.
239
240             b) Land Transfer Tax Exemptions. – Transactions including, but not limited
241                to, purchase, inheritance, and donation of land, which is one (1) hectare
242                and below, to young farmers, as defined under this Act, are hereby

                                                  6
243                exempt from all taxes of whatever kind: Provided, however, That the
244                beneficiary guarantees that the land will be kept and maintained by him
245                or her for at least five (5) years.
246
247             c) Land and Farm Planning Management. – The DA, together with the
248                LGUs and other related agencies, shall provide full assistance to young
249                farmers, from the national scale down to community levels, regarding
250                farm planning and management to ensure maximum utilization,
251                production, profitability, and sustainability of their farms. The Department
252                shall utilize the existence of Farmer Field Schools and Agricultural
253                Extension Training Centers as learning sites to ensure full-scale
254                assistance to young farmers.
255
256          SEC. 12. Access to Market Development Services. – The State shall guarantee
257   the young farmer’s rights to market spaces, farm-to-market roads, and other
258   mechanisms that will ensure that farm produce will reach the family table. In doing so,
259   the State shall provide the following:
260
261             a) Establishment of Farm-to-Market Roads (FMR). – The DA, DPWH, and
262                LGU shall endeavor to hasten the creation of farm-to-market as per the
263                Farm-to- Market Road Network Plan (FMRNP) and the Comprehensive
264                FMR National Master Plan 2020 to 2030.
265
266             b) Creation of a Provincial Market. – The DA Regional Office and the
267                Governor's Office shall establish a central market within each province.
268                These markets shall cater to all registered farmers of the different
269                municipalities of the province who wish to sell their farm produce.
270
271             c) Establishment of Municipal Cold Storage and Post-Harvest Facility. –
272                The DA, through the support of the LGU, shall ensure the establishment
273                of cold storage and post-harvest facilities that are accessible to the local
274                farmers.
275
276             d) National Young Farmers Roadmap. – The DA, through the National
277                Young Farmers Council, including its attached agencies and
278                corporations, in coordination with the Department of Agrarian Reform
279                (DAR), Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR),
280                Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Science and
281                Technology (DOST), Department of Education (DepEd), National
282                Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), National Youth
283                Commission (NYC), Philippine Coconut Authority (PCA), Commission
284                on Higher Education (CHED), National Anti-Poverty Commission
285                (NAPC), and all agricultural-related agencies attached under the Office
286                of the President (OP), shall formulate and implement a National Young

                                                 7
287                Farmers Plan within one hundred and twenty (120) days from the
288                creation of the National Young Farmers Council. The roadmap shall
289                serve as the convergence program of government line agencies to
290                ensure the maximum role of young farmers in food security. It shall also
291                present measures that will allocate a safe, culturally acceptable,
292                nutritionally adequate diet through a sustainable food system that
293                maximizes self-reliance and social justice. The plan shall include the
294                following:
295
296                    1. A color-coded map of the national status of the country's food
297                       security;
298                    2. Baseline data research on young farmers through a Young
299                       Farmers Registry System, which includes young farmers' socio-
300                       economic status;
301                    3. Community-based Food Security Plan that shall identify viable
302                       food security systems, particularly in the roles of young farmers
303                       along the food value-supply chain; and
304                    4. Index of programs of identified government agencies for the
305                       development of young farmers.
306
307             e) Young Farmer Community Seed Banks. – DA shall facilitate the
308                selection, establishment, and management of strategic community-
309                based seed banks to ensure sustainable food and agricultural resources
310                for young farmers.
311
312          SEC. 13. Environmental Protection. – DA, in partnership with DENR and DOST,
313   shall establish model farms that feature sustainable farming practices such as
314   integrated and diverse organic farming systems, climate-smart and resilient
315   agriculture, organic urban farming systems, and the like to serve as knowledge
316   transfer and training institutions for young farmers and agricultural workers and
317   provide an avenue for the active participation of young farmers in environmental
318   protection and practice of sustainable farming.
319
320           SEC. 14. Recognition and Preservation of Cultural Identity and Indigenous
321   Farming Traditions. – The State shall respect, preserve, and maintain knowledge,
322   innovations, and practices of indigenous and local communities of young farmers
323   embodying traditional lifestyles relevant to the conservation and sustainable use of
324   biological diversity. These shall be considered in the formulation and implementation
325   of national policies and programs.
326
327          SEC. 15. Protection from Discrimination, Red-tagging, and Violence. – The
328   State shall ensure that all young farmers shall be protected from all forms of
329   discrimination, red-tagging, and violence as provided for in existing laws. They shall
330   be ensured access to free legal services from the Public Attorney's Office (PAO).

331          SEC. 16. Representation and Participation. – The State shall ensure young

348                                  INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM
349
350          SEC. 18. Creation of the National Young Farmers Council. – There is hereby
351   created the National Young Farmers and Fisherfolk Council, which shall be attached
352   to the DA. The Council shall have the following powers and functions:
353
354             a) Formulate, recommend, and implement national policies, plans,
355                programs, and guidelines to ensure young farmers' empowerment,
356                productivity, and competitiveness in local and international trade.
357             b) Support and assist the DA and relevant government agencies in building
358                strong and harmonious partnerships with foreign counterparts and
359                relevant agencies, non-government organizations, and civic groups in
360                order to facilitate the implementation of strategies and programs for the
361                protection and promotion of the rights and well-being of young farmers.
362             c) Develop an online self-service portal for young farmers to access, apply
363                for, and implement the different DA programs and services.
364             d) Establish Young Farmer Coordinators in each municipality to direct
365                outreach and help young farmers access information on agricultural and
366                fisheries programs and services.
367             e) Spearhead the drafting and finalization of the National Young Farmers’
368                Roadmap.
369
370          SEC. 19. Farm Insurance. – Young Farmers shall automatically become
371   members of the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC) as protection from
372   losses of non-crop agricultural assets from force majeure.
