                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Backyard and small- hog raising provides livelihood and income to
 thousands of Filipino families, including many households in Nueva
 Vizcaya. Small-hold farms accounted for 70.0 percent of the country’s
 swine inventory as of 1 January 2023, underscoring their importance to
 the domestic pork supply and rural economy.1 Yet local hog raisers remain
 particularly vulnerable to low farmgate prices, rising production costs, weak
 bargaining power, and an increasingly complex livestock marketing chain.
 The Department of Agriculture has acknowledged that farmgate prices had
 fallen sharply without corresponding relief to consumers.2 Even the existing
 law provides significant resources for animal industry development,
 including repopulation, disease response, credit, and related support,3 the
 core market is still facing challenges as local producers remain largely
 unresolved.
       The measure is anchored on the constitutional policies promoting
agricultural support, reduction of economic inequalities, preferential use of
locally produced goods, and regulation of unfair competition.4
       For these reasons, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                 AN ACT
     STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION OF LOCAL HOG RAISERS,
  ENSURING FARMGATE PRICE TRANSPARENCY AND FAIR TRADING
                 PRACTICES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Local Hog Raisers
Protection and Fair Pricing Act.”
       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to promote
comprehensive rural development, to provide support to agriculture through
adequate financial, production, marketing, and other support services, to promote
the preferential use of locally produced goods, and to prohibit combinations in
restraint of trade and unfair competition, consistent with Sections 1 and 5 of
Article XIII, Sections 1, 6, 12, 13, and 19 of Article XII, and Section 21 of Article II
of the Constitution. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the fixing of
the purchase or selling price of any animal or animal product.
      SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall apply to farmgate transactions in swine
throughout the country.
       SEC. 4. Livestock Transaction Reporting System. The Bureau of Animal
Industry (BAI) shall establish and maintain a Livestock Transaction Reporting
System. Every buyer covered by the implementing rules shall report the date,
location, volume, price, and terms of payment of each farmgate transaction,
without disclosing the identity of the selling raiser without the raiser's written
consent. Reported information shall be published only in aggregate form and shall
not be used as the sole basis for any tax assessment or admitted in evidence
against the reporting party, except in a proceeding for violation of this Act.
       SEC. 5. Reference Farmgate Price. The Department of Agriculture (DA), in
coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority, shall compute and publish
weekly a reference farmgate price for hogs, at the national and regional levels,
derived from data collected under Section 4. The reference price shall be published
on official websites and disseminated to local agriculture offices and accredited
raisers' associations, and shall be clearly identified as an administrative indicator
distinct from official statistical series.

       SEC. 6. Standard Weighing and Receipts. All farmgate transactions shall
be on a liveweight basis using weighing instruments calibrated and sealed in
accordance with existing law. Every buyer shall issue the selling raiser, upon
delivery, a receipt stating the number of heads, recorded liveweight, agreed price
per kilogram, total consideration, and agreed date of payment.
      SEC. 7. Prohibited Unfair Trading Practices. It shall be unlawful for any
buyer, in a farmgate transaction with a small livestock raiser or accredited
association, to:
      (a) pay later than fifteen (15) days from delivery, absent a prior
      written agreement providing otherwise; ​
      ​      ​
      (b) unilaterally deduct from the agreed price or recorded weight after
      delivery without a documented and disclosed basis; ​
      ​
      (c) refuse to issue the receipt required under Section 6 or to execute a
      written supply agreement upon the raiser's request; ​
      ​
      (d) refuse without justifiable cause, and on unreasonably short
      notice, to accept delivery of animals already accepted for purchase; ​
      ​
      (e) retaliate against a raiser or association for a complaint filed or
      information given under this Act; or ​
      ​
      (f) collude with other buyers to fix, depress, or allocate the purchase
      price of hogs, or to allocate raisers or areas of purchase among
      themselves, without prejudice to the application of the Philippine
      Competition Act.
       SEC. 8. Adjudication. A complaint for violation of Section 7 may be filed
with the BAI by the aggrieved raiser, an accredited association on the raiser's
behalf, or the DA motu proprio.
        The BAI shall resolve the complaint within sixty (60) days from filing of the
answer, with notice and opportunity to be heard, and its decision shall be
appealable to the Secretary of Agriculture and thereafter in accordance with the
Rules of Court. The BAI shall offer mediation at the provincial level, not exceeding
thirty (30) days, before adjudication.
       SEC. 9. Local Sourcing Preference. Meat and meat products procured for
any government food distribution program shall be sourced from domestic
producers, preferably small livestock raisers and accredited associations, through
negotiated procurement under existing procurement law. Not less than fifty percent
(50%) of the total volume procured for such programs in any calendar year shall be
of domestic origin, phased in at not less than thirty percent (30%) in the first year
and forty percent (40%) in the second year of effectivity.
        Imported meat may be allocated to such a program only upon a written
certification by the Secretary of Agriculture, published and valid for not more than
six (6) months, that domestic supply is insufficient.
       SEC. 10. Penalties. After notice and hearing, the BAI shall impose upon an
erring buyer:

                                      Page 3 of 4
      (a) a fine of Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP 10,000.00) to One Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (PhP 100,000.00) for failure to report, or for late
      reporting, under Section 4;
      (b) a fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000.00) to Five Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (PhP 500,000.00) for false reporting or violation of
      Section 6; and
      (c) a fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP 100,000.00) to One
      Million Pesos (PhP 1,000,000.00), plus restitution to the aggrieved
      raiser, for violation of Section 7.
      A second violation shall double the fine; a third shall carry suspension
      of registration for up to one (1) year; and a fourth shall carry
      revocation. These sanctions are without prejudice to criminal liability
      under existing law.
       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from effectivity, the Secretary of Agriculture, in consultation with the Department
of Trade and Industry, the Philippine Competition Commission, accredited
associations, and consumer groups, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations, including reporting thresholds, forms, aggregation parameters, and the
schedule of fines. Failure to promulgate the rules within this period shall not
suspend the effectivity of this Act.
      SEC. 12. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DA, the BAI, and other concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.
       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SEC. 15. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.​
