                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Millions of Filipinos depend on public transportation each day.
Accordingly, passengers should be assured of safe travel and have timely
access to assistance in the event of accidents, crimes, harassment, or other
emergencies.

      Technology such as GPS, CCTV, dashcams and emergency alert systems
can help government agencies and transport operators respond quickly to
incidents and investigate complaints. However, these technologies must be part
of a coordinated system rather than being installed merely as standalone
devices.

      This bill therefore seeks to establish a Public Transport Safety Monitoring
System that will allow government to monitor public transport vehicles, receive
emergency alerts, preserve relevant recordings, and coordinate with law
enforcement and emergency responders.

      The measure also protects the privacy of passengers, drivers and
operators by requiring compliance with existing data privacy laws.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING A PUBLIC TRANSPORT SAFETY MONITORING
 SYSTEM AND PROVIDING FOR THE USE OF SAFETY MONITORING AND
    EMERGENCY RESPONSNE TECHNOLOGY IN PUBLIC TRANSPORT
                           VEHICLES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Public Transport
Safety Monitoring Act.”

SECTION. 2. Declaration of Policy.— It is the policy of the State to protect
the safety and security of passengers, drivers and other persons using public
transportation and to use technology to improve emergency response and
accountability in public transport.

SECTION. 3. Coverage.— This Act shall apply to public transport vehicles
authorized by the government.

SECTION. 4. Safety Monitoring System.— The DOTr shall establish a Public
Transport Safety Monitoring System which shall, when applicable:

           a. Monitor the location of public transport vehicles;
           b. Receive emergency alerts;
           c. Record and preserve safety-related information;
           d. Assist in responding to reported incidents; and
           e. Provide information necessary for the investigation of safety
              incidents.

SECTION. 5. Required Safety Devices.— Covered public transport vehicles
shall be equipped with appropriate safety monitoring devices, which may
include:

           a. GPS or equivalent vehicle-location technology;
           b. CCTV or interior camera;
           c. Forward-facing dashcam; and
           d. Emergency alert or panic-button system.

      The DOTr shall determine which devices are required for each type of
public transport vehicle.
SECTION. 6. Emergency Alert System.— Every covered public transport
vehicle shall have an emergency alert mechanism accessible to the driver and,
where practicable, to passengers. The information shall be transmitted to the
appropriate monitoring or emergency-response center.

SECTION. 7. Passenger Safety Information.— Every covered public transport
vehicle shall prominently display or make readily accessible:

            a. The vehicle’s plate number or identification number;
            b. The driver’s identification number, when applicable;
            c. The operator’s identification;
            d. Emergency contact information; and
            e. The procedure for reporting a safety incident.

      For app-based transport services, the same information shall be made
available through the application.

SECTION. 8. Incident Reporting.— Transport operators shall report serious
safety incidents involving their vehicles or drivers to the appropriate
government agency within the period prescribed by the DOTr.

SECTION. 9. Preservation of Recordings.— Any recordings obtained in
relation to a certain incident, like CCTV footage, dashcam recordings, GPS data,
and other safety-related records, shall be retained for a minimum period of
thirty (30) days, unless a longer retention period is necessary because such
records are relevant to an ongoing investigation, administrative proceeding, or
judicial case. No person shall intentionally destroy, modify, conceal, or
otherwise tamper with safety records pertaining to a reported incident without
proper authorization.

SECTION. 10. Access to Records.— Safety records may be accessed by the
appropriate government agencies, law enforcement authorities, and courts
when authorized by law. Passengers or other persons directly involved in an
incident may request access to relevant records subject to the Data Privacy Act
and other applicable laws.

SECTION. 11. Data Privacy.— All information collected under this Act shall
be collected, stored, processed and disclosed in accordance with Republic Act
No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012. The DOTr and transport operators
shall implement reasonable measures to prevent unauthorized access,
disclosure, alteration or destruction of information.

SECTION. 12. Public Transport Safety Database.— The DOTr shall establish
a centralized Public Transport Safety Database containing relevant and lawfully
obtained information concerning:

         a. Public transport vehicles;
         b. Authorized operators;
         c. Reported safety incidents;
         d. Confirmed safety violations; and
         e. Other information necessary for public transport safety.

      The database may be shared with the LTFRB, LTO, PNP, MMDA, LGUs
and other concerned government agencies, subject to applicable laws.
SECTION. 13. Inter-Agency Coordination.— The DOTr shall coordinate with
the LTFRB, LTO, PNP, MMDA, LGUs, DICT, and other concerned agencies to
establish procedures for:

         a. Emergency response;
         b. Investigation of incidents;
         c. Sharing of relevant information; and
         d. Enforcement of transportation safety regulations.

SECTION. 14.       Tampering     with   Safety   Devices.—     No   person   shall
intentionally:

         a. Disable a required GPS, CCTV, dashcam or emergency alert system;
         b. Destroy or alter a safety recording;
         c. Manipulate vehicle-location information; or
         d. Interfere with an emergency alert.

        Violations shall be subject to applicable administrative, civil or criminal
liability under existing laws and regulations.

SECTION. 15. Administrative Penalties.— The LTFRB or LTO, as applicable,
may impose appropriate administrative penalties for violations of this Act,
including fines, suspension or cancellation of the applicable authority to
operate, subject to due process.

SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Transportation, in
coordination with the DILG, MMDA, and LTO, shall issue the Implementing
Rules and Regulations, including the guidelines for the formulation of a
uniform rules of procedure, for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 17. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this Act or any provision
not affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.

SECTION 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations and other issuances, or any part thereof,
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

SECTION 19. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.
