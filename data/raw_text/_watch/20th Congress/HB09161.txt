                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

            Cooperatives are recognized under Republic Act No. 9520 as vital
      instruments for promoting social justice, economic development, and people
      empowerment. The law affirms the State’s policy to foster the creation and
      growth of cooperatives as practical vehicles for self-reliance and grassroots
      development, particularly among marginalized and underserved sectors.

             Complementing this mandate, Republic Act No. 11364 strengthened
      the institutional framework for cooperative regulation and development
      through the reorganization of the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA).
      The CDA is tasked with registration, regulation, and capacity-building
      initiatives; however, persistent sectoral gaps remain in higher-level
      education, advanced research, and professionalization of cooperative
      management.

            Furthermore, Republic Act No. 7722 mandates the State to promote
      quality higher education and research, while Republic Act No. 10931
      expands access to tertiary education for Filipino students. These laws
      collectively underscore the State’s obligation to invest in human capital
      formation, particularly in specialized fields that support national
      development.

            Despite this robust legal framework, many cooperatives continue to
      face structural constraints, including weak governance systems, limited
      managerial expertise, inadequate research support, and insufficient access
      to continuing education. These challenges hinder their ability to fully realize
      the developmental objectives envisioned under RA 9520 and related laws.

            The Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP), with its long-
      established leadership in cooperative education, is strategically positioned to
      address these gaps. Its historical role as a training center for non-agricultural
      cooperatives and pioneer of the Bachelor in Cooperatives program provides a
      strong institutional foundation for expanded national engagement.
      This measure therefore proposes the establishment of the National
Center for Cooperative Development (NCCD) within PUP. The Center shall
serve as a specialized national institution dedicated to advancing cooperative
education, research, and extension services in support of the policy objectives
enshrined in RA 9520 and other related laws.

     Specifically, the NCCD shall:

   a) Provide degree and non-degree programs aligned with the competency
      standards and development priorities identified under the Cooperative
      Development Authority framework;

   b) Deliver continuing education and certification programs to enhance
      the capabilities of cooperative officers, in line with the governance and
      training requirements under RA 9520;

   c) Conduct applied research to support policy development, legislative
      initiatives, and evidence-based interventions for the cooperative sector;

   d) Extend technical assistance and consultancy services, particularly for
      micro and small cooperatives, to strengthen compliance,
      sustainability, and growth; Promote linkages among academic
      institutions, government agencies, and cooperative organizations to
      ensure coordinated sectoral development.

   The establishment of the NCCD likewise complements national
development strategies, including the Philippine Development Plan,
Ambisyon Natin 2040, and the United Nations Sustainable Development
Goals (SDGs), by strengthening inclusive economic institutions and
promoting equitable access to opportunities.

   By institutionalizing a dedicated national center for cooperative
development within a state university, this measure operationalizes the
State’s commitments under RA 9520, RA 11364, and related laws—bridging
the gap between policy, education, and practice. It ensures that cooperatives
are supported not only through regulation, but through sustained
investments in knowledge, innovation, and leadership development.

   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                               AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL CENTER FOR COOPERATIVE
      DEVELOPMENT     IN  THE   POLYTECHNIC   UNIVERSITY OF
      THE PHILIPPINES AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
 Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Polytechnic
 University of the Philippines National Center for Cooperative Development Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Consistent with Republic Act No. 9520,
 it is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote the viability, growth, and
 sustainability of cooperatives as vital instruments of economic development,
 social equity, and democratic participation. The State recognizes these
 institutions as transformative vehicles that embody the principles of self-help,
 mutual aid, and collective empowerment that contribute to advancing inclusive
 growth, addressing structural poverty, and fostering community resilience.

     Pursuant to this policy, the State shall institutionalize mechanisms for
 cooperative education, research, and capacity-building to ensure the continuing
 development of competent, values-driven, and socially committed cooperative
 leaders and practitioners.

      The State likewise affirms the importance of providing an enabling
 environment where cooperatives may thrive free from external interference, while
 enjoying the benefits of technical, academic, and policy support from public
 institutions.

      In recognition of the indispensable role of knowledge production and
 human capital in the cooperative movement, the State shall support the
 establishment of a dedicated national center that will advance cooperative studies,
 strengthen academic and research linkages, and provide integrated services for
 the benefit of cooperatives and cooperative development stakeholders.
     SECTION 3. Establishment of the National Center for Cooperative
Development. – There is hereby established in the Polytechnic University of the
Philippines (PUP) the National Center for Cooperative Development, hereinafter
referred to as the “Center.”

     The Center shall establish and expand program offerings focused on training
poor but deserving Filipino youth to become the next generation of cooperative
leaders and managers, thereby enhancing the resilience and sustainability of
registered cooperatives in the country and contributing to economic recovery and
national development.

     It shall likewise serve as a centralized national hub providing integrated
services to the cooperative sector, where their activities can be centralized in order
to fulfill a much greater responsibility to address the needs of the cooperative
sector in the Philippines and the Southeast Asian (SEA) region.

    The program trajectory of the Center shall be aligned with the
Sustainable Development         Goals     (SDGs),   International  Cooperative
Development Agenda, the Philippine Development Plan (PDP), and Ambisyon Natin
2040, as it endeavors to address poverty, pursue economic development, promote
empowerment, resiliency, and social innovation through cooperative social
enterprise.

    SECTION 4. Functions of the Center. – The Center shall have the following
functions:

  (a) Offer a Bachelor of Science in Cooperatives degree program, which shall be a
      four-year course with specializations in Cooperative Social Enterprise
      Management and Cooperative Education and Community Development,
      designed to produce globally competitive graduates equipped with values-
      based managerial competence in cooperative organizing, leadership, and
      technopreneurship;

  (b) Implement field deployment programs through on-the-job training (OJT)
      that assign students and staff to micro, small, and medium cooperatives
      to provide support as research enumerators, marketing personnel, and
      administrative assistants;

  (c) Provide modular certificate programs and continuing education services
      for cooperative directors, officers, managers, and support staff through
      regional partnerships nationwide to enhance professional competencies;

  (d) Conduct applied research addressing gaps in cooperative literature and
      supporting legislative development, policy formulation, and evidence-
      based cooperative advancement through a dedicated cooperative research
      group;

  (e) Develop instructional modules, audio-visual materials, and popular
      education tools for elementary, junior high school, senior high school,
      higher education covering cooperative enterprise, social economy, and social
      entrepreneurship;

  (f) Operate a student laboratory cooperative facility where participants
      access cooperative products and services and earn OJT credits through
      documented volunteer work;

  (g) Host annual cooperative congresses, international conferences, and
      training workshops in collaboration with government agencies, industry
      partners, and SEA cooperative alliances;

  (h) Deliver consultancy services assisting cooperative in strategic planning,
      performance auditing, and organizational development by leveraging
      faculty expertise;

  (i) Establish partnerships facilitating local to international knowledge
      exchange on best practices, pandemic resilience strategies, and
      community      development initiatives in accordance with cooperative
      principles; and

  (j) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the purpose
      and objectives of the Center.

    The Center shall coordinate with the Cooperative Development Authority,
Commission on Higher Education, and other relevant agencies in the
implementation of its programs.

     SECTION 5. Delegation of Authority to the Board of Regents. – The Board
of Regents of the PUP is hereby authorized to establish the Center under the
direct supervision of the Office of the President of the University. The Center shall
have cooperative specialists with a high degree of competence and expertise
in the disciplines of business management, financial management, and
community development, who may be appointed from within or outside the
University, subject to applicable civil service and procurement laws.

     SECTION 6. Implementing Authority. - The Board of Regents shall
promulgate the necessary implementing guidelines to carry out the provisions of
this Act and to take appropriate steps to ensure that the establishment of the
Center shall aim at the speedy realization and execution of its functions and
objectives as stated above. Towards this end, the Board shall determine the
structure, composition, and size of the Center’s faculty and staff.

     SECTION 7. Appropriations. - The initial amount of One Hundred Fifty
Million Pesos (₱150,000,000.00) which shall be charged against available
appropriations of the National Government shall be added to the annual budget
of PUP for the purpose of carrying out the initial implementation of this Act.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

     SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

    SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders,
implementing rules and regulations, issuances, or any part thereof inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act are deemed repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

     SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.

    Approved.
