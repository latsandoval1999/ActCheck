                          EXPLANATORY NOTE


      The increasing prevalence of vaping among children and adolescents
has become a growing public health concern in the Philippines. While
vapor products were originally introduced as alternatives for adult smokers,
the proliferation of flavored variants, such as fruit, candy, dessert, and
beverage flavors, has made these products especially appealing to minors,
leading to early nicotine addiction and long-term health risks.

      Numerous studies and health authorities, including the Department of
Health, have warned that nicotine exposure during adolescence can impair
brain development, increase susceptibility to addiction, and serve as a
gateway to conventional smoking. Despite existing laws regulating tobacco
and vapor products, the availability and aggressive marketing of flavored
vape products continue to undermine efforts to protect minors from harmful
substances.

      This proposed measure seeks to address this gap by specifically
prohibiting the sale, distribution, and promotion of flavored vapor products
to minors. By focusing on flavors that are designed to attract younger
consumers, the bill reinforces the State’s duty to safeguard the health and
welfare of children without unduly restricting adult access to regulated
products.

      The measure also strengthens accountability among retailers and
online sellers by requiring strict age verification and imposing graduated
penalties for violations. In doing so, it complements existing laws such as
Republic Act No. 9211, Republic Act No. 11467, and Republic Act No.
11900, and ensures that the protection of minors remains paramount in the
regulation of vapor products.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT​
   PROHIBITING THE SALE, DISTRIBUTION, AND PROMOTION OF
   FLAVORED VAPOR PRODUCTS TO MINORS, AND PROVIDING
                    PENALTIES THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:



     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Flavored
Vape Products Protection for Minors Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. —​
 It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the health and
well-being of children and the youth from the harmful effects of nicotine
addiction and exposure to hazardous substances. The State recognizes
that flavored vapor products are particularly attractive to minors and
contribute to the initiation of smoking and vaping among the youth.
Accordingly, the State shall regulate and restrict access to such products to
safeguard public health.

     SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. —​


     As used in this Act:

     (a) Minor refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age.

     (b) Vapor Product or Vape Product refers to any non-combustible
product, whether electronic or mechanical, that delivers nicotine or any
other substance through aerosolization, including electronic cigarettes,
pods, cartridges, and similar devices, as defined under existing laws.

      (c) Flavored Vapor Product refers to any vapor product that imparts
a distinguishable taste or aroma other than tobacco, including but not
limited to fruit, candy, dessert, chocolate, mint, menthol, beverage, or
similar flavors.

       (d) Retailer refers to any person or entity engaged in the sale or
distribution of vapor products, whether through physical stores, online
platforms, or other means.

       SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any person or
entity to:

     (a) Sell, distribute, or provide flavored vapor products to minors;

      (b) Allow the purchase of flavored vapor products by minors through
online platforms, delivery services, or other indirect means;

      (c) Sell flavored vapor products without requiring valid proof of age
from the purchaser; and

    (d) Promote, advertise, or market flavored vapor products in a
manner that is appealing to or primarily directed at minors.

      SEC. 5. Duties of Retailers and Online Sellers. — All retailers and
online sellers of vapor products shall:

      (a) Require and verify valid government-issued identification prior to
the sale of any vapor product;

     (b) Implement age-verification mechanisms for online sales; and

     (c) Display conspicuous notices stating that the sale of flavored vapor
products to minors is prohibited by law.

      SEC. 6. Penalties. — Any person or entity found guilty of violating
this Act shall be subject to the following penalties:

     (a) First offense — A fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos
(₱50,000.00) and mandatory attendance in a government-approved health
education program;

      (b) Second offense — A fine of not less than One Hundred
Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) and suspension of business license for a
period of thirty (30) days;

       (c) Third and subsequent offenses — A fine of not less than Two
Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00), revocation of business license,
and imprisonment of not more than six (6) months, at the discretion of the
court.
If the offender is a corporation, partnership, or association, the penalty shall
be imposed upon the responsible officers.

       SEC. 7. Enforcement. — The Department of Health (DOH), the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Local Government Units
(LGUs), and other concerned agencies shall jointly enforce the provisions
of this Act and issue the necessary rules and regulations.

      SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the
DTI and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary
implementing rules and regulations.

        SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain
in full force and effect.

      SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
