HOUSE BILLNO. 11749

Introduced by REPRESENTATIVE AUDREY KAY T. ZUBIRI

AN ACT
MANDATING FOOD MANUFACTURERS TO PROMINENTLY DISPLAY
WARNING LABELS ON PREPACKAGED FOOD PRODUCTS CONTAINING HIGH
LEVELS OF SUGAR, SODIUM, CALORIES, OR FAT, AND PROVIDING FOR
RELATED PURPOSES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

A healthy nation begins with an informed citizenry, and an informed citizenry begins

with knowing what is on its plate.

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), heart diseases remain the
leading cause of death in the Philippines, accounting for approximately 21.2% of registered
deaths, followed by neoplasms at 9.9%, cerebrovascular diseases at 9.6%, pneumonia at 8.0%,

and diabetes at 5.7%!.

While these conditions arise from multiple and complex causes, unhealthy dietary
patterns are among the significant and preventable risk factors associated with many of them.
Their continued prevalence highlights the need for policies that go beyond treating illness and
instead strengthen prevention, promote healthier food choices, and empower Filipinos to make

informed decisions about their health.

Diets high in sugar, sodium, saturated fat, and calories are associated with various
health risks, particularly when consumed excessively and regularly. According to the

Department of Science and Technology (DOST), Filipinos consume more than twice the

recommended limit of sodium and high amounts of sugar from processed foods, while the
country continues to face widespread micronutrient deficiencies. These concerns are
compounded by the consumption of ready-made and highly processed products commonly
found in groceries and other retail establishments, including instant noodles, processed meats,
salted eggs, and other processed or preserved foods. This reflects the country's dual nutritional
challenge: inadequate intake of essential micronutrients alongside excessive consumption of
nutrients associated with diet-related diseases. Addressing this problem requires not only
promoting healthier diets but also ensuring that consumers can readily identify the nutritional

risks of the products they purchase.

The 1987 Constitution provides a clear foundation for such measures. Article II, Section
15 declares that the State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill
health consciousness among them. Article XIII, Section 11 directs the State to adopt an
integrated and comprehensive approach to health development, while Article XIII, Section 12
provides for an effective food and drug regulatory system responsive to the country's health

needs.

Existing legislation likewise provides consumer safeguards. Republic Act No. 7394, or
the Consumer Act of the Philippines, requires manufacturers to provide appropriate
information about their products, including their contents and ingredients. However, disclosure
alone may not ensure that consumers recognize excessive levels of sugar, sodium, fat, or
calories. Consumers may have to examine detailed nutrition facts, interpret serving sizes, and
compare nutrient quantities before determining the nutritional risks of a product. Information

that is technically available may therefore remain difficult to interpret at the point of purchase.

Hence, this measure seeks to strengthen existing food-labeling and consumer-
protection policies by requiring manufacturers of prepackaged food products to display
prominent warning labels on products containing excessive levels of sugar, sodium, calories,
saturated fat, and other food components that may pose health risks when consumed
excessively, based on scientifically established thresholds and standards to be determined by
the appropriate regulatory authorities. The measure does not prohibit consumers from
purchasing or consuming these products. Instead, it enables them to make informed choices by
providing an immediate indication of products that exceed established nutritional thresholds.
In doing so, the measure advances consumer protection while supporting the constitutional

mandate to instill health consciousness among Filipinos.

===== [page 3] =====
The potential of this approach is demonstrated by Chile, which implemented nutrient
warning labels as part of a broader strategy addressing unhealthy food consumption. During
Phase 2 of its implementation, researchers found reductions in household purchases of products
subject to the warning-label regulations”. While the Philippine context differs from Chile in
terms of dietary practices, markets, and socioeconomic conditions, the study provides relevant
evidence that front-of-package warnings can form part of a public-health strategy and

encourage healthier purchasing decisions.

Ultimately, addressing the country's burden of heart disease, cancer, diabetes, and other

diet-related noncommunicable diseases require a comprehensive preventive approach.

Healthcare must not begin only when a person enters a hospital; it must also begin with
the choices made before illness develops. This measure complements existing nutrition
programs, healthcare services, food regulations, and consumer-protection laws by giving
Filipinos a simpler way to recognize potential nutritional risks in the products they purchase.
By making excessive levels of sugar, sodium, calories, saturated fat, and other regulated
nutrients immediately visible, the measure seeks to promote health consciousness, strengthen
consumer protection, encourage responsible food manufacturing, and contribute to disease

prevention.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Check the Label Act”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health, uphold consumer welfare, and promote health consciousness by
ensuring access to accurate, clear, and understandable information on the nutritional

composition of food products.

Pursuant to Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution on the right to health, Article XIII,
Section 11 on an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development, and Article
XIII, Section 12 on an effective food and drug regulatory system, the State shall promote

informed and health-conscious food choices through appropriate food labelling measures.

Consistent with Republic Act No. 7394, or the Consumer Act of the Philippines, and Republic
Act No. 10611, or the Food Safety Act of 2013, such measures shall ensure that information
provided to consumers is truthful, accessible, and not misleading, while supporting the safe and

informed consumption of food products.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

(a) Food refers to any substance or product whether processed, partially processed, or
unprocessed that is intended for human consumption, including drinks, chewing gum, and any

(b) Food Manufacturer refers to an establishment engaged in any and all operations involved
in the production of food products, including preparation, processing, formulation, filling,
packing, repacking, altering, finishing, and labeling for purposes of storage, sale, or

distribution;

(c) Food Product refers to food intended for human consumption that is manufactured,

processed, prepared, packaged, labelled, distributed, or sold for commercial purposes;

(d) Label refers to the display of written, printed, or graphic matter upon the immediate
container, tag, literature, or other suitable material affixed thereto for the purpose of giving
information as to the identity, components, ingredients, attributes, directions for use,

specifications, and other information required by law or regulation;

(e) Labeling refers to all labels and other written, printed, or graphic matter upon or
accompanying a food product, including information required by law or regulation to be

displayed to consumers;

(f) Prepackaged Food refers to food packaged or placed in a container before being offered for

sale to consumers and intended for direct sale to consumers;

(g) Front-of-Pack refers to the principal display panel or other portion of the package that is
most likely to be displayed, presented, shown, or examined by consumers under normal and

customary conditions of retail display;

SEC. 4. Mandatory Front-of-Pack Warning Label. - All manufacturers, importers,
distributors, and other persons responsible for placing prepackaged food products in the
Philippine market shall ensure that a food product bears a front-of-pack warning label
whenever the product exceeds or meets the applicable threshold for sugar, sodium, calories, or

fat prescribed under this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

A separate warning shall be required for each nutrient or energy component for which the

applicable threshold is met or exceeded.

Accordingly, a food product may bear more than one warning label.

SEC. 5. Categories of Warning Labels. - The following standardized warnings shall be used:
(a) “HIGH IN SUGAR”;

(b) “HIGH IN SODIUM”;

(c) “HIGH IN CALORIES”; and

(d) “HIGH IN FAT.”

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) may prescribe additional or more specific
terminology, including a distinction between total fat and saturated fat, when warranted by

current scientific evidence and international nutrition standards.

SEC. 6. Nutrient Profile Model. (NPM).

(a) The Department of Health (DOH) shall develop and adopt a nutrient profile model
(NPM) for classifying and categorizing food according to their nutrient threshold and/or
level of processing. Pending the development of a local NPM, a conflict-free, evidence-
based NPM recommended by the World Health Organization shall used as the basis for

such food classification.

SEC. 9. Prohibition Against Misleading Presentation. - No manufacturer, importer,
distributor, seller, or other person shall alter, conceal, minimize, or otherwise manipulate the

presentation of a warning label in a manner that diminishes its visibility or effectiveness.

No person shall use words, images, symbols, health claims, or other representations that falsely
imply that a food product is healthy, nutritious, or free from health risks when such

representation is inconsistent with the applicable warning required under this Act.

Nothing in this Section shall prohibit truthful nutritional information or claims that comply

with applicable FDA regulations.

SEC. 10. Responsibility of Manufacturers and Importers. - Food manufacturers and importers
shall be primarily responsible for ensuring that covered products comply with the warning-

label requirements before the products are distributed or offered for sale in the Philippines.

Imported prepackaged food products shall comply with this Act before being released for

commercial distribution.

SEC. 11. Small and Micro Food Manufacturers. - The FDA, in coordination with the DTI
and other relevant agencies, shall establish reasonable compliance assistance and transition

mechanisms for micro and small food manufacturers.

Such assistance may include technical guidance, standardized templates, educational programs,

and reasonable transition periods.

Nothing in this Section shall exempt a covered product from the substantive warning-label

SEC. 12. Exemptions. - The FDA may exempt certain categories of food products from the
requirements of this Act when the nature, packaging, intended use, or manner of sale of such

products makes the warning-label requirement unnecessary or impracticable.
Such exemptions shall be limited and shall be based on objective, science-based criteria.

The FDA shall publish and periodically review the list of exempt products.

SEC. 13. Compliance Period. - All covered food products manufactured, imported, or
distributed in the Philippines shall comply with this Act within eighteen (18) months from the

effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations.

Food products already manufactured or imported before the end of the compliance period may
be sold until their expiration date, unless otherwise determined by the FDA for reasons of

public health and safety.

of the warning-label requirements of this Act shall be subject to an administrative fine of not
less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P500,000.00), depending on the gravity and frequency of the violation.

For a second and subsequent violation, the administrative fine may be increased to not less than

SEC. 18. Public Information and Education Campaign. - The DOH, in coordination with the
FDA, Department of Education (DepEd), DTI, DA, local government units, and other relevant

agencies, shall conduct public information and education campaigns regarding:

(a) the meaning and purpose of front-of-pack warning labels;

(b) the health significance of consuming foods high in sugar, sodium, calories, or fat;
(c) the proper interpretation of nutrition information; and

(d) the importance of balanced and healthy dietary choices.

The campaign shall be designed to ensure that consumers, including children and vulnerable

populations, can understand the warning-label system.

SEC. 19. Inter-Agency Coordination. - The FDA shall coordinate with the DOH, DTI, DA,
Department of Education, local government units, and other relevant government agencies to

ensure the effective and uniform implementation of this Act.

The agencies may enter into agreements and establish mechanisms for information-sharing,

inspection, enforcement, technical assistance, and public education.

SEC. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred eighty (180) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the FDA, in coordination with the DOH and other relevant agencies,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this

Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall include:
(a) the nutrient and calorie thresholds;

(b) the official warning-label design;

(c) minimum size and placement requirements;

(d) procedures for determining compliance;

(e) laboratory testing and verification procedures;

(f) exemptions and special product categories;

(g) compliance periods and transition requirements;
(h) administrative penalties; and

(i) procedures for inspection, enforcement, recall, and consumer complaints.

SEC. 21. Periodic Review. - The FDA and DOH shall review the implementation of this Act

at least once every three (3) years.

The review shall consider developments in nutrition science, consumer understanding, industry

compliance, technological developments, and relevant international standards.

The FDA may recommend amendments to the implementing rules and regulations when

necessary to protect public health and improve the effectiveness of the warning-label system.

SEC. 22. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the FL DA and the DOH.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continyed implementation of this Act shall

be included in the annual General Apraepuians Act.
