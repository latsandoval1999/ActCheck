EXPLANATORY NOTE

The fundamental laws of the Philippines have long recognized and protected
academic freedom. Expanded from state universities under the 1935 Constitution to
all institutions of higher learning under the 1973 and 1987 Constitutions, the State
has affirmed that institutions of higher learning shall enjoy academic freedom.
Furthermore, under the declaration of principles of Republic Act No. 7722 or the
Higher Education Act, the State shall “ensure and protect academic freedom and
shall promote its exercise and observance.” In Calleja v. Executive Secretary (2021),
citing a long line of jurisprudence on academic freedom, this freedom is vital to
intellectual liberty, allowing university communities to “follow the argument
wherever it may lead,” free from internal and external interference or pressure.’

However, the current climate for academic freedom in the Philippines and
globally is increasingly precarious. The Free to Think 2025 Report by the Scholars at
Risk (SAR) Academic Freedom Monitoring Project underscores a disturbing global
trend: state leaders are mounting escalating and aggressive attacks on scholars,
students, and universities.” In 2020, the United Nations (“UN”) Special Rapporteur

The 2026 Report of the UN Special Rapporteur on the rights to freedom of

refrain from co-opting their associations; review and reform norms that restrict
critical thinking and civic education; strictly refrain from using surveillance and
arbitrary disciplinary punishments to penalize students for peaceful dissent or
participation in independent student organizations; and implement specific
safeguards for marginalized young people including Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual,
Transexual, Queer, Intersex (LGBTQI+), Indigenous groups, and young persons with
disabilities. This bill adopts these standards.

In the Philippines, the unilateral abrogation of the 1989 UP-DND Accord and
the 1990 PUP-DND Accord in 2021 and 2022, respectively, marked a significant
turning point, removing institutional safeguards that previously protected academic
spaces from the coercive presence of state armed forces. Without these protections,
universities have become vulnerable to intimidation. The Free to Think 2025 Report

Without statutory intervention, threats of forcible entry and harassment will

To preserve the integrity of educational institutions as true zones of peace and
centers of critical inquiry, it is imperative to enact the Academic Freedom Act of
2026. This bill seeks to codify the protection of academic spaces by explicitly
prohibiting the arbitrary entry and presence of state armed forces. It addresses the
systematic reduction of institutional safeguards by establishing clear prohibited acts,
including surveillance, the use of intelligence networks, and the red-tagging of
constituents, arbitrary disciplinary retaliation against student activists, co-optation of
student organizations, and discrimination against marginalized youth groups.

Furthermore, this Act establishes a Coordination and Monitoring Group in
each academic institution to ensure transparency and compliance, protects digital
civic space for youth activists, and provides for strict penalties~including fines,
suspension, and imprisonment -— for public officials who violate these protections. By
insulating learning environments from coercive state presence, this legislation
ensures that students, teachers, and education stakeholders can exercise their rights
to peaceably assemble and engage in critical discourse without fear of persecution.

The Free to Think 2025 Report emphasizes that academic freedom is as crucial
to a free society as a free press or an independent judiciary. The 2026 UN youth
activism report adds that human rights —including peaceful assembly, association,
participation and expression —are learned through practice, not just theory, and that
campuses must be vital arenas where democratic values are actively exercised. It
serves as a bulwark of democracy, training students to be critical, engaged members
of civil society. The passage of this bill is a necessary, proactive step to fulfill the

AN ACT
DEFINING AND DEFENDING ACADEMIC FREEDOM IN ALL ACADEMIC
INSTITUTIONS, INSTITUTIONALIZING MEASURES FOR ITS PROTECTION,
AND PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Academic Freedom Act
of 2026.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State shall protect, foster and promote
the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and shall take appropriate
steps to ensure that education shail be accessible to all. The State shall likewise
ensure and protect academic freedom, which is an integral part of the entitlement to
provide and receive quality education, which must be protected at all levels of
education. The State shall promote the exercise and observance of academic freedom
for the continuing intellectual growth, the advancement of learning and research, the
development of responsible and effective leadership, and the enrichment of our
historical and cultural heritage.

The State recognizes academic freedom as an essential right of academic, research,
and teaching staff and students, vital to the production and dissemination of
knowledge, democratic self-governance, and the promotion of the general welfare.

Consistent with the Constitution and the Republic’s international commitments
under the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the International Covenant on

===== [page 6] =====
Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, the International Covenant on Civil and
Political Rights, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, and relevant UNESCO
recommendations, the State shall protect, promote, and fulfill the right to academic
freedom at all levels of education. The State shall likewise prohibit and penalize all
acts that threaten, undermine, or violate this right, including repression of student
activism and peaceful assembly.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For the purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

(a) “Academic freedom” refers to the right to acquire, develop, transmit, apply,
and engage with a diversity of knowledge and ideas through research,
teaching, learning, and discourse, including intramural and extramural
expression, without fear of retaliation or sanction based on the content of such
activity.

(b) “Educational institution” means any public or private entity engaged in early
childhood care and education, primary, secondary, technical-vocational,
tertiary, or adult education, including universities, colleges, and research
institutes.

(c) “Academic, research, and teaching staff’ refers to persons employed or
engaged in teaching, research, or administrative leadership in educational
institutions.

(d) “Students” refers to all persons enrolled or participating in any level of
education, including student organizations.

(e) “Intramural expression” means the exercise of academic freedom within the
academic community.

(f) “Extramural expression” means the exercise of academic freedom outside the
academic community, including engagement with the public.

(g) “Militarization” means the presence, use, occupation, or control of
educational institutions by armed forces, police, or paramilitary groups ina
manner that interferes with teaching, research, or the safety of the academic
community.

(h) “Peaceful assembly” means the right of students, staff, and academic
communities to gather, protest, march, or mobilize peacefully for political,
social, educational, or cultural causes without violence.

(i) “Political dissent” means the expression of contrary or critical views on
matters of public interest, policy, governance, or institutional practice.

===== [page 7] =====
(j) “Surveillance and profiling tools” means any technological, digital, or human
intelligence method used to monitor, track, identify, or profile students or staff
based on their activism, beliefs, or association.

(k) “Marginalized students” means students belonging to groups facing
structural exclusion, including but not limited to LGBTQI+ persons,
Indigenous peoples, persons with disabilities, young women, and other
minority communities.

(l) “Student organization” means any formal or informal association of students
for the purpose of representing peers, advocating rights, or engaging in civic
and political education, and activities or pursues objectives related to student,
educational, social, cultural, civic, athletic, or community interests.

SECTION 4. Right to Academic Freedom and Expression. — Every academic,
research, and teaching staff and student shall have the right to:

(a) Access, disseminate, and produce information; think; and develop, express,
apply, and engage with a diversity of knowledge within or related to one's
field of study, whether intramural or extramural;

(b) Inquire into, express, or act on matters relating to the conditions, actions, or
policies of academic, research, or teaching institutions, including with the
public;

(c) Share the content of research, teaching, or discourse with the public through
publications, testimonies, media, exhibits, demonstrations, and other events;

(d) Exercise the social responsibility to respond to contemporary problems and
needs of society through truth-seeking and the imparting of information
according to ethical and professional standards; and

(e) Exercise the right to peaceful assembly and free association for political,
social, educational, or cultura] causes, both on and off campus, without fear of
reprisal.

SECTION 5. Institutional Autonomy. — The State, through its officials and
agencies, shall respect the autonomy of educational institutions. No law, policy, or
practice shall:

(a) Impose militarization, surveillance, or other measures that undermine
academic freedom and autonomy;

(b) Sanction or threaten to sanction educational institutions or their
leadership—including by removal from office, withholding or reduction of
budgetary allocations, or deprivation of privileges—based on the content of
academic research, teaching, or discourse alone; or

===== [page 8] =====
(c) Allow actors outside the education sector to control, sanction, or privilege the
content of teaching, research, or discourse through systems of financing or
accountability.

Academic, research, and teaching staff shall enjoy professional freedom to judge,
select, and deploy teaching materials, aids, and methods commensurate with their
qualifications. Appointment, hiring, promotion, tenure, and dismissal rules must be
free from political, commercial, or religious and discrimination. Educational
institutions shall allow for the renewal of student leaderships and shall refrain from
co-opting student associations or substituting them with institution-controlled
bodies.

SECTION 6. Access to Information and Resources. — The State shall ensure that
systems for authorization of access to information archives — public or private —are
transparent, equitable, and not overly restrictive. For-profit or commercial
limitations on access to information that unduly burden academic work shall be
balanced by open access systems, copyright exemptions for academic purposes, and
public benefit exceptions. Restrictions on access to personal information necessary to
protect privacy shall not be construed as violations of academic freedom.

SECTION 7. Freedoms of Movement and Association. — The State shall respect and
encourage international contacts and cooperation among academic, research, and
teaching staff and students, including through supportive visa and travel policies.
No restriction on entry, exit, or movement shall be imposed to punish, deter, or
impede the research, teaching, or discourse of members of the education sector. Staff
and student organizations shall be allowed to form and operate without political or
outside interference, surveillance, or intimidation. The State shall guarantee the right
of students under 18 to establish and join associations in line with the Convention on
the Rights of the Child.

SECTION 8. Academic Freedom at All Levels and for Students. — Academic
freedom shall be protected at all levels of education, from early childhood through
adult education. Students shall enjoy the right to academic freedom consistent with
their evolving capacities, including the right to organize, express, and protest
without threat of suspension, expulsion, arrest, prosecution, or violence. The State
and educational institutions shall ensure equitable access to education and take
affirmative measures for traditionally underrepresented groups.

Students shall not be subjected to arbitrary disciplinary punishments such as
suspension, expulsion, or revocation of scholarships to restrict, dissuade, suppress,
or penalize them for peaceful political dissent or participation in independent
student organizations. Educational institutions shall implement specific protection
safeguards addressing gender-based violence and the distinct needs of marginalized
students including LGBTQI+ persons, Indigenous groups, and young persons with
disabilities, and shall ensure that organizations representing such marginalized
youth communities are provided institutional protection and can access adequate
funding mechanisms.

===== [page 9] =====
SECTION 8-A. Protection of Digital Civic Space in Education. — The State and
educational institutions shall respect digital platforms as primary infrastructure for
modern youth communication, assembly, and political expression. They shall:

(a) Refrain from imposing blanket agebased social media bans or
shadow-banning student activists, and from enforcing arbitrary network
shutdowns that prevent student mobilization;

(b) Strictly refrain from using digital surveillance, biometric tracking, or profiling
tools to chill student activism or identify participants in peaceful assemblies;
and

c) Review digital services, content moderation, and surveillance architectures in
&
direct consultation with diverse student representatives to ensure rights are
protected by design.

SECTION 9. Shared Responsibility and Monitoring. — The State bears the prime
duty to protect, promote, and fulfill academic freedom. The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), the Department of Education (DepEd), and the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in consultation with civil
society, academic staff, and student organizations, shall:

(a) Promulgate a national statement of values affirming academic freedom and
institutional autonomy;

(b) Establish an Academic Freedom Ombudsperson within each agency to receive
complaints, conduct investigations, and issue recommendations;

(c) Conduct and publish an annual academic freedom assessment covering all
levels of education, with specific indicators on student disciplinary retaliation,
campus surveillance, and marginalized group inclusion; and

(d) Develop and disseminate education programs on academic freedom for state
officials, educators, students, and the public.

SECTION 10. Prohibited Acts and Violations. — The following shall constitute
violations of this Act:

(a) Imposing sanctions, threats, or retaliations against educational institutions,
leadership, staff, or students based on the content of academic research,
teaching, or discourse;

(b) Militarizing, surveilling, or occupying educational institutions in a manner
that undermines autonomy or endangers the academic community;

(c) Withholding, reducing, or conditioning public funds or privileges to punish
the exercise of academic freedom;

===== [page 10] =====
(d) Restricting the movement, entry, exit, or association of academic personnel or
students to deter or impede their scholarly or expressive activity;

(e) Infiltrating, surveilling, or obstructing student organizations or staff
associations;

(f} Imposing pre-approval requirements on publications, curricula, or events on
the grounds of political dissent;

(g) Disseminating false or misleading information by state officials to disparage
or marginalize educational institutions, staff, or students;

(h) Attacks, threats, arrests, prosecutions, or violence against students, teachers,
schools, and universities, including military use of educational facilities
during armed conflict or emergency;

(i) Imposing arbitrary disciplinary punishments such as suspension, expulsion,
or scholarship revocation against students for peaceful political dissent or
participation in independent student organizations;

(j) Co-opting student associations, preventing the renewal of student
leaderships, or replacing independent organizations with State-affiliated
bodies;

(k) Using surveillance or profiling tools to monitor students or staff based on
political or academic expression;

(1) Denying institutional protection, venue, funding, or recognition to
organizations of marginalized students including LGBTQI-+, Indigenous, or
disabled groups;

(m) Imposing blanket digital restrictions, social media bans, or network
shutdowns that prevent student mobilization and assembly; and

(n) Any act contrary to the principles of academic freedom enshrined in this Act.

SECTION 11. Penalties. — Any person, public official, or private entity found guilty
of violating Section 10 shall be penalized as follows:

(a) For violations under paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (£), (h), (i), and (k) of Section 10:
imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more than six (6) years,
and a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand pesos (PHP 100,000) but
not more than Five Hundred Thousand pesos (PHP 500,000). Public officials
shall likewise suffer perpetual disqualification from holding public office.

(b) For violations under all other paragraphs under Section 10: imprisonment of
not less than one (1) month but not more than two (2) years, and a fine of not

===== [page 11] =====
less than Fifty Thousand pesos (PHP 50,000) but not more than Two Hundred
Thousand pesos (PHP 200,000).

Educational institutions or private entities complicit in violations shall be subject to
suspension of operating permits, cancellation of contracts, and forfeiture of public
subsidies or grants.

Victims of violations shall be entitled to legal remedies, restitution, and
reinstatement to position or status without prejudice, and psychosocial support
where applicable.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out the provisions
of this Act shall be charged against the current budgets of CHED, DepEd, and
TESDA and shall thereafter be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within thirty (30) days from
the effectivity of this Act, CHED, DepEd, and TESDA, in consultation with civil
society groups, academic staff unions, and student organizations, shall promulgate
the necessary Implementing Rules and Regulations.

SECTION 14. Separability Clause. — Any provision or portion of this Act that may
be declared unconstitutional shall not have the effect of nullifying other parts
thereof.

SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders,
issuances, and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to, and
inconsistent with any provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 16. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
