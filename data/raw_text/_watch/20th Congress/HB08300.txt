                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Clinical research plays a vital role in advancing medical science, improving
  patient outcomes, and expanding access to innovative therapies. As global
  health innovation accelerates, countries that maintain clear, efficient, and
  ethically robust regulatory systems for clinical trials are better positioned to
  attract investments, participate in multi-regional clinical trials (MRCTs), and
  ensure early access to emerging treatments for their citizens.

      At present, while the Philippines regulates clinical trials through the Food
  and Drug Administration (FDA) pursuant to Republic Act No. 9711 and
  coordinates health research under Republic Act No. 10532, there is no
  comprehensive statutory framework that consolidates clinical trial
  governance, institutionalizes streamlined review mechanisms, strengthens
  ethical safeguards, and promotes national competitiveness in this field.

      This proposed measure establishes a coherent legal framework for the
  governance, regulation, and promotion of clinical trials in the Philippines. It
  creates a specialized Clinical Trials Regulatory Office within the FDA to
  strengthen oversight of pre-commercial clinical trials while preserving the
  FDA’s authority as the National Regulatory Authority. The bill institutionalizes
  risk-based review pathways, a national clinical trial registry, enhanced ethics
  oversight, and clear participant protection mechanisms consistent with
  international standards of Good Clinical Practice.

      The measure likewise promotes equitable access for indigent Filipinos
  participating in clinical trials, encourages responsible public-private
  partnerships, and positions the Philippines as a credible and competitive site
  for global clinical research, including participation in MRCTs.
    By strengthening regulatory efficiency while upholding ethical integrity
and participant safety, this bill advances the State’s constitutional mandate
to protect and promote the right to health and to ensure a responsive and
effective health regulatory system.

   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                              CHAPTER 1
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title - This Act shall be known as the "Philippine
 Clinical Trial Act of 2026"

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy - The State recognizes that clinical
 research enhances access to investigational medicinal products and
 qualified healthcare professionals that address the needs of the Filipino
 people. Such research is anchored on the principles of beneficence and
 promoting scientific advancement and medical research. Hence,
 highlighting the need to establish a centralized, efficient, and transparent
 regulatory framework for clinical trials in the field of human and veterinary
 medicine, food safety, medical technology, and cosmetics.

      It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the right to health
 by ensuring that clinical trials involving human participants are
 conducted ethically, scientifically, transparently, and in accordance with
 internationally recognized standards of Good Clinical Practice (GCP).
     Accordingly, the State shall:

     a. Establish a specialized statutory and institutional framework for
        clinical trial governance;
     b. Safeguard the rights, safety, dignity, and welfare of participants;
     c. Promote equitable access of indigent Filipinos to innovative
        therapies;
     d. Position the Philippines as a competitive hub for ethical clinical
        research and Multi- Regional Clinical Trials (MRCTs)
     e. Encourage responsible public-private collaboration;
     f. Ensure transparency and accountability in clinical research
        activities.

     Section 3. Scope and Coverage This Act applies to all clinical trials
conducted within Philippine jurisdiction involving human participants and
investigational health products consistent with the regulatory authority of
the Food and Drug Administration under Republic Act No. 9711,
including:

     a. Drugs, biologics, vaccines and advanced therapies;
     b. Medical devices and In Vitro diagnostics;
     c. Food products requiring human safety, nutritional, or functional
        substantiation,
     d. Cosmetic products requiring human safety or performance
        evaluation;
     e. Other health-related products as determined by the Department
        of Health (DOH).

      This Act covers commercial, non-commercial, academic, government-
initiated, investigator-initiated, and multi-regional clinical trials (MRCTs).

     Nothing in this Act, however, shall be construed to regulate routine
laboratory testing, product quality control procedures, or post-marketing
surveillance activities already governed by existing FDA regulations.

     Section 4. Definition of Terms - For purposes of this Act, the
following terms shall have the meanings provided below:

     a. Clinical Trial - A systematic investigation involving human
        participants    to   evaluate    safety,  efficacy, performance,
        pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, or other effects of an
        investigational health product.
     b. Contract Research Organization (CRO) - An entity contracted by
        the sponsor to perform trial-related functions.
     c. Ethics Review Committee (ERC) - An independent body tasked
        with ethical review and protection of participants.
     d. Indigent Patient - A Filipino citizen classified as financially
        incapable of affording necessary medical services as defined by
        DOH guidelines.
     e. Investigational Health Product - A product not yet authorized for
        commercial distribution, used in a clinical trial.
     f. Investigator - A qualified individual responsible for the conduct of
        a clinical trial at a site.
     g. Good Clinical Practice (GCP)- International ethical and scientific
        quality standards for designing, conducting, recording, and
        reporting clinical trials.
     h. Multi-Regional Clinical Trial (MRCT) - A clinical trial conducted
        simultaneously in multiple countries under a common protocol.
     i. Sponsor - A person, company, institution, or organization that
        initiates, manages and/or finances a clinical trial.

     Section 5 Creation of the Philippine Clinical Trials Regulatory
Office (PCTRO)-

      There is hereby established a Philippine Clinical Trials Regulatory
Office (PCTRO) within the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), which
shall serve as the specialized unit responsible for strengthening and
institutionalizing the regulation, coordination, and oversight of pre-
commercial clinical trials in the Philippines.

     The PCTRO shall operate under the supervision and control of the
FDA Director General and shall exercise delegated functions consistent
with the powers of the FDA under Republic Act No. 9711.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish, transfer, or modify
the statutory authority of the FDA as the National Regulatory Authority
over health products, including investigational products used in clinical
trials.

      The PCTRO shall provide a centralized and streamlined framework for
clinical trial authorization, inspection, registry management, ethics
coordination, and compliance monitoring, in accordance with applicable
laws and internationally recognized standards of Good Clinical Practice
(GCP).

     The PCTRO shall be headed by a Director appointed by the Secretary
of Health upon recommendation of the FDA Director General and shall be
supported by technical, legal, pharmacovigilance, inspection, and ethics
support divisions, subject to civil service laws, government auditing rules,
and existing FDA organizational structures.
      Section 6. Powers and Functions of the PCTRO.

      The PCTRO shall:

      a. Review and grant Clinical Trial Authorizations (CTA);
      b. Establish risk-based review pathways (standard, expedited,
         priority);
      c. Conduct routine and for-cause inspections and audits;
      d. Recommend enforcement actions and, where authorized by the
         FDA Director General, issue administrative orders relating to
         clinical trial authorization, suspension, or termination;
      e. Accredit and monitor Ethics Review Committees for purposes of
         FDA-regulated clinical trials, in coordination with the Philippine
         National Health Research System under Republic Act No. 10532;
      f. Establish and maintain the Philippine Clinical Trial Registry;
      g. Develop national GCP training and certification programs;
      h. Coordinate with international regulators for MRCT participation;
      i. Issue guidance documents, circulars, and technical standards.

      Section 7. Delineation of Functions Within the FDA. — The Philippine
Clinical Trials Regulatory Office (PCTRO) shall exercise delegated authority,
under the supervision and control of the FDA Director General, over the review,
authorization, inspection, and monitoring of pre-commercial clinical trials.

      The FDA shall retain ultimate regulatory authority under Republic Act No.
9711, including but not limited to:

   a. Evaluation of clinical trial data submitted for product registration;
   b. Issuance of market authorization and certificates of product registration;
   c. Post-marketing surveillance, pharmacovigilance, and enforcement actions
      involving registered products;
   d. Enforcement of regulatory actions relating to investigational products used
      in clinical trials.

     Nothing in this Act shall be construed as creating a separate regulatory
 authority apart from the FDA or as transferring jurisdiction granted under
 Republic Act No. 9711.

      Section 8. Scope of Clinical Trials covered by the Regulatory
 Framework on Clinical Trials Regulated in the Philippines, including but
 not limited to:

      a. Investigational Drug Products
     i.   New chemical entities (NCEs).
    ii.   Biologics, including vaccines, gene therapies, and cell-based
          therapies.
   iii.   Biosimilars and reformulated drug products.

b. Bioequivalence Studies Involving Human Participants

     i.   Studies designed to compare the rate and extent of
          absorption of an investigational product with that of a
          reference drug product.
    ii.   Required for the approval of generic drug products and
          locally manufactured equivalents.
   iii.   Subject to Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Good Laboratory
          Practice (GLP), and ethical review, with mandatory informed
          consent and safety oversight.

c. Food Investigational Products

     i.   Functional Foods- Foods fortified with vitamins, minerals,
          or bioactive compounds (e.g., calcium fortified orange juice,
          omega-3-enriched eggs), Probiotic and prebiotic foods (e.g.,
          yogurt with live cultures, fiber-enriched products).
    ii.   Nutraceuticals and Dietary Supplements - Products
          containing bioactive compounds.
   iii.   Novel Foods - Foods derived from new sources or
          technologies, such as: Lab-grown or cultured meat, plant-
          based meat alternatives, foods with genetically modified
          organisms (GMOs).
   iv.    Fortified and Functional Beverages Drinks with added
          health-promoting ingredients, such as: Energy drinks with
          herbal extracts, functional waters containing vitamins,
          minerals, or adaptogens.
    v.    Allergen-Free or Hypoallergenic Foods- Foods marketed for
          individuals with food allergies or intolerances.
   vi.    Weight Management Products - Meal replacements, shakes,
          or bars claiming weight loss or appetite suppression
          benefits.
   vii.   Foods for Special Populations - Infant formula or baby foods,
          products for the elderly.
  viii.   Bioengineered or Fermented Foods - Foods with novel
          fermentation processes or bioengineered components.
   ix.    Claimed Anti-Disease Foods- Products claiming to reduce
          the risk of illnesses.

d. Medical Devices
          i.    Implantable       Devices      (Pacemakers,      Implantable
                defibrillators, Artificial heart valves, Cochlear implants,
                Orthopedic implants).
         ii.    Diagnostic Devices (MRI and CT scanners, In-vitro
                diagnostic tests for cancer and infectious diseases), Genetic
                testing platforms.
         iii.   Life-supporting or life-sustaining devices (Ventilators,
                Dialysis machines, Heart-lung machines).
         iv.    Novel or Innovative Devices, Robotic Surgical Systems,
                Wearable glucose monitors with insulin delivery (closed-loop
                systems), AI-powered diagnostic tools.
          v.    Devices with Drug Combinations (Drug-eluting stents,
                Contraceptive implants, Infusion pumps with medication
                reservoirs).
         vi.    Devices with a New Mechanism or Material (Devices made
                from new Biomaterials (e.g., biodegradable implants),
                Devices using nanotechnology for treatment or diagnosis)

     e. Veterinary Investigational Products

           i.   New Veterinary Drugs.
          ii.   Vaccines.
        iii.    Biological Products.
         iv.    Medicated Feed Additives.
          v.    Veterinary Medical Devices.
         vi.    Novel Delivery Systems or Formulations.
        vii.    Generic Drugs

      Oversight of veterinary investigational products shall be undertaken in
coordination with the Department of Agriculture and the Bureau of Animal
Industry, consistent with applicable laws.

     f. Cosmetics and Personal Care Products.

          i.    Products requiring human safety testing,             dermal
                irritation/efficacy trials, and claims validation.

     Section 9. Public-Private Partnerships (PPP) in Clinical Trials

       The PCTRO shall promote public-private partnerships through the
following programs:

     a. Foreign investment will be promoted through streamlined
        regulatory pathways subject to existing tax and investment
        incentive laws for foreign-funded clinical trials, while ensuring
        rigorous regulatory oversight to safeguard ethical and scientific
         standards.
      b. Foreign investors, with assistance from the Philippine Board of
         Investments (BOI) can primarily facilitate investment promotions
         and policy guidance, rather than direct financial assistance.
      c. Strengthening of the Clinical Trial Registry to provide streamlined
         access to qualified patient populations, thereby minimizing
         recruitment time for Clinical Trial Sponsors and encourage the
         conduct of Pre-Clinical and Phase I Studies in the Philippines.

      Section 10. National Strategy for MRCT Participation

      The PCTRO shall adopt harmonized standards to facilitate Philippine
inclusion in global MRCTs. Sponsors with clinical trial sites in the Philippines
may receive the following incentives, subject to investment laws:

      a. Streamlined importation of investigational products.
      b. Priority review pathways.
      c. Incentives for sponsors developing innovative health products,
         subject to existing investment and fiscal laws.

      Section 11. Equitable Access and Public Health Benefits

       Access of Indigent Filipinos shall be maintained by the Sponsor through
the following:

      a. No cost participation;
      b. Coverage of diagnostics and monitoring;
      c. Post-trial access for participants who benefit from the
         investigational health product through a Post-Trial Access Plan
         subject to regulatory approval, safety evaluation, feasibility, and
         applicable intellectual property laws;
      d. Coordination with public hospitals

      Section 12. Licensing of Sponsors and CROs

       All Sponsors and Contract Research Organizations (CROs) shall
 obtain a License to Operate (LTO) from the FDA prior to engaging in any
 trial-related activities. Foreign sponsors shall appoint a duly authorized
 local representative accountable for compliance with this Act.

      Sec. 13. Clinical Trial Authorization.

      Sponsors shall secure a Clinical Trial Authorization (CTA) through the
 PCTRO acting under delegated authority of the FDA prior to the conduct
 of any clinical trial activities.
      An application for CTA shall include evidence of:

      a. Trial protocol and investigator brochure;
      b. Approval from a Level III- accredited Ethics Review Committee;
      c. Scientific and safety justification for the trial;
      d. Risk-benefit assessment;
      e. Investigator qualifications and site capability;
      f. Proof of insurance or compensation mechanism for trial-related
         injury;
      g. Clinical Performance Study Approval and ISO 13485 Certification
         for Medical Device Clinical Trials;
      h. Post-Trial Access Plan;
      i. Data management and safety monitoring plan.

     Ongoing clinical trials shall be required to submit semi-annual Progress
Reports summarizing the status of the clinical trial.

     Risk-based review shall be applied proportionate to product type and study
phase. The PCTRO shall establish predictable timelines:

      a. Standard review: 60-90 working days:
      b. Expedited review (public health priority): 30-45 working days.

      Section 14. Ethics Review Governance and Participant Protection.

    All clinical trials shall be reviewed and approved by an Ethics Review
Committee (ERC). The PCTRO shall accredit ERCs and conduct periodic audits.

       Informed consent shall be obtained from all participants prior to trial
enrollment following ICH-GCP E6 (R3). Informed consent must be voluntary,
documented, understandable, and culturally appropriate. Where the individual
is a member of a vulnerable population, additional safeguards shall be
implemented to ensure that consent is truly informed and free from coercion or
undue influence.

      Confidentiality of participant data shall be strictly observed in accordance
with the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

      Section 15. Clinical Trial Registry and Data Privacy.

      A Philippine Clinical Trial Registry (PCTR) shall be established and
 maintained under the PCTRO. The PCTR shall be built upon the existing
 Philippine Health Research Registry (PHRR). Pertinent information
 regarding ongoing clinical trials shall be encoded and regularly updated in
 the database.
      All approved clinical trials shall be registered prior to participant
enrollment, and sponsors shall ensure public disclosure of study results, subject
to confidentiality and intellectual property considerations.

      Sponsors, investigators, and the PCTRO shall comply with Republic Act
No. 10173 and applicable data privacy regulations.

     Section 16. Regulatory Inspection by the FDA as the National
 Regulatory Authority (NRA).

      a. The NRA shall establish and implement an inspection program to
         ensure compliance of ongoing clinical trials in the Philippines with
         ICH-GCP, WHO guidelines, ASEAN guidelines, ethical standards, and
         applicable regulations in the country.

      b. Inspections will be carried out by qualified inspectors possessing valid
         credentials and relevant knowledge, experience, and training in
         implementing Good Clinical Practice.

      c. The NRA exercises the right to conduct inspections, enter any area of
         the premises where activities related to clinical trials and clinical
         research are carried out, interview relevant personnel, obtain
         information, access clinical trial data, and carry out other activities as
         necessary to verify compliance with existing standards governing
         clinical research in the Philippines;

      d. Inspections may be conducted as announced or unannounced, as
         necessary.

      e. Inspections may be carried out at Ethics Review Committees (ERCs),
         Contract Research Organizations (CROs), Sponsors, and Clinical Trial
         Sites to verify information and compliance with GCP.

      f.   All findings and results of inspections shall be documented and made
           accessible to related parties. Inspections with noted deficiencies and
           violations may require the submission of a Corrective Action and
           Preventive Action (CAPA) Plan subject to the evaluation and approval
           of the involved inspectors;

      g. Inspections where major GCP violations resulting in existing or
         potential significant harm to the clinical trial participants are noted
         may result in the imposition of a Violation subject to appropriate legal
         action:

      Section 17. Funding and Appropriations. - An initial budget shall be
allocated from the General Appropriations Act (GAA) for the establishment of the
PCTRO. Additionally, the PCTRO may receive additional funding from Public-
Private Partnerships (PPPs) with private hospitals, universities, and
Pharmaceutical Firms, and international research grants and foreign investment
incentives.

       No regulated entity shall directly fund regulatory approval or inspection
activities of the PCTRO.

      Section 18. National Clinical Research and Innovation Fund.

       A National Clinical Research and Innovation Fund shall be established to
support local investigator-initiated trials, ethics training, and capacity building
for regulatory and ethics oversight. The Fund shall support:

      a.    Trials addressing priority diseases;
      b.    MRCT participation;
      c.    Investigator-initiated trials;
      d.    Regulatory and ethics capacity building.

     Funding sources include appropriations, grants, and lawful contributions
under transparency safeguards.

      Section 19. Enforcement and Penalties

      a.    Administrative Penalties - Any person or entity found in
            violation of this Act may be subject to administrative fines,
            suspension, or revocation of authorization or license.

      b.    Criminal Penalties - Any person who willfully falsifies trial data,
            conducts unapproved trials, or endangers participant safety
            shall be liable to imprisonment of not less than two (2) years
            but not more than ten (10) years, or a fine of not less than Five
            Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000) but not more than Five
            Million Pesos (₱5,000,000), or both.

      c.    The imposition of penalties under this Act shall be without
            prejudice to other administrative, civil or criminal liabilities
            under existing laws.

      Sec. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) - Within
 ninety (90) days from effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with
 the DOST, and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
 implementing Rules and regulations (IRR).
      Section 21. Separability Clause.

      If any provision, section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause or
phrase of this Act is declared unconstitutional, invalid, or unenforceable by a
court of competent jurisdiction, such declaration shall not affect the validity or
enforceability of the remaining provisions of this Act.

      The remaining provisions shall continue to be in full force and effect,
provided that the invalidated portion does not render the implementation of the
remaining provisions impossible or contrary to legislative intent.

      In the event that any specific application of a provision is declared invalid,
such invalidity shall not affect other applications of the same provision which
can be given effect without the invalid portion. It is the intent of Congress that
this Act would have been enacted even if any one or more provisions were
declared invalid.

      Section 22. Repealing Clause.

       All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative issuances, rules, and
regulations, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with any provision of this
Act are hereby repealed amended, or modified accordingly. Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to:

      a.   Repeal, amend, or diminish the authority of the Food and Drug
           Administration under Republic Act No. 9711 (FDA Act of 2009) with
           respect to product registration, market authorization, post-marketing
           surveillance, and regulation of commercially distributed health
           products;

      b.   Repeal or modify the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No.
           10173)

      c.   Repeal or modify the Philippine National Health Research System Act
           (Republic Act No. 10532), except insofar as necessary to harmonize
           institutional coordination with the Philippine Clinical Trial Regulatory
           Office;

      d.   Repeal or override existing procurement, public-private partnership,
           or government auditing laws;

      e.   Impair vested rights lawfully acquired under existing laws prior to the
           effectivity of this Act.
       Administrative orders, circulars, and guidelines inconsistent with this Act
shall be reviewed and harmonized within one (1) year from its effectivity.
       Section 23. Effectivity - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the official gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
