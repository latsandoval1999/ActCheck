                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Republic Act No. 9136, or the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA),
declares it the policy of the State to ensure the quality, reliability, security and affordability of
the supply of electric power. It entrusts the reliability, stability and integrity of the grid to the
system operator, and it also separates generation from transmission to keep generation
competitive. These two commitments now pull against each other. The system operator must
keep enough reserves to hold the grid together, but it may only buy them from private facilities
that already exist and have passed technical validation. When no qualified facility is available,
the operator has no means of its own to close the gap. The operator has taken the position that
the EPIRA and its franchise prevent it from building or running generating plants. That position
finds support in the cross-ownership rule in Section 45 of the EPIRA, which bars the
transmission concessionaire from holding any interest in a generation company and carries
criminal penalties.

         The consequences are felt by consumers. In 2026 the Visayas grid has been placed on
repeated yellow and red alerts, and the Energy Regulatory Commission has recorded 123 such
alerts from January to early September. Consumers pay for reserves and then for the disruption
when reserves are not there.

        This bill closes the gap in a narrow and disciplined way. It authorizes the system
operator to own and run Grid Stability Facilities. The facilities may be used only for ancillary
services and reserve, sized to the verified shortfall, kept apart from the system operator’s other
business, dispatched on the same rules as private suppliers, and paid for only after a prudence
review. Energy sold when reserves are called upon is credited back to consumers. The bill
keeps every other prohibition in Section 45 in force, so the system operator still may not hold
an interest in any other generation company or any distribution utility. This is a reserve tool
and not a substitute for new baseload capacity, which the country still needs.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
AUTHORIZING THE SYSTEM OPERATOR TO OWN AND OPERATE GRID
STABILITY FACILITIES FOR ANCILLARY SERVICES AND RESERVE
PURPOSES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 4, 9, 36, 43 AND 45 OF
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC POWER
INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001,” AND SECTION 7 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO.
9511, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title – This Act shall be known as the “Grid Reserve Security Act
of 2026.”

         SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy – It is the policy of the State to ensure the quality,
reliability, security and affordability of the supply of electric power, consistent with Section
2(b) of Republic Act No. 9136. Towards this end, the State shall ensure that the grid always
has sufficient reserves to maintain its stability, and shall equip the system operator with the
means to secure those reserves when they cannot be procured from the market.
      SECTION 3. Additional Definitions – Section 4 of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby
amended by adding the following paragraphs at the end thereof:
       “(eee) “Grid Stability Facility” refers to a generation facility, energy storage facility, or
       combination thereof, owned and operated by the System Operator under Section 9-A
       of this Act, whose capacity is dedicated solely to Ancillary Services and Reserve;
       “(fff) “Reserve” refers to the capacity and energy held available to the System Operator
       to restore the balance of supply and demand and to maintain the stability, reliability and
       security of the grid, in accordance with the Grid Code and the Ancillary Services
       Procurement Plan approved by the ERC; and
       “(ggg) “System Operator” refers to the TRANSCO, its concessionaire, or any successor
       entity, by whatever name called, that performs the system operation function of the
       nationwide transmission system.”
       SECTION 4. Grid Stability Facilities of the System Operator – Section 9 of
Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph (g), with the punctuation
of the preceding paragraphs adjusted accordingly, and a new Section 9-A is inserted after
Section 9, to read as follows:
       “(g) Own, operate and maintain Grid Stability Facilities in accordance with Section 9-
       A of this Act.
       “SEC. 9-A. Grid Stability Facilities of the System Operator. – Notwithstanding the
       provisions of Sections 6, 36 and 45 of this Act and Section 7 of Republic Act No. 9511,
       the System Operator may develop, own, operate and maintain Grid Stability Facilities,
       directly or through a wholly-owned and ring-fenced special purpose subsidiary, for the
       exclusive purpose of providing Ancillary Services and Reserve needed to maintain the
       reliability, adequacy, security, stability and integrity of the grid.
              “(a) Exclusive Use. – The capacity of a Grid Stability Facility shall be reserved
              exclusively for Ancillary Services and Reserve. It shall not be offered or sold
              under bilateral contracts, to the contestable market, or to any distribution utility
              or supplier. Energy may be injected into the grid only when dispatched in the
              course of an Ancillary Service activation or during a grid emergency declared
              under the Grid Code, and shall be settled at the lower of the prevailing wholesale
              electricity spot market price or a price cap approved by the ERC. Net revenues
              from such energy shall be credited against the Ancillary Services charges of
              end-users.
              “(b) Ring-Fencing and Non-Discrimination. – The System Operator shall
              maintain separate books, accounts and management for its Grid Stability
              Facilities. No cross-subsidy shall flow between the transmission, system
              operation and Grid Stability Facility businesses. A Grid Stability Facility shall
              be dispatched under the same Grid Code rules and merit order, and shall be
              subject to the same reserve market rules, as any private Ancillary Service
              provider, and shall receive no preferential access to market, system or
              competitor information. The ERC shall prescribe separate monitoring by the
              Market Operator and the ERC.

              “(c) Competitive Procurement. – The design, engineering, procurement and
              construction of a Grid Stability Facility shall be undertaken through transparent
              competitive bidding under rules approved by the ERC.
              “(d) Cost Recovery. – The ERC shall determine the prudent and reasonable
              costs of a Grid Stability Facility, and a reasonable return thereon, after
              verification under Section 43(f) of this Act. Costs shall be recovered through a
              separate, itemized Ancillary Services charge and shall not be included in the
              transmission rate base. Costs arising from unexcused project delays, penalties,
              or management inefficiencies shall not be recoverable.

              “(e) National Security. – Operational control of Grid Stability Facilities,
              including their control systems and software, shall be exercised only by Filipino
              citizens and shall comply with the cybersecurity standards prescribed by the
              DOE in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
              Technology.
              “(f) Reporting. – The System Operator shall submit quarterly reports on the
              construction, operation, dispatch, revenues and costs of each Grid Stability
              Facility to the ERC and the DOE, and an annual report to the Committees on
              Energy of the Senate and the House of Representatives. The ERC shall publish
              the same.”

       SECTION 5. Unbundling – The fourth paragraph of Section 36 of Republic Act No.
9136 is hereby amended by adding at the end thereof the following proviso:

       “PROVIDED, THAT THE OWNERSHIP AND OPERATION OF GRID STABILITY
       FACILITIES BY THE SYSTEM OPERATOR UNDER SECTION 9-A OF THIS ACT
       SHALL SATISFY THE REQUIREMENT OF FUNCTIONAL AND STRUCTURAL
       UNBUNDLING IF SEGREGATED IN THE MANNER PROVIDED IN SECTION 9-
       A(D).”
      SECTION 6. Cross-Ownership – The second paragraph of Section 45 of Republic
Act No. 9136 is hereby amended by adding at the end thereof the following provisos:
       “PROVIDED, THAT THE OWNERSHIP, OPERATION AND MAINTENANCE BY
       THE SYSTEM OPERATOR OF GRID STABILITY FACILITIES IN
       ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 9-A OF THIS ACT, WHETHER DIRECTLY OR
       THROUGH A WHOLLY-OWNED SPECIAL PURPOSE SUBSIDIARY, SHALL
       NOT BE A VIOLATION OF THIS SECTION, NOR SHALL THE SYSTEM
       OPERATOR OR SUCH SUBSIDIARY BE CONSIDERED A GENERATION
       COMPANY OR AN AFFILIATE OF A GENERATION COMPANY FOR
       PURPOSES OF THIS SECTION SOLELY BY REASON THEREOF: PROVIDED,
       FURTHER, THAT EXCEPT AS HEREIN PROVIDED, THE PROHIBITIONS IN
       THIS SECTION SHALL CONTINUE TO APPLY, AND THE SYSTEM OPERATOR
       AND ITS STOCKHOLDERS, DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS SHALL NOT HOLD
       ANY INTEREST IN ANY OTHER GENERATION COMPANY OR
       DISTRIBUTION UTILITY.”
       SECTION 7. Functions of the ERC – Section 43 of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby
amended by adding a new paragraph at the end of the enumeration of functions, to read as
follows:

       “Monitor their operation and dispatch; investigate and penalize any anti-competitive
       behavior, cross-subsidization or misuse of such facilities; and require the submission
       of such reports and data as may be necessary for these purposes.”
        SECTION 8. Franchise of the Grantee – The last sentence of the seventh paragraph
of Section 7 of Republic Act No. 9511 is hereby amended to read as follows:
       “THE GRANTEE MAY PROVIDE ANCILLARY SERVICES, INCLUDING
       THROUGH GRID STABILITY FACILITIES OWNED AND OPERATED IN
       ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 9-A OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, AS
       AMENDED, OR ENGAGE IN ANY RELATED BUSINESS WHICH MAXIMIZES
       UTILIZATION OF ITS ASSETS: PROVIDED, THAT THE OWNERSHIP OF GRID
       STABILITY FACILITIES SHALL NOT, BY ITSELF, MAKE THE GRANTEE OR
       ITS SPECIAL PURPOSE SUBSIDIARY A POWER INDUSTRY PLAYER FOR
       PURPOSES OF THIS SECTION.”
        SECTION 9. Penalties – Any violation of Section 9-A of Republic Act No. 9136, as
inserted by this Act, including the use of a Grid Stability Facility for sales or purposes not
authorized therein, shall be treated as a prohibited act under Section 45 and shall be subject to
the fines, penalties and sanctions provided under Section 46 of Republic Act No. 9136, without
prejudice to such other remedies as the ERC may impose.

        SECTION 10. Transitory Provision – Ancillary Services Procurement Agreements
validly entered into or provisionally approved by the ERC before the effectivity of this Act
shall remain valid and shall not be impaired. The procurement of Ancillary Services from
private providers shall remain the primary means of securing Reserve.

        SECTION 11. Review – Five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act, and every five
(5) years thereafter, the ERC and the DOE shall jointly submit to the Committees on Energy
of the Senate and the House of Representatives a report on the use, cost, and effect on
competition and consumers of Grid Stability Facilities, together with recommendations.

        SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOE and the ERC, in consultation with the System Operator, the
Market Operator and other electric power industry participants, shall issue the rules and
regulations necessary to implement this Act.
       SECTION 13. Separability Clause – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

        SECTION 14. Repealing Clause – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.
       SECTION 15. Effectivity – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
