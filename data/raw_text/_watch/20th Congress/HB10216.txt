                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

     Education is a shared responsibility of the government, the private sector, civil
society, and the broader community. While the Constitution primarily mandates the
State to establish and maintain a complete, adequate, and integrated system of
education, it likewise recognizes the indispensable role of private institutions and
encourages their participation in complementing government efforts to improve
educational access, quality, and relevance. Strong partnerships between public
educational institutions and private stakeholders remain essential in preparing Filipino
learners to meet the demands of an increasingly knowledge-driven and globally
competitive economy.

      Republic Act No. 8525, otherwise known as the Adopt-a-School Act of 1998, has
encouraged private sector support for public schools through donations and
educational assistance. However, after more than two decades of implementation, the
scope of the law has become inadequate to address the evolving needs of the
Philippine education sector. Existing incentives no longer sufficiently encourage
sustained private sector participation, while current administrative processes, limited
coverage, and outdated mechanisms constrain the full potential of public-private
collaboration across all levels of education.

      This measure seeks to modernize and strengthen the Adopt-a-School Program
by expanding its coverage to include early childhood education, technical-vocational
education and training, alternative learning systems, and public higher education
institutions. It likewise broadens the forms of assistance that may be provided,
including teacher development, scholarships, industry certifications, digital learning
resources, laboratory facilities, health and nutrition interventions, assistive
technologies for learners with disabilities, and other educational support necessary to
improve learning outcomes and institutional capacity. At the same time, the bill
establishes an Adopt-a-School One-Stop Shop and an integrated online portal to
streamline applications, coordination, and the processing of incentives for participating
private individuals and entities.



                                           1
      The proposed measure further enhances incentives by increasing allowable tax
deductions for educational assistance, granting additional incentives for enterprises
that employ graduates of partner public senior high schools, and exempting qualified
donations from customs duties, value-added tax, excise tax, and donor's tax, subject
to appropriate safeguards. It also strengthens institutional governance through an
expanded Adopt-a-School Coordinating Council and requires education agencies to
regularly identify priority needs, ensuring that private sector contributions are directed
toward communities and institutions where they will produce the greatest public benefit.

     By fostering stronger collaboration between educational institutions and industry,
the measure promotes workforce readiness, expands employment opportunities for
graduates, improves educational infrastructure, enhances teacher competency, and
encourages innovation throughout the public education system. More importantly, it
enables the private sector to become a more active partner in nation-building by
investing directly in human capital development, thereby contributing to inclusive
economic growth and long-term national competitiveness.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
