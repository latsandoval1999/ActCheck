                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Unmanned systems, commonly known as drones, have become
increasingly accessible and are now widely used for recreational, commercial,
agricultural, scientific, media, disaster response, and government purposes.
Their continued development has contributed to innovation, improved
efficiency, and economic development across various sectors.

      The proliferation of unmanned systems, however, has also raised
concerns relating to public safety, national security, privacy, aviation safety,
and the protection of critical infrastructure. Unauthorized operations near
airports, military installations, government facilities, densely populated
areas, and public events demonstrate the need for a clear and comprehensive
legal framework governing the registration, possession, operation, and
accountability of unmanned systems.

      At present, regulations issued by the Civil Aviation Authority of the
Philippines (CAAP) principally govern unmanned aircraft operations. There
remains a need for legislation establishing a broader and coordinated
framework covering unmanned systems operating in the air, on land, and in
maritime environments, while recognizing the respective mandates of
concerned government agencies.

      This proposed measure seeks to establish a comprehensive regulatory
framework governing the possession and operation of unmanned systems. It
provides for appropriate registration and licensing requirements, operational
standards, prohibited acts, privacy safeguards, enforcement mechanisms,
and penalties, while recognizing legitimate uses of unmanned systems for
commercial, educational, research, agricultural, humanitarian, disaster
response, and other lawful purposes.

       The measure likewise establishes an inter-agency mechanism to
promote coordination among concerned government agencies and to ensure
that regulation keeps pace with technological developments. It recognizes the
need to balance public safety and national security with innovation, research,
economic activity, and the responsible development of the unmanned systems
industry.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      REGULATING THE POSSESSION AND OPERATION OF
      UNMANNED    SYSTEMS,   PROVIDING   PENALTIES FOR
      VIOLATIONS THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

          Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
   Philippines in Congress assembled:

                                  ARTICLE I
                              GENERAL PROVISIONS

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Unmanned
Systems Regulation and Safety Act of 2026."

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the
State to promote and govern the use of technology for national development
while safeguarding the life, liberty, property, privacy, and security of its
citizens.

      The State recognizes the immense potential of drones, formally known
as Unmanned Systems (US), for commercial, recreational, scientific,
agricultural, humanitarian, disaster response, and public service
applications. However, the State likewise recognizes the risks they may pose
to public safety, national security, aviation safety, privacy, and critical
infrastructure.

      Therefore, the State shall establish a comprehensive regulatory
framework to ensure that the importation, manufacture, sale, ownership,
possession, and operation of unmanned systems are conducted responsibly
and safely. This framework shall balance technological innovation with
public welfare and security, promote coordination among government
agencies, and prescribe clear standards and penalties for unlawful use.
       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the
following terms shall mean:

        a. Agricultural Operation - refers to the use of an Unmanned
           System for agricultural purposes, including but not limited
           to crop dusting, seeding, pest control, crop health
           monitoring, and livestock management;

        b. CAAP Regulations - refers to the applicable rules and
           regulations of the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines,
           including relevant Civil Aviation Regulations and issuances
           governing unmanned aircraft operations;

        c. Commercial Operation - refers to any authorized use of an
           unmanned system for work, business, or in connection with
           any form of compensation or hire;

        d. Controlled Airspace - refers to airspace of defined
           dimensions within which air traffic control service is
           provided;

        e. Critical Infrastructure - refers to assets, systems, and
           networks so vital to the Republic of the Philippines that their
           incapacitation, disruption, or destruction would have a
           debilitating impact on national security, economic stability,
           public health, or safety;

        f. Unmanned System Operator or Remote Pilot - refers to any
           natural or juridical person who commands, controls,
           operates, or otherwise exercises operational control over an
           Unmanned System;

        g. Drone Owner - refers to the natural or juridical person in
           whose name the drone is registered;

        h. Law Enforcement Agency (LEA) - refers to the Philippine
           National Police (PNP), National Bureau of Investigation (NBI),
           Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA), and other
           government agencies authorized by law to perform law
           enforcement functions;

        i. Law Enforcement Operation - refers to the activities
           undertaken by government agents to enforce the law,
           maintain public order, prevent and detect crime, and
           apprehend offenders;
j. Modification - refers to any alteration to a drone's hardware
   or software that materially changes its flight capabilities,
   payload capacity, communication systems, or operational
   range from its original manufacturer specifications;

k. Prohibited or No-Fly Zone - refers to a designated area or
   airspace within which unmanned system operations are
   prohibited for national security, public safety, or aviation
   safety reasons;

l. Public Safety and Security Operation - refers to the
   authorized use of an Unmanned System by a government
   entity for public service purposes, such as search and
   rescue,   law    enforcement,    reconnaissance,    tactical
   operations, disaster response, and other purposes necessary
   to promote law and order, safety, and security;

m. Recreational Operation - refers to the authorized use of an
   Unmanned System for personal interest, hobby, or
   amusement purposes, with no commercial or professional
   intent;

n. Restricted Zone - refers to a designated area or airspace
   within which unmanned system operations are subject to
   specified conditions or limitations;

o. Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) - refers to an unmanned
   system designed for flight in the atmosphere;

p. Unmanned Ground-Based System (UGS) - refers to an
   unmanned system designed for operation on land surfaces;

q. Unmanned Maritime System (UMS) - refers to an unmanned
   system designed for operation on the surface of or
   underneath the water, including submersible and semi-
   submersible vehicles;

r. Unmanned System (US) - refers to any remotely,
   automatically, or autonomously controlled vehicle and its
   associated control systems, operating in the air, on land, or
   in water, without a human pilot or operator on board;

s. Visual Line of Sight (VLOS) - refers to the operator's ability
   to maintain direct unaided visual contact with the
   unmanned system during flight; and
         t. Weaponized Drone - refers to any Unmanned System
            equipped with or used to deploy any firearm, explosive,
            chemical, biological, radiological, or other hazardous
            payload intended to cause harm, injury, or death.

     SECTION 4. Categories and Classifications of Drones. - All
drones shall be classified based on their Maximum Take-Off Weight
(MTOW) and/or operational risk as follows:

      a. Based on Weight:

         1. Category 1 (Micro): Less than 250 grams. Exempt from
            registration if used exclusively for recreational purposes,
            subject to applicable CAAP regulations;

         2. Category 2 (Small): 250 grams to 7 kilograms. Requires
            registration and applicable operator licensing;

         3. Category 3 (Standard): Over 7 kilograms but not exceeding
            25 kilograms. Requires stricter certification; and

         4. Category 4 (Large): Over 25 kilograms. Subject to regulatory
            requirements appropriate to their size, weight, and
            operational risk.

      b. Based on Operational Risk:

         1. Class 1 (Low-Risk): Recreational flights in approved areas;

         2. Class 2 (Medium-Risk): Commercial operations within
            VLOS, such as aerial photography or mapping; and

         3. Class 3 (High-Risk): Specialized operations such as Beyond
            Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) flights, nighttime operations, or
            drone delivery services, which require special authorization
            or permits.


                        ARTICLE II
          GOVERNANCE, REGISTRATION, AND LICENSING

       SECTION 5. Inter-Agency Council on Unmanned Systems (IAC-
US). - To ensure a holistic and harmonized regulatory approach, an Inter-
Agency Council on Unmanned Systems is hereby created.
      It shall be chaired by the Secretary of the Department of
Transportation (DOTr) and shall be composed of the heads or duly
authorized representatives of the following agencies: Department of
National Defense (DND), Department of Interior and Local Government
(DILG), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of
Information and Communication Technology (DICT), Department of
Science and Technology (DOST), Department of Agriculture (DA),
National Security Council (NSC), Civil Aviation Authority of the
Philippines (CAAP), Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), Philippine
National Police (PNP), Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), and Philippine
Coast Guard (PCG).

      The IAC-US shall serve as the primary inter-agency coordinating
body for policy development and implementation of this Act, without
prejudice to the statutory mandates and regulatory authority of the
member agencies.

      SECTION 6. Mandatory Unmanned System Registration. - All
Unmanned Systems, except UAS weighing less than 250 grams used
exclusively for recreational purposes, shall be registered in the Philippine
Unmanned Systems Registry, subject to applicable regulations and
exemptions established pursuant to this Act.

      The IAC-US shall establish and maintain an interoperable registry
framework. The CAAP shall administer the registration of UAS, the PCG
shall administer the registration of UMS, and the appropriate
government agency designated pursuant to the implementing rules and
regulations shall administer the registration of UGS.

       Registration shall be renewable every three (3) years, unless a
different period is prescribed by the appropriate regulatory agency based
on the nature and risk of the unmanned system.

      SECTION 7. Mandatory Operator Licensing. - No person shall
operate an unmanned system subject to mandatory registration without
the appropriate and valid Remote Operator's License or equivalent
authorization required under applicable regulations.

      The IAC-US, through the appropriate regulatory agencies, shall
establish license categories based on the domain, class, and risk level of
the operation. Applicants shall satisfy the applicable knowledge,
competency, and practical skills requirements relevant to their intended
operation.

      SECTION 8. Accreditation of Importers, Manufacturers, and
Sellers. - Any entity engaged in the importation, manufacture, assembly,
sale, distribution, or lease of drones in the Philippines shall secure the
appropriate accreditation or authorization from the competent
government agency, in coordination with the CAAP and other concerned
agencies, as applicable.

      Accredited entities shall:

       a. Maintain records of sales, including the buyer's name and
          contact information and the drone's serial number, subject to
          the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws;

       b. Provide a point-of-sale safety briefing or information material to
          buyers outlining the key provisions of this Act and applicable
          operating requirements; and

       c. Submit periodic sales reports to the appropriate regulatory
          agency in accordance with the implementing rules and
          regulations.


                             ARTICLE III
                         RULES OF OPERATION

         SECTION 9. General Operating Rules. - All unmanned system
   operations shall adhere to the following unless otherwise authorized
   by the appropriate regulatory authority:

      a. Operate within prescribed operational boundaries;

      b. Maintain continuous VLOS with the system, unless specifically
         authorized for BVLOS, autonomous, or other operations;

      c. For UAS, fly at a maximum altitude of 400 feet (122 meters)
         above ground level, unless otherwise authorized under
         applicable aviation regulations;

      d. Do not operate over crowds, densely populated assemblies,
         schools, hospitals, or public gatherings in a manner that
         presents a risk to persons or property, except when specifically
         authorized or when necessary for lawful emergency, public
         safety, medical, scientific, or other legitimate operations;

      e. Do not operate a drone from a moving vehicle unless specifically
         authorized by the appropriate regulatory authority;
     f. Yield the right-of-way to all manned aircraft, vessels, and other
        vehicles, as applicable;

     g. Do not operate over critical infrastructure or designated public
        gatherings without the required authorization; and

     h. The operator must not be under the influence of alcohol or
        prohibited substances while operating an unmanned system.

     SECTION 10. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are declared
unlawful:

     a. Operating a drone without the registration, license, permit, or
        authorization required under this Act or applicable regulations;

     b. Importing, manufacturing, assembling, leasing, selling, or
        distributing drones or covered components without the
        required accreditation or authorization;

     c. Selling or leasing a drone to a person without the required
        license or authorization, when such license or authorization is
        legally required;

     d. Weaponizing a drone or equipping it with firearms, explosives,
        biological agents, or other dangerous or hazardous materials for
        unlawful purposes;

     e. Using a drone for unlawful surveillance, harassment,
        eavesdropping, or other conduct that unlawfully violates the
        privacy or other rights of any person, in violation of this Act,
        Republic Act No. 10173, or other applicable laws;

     f. Using a drone to aid or commit any crime, including but not
        limited to smuggling, illegal drug trafficking, or reconnaissance
        for criminal or terrorist activities;

     g. Intentionally interfering with lawful law enforcement,
        emergency response, or disaster relief operations;

     h. Hacking, spoofing, jamming, or otherwise illegally interfering
        with the command, control, or navigation signal of a registered
        drone;

     i. Operating a drone while under the influence of alcohol or
        prohibited drugs; and
      j. Using a drone in furtherance of an unlawful activity or
         committing similar acts prohibited under this Act or other
         applicable laws.

      SECTION 11. Prohibited and Restricted Zones. - Unmanned
system operations shall be prohibited or restricted in areas and airspace
designated by the appropriate regulatory authority, consistent with
applicable aviation, national security, and public safety regulations.

      Unless otherwise authorized, it shall be strictly prohibited to
operate an unmanned system:

      a. Within restricted or prohibited airspace established around
         airports or airfields pursuant to applicable CAAP regulations;

      b. Over critical infrastructure and designated national security
         areas, including military bases, Malacañang Palace, and major
         government installations, when such operations are prohibited
         or restricted by competent authority; and

      c. Over areas where a Temporary Flight Restriction (TFR) is in
         effect, as declared by the CAAP or other competent authority for
         emergencies, major events, or public safety and security
         operations.

      SECTION 12. Protection of Privacy. - The use of a drone for
surveillance, eavesdropping, or capturing, processing, or transmitting
images, videos, audio, or other personal data in circumstances that
unlawfully intrude upon a person's reasonable expectation of privacy is
prohibited.

      Nothing in this section shall be construed to prohibit lawful
photography, documentation, journalism, scientific research, public
safety operations, disaster response, or other legitimate activities,
subject to Republic Act No. 10173, the "Data Privacy Act of 2012," and
other applicable laws.

     Violations of this section shall be without prejudice to liability
under Republic Act No. 10173 and other applicable laws.

      SECTION 13. Regulation on Drone Modification. - Any
modification to a drone that materially alters its flight performance,
weight, payload capacity, or communication systems shall be declared to
and, when required by applicable regulations, approved by the
appropriate regulatory authority.
      The weaponization of a drone by attaching firearms, explosives,
biological agents, hazardous materials, or any weapon intended for
unlawful harm is prohibited.


                      ARTICLE IV
  ROLE OF GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND SPECIAL OPERATIONS

      SECTION 14. Role of Local Government Units. - Consistent with
the Local Government Code and other applicable laws, LGUs may enact
ordinances governing the operation of drones within areas under their
jurisdiction, such as public parks, plazas, and heritage sites.

       Such ordinances shall be supplementary to this Act and its
implementing rules and regulations and shall not contradict national
policies, aviation regulations, or standards established by competent
national government agencies.

      SECTION 15. Use for National Security and Law Enforcement.
- Duly constituted government agencies, including the Armed Forces of
the Philippines (AFP) and the Philippine National Police (PNP), may
operate unmanned systems for national security, intelligence, law
enforcement, border control, counter-terrorism, and other lawful
government operations.

      The use of such systems, including counter-unmanned system
technology, shall be governed by applicable laws, rules of engagement,
constitutional safeguards, and protocols established in the implementing
rules and regulations.

      SECTION 16. Use for Disaster and Emergency Response. -
Government agencies such as the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Council (NDRRMC), Philippine Institute of Volcanology and
Seismology (PHIVOLCS), BFP, National Mapping and Resource
Information Authority (NAMRIA), and PCG are authorized to deploy and
operate unmanned systems as a component of disaster risk assessment,
monitoring, search and rescue, emergency response, and post-disaster
recovery operations.

      During a declared state of calamity or emergency, appropriate
exemptions from ordinary operating requirements may be granted by the
competent regulatory authority when necessary for life-saving or
emergency operations, provided that reasonable safety measures are
observed.
       SECTION 17. Use for Research and Development. - The State
shall encourage the use of unmanned systems for scientific research and
technological development.

      The Department of Science and Technology (DOST), in
coordination with the IAC-US and appropriate regulatory agencies, shall

      a. Establish designated special-use areas for safe testing and
         experimentation;

      b. Develop a streamlined process for issuing experimental or
         research permits to accredited academic and scientific
         institutions; and

      c. Promote partnerships between the government, academe, and
         industry to advance local unmanned systems technology.

      SECTION 18. Use of Drones for Agricultural Purposes. - The use
of drones for agricultural activities is hereby recognized as a vital tool for
enhancing food security and modernizing the agricultural sector. Such
use shall be governed by the following:

      a. Authorization and Scope. - The State shall support and regulate
         the use of agricultural drones for purposes including, but not
         limited to:

         1. Precision dispensing in applying fertilizers, pesticides,
            herbicides, seeds, or water;

         2. Crop management in conducting crop health monitoring,
            soil analysis, irrigation assessment, and yield estimation
            through multispectral or thermal imaging; and

         3. Livestock monitoring to oversee livestock location, health,
            and grazing patterns.

      b. Special Permit and Operator Certification. - The operation of a
         drone for dispensing any chemical, biological, or agricultural
         substance shall require the appropriate permit from the CAAP
         and other applicable authorizations.

         Operators shall obtain any specialized agricultural drone
         certification required under applicable regulations. Such
         certification shall include appropriate training in the safe
         handling and application of agrochemicals, spray drift
         mitigation, and operation of drone-mounted dispersal systems,
          conducted by training organizations         accredited   by   the
          appropriate government agencies.

     c. Operational Safety and Coordination. - All agricultural drone
        operations involving chemical dispensing shall adhere to safety
        protocols established by the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority
        (FPA), DA, CAAP, and other concerned agencies, which shall
        include:

          1. Buffer Zones: Maintaining appropriate buffer zones from
             residential areas, schools, bodies of water, and other
             sensitive areas to prevent contamination;

          2. Public Notification: Providing prior notification to the
             concerned local government unit or barangay before
             conducting large-scale spraying operations, when required
             by applicable regulations; and

          3. Extended Visual Line of Sight (EVLOS): To facilitate
             operations over large plantations, operations beyond the
             direct sight of the remote pilot may be allowed, provided that
             trained visual observers are utilized to keep the drone in
             sight and maintain communication with the pilot, subject to
             CAAP approval and applicable safety requirements.

      SECTION 19. Enforcement Authority. - The PNP and other law
enforcement agencies authorized by law shall enforce the provisions of
this Act.

       They shall have the authority, subject to the Constitution and
existing laws, to:

     a.    Demand the presentation of the drone's registration,
           operator's license, permit, or other required authorization
           during a lawful inspection;

     b.    Order the immediate landing or grounding of any drone
           reasonably believed to be operating in violation of this Act,
           when necessary to protect public safety or security;

     c.    Seize, confiscate, and impound any drone operated in
           violation of this Act or used in the commission of a crime,
           subject to applicable laws, rules of procedure, and evidentiary
           requirements; and
      d.   Employ authorized counter-drone technology when a drone
           poses an imminent threat to public safety, national security,
           or the security of a critical or high-value installation or event,
           subject to applicable laws, rules of engagement, and standard
           operating procedures.

      SECTION 20. Counter-Drone Measures. – The PNP, AFP, and
other authorized national security agencies may use reasonable and
necessary measures, including approved counter-drone technologies, to
detect, identify, disable, or neutralize an unmanned system that poses
an imminent threat to public safety, critical infrastructure, or national
security.

      The use of such measures shall be governed by applicable laws
and protocols established in the implementing rules and regulations,
including safeguards to minimize risks to persons, property, and other
lawful unmanned system operations.

                              ARTICLE V
                           PENAL PROVISIONS

      SECTION 21. Fines and Penalties. – Any person who violates the
provisions of this Act shall be subject to the applicable administrative or
criminal penalties provided herein, without prejudice to liability under
other applicable laws.

      a. Any person operating an unregistered drone required to be
         registered, or failing to mark the drone with its registration
         number, shall be fined from Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000) to
         One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000), without prejudice to
         the suspension or revocation of the applicable license or
         authorization;

      b. Any person operating a drone without a valid Remote Pilot
         License or required authorization shall be fined from One
         Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000) to Two Hundred
         Thousand Pesos (₱200,000) and may be disqualified from
         applying for a license for a period not exceeding one (1) year,
         subject to applicable administrative procedures;

      c. Any person violating the General Operating Rules under Section
         9 shall be fined Twenty Thousand Pesos (₱20,000) to Fifty
         Thousand Pesos (₱50,000) per violation;

      d. Any person or entity that fails to comply with the accreditation
         requirements for importers, manufacturers, and sellers shall be
   fined from Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱300,000) to One
   Million Pesos (₱1,000,000), without prejudice to the suspension
   or revocation of the entity's applicable permit or accreditation;

e. Any person who willfully operates a drone in a Prohibited Zone
   or in a manner that violates the conditions of a Restricted Zone
   shall be punished by prisión correccional in its medium and
   maximum periods and a fine of Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
   (₱200,000) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000),
   without prejudice to liability under other applicable laws;

f. Any person who uses a drone to commit unlawful surveillance,
   recording, or other privacy-intrusive acts in violation of Section
   10 shall be punished by prisión correccional in its minimum
   and medium periods and a fine of One Hundred Thousand
   Pesos (₱100,000) to Three Hundred Thousand Pesos
   (₱300,000), without prejudice to separate liability under
   Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, Republic
   Act No. 10175, the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012, and
   other applicable laws;

g. The act of attaching any firearm, explosive, chemical, biological,
   radiological, or other deadly weapon or hazardous material to a
   drone for an unlawful purpose shall be punished by reclusión
   temporal in its maximum period to reclusión perpetua, and a
   fine of One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000) to Two Million Pesos
   (₱2,000,000), without prejudice to liability under other
   applicable laws;

h. Any person who intentionally uses a drone to interfere with the
   lawful operations of law enforcement officers, firefighters,
   emergency responders, or disaster response personnel shall be
   punished by prisión correccional in its maximum period and a
   fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000);

i. Any person who uses a drone to unlawfully gather, collect,
   record, or transmit information, images, or electronic data from
   a military installation, critical infrastructure, or government
   facility for purposes of espionage or to aid a foreign power or
   entity hostile to the Republic of the Philippines shall be
   punished by reclusión perpetua and a fine of Five Million Pesos
   (₱5,000,000) to Ten Million Pesos (₱10,000,000), without
   prejudice to liability under applicable national security laws;

j. If the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalty of
   perpetual absolute disqualification from public office shall be
         imposed when authorized by law and applicable to the offense,
         in addition to the imprisonment and fine; and

      k. If the offender is a foreign national, the offender shall be
         prosecuted and, after service of sentence and compliance with
         applicable legal proceedings, may be subjected to deportation
         in accordance with Philippine immigration laws.

      SECTION 22. Use of an Unmanned System as a Special
Aggravating Circumstance. - In any crime punishable under the
Revised Penal Code or any special penal law, the use of an Unmanned
System in the planning, commission, facilitation, or concealment of such
crime may, when provided by law and established in accordance with
applicable rules, be considered an aggravating circumstance.

      When applicable, the penalty shall be imposed in accordance with
the provisions of the Revised Penal Code or the special penal law
governing the offense.

                         ARTICLE VI
             IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS

      SECTION 23. Authority to Promulgate Implementing Rules
and Regulations (IRR). - The DOTr, through the CAAP, as the lead
implementing agency for UAS-related matters, shall formulate, in
consultation with the IAC-US and relevant stakeholders, the rules and
regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

      The IRR shall be prepared in coordination with the following
government agencies:

      a. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

      b. Department of National Defense (DND);

      c. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);

      d. Department of Information and Communications Technology
         (DICT);

      e. Department of Agriculture (DA);

      f. Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR);

      g. National Privacy Commission (NPC);
      h. Philippine National Police (PNP);

      i. Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP); and

      j. Other agencies as may be deemed necessary.

      SECTION 24. Scope and Content of the IRR. - The IRR shall be
promulgated within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act and
shall provide detailed procedures and standards for the following
matters:

      a. Registration and Marking: The procedure for drone registration,
         renewal, transfer of ownership, de-registration, fees, and the
         specific requirements for displaying registration markings;

      b. Licensing and Training: The curriculum, standards, and
         procedures for the aeronautical knowledge examination,
         practical skills assessment, and issuance of Remote Pilot
         Licenses for various categories and classes of operations,
         including standards for accredited drone pilot training
         organizations;

      c. Airspace and Flight Rules: The precise definition and
         publication of Prohibited and Restricted Zones and the process
         for establishing Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFRs) for
         emergencies or special events;

      d. Special Operations Permits: The application process and safety
         requirements for Class 3 (High-Risk) operations, including
         BVLOS, nighttime operations, and drone delivery services;

      e. Law Enforcement Protocols: The rules of engagement and
         standard operating procedures for the PNP and other law
         enforcement agencies in the inspection, seizure, and disposition
         of drones, as well as the authorized use of counter-drone
         technologies;

      f. Governmental Use Protocols: The specific guidelines for drone
         use by the BFP, PNP, AFP, and other government agencies,
         including inter-agency coordination during joint operations;

      g. Privacy and Data Protection: The specific guidelines to ensure
         compliance with the Data Privacy Act, particularly for drones
         equipped with advanced surveillance and data-capturing
         technologies;
      h. Accreditation and Sales Monitoring: The detailed requirements
         and procedures for the accreditation of drone importers,
         manufacturers, and sellers and the mechanism for reporting
         sales data; and

      i. Fines and Penalties: A detailed schedule of administrative fines
         and a clear procedure for the adjudication of administrative
         offenses, consistent with due process and the authority of the
         appropriate regulatory agency.

      SECTION 25. Transitory Provision. - Within six (6) months from
the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, owners and
operators of unmanned systems that are required to be registered or
licensed under this Act shall be given a period within which to comply
with the registration, licensing, and permitting requirements without
incurring penalties solely for failure to comply during such transition
period, provided that the unmanned system is not used in the
commission of an offense or in a manner that poses an imminent threat
to public safety, national security, or aviation safety.

       SECTION 26. Separability Clause. - If for any reason, any
provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, such parts
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 27. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

     SECTION 28. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in any two (2)
