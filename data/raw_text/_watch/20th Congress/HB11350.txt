                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of the Philippines’ most important
natural ecosystems and a vital environmental and disaster-resilience asset. Stretching
across eastern Luzon, it serves as a natural barrier that helps lessen the impacts of
typhoons, heavy rainfall, flooding, landslides, and other natural calamities.

       In line with this, this measure seeks to strengthen the protection, conservation,
restoration, and sustainable management of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range by
establishing a dedicated Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority and providing a
comprehensive framework for its long-term management.

        Among its salient features are: (1) the creation of a dedicated authority to
coordinate conservation, enforcement, restoration, climate adaptation, and disaster-
risk reduction efforts; (2) the formulation of a 25-year Sierra Madre Master Plan
integrating biodiversity conservation, watershed management, climate resilience,
disaster risk reduction, sustainable livelihoods, land-use planning, and the rights and
traditional knowledge of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples; and
(3) the establishment of landscape zoning, ecological corridors, restoration measures,
and stronger enforcement against activities that degrade the mountain range.

       The measure is firmly anchored in Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Philippine
Constitution which declares the State’s duty to protect and advance the people’s right
to a balanced and healthful ecology. It also advances the State’s responsibility to
protect the nation’s natural resources for present and future generations. By treating
the Sierra Madre as a critical natural foundation for disaster risk reduction, climate
resilience, and public safety, the bill gives concrete effect to the constitutional mandate
of environmental protection.

       The protection of the Sierra Madre is therefore not merely an environmental
concern. It is a matter of public safety, national resilience, food and water security,
and national survival. The mountain range functions as one of Luzon’s principal natural
defenses against typhoons and extreme weather, while healthy forests and
watersheds help reduce flooding and landslide risks. The bill accordingly recognizes
the Sierra Madre as a critical natural foundation for national security, disaster risk
reduction, and climate resilience, and directs government agencies to integrate
ecosystem-based approaches into disaster and national security planning.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 4   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Sierra Madre Mountain
 5   Range Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development Act.”
 6
 7   Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. Consistent with Article II, Section 16 of the 1987
 8   Constitution, which mandates that the State shall protect and advance the right of the
 9   people to a balanced and healthful ecology, it is hereby declared the policy of the
10   State to protect, conserve, restore, and sustainably manage the Sierra Madre
11   Mountain Range as a nationally significant ecological, climate, water-security, and
12   disaster-resilience system. The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of Luzon’s
13   principal ecological systems. Its topography influences tropical-cyclone winds and
14   rainfall. The range includes the Northern Sierra Madre, which Republic Act No. 9125
15   recognizes as the widest remaining tropical rainforest in Luzon, together with other
16   forest, watershed, riverine, and coastal ecosystems, some of which are fragmented or
17   degraded. These ecosystems store and sequester carbon, sustain biodiversity and
18   ecological connectivity, and act as a regulator of climate and water flow. Protecting
19   the Sierra Madre is thus indispensable to biodiversity conservation, climate resilience,
20   water security, and disaster risk reduction. Their protection, restoration, and
21   sustainable management are therefore of national importance.
22
23   To achieve these ends, the State shall adopt a science-based, ecosystem-based, and
 1   landscape-scale approach to management, integrating conservation, restoration, and
 2   sustainable development. This includes applying ridge-to-reef planning, recognizing
 3   the ecological and hydrological links among uplands, lowlands, rivers, and coastal
 4   areas, and using nature-based solutions where appropriate to strengthen resilience.
 5
 6   The State shall adopt a Nature-Based Solutions-First and resilience-investment
 7   approach in addressing climate change, disaster risk, flooding, landslides, drought,
 8   water insecurity, and other climate-related risks affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain
 9   Range and its downstream beneficiary areas. Public and private investments shall,
10   where scientifically and technically appropriate, prioritize the protection, restoration,
11   and enhancement of natural infrastructure, including forests, watersheds, wetlands,
12   riparian areas, floodplains, mangroves, ecological corridors, and other natural systems
13   that provide climate and disaster-risk reduction services.
14
15   The State shall likewise mobilize investments from beneficiaries of Sierra Madre
16   ecosystem services and establish mechanisms through which verified ecological and
17   resilience outcomes may be financed through Nature Credits, blended finance, public-
18   private partnerships, climate finance, sustainable livelihood investments, and other
19   lawful market-based mechanisms.
20
21   The State recognizes that degradation persists because existing economic and
22   governmental decision systems permit land conversion, extraction, and conventional
23   development to generate immediate and identifiable financial returns, while
24   ecological, climate, water, and disaster costs are transferred to the public. Institutions,
25   Indigenous Peoples, local communities, and lawful tenure holders responsible for
26   protection, restoration, and long-term stewardship do not receive reliable revenues
27   proportionate to the ecosystem services they maintain. Fragmented authority enables
28   this misalignment and prevents ecological limits and economic responsibilities from
29   being applied consistently across the mountain range.
30
31   Accordingly, the State shall: (a) establish binding ecological limits, no-go areas,
32   decision principles, and consistency requirements governing covered plans, zoning
33   ordinances, permits, projects, procurement, and public investment; (b) account for
34   the extent, condition, service flows, beneficiaries, and economic contribution of Sierra
35   Madre ecosystems in accordance with Republic Act No. 11995, or the Philippine
36   Ecosystem and Natural Capital Accounting System Act; (c) require Covered Enterprises
37   to satisfy Nature Credit Purchase Obligations proportionate to their measured
38   dependency on and benefit from Sierra Madre ecosystem services; (d) require persons
39   causing environmental damage to avoid harm and fully restore affected ecosystems,
40   independently of and in addition to compliance with the Philippine Environmental
41   Impact Statement System; and (e) direct Sierra Madre Nature Credit revenues to
42   lawful rights holders, registered nature-project developers, and ecosystem stewards
43   in accordance with existing tenurial instruments, customary rights, and verified
44   performance.
45
46   The avoidance hierarchy, precautionary principle, polluter-pays principle, beneficiary-
47   pays principle, ecological connectivity, intergenerational equity, and the more
48   protective applicable standard shall govern implementation. No purchase, credit, or
 1   payment under this Act shall authorize prohibited activity, excuse noncompliance with
 2   an Environmental Compliance Certificate, or compensate for harm to an irreplaceable
 3   ecosystem that should have been avoided.

 5   The Authority established under this Act shall harmonize, interface, and rationalize the
 6   mandates, programs, and functions of national agencies, local government units, and
 7   other stakeholders, thereby ensuring a coherent, integrated, and ecosystem-scale
 8   governance framework. All policies and actions shall be evidence-based, incorporating
 9   the latest scientific data on biodiversity, hydrology, and climate impacts. The State
10   shall likewise strengthen enforcement and penalties against illegal activities like illegal
11   logging, mining, wildlife trafficking, land conversion, and the like, to deter destruction
12   of this critical landscape.
13
14   In line with Republic Act No. 8371, known as the Indigenous Peoples Rights Act (IPRA)
15   of 1997 and the Constitution, the State recognizes and protects the rights of
16   Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) within the Sierra
17   Madre. All management plans and projects shall respect ancestral domains, recognize
18   customary laws, and secure Free and Prior Informed Consent (FPIC) from affected
19   communities. Indigenous and local communities shall have meaningful participation in
20   decision-making and benefit-sharing, as stewards of the environment. Nothing in this
21   Act shall diminish the lawful powers of the DENR, LGUs, PAMBs, the NCIP, or
22   Indigenous governance institutions; such powers shall be exercised consistently with
23   the ecological floors, conflict rules, and decision procedures established herein.

25   Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage. This Act applies to the entire Sierra Madre Mountain
26   Range, the longitudinal mountain chain from Cagayan Province in the north to Quezon
27   Province in the south, including all existing and future protected areas within it. It
28   covers all forestlands, watersheds, river basins, and coastal zones up to their tidal
29   limits that lie within the range. Buffer zones, ecological corridors, and ridge-to-reef
30   linkages necessary to maintain connectivity and integrity are included. All components
31   of the National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS), as amended, within Sierra
32   Madre shall be governed by this integrated framework alongside their existing
33   management bodies.

 4   Sec. 5. Creation of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority. There is
 5   hereby created the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA),
 6   hereinafter referred to as the Authority, as a government-owned or -controlled
 7   corporation with an original charter under Republic Act No. 10149, vested with the
 8   corporate and regulatory powers expressly granted by this Act. It shall be attached to
 9   the Office of the President for policy coordination and administrative supervision, and
10   shall be subject to the oversight of the Governance Commission for GOCCs and other
11   applicable laws. For policy and technical alignment, the Authority shall closely
12   coordinate with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and
13   other relevant national agencies. The Authority shall be the lead policy, planning,
14   coordinating, and regulatory body, and the range-wide ecological governance and
15   nature-market authority, tasked with managing the Sierra Madre landscape as a
16   whole.
17
18   Its principal mandate is to change the governmental and economic decision systems
19   affecting the mountain range by: (a) establishing and enforcing the Sierra Madre
20   Master Plan and binding ecological limits, and administering the Sierra Madre Master
21   Plan Conformity Certificate process; (b) compiling operational ecosystem, enterprise-
22   dependency, and sector-dependency accounts in coordination with the agencies
23   responsible under Republic Act No. 11995; (c) delineating and periodically updating
24   Ecosystem-Service Catchments and identifying the cities, municipalities, economic
25   zones, industrial estates, utility service areas, locators, and other beneficiary areas
26   within them; (d) establishing methodologies, safeguards, and integrity requirements
27   for Sierra Madre Nature Credits; (e) administering project and developer registration,
28   credit issuance, ownership, transfer, purchase, retirement, and the Sierra Madre
29   Nature Credit Registry; (f) determining and enforcing Nature Credit Purchase
30   Obligations of Covered Enterprises; (g) assessing ecological loss and enforcing
31   restoration performance; (h) enabling private, community, Indigenous, and public
32   project proponents to access the standards, information, lawful tenure pathways, and
33   regulated demand needed to finance protection, restoration, and long-term
34   stewardship; and (i) integrating the exercise of the lawful mandates of the DENR,
35   LGUs, PAMBs, the NCIP, and other institutions through the decision rules and
36   procedures established by this Act.
37
38   The Authority shall not originate, sponsor, design, develop, construct, implement,
39   own, operate, manage, finance, invest in, underwrite, or guarantee any nature,
40   restoration, infrastructure, or other project that is eligible or expected to seek
41   registration, Sierra Madre Nature Credits, a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
42   Certificate, or another decision from the Authority. It shall not act as project
43   proponent, developer, investment adviser, arranger, broker, trader, buyer, or
44   beneficial owner of Sierra Madre Nature Credits.
45
46   In carrying out these functions, the Authority shall observe the non-duplication
47   safeguard in Section 12 and the functional delineation with the DENR in Section 68.
48   Ecosystem accounting shall inform decisions under this Act but shall not, by itself,

24   Sec. 9. Sierra Madre as Critical Natural Foundation for National Security.
25   The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is hereby declared a critical natural foundation
26   essential to national security, disaster risk reduction, and climate resilience.
27
28   Recognizing its function as one of the country’s longest natural barrier against
29   typhoons, storm surges, flooding, and landslides, the State shall prioritize its
30   protection as a matter of national survival and public safety.
31
32   Accordingly:
33
34       a. All policies, plans, and programs affecting the Sierra Madre shall consider
35          national security implications, particularly in relation to disaster risk reduction
36          and protection of life, property, and critical foundation;
37       b. The Authority shall coordinate with the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
38          Management Council (NDRRMC), Department of National Defense (DND), and
39          other relevant agencies to integrate ecosystem-based disaster risk reduction
40          strategies into national security planning; and
41       c. Any activity that significantly weakens the ecological integrity of the Sierra
42          Madre and increases disaster vulnerability shall be deemed contrary to
43          national security and may be subject to prohibition or heightened regulatory
44          scrutiny under this Act.

29   Sec. 23. Formulation and Adoption. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of
30   this Act, the Authority shall formulate, adopt, and commence the implementation of a
31   Sierra Madre Master Plan covering a period of twenty-five (25) years. The Sierra Madre
32   Master Plan shall serve as the comprehensive, science-based, ecosystem-oriented,
33   and climate-resilient blueprint for the conservation, protection, restoration, and
34   sustainable development of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
35
36   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall integrate, among others, land use and
37   development planning; biodiversity conservation; watershed and ridge-to-reef
38   ecosystem management; climate change adaptation and mitigation; disaster risk
39   reduction and management; sustainable livelihoods; infrastructure compatibility; and
40   the traditional ecological knowledge, customary governance systems, and sustainable
41   resource management practices of ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371, otherwise
42   known as the "Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997." The Authority shall likewise
43   recognize ICCAs and support indigenous co-management approaches consistent with
44   applicable laws.
 1   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall harmonize and integrate national, regional, and
 2   local development and land use plans affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range,
 3   including Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), Regional Development Plans
 4   (RDPs), ADSDPPs, protected area management plans, and other relevant sectoral
 5   plans, to ensure policy coherence and an integrated ridge-to-reef management
 6   approach.
 7
 8   In formulating the Sierra Madre Master Plan, the Authority shall build upon existing
 9   management plans prepared under the NIPAS, as amended, utilize the best available
10   scientific information and ecological data, and conduct meaningful consultations with
11   concerned national government agencies, local government units, FICCs/IPs, local
12   communities, the academe, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders.
13
14   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall expressly identify binding ecological limits, no-go
15   areas, permissible uses, restoration priorities, and decision rules. Harmonization shall
16   be governed by ecological integrity, precaution, avoidance before mitigation, polluter
17   pays, beneficiary pays, connectivity, intergenerational equity, the more protective
18   applicable standard, and respect for ancestral domains, customary law, tenurial rights,
19   and FPIC.
20
21   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years
22   and may be updated earlier, whenever necessary, to incorporate advances in scientific
23   knowledge, respond to fortuitous events and environmental emergencies, address
24   emerging threats, or reflect significant changes in ecological, climatic, or socio-
25   economic conditions.
26
27   Sec. 24. Landscape Zoning System. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall establish
28   a Landscape Zoning System that operationalizes the statutory classes under this Act
29   and shall be spatially organized into the following broad management groupings: (a)
30   Core Zone; (b) Sustainable Use Zone; (c) Buffer Zone; and (d) Special Management
31   Zone. The zoning system shall guide conservation, protection, restoration, sustainable
32   resource use, settlement, development, and disaster-risk management throughout the
33   Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
34
35   The Landscape Zoning System shall use the following statutory classes: (1) Strict
36   Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones; (2) Ecological Corridor and
37   Connectivity Zones; (3) Watershed and Catchment Protection Zones; (4) Restoration
38   and Recovery Zones; (5) Sustainable Production and Community Stewardship Zones;
39   and (6) Existing Settlement and Compatible Development Zones. The Sierra Madre
40   Master Plan may create subzones only within these classes and for the purposes stated
41   in this Act. The Master Plan shall likewise identify the corresponding broad
42   management grouping and applicable subzone for each geographically delineated
43   area.
44
45        1. Core Zone. The Core Zone shall comprise areas requiring the highest level
46           of ecological protection and shall include the following statutory classes and
47           corresponding areas: (a) Strict Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones;
48           (b) Ecological Corridor and Connectivity Zones; (c) Watershed and Catchment
 1           Protection Zones; and (d) Restoration and Recovery Zones. It shall include,
 2           where applicable, Protected Areas under the National Integrated Protected
 3           Areas System (NIPAS), Local Conservation Areas (LCAs), Other Effective Area-
 4           Based Conservation Measures (OECMs), Critical Habitats, and Indigenous
 5           Peoples’ and Community Conserved Areas (ICCAs).
 6
 7        2. Sustainable Use Zone. The Sustainable Use Zone shall comprise areas
 8          where environmentally sustainable and regulated economic, livelihood,
 9          community, cultural, educational, and compatible development activities may
10          be undertaken. It shall include the following statutory classes and subzones:
11          (a) Sustainable Production and Community Stewardship Zones; (b) Existing
12          Settlement and Compatible Development Zones; (c) Cultural/Traditional and
13          Controlled/Special Use Zones; and (d) Recreational and Educational Use
14          Zones. Activities within these areas shall be subject to the ecological limits,
15          permitted and prohibited uses, and development standards established by the
16          Sierra Madre Master Plan and this Act.
17
18        Within ancestral domains, activities in the Sustainable Use Zone shall be
19          consistent with the applicable ADSDPPs, customary laws, and the rights of
20          ICCs/IPs under Republic Act No. 8371.
21
22        3. Buffer Zone. The Buffer Zone shall provide an additional layer of protection
23           to core conservation areas and other environmentally sensitive areas and shall
24           be managed to minimize adverse impacts from adjacent land uses and
25           enhance ecological connectivity. The Buffer Zone shall include: (a) a forty-
26           meter (40 m) easement on all bodies of water; and (b) a twenty-meter (20
27           m) setback for areas classified as Sustainable Use Zones, subject to existing
28           laws and applicable rights and standards.
29
30        4. Special Management Zone. The Special Management Zone shall comprise
31           areas requiring specific management interventions due to unique ecological,
32           cultural, socio-economic, or disaster risk considerations. It may include
33           multiple-use areas, ecological restoration sites, hazard-prone areas, cultural
34           landscapes, and other areas requiring special management measures
35           identified through the Sierra Madre Master Plan.
36
37   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall identify and map the boundaries, applicable
38   statutory class, broad management grouping, subzone, permitted and prohibited uses,
39   management objectives, and applicable development standards for each zone. Zoning
40   shall be based on the latest available geospatial data, sound science, hazard
41   assessments, and relevant Indigenous and local knowledge.
42

29   Sec. 28. Sierra Madre Environmental Enforcement System (SMEES). An
30   integrated enforcement system is hereby established for the Sierra Madre,
31   institutionalizing a whole-of-government, whole-of-society approach. The SMEES shall
32   coordinate national oversight with regional operations and community-based actions
33   to prevent, detect, and prosecute environmental crimes and illegal resource activities
34   in the range.
35
36   Sec. 29. Structure of SMEES. The SMEES shall consist of:
37
38        (a) National Coordinating Council. Chaired by DENR, this council provides
39            strategic command. Members include heads of PNP, AFP, National Bureau of
40            Investigation (NBI), Department of Justice (DOJ), and Bureau of Customs
41            (BOC). The council formulates national enforcement strategies, ensures
42            inter-agency coordination, allocates resources, and issues directives for
43            operations in the Sierra Madre;
 1        (b) Regional Enforcement Task Forces. Established in key Sierra Madre
 2            regions, each task force includes DENR field officers, PNP, including maritime
 3            and environmental units, specialized AFP units, and LGU representatives.
 4            These task forces plan and execute joint enforcement raids, intelligence
 5            sharing, and rapid responses to violations within their jurisdictions, guided
 6            by the council’s strategies; and
 7        (c) Local Rapid Response Units (LRRUs). At the barangay or municipality
 8            level, community-based teams are organized composed of forest rangers,
 9            barangay officials, and trained representatives of Indigenous Peoples. LRRUs
10            act as frontline defenders: monitoring forests, reporting illegal activities, and
11            conducting initial patrols under the oversight of the regional task forces.
12
13   Sec. 30. Anti-Illegal Logging Task Force. A specialized Anti-Illegal Logging Task
14   Force (AILTF) is created under the SMEES. This unit focuses on timber syndicates and
15   trafficking networks. Key activities include: intelligence surveillance to identify culprits;
16   permanent and mobile checkpoints on logging routes; mobile ground patrols and aerial
17   reconnaissance of forests; coordination with courts for expedited search warrants; and
18   maintaining a shared database of offenders, seized logs, and enforcement outcomes.
19   Successful models like interagency aids have shown that coordinated action is
20   essential to stop chainsaw mills before they move stolen timber out of the mountains.

23   Systems. The Authority, in coordination with concerned national government
24   agencies, LGUs, and enforcement units, shall adopt and utilize modern, science-based,

36                                    Article VII
37        Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development Programs
38
39   Sec. 37. Guiding Principles. All programs under this Article shall be guided by
40   constitutional and international principles: intergenerational equity, the precautionary
41   and public trust doctrines, and the polluter-pays principle. The Sierra Madre is declared
42   an inviolable ecological zone; its protection is as much a human rights issue as an
43   environmental one.
44
45   Sec. 38. Forest Protection and Management. The Authority shall enforce strict
46   forest governance:
47
 1     (a) No-logging in Primary Forests. All logging, road-building for extraction, and
 2         forest conversion in primary old-growth forests are absolutely prohibited. Only
 3         scientifically justified removals like for research or severe pest control may be
 4         allowed by special authority. This reflects findings that even small disturbances
 5         in intact forest can undermine the Sierra Madre’s role as storm shield.
 6     (b) Criminalization of Illegal Logging. Illegal logging of any forest within the
 7         Sierra Madre like harvesting, transport, and possession or trade of timber
 8         without proper permit, shall be punished by severe penalties: imprisonment of
 9         six (6) to twelve (12) years; fines from ₱1,000,000 to ₱10,000,000; forfeiture
10         of equipment and vehicles used; and permanent disqualification from public
11         office if the offender is an official. These penalties are without prejudice to
12         penalties under Republic Act No. 9175 or the Illegal Logging Act.
13
14     (c) Sierra Madre Forest Protection Corps. The Authority shall organize a
15         special Forest Protection Corps, trained and deputized to work with the PNP
16         and AFP in forest patrols and enforcement. This corps will carry out joint
17         operations, establish rapid response teams, and assist in disaster monitoring.
18         Their creation addresses gaps in manpower and ensures forest protection is
19         sustained.
20
21     (d) Technology-Assisted Monitoring. Mandatory use of satellite imagery,
22         drone surveillance, and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted forest analytics shall
23         be integrated into enforcement. Real-time alerts shall trigger patrols. A public
24         website or dashboard may be established so communities and NGOs can also
25         monitor alerts, similar to citizen science platforms.
26
27     (e) Restoration Obligation. Any individual, company, or government entity
28         found responsible for forest destruction, through illegal logging or conversion,
29         shall be ordered to undertake full ecological restoration of the affected area at
30         their own expense. Restoration standards shall achieve ecological equivalence,
31         specifically similar forest structure and species diversity, and be supervised by
32         the Authority and DENR. Failure to restore shall be an additional offense, liable
33         to fines or injunctions.
34
35   Sec. 39. Biodiversity Conservation. To safeguard endemic and endangered
36   species:
37
38      a. All hunting, capture, killing, or trade of threatened and or endemic species is
39         strictly prohibited.
40      b. Violators shall face imprisonment of six (6) to ten (10) years and fines of
41         ₱500,000 to ₱5,000,000. Corporate offenders may have licenses revoked and
42         be blacklisted. These penalties are in addition to those under the Wildlife Act
43         or RA 9147.
44      c. The Authority shall establish wildlife rescue and rehabilitation centers in or
45         near the Sierra Madre, for injured or confiscated animals and exotics, and
46         encourage captive breeding of critically endangered species.
 1       d. Critical habitat designation. Key habitats such as breeding grounds,
 2          nesting sites, spawning rivers, among others, not already in PAs shall be
 3          designated as critical and strictly protected.
 4       e. Ecological corridors. The Authority will, in coordination with DENR, formally

 6          development buffers along them, recognizing that species from the famed
 7          Philippine Eagle to lowland fishes rely on such connectivity.

 9          including habitat destruction or pollution, shall be liable for ecological
10          damages.

16   Sec. 40. Strict Regulation of Mining, Quarrying, and Other Extractive
17   Activities. All commercial and non-commercial extractive activities within the Sierra
18   Madre Mountain Range shall be subject to the highest standards of environmental
19   protection and sustainable resource management, without prejudice to the powers of
20   the DENR under existing laws.
21
22      (a) Commercial and Non-Commercial Extractive Activities. Commercial
23          mining, quarrying, sand and gravel extraction, earth extraction, and other
24          extractive activities undertaken for profit or commercial purposes shall be
25          subject to the requirements of this Act and all applicable environmental,
26          mining, and natural resources laws. Non-commercial extraction of minerals,
27          sand, gravel, rocks, and other naturally occurring materials intended solely for
28          personal, household, subsistence, cultural, or community use, and not for sale
29          or commercial distribution, may be allowed only in areas not otherwise
30          prohibited by law and subject to such rules, limitations, and safeguards as may
31          be prescribed by the Authority, in coordination with the DENR and concerned
32          LGUs.
33
34      (b) Landscape-Scale and Cumulative Impacts under the Philippine EIS
35          System. For all proposed commercial extractive projects covered by the
36          Philippine Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System, the DENR or the
37          EMB shall ensure that the environmental impact assessment, at the level of
38          detail appropriate to the project and site, evaluates the direct, indirect,
39          induced, cumulative, and long-term environmental, social, and economic
40          impacts of the proposed activity on forest ecosystems, watersheds,
41          biodiversity, ecological connectivity, climate and disaster resilience, ecosystem
42          services, indigenous cultural communities, and affected local communities.
43
44         The Authority shall transmit to the DENR or the EMB the applicable Sierra Madre
45         Master Plan maps, ecological limits, management zones, corridor and
46         catchment data, carrying-capacity information, and adopted scientific
47         standards, and may participate as a resource agency in scoping and technical
48         review. The DENR or the EMB shall retain responsibility for EIA scoping, impact
 1         assessment, public participation, technical review, and the ECC decision. No
 2         separate Cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment or Authority-issued
 3         ecological impact clearance shall be required under this Act.
 4
 5      (c) Strict Liability. Operators of commercial extractive industries shall be held
 6          strictly liable, regardless of fault or negligence, for any environmental damage,
 7          habitat degradation, pollution, or loss of biodiversity resulting from their
 8          activities, without prejudice to applicable civil, criminal, and administrative
 9          liabilities under existing laws.
10
11      (d) Rehabilitation and Environmental Guarantee Fund. No commercial
12          mining or quarry permit shall be issued without proof of a Rehabilitation and
13          Environmental Guarantee Fund, rehabilitation bond, or equivalent financial
14          security sufficient to cover the full cost of site restoration, environmental
15          remediation, and post-closure monitoring, as determined by the DENR and the
16          Authority. Such fund or bond shall be maintained throughout the life of the
17          project and may be forfeited or utilized by the government should the operator
18          fail to undertake the required rehabilitation.
19
20      (e) Suspension and Revocation. Any commercial extractive operation that
21          causes significant environmental damage, violates the terms and conditions of
22          its ECC, permit, or other environmental authorization, or poses an imminent
23          threat to public health, public safety, or ecological integrity shall be subject to
24          the immediate suspension or revocation of its permit, without prejudice to the
25          imposition of other administrative, civil, or criminal sanctions provided by law.
26
27      (f) Penalties. Any person, corporation, partnership, association, government-
28          owned or controlled corporation, or public official found guilty of violating this
29          Section shall, in addition to any penalties provided under existing
30          environmental and mining laws, suffer imprisonment of eight (8) years to
31          fifteen (15) years, a fine of Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00) to Fifty Million
32          Pesos (₱50,000,000.00), and the mandatory closure of the commercial
33          extractive operation. Where the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible
34          officers shall be held personally liable, without prejudice to the suspension or
35          revocation of the entity's permits, licenses, or authority to operate in
36          accordance with law.

 5   Sec. 42. Ecosystem Restoration and Rehabilitation. The Authority shall launch

 7

 9         suited to local ecology. Exotic plantation forests are discouraged in protection
10         zones.

22         government, failing to meet court- or Authority-ordered restoration shall be

27   Sec. 43. Ecosystem Connectivity and Fragmentation. The fragmentation of
28   habitats undermines biodiversity and ecosystem services, and violates the State’s duty
29   under Article II, Section 16. The Authority shall implement the following measures to
30   preserve and restore connectivity:
31
32      a. Biodiversity Corridors. Identify and formally establish wildlife corridors such
33         as connecting forest fragments, riparian zones, and elevational gradients,
34         among others as protected areas. These corridors shall be managed to allow
35         species migration and climate-driven range shifts. The Authority shall
36         coordinate their integration into local land use plans.
37      b. Avoidance and Infrastructure Planning. All proponents shall demonstrate
38         avoidance of core forests, critical habitats, corridors, riparian systems, and
39         irreplaceable ecosystems. Require that all new infrastructure such as roads,
40         bridges, and power lines in the Sierra Madre be planned to avoid habitat
41         fragmentation. Projects that would bisect core forests or corridors are
42         prohibited unless absolutely unavoidable for overriding public interest. If no
43         alternative exists, the proponent shall minimize harm and fully restore the
44         affected ecosystem and service flows, including through wildlife crossings and
45         habitat restoration. Prohibited or irreversible harm shall not be approved or
46         offset. Departments like DPWH must submit proof of compliance like corridor
47         gap analyses before project approval.
48
 1   c. Conformity with the Sierra Madre Master Plan. No conversion of land or
 2      fragmenting project within the Sierra Madre shall receive final approval without

 4      Authority shall determine only whether the proposed location, land use, and
 5      development footprint conform with the applicable management zone,
 6      ecological limits, no-go areas, biodiversity corridors, watershed and catchment

46       compensatory credits usable against unrelated harm.

32   Sec. 45. Ecologically Compatible and Climate-Resilient Infrastructure. All

 9   Sec. 49. Sierra Madre Protected Area Network (SMPAN). All protected areas,
10   protected landscapes and seascapes, critical habitats, and other conservation areas
11   within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range shall be managed as an integrated Sierra
12   Madre Protected Area Network (SMPAN) under a landscape-scale conservation
13   approach to strengthen ecological connectivity, biodiversity conservation, and climate
14   resilience. The management regime for the Network shall promote the
15   interconnectivity of ecological corridors among protected areas and other key
16   biodiversity areas to ensure the long-term ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre
17   Mountain Range. Coordinated planning, zoning, monitoring, and enforcement shall be
18   undertaken to prevent conservation leakage and to enhance the effectiveness of
19   protected area management.
20
21   In particular:
22        (a) The Authority, in coordination with the DENR-Biodiversity Management
23              Bureau (BMB), DENR-Regional Offices (2, 3, and 4-A), PAMBs, concerned
24              local government units, ICCs/IPs, and other stakeholders, shall establish,
25              restore, and maintain a network of ecological corridors linking fragmented
26              protected areas and other critical habitats to facilitate wildlife movement,
27              gene flow, and climate-induced species migration, including those of
28              threatened and endemic species such as the Philippine Eagle; and
29
30        (b) The Authority shall utilize geospatial technologies, biodiversity
31            assessments, and scientific data to identify ecological bottlenecks and
32            habitat fragmentation between the northern, central, and southern Sierra
33            Madre ranges, and shall prioritize the restoration and reconnection of these
34            ecological corridors through appropriate conservation and habitat
35            restoration measures.
36

13   Sec. 63. Adoption of Global Conservation Standards. The Authority shall
14   implement adaptive, landscape-level governance in accordance with international best
15   practices:
16       1. Landscape-Level Governance. Policies and management shall transcend
17          administrative boundaries, focusing on ecological units;
18       2. Ridge-to-Reef (R2R) Management. Emphasize hydrological linkages.
19          Upland forest protection shall be coordinated with coastal and fisheries
20          management, recognizing the Sierra Madre’s role in watershed regulation and
21          marine ecosystem health;

23          the latest science like climate projections and biodiversity assessments, and
24          adjust strategies accordingly; and
25       4. Nature-Based Solutions (NbS). Priority shall be given to ecosystem-based
26          adaptation and mitigation such as restoration to prevent floods and mangrove
27          restoration for storm buffering. The Authority shall draw lessons from global
28          examples where NbS have effectively enhanced resilience.

31   evaluated as a functional component of disaster-risk reduction and water-security

39   Sec. 65. Sierra Madre Nature Credit System and Conservation Incentives.
40   There is hereby established a compliance-enabled system through which eligible
41   protection, restoration, and long-term stewardship results within the Sierra Madre
42   Mountain Range are independently verified, issued as Sierra Madre Nature Credits,
43   purchased by Covered Enterprises, and retired to satisfy Nature Credit Purchase
44   Obligations.

 1   Sierra Madre Nature Credits may represent independently verified ecological and
 2   climate-resilience outcomes, including watershed protection, flood attenuation,
 3   landslide-risk reduction, sediment retention, water-security improvement, restoration
 4   of wetlands and riparian systems, ecological restoration, avoided degradation,
 5   biodiversity conservation, ecological connectivity, and qualifying Early Warning System
 6   services.

 8   outcome, geographic area or Ecosystem-Service Catchment, baseline, methodology,
 9   duration, beneficiaries, monitoring requirements, verification requirements, and
10   applicable safeguards.

17       a. Promotion Branding. Private investors who finance Sierra Madre
18          conservation such as restoration projects, corridor restoration, and sustainable
19          agroforestry, shall be eligible for income tax credits of up to 50% of their
20          investment. This is to encourage corporate stewardship of the landscape.

22       b. Ecosystem-Service Catchments and Covered Enterprises. For each
23          material ecosystem service, the Authority shall delineate the Ecosystem-Service
24          Catchment and publish a map and beneficiary schedule identifying the cities,
25          municipalities, economic zones, industrial estates, utility service areas, and
26          other locations that receive or depend on that service. The Authority shall use
27          these maps to identify the revenue-generating enterprises and locators that
28          must undergo a Covered Enterprise assessment. An enterprise shall be finally
29          designated as a Covered Enterprise only upon evidence that it receives a
30          material, measurable, and geographically traceable direct or indirect economic
31          benefit from, or that its revenue-producing operations are materially dependent
32          on, the specified service. During the initial implementation period, Covered
33          Enterprise assessments shall be limited to water utilities and concessionaires;
34          irrigation and agribusiness enterprises; hydropower and other energy
35          operators; industrial estates and locators; tourism and real-estate enterprises;
36          transport and other infrastructure operators; and other businesses whose
37          revenues, assets, operations, or supply chains materially depend on water
38          regulation, sediment retention, flood and landslide attenuation, climate
39          regulation, biodiversity, connectivity, or related services. Location within a
40          mapped catchment shall establish geographic eligibility for assessment but shall
41          not, by itself, create a Nature Credit Purchase Obligation. Households,
42          subsistence users, public schools and hospitals, non-revenue public services,
43          and entities below materiality thresholds shall not be Covered Enterprises solely
44          because they receive a general public benefit.

46       c. Catchment and Sector-Dependency Assessment. For each ecosystem
47          service, the Authority shall determine the source ecosystem, physical service
48          flow, geographic catchment, beneficiary class, materiality, and economic
 1     consequence of impairment. The assessment shall consider: (1) quantity and
 2     criticality of service use; (2) revenue, value added, production, or assets
 3     demonstrably exposed; (3) additional operating or treatment cost; (4)
 4     replacement or substitute infrastructure cost; (5) risk-mitigation, insurance,
 5     and business-continuity cost; (6) avoided losses and service interruptions; (7)
 6     causal and geographic connection to the relevant ecosystem; and (8) enterprise
 7     size and capacity. The Authority shall coordinate with the PSA, NAMRIA, DENR,
 8     DILG, DHSUD, DTI, SEC, PEZA, and other economic-zone or sector authorities,
 9     utilities, and affected LGUs, and shall publish the maps, nonconfidential data,
10     and methodology used. Monetary valuation shall not replace physical accounts,
11     ecological limits, or protection of intrinsic and cultural values.

13   d. Statutory Standards and Sector Rules. A class may be covered only upon
14      a public finding, supported by substantial evidence, that: (1) its revenue-
15      producing activity has the nexus stated in subsection (b); (2) the applicable
16      ecosystem service and service area can be measured with sufficient reliability;
17      (3) the annual ecological outcome need is identified in the Sierra Madre Master
18      Plan; (4) the allocation method is proportionate, administrable, and no more
19      burdensome than reasonably necessary to advance that need; and (5)
20      equivalent obligations under another national law are credited to prevent
21      double compliance. Before effectivity, a sector rule shall undergo the procedure
22      in Section 12 and obtain concurrence from the relevant sector regulator and
23      the DOF as to regulatory and distributional impacts.

25   e. Annual Nature Credit Purchase Obligation. The Sierra Madre Master Plan
26      shall state, separately for each ecosystem service, outcome class, and
27      Ecosystem-Service Catchment, the annual quantity of Sierra Madre Nature
28      Credits required to achieve watershed, corridor, and landscape targets.
29      Quantities from different services or catchments shall not be combined into a
30      single undifferentiated requirement. Each Covered Enterprise's Nature Credit
31      Purchase Obligation shall be the lesser of: (1) its proportionate share of that
32      approved need, calculated from dependency weights and measured benefit or
33      exposure; and (2) its attributable share of the verified avoided-loss,
34      replacement, treatment, risk-mitigation, and business-continuity cost
35      associated with impairment of the relevant service over the compliance period,
36      adjusted for enterprise size, capacity, and applicable rate regulation. The
37      resulting obligation shall not exceed the enterprise's documented annual
38      Ecosystem-Service Dependency value for the relevant service, that is, the
39      amount that the enterprise would reasonably and efficiently incur during the
40      same period to replace, treat, secure, insure against, or operate without the
41      impaired service. This is the statutory maximum and may not be increased by
42      the Board or the implementing rules and regulations. Congress may enact lower
43      sector ceilings after considering pilot, affordability, and fiscal evidence. The
44      formula shall not be based on gross revenue alone and shall state data sources,
45      materiality thresholds, de minimis exclusions, sector weights, treatment of
46      MSMEs, and correction for benefits already financed by the enterprise. Each
47      enterprise shall receive a written assessment, access to supporting
 1     nonconfidential data, and an opportunity to correct data and seek independent
 2     administrative review.

 6      performance obligation satisfied through the purchase and retirement of
 7      independently verified Sierra Madre Nature Credits rather than payment to the
 8      Authority. It is not an Authority fee, tax, permit to operate, or payment for
 9      permission to cause harm. The price of the credits shall pass through an
10      approved escrow or settlement arrangement to entitled credit owners and
11      project-delivery parties. Only the specific fee or Market Administration Charge
12      authorized by Section 10 may be deducted or collected for the Authority, and
13      it shall be separately shown in the transaction record. Any tax, special
14      assessment, or cash collection for general public purposes shall require
15      separate statutory authority.

26   h. Eligible Nature Credits and Methodologies. Separate Sierra Madre Nature
27      Credit classes may represent verified avoided degradation, improved ecosystem
28      condition, watershed security, flood and landslide resilience, sediment
29      retention, biodiversity, ecological connectivity, restoration, or carbon benefits.
30      These classes are not interchangeable. A credit represents only the measured
31      ecological outcome stated in its methodology and does not confer ownership
32      of land, water, biodiversity, an ecosystem, sacred knowledge, or a cultural
33      value. Every methodology shall specify the baseline, counterfactual, unit,
34      project boundary, service class, eligible catchment, temporal matching,
35      safeguards, additionality where applicable, durability, leakage, reversal, buffer
36      or replacement mechanism, monitoring, uncertainty, and verification
37      requirements, and shall undergo public consultation, independent scientific
38      peer review, and periodic reassessment. A credit may satisfy an obligation only
39      when its service class, catchment or approved connected area, vintage, and
40      durability match the dependency on which the obligation was based. Cross-
41      service substitution is prohibited; geographic substitution may be allowed only
42      where an approved methodology demonstrates the same service flow and
43      beneficiaries. Credits shall be issued only after independent ex post verification
44      and recorded with unique serial numbers. An avoided-degradation methodology
45      shall use a conservative, independently reviewed counterfactual, exclude
46      protection already secured by an adequately enforced legal duty unless the
47      methodology proves a financing gap material to performance, and discount
48      uncertainty and reversal risk.

34   Sec. 72. Prohibited Acts. In addition to the acts prohibited under existing
35   environmental and natural resources laws, the following acts are likewise prohibited
36   within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range:
37
38       1. Unauthorized Land Conversion. The conversion of forest lands to
39          agricultural or other non-forest uses without the required authority under
40          existing laws and, where applicable, without first securing a Sierra Madre
41          Master Plan Conformity Certificate;
42
 1       2. Destructive Extractive Activities. The conduct of mining or commercial or
 2          non-commercial quarrying in a manner that causes significant or irreversible
 3          environmental damage, including, but not limited to, the destruction of old-
 4          growth forests, critical habitats, or watersheds, and the pollution or
 5          contamination of rivers and other water bodies;
 6
 7       3. Introduction of Invasive Species. The deliberate introduction, release, or
 8          propagation of non-native or invasive species of flora or fauna that harm
 9          native species, biodiversity, or ecosystem integrity;
10
11       4. Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Beginning, financing,
12          directing, or causing site clearing, tree cutting, grading, excavation, road
13          opening, land development, construction, installation, expansion, or other
14          physical works for a covered project before obtaining a valid Sierra Madre
15          Master Plan Conformity Certificate or other Authority document expressly
16          required by this Act, together with the ECC, PAMB approval, FPIC, tenure
17          instrument, LGU permit, or sector authorization otherwise required by law. A
18          permit issued by one institution shall not excuse the failure to obtain a
19          separate certification, approval, or consent required by this Act or another
20          law;
21
22       5. Continuation after Notice or Order. Continuing covered works after
23          receipt of a lawful stop-work, cease-and-desist, suspension, or restoration
24          order issued by the Authority, the DENR, a PAMB, an LGU, a court, or another
25          competent body, or removing an official seal or obstruction placed to enforce
26          that order; and
27
28       6. False Compliance Evidence. Knowingly submitting, using, altering, or
29          causing the submission or use of a false Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
30          Certificate, Nature Credit Compliance Clearance, registry record, electronic
31          confirmation record, or other Authority document to obtain a permit,
32          financing, procurement award, disbursement, or authority to proceed.
33
34   Emergency works strictly necessary to prevent an imminent threat to life or serious
35   damage to essential public infrastructure shall not constitute an offense if the
36   proponent gives notice to the Authority and the competent permitting agencies within
37   twenty-four (24) hours, limits the work to the emergency, and submits to post-action
38   review, restoration, and all other requirements of law.

40   Sec. 73. Penalties.
41      (a) Administrative Compliance Violations. Failure to file a required report,
42          maintain an accurate account, timely purchase and retire a final assessed
43          requirement, or comply with a procedural or Registry duty, without fraud or
44          material ecological harm, shall be subject only to the administrative sanctions
45          expressly provided in this Section after notice and hearing.
 1   (b) False Documents and Market Fraud. A person who knowingly falsifies or
 2       alters an Authority certificate, compliance clearance, Registry record,
 3       verification report, or settlement confirmation; intentionally causes double
 4       issuance or double claiming; manipulates the Registry; or diverts credit
 5       revenues shall, upon conviction, be punished by imprisonment of two (2)
 6       years and one (1) day to six (6) years and a fine of not less than Five Hundred
 7       Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) nor more than Ten Million Pesos
 8       (₱10,000,000.00), or three (3) times the economic benefit obtained,
 9       whichever is higher, without prejudice to prosecution under another law
10       carrying a higher penalty.
11
12   (c) Unauthorized Works Causing Material Ecological Harm. Violators of
13       Sec. 72 on prohibited acts who willfully begin or continue works without the
14       required Authority document and other approvals, and thereby cause material
15       ecological harm, shall, upon conviction, be punished by imprisonment of six
16       (6) years and one (1) day to twelve (12) years and fines of ₱1,000,000 to
17       ₱50,000,000, depending on the harm caused. The highest range shall apply
18       only where the act affects an irreplaceable ecosystem, causes serious or
19       irreversible harm, continues after a lawful stop-work order, or forms part of
20       repeated or organized unlawful conduct. In addition, corporate offenders may
21       have their business permits revoked by the issuing agency or a competent
22       court, and their assets restrained only upon judicial order or express statutory
23       authority, to cover rehabilitation costs. These penalties are in addition to other
24       penalties under existing environmental laws.

26   (d) Responsible Officers. Where the offender is a juridical entity, criminal
27       liability shall attach only to an officer or employee who authorized, directed,
28       knowingly participated in, or knowingly failed to prevent the offense despite
29       a legal duty and actual capacity to do so. The court may order restitution,
30       restoration, and forfeiture as authorized by law.
31
32   (e) Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Without prejudice to
33       criminal prosecution and penalties under another law, a private person or
34       juridical entity that violates Sec. 72 (4) shall, after notice and hearing, be
35       subject to: (1) an immediate stop-work or cease-and-desist order; (2) an
36       administrative fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
37       (₱100,000.00) nor more than Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00), or three (3)
38       times the documented economic benefit or compliance cost avoided,
39       whichever is higher, plus not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) nor
40       more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00) for each day the work
41       continues after a lawful notice or order; (3) restoration of the affected
42       ecosystem at full cost; (4) payment of interim public loss until verified
43       recovery; and (5) forfeiture of the economic benefit obtained or compliance
44       cost avoided, as determined by a court or other authority expressly
45       empowered by law.

31       and may recommend suspension from a benefit or procurement program only

45       benefit obtained, whichever is higher. Criminal conduct shall be referred to
46       the Department of Justice or other competent body.

 1   (i) Restoration Nonperformance. Failure to comply with a final restoration

23             not conduct a separate environmental impact assessment, issue an
24             Ecological Impact Clearance, or determine whether a project is

28             requirement for final action on its ECC application. The EMB may accept

33             and the DENR or the EMB has electronically confirmed that it is valid and
34             current.

36             written findings shall state the project and proponent identifiers, proposed
37             location, land use, and development footprint; applicable management
38             zone, ecological limits, no-go areas, corridors, catchment protections,
39             permitted and prohibited uses, and development standards; conformity
40             finding; validity period; and electronic confirmation reference. They shall

42             shall form part of the ECC administrative record.

44             statutory prerequisite, not an ECC or substitute permit. The DENR shall

48             approval.
