                             AN ACT
       INSTITUTING THE UNBUNDLING OF PRICES IN THE SALE OF
            PETROLEUM PRODUCTS BY THE DOWNSTREAM
                 OIL INDUSTRY IN THE PHILIPPINES


                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Republic Act No. 8479 or the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of
1998 is a failure.

        For more than 25 years since its enactment, R.A. 8479 failed to fulfill its promise
to “ensure a truly competitive market under a regime of fair prices” through the
liberalization and the deregulation of the local downstream oil industry. From January
to December 16, 2025 alone, gasoline price increases have totaled to P21.30 per liter;
diesel, P21.55; kerosene, P9.51; and LPG, at around P6.55.

       To begin with, R.A. 8479 had the wrong premise that the world pricing of
petroleum and petroleum products is fair and justifiable. Coupled with a lack of
knowledge on all data like the actual cost of fuel, the Philippine government became
incapable of determining predatory pricing and overpricing. This is the tragic folly of
deregulation.

       From the very beginning, R.A. 8479 was bound to fail in implementing fair
prices since it ignored monopoly-pricing practices and cartel behavior by the giant
transnational oil corporations. Worse, deregulation had given transnational oil
corporations even more room to manipulate prices through automatic oil price hikes


                                                                                                        1
(OPH); less transparency in pricing, profits, and operations; and virtually no
accountability to the consuming public.

       Petroleum is a sensitive commodity, since its price directly affects the cost of
almost all other commodities and services, including essentials such as food, housing,
social services, as well as transportation. Deregulation has allowed oil price increases
to go unchecked. Deregulation has given transnational oil corporations even more
leeway to influence the country’s cost of living, livelihood, business and commerce,
employment, and the National Budget.

       There is a pressing need to interdict the hidden and unchecked transfer-pricing
between oil company subsidiaries, including probable price-padding in the sale of
petroleum and petroleum products between refiners and local subsidiaries, to protect
the majority of Filipinos from current runaway increases in oil prices.

       Since 2017, Bayan Muna, along with other progressive groups and organizations
had been demanding for the oil pricing data from oil companies in numerous hearings
in Congress but to no avail. They argue that there is nothing confidential about the actual
cost of producing oil. But these data would have capacitated the government in
protecting the consuming public from the abuses of the oil companies. Furthermore,
deregulation has been here for 20 years and the consumers could have overpaid billions
of pesos in overpriced fuel.

       In the 17th and 18th Congresses, the Makabayan Bloc filed House Resolutions
Nos. 415 and 9, respectively, to investigate the alleged predatory pricing and
overpricing of oil products, hidden by their unbundled oil prices. These prompted the
Department of Energy (DOE) to issue Department Circular 2019-05-008, or the Revised
Guidelines for the Monitoring of Prices in the Sale of Petroleum Products by the
Downstream Oil Industry in the Philippines, which the Supreme Court First Division
affirmed in 31 July 2024. However, despite the legal victory, the DOE has yet to fully
implement the unbundling of oil prices for the public's perusal.

       This Bill institutionalizes the unbundling of oil prices in the downstream oil
industry in the Philippines. It is merely pursuant to the powers of the DOE and the DOE
Secretary1 which among others, are to monitor both the international and domestic price
movements of petroleum products and require persons or entities engaged in a particular
activity of the industry to file an annual or special report, or both in such form as the
Congress and or the Secretary may prescribe.

                            AN ACT
       INSTITUTING THE UNBUNDLING OF PRICES IN THE SALE OF
            PETROLEUM PRODUCTS BY THE DOWNSTREAM
                 OIL INDUSTRY IN THE PHILIPPINES


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Republic of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Unbundling of Oil Prices
Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is enshrined in the 1987 Philippine
Constitution that “the State shall promote social justice in all phases of national
development” (Article II, Section X) and “shall pursue a trade policy that serves the
general welfare and utilizes all forms and arrangements of exchange on the basis of
equality and reciprocity” (Article XII, Section XIII).

Moreover, the Constitution also mandates that, “the State shall regulate or prohibit
monopolies when the public interest so requires. No combinations in restraint of trade
or unfair competition shall be allowed” (Article XII, Section XIV).

Consistent with the foregoing, crude oil and socially and economically sensitive refined
petroleum or petroleum products, being vital to national security and their supply at
reasonable prices being essential to the general welfare, it is hereby declared to be the
policy of the State to ensure transparency in every aspect of the oil trade to come up
with reasonable prices in such products. Moreover, it is hereby declared a policy of the


                                                                                                       4
State to protect the public interest against monopoly and unscrupulous practices that
affect the rates and services in the oil industry.

SECTION 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all persons or entities
engaged in any and all activities of the domestic downstream oil industry, specifically
on the sale of the following petroleum products: gasoline, automotive and industrial
diesel, kerosene, jet fuel and aviation gas, and household and automotive Liquefied
petroleum gas (LPG).

SECTION 4. Unbundled Oil Prices. – All oil companies shall submit to the DOE a
detailed breakdown of the prices of all its petroleum products. It shall include the
following:

   a) International Content
          i. Import Cost (crude or finished product);
         ii. Freight Cost;
       iii. Insurance; and
        iv. Foreign Exchange Rate
   b) Taxes and Duties
          i. Duties;
         ii. Excise Tax;
       iii. Value Added Tax; and
        iv. Other Imposts
   c) Biofuel Cost
   d) Oil Company Take Components
          i. Port Charges;
         ii. Other Imposts;
       iii. Refining Cost (for crude);
        iv. Storage Cost;
         v. Handling Cost;
        vi. Marketing Cost;
       vii. Transshipment Cost;
      viii. Other Cost;
        ix. Oil Company Profit Margin; and
         x. Total Oil Company Price
   e) Other relevant information.

SECTION 5. Prior Notice of Price Adjustment. – Oil companies shall issue a notice
to the Department of Energy (DOE) for any oil price adjustment prior the
implementation thereof: Provided, That the notice shall include a detailed computation
with corresponding explanation and supporting documents on the causes or reasons of
the movement of the individual unbundled price adjustment item.

SECTION 6. Unbundled Retail Prices. – All liquid fuel retail outlets, LPG refillers
and LPG dealers shall issue an official receipt of sale of petroleum products with an
unbundled computation detailing the following:


                                                                                      5
       1.   Oil Company Price;
       2.   Hauler’s Fee
       3.   Taxes;
       4.   Fixed Cost;
       5.   Variable Cost;
       6.   Profit Margin;
       7.   Total LF Retail Price or LPG Refiller’s/Dealer’s Pick-up Price; and
       8.   Other relevant information.

SECTION 7. Price Display Board. – For the awareness and information of the public,
all liquid fuel retail outlets, LPG refillers and LPG dealers shall display prominently
and conspicuously updated prices in their display boards as required by existing DOE
Circulars.

SECTION 8. Transparency. – The DOE shall immediately publish and make available
to the public all of the information regarding unbundled oil prices, the prior notice on
oil price adjustment, and other submissions of the oil companies.

SECTION 9. Additional powers of the DOE. – The DOE:

   a) May conduct a motu proprio investigation for possible overpricing, predatory
      pricing and other anti-competitive behavior;
   b) Shall establish a grievance mechanism to receive ground complaints from
      consumer;

SECTION 10. Penalties. - Any person who violates any of the provisions of this Act
shall suffer the penalty of three (3) months to one (1) year imprisonment and a fine
ranging from Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) to Three hundred thousand pesos
P300,000.00).

Whenever any of the violations described above is committed by a corporation or
association, the president and each one of its agents or representatives in the Philippines
in case of a foreign corporation or association, who shall have knowingly permitted or
failed to prevent the commission of such offense, shall be held liable as principals
thereof.

In addition to the preceding penal provision, the DOE shall have the power to suspend
or cancel the acknowledgement to engage in any activity in the Downstream Oil
Industry, the Certificate of Compliance or the Standard Compliance Certificate,
respectively, of the non-compliant oil company, liquid fuel retail outlet, LPG refiller
and LPG dealer thereby suspending or cancelling the further processing of any
application or request to the DOE in relation to its downstream oil operations.

The DOE may further recommend to the proper government agency the suspension or
revocation or termination of the business permit of the non-compliant oil company or
liquid fuel retail outlet, LPG refiller and LPG dealer.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulation. – The DOE shall formulate and
issue the necessary implementing rules and regulations within thirty (30) days after the
effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 12. Amendatory and Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders,
resolutions, revenue regulations, ordinances or circulars inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly or declared null and
void and inoperative.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any section or provision of this
Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, such other sections or provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days upon its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
