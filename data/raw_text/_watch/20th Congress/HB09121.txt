MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF PERSONS WITH
DISABILITY AFFAIRS OFFICES (PDAOs) IN ALL LOCAL
GOVERNMENT UNITS, FURTHER AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTION 40 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7277, AS AMENDED BY
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10070, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS AN ACT
ESTABLISHING INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM TO ENSURE THE
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROGRAMS AND SERVICES FOR PERSONS
WITH DISABILITIES IN EVERY PROVINCE

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       More than four decades since the enactment of the country’s first disability
legislation, Batas Pambansa Blg. 344, otherwise known as the Accessibility Law,
and more than a decade since the Philippines ratified the United Nations
Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), the
government has undertaken various legislative and policy initiatives to promote
the rights and welfare of persons with disabilities.

       Among these measures is Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the
Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities, which recognizes the rights of persons
with disabilities to rehabilitation, self-development, self-reliance, and full
participation in the mainstream of society. Subsequent laws further strengthened
these protections, including Republic Act No. 10524, which promotes
employment opportunities for persons with disabilities, and Republic Act No.
10754, which expanded the benefits and privileges of persons with disabilities.
       In recognition of the need to ensure that programs and services for persons
with disabilities are effectively implemented at the local level, Republic Act No.
10070 encouraged local government units (LGUs) to establish Persons with
Disability Affairs Offices (PDAOs). These offices are intended to serve as focal
points for the implementation of disability-related programs, policy coordination,
and the delivery of services to persons with disabilities within their respective
jurisdictions.

       However, more than a decade since the passage of Republic Act No. 10070,
compliance among LGUs remains limited. Records from the National Council on
Disability Affairs (NCDA) indicate that only a portion of LGUs have established
their respective PDAOs, largely because the creation of these offices remains
discretionary and subject to the availability of local funds. As a result, many
persons with disabilities continue to experience limited access to programs,
services, and institutional support at the community level.

       Moreover, reliable and updated data on persons with disabilities remains
insufficient. The lack of a standardized and comprehensive database at the local
level hampers effective policy formulation, program implementation, and service
delivery. This underscores the need to strengthen institutional mechanisms that
will ensure the systematic collection of data and the effective coordination of
programs for the disability sector.

       According to estimates from the World Health Organization (WHO),
approximately ten percent (10%) of the population lives with some form of
disability. In the Philippines, this translates to millions of individuals who require
accessible public services, inclusive development programs, and effective
representation in local governance.

      To address these, this measure seeks to strengthen the institutional
framework for the protection and promotion of the rights and welfare of persons
with disabilities by amending Republic Act No. 7277, as amended by Republic
Act No. 10070. Specifically, this bill aims to:
      1. Mandate the establishment and operation of Persons with Disability
         Affairs Offices (PDAOs) in all local government units;
      2. Provide clear institutional and funding mechanisms for the
         establishment and operation of PDAOs;
      3. Strengthen the functions, coordination mechanisms, and institutional
         role of PDAOs in the implementation of disability-related laws and
         programs;

                                                                                    2
        4. Establish a uniform and standardized local database of persons with
           disabilities linked to a national registry system;
        5. Enhance capacity building, policy coordination, and public information
           efforts for the disability sector; and,
        6. Ensure a more responsive and inclusive system of local governance that
           advances the rights, welfare, and participation of persons with
           disabilities.

         By institutionalizing the establishment of Persons with Disability Affairs
  Offices in all local government units, the State affirms its commitment to
  inclusive governance and to ensuring that persons with disabilities are
  empowered, protected, and fully integrated into the nation’s social and economic
  life.

      During the 19th Congress, this bill was also filed and was referred to the
  Committee on Local Government.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Section 3. Reference as Persons with Disabilities. – References to the
term “Disabled Persons” on the title and other sections of the same Act are hereby
amended to read as PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES.


      Section 4. Mandatory Implementation of PDAO in All Classes of
Municipalities. –    Within three (3) years after the effectivity of this Act,
appropriate agencies, headed by the DILG, in consultation with the DSWD,
NCDA, and relevant NGOs and Pos, shall review its implementation and
recommend to Congress the need to mandate the establishment of a PDAO in 4th,
5th, and 6th class municipalities.


      Section 5. Role of the Department of Interior and Local Government
(DILG). – The Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall
establish and maintain a monitoring team solely for the purpose of ensuring the
establishment and the effective operations of PDAOs or the appointment of focal

                                                                                 8
persons, as the case may be, in every local government unit with the following
specific functions:


      (a) Ensure the creation, compliance, and implementation of all LGUs in the
         establishment of PDAOs or the appointment of focal persons in their
         respective jurisdictions;
      (b) Ensure that the annual budget of every province, city, and municipality
         includes provisions to fund the establishment and operations of a PDAO
         or the programs and activities of the focal persons in their respective
         jurisdiction;
      (c) Provide technical assistance to LGUs such as, but not limited to,
         capacity     building   and facilitating   a smooth    transition   from
         provincial/city/municipal social welfare and development office into a
         PDAO;
      (d) Establish a minimum standard of policies, plans, and programs aimed
         at promoting the empowerment, rights, and welfare of persons with
         disabilities;
      (e) Incorporate the presence and functionality of PDAO and their programs
         as indicators monitored through the Local Governance Performance
         Management System (LGPMS) and Seal of Good Governance (SGLG);
      (f) Incentivize or recognize the effectiveness and performance of PDAO or
         the focal person, and integrate this as a key factor in assessing the
         performance of local government units through the LGPMS or SGLG
         programs;
      (g) Coordinate with other national agencies regarding concerns of persons
         with disabilities within the LGU; and,
      (h) Initiate and conduct immediate investigation of LGUs that are non-
         compliant with the establishment and effective operation of PDAOs or

                                                                                9
          focal persons, and recommend appropriate action without delay.



      The DILG Secretary is granted the authority to restructure its internal
organizational framework as deemed necessary to efficiently and effectively
fulfill the objectives of this provision.

      Section 6. Creation of a Database Management System. – All PDAOs in
every province, city, and municipality under the supervision of the DILG, and
subject to the requirements of the data privacy laws, are hereby authorized and
required to create, collect, and maintain a uniform and standardized local database
of persons with disabilities linked to a National Registry for Persons with
Disabilities (NRPWD) System. The DILG, DOST, and DOH are mandated to
facilitate the creation and implementation of a uniform local database for persons
with disabilities with categories such as gender, type of disability in accordance
with the International Classification of Functioning, Disability, and Health (ICF),
address, educational attainment, employment status, and other relevant
information.


      Section 7. Establishment of a Uniform Persons with Disability
Identification (PWD ID) System. – The data gathered under the immediately
preceding section shall serve as the basis for establishing a uniform PWD ID
system across the country: Provided, however, That the issuance of PWD ID shall
be based on a medical abstract issued by a licensed medical doctor. Medical
examinations for the purpose of classifying the disability of a PWD shall be free
of charge from both public or private licensed medical doctors.


      Section 8. Appropriations. –          The amount necessary to fund the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act

                                                                                10
(GAA) under the DILG.


      To effectively achieve the objectives of this Act, every province, city, and
municipality shall allocate the necessary funds from their annual budget for
programs, projects, and activities (PPA) for persons with disabilities, which must
be included in their respective annual investment plans (AIPs). Failure to provide
the appropriations and inclusion of PPAs for Persons with Disabilities in the
LGU’s AIP shall render the annual budget inoperative. Any attempt to enforce an
invalid annual budget shall constitute sufficient grounds for the suspension or
dismissal of the official or employee concerned by court order.


      Section 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in coordination with the Department
of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), National Council on Disability
Affairs (NDCA), Department of Health (DOH), Commission on Human Rights
(CHR), and Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall promulgate and issue the
necessary guidelines for the mandatory establishment of PDAOs in all LGUs.


      Section 10. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation
contrary to, or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed,
modified, or amended accordingly.


      Section 11. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or
provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.


      Section 12. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15)
