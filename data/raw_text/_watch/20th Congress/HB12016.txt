EXPLANATORY NOTE

Sa tuwing may lumalabas na balita o usap-usapan tungkol sa isang mataas na
personalidad na matagal na nananatili sa pribadong ospital habang nasa ilalim ng kustodiya,
may isang ordinaryong Pilipino ring naghihintay sa loob ng kulungan para sa parehong
pagsusuri, parehong espesyalista, o parehong agarang lunas. Ang kaniyang hawak ay hindi
pribilehiyo kundi ang pag-asang maririnig ang kaniyang reklamo sa katawan at madadala siya
sa pagamutan kapag kinakailangan. Ang paulit-ulit na pampublikong pag-aalalang ito ay
hindi panawagang ipagkait ang paggamot sa sinuman. Ito ay panawagang tiyakin na ang
karapatang magpagamot ay hindi nagbabago ayon sa pangalan, yaman, impluwensiya, o

katayuan ng taong nasa kustodiya.

The difficult truth is that the question is not whether a person deprived of liberty
remains entitled to medical care. That entitlement is inseparable from the State’s responsibility
once it exercises physical custody over a person. The question is whether the country has a
uniform, medically sound, and accountable system for deciding when inpatient care is
necessary, where it must be delivered, how long it may continue, who reviews it, and how the
public may be assured that a medical transfer is neither delayed by neglect nor extended by
privilege. In the absence of national standards, decisions that should rest on clinical necessity
and documented security arrangements may appear discretionary, inconsistent, or vulnerable
to abuse. This measure answers that institutional problem without converting illness into
misconduct, without treating doctors as adversaries, and without diminishing the authority of

the courts or the lawful rights of persons under detention.

The figures that matter in this proposal are not statistics offered to dramatize a problem
without proof; they are enforceable timelines designed to prevent uncertainty from becoming
either danger or impunity. The bill requires a clear medical-hierarchy pathway, a 24-hour
transfer after stabilization when a private facility has been used for immediate life-saving
care, and a 24-hour, 48-hour, and 24-hour sequence for the responsible review process. It
further requires periodic review every 30 days for continued hospital confinement and prompt
furnishing of the appropriate court with the necessary records. These safeguards build upon
the established national policy that emergency or serious cases must receive appropriate
initial medical treatment and support under Republic Act No. 8344, An Act Penalizing the
Refusal of Hospitals and Medical Clinics to Administer Appropriate Initial Medical
Treatment and Support in Emergency or Serious Cases, Amending for the Purpose Batas

Clinics in Certain Cases" as amended further by Republic Act 10932.' They also remain
consistent with the protection against demands for deposits or advance payment in emergency
treatment under Batas Pambansa Blg. 702, An Act Prohibiting the Demand of Deposits or
Advance Payments for the Confinement or Treatment of Patients in Hospitals and Medical
Clinics in Certain Cases. This bill is a stand-alone measure. It does not amend those laws; it
applies their emergency-treatment duties to persons deprived of liberty and adds the rules on

custody, review, and accountability that those laws do not contain.

The Government has already established vital foundations that this measure seeks to
complete. Public hospitals, custodial institutions, law-enforcement agencies, health
professionals, and emergency-care workers carry the daily burden of protecting life while
preserving lawful custody, often amid limited specialist capacity, congested facilities, and the
difficult continuity needs of chronic illness and mental-health conditions. The bill recognizes
this work and directs the Department of Health, Department of Justice, Department of the
Interior and Local Government, Bureau of Jail Management and Penology, Bureau of
Corrections, Philippine National Police, and National Bureau of Investigation to maintain

designated hospitals and custodial wards. It complements the pending proposal for a unified

AN ACT ESTABLISHING NATIONAL STANDARDS FOR THE MEDICAL
REFERRAL, HOSPITALIZATION, TREATMENT, SECURITY,
DOCUMENTATION, AND OVERSIGHT OF PERSONS DEPRIVED OF LIBERTY,
PROVIDING SAFEGUARDS AGAINST THE ABUSE OF MEDICAL
CONFINEMENT, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Tiyak-Gamot Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article IT, Section 11, Article II,
Section 19(2), and Article XIII, Section 11 of the Constitution, the State shall protect the
health, dignity, and equal treatment of every person deprived of liberty. Medical care shall be
timely, clinically necessary, professionally independent, and free from privilege, favoritism,
discrimination, or improper influence arising from wealth, public office, political status,

notoriety, or personal connection.

Hospitalization shall neither become a means to evade lawful custody nor a reason to
withhold or delay necessary medical treatment. A person deprived of liberty who requires
inpatient care shall ordinarily be confined in a government hospital, in a secure custodial ward
when clinically appropriate. Private-hospital admission may occur only when immediate life-

saving stabilization cannot be safely provided at the nearest capable government hospital.

The distinction between government-hospital confinement and the narrow private-
hospital stabilization exception is based on the availability of necessary immediate clinical
capability, the urgency of the patient's condition, and the least restrictive arrangement
consistent with treatment and public safety. It shall not be based on the identity, social

standing, financial capacity, alleged offense, or political affiliation of the person deprived of

liberty.

In establishing designated custodial wards, referral systems, and review mechanisms,
the concerned agencies shall prioritize geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas,
facilities with limited access to specialists, and detention facilities serving persons with
limited means. No local government unit shall be required to create, staff, equip, or operate a
custodial ward or undertake an obligation under this Act without corresponding national
government funding, an existing funded program, or a voluntary written agreement

identifying the source of support.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Person deprived of liberty refers to a person lawfully arrested, detained,
committed, imprisoned, or otherwise held in the custody of a law enforcement, jail,
correctional, immigration, military, or other government custodial authority, whether

awaiting investigation, trial, judgment, or service of sentence;

(b) Custodial authority refers to the government office, unit, or officer with immediate

legal responsibility for the safekeeping and production of a person deprived of liberty;

(c) Government hospital refers to a national, local, or government-owned and

controlled hospital or medical center authorized to provide inpatient services;

(d) Designated custodial ward refers to a secure area, room, unit, or patient-
management arrangement in a government hospital designated for the treatment and

safekeeping of persons deprived of liberty, consistent with clinical requirements and patient

dignity;

SECTION 4. Right to Referral, Initial Treatment, and Continuity of Care. — A
custodial authority that observes, receives a report of, or is informed by a qualified health
professional of signs or symptoms reasonably indicating an emergency or serious case shall
promptly arrange evaluation and, when indicated, transportation to the nearest capable health
facility. No custodial authority, health facility, or health professional shall delay appropriate
initial medical treatment and support on account of custodial status, security classification,
absence of a court order, inability to pay, incomplete documentation, or the anticipated cost

of care.

The attending health professional shall determine the clinical need for referral,
admission, treatment, discharge, and transfer according to accepted professional standards. A
custodial authority shall not direct, countermand, or improperly influence a clinical
determination. Security concerns may be communicated to the attending health professional,

but shall not be used to deny clinically necessary treatment.

The custodial authority and receiving health facility shall maintain continuity of
treatment, including prescribed medicines, clinically necessary assistive devices, treatment
plans, relevant medical history, and access to appropriate mental-health assessment and
intervention. Continuity measures shall be reasonable, clinically indicated, and subject to

available appropriations and existing funded programs.

The psychiatric assessment, admission, and treatment of a person deprived of liberty
shall comply with Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the "Mental Health Act,"
including its rules on informed consent, involuntary admission, and the confidentiality of

mental-health records.

Stabilization. — Inpatient medical confinement shall be in a government hospital whenever
a capable government hospital can provide the required level of care without unreasonable
risk from delay or transport. The Department of Health, in coordination with the Department
of Justice, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, the Bureau of Jail
Management and Penology, the Bureau of Corrections, the Philippine National Police, the

National Bureau of Investigation, and other concerned custodial authorities, shall establish

The private hospital shall provide only the treatment and stay clinically necessary for

the custodial authority shall transfer the patient to a capable government hospital within
twenty-four (24) hours, unless the attending physician and an independent physician jointly
certify in writing that transfer within that period presents an unreasonable clinical risk. Each

extension shall state the specific clinical basis and shall be reviewed by the Panel.

Nothing in this Act shall prevent emergency treatment at the nearest capable facility
or diminish the duties imposed by this Act and by Batas Pambansa Bilang 702, entitled "An
Act Prohibiting the Demand of Deposits or Advance Payments for the Confinement or

Treatment of Patients in Hospitals and Medical Clinics in Certain Cases".

immediate emergency treatment is required, a request for inpatient medical confinement shall

Before making a recommendation adverse to the person deprived of liberty, the Panel shall
give that person or counsel an opportunity to submit a written statement, unless the delay

would endanger the patient.

For an emergency private-hospital admission, the custodial authority shall notify the
Panel within twenty-four (24) hours. The Panel shall cause an independent clinical assessment
within forty-eight (48) hours after notice, insofar as clinically feasible, and issue its written
recommendation within twenty-four (24) hours after receiving that assessment. The
recommendation shall address clinical necessity, transfer readiness, appropriate security

measures, and the need for continuing inpatient care.

SECTION 8. Security, Dignity, and Patient Confidentiality. —- Security
arrangements in a health facility shall be individually assessed and shall use the least
restrictive means reasonably necessary to prevent escape, harm, interference with treatment,
or unauthorized access. Restraints, constant guarding, visitor restrictions, and physical-
security measures shall be clinically compatible, documented, and periodically reassessed.
No security measure shall be used as punishment or in a manner that unnecessarily obstructs
examination, treatment, sleep, hygiene, communication with counsel, or confidential

consultation with health professionals.

Medical records shall be confidential. Access shall be limited to the patient,
authorized health professionals, the custodial authority to the minimum extent necessary for
safety and custody, counsel as authorized by the patient or law, the Panel, and a court or
lawful oversight body acting within its authority. Any public disclosure concerning a person
deprived of liberty shall omit diagnosis, prognosis, treatment details, and other personally
identifying health information, except as required by law or court order. Where a case
concerns alleged corruption or misuse of public office, an authorized oversight body may
disclose only the minimum nonmedical information necessary to explain the existence and

outcome of the review, while protecting patient confidentiality.

SECTION 9. Records, Notice, and Audit. — The custodial authority and health
facility shall create and preserve a medical confinement record containing: (a) the referral and
admitting assessments; (b) the clinical basis for hospital admission and any private-hospital
exception; (c) transfer, discharge, and return-to-custody orders or certifications; (d) security
measures and their reviews; (e) Panel notices and recommendations; and (f) the dates and

persons receiving required notices.

emergency or serious case, the transfer may be made at once, and the custodial authority shall
inform the court in writing within twenty-four (24) hours, attaching the medical referral or

the certification of the attending physician.

SECTION 11. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — Any person who knowingly and
willfully:

(a) makes or causes the making of a materially false medical certification, referral,

record, or representation to obtain, continue, or conceal improper medical confinement;

(b) conspires, colludes, coerces, or offers an improper benefit to obtain a false

certification or improper medical confinement;

(c) knowingly obstructs a clinically necessary referral, emergency treatment, transfer,
independent assessment, Panel review, or return to lawful detention without a documented

lawful and clinically justified basis, or;

(d) knowingly uses medical confinement primarily to defeat, frustrate, or evade lawful

custody rather than to provide clinically necessary care,

shall, upon conviction, be punished by imprisonment of not less than one (1) year but
not more than six (6) years, or a fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos
P100,000.00) but not more than Five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00), or both, at the

discretion of the court, without prejudice to liability under other applicable laws.

penalty of temporary or perpetual disqualification from public office, as warranted by the
offense. If the offender is a licensed health professional, the judgment shall be furnished to

the appropriate professional regulatory authority for action under applicable rules.

on a medical record or certification that appears regular on its face, shall be a defense to a

prosecution under this Section.

SECTION 12. Funding, Implementation, and Continuing Effect of Existing
Issuances. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged
against the current appropriations of the Department of Health and the concerned custodial
authorities. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Subject to available appropriations,
the Department of Health and concerned custodial authorities shall prioritize public hospitals
and detention facilities in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, facilities with

limited specialist access, and facilities serving persons with limited means.

No local government unit shall incur a mandatory expenditure, personnel requirement,
facility-construction obligation, or operating duty under this Act unless funds, personnel
support, equipment support, or a lawful funding arrangement has first been provided by the
national government or another authorized source. This limitation shall not prevent a local

government unit from voluntarily undertaking a supported activity within its lawful powers.

Existing lawful issuances, protocols, and circulars of the Department of Health and
concerned custodial authorities on emergency referral, hospital security, medical records,
transfer, and continuity of care shall continue in effect to the extent that they are consistent

with this Act, until amended, repealed, or superseded by rules issued under Section 13.

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations; Relationship to Other Laws. —
Within ninety (90 days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in
coordination with the Department of Justice, the Department of the Interior and Local
Government, the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology, the Bureau of Corrections, the
Philippine National Police, the National Bureau of Investigation, and other concerned
agencies, shall promulgate implementing rules and regulations after consultation with
government hospitals, local government units, health professionals, persons with lived

experience of detention, and civil-society organizations.

SECTION 14. Transitory Provisions. — Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity
of this Act, each concerned custodial authority shall identify all persons deprived of liberty
then receiving inpatient care and shall submit to the appropriate Regional Medical
Confinement Review Panel a confidential inventory containing the place of confinement,

admission date, clinical basis for continued stay, and status of any planned transfer.

Persons then confined in private hospitals shall be assessed under Section 7 of this
Act, as soon as clinically feasible. Nothing in this section shall require a transfer that the
attending physician and an independent physician certify would create an unreasonable
clinical risk. The concerned agencies shall prioritize the timely review and supported transfer
of persons in facilities serving geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas and facilities

with limited specialist access.
SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,

regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby

repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
