                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A NATIONAL ADULT IMMUNIZATION
PROGRAM PROVIDING FREE VACCINES AGAINST INFLUENZA,

ADOPTING A LIFE-COURSE IMMUNIZATION STRATEGY, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR


                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Vaccination is one of the most cost-effective and impactful public health
interventions for preventing disease, reducing healthcare expenditures, and saving
lives. While the Philippines has made significant progress in childhood
immunization, adult immunization remains fragmented and limited in scope, leaving
millions of Filipinos vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases.
      Seasonal influenza continues to contribute to substantial morbidity and
productivity loss, particularly among the elderly and individuals with underlying
health conditions. Pneumonia remains a leading cause of mortality nationwide,
disproportionately affecting senior citizens and immunocompromised individuals.
Meanwhile, Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a major cause of cervical cancer and


                                                                                   1
 other HPV-related diseases, which continue to impose a significant burden on the
 healthcare system.


       Despite the availability of safe and effective vaccines, access remains
 inequitable due to financial barriers, limited government coverage, and persistent
 vaccine hesitancy. A life-course approach to immunization—extending protection
 beyond childhood—is essential to achieving universal health care and strengthening
 preventive health systems.

        This measure seeks to institutionalize a National Adult Immunization
 Program that ensures free and equitable access to priority vaccines, beginning with
 high-risk populations and progressively expanding coverage. It integrates national
 and local health systems, strengthens vaccine supply chains, addresses demand-side
 barriers, and ensures accountability through robust monitoring and evaluation
 mechanisms.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Free Adult
Immunization Act.”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them. The State shall adopt a life-course, preventive, and



                                                                                1
primary care-based approach consistent with the Universal Health Care framework,
ensuring equitable, accessible, and gender-responsive immunization services for all.


      Towards this end, the State shall prioritize underserved populations,
strengthen local health systems, and ensure that essential vaccines are made
available free of charge to those most at risk.


      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:


      (a) Adult refers to any Filipino citizen aged eighteen (18) years and above;
      (b) Life-course Immunization refers to the provision of vaccines at different
      stages of life to reduce disease risk across the lifespan;
      (c) Influenza Vaccine refers to vaccines that protect against seasonal influenza
      viruses;
      (d) Pneumococcal Vaccine refers to vaccines that protect against
      pneumococcal diseases, including pneumonia;
      (e) HPV Vaccine refers to vaccines that protect against human papillomavirus
      infections;
      (f) Priority Population refers to individuals at higher risk of severe disease,
      including senior citizens, persons with comorbidities, healthcare workers,
      indigent populations, and other groups as determined by the Department of
      Health (DOH);
      (g) Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDA) refers to
      communities with limited access to basic services due to geographic,
      socioeconomic, or security conditions;




                                                                                     2
      (h) Cold Chain System refers to the system of storage and transport of vaccines
      at recommended temperatures;
      (i) Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI) refers to any untoward
      medical occurrence following immunization;
      (j) National Immunization Registry refers to the centralized digital system for
      recording and tracking vaccination data.


      SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Adult Immunization Program.
There is hereby established a National Adult Immunization Program (NAIP) under
the Department of Health (DOH) to provide free vaccination against influenza,
pneumococcal disease, and human papillomavirus infections (HPV).


      The Program shall be implemented in a phased and prioritized manner,
beginning with high-risk populations and progressively expanding coverage based
on public health needs and available resources.


      SECTION 5. Coverage and Priority Beneficiaries – The Program shall adopt
a tiered prioritization system, as follows:


      (a) Priority Tier 1 (Mandatory Coverage). – This tier shall include senior
      citizens, persons with comorbidities, healthcare workers and frontline
      personnel, indigent populations, and beneficiaries of government social
      protection programs;




                                                                                   3
      (b) Priority Tier 2 (Expansion Phase). – This tier shall include essential
      workers, including teachers and uniformed personnel, and individuals in
      congregate settings; and


      (c) Priority Tier 3. – This tier shall include all other adults, subject to further
      program expansion and resource availability.


      The Department of Health (DOH) shall periodically update the prioritization
guidelines based on prevailing epidemiological data and available resources.


      SECTION 6. Program Components. – The Department of Health (DOH), in
coordination with Local Government Units (LGUs) and other stakeholders, shall
implement the following:


      (a) Vaccine Procurement and Supply Management;
      (b) Cold Chain Strengthening, especially in GIDA areas;
      (c) Establishment of Fixed and Mobile Vaccination Sites;
      (d) Deployment and Training of Health Workforce, including nurses,
      midwives, and pharmacists;
      (e) Adverse Events Monitoring and Response System;
      (f) National Immunization Registry, ensuring interoperability with existing
      health information systems;
      (g) Public Health Promotion and Demand Generation Campaigns to address
      vaccine hesitancy and misinformation;
      (h) Workplace and Community-Based Vaccination Programs;




                                                                                       4
      (i) Integration with Primary Care Services under the Universal Health Care
      framework.


      SECTION 7. Role of Local Government Units (LGU) – The LGUs shall be
primarily responsible for last-mile delivery and shall facilitate the identification and
prioritization of eligible beneficiaries and provide logistical, operational and
community engagement support. This includes mobilizing local health workers and
barangay health workers.


      The LGU shall ensure to accomplished the annual vaccination targets by the
DOH and submit regular reports to the DOH.


      SECTION 8. Public-Private Partnership. — The DOH may accredit private
healthcare providers, pharmacies, workplaces, and civil society organizations to
participate in the Program, subject to standards and guidelines.


      SECTION 9: Special Provisions on HPV Vaccinations. – The Program shall
adopt a gender-responsive, life-course approach to HPV vaccination, ensuring
equitable access for eligible adults, particularly women at risk of cervical cancer and
other HPV-related diseases. Catch-up vaccination shall be provided to eligible
individuals beyond adolescence, based on prevailing clinical guidelines, and may
include both women and men to reduce overall transmission and disease burden. The
DOH shall integrate HPV vaccination with existing sexual and reproductive health
services, cancer prevention programs, and community-based screening initiatives to
maximize impact.




                                                                                      5
      SECTION 10. Vaccine Supply Security. — The State shall ensure a stable,
sufficient, and timely supply of vaccines through strategic procurement and supply
chain management mechanisms. The DOH shall adopt multi-year procurement
planning, pooled purchasing strategies, and framework agreements to secure cost-
effective and uninterrupted vaccine supply, while maintaining quality and safety
standards.


      SECTION 11. Funding. – The implementation of this Act shall be funded
through annual appropriations under the General Appropriations Act, with priority
allocation for high-risk populations and underserved areas. Funding shall be
programmed on a multi-year basis to ensure continuity, scalability, and sustainability
of the Program.


      Additional funding sources may include a designated portion of sin tax
revenues, PhilHealth reimbursements for vaccine administration in accredited
facilities, official development assistance, and grants or donations from local and
international partners. The DOH shall ensure efficient, transparent, and accountable
utilization of funds, subject to existing auditing and budgeting laws.


      SECTION 12. Monitoring, Evaluation and Transparency. – The DOH shall
establish a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system to assess program
performance, coverage, equity, and health outcomes. This shall include the
development of a real-time, interoperable National Immunization Registry and a
publicly accessible dashboard presenting disaggregated data by age, sex, geographic
location, and priority group.




                                                                                    6
      Annual reports shall be submitted to Congress detailing program
implementation, fund utilization, and key performance indicators. The DOH may
engage independent institutions, including academic and research organizations, to
conduct periodic evaluations to ensure accountability, inform policy adjustments,
and promote transparency in program delivery.


      SECTION 13. Data Privacy and Protection. – All personal and health-
related data collected under this Act shall be processed, stored, and shared in
accordance with applicable data privacy laws, rules, and regulations. The DOH shall
implement appropriate organizational, technical, and physical safeguards to ensure
the confidentiality, integrity, and security of all information within the National
Immunization Registry and related systems.


      Data sharing among authorized government agencies and accredited partners
shall be limited to legitimate public health purposes and shall adhere to principles of
proportionality, necessity, and informed consent, where applicable.


      SECTION 14. Prohibited Acts and Penalties – The following acts are
prohibited:


      (a) Misuse or misappropriation of program funds;
      (b) Unauthorized sale or distribution of free vaccines;
      (c) Falsification of vaccination records;
      (d) Gross negligence resulting in vaccine wastage, including failure to comply
      with established storage, handling, transport, inventory, or administration
      protocols issued by the Department of Health (DOH).


                                                                                     7
      Any person or entity found guilty of such acts shall be subject to appropriate
administrative, civil, and criminal liabilities under existing laws, without prejudice
to the imposition of additional sanctions such as suspension or revocation of
licenses, disqualification from public office or government contracts, and restitution
of misused public funds.


      SECTION 15. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR) – Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with relevant
government agencies, LGUs, professional associations, and civil society
organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.


      SECTION 16. Congressional Oversight. – A Joint Congressional Oversight
Committee is hereby constituted to monitor and evaluate the implementation of this
Act, composed of members from the relevant committees of both Houses of
Congress. The Committee shall review program performance, funding utilization,
and policy outcomes, and may recommend legislative or administrative measures to
strengthen implementation.


      The DOH and other implementing agencies shall submit periodic reports and
provide access to relevant data and documents as may be required by the Oversight
Committee in the exercise of its functions.

      SECTION 12. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


      SECTION 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
