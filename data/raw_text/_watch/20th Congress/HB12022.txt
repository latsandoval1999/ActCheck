EXPLANATORY NOTE

Para sa batang may cancer, ang paghihintay ay may sariling tunog at bigat: ang
kaluskos ng mga papeles sa referral desk, ang mahinang tawag ng magulang sa telepono
upang humanap ng masasakyan, ang amoy-singaw ng ward, at ang tahimik na tanong kung
makararating ba sila sa susunod na paggamot. Hindi dapat ang pamasahe, tutuluyan,
kakulangan ng malinaw na referral, o isang dokumentong hindi agad maiproseso ang

magpasya kung matatapos ng isang bata ang kaniyang gamutan.

The essential truth is that the interruption of pediatric cancer care often occurs outside
the treatment room. A diagnosis may be made, a protocol may be available, and a benefit may
exist, yet a child can still lose the continuity that makes treatment effective when the family
cannot navigate a referral, reach the next facility, complete administrative requirements, or
sustain the repeated travel required by care. This measure therefore does not create a separate
and duplicative cancer-benefit structure. It strengthens the last-mile delivery of existing
public protection by establishing a National Pediatric Cancer Care Continuity Program
focused on timely diagnosis, navigated referral, uninterrupted treatment, and essential

nonmedical support.

writing in Cancer Control (2015), the crude rate for childhood cancer in the Philippines, based

on the Manila and Rizal cancer registries, is 103 annual new cases per million children, which

The National Integrated Cancer Control Program, established under Republic Act No.
11215, otherwise known as the “National Integrated Cancer Control Act,” already
contemplates multisectoral coordination, patient and family engagement, multidisciplinary
care, patient navigation, and survivors’ follow-up care. This bill gives those principles a
defined pediatric continuity mechanism: linked referral networks, trained navigators,
coordinated benefit access, early-diagnosis and awareness support, regional capacity-
building, and qualified support for transport and temporary lodging. It is designed to help a
child move through the system already established by law, rather than require a frightened

family to assemble that system alone during illness.

The strongest objection to any additional program is legitimate: public funds must not
be scattered across parallel schemes, and assistance must be accountable, targeted, and
coordinated with PhilHealth and cancer-assistance mechanisms. This measure answers that
concern precisely by making continuity the organizing function. Reported challenges in the
uptake of the Z Benefit Package include doctors in participating hospitals not being aware
that their institution is PhilHealth-accredited and therefore not referring eligible patients, as
well as treatment abandonment and administrative barriers. The antagonist is not the
physician, nurse, social worker, or hospital staff, each of whom works within demanding
constraints. The antagonist is the missing system that leaves eligibility undiscovered, referrals
uncompleted, and a medically necessary course of care broken by avoidable logistical

hardship.

Kapag gumana ang batas na ito, mararamdaman ng isang magulang ang isang payak
na ginhawa: hindi na siya nag-iisa sa pagitan ng diagnosis at paggamot. May taong tutulong
magpaliwanag kung saan pupunta, may malinaw na referral sa susunod na ospital, at may
tulong sa biyahe o pansamantalang matutuluyan para hindi kailangang pumili ang pamilya sa
pagitan ng pamasahe at ng pagbalik ng bata para sa gamutan. Hindi nito binabawasan ang
hirap ng cancer, ngunit binabawasan nito ang mga hadlang na likha ng sistema, at ibinabalik

nito ang oras, direksiyon, at dangal sa pamilya.

Time is the urgent element. Childhood cancer treatment is not secured by a single
hospital admission or one approved request; it depends on timely diagnosis, repeated
treatment, follow-up, and the ability to return when the clinical schedule requires it. Every

delayed referral, missed appointment, unresolved benefit claim, or unaffordable trip can

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL PEDIATRIC CANCER CARE
CONTINUITY PROGRAM TO ENSURE TIMELY DIAGNOSIS, NAVIGATED
REFERRALS, UNINTERRUPTED TREATMENT, AND ESSENTIAL
NONMEDICAL SUPPORT FOR CHILDREN WITH CANCER, STRENGTHENING
THE DELIVERY OF EXISTING PHILHEALTH AND CANCER ASSISTANCE
BENEFITS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11215,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “NATIONAL INTEGRATED CANCER
CONTROL ACT,” AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Kumpleto-Lunas Act".

SECTION 2. Amendment of the National Integrated Cancer Control Act. —- A new
Section 22-A is hereby inserted after Section 22 of Republic Act No. 11215, otherwise known

as the “National Integrated Cancer Control Act,” to read as follows:

"SECTION 22-A. National Pediatric Cancer Care Continuity Program. — There is
hereby established, under the National Integrated Cancer Control Program, a National
Pediatric Cancer Care Continuity Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program, to support
the timely diagnosis, referral, treatment completion, follow-up care, and palliative care of

children with cancer.

SECTION 3. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article II, Section 15, Article XIII,
Section 11, and Article XV, Section 3(2) of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State to
ensure that no child with cancer loses continuity of care because a parent or guardian cannot
obtain a referral, complete benefit requirements, travel to a treatment facility, secure
temporary lodging during medically necessary treatment, or understand the next required step

in care.

The State recognizes that pediatric cancer care requires coordinated action from the
first suspicion of cancer through diagnosis, treatment, follow-up, survivorship care, or
palliative care. The State shall prioritize the removal of practical barriers that interrupt
medically indicated care while preserving clinical judgment, patient choice, data privacy, and

the integrity of existing health benefits.

Priority under this Act shall be given to children residing in geographically isolated
and disadvantaged areas, children whose household lacks the means to meet documented
transport or temporary lodging costs necessary for scheduled care, and children whose
treating team identifies a documented risk of treatment interruption. Such priority shal! not

be used to deny assistance to any otherwise qualified child.

SECTION 4. Pediatric Cancer Care Networks and Navigation. — The following
provisions are hereby inserted as subsections of the new Section 22-A of Republic Act No.

11215:

"(a) The Department of Health, hereinafter referred to as the Department, shall
organize pediatric cancer care networks composed of designated referral facilities,
government hospitals, local health offices that voluntarily participate, accredited health care

providers, social workers, patient navigators, and other appropriate partners.

"(b) Each network shall identify referral pathways for suspected or confirmed
pediatric cancer cases, including pathways for diagnostic workup, pathology, imaging,
treatment planning, chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, supportive care, palliative care,

follow-up, and emergency referral, as clinically applicable.

"(c) The Department shall designate or support patient navigators in participating
referral facilities, subject to available appropriations. A navigator shall assist the parent or
guardian, with informed consent, in understanding the referral pathway, scheduling medically
indicated services, identifying available benefits and assistance, preparing documentary

requirements, and coordinating with the receiving facility.

"(d) A navigator shall provide an understandable written or electronic care itinerary,
where feasible, identifying the receiving facility, the next scheduled service, available contact
channels, and the documents required for benefit or assistance applications. The navigator
shall not make clinical decisions, condition care on the acceptance of assistance, or disclose
patient information except as authorized by Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other applicable laws, and by the parent or guardian when

consent is required.

"(e) A referring facility or health worker that follows the applicable referral protocol
shall not be held administratively accountable for a missed appointment, delayed treatment,
or treatment interruption caused by unavailable transport, unavailable lodging, unavailable
medicine or equipment, lack of receiving-facility capacity, delayed benefit processing, or any
cause outside its reasonable control. The applicable network shall record the cause of

interruption and coordinate the earliest practicable next step."

SECTION 7. Relationship to Other Laws. — This Act completes and operationalizes
the patient navigation, family engagement, coordination, capacity development, and funding-
assistance objectives of Republic Act No. 11215. Nothing in this Act shall diminish any right,

benefit, program, fund, authority, or protection established under the said Act.

Existing lawful issuances, referral protocols, benefit rules, and assistance procedures
consistent with this Act shall continue to have effect until amended, repealed, or superseded
by a rule or issuance that affords at least equivalent protection for continuity of pediatric

cancer Care.

SECTION 8. Funding and Local Government Participation. — The amount necessary
for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
of the Department and the funds lawfully available under the National Integrated Cancer
Control Program. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued

implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

No local government unit shall be required to establish, finance, staff, or operate a
service under this Act without a corresponding appropriation, voluntary agreement, or other
lawful source of support. Local government units may participate in referral, navigation,

transport, lodging, and awareness activities using funds lawfully available for such purpose.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department shall, in coordination with the Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation, concerned public agencies, patient representatives, parents or
guardians of children with cancer, health professionals, and other stakeholders, promulgate

the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

The absence or delay of the implementing rules and regulations shall not suspend

rights and duties that are sufficiently defined by this Act.

SECTION 10. Report to Congress. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days after
the end of each calendar year, the Department shall submit to the Senate and the House of
Representatives an annual report on the implementation of this Act. The report shall include
aggregate information on network coverage, referrals, navigation, nonmedical support,
documented causes of treatment interruption, capacity-building activities, and
recommendations for improving continuity of care. No personally identifiable patient

information shall be included in the report.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force

and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby

repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its

publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
