       In current medical practice in the Philippines, the determination of who may
provide consent on behalf of a patient who lacks decision-making capacity is often guided
by the customary application of the “next-of-kin” principle. However, this custom does
not have a clear statutory basis under existing laws, including the Family Code and the
Civil Code of the Philippines. While informal practices may sometimes accommodate
companions or partners, such recognition is inconsistent and dependent on institutional
discretion rather than guaranteed rights.

        At present, the closest reference to “next-of-kin” in the context of healthcare
decision-making may be found in the Code of Ethics of the Philippine Medical Association
(PMA), which provides that consent may be obtained from the patient’s “next-of-kin or
their legally authorized representative.”

        This gap in the law has led to delays in treatment, ethical uncertainty among
healthcare providers, and, in some cases, preventable harm to patients. It
disproportionately affects individuals in nontraditional or underserved family
arrangements, including unmarried partners, solo parents, and those estranged from
their biological families, whose chosen caregivers are frequently excluded from
participating in critical medical decisions. The absence of a legally recognized mechanism
to designate a trusted decision-maker does not uphold the fundamental principle of
patient autonomy and fails to reflect the evolving realities of Filipino family structures.

        The Right to Care Act, seeks to address these longstanding gaps by establishing a
legal framework for the recognition of Healthcare Proxies. Under this Act, any qualified
adult may designate a Healthcare Agent of their choosing, who shall be authorized to
make healthcare decisions on their behalf in the event of incapacity. The authority
granted to the Healthcare Agent shall be limited exclusively to medical and healthcare-
related decisions and shall not extend to matters involving property, inheritance, or civil
status.

        The proposed measure seeks to promote timely and appropriate medical
intervention while addressing the uncertainty and disputes that often arise in the absence
of a clearly recognized decision-maker. It provides a more coherent legal basis for
healthcare providers in the exercise of their duties and, at the same time, upholds the
individual’s right to designate a trusted person to act on their behalf during periods of
incapacity. In doing so, the measure supports the development of a more responsive,
inclusive, and patient-centered healthcare system.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
     RECOGNIZING THE RIGHT OF PATIENTS TO DESIGNATE A HEALTHCARE
     AGENT THROUGH A HEALTHCARE PROXY, PROMOTING ADVANCE CARE
     PLANNING, AND PROVIDING FOR SUPPORTED HEALTHCARE DECISION-
     MAKING IN THE HEALTHCARE SYSTEM

           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Right to Care Act”.
 2
 3          Sec 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes health as a fundamental human
 4   right and affirms the dignity, autonomy, and well-being of every person in the delivery of
 5   healthcare services.
 6
 7        Pursuant to Article II, Section 15 of the Constitution, the State shall protect and
 8   promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
 9
10         Towards this end, the State shall:
11
12         a)    Adopt a rights-based approach to healthcare, recognizing the right of every
13               person to participate in decisions affecting the person’s health and medical
14               care;
15
16         b)    Promote advance care planning by enabling individuals to designate a
17               Healthcare Agent through a Healthcare Proxy;
18
19         c)    Strengthen universal access to healthcare services, consistent with the
20               Universal Health Care Act and the country’s commitments to the Sustainable
21               Development Goals; and
 1         d)    Promote supported decision-making that respects the rights, will, and
 2               preferences of individuals.
 3
 4          And finally, the recognition of Healthcare Agents and Healthcare Proxies shall be
 5   implemented in an inclusive, non-discriminatory, and person-centered manner within the
 6   healthcare system.
 7
 8         Sec 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act:
 9
10         a)    Advance Directive refers to a written or recorded instruction indicating a
11               person’s preferences concerning the provision, withholding, or withdrawal of
12               Healthcare under specified conditions;
13
14         b)    Healthcare refers to any care, treatment, service, or procedure undertaken
15               to maintain, diagnose, monitor, or otherwise affect a person’s physical or
16               mental condition;
17         c)    Healthcare Agent refers to a natural person at least twenty-one (21) years
18               of age designated by a Principal through a Healthcare Proxy to make
19               Healthcare Decisions on the Principal’s behalf;
20
21         d)    Healthcare Decision refers to any decision to consent to, refuse, or withdraw
22               consent to the provision of Healthcare on behalf of the Principal;
23
24         e)    Healthcare Provider refers to any individual, facility, or institution licensed,
25               accredited, or otherwise authorized by law to provide Healthcare;
26
27         f)    Healthcare Proxy refers to a written instrument executed by a Principal
28               designating a Healthcare Agent to make Healthcare Decisions on the
29               Principal’s behalf;
30
31         g)    Incapacity refers to a temporary or permanent loss of decision-making
32               capacity characterized by the inability of a person to understand information
33               concerning the person’s health condition; understand the consequences of
34               decisions or actions on the person’s life or health or on the life or health of
35               others; understand information about the nature of a proposed treatment,
36               including its methodology, effects, and possible side effects; or communicate
37               consent to treatment or hospitalization or information regarding the person’s
38               own condition or as determined by the healthcare provider;
39
40         h)    Next of Kin refers to the closest living relative of the Principal in the following
41               order of priority, namely, spouse, adult child, parent, adult sibling,
42               grandparent, or other relative as determined under applicable laws;
43
44         i)    Principal refers to a natural person who designates a Healthcare Agent
45               through a Healthcare Proxy; and
 1
 2         j)     Supported Healthcare Decision-Making refers to assistance provided by
 3                persons chosen by an individual to help the individual understand, make, or
 4                communicate decisions while respecting the individual’s rights, will, and
 5                preferences.
 6
 7         Sec 4. Right to Designate a Healthcare Agent. – Every person may designate a
 8   Healthcare Agent through a Healthcare Proxy to make Healthcare Decisions on the
 9   person’s behalf in the event of incapacity.
10
11          The designation of Healthcare Agent shall be valid if it is freely and voluntarily
12   made by the Principal; executed without fraud, coercion, undue influence or
13   misrepresentation; based on the Principal’s freedom of choice; and, reduced into a
14   Healthcare Proxy: Provided, that the Principal may modify, replace, or revoke the
15   Healthcare Proxy at any time and such designation shall not constitute a waiver of the
16   Principal’s right to make Healthcare Decisions whenever capable of doing so.
17
18         In the absence of a Healthcare Proxy, Healthcare Decisions may be made by the
19   Next of Kin and such authority shall only apply when no Healthcare Agent has been
20   designated or when the designated Healthcare Agent is unavailable.
21
22          Sec 5. Healthcare Proxy Form and Acceptance. – A Healthcare Proxy may be
23   executed in any written form at any given time and shall indicate the name of the Principal
24   and the Healthcare Agent; the authority of the Healthcare Agent to make Healthcare
25   Decisions; and acceptance by the Healthcare Agent: Provided, that the acceptance may
26   be indicated in the Healthcare Proxy or made at the point of care when the Healthcare
27   Agent is called upon to act.
28
29           Sec 6. Authority and Standards of the Healthcare Agent. – Subject to any
30   limitations expressly provided in the Healthcare Proxy or in any Advance Directive
31   executed by the Principal, the Healthcare Agent shall have the authority to make
32   Healthcare Decisions on behalf of the Principal within healthcare settings.
33
34         The Healthcare Agent shall have the right to receive relevant medical information
35   and records necessary to make informed Healthcare Decisions. Disclosure of such
36   information shall be deemed authorized for purposes of this Act.
37
38          Healthcare Decisions made by the Healthcare Agent shall be exercised while
39   physically present, and shall be made in accordance with the known wishes, will, and
40   preferences of the Principal, or, in the absence thereof, in the best interests of the
41   Principal.
42
43         In the event of the death of the Principal, the Healthcare Agent may likewise make
44   the necessary arrangements for the funeral and burial of the Principal.
45
 1           Healthcare Decisions made by a duly designated Healthcare Agent shall have
 2   priority over decisions made by any other person, including members of the Principal’s
 3   family, except where the Principal has expressly stated a contrary wish. In cases of
 4   disagreement among family members or other persons, the decision of the Healthcare
 5   Agent shall prevail.
 6
 7         A Healthcare Agent shall be free from liability for Healthcare Decisions made in
 8   good faith and in accordance with this Act.
 9
10           Where neither the Healthcare Agent nor the Next of Kin is available or able to
11   decide on behalf of the Principal, the Healthcare Provider may determine the appropriate
12   course of action in accordance with applicable laws, clinical practice guidelines, and
13   institutional protocols.
14
15           Nothing in this Act shall prevent a Healthcare Provider from seeking ethical
16   consultation, mediation, or judicial relief in cases involving substantial uncertainty,
17   conflict, or allegations of abuse or bad faith in the exercise of authority by the Healthcare
18   Agent.
19
20          Sec 7. Commencement and Termination of Authority. – The authority of the
21   Healthcare Agent shall commence upon a determination of Incapacity by a Healthcare
22   Provider and shall cease once the Principal regains decision-making capacity and upon
23   the revocation of the healthcare proxy.
24
25          The Principal may revoke the Healthcare Proxy by notifying a Healthcare Provider
26   orally or in writing; executing a subsequent Healthcare Proxy; or by any act clearly
27   indicating the intent to revoke the designation.
28
29          Sec 8. Obligations of Healthcare Providers. – Healthcare Providers shall recognize
30   a valid Healthcare Proxy; determine the presence or absence of incapacity in accordance
31   with existing laws and clinical guidelines; make reasonable efforts to notify the Healthcare
32   Agent when the Principal is unable to make Healthcare Decisions; and comply in good
33   faith with Healthcare Decisions made by the Healthcare Agent.
34
35          Healthcare Providers shall not be held civilly, criminally, or administratively liable
36   for actions taken in good faith reliance on Healthcare Decisions made by a Healthcare
37   Agent under this Act.

24          Sec 10. Liability of Healthcare Cost. – Healthcare provided pursuant to decisions
25   made by a Healthcare Agent shall be treated as if such healthcare had been provided
26   pursuant to the decision of the Principal. The cost incurred shall remain the obligation of
27   the Principal, and unpaid healthcare costs shall form part of the obligations recoverable
28   from the Principal, subject to applicable laws. The Healthcare Agent shall not be
29   personally liable for any costs incurred in the provision of healthcare under this Act.
30
31          Subject to applicable social and financial protection laws, programs, and services,
32   the Healthcare Agent may apply for government assistance on behalf of the Principal:
33   Provided, That the Healthcare Agent shall not derive any financial benefit therefrom.
34
35           Sec 11. Non-Discrimination and Non-Limitation of Rights. – No person or entity
36   shall require, deny, or prohibit the execution of a Healthcare Proxy as a condition for the
37   provision of healthcare or related services, including but not limited to services provided
38   by health management organizations, insurance companies, or other healthcare
39   institutions.
40
41          Nothing in this Act shall create, expand, diminish, impair, or supersede any existing
42   authority of a Principal to make or express decisions, wishes, or instructions regarding
43   Healthcare, whether or not expressed in a Healthcare Proxy.
44
45         Nothing in here shall be construed to permit the Healthcare Agent to consent to
46   any act or omission to which the Principal could not consent under the law.
