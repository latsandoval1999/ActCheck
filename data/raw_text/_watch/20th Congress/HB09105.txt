                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Preamble is an introductory statement or introductory part of a constitution
that usually states the reasons for the intent of the law. The Preamble of the Philippine
Constitution for example is a statement that outlines the aspirations of the Filipinos, it
aims to establish a government that embodies the ideals and aspirations of the Filipino
people. Certainly, marriage in Filipino culture is deeply rooted in tradition, family ties,
and community which is why no less than the 1987 Constitution under Article XV Section
1 and 2 states that
        The State recognizes the Filipino family as the foundation of the nation. Accordingly, it
shall strengthen its solidarity and actively promote its total development, and marriage, as an
inviolable social institution, is the foundation of the family and shall be protected by the State.

       Moreover, the Constitution of the Philippines, the highest law of the land regards
marriage as an inviolable social institution making the Philippines one of the few, if not
the only, nation to do so. For instance, The Italian Constitution although it recognizes the
family as a fundamental unit of society, does not explicitly state that the family or
marriage is inviolable. Article 29 of the Italian Constitution which states that,
       The Republic recognises the rights of the family as a natural society founded on marriage
and Marriage is based on the moral and legal equality of the spouses within the limits laid down
by law to guarantee the unity of the family.

       Also, in the Spanish Constitution, although it recognizes the family as the basic
social institution, it never describes marriage as inviolable. Section 39 of the Spanish
Constitution, it is stated that
      The public authorities ensure social, economic and legal protection of the family.

These are the few reasons why in contrast to our jurisdiction, they have many options or
avenues to dissolve marriages solemnized under their Family and Marriage Laws.

       It is noteworthy that in interpreting our 1987 Constitution, the first rule is verba
legis (Plain meaning rule) meaning, the words of the Constitution must be given their
ordinary meaning except where technical terms are employed. The Constitution is not a
lawyer’s document, its language as much as possible should be understood in the sense
they have in common use. Words should mean what they mean. The marriage that is
enshrined in our Philippine Constitution is unique because of its inviolable character.

       Hence, the State as mandated by the Constitution must not introduce further
means to dissolve a marriage, a key that may unlock and could open up Pandora’s box
of problems destroying the foundation of the family that runs counter to the fundamental
law. No less than the Family Code has already established modes for the dissolution of
marriage. Accordingly, this proposed legislation will still uphold the inviolability of
marriage under our Philippine Constitution while also adding new grounds for the
dissolution of marriage beyond what is currently available.



      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in session
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Expanded Dissolution
of Marriage Act”

      SEC. 2 Amendments. – Articles 45, 55, and 57 of Executive No. 209, or the Family
Code of the Philippines, is hereby amended to read as follows:

       Art. 45. A marriage may be annulled for any of the following causes, existing at
       the time of the marriage:

       (1)    That the party in whose behalf it is sought to have the marriage annulled
              was eighteen years of age or over but below twenty-one, and the marriage
              was solemnized without the consent of the parents, guardian, or person
              having substitute parental authority over the party, in that order, unless
              after attaining the age of twenty-one, such party freely cohabited with the
              other and both lived together as husband and wife;

       (2)    That either party was of unsound mind, unless such party after coming to
              reason, freely cohabited with the other as husband and wife;




                                            Page 3 of 7
(3)    That the consent of either party was obtained by fraud, unless such party
       afterward, with full knowledge of the facts constituting the fraud, freely
       cohabited with the other as husband and wife;

(4)    That the consent of either party was obtained by force, intimidation, or
       undue influence unless the same having disappeared or ceased, such party
       thereafter freely cohabited with the other as husband and wife;

(5)    That either party was physically incapable of consummating the marriage
       with the other, and such incapacity continues and appears to be incurable;

(6)    That either party was afflicted with a sexually-transmissible disease found
       to be serious and appears to be incurable;

       PROVIDED, THAT GROUNDS ABOVE MENTIONED IN
       PARAGRAPHS 2, 5, AND 6 HEREIN EXISTED EITHER AT THE TIME
       OF THE MARRIAGE OR APPEARED AFTER THE MARRIAGE;

(7)    PSYCHOLOGICAL INCAPACITY OF EITHER SPOUSE AS PROVIDED
       FOR IN ARTICLE 36 OF THIS CODE, WHETHER OR NOT THE
       INCAPACITY EXISTED AT THE TIME OF MARRIAGE OR APPEARS
       AFTER THE MARRIAGE;

(8)    HOMOSEXUALITY AND LESBIANISM OF THE RESPONDENT OR
       WHEN ONE OF THE SPOUSES UNDERGOES A SEX REASSIGNMENT
       SURGERY OR TRANSITION FROM ONE SEX TO ANOTHER;

(9)    REPEATED DOMESTIC, MARITAL ABUSE OR AGGRESSIVE
       BEHAVIOR WITHIN THE HOME WHICH MAY BE COMMITTED
       THROUGH PHYSICAL VIOLENCE, PSYCHOLOGICAL AND
       EMOTIONAL VIOLENCE, SEXUAL VIOLENCE, OR ECONOMIC
       ABUSE;

(10)   SERIOUS PHYSICAL VIOLENCE OR GROSSLY ABUSIVE CONDUCT
       DIRECTED AGAINST THE PETITIONER, A COMMON CHILD, OR
       CHILD OF THE PETITIONER WHETHER BY NATURE OR BY
       ADOPTION;

(11)   SERIOUS PHYSICAL VIOLENCE OR MORAL PRESSURE TO
       COMPEL THE PETITIONER TO CHANGE RELIGIOUS OR
       POLITICAL AFFILIATION;

(12)   ATTEMPT OF RESPONDENT TO CORRUPT OR INDUCE THE
       PETITIONER, A COMMON CHILD, OR A CHILD OF THE

       PETITIONER, TO ENGAGE IN PROSTITUTION, GAMBLING, OR

       PARDONED;

Article 55. A petition for legal separation may be filed on any of the following
       grounds:

(1)    SERIOUS physical violence or grossly abusive conduct directed against the
       petitioner, a common child, or a child of the petitioner WHETHER BY
       NATURE OR BY ADOPTION;

(2)    SERIOUS Physical violence or moral pressure to compel the petitioner to
       change religious or political affiliation;

(3)    Attempt of respondent to corrupt or induce the petitioner, a common child,

SEC. 3. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is declared
      unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected
      thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, memorandum
      orders, memorandum circulars, administrative orders, ordinances, or parts
      thereof, which are inconsistent with the right to adequate food and the
      provisions of this Act, are hereby deemed repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
      in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
