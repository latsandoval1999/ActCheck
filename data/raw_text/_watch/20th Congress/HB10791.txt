                                   AN ACT
                   TO ESTABLISH MAGNA CARTA FOR BARANGAYS

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The barangay serves as the bedrock of local governance and the most fundamental
political unit in the Republic of the Philippines. Currently, there are 42,001 barangays across the
country, each functioning as the critical interface between the State and its citizens. Enshrined
in the 1987 Constitution and operationalized by Republic Act No. 7160 or the Local Government
Code (LGC) of 1991, these territorial subdivisions are guaranteed genuine local autonomy. This
constitutional mandate empowers them to transform into self-reliant communities and
indispensable partners in national progress. Consistently, Philippine jurisprudence has reinforced
that meaningful decentralization must reach the barangay level to ensure that grassroots
governance remains dynamic and effective.

        Functioning as the government's primary planning and executing body at the community
level, the barangay acts as the frontline for public service delivery, dispute resolution, and
participatory governance. The sheer scale of this civic engagement was evident during the 2023
Barangay and Sangguniang Kabataan Elections (BSKE), where over 828,000 candidates vied
for leadership posts to represent their communities.1 It is within this foundational tier that the
collective voice of the people is formalized into actionable initiatives. Under the bottom-up
development model established by the LGC, barangay development plans serve as the crucial
building blocks for broader municipal, provincial, and national socio-economic strategies.

        Despite their indispensable role in nation-building, barangay officials and workers are
plagued by a systemic lack of institutional support, limited social protection, and severe
economic insecurity. Under current provisions, such as DBM Local Budget Circular No. 63,
minimum honoraria remain starkly disproportionate to the workload: set at a baseline of just PHP
1,000 monthly for the Punong Barangay and PHP 600 for Sangguniang Barangay members,
though some local government units augment this based on available local funds.2 This meager
compensation persists even as their responsibilities multiply. During the COVID-19 pandemic
and amidst frequent natural disasters, barangay officials and Barangay Health Workers (BHWs)
functioned as the ultimate frontliners, managing contact tracing, enforcing quarantines,
distributing relief goods, and maintaining peace and order, often without hazard pay,

         The push for a comprehensive Magna Carta for Barangay Officials is a decades-old
legislative battle. The earliest attempt dates back to 1987 with House Bill No. 6925, followed by
a broader Senate measure in 2007 that sought to elevate barangay officials to regular government
personnel status, entitling them to fixed salaries, insurance, retirement security, and capacity-
building resources. For years, these measures stagnated at the committee level. A notable
breakthrough occurred recently in August 2026, when the Senate successfully approved on the
third reading the Magna Carta for Barangay Health Workers, guaranteeing at least PHP 3,000 in
monthly benefits, hazard allowances, and free legal assistance.3 This recent legislative victory
sets a strong precedent for passing the broader Magna Carta covering all barangay officials.

         This proposed measure seeks to permanently close this long-standing legislative gap. It
reaffirms the State's constitutional duty to promote the general welfare of its citizens by first
elevating the economic and social dignity of its grassroots public servants. By officially
institutionalizing financial support, professionalizing the barangay workforce, and ensuring local
communities have the basic facilities for a dignified life, this bill transforms barangays into true
engines of inclusive and sustainable national development.

        The enactment of this Magna Carta is no longer just timely; it is a moral imperative. It
stands as a long-overdue validation of the sacrifices made by our community leaders and a
definitive step toward fulfilling the constitutional promise of genuine, robust local autonomy.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 3   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta for Barangays.”
 4   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to uphold genuine and meaningful
 5   local autonomy, recognize the barangay as the basic political unit and primary planning and
 6   implementing unit of government at the community level, and promote the general welfare and
 7   quality of life of barangay residents through participatory governance and amicable dispute
 8   settlement. The State shall ensure a responsive system of decentralization through the
 9   progressive transfer of powers, authority, responsibilities, and resources, raise the economic and
10   social status of barangay officials and personnel through just compensation, appropriate benefits,
11   and institutional support, and provide every barangay with the basic facilities and services
12   necessary for decent, healthy, and comfortable living.
13   SEC. 3. General Principles. – The following are the general principles of this Act:
14      a. The national government shall prioritize the development and strengthening of the
15         barangay as the basic political unit, primary service delivery platform, and foundation of
16         participatory local governance and inclusive national development.
17      b. Every barangay shall be empowered with adequate authority, resources, and institutional
18         capability to address local needs, resolve community concerns, and implement
19         development initiatives at the barangay level, consistent with the policy of
20         decentralization.
21      c. Active people's participation, self-help, and collective responsibility shall be the guiding
22         principles in the formulation, implementation, and sustainability of all barangay policies,
23         programs, and development projects.

58   SEC. 6. Membership of Barangay Officials and Personnel in the Government Service
59   Insurance System. – The Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) shall establish a social
60   insurance program to provide life insurance and social protection to barangay officials and
61   personnel. It shall be mandatory for barangay governments to pay the monthly contributions to
62   life insurance, retirement, and social protection coverage of their respective barangay officials
63   and personnel equivalent to twenty-one percent (21%) of their average monthly compensation,
64   which corresponds to the twelve percent (12%) barangay government contribution and the nine
65   percent (9%) employee contribution to be subsidized by the barangay government.
66   Barangay governments shall include in its annual appropriations the amounts necessary for its
67   share of contributions, plus any additional premiums that may be required on account of the
68   hazards or risks of the occupation of barangay officials and personnel. The mode of collection
69   and remittance of contributions, including penalties for violations thereof, shall be in accordance
 70   with Republic Act No. 8291, otherwise known as "The Government Service Insurance System
 71   Act of 1997."
 72   SEC. 7. Automatic Coverage of Barangay Officials and Personnel in the National Health
 73   Insurance Program of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation. – Barangay Officials and
 74   Personnel shall be entitled to automatic coverage under the National Health Insurance Program
 75   (NHIP) being administered by the PhilHealth with premium contributions to be paid by the
 76   national government.
 77   SEC. 8. Membership of Barangay Officials and Personnel in the Mandatory Membership
 78   Program of the Home Development Mutual Fund. – Barangay officials and personnel shall be
 79   covered under the mutual provident savings system of the Home Development Mutual Fund
 80   (PagIBIG Fund).
 81   Barangay governments shall include in its annual appropriations the amounts necessary for its
 82   share of contributions. The amount of contributions due from barangay officials and personnel
 83   and barangay governments, mode of collection and remittance of contributions, including
 84   penalties for violations thereof, shall be in accordance with Republic Act No. 9679, otherwise
 85   known as the "Home Development Mutual Fund Law of 2009."
 86   SEC. 9. Hazard Allowance. – Barangay officials and personnel who render service in areas
 87   declared under state of calamity or emergency shall be entitled to a hazard allowance equivalent
 88   to ten percent (10%) of their monthly salary or honoraria, whichever is applicable, until such
 89   declaration has been lifted by a competent authority.
 90   SEC. 10. Death and Burial Benefits. – Barangay officials and personnel shall be entitled, during
 91   their incumbency, to insurance coverage for disability and accident, including death and burial
 92   benefits. The minimum benefits shall be guaranteed by law; however, the amounts of death and
 93   burial benefits shall not be fixed or limited to those provided under Republic Act No. 6942.
 94   Such benefits shall be subject to periodic review and adjustment, commensurate with prevailing
 95   market value, inflation, and economic conditions, to be determined by the Department of Interior
 96   and Local Government (DILG), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and other
 97   concerned agencies at intervals not exceeding three (3) years, and funded through the General
 98   Appropriations Act (GAA).
 99   SEC. 11. Civil Service Eligibility. – Qualified barangay officials and personnel shall be
100   conferred with the appropriate civil service eligibility in accordance with existing laws,rules and
101   regulations.
102   SEC. 12. Legal Indemnification. – Barangay officials and personnel shall be entitled to an
103   equitable legal indemnification package in the form of legal assistance and other forms of legal
104   protection and indemnification for all legal costs and expenses reasonably incurred in any
105   administrative, civil, or criminal action, suit or proceeding to which they have been made a party
106   by reason of the performance of their official functions or duties, unless they are finally adjudged
107   in such action or proceeding to be guilty of an offense.
108   SEC. 13. Employment Preference in Agencies and Instrumentalities of Government. –
109   Qualified barangay personnel shall be accorded with preferential treatment in employment with
110   national government agencies, local government units, or government-owned or -controlled
111   corporations, subject to the minimum standards and guidelines prescribed by the CSC, and in
112   accordance with existing laws, rules and regulations.

115   SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The funds necessary for the implementation of this Act shall be
116   sourced from available national and local government funds.
117   The Department of Budget and Management (DBM), in coordination with the Department of the
118   Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the Department of Finance (DOF), shall issue the
119   necessary guidelines to:
120      (a) Identify barangays with insufficient funds;
121      (b) Establish criteria for national government subsidy; and
122      (c) Ensure the equitable and efficient implementation of the standardized salary scheme.
123   An initial Barangay Personnel Compensation Adjustment Fund (BPCAF) shall be included in
124   the annual General Appropriations Act to assist barangays in the transition. City or municipal
125   governments may also extend financial assistance to their component barangays.
126   SEC. 15. Consistency with the Local Government Code. – The implementation of this Act shall
127   be consistent with Section 331(b) of Republic Act No. 7160, as amended, which limits barangay
128   appropriations to the income certified as collectible by the city or municipal treasurer.
129   Barangays unable to comply with the provisions of this Act due to insufficient resources may
130   seek assistance from the national government through the BPCAF Barangay Personnel
131   Compensation Adjustment Fund or other subsidy mechanisms, subject to the guidelines of the
132   DBM, DILG, and DOF.
133   SEC. 16. Registry System for Barangay Officials and Personnel. – The Department of the
134   Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall develop, maintain and update a registry system for
135   barangay officials and personnel to effectively implement the provisions of this Act. The registry
136   system shall be the basis for the release of appropriations for the payment of salaries to barangay
137   officials and personnel as provided in Section 5 of this Act, and the remittance of mandatory
138   contributions to GSIS, PhilHealth and PagIBIG Fund as provided in Sections 6, 7, and 8 of this
139   Act.
140   SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – DILG, in coordination with the Department
141   of Budget and Management (DBM), Department of Finance (DOF), Civil Service Commission
142   (CSC), Liga ng mga Barangay, and other concerned government agencies, promulgate the
143   implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of this Act within thirty (30) days from its effectivity.
144   The IRR shall include, but not be limited to, the guidelines and mechanisms necessary to
145   operationalize the funding support, position classification, benefit entitlements, and transition
146   measures outlined in this Act.
147   SEC 18. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, acts, presidential decrees, rules and regulations or
148   parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, appealed, or
149   modified accordingly.
150   SEC. 19. Separability Clause. - Should any provision or part of this Act be declared
151   unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions and parts hereof, insofar as they are separable
152   from the invalid ones, shall remain in full force and effect.
153   SEC. 20. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
154   Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
