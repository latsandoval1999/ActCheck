                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is recognized globally as one of the gravest
threats to public health, food security, and economic development. The misuse,
overuse, and improper dispensing of antibiotics have accelerated the emergence of
drug-resistant microorganisms, reducing the effectiveness of essential medicines and
increasing the risk of severe illness, prolonged hospitalization, disability, and death.

       In the Philippines, despite existing laws requiring prescriptions for antibiotics,
unauthorized over-the-counter dispensing, self-medication, incomplete treatment
courses, and weak enforcement mechanisms continue to contribute to irrational
antibiotic use. Inadequate public awareness, uneven access to healthcare services,
and the growing availability of online medicine sales further exacerbate the problem.

health interventions.

      This measure seeks to institutionalize a whole-of-government and whole-of-
society approach to antimicrobial resistance prevention by strengthening regulatory
enforcement, improving public access to legitimate medical consultation,
modernizing monitoring systems, institutionalizing antimicrobial stewardship
programs, promoting responsible prescribing and dispensing practices, and
expanding public awareness and research initiatives.

      Ultimately, this bill seeks to preserve the effectiveness of antibiotics, protect
public health, and ensure that future generations continue to benefit from life-saving
antimicrobial medicines.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the Antibiotic Access
and Misuse Prevention Act1

      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy.       It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the right to health of the people by ensuring the rational,
appropriate, evidence-based, and medically supervised use of antibiotics and other
antimicrobial agents. Towards this end, the State shall adopt a comprehensive and
integrated approach to antimicrobial resistance prevention consistent with Republic

      SEC 3. Definition of Terms.      For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are hereby defined:

      (a) Antibiotics refer to antimicrobial drugs used for the prevention, treatment,
      or control of bacterial infections in humans;

      (b) Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) refers to the ability of microorganisms,
      including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites, to resist the effects of
      antimicrobial agents;

      (c) Antimicrobial Stewardship Program (ASP) refers to coordinated
      interventions designed to promote the appropriate selection, dosage, duration,
      and administration of antimicrobial medicines;

      (d) Dispensing refers to the preparation, packaging, labeling, and release of
      medicines pursuant to a lawful prescription by a licensed healthcare
      professional;

      (e) Valid Prescription refers to a written, electronic, or digitally authenticated
      order issued by a duly licensed physician or other authorized healthcare
      provider in accordance with existing laws and regulations;

      (f) Online Pharmacy Platform refers to any digital or internet-based system
      facilitating the sale, distribution, or delivery of medicines;
      (g) Rational Drug Use refers to the appropriate use of medicines in accordance
      with clinical needs, proper dosage, adequate duration, and lowest possible cost
      to patients and communities.

       SEC 4. Regulation of Antibiotic Dispensing. Consistent with existing
statutes, all antibiotics shall remain strictly classified as prescription-only medicines
and shall not be dispensed, sold, supplied, distributed, or delivered without a valid
prescription issued by a duly licensed physician or authorized healthcare provider.

       All pharmacies, drugstores, healthcare facilities, and online medicine
platforms shall ensure strict compliance with prescription requirements and
verification procedures prior to dispensing antibiotics. Electronic prescriptions and
telemedicine-issued prescriptions shall be recognized, subject to standards and
safeguards prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH), the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA), and the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC).

       No person or entity shall engage in the unauthorized repacking, relabeling,
promotional distribution, or retail sale of antibiotics outside duly licensed
establishments. The sale of partial antibiotic courses, except as medically indicated
and prescribed, shall likewise be prohibited. The FDA shall require all antibiotic
packaging and advertising materials to prominently display warnings on
antimicrobial resistance, misuse risks, and the requirement of a valid prescription.

      SEC 5. National Antibiotic Dispensing and Monitoring System.            A
National Antibiotic Dispensing and Monitoring System is hereby established under
the supervision of the FDA and DOH to strengthen surveillance, compliance
monitoring, and regulatory enforcement.

      The system shall integrate digital reporting mechanisms for antibiotic
dispensing, prescription validation, inventory monitoring, and detection of irregular
prescribing or dispensing patterns. All licensed pharmacies, hospitals, and
authorized medicine outlets shall maintain dispensing records and submit periodic
reports in accordance with data privacy and cybersecurity laws. The Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall assist in establishing
secure     and     interoperable     digital    systems      for     implementation.

      SEC 6. Strengthening Regulatory Enforcement and Inspection Powers.
The FDA, in coordination with the DOH, local government units (LGUs), and law
enforcement agencies, shall strengthen inspection, surveillance, and enforcement
operations against illegal antibiotic dispensing and distribution activities.

      Regulatory authorities shall have the power to conduct compliance
inspections, issue cease-and-desist orders, confiscate unlawfully dispensed
medicines, suspend or revoke licenses, and initiate administrative, civil, or criminal
proceedings against violators. LGUs shall support enforcement efforts through local
health offices, business permit regulations, and community-based reporting
mechanisms.

      SEC 7. Institutionalization of Antimicrobial Stewardship Programs. All
public and private hospitals shall establish and maintain Antimicrobial Stewardship
Programs as part of their licensing and accreditation requirements. It shall include
evidence-based prescribing guidelines, monitoring systems for antimicrobial use,
continuing professional education, infection prevention measures, and
multidisciplinary oversight mechanisms.

Primary care facilities, rural health units, and community healthcare providers shall
adopt simplified stewardship protocols appropriate to their service capacity. The
DOH shall develop national standards and provide technical assistance for
implementation.

       SEC 8. Expansion of Access to Legitimate Medical Consultation and
Primary Care Services. Consistent with the Republic Act No. 11223 otherwise
known as the Universal Health Care Act, the State shall strengthen access to primary
care services to reduce self-medication and inappropriate antibiotic use.

      The DOH, PhilHealth, LGUs, and healthcare provider networks shall expand
access to affordable consultations, telemedicine services, and community-based
healthcare programs, particularly in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas
(GIDAs). The issuance of legitimate prescriptions through telemedicine platforms
shall be allowed in accordance with standards established by the DOH and PRC.

       SEC 9. Public Education and Awareness Campaign. The DOH shall lead
a sustained nationwide education and behavioral change campaign on antimicrobial
resistance and rational antibiotic use. The campaign shall include public information
dissemination through television, radio, social media, schools, healthcare
institutions, and community programs, focusing on dangers of self-medication,
importance of completing prescribed treatment, risks associated with antimicrobial
resistance       and         proper        infection      prevention        practices.

       SEC 10. Research, Surveillance, and International Cooperation. The
DOH and DOST shall strengthen national antimicrobial resistance surveillance
systems and support research on antimicrobial use, resistance trends, alternative
therapies, diagnostics, vaccines, and new treatment approaches. Collaboration with
international organizations, institutions and foreign governments shall be
encouraged in accordance with global antimicrobial resistance action plans and
international health regulations.

       SEC 11. Regulation of Antibiotic Promotion and Advertising.                 No
person, pharmaceutical company, or entity shall advertise or promote antibiotics
directly to consumers in a manner encouraging self-medication, irrational use, or
misleading therapeutic claims. All promotional and marketing activities involving
antibiotics shall be subject to FDA regulation and ethical standards prescribed by the
DOH and PRC.

      SEC 12. Capacity Building and Professional Education. The DOH, PRC,
CHED, and relevant professional organizations shall strengthen continuing
education and competency programs on antimicrobial stewardship, rational
prescribing, infection prevention, and antimicrobial resistance management for
physicians, pharmacists, nurses, and other healthcare professionals.

modified accordingly.
