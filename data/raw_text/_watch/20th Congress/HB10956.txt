                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


This bill, the "Household Solar Energy Adoption Act” addresses the Philippines'
vulnerability to global fossil fuel volatility and climate shocks by shifting the
national energy architecture toward household-level, distributed renewable
energy systems.

Recent geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East have highlighted the fragility of
an energy grid heavily reliant on imported fossil fuels, driving inflation and high
electricity costs for Filipino households. Beyond these short-term crises, this
measure serves as a long-term investment in generational security, economic
resilience, and climate survival. By deploying rooftop solar, energy storage, and
smart grids, the state can transition homes from passive consumers into active
energy producers—democratizing access, lowering utility bills, and building
localized disaster resilience.

While Republic Act No. 9513 (the Renewable Energy Act of 2008) laid the
foundation for large-scale renewable energy development, nearly two decades
of implementation reveal a critical gap: the limited penetration of clean energy
technologies at the household level. This measure bridges that gap by directly
translating national climate commitments into localized, household-centered
energy transformation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
PROMOTING HOUSEHOLD SOLAR ENERGY ADOPTION, PROVIDING
INCENTIVES AND FINANCING MECHANISMS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR


Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Household Solar Energy
Adoption Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
achieve national energy self-reliance and climate resilience by transitioning toward
sustainable, decentralized, and renewable energy systems, thereby reducing
dependence on imported fossil fuels and insulating the economy from international
market price volatility.
Pursuant to this policy, the State shall mandate and promote the mainstream adoption
of household-level solar energy systems to lower electricity costs, enhance energy
security, and democratize access to clean energy technologies, particularly for low-
income households, rural communities, and disaster-vulnerable areas.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
      (a) Distributed energy resources refer to decentralized energy systems
      including rooftop solar;
      (b) Energy service company refers to Entities providing solar installation and
      financing                                                             services;
      (c) Household solar system refers to small-scale solar photovoltaic systems
      installed in residential properties;
      (d) Net metering refers to a system, appropriate for distributed generation, in
      which a distribution grid user has a two-way connection to the grid and is only
      charged for their net electricity consumption and is credited for any overall
      contribution to the electricity grid;
      (e) Off-grid systems refer to electrical systems not connected to the wires and
      related facilities of the on-grid systems of the Philippines;
      (f) On-grid systems refer to electrical systems composed of interconnected
      transmission lines, distribution lines, substations, and related facilities for
      conveyance of the bulk power on the grid of the Philippines;
      (g) Renewable energy systems refer to energy systems which convert
      renewable energy resources into useful energy forms, like electrical,
      mechanical, and others;
      (h) Rural electrification refers to the delivery of basic electricity services,
      consisting of power generation, sub-transmission, and/or extension of
      associated power delivery system that would bring about important social and
      economic benefits to the countryside;
      (i) Solar energy refers to the energy derived from solar radiation that can be
      converted into useful thermal or electrical energy;
      (j) Solar energy systems refer to energy systems which convert solar energy
      into thermal or electrical energy; and
      (k) Solar home system refers to a stand-alone photovoltaic system that offers a
      cost-effective mode of supplying amenity power for lighting and appliances to
      remote off-grid households. Depending on viability, solar home systems may
      be grid-tie, off-grid, or hybrid solar photovoltaic.


SEC. 4. National Household Solar Energy Roadmap. —The Department of Energy
(DOE), in joint leadership with the Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development (DHSUD), and in consultation with the National Housing Authority
(NHA), Social Housing Finance Corporation (SHFC), Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), Department of Environment and Natural Resources
(DENR), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Finance (DOF),
Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev), Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC), National Electrification Administration (NEA), and other pertinent
government bodies, shall formulate and execute a Comprehensive Household Solar
System Roadmap equipped with an annual implementation strategy to expedite the
deployment, commercial viability, and public adoption of residential solar technologies
nationwide.
The Roadmap shall prioritize the following strategic objectives:
   1. Accelerating the nationwide deployment and adoption of residential solar
      systems;
   2. Lowering retail electricity rates while strengthening household-level energy
      security;
   3. Enhancing power grid stability through the integration of distributed energy
      resources;
   4. Promoting low-carbon development, environmental sustainability, and climate
      disaster preparedness; and
   5. Expanding clean energy access to marginalized, low-income, off-grid, and
      unviable areas across the country.
The roadmap shall incorporate, but shall not be limited to, the following core
components:

   1. National Capacity Targets — Establishment of time-bound installation
       milestones for household solar energy, aligned with national renewable energy
       goals.
   2. Regulatory Streamlining and Market Reforms — Creation of a unified digital
       platform for expedited permitting and interconnection; enforcement of
       standardized timelines with automatic approvals for compliant systems;
       expansion of fair-compensation net-metering and net-billing frameworks; and
       simplified regulations for small-capacity systems.
   3. Financing, Incentives, and Capital Mobilization — Provision of accessible
       financing solutions (including low-interest loans, on-bill financing, and
       subscription models); targeted grants for low-income families; tax incentives
       for equipment, installation, and domestic manufacturing; and the mobilization
       of climate finance, green bonds, and carbon credit monetization.
   4. Grid Integration, Utility Coordination, and Energy Storage — Formulation of
       technical standards for grid connection, distribution infrastructure upgrades,
       and household battery storage; alongside rules for aggregating distributed
       resources to participate in ancillary services markets.
   5. Solar-Ready Building Standards — Integration of mandatory solar-ready
       requirement provisions in building codes for new residential developments,
       technical rooftop standards, and incentives for structural retrofitting.
   6. Workforce Certification and Industry Support — Implementation of national
       training programs, accreditation systems for service providers, and support for
       domestic assembly and manufacturing of solar components.
   7. Consumer Protection and Public Information — Establishment of equipment
       quality and warranty standards, transparent pricing templates, complaint
       resolution mechanisms, and public educational campaigns.
   8. Digital Registry and Smart Data Platforms — Development of a National
       Rooftop Solar Registry integrated with smart meters and utility monitoring
       systems to facilitate real-time data tracking and grid planning.
   9. Local Government Unit (LGU) Alignment — Harmonization of local permits and
       zoning rules, capacity-building programs for LGUs, and performance-based
       incentives for target achievement.
   10. Performance Evaluation and Adaptive Policy — Adoption of key performance
       indicators (measuring capacity, adoption, savings, and emission reductions)
       coupled with data-driven policy adjustment mechanisms.

The Department of Energy (DOE) shall serve as the primary implementing agency and
shall establish an Inter-Agency Household Solar Task Force to oversee the execution
and monitoring of the Roadmap.

SEC. 5. Institutional Framework and Agency Mandates. — For the effective and
coordinated implementation of this Act, the relevant government agencies and local
government units shall exercise the following duties and functions:
(a) Department of Energy (DOE). — The DOE shall serve as the lead implementing
authority under this Act and shall:
      i. Formulate, update, and monitor the execution of the Roadmap;
      ii. Promulgate national capacity targets and technical performance standards
          for residential distributed solar energy systems;
      iii. Direct inter-agency operations, program evaluations, and policy alignment;
      iv. Promote the widespread adoption of residential solar energy adoption and
          generation and energy storage systems;
      v. Coordinate the removal of national and local administrative barriers to the
         installation of solar energy systems;
      vi. Facilitate access to financing, qualified installers, and reliable equipment;
      vii. Develop and disseminate information and technical guidance necessary for
           their development; and
      viii. Prepare and submit annual progress reports to both Houses of Congress.
(b) Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD). — The
DHSUD, in coordination with the National Housing Authority (NHA) and the Social
Housing Finance Corporation (SHFC), shall:
      i. Mandate solar-ready and solar-installation standards across national
         housing policies, building codes, and urban development plans;
      ii. Require the integration of rooftop solar energy systems in government-
          funded socialized and low-cost housing developments;
      iii. Establish targeted financial assistance, subsidies, and credit facilities for
           low-income and informal settler families; and
      iv. Implement structural retrofitting programs for existing housing projects.
(c) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG). — The DILG shall:
      i. Direct Local Government Units (LGUs) to align local plans and ordinances
         with the national household solar policy;
      ii. Provide institutional support and technical capacity-building to LGUs for
          localized execution.
(d) Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR). — The DENR
shall:
      i. Formulate environmental standards governing solar equipment disposal,
         electronic waste recycling, and end-of-life management for photo-voltaic
         panels and energy storage systems; and
      ii. Integrate household solar generation data into national carbon accounting
          and climate action reporting frameworks.
(e) Department of Trade and Industry (DTI). — The DTI shall:
      i. Enforce mandatory product safety, quality, certification, and warranty
         standards for all imported and locally distributed residential solar
         technologies;
      ii. Promulgate consumer protection rules governing price transparency,
          service warranties, and commercial practices; and
      iii. Provide fiscal and technical development programs for micro, small, and
           medium enterprises (MSMEs) operating within the domestic solar supply
           chain.
(f) Department of Finance (DOF). — The DOF shall:
      i. Administer tax incentives, duty exemptions, and financial relief structures
         authorized under this Act;
      ii. Mobilize international climate finance, green bonds, and concessional loans
          through government financial institutions; and
      iii. Create administrative rules for the monetization of carbon credits derived
           from household solar installations.
(g) Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) — The
DEPDev shall:
      i. Incorporate residential solar targets into national, sectoral, and regional
         development frameworks; and
      ii. Conduct periodic economic impact evaluations and strategic policy reviews.
(h) Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). — The ERC shall:
      i. Promulgate and enforce rules governing net-metering, net-billing, feed-in
         tariffs, and ancillary service aggregation for residential distributed energy
         resources;
      ii. Establish mandatory, non-discriminatory, and expedited interconnection
          standards for distribution utilities; and
      iii. Exercise regulatory oversight over distribution utilities to enforce consumer
           protections and prevent market abuses.
(i) National Electrification Administration (NEA) — The NEA shall:
      i. Assist electric cooperatives in modernizing distribution networks to
         accommodate high levels of household solar integration; and
      ii. Provide technical assistance and capital financing to extend residential solar
          solutions to off-grid, unviable, and missionary electrification areas.
(j) Local Government Units (LGUs) — All provinces, cities, municipalities, and
barangays shall serve as primary frontline implementers and shall:
      i. Enact local ordinances establishing fee waivers, property tax discounts, and
         streamlined approval processes for household solar projects;
      ii. Operate digital or physical One-Stop-Shop Permitting Systems for
          residential solar applications with fixed processing timelines;
      iii. Incorporate solar targets into their Local Development Plans,
           Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), and Local Climate Change
           Action Plans (LCCAPs);
      iv. Designate a Local Energy Officer responsible for program execution,
          registry maintenance, and local reporting; and
      v. Prioritize low-income, marginalized, and climate-vulnerable communities in
         local solar deployment programs: Provided, That LGUs meeting or
         exceeding national deployment metrics shall be eligible for performance-
         based national grants and financial assistance.
(k) Inter-Agency Household Solar Council — There is hereby created an Inter-Agency
Household Solar Council, chaired by the Secretary of Energy and composed of the
heads of the agencies enumerated in this Section. The Council shall ensure policy
harmonization, eliminate regulatory bottlenecks, maintain a shared data monitoring
system, and submit a consolidated annual report to Congress.
SEC. 6. Household Solar Registry. — The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), in
coordination with the Department of Energy (DOE), shall establish and maintain a
Household Solar Registry as the national database for all residential distributed
renewable energy systems.
      (a) Integration with PhilSys — The Registry shall be integrated into the
      Philippine Identification System (PhilSys) and utilize the PhilSys Number (PSN)
      as its primary unique identifier.
      (b) Minimum Data Requirements — The Registry shall record installed capacity,
      geographic location, technical specifications, grid interconnection status, and
      relevant household beneficiary data.
      (c) Authorized Use — Collected data shall be utilized strictly for policy
      formulation, program design, targeted subsidies, grid management, and
      disaster response operations.
      (d) Data Privacy — The PSA and the DOE shall enforce strict compliance with
      Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012," to
      guarantee data security and confidentiality, and shall jointly issue the
      implementing rules necessary for this Section.
SEC. 7. Mandatory Integration of Solar Financing in Socialized Housing. — All
present and future socialized housing financing frameworks shall mandate the
inclusion of a dedicated household solar financing component to cover the upfront
costs of installing rooftop solar photovoltaic systems for low-income beneficiaries in
both in-city and off-city resettlement projects.
      (a) Annual Targets — Annual installation targets for solar-equipped socialized
      housing shall be aligned with the aggregated annual targets set by LGUs, the
      NHA, and the SHFC.
      (b) Budgetary Allocation — The financial allocation per housing unit for the solar
      energy system shall be calculated based on prevailing market rates, added
      directly on top of standard housing production costs.
      (c) Financial Structure and Affordability — Financing terms—which may consist
      of loans, direct grants, or equity contributions from key shelter agencies—shall
      be structured such that a household's medium-term electricity expenditures do
      not exceed the costs they would otherwise incur under standard grid
      connectivity.
SEC. 8. Green Financing Window for Household Solar Systems. — Government
Financial Institutions (GFIs), in coordination with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas
(BSP), shall establish concessional credit schemes—including pay-as-you-save,
lease-to-own, and credit guarantee programs—to facilitate household solar adoption,
incentivizing private commercial banks and financial technology (fintech) firms to
participate.
      (a) Mandated Green Financing Window. — The Land Bank of the Philippines
      (LBP) and the Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) are hereby
      mandated to open and maintain a dedicated Green Financing Window offering
      zero-interest loans for the purchase and installation of residential rooftop solar
      energy systems.
      (b) Loan Limits and Terms. — Loans extended under this Window shall be
      capped at a maximum principal amount of Three Hundred Fifty Thousand
      Pesos (₱350,000.00) per household, amortized over flexible repayment terms
      not exceeding ten (10) years, with streamlined application requirements and
      zero-to-minimal collateral constraints.
      (c) Priority Beneficiaries. — Preferential access to zero-interest credit shall be
      granted to low-income and climate-vulnerable households identified through
      official government social registries.
      (d) Implementing Guidelines. — The Department of Finance (DOF), BSP, LBP,
      and DBP shall jointly promulgate rules, eligibility criteria, and risk-sharing
      mechanisms necessary for the operation of the Green Financing Window.
SEC. 9. Mandatory Battery Energy Storage for Government Solar Projects. — All
solar photovoltaic systems funded, subsidized, or facilitated by the government shall
integrate a battery energy storage component to ensure emergency power during
disasters or grid outages.
      (a) Technical Standards — Battery systems shall comply with capacity, safety,
      and interoperability standards issued by the DOE in coordination with the ERC.
      (b) Priority Services — Storage capacity shall prioritize basic household needs,
      including emergency lighting, communication devices, and essential
      appliances.
       (c) Flexible Guidelines — Implementing agencies may establish tailored
       requirements for off-grid, island, and disaster-prone areas to optimize cost-
       effectiveness and resiliency.
SEC. 10. Integration in Local Climate Change Action Plans (LCCAPs). — All LGUs
are hereby mandated to incorporate household solar energy strategies into their
LCCAPs, Local Development Plans, and local investment program budgets, in
coordination with the DOE and DILG.
       (a) Plan Components — Local plans shall specify renewable energy adoption
       targets, identify priority barangays and beneficiary households, and align with
       disaster risk reduction priorities.
       (b) Compliance and Evaluation — Adherence to this Section shall form part of
       the evaluation criteria for national local governance performance ratings and
       climate compliance reporting.
SEC. 11. Streamlined Net-Metering Framework — The ERC and the DOE shall
institute simplified net-metering application rules and mandate fixed processing
timelines for distribution utilities.
SEC. 12. Mandatory Smart Inverters for Residential Systems — All residential
distributed solar installations shall utilize grid-compliant smart inverters capable of
voltage and frequency regulation, anti-islanding protection, and remote monitoring.
       (a) Certification and Interconnection — Distribution utilities and electric
       cooperatives shall grant interconnection approvals strictly to systems equipped
       with smart inverters certified under DOE and ERC testing standards.
       (b) Transition Period — Existing non-compliant systems shall be granted a
       reasonable grace period, as defined in the Implementing Rules and
       Regulations, to retrofit compliant smart inverters.
SEC. 13. Grid Modernization and Distribution Network Upgrades. — All
distribution utilities (DUs) and electric cooperatives shall modernize and reinforce their
distribution networks to ensure the safe, reliable, and efficient integration of household
renewable energy systems.
       (a) Network Investments — Required infrastructure upgrades shall include
       advanced metering infrastructure, smart grid technologies, voltage regulation
       equipment, feeder expansions, and energy management systems.
       (b) Distribution Development Plan (DDP) — DUs shall formulate and submit to
       the DOE and ERC a DDP outlining implementation timelines, technical
       protocols, and capital investment requirements for distributed generation
       expansion.
       (c) Cost Recovery and Safeguards — Capital investments prudently incurred
       under this Section shall be recoverable through regulated tariffs, subject to ERC
       performance benchmarks and consumer protection safeguards.
      (d) Penalties — non-compliance with grid modernization requirements shall
      subject the defaulting DU to administrative fines, disallowance of cost recovery,
      or other sanctions imposed by the ERC.
SEC. 14. Mandatory Interconnection Timelines and Penalties. — DUs and electric
cooperatives shall evaluate, process, and execute interconnection applications for
qualified household renewable energy systems within thirty (30) calendar days from
receipt of complete documentation.
      (a) Penalties for Delay — Unjustified failure to complete processing within the
      30-day mandate shall constitute a regulatory violation punishable by
      administrative fines, direct compensation to the applicant, and additional
      sanctions determined by the ERC.
      (b) Systemic Non-Compliance — Chronic or habitual delays shall warrant
      escalated regulatory actions, including increased fines, disallowance of rate
      recovery, or temporary suspension of authority to process new
      interconnections.
      (c) Monitoring and Compliance — The ERC shall establish a tracking platform
      to monitor adherence to statutory interconnection timelines and enforce
      operational transparency.
SEC. 15. Compliance with Grid Standards. — All household solar photovoltaic
installations shall strictly conform to safety, reliability, and performance standards
prescribed by the DOE and the ERC.
SEC. 16. Fiscal Incentives. — Qualified individual end-users installing residential
solar energy systems shall be entitled to the following fiscal privileges:
      (a) Zero-Value Added Tax (VAT) Rate — Sale and purchase of solar panels,
      inverters, batteries, and related installation equipment shall be subject to zero
      percent (0%) VAT;
      (b) Duty-Free Importation — Importation of components, machinery, and
      equipment necessary for household solar generation not locally available in
      sufficient quantity or comparable quality shall be exempt from custom duties;
      and
      (c) Real Property Tax Exemptions — Real property tax incentives or discounts
      on solar-equipped residential structures, subject to ordinances enacted by the
      respective LGUs.
SEC. 17. Streamlined Permitting and Expedited Processing. — All LGUs and
distribution utilities shall institute a unified One-Stop-Shop Permitting System for
household solar applications under standardized procedures.
      (a) Mandatory Processing Period — Evaluation and approval of local permits
      and utility interconnection clearances shall not exceed thirty (30) calendar days
      from submission of complete requirements.
      (b) Permit-Free Registration — Small-scale residential systems falling below
      capacity thresholds defined by the DOE shall qualify for automated registration
      or simplified notification procedures in lieu of formal building permits.
SEC. 18. Net Billing Framework for Excess Energy Sales. — Residential end-users
operating distributed renewable energy systems with an installed capacity not
exceeding fifty kilowatts (50 kW) shall be permitted to export and commercialize
excess generated power to the local distribution grid.
      (a) Fair Market Rates — Exported power shall be credited or compensated at
      fair market generation rates established and periodically adjusted by the Energy
      Regulatory Commission (ERC).
      (b) Utility Obligations — Distribution utilities and electric cooperatives shall
      provide non-discriminatory grid interconnection, install appropriate bi-
      directional or smart metering equipment, and ensure timely financial
      settlements pursuant to DOE and ERC guidelines.
SEC. 19. Establishment of the "Solar for All Fund" and Barangay Grants. —
There is hereby created a Special Account to be known as the "Solar for All Fund,"
administered by the DOE, with an annual appropriation of Ten Billion Pesos
(₱10,000,000,000.00) to fund household solar installations for indigent families and
climate-vulnerable barangays.
      (a) Grant Scope. — Grants awarded under this Section shall cover full or partial
      costs of solar photovoltaic units, mounting hardware, necessary wiring, and
      basic emergency battery storage systems.
      (b) Beneficiary Selection — Target households and communities shall be
      selected in coordination with LGUs and validated through national social
      protection databases to ensure accurate targeting.
      (c) Equitable Distribution — The DOE shall ensure fair geographic allocation
      across regions, prioritizing off-grid, missionary electrification, underserved, and
      high-risk disaster areas.
(d) Operational Guidelines. — The DOE shall issue guidelines detailing grant limits,
procurement standards, eligibility criteria, and auditing procedures.
SEC. 20. Capacity Building, Training, and Public Education. — The Department
of Energy shall execute nationwide public information drives and institutional
development programs to support residential solar adoption.
      (a) Public Awareness — The DOE, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall
      conduct public education campaigns regarding the economic, environmental,
      and practical benefits of household solar energy systems.
      (b) Technical Workforce Training — The DOE, in collaboration with the
      Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), shall establish
      technical training, accreditation, and certification standards for solar system
      installers, technicians, and service providers.
       (c) Quality and Safety Standards — The DOE shall promote and enforce
       national quality, safety, and performance standards for residential renewable
       energy equipment and installation.
SEC. 21. Monitoring, Reporting, and Public Transparency. — The DOE, in
coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), shall monitor, analyze, and
track nationwide household solar deployment metrics.
       (a) Annual Congressional Report— The DOE shall submit an annual progress
       report to both Houses of Congress detailing national residential solar adoption
       metrics, including total installed capacity, number of connected households,
       disbursement of state subsidies, and grid integration performance.
       (b) Public Data Dashboard — The DOE shall establish and maintain an
       accessible, real-time online public dashboard tracking national household solar
       deployment to promote operational transparency and public accountability.
SEC. 24. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DOE and
partner agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DOE, in coordination with
the government agencies concerned and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations within ninety (90) days upon the
effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 26. Separability Clause. - If any provision, section, or part of this Act shall be
declared unconstitutional or invalid, such judgment shall not affect, invalidate, or
impair any other provisions, sections, or parts thereof.


SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. - All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, issuances,
and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to and inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 28. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
