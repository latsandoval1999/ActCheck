                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines' fisheries and aquaculture sector constitutes a critical pillar of
our national economy, livelihood, and food security. According to the Bureau of
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources , in the first quarter of 2026 alone,           fisheries
production reached approximately 856.29 thousand metric tons, valued at roughly
PHP 78.58 billion. The sector likewise contributed an estimated 9.6% to the Gross
Value Added (GVA) in Agriculture, Forestry, and Fishing.


       Notwithstanding its economic contribution, the sector faces compounding and
systemic threats that undermine both fisherfolk livelihoods and the nation’s long-term
food security. Overfishing, driven by excessive fishing efforts , have resulted in the
steady decline of major fish stocks. This is further exacerbated by the impacts of
climate change, which not only disrupt marine ecosystems but also pose a grave
challenge to our municipal fisherfolk, who often lack the resources and seaworthy
vessels needed to safely and productively fish in increasingly unpredictable conditions.


       Given these challenges, there is an urgent need to revisit Republic Act No.
8550, as amended by Republic Act No. 10654, otherwise known as The Philippine
Fisheries Code of 1998. The law, while providing a foundational framework for the
conservation, management, and sustainable use of fisheries and aquatic resources, has
shown outdated enforcement mechanisms and adaptive capacity in responding to the
effects of climate change and shifting socioeconomic realities.




1
       This bill introduces amendments such as the adoption of the Ecosystem
Approach to Fisheries Management (EAFM) which institutionalizes a science based,
holistic management framework that accounts for ecological interactions, habitat
integrity, and human dimensions of fisheries. It likewise expands the scope of
municipal fisherfolk registration to include persons engaged in fisheries culture
activities, thereby addressing a longstanding regulatory gap that has excluded
aquaculture practitioners from formal recognition, entitlement to municipal water
rights, and access to government support programs.


       This bill seeks to ensure that the Philippine fisheries regulatory framework
remains responsive, scientifically grounded, and equitably administered in the face of
mounting environmental and socioeconomic challenges.


       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Section 1. Section 4 of Republic Act 8550, as amended is hereby further amended to
read as follows:
             “Section 4. Definition of Terms.- As used in this Code, the Following
             terms and phrases shall mean as follows:


                   A. Commercial Fishing- the taking of fishery species by passive or
                   active gear for trade, business & profit beyond subsistence or sports
                   fishing, to be further classified as:


                   1)   SEMI- COMMERCIAL FISHING- FISHING WITH
                   PASSIVE OR ACTIVE GEARS THAT ARE SPECIFICALLY
                   MADE FOR SPECIFIC TARGET SPECIES, IN THE RIGHT
                   SIZES, IN AN OPEN FISHING SEASON AND USING GEARS
                   THAT DOES NOT TUCH THE BOTTOM, UTILIZING
                   BOATS WITH A THREE POINT ONE (3.1) GROSS TONS
                   (GT) UP TO FIFTEEN (15) GT;


                   2)   Small-Scale Commercial Fishing- fishing with passive or active
                   gear utilizing BOATS OF FIFTEEN AND ONE (15.1) GT UP
                   TO THIRTY (30) GT;

    vessels of more than one hundred fifty (150) GT.


    B. DESTRUCTIVE FISHING - IS FISHING WITH THE USE
    OF NOXIOUS OR POISONOUS SUBSTANCES, BLAST

    MORE KEY COMPONENTS OF AN ECOSYSTEM ARE

Section 3. Section 16 of Republic Act 8550, as amended is hereby further amended to
read as follows:


       “Section 16. Jurisdiction of Municipal/ City Government- The municipal/ city
       government shall have jurisdiction over municipal waters as defined in this
       Code. The municipal/ city government, in consultation with the FARMC shall
       be responsible for the management, conservation, development, protection,
       utilization and disposition of all fish and fishery/ aquatic resources within their
       respective municipal waters USING THE ECOSYSTEM APPROACH TO
       FISHERIES MANAGEMENT OR EAFM.


       The municipal/ city government SHALL, in consultation with the FARMC,
       enact appropriate ordinances for this purpose in accordance with the National
       Fisheries Policy. The ordinances enacted by the municipality and component
       city shall be reviewed pursuant to Republic Act No. 7160 by the Sanggunian
       of the province which has jurisdiction over the same.


       The LGUs shall also enforce all fishery laws, rules and regulations as well as
       valid fishery ordinances enacted by the municipal/ city council.


       The management of contiguous fishery resources such as bays which straddle
       several municipalities, cities or provinces, shall be done in an integrated

       order to facilitate their management as a single resource systems. The LGUs
       which share or border such resources may group themselves and coordinate
       with each other to achieve the objectives of integrated fishery resource
       management. The Integrated Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management
       Councils (FARMCs) established under Section 76 of this Code shall serve as
       the venues for close collaboration among LGUs in the management of
       contiguous resources.

Section 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- The Department of Agriculture,
through the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic resources, shall coordinate with the
Department of Interior and Local Government and other concerned agencies including
fisherfolk association, non-government organizations, civil society organizations,
academe, and other stakeholders, to issue the implementing rules and regulations of
this Act.


Section 8. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.


Section 9. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees or
issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.


Section 10. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or     at least two (2)    newspapers of general
circulation.
