                          AN ACT
  PROMOTING THE RE-ENTRY INTO THE WORKFORCE OF PERSONS
    WHO TOOK A CAREER BREAK, PROVIDING INCENTIVES TO
          EMPLOYERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


   The 1987 Constitution repeatedly affirms the country’s regard for its
   people: the family, the worker, the women, and many more.

   This bill seeks to address a pressing social and economic challenge: the
   difficulty faced by individuals who temporarily leave the workforce due to
   pregnancy, childbirth, further studies, or caregiving responsibilities for
   parents, and who later wish to return to gainful employment.

   Many skilled and experienced workers—particularly women—are forced
   to interrupt their careers to fulfill essential family duties. Unfortunately,
   when they attempt to re-enter the workforce, they often encounter
   barriers such as outdated skills, employer bias, or lack of flexible work
   arrangements. This results in lost productivity for the economy and
   diminished opportunities for families.

   This measure aims to:
        Provide incentives to employers who actively recruit and support
         career re-entry workers.
        Establish training and counseling programs to refresh skills and
         build confidence among returning workers.
     Encourage flexible work arrangements to ease the transition
      back into employment.
     Recognize and value caregiving as a vital contribution to society,
      while ensuring that it does not permanently disadvantage
      individuals in their careers.

Through this measure, the State affirms its commitment to inclusivity,
gender equality, and family support. It also strengthens the labor force
by tapping into the talents of experienced workers who might otherwise
remain sidelined.

The government income “lost” through the grant of incentives may be
quickly justified by the opportunities given to its people, the multiplier
effect of the earned purchasing power, the resources saved by the
government from “ayuda” when its people are able to support
themselves, among many other reasons.

In view of the above cited reasons, approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
   PROMOTING THE RE-ENTRY INTO THE WORKFORCE OF PERSONS
     WHO TOOK A CAREER BREAK, PROVIDING INCENTIVES TO
           EMPLOYERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
 Philippines in Congress assembled:

Section 1. Short Title – This Act shall be known as the “Career Re-Entry
Support Act of 2026.”

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:
  1. Promote equal opportunity in employment.
  2. Support individuals who temporarily left the workforce due to
      pregnancy, childbirth, further studies, or caregiving responsibilities for
      parents.
  3. Encourage employers to provide career re-entry opportunities through
      incentives and supportive workplace practices.

Sec. 3. Definition of Terms – For purposes of this Act:
   1. Career Re-Entry Worker – Any person who has been unemployed for at
      least six months due to pregnancy, childbirth, further studies, or
      caregiving responsibilities for parents, and who seeks to return to the
      workforce.
   2. Re-Entry Program – Structured initiatives by employers to recruit, train,
      and integrate career re-entry workers.
Sec. 4. Employer Incentives – Employers who hire career re-entry workers
shall be entitled to the following incentives:
   1. Tax Credits – Up to twenty per cent (20%) of the re-entry worker’s
      annual salary may be claimed as a tax credit for the first two years of
      employment.
   2. Training Grants – Employers may apply for government-funded training
      support to upskill re-entry workers.
   3. Reduced Social Security System (SSS) Contributions – Employers may
      receive temporary discounts on mandated employer contributions for re-
      entry workers.

Sec. 5. Worker Support Programs – The government shall establish
programs to assist career re-entry workers, including:
   1. Skills Refresher Courses – Free or subsidized training in office
      management, digital literacy, professional skills, and industry-specific
      updates.
   2. Career Counseling Services – Guidance on job applications, interviews,
      and workplace reintegration.
   3. Flexible Work Arrangements – Encouragement of employers to adopt
      part-time, remote, or flexible schedules to ease transition.

Technical Education Skills and Development Authority (TESDA) and the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall give priority to individuals who
fall under this Act, who wish to refresh their skills or upskill themselves in order
to reenter the labor force after a hiatus for the above reasons.

Sec.6. Registration with DOLE – Employers must register their re-entry
programs with DOLE to qualify for incentives.

DOLE shall submit annual reports to Congress on the effectiveness of the Act.

Sec. 7. Appropriations - The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Sec. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations – The Secretary of the
Department of Labor and Employment, in collaboration with the Secretary or
representative of Department of Migrant Workers, TESDA, CHED, SSS and
Department of Finance shall jointly craft, adopt, and execute the IRR of this
Act within sixty (60) days from its enactment into law.

Sec. 9. Separability Clause - If any provision of this Act is declared not valid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and
effect.
Sec. 10. Effectivity - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two newspapers of general
circulation.
