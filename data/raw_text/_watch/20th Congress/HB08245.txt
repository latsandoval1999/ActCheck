                                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

There is an alarming increase in overweight and obesity among Filipino children. In
the Philippines, the prevalence of overweight and obesity among school-age children
(5 to 10 years old) has increased from 5.7% in 2003 to 12.9% in 2023. Among
adolescents (10-19 years old), the prevalence increased from 4.9% in 2003 to 12.5%
in 2023. The figures are estimated to increase by 30% by 2030 if no actions are
taken.1 As they grow up, overweight or obese children are stigmatized and develop
noncommunicable diseases such as cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and
chronic respiratory diseases.2

The 2025 Child Nutrition Report – ‘Feeding Profit: How food environments are
failing children’ – highlights how unhealthy food environments drive the global rise
in overweight and obesity among children and adolescents.3 Filipino children live in

This involves providing them with access to a wide range of health services

With the onset of accessibility to social media and other forms of multi-media,
Filipino children are at the mercy of targeted food and beverages marketing that are
not necessarily healthful and contribute to frequent visits to the hospital to seek
medical attention for various ailments related to poor eating habits.7 Admittedly,

defenses leading to higher consumption of calorie-dense and ultra-processed food
and beverages that are rich in salt, sugar, and fat. Such diets are associated with
accelerated increase in rates of childhood obesity. Despite the clear negative impact
of deceitful and exploitative marketing of unhealthy food and beverages to
children’s health, there are, yet no mechanisms to regulate these activities. These
insidiously harmful marketing and advertising tactics that are left unchecked
continue to influence children’s eating habits and food preferences.8

 The World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children’s Fund
 (UNICEF)12, both agencies focused on protecting the rights and welfare of children,
 support the regulation of the power of and exposure of children to unhealthy food
 marketing and its persuasive appeal.13

 The current policy landscape, however, fails to realize this vision for children and
 leaves them vulnerable to exploitation. In 2021, UNICEF assessed that the market
 for highly processed food products including soft drinks, sweet snacks, and savory
 snacks is growing in the Philippines, with demand driven by creative marketing
 strategies by the food manufacturing industry.14 These marketing strategies include
 the use of misleading nutrition, quality and health claims, product reformulation to
 make items appear ‘healthier’, collaborations with movies, and giving away prizes,
 among others, to target children. None of these strategies are regulated to address
 the harm brought about by aggressive marketing of unhealthy food and beverages to
 children.

 This bill seeks to fill a gap in public health policy: the lack of protection against the
 targeted promotion of unhealthy foods and beverages to children. It hampers
 informed food choices and fosters an unhealthy food environment. The law aims to
 clearly define what foods are considered healthy or unhealthy and to implement
 measures to shield children from unrestricted promotion of unhealthy options,
 involving children more actively in their health decisions.

           In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 2.          Declaration of policy. It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health and instill health consciousness among the Filipino
people, especially children. To this end, the State supports the right of every child to
enjoy the highest attainable standard of health and healthy environments and defend
its right to proper care and nutrition.


The State has adopted a national policy of health promotion and preventive care
which guarantees Filipinos to access information on healthy food choices and avoid
unhealthy foods and non-alcoholic beverages.


SECTION 3.          Objectives. This Act, as part of a comprehensive suite of
government efforts, aims to:
    a. reduce the exposure of children to marketing of unhealthy foods and brands
       associated with unhealthy foods;
    b. decrease the purchase of unhealthy food by and for children;
    c. contribute to the prevention of and decrease in childhood overweight, obesity,
       and all forms of malnutrition; and
    d. guarantee children’s right to adequate and nutritious food and the right to the
       highest attainable standard of health.

SECTION 5.             Nutrient Profile Model (NPM). (a) The Department of Health
(DOH) shall develop and adopt a nutrient profile model (NPM) for classifying and
categorizing food according to their nutrient composition and/or level of processing.
Pending the development of a local NPM, a conflict-free, evidence-based NPM
recommended by the World Health Organization shall be used as the basis for such
food classification.


The DOH shall be guided by the Philippine national dietary guidelines and
applicable regional nutrient profile models. Under this Act, the DOH shall not only
create an NPM but also regulate exposure of children to and the power of marketing
of unhealthy food.


(b) The NPM shall be used to determine whether food and beverages are likely to
be part of a healthy diet from those that are likely to contribute to excess
consumption of sugar, salt and fat and, are therefore, categorized as unhealthy food.
Food and beverages classified as unhealthy food shall be prohibited for marketing
to children and shall be accompanied by appropriate mandatory front-of-pack
nutrition labels, among others, within one (1) year from the date of effectivity of this
Act.


(c) Without prejudice to the provisions above, the following categories of food and
beverages are not permitted to be marketed to children at any time:

SECTION 8. Prohibition against Nutrition, Quality and Health Claims on
Unhealthy Food. (a) When a specific food or beverage is classified as an unhealthy
food under this Act, it is absolutely prohibited for such to contain any nutrition,
quality and health claims or to be marketed using a nutrition, quality or health claim.


(b) The prohibition under this Section shall be implemented no later than two (2)
years from the effectivity of this Act. The FDA shall enforce the prohibition in this
section.


SECTION 9.          Mandatory Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labels (FOPNL). (a)
Prepackaged food and beverages categorized as unhealthy food and prohibited to be

marketed to children shall be accompanied by appropriate mandatory front-of-pack
nutrition labels on the components that make such food or beverage unhealthy and
harmful to children’s health under Section 5 of this Act.


(b) Within one (1) year from the date of effectivity of this Act, the DOH, together

Alimentarius guidelines.

      and beverage product packaging, without any marketing features on the
      package;
   b. Dissemination or reporting of information on company practices that do not
      involve contributions to other parties, where required for necessary business
      administration or for mandated corporate reporting;
   c. Legitimate journalistic, artistic, or academic expression of food and beverage
      content and genuine social or political commentary, authored or created by

      and
   e. Food and beverage manufacturers’ newsletters destined for distribution within

SECTION 11. Protecting children from data processing. The processing of
personal data of children for the development of marketing of prepackaged food
products required to have a front of pack nutrition label under Section 9 of this Act
shall be prohibited.
Large digital platforms and search engines shall maintain and publicly disclose
comprehensive marketing repositories, allowing scientific research, civil society
and competent authorities to monitor all marketing sold on their service. These
repositories shall include information such as the identity of the marketing buyer,
the period in which the marketing material was displayed, the demographics of the
target audience and information on how the marketing was viewed.


SECTION 12. Promoting Transparency in Food and Beverage Marketing. Within
two (2) years from the date of effectivity of this Act, large enterprises, as defined by
the Department of Trade of Industry (DTI), in the food and beverage industry shall
submit an annual report to DOH, copy furnishing the FDA, detailing all expenditures
relating to marketing of food and beverage products. The DOH shall prescribe the
manner and form of such report and shall provide sanctions for non-compliance.

shall develop and implement programs to enable citizen participation in compliance
with this Act.


(b) Any citizen or organization may file an appropriate civil, criminal, or
administrative action in the proper courts or administrative body against any public
officer who willfully or grossly neglects the performance of a responsibility
specifically enjoined by this Act and its implementing rules and regulations, or
against any member of the food and beverage industry, including persons working
on their behalf, who violates any provision of this Act and its implementing rules
and regulations. The court shall exempt such action from the payment of filing fees
and shall exempt the plaintiff from the filing of an injunction bond for the issuance
of preliminary injunction. The court may award reasonable attorney’s fees, moral
damages, and litigation costs as appropriate. The administrative, civil, and criminal
actions allowed under this paragraph shall be cumulative and shall not preclude any
other person from filing a similar action.


SECTION 16. International Cooperation and Provision on Technical and
Financial Assistance. The State recognizes that international and regional
cooperation is an important component of regulating cross-border marketing of food
and beverages that harm children’s health. For this purpose, implementing agencies
and its members may seek and receive technical and/or financial assistance from


                                                                                   17
international organizations, except those that belong to or are affiliated with the food
and beverage industry as defined herein, for the purpose of implementing the
provisions or pursuing the objectives of this Act, subject to existing laws, rules, and
regulations.




                                      ARTICLE III
                                IMPLEMENTATION


SECTION 17. Lead agency. The Department of Health (DOH) is tasked with
ensuring the implementation of the provisions of this Act, and shall have the power
to issue other policies, rule, regulations, and standards for the implementation of this
Act. The DOH shall also convene and lead the inter-agency council in Section 18
for the implementation of this Act.


SECTION 18. Council for Healthy Food Environment. (a) The Council for
Healthy Food Environment shall be convened and chaired by the Secretary of the
DOH with the Director General of the FDA and the Executive Director of the CWC
sitting as vice chairpersons. Other members shall comprise of the following:


    National Nutrition Council (NNC)
    Department of Education (DepEd)
    Early Childhood Care and Development Council (ECCD Council)
    Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG)
    Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)
    Land Transportation and Franchise Regulatory Board (LTFRB)


                                                                                     18
    National Council for Children’s Television (NCCT)


(b) The Council shall also consult with the National Council for Indigenous Peoples
(NCIP) and the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos (NCMF). The
Department Secretaries or Chairpersons may designate permanent and alternate
authorized representatives to the Council.


(c) The Council may, as necessary, invite to its meetings and activities, other
government agencies, NGOs, and individual experts, who have relevant mandates
and/or expertise that may be useful to accomplish its functions under this Act.


SECTION 19. Local implementation. (a) LGUs, through appropriate issuances
such as ordinances or executive orders, shall establish mechanisms to enforce the
provisions of this Act within their jurisdictions and shall impose penalties for
violations thereof. For this purpose, City and Municipal Nutrition Committees and
local councils for the protection of children (LCPCs) shall regularly monitor the
compliance of LGUs with the provisions of this Act.


(b) LGUs may mobilize the Special Health Fund under Republic Act No. 11223 or
the Universal Health Care Act, among other resources available, to implement local
projects, programs, and activities consistent with this Act.




                                   ARTICLE IV
                        PROMOTING HEALTHY DIETS




                                                                                  19
SECTION 20. Institutionalization of Programs Promoting Healthy Diets and
Lifestyles. (a) National government agencies in the council shall establish and ensure
the full implementation of all programs and projects that seek to promote healthy
diets, the ultimate aims of which are to address overweight and obesity in children
and eradicate malnutrition among them. These programs and projects include, but
are not limited to, the following:


  i.   “Green Zone” program. The DepEd, DOH, ECCD, and other national
       government agencies shall designate the child-centered settings as defined in
       this Act as “green zones,” or zones where whole and minimally processed
       food and beverages, as well as clean and safe water, are readily available,
       accessible, and affordable. To this end, only individuals or entities that sell
       food and beverages not otherwise prohibited to be marketed to children under
       this Act shall be included as suppliers or concessionaries in these Green
       Zones.
 ii.   Research and development program. The National Task Force shall establish
       a research and development program to be spearheaded by the NCCT. The
       NCCT, in coordination with other national government agencies, shall
       undertake studies, among others, that measure the impact of harmful food
       marketing to the lives of Filipinos and Filipino children, with the goal of
       building evidence for policies that continually and gradually reduce exposure
       to the harmful impacts of food and beverage marketing. The DepEd shall
       allocate institutional research funding to support this program.
iii.   Social and behavioral change (SBC) program. The DOH-HPB shall lead the
       development and implementation of age and development-specific and
       culturally-appropriate SBC program to popularize the consumption of healthy


                                                                                    20
         diets among Filipinos and increase its demand among them. The DOH-HPB
         shall ensure the dissemination of its messages across all media forms and shall
         conduct regular evaluations of the impact of its SBC program.
iv.      Instruction on the harmful impact of food marketing as part of the school
         curriculum.        The DepEd shall direct the inclusion of discussions on food
         marketing to children, as part of the media and information literacy subject in
         senior high school.


The DOH and NNC shall also update its health promotion playbooks, job aids,
manuals, and health standards to include and recommend the implementation of
measures that reduce the exposure of children to the harmful impacts of food
marketing and minimize their power.




                                        ARTICLE V
                                        PENALTIES


SECTION 21. Penalties. The following penalties shall be imposed for each
violation of the prohibitions in this Act:


      a. Any entity considered as a large enterprise by the DTI, which violates
         Section 6, 7, or 8, or fails to comply with the requirements in Section 6(d) of
         this Act, shall:
           i.   Pay a fine of not less than five hundred thousand pesos (PHP 500,000)
                but not more than one million pesos (PHP 1,000,000), for the first
                offense;


                                                                                      21
    ii.   Pay a fine of not less than one million pesos (PHP1,000,000) but not
          more than five million pesos (PHP 5,000,000), for the second offense;
   iii.   Suffer the penalty of imprisonment ranging from one (1) year but not
          more than ten (10) years, for the third offense.


b. Any entity considered as micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises
   (MSMEs) by the DTI, or are otherwise not considered large enterprises by the
   DTI, which violates Section 6, 7 or 8, or fails to comply with the requirements
   in Section 6(d) of this Act, shall:
    i.    Pay a fine of not less than one thousand pesos (PHP 1,000) but not more
          than five thousand pesos (PHP 5,000) for the first offense;
   ii.    Pay a fine of not less than five thousand pesos (PHP 5,000) but not more
          than ten thousand pesos (PHP 10,000) for the second offense;
  iii.    Pay a fine of not less than ten thousand pesos (PHP 10,000) but not
          more than twenty thousand pesos (PHP 20,000) for the third offense.


c. Any natural person who violates Section 6, 7, or 8 of this Act shall:
     i.   Pay a fine of not more than ten thousand pesos (PHP10,000), for the
          first offense;
    ii.   Pay a fine of not more than fifty thousand pesos (PHP50,000.00), for
          the second offense;
   iii.   Pay a fine of not more than one hundred thousand pesos (PHP100,000)
          or imprisonment of not more than one (1) year, or both, at the discretion
          of the court, for the third offense.


d. In addition to the foregoing fines and penalties, the following corrective


                                                                                22
   actions, if not initiated by the offenders themselves, shall be imposed by the
   appropriate authorities to the entities that violate or fail to comply with the
   requisites of this Act:
     i.   Seizure and condemnation, destruction, and/or appropriate disposition
          of items of prohibited marketing;
    ii.   Issuance of takedown orders against banned advertising, promotions,
          and sponsorship content;
   iii.   Cancellation of applicable licenses, registrations, and permits to
          operate for business establishments and operators or drivers of public
          utility vehicles;
   iv.    Closure of the food and beverage establishment by LGUs having
          jurisdiction over premises where a violation of this Act has been
          committed.


e. A violation under Section 13 or 14 of this Act, committed by a government
   official or employee, shall have the following penalties:
     i.   Public officials and employees within the jurisdiction of the Civil
          Service Commission (CSC) shall be subject to administrative
          disciplinary action pursuant to Rule XIV (Discipline) of the Omnibus
          Rules Implementing Book V of EO 292, as may be amended, modified,
          changed, added to, deleted from, or superseded by the enactment of any
          new law;
    ii.   Local elective officials shall be subject to administrative disciplinary
          action by the Secretary of Interior and Local Government, or the Office
          of the President, as appropriate, and provided by relevant laws, rules
          and regulations.


                                                                                     23
       iii.   Other government officials shall be subject to administrative
              disciplinary action by the Office of the Ombudsman or the Office of the
              President, as provided by relevant laws, rules, and regulations.


SECTION 22. Liability of Juridical and Other Persons. Should a violation of this
Act be committed by a juridical person in Section 21 (a) or (b), the Chair of the
Board, the President, the General Manager, or the partners and/or the persons
directly responsible therefor shall be penalized. If the offender is an alien, they shall
be deported after service of sentence and/or payment of fine without further
deportation proceedings and shall be permanently barred from re-entering the
Philippines. In case a violation is committed by, or in the interest of, a foreign
juridical person duly licensed to engage in business in the Philippines, such license
to engage in business in the Philippines shall be immediately revoked.




                                    ARTICLE VI
                               FINAL PROVISIONS


SECTION 23. Healthy Children Trust Fund. — A Healthy Children Trust Fund is
hereby created. All fines collected under Article V of this Act shall be held in favor
of and deposited to said trust fund account and shall be used exclusively for the
implementation of the Philippines’ programs to combat overweight and obesity
among children. The DOH shall be responsible for the management and control of
the Trust Fund.


SECTION 24. Compliance Monitoring. Not later than two (2) years after the date


                                                                                      24
of the effectivity of this Act, and annually thereafter, the DOH shall submit to the
President of the Philippines and to both Houses of Congress a monitoring report on
the compliance of the food and beverage industry with this Act and all related laws,
rules, regulations, and ordinances. All reports required under this Act shall be made
available to the public primarily via the DOH website and other means to readily
access such information.


The DOH is hereby authorized to report to Congress any instance of delay or non-
compliance by the national agencies and LGUs herein of the timelines in
implementing this Act. The DOH is also authorized to demand accountability from
such agencies or LGUs for failure to fulfill its duties under this Act, without
prejudice to other remedies or penalties and sanctions that are available or may be
imposed by law for such delay or non-compliance.


SECTION 25. Implementing Rules. The DOH, in consultation with the members
of the Council in Section 18, shall promulgate such rules and regulations necessary
for the effective implementation of this Act within six (6) months from the date of
publication of this Act, provided that in the event the implementing rules and
regulations are not promulgated within the specified period, the specific provisions
of this Act shall immediately be executory.


SECTION 26. Appropriations. The amount necessary to implement the provisions
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the concerned
national government agencies. Thereafter, such funds as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the budgets of the
concerned national government agencies under the annual General Appropriations


                                                                                    25
Act.


SECTION 27. Repealing clause. All laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative
orders, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with this
Act are repealed or amended accordingly.


SECTION 28. Separability clause. If any part or provision of this Act shall be
subsequently declared unconstitutional, invalid or inconsistent with other prevailing
laws, the other provisions or parts which are not affect shall remain in full force and
effect.


SECTION 29. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
