                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

In May 2026, the Global Preparedness Monitoring Board (GPMB) issued its final
report regarding the global preparedness in the face of health emergencies. As the
body created by the World Health Organization ten years ago to drive reforms to
make the world safer, its last report stated that local preparedness has been outpaced
by emerging trends. It further stated that momentum towards equitable access to
vaccines, therapeutics, diagnostics, and countermeasures have begun to reverse. The
conclusions of the GPMB were not only brought about by the COVID-19 pandemic,
but also by the confluence of all the other health emergencies experienced by the world
since then.

According to the 2025 World Risk Index Report conducted by by Germany’s Bündnis
Entwicklung Hilft and Ruhr University Bochum, the Philippines ranked first in the
list of disaster-prone countries in the world. This, along with report of the GPMB,
highlights the need for a continuous supply of essential and critical drugs and
medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials to effectively and swiftly address public
health emergencies.

This bill aims to address this critical situation by mandating the procurement and
stockpiling of critical and strategic pharmaceuticals and medical devices as well as the
supply of raw materials in preparation for health emergencies.

Hence, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
        PROVIDING FOR THE STOCKPILING OF STRATEGIC AND CRITICAL
         DRUGS AND MEDICINES, VACCINES, DEVICES, AND MATERIALS
      FOR PUBLIC HEALTH EMERGENCIES, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE
        HEALTH PROCUREMENT AND STOCKPILING BUREAU UNDER THE
       DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:


 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the “Health
 2   Procurement and Stockpiling Act”.

 3         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
 4   promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
 5   The State also mandates the adoption of an integrated and comprehensive approach
 6   to health development.
 7         Towards this end, it shall protect public health and safety by preventing and
 8   controlling the spread of diseases and other health hazards through the stockpiling of
 9   essential and critical drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials to
10   effectively and swiftly address the devastating consequences of public health
11   emergencies.

11          SEC. 4. Creation of a Health Procurement and Stockpiling Bureau. - There is
12   hereby created a body under the Department of Health (DOH) to be known as the
13   Health Procurement and Stockpiling Bureau, hereinafter referred to as the Bureau. It
14   shall serve as the principal agency mandated to undertake a transparent, fair,
15   proactive, and innovative procurement service for the DOH and to stockpile,
16   conserve, and facilitate the release of adequate amounts of potentially life-saving
17   pharmaceuticals, vaccines, devices, and materials in times of public health
18   emergencies.
19          SEC. 5. Organization. - The Bureau shall absorb the Procurement Service and
20   Logistics Management Division of the DOH. The Secretary of Health shall determine
21   the organizational structure and staffing modifications of the Bureau subject to the
22   approval of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) in accordance with
23   the existing civil service laws, rules and regulations.
24          The DOH shall ensure an effective and efficient monitoring system for the
25   proper implementation of the procurement and stockpiling of drugs and medicines,
26   vaccines, devices and materials.
27          SEC. 6. Functions and Responsibilities. - The Bureau shall perform the
28   following functions and responsibilities:

18          SEC. 7. Sources. - Consistent with the country's obligations under international
19   treaties and agreements, drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials may

21   open to all eligible suppliers. manufacturers and distributors. However, in the interest
22   of availability, efficiency, and timely delivery of drugs and medicines, vaccines,
23   devices, and materials, the Bureau shall encourage the development of domestic
24   sources to ensure steady, available and adequate supply of such drugs and medicines,
25   vaccines, devices, and materials that are essential in responding to public health
26   emergencies, and in such manner as may be allowed by law, to include countertrade
27   and industrial cooperation to augment stockpiling and availability of critical materials
28   by:

 3          SEC. 9. Establishment of Medical Stockpiling Fund and Tax Exemptions. – All
 4   donations, contributions, grants, bequests, or gifts, in cash or in kind, received from
 5   various sources, shall be placed into a fund. to be known as the Medical Stockpiling
 6   Fund. This Fund shall be expended to support the National Drug and Device Security
 7   Program in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and
 8   regulations.
 9          The DOH may solicit and receive grants, bequests, endowments, donations and
10   contributions which shall form part of the Fund. Said grants, bequests, endowments,
11   donations and contributions used actually, directly and exclusively for the purpose of
12   the Fund shall be exempt from donor's tax and the same considered as allowable
13   deduction from gross income for purposes of computing the taxable income of the
14   donor, in accordance with Section 34 (H)(2) (a) of the National Internal Revenue Code
15   of 1997, as amended, Likewise, fund raising activities may be conducted by the DOH
16   and the proceeds of which shall accrue to the Fund and shall be exempt from any and
17   all taxes.
18          Receipts from donations, whether in cash or in kind, shall be accounted for by
19   the DOH in accordance with accounting and auditing rules and regulations. The
20   receipts from cash donations and proceeds from the sale of donated commodities shall
21   be deposited with the National Treasury and recorded as a special account in the
22   General Fund and shall be made available to the DOH through a special budget
23   pursuant to Section 35, Chapter 5, Book VI of Executive Order No. 292. The cash value
24   of the donations shall be deemed automatically appropriated for the purpose specified
25   by the donor. Donations with a term not exceeding one (1) year shall be treated as
26   trust receipts.
