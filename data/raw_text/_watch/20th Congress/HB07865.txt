                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution provides that arts and letters shall enjoy the patronage of the
State1 and that the State shall encourage and support researches and studies on the
arts and culture.2 This constitutional mandate recognizes the vital role of artists in
shaping our country’s identity and sharing our culture.

Indeed, the Philippines has an abundance of talented artists, from renowned painters,
digital animators, musicians, composers, theater actors, film directors and producers,
sculptors, and many others. Many Filipino artists have gained international
recognition. Just last January, Filipino-Canadian artist Marigold Santos won the ART
SG Futures Prize in Singapore.3 Meanwhile, the late actress Jaclyn Jose was the first
Southeast Asian to win Best Actress at the Cannes Film Festival.4 Filipino singers,
such as Sofronio Vasquez, have likewise showcased their talents on the global stage

Filipino talent is undeniable. In recognition of this, the government has made strides
in supporting the creative industries in the country through the enactment of the Law
Creating the National Commission for Culture and the Arts, and more recently, the
Philippine Creative Industries Development Act. Despite these measures, however,
Filipino artists continue to face multiple difficulties. Artists, particularly at the start of
their careers, often struggle to sustain their art due to financial constraints, lack of
opportunities, and limited institutional support. Art materials, tools and equipment,
studio spaces, and other production costs are expensive, making it difficult for
promising artists to pursue their craft and build their portfolios even further.

One country that has successfully incentivized the growth of its arts sector is South
Korea, now globally known for its flourishing creative industries, particularly in music
and film. In 2025, the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism’s budget was 7 trillion
won, and the largest portion of the budget, at 2.38 trillion won, will be allocated to the
culture and arts sector.6 Among South Korea’s programs is the Artists Get Ready
Grants, which supports artists in the “preparatory stage of artistic activities.”7 One
track in this program is the Emerging Artist Track, which provides newly certified artists
with a one-time grant of 2 million won to help them begin their professional careers.
In 2024 alone, 3,000 artists received financial assistance under this track.

In the Philippines, the NCCA administers the Competitive Arts Program, funded by
the National Endowment Fund for Culture and the Arts. However, this program
generally requires applicants to submit a comprehensive curriculum vitae and
established track record, making it less accessible to artists who are only starting out.8

This measure therefore aims to strengthen our current laws and programs, and take
inspiration from programs abroad that have been effective in stimulating the arts
industry. This measure seeks to use a portion of the Creative Industry Development
Fund to establish a grants program for emerging Filipino artists, including those with
no prior professional experience in their chosen field but can demonstrate their artistic
potential or aptitude, with priority given to applicants who come from households
whose income falls below the poverty threshold. This measure will show our
dedication to further honing our home-grown talents.

  CREATIVE INDUSTRIES DEVELOPMENT ACT, AND TO PROVIDE FUNDS
                            THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Grants for Emerging Artists
Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to give
priority to and support the study of arts and culture. The State recognizes that
emerging artists may face significant economic barriers to the initial stages of their
professional careers. To this end, the State shall prioritize the arts industry by
establishing a financial grants program to support emerging artists and encourage the
development of new artistic voices throughout the country.

SECTION 3. Section 19 of Republic Act No. 11904, otherwise known as the Philippine
Creative Industries Development Act, is hereby amended to read as follows:

      SECTION 19. The Creative Industry Development Fund. — There is
      hereby established a Special Account in the General Fund with the
      National Treasury to be called the Creative Industry Development
      Fund, herein referred to as the Fund. The purpose of the Fund is for
      research and development, trade promotion, human resource
      development in the creative industry, and for the welfare of artists,
      workers and other stakeholders through business support


                                          4
      organizations and creative workers associations. The Fund shall be
      capitalized and administered by the Council in accordance with
      existing government budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and
      regulations. The Fund shall be sourced from ALLOCATIONS IN
      THE GENERAL APPROPRIATIONS ACT, the loans, contributions,
      grants, bequests, gifts, and donations, whether from local or foreign
      sources: Provided, That acceptance of grants, bequests,
      contributions and donations from foreign governments shall be
      subject to the approval of the President upon recommendation of the
      DFA Secretary: Provided, further, That such grants, bequests,
      contributions, and donations shall be deductible for income tax
      purposes in accordance with the provisions of the National Internal
      Revenue Code, as amended.

      PROVIDED, THAT A PORTION OF THE FUND SHALL BE USED
      FOR THE ESTABLISHMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION OF A
      GRANTS PROGRAM FOR EMERGING ARTISTS WHO ARE
      FILIPINO CITIZENS, WHICH SHALL PROVIDE FINANCIAL
      ASSISTANCE TO QUALIFIED ARTISTS AT THE BEGINNING OF
      THEIR PROFESSIONAL CAREERS. FOR PURPOSES OF THIS
      ACT, EMERGING ARTISTS INCLUDE ARTISTS WITH NO PRIOR
      PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE IN THEIR CHOSEN ARTISTIC
      FIELDS BUT WHO CAN DEMONSTRATE ARTISTIC POTENTIAL
      OR APTITUDE THROUGH SAMPLE WORKS, PORTFOLIOS AND
      OTHER RELEVANT EVIDENCE AS MAY BE DETERMINED BY
      THE COUNCIL. PROVIDED, THAT THE COUNCIL SHALL ISSUE
      THE NECESSARY GUIDELINES FOR THE EFFECTIVE
      IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS PROGRAM. PROVIDED, FINALLY,
      THAT PRIORITY SHALL BE GIVEN TO APPLICANTS WHO
      COME FROM HOUSEHOLDS WHOSE INCOME FALLS BELOW
      THE POVERTY THRESHOLD AS DEFINED BY THE NATIONAL
      ECONOMIC AND DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY

      The program of expenditure for the programs, projects, and activities
      to be funded by the Fund shall be itemized on a project-to-project
      basis and submitted annually to Congress. In the identification and
      prioritization of specific programs, projects, and activities, the
      Council shall conduct prior consultation with the representatives of
      the creative industry.

SECTION 4. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 5. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
