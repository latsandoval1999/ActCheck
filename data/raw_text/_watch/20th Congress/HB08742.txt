                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Across our nation, dams play a vital role in providing water for irrigation, power generation,
and domestic use. The Philippines currently has approximately 29 hydroelectric facilities, including
14 conventional dams and 15 run-of-the-river systems. The National Power Corporation
(NAPOCOR) manages 22 dams across Luzon and Mindanao for power generation and watershed
protection, while the National Irrigation Administration (NIA) oversees additional irrigation dams,
with total storage capacity reaching 6,274.5 million cubic meters as of 2006. The government plans
to construct 20 medium dams and 3–5 large dams by 2028 to boost water supply, irrigation, flood
control, and power, with 10 new dams slated to start construction in 2024 alone.

        However, these same structures pose serious risks when poorly managed, neglected, or
overwhelmed by natural disasters. Historical incidents highlight this vulnerability, including the
1996 Marcopper tailings disaster in Marinduque, the 2005 Lafayette incident in Albay, the 2007
TVI failure in Zamboanga del Norte, and a 2024 tailings facility landslide in Surigao del Norte that
displaced 55 families. Even small dam collapses worsened natural events such as the 2011 flash
floods in Cagayan de Oro. Economically, water-related disasters—including storms, floods, and
droughts—are projected to cost the Philippines approximately ₱6.94 trillion from 2022 to 2050,
with the agricultural sector potentially losing up to 8% of annual output by mid-century.

        This bill seeks to institute a comprehensive National Dam Safety Program anchored on
transparency, preparedness, and accountability. It establishes the National Dam Safety Authority
(NDSA) to oversee the control, management, and operation of all dams in the country. The
Authority will ensure the safe construction, operation, and maintenance of dams; formulate a
national dam safety plan; and promote coordination among government agencies, local government
units, and communities.

        The measure further mandates the use of early warning systems, strengthens disaster risk
reduction protocols, and requires community consultations, particularly for emergency
preparedness. To uphold trust and accountability, penalties are provided for failure to disclose vital
information or to engage in proper consultation.

           AN ACT INSTITUTING A NATIONAL DAM SAFETY PROGRAM,
       ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL DAM SAFETY AUTHORITY, PENALIZING
               VIOLATIONS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Dam Safety Act”.

2          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

 3             a) Uphold the people’s constitutional rights to life and property by instituting a
 4                National Dam Safety program that is pro-active, consultative, transparent,
 5                responsible, and gives due regard to the possible occurrence of disasters and
 6                destruction to property and loss of lives;
 7
 8             b) Adhere to universal norms, principles, and standards of humanitarian assistance
 9                and the global effort on risk reduction as concrete expression of the country’s
10                commitment to overcome human sufferings due to recurring disasters;
11
12             c) Incorporate internationally accepted principles of management in the creation and
13                implementation of national disasters;
14
15             d) Adopt a national dam safety program that is holistic, comprehensive, integrated,
16                and proactive in lessening the socio-economic and environmental impacts of dams
17                including possible destruction to property and loss of lives and promote the
18                involvement and participation of all stakeholders concerned, at all levels, especially
19                the local community and the local government units;
20
21             e) Recognize and strengthen the capacities of LGUs and communities in mitigating
22                and preparing for, responding to, and recovering from the impact of disasters.

23         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall refer to:


                                                                                                      1
 1               a) Early warning system - refers to the set capacities needed to generate and
 2                  disseminate timely and meaningful warning information to enable individuals,
 3                  communities and organisations threatened by a hazard to prepare and to act
 4                  appropriately and in sufficient time to reduce the possibility of harm or loss. A
 5                  people-centered early warning system necessarily comprises four (4) key elements:
 6                  knowledge of the risks; monitoring, analysis and forecasting of the hazards;
 7                  communication or dissemination of alerts and warnings; and local capabilities to
 8                  respond to the warnings received. The expression “end-to-end warning system” is
 9                  also used to emphasize that warning systems need to span all steps from hazard to
10                  community response;
11
12               b) Emergency - refers to unforeseen or sudden occurrence, especially danger,
13                  demanding immediate action;
14
15               c) Emergency management - refers to the organization and management of resources
16                  and responsibilities for addressing all aspects of emergencies, in particular,
17                  preparedness response and initial recovery steps;
18
19               d) National Dam Safety Plan - refers to a set procedures, processes or protocols as
20                  promulgated by the National Dam Safety Authority to be observed in the
21                  management and control of dams, especially during emergency situations.

22         SEC. 4. The National Dam Safety Authority. – The National Dam Safety Authority
23   (NDSA) is hereby created, hereinafter referred to as the Authority, which is tasked to control,
24   manage, and coordinate operations of all dams in the country.

25           SEC. 5. Principal Office and Term. – The Authority shall have its main office in Metro
26   Manila and may establish area offices as may be determined by it, if necessary, to achieve the
27   objectives of this Act. The Authority shall have a term of twenty-five (25) years from the effectivity
28   of the Act and may be renewed for the same period unless otherwise provided by the law.

29          SEC. 6. Power and Functions of the NDSA. – The Authority shall have the following
30   powers and functions:

34                  under the direct control and management of the Authority during emergency;
35
36               b) Formulate a comprehensive and holistic national dam safety plan that is consistent
37                  with the declared principles of this Act, which shall include provisions on early
38                  systems, consultation and coordination with all concerned agencies including
39                  LGUs and the private sector;
40
41               c) Integrate, prioritize, program and implement, whenever allowed and authorized by
42                  applicable laws, in relation to control, management and operation of dams;

20           SEC. 11. Organizational Structure. – The Chairperson, upon approval of the
21   Board, shall determine the organizational structure, staffing pattern and pay scales of the
22   Authority subject to existing civil service and compensation laws. The Chairperson may
23   reorganize the Authority and may create or abolish divisions, units or branches therein as
24   the exigencies of the affairs of the Authority may require.

25           SEC. 12. Merit System. – All officials and employees of the Authority shall be
26   selected and appointed on the basis of merit and fitness in accordance with civil service
27   law, rules and regulations. The recruitment, transfer, promotion, and dismissal of all its
28   personnel including temporary workers shall be governed by a merit system that will be
29   established by the NDSA in compliance with existing laws, rules and regulations.

30           SEC. 13. Transitory Provision – The present agencies tasked to manage and
31   operate dams shall make the necessary preparations and turn-over the management, control
32   and operation of the dams under the control and management to the Authority one year
33   after the affectivity of this Act. Provided, That such present agencies may retain
34   management and control of dams if the Authority shall delegate the same as may be
35   determined by the Board. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the Authority
36   shall submit to the President and to both houses of Congress the National Dam Safety Plan.
