                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

Many individuals and families affected by sudden, severe, or unforeseen
events—medical emergencies, accidents, displacement, death in the family,
trafficking, and other crisis situations—often require immediate financial,
material, medical, psychological, and transport support to stabilize their
circumstances. While AICS interventions have been provided on an ad hoc
basis, the absence of a statutory mandate limits predictable funding,
standardization of eligibility and benefits, and coordinated delivery across
national and local levels. Institutionalizing AICS will ensure timely,
standardized, and accountable assistance to those in urgent need, with priority
given to the poor, vulnerable, marginalized, and disadvantaged

The AICS program is hereby institutionalized to provide immediate and timely
assistance to individuals and families assessed to be experiencing crises or
extreme difficult situations needing financial or material support. The AICS
programs serves as a stop-gap measure to support the recovery of individuals
and families suffering from unexpected life events or crises.

In addition to the usual assistance under the program, there is a need to
expand the scope of assistance to include Fuel Subsidy allowance. A targeted
fuel subsidy for motorists facing crisis situations is warranted to ensure
immediate mobility and access to essential services during emergencies.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE ASSISTANCE TO INDIVIDUALS IN
   CRISIS SITUATION (AICS) PROGRAM UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF
  SOCIAL WELFARE AND DEVELOPMENT (DSWD), PROVIDING FUNDING,
   ESTABLISHING OVERSIGHT AND IMPLEMENTING RULES, AND FOR
                         OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Assistance to
Individuals in Crisis Situation (AICS) Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote a
just and dynamic social order free the people from poverty through policies that
provide adequate social services and immediate relief to those who are in a
crisis situation.

To this end, the State shall ensure that the vulnerable, marginalized, and
disadvantaged sectors of our society by providing access to basic social services
during those difficult times.

Section 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following
terms are defined as follows:

   a. Assistive Devices and Technologies- refer to assistive devices, such as,
      but not limited to wheelchairs, canes, walkers and appropriate
      technologies such as, but not limited to, hearing aids, vocal aids, visual
      aids, and specialized computer software and hardware that increase
      mobility, hearing, vision, or communication capabilities;

   b. Beneficiary- refers to the person who actually needs the assistance, or on
      whose behalf the assistance is being sought from the DSWD through an
      authorized representative. The beneficiary is the ultimate recipient of the
      assistance;

   c. Cash Assistance for Other Support Services- refers to the assistance
      given in the form of outright cash, provided to individuals and families in
      crisis or in extremely difficult circumstances;

   d. Crisis Situation - refers to the process of counter checking the
      information or data sets provided by the client who seeks the assistance
      of the DSWD;
e. Educational Assistance- refers to the assistance given to a student-in-
   crisis, to help defray school expenses and/or cost of sending
   students/children to school such as school fees, school supplies, school
   projects, allowances, and other related expenses;

f. Family Food packs/ Other Food items- refers to food packs, hot/ready-
   to-eat/precooked/prepacked meals, or food voucher;

g. Financial assistance- refers to cash or Guarantee Letter provided to
   qualified beneficiaries or through the service providers;

h. Fuel Subsidy assistance- refers to time-bound cash or cash-equivalent
   grant intended specifically to cover fuel or transportation-related costs
   arising from an assessed crisis situation;

i. Food Assistance- refers to the assistance given to beneficiaries to meet
   the need for food and other nutritional requirements for sustenance;

j. Funeral assistance- refers to funeral and other related expenses,
   including, but not limited to bringing the remains of the deceased to his
   or her residence/hometown, interment, cremation, and/or burial site in
   accordance with existing customary practices of the family especially
   among indigenous Cultural Communities/ Indigenous People (Ips) and
   Moros;

k. Hygiene and sleeping kits - refer to hygiene and/or sleeping kits that are
   regularly provided during disaster operations;

l. Medical assistance- refers to hospitalization expenses, cost of medicines,
   and other medical treatment or procedures such as implants, common
   laboratory tests and diagnostic imaging procedures for any illness or
   ailment, including postpartum complications, and also provision of
   assistive devices;

m. Psychological Support- refers to the support given to help meet the
   psychological, emotional, and social needs of beneficiaries and their
   families.

n. Psychological First Aid (PFA)- refers to immediate assistance in the form
   of comfort giving, clarificatory counseling provided to children,
   adolescents, adults, and families who are affected by a disaster or
   traumatic incident;

o. Referral Services- refers to assistance provided when requested support
   is not within the range of AICS programs and services or DSWD services.

p. Social Work Counseling- refers to counseling to help clients gain insights
   into their feelings and which affects behavior, and/or enable them to
   process their thoughts and feelings;

q. Transport Assistance- refers to the assistance in the form of purchase or
   payment of transport (air/sea/land) tickets and/or expenses for travels
   of beneficiary within the Philippines, for purposes not limited to: return
   to home provinces permanently, or seek medical intervention/s in
   another place, or attendance to emergency concerns such as death or
   care of sick family member or relative, rescue of abused or trafficked
   relatives, or events of disaster/calamity that require immediate presence
   of the beneficiary;
Section 4. AICS Program- The AICS program is established to deliver prompt
assistance to individuals and families assessed to be facing crises or extreme
difficult situations that require financial or material support. It functions as a
temporary measure to aid recovery from unforeseen life events or emergencies
and may supplement aid from the Local Government Unit based on DSWD
assessment. Priority is given to the poor, vulnerable, marginalized, and
disadvantaged sectors of society.

Section 5. Program Coverage. – The Program shall provide appropriate and
responsive assistance to qualified beneficiaries who are experiencing crises or
extreme difficult situations needing financial or material support. The
assistance that may be extended under the Program shall include, but shall
not be limited to the following::

   1. Financial Assistance
         a. Medical Assistance;
         b. Funeral Assistance;
         c. Transportation Assistance;
         d. Educational Assistance;
         e. Food Assistance;
         f. Fuel Subsidy assistance;
         g. Cash Assistance for other Support Services;
   2. Material Assistance
         a. Family Food Packs/ Other food items;
         b. Hygiene and Sleeping kits;
         c. Assistive Devices and technologies
   3. Psychological Support
         a. Psychological First Aid
         b. Social Work Counseling
   4. Referral Services
In determining the appropriate assistance, the DSWD shall consider the nature,
severity, and duration of the crisis; the specific needs and circumstance of the
beneficiary; and the prioritizing the Vulnerable, Marginalized and
Disadvantaged sectors of the society.

Nothing in this section shall be construed to prevent the DSWD from providing
more than one type of assistance to a qualified beneficiary when the
circumstances warrant, subject to the eligibility requirements, program
guidelines, and availability of funds.

Section 6. Rates of Assistance and Frequency of Availment. — The DSWD
as the implementing agency of the program, shall determine the rates, amounts,
ceilings and frequency of availment of the assistance as provided under this Act.

The rates, amounts, ceilings, and frequency of availment shall be reviewed by
the DSWD at least once a year, or more frequently when necessary, taking into
consideration changes in the cost of goods and services and the prevailing
needs of qualified beneficiaries.

Section 7. Conditions for Eligibility. — The DSWD, as the implementing
agency, shall prescribe the conditions, criteria, and requirements for eligibility
and availment of assistance under this Act.

Such conditions and requirements shall include, but shall not be limited to,
the documentary requirements for each type of assistance, as well as the
verification and assessment of the needs and circumstances of the applicant or
beneficiary.
Section 8. Program Oversight. — The DSWD shall serve as the Lead agency
in the coordination, implementation and monitoring of this program.

Section 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in consultation with DBM, DILG,
DOH, DICT, LTFRB and other relevant agencies, LGUs, shall promulgate the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act.

Section 10. Appropriation. — The Amount necessary to implement this Act
shall be included in the General Appropriations At of the year following its
enactment and thereafter.

Section 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions which are not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, rules and
regulations, memoranda, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent
with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved.
