                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to ensure that representation in the House of Representatives
genuinely reflects marginalized and underrepresented sectors it was designed to
serve. Article VI, Section 5 of the 1987 Constitution mandates a party-list system to
broaden democratic participation, while Article II, Section 26 affirms equal access to
public service. These provisions promote inclusive representation and prevent the
concentration of political power. However, the current framework has not
adequately protected seats intended for those who remain underrepresented.

        The Supreme Court has consistently held that the party-list system acts as a
social justice tool intended to level the political playing field. Yet, vague eligibility
requirements have allowed organizations with established constituencies and
substantial resources to compete against genuine sectoral groups.

       This bill refines the definition of marginalized and underrepresented sectors
and by requiring that party-list organizations and their nominees genuinely belong
to and represent these sectors. Republic Act No. 7941, otherwise known as the
“Party-List System Act”, lacks precise and enforceable standards to ensure authentic
sectoral representation. This proposed measure strengthens those standards and
reinforces the authority of the Commission on Elections to refuse or cancel
registration in cases of misrepresentation or noncompliance , subject to due process.

       These reforms align the law with constitutional intent and reinforce the
integrity of sectoral representation. It also reaffirms the State’s commitment to a
truly inclusive democracy where those at the margins have a direct voice in shaping
national policy.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

      SEC. 3. Section 9 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

             “Sec. 9. Qualification of Party-List Nominees. – No person shall be
      nominated as party-list representative unless he is a natural born
      citizen of the Philippines, a registered voter, a resident of the
      Philippines for [a period of] not less than one (1) year immediately
      preceding the day of the election, able to read and write, A bona fide
      member of the party or organization which he seeks to represent for at
      least ninety (90) days preceding the day of the election, and [is] at least
      twenty-five (25) years of age on the day of the election.

          “THE   NOMINEES     MUST    BELONG TO THE
      MARGINALIZED AND UNDERREPRESENTED SECTORS,
      ORGANIZATIONS AND PARTIES, HAVING THE COMMON
      CHARACTERISTIC OF ITS MEMBERSHIP AND NOT JUST
      SHARING THE SAME BELIEFS OR ADVOCACY.

             “In THE case of a nominee of the youth sector, he must BE at
      least twenty-five (25) but not more than thirty (30) years of age on the
      day of the election. Any youth sectoral representative who attains the
      age of thirty (30) during his term [shall be allowed to] MAY continue
      IN OFFICE until the expiration of his term.”

        SEC. 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and
effect.

       SEC. 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders and rules, and regulations
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly.

      SEC. 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
