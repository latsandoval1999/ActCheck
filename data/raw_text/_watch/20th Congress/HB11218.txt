                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       No learner’s right to education should be curtailed by a lack of security, yet
Philippine public schools continue to send children into classrooms that the
Department of Education (DepEd) itself admits it cannot adequately protect.

        This bill seeks to establish a Comprehensive School Safety and Security
Program covering all public and private basic education institutions. It creates
permanent civil service plantilla positions for School Security Officers (SSOs) in
every public school, mandates a dedicated Emergency Hotline System with
prioritized routing to the Philippine National Police (PNP) and local medical
services, directs DepEd to fund metal detectors, CCTV systems, and fortified
perimeter fencing, and institutes mandatory safety training for teachers and
learners.

       Over the past year, campus violence in the Philippines has escalated from
isolated incidents into a recurring national concern. A gun attack at San Jose
National High School in Tacloban City in June 2026 left three (3) students dead and
twenty (20) others wounded, barely two (2) months before a second fatal shooting
struck the Ateneo de Zamboanga University Junior High School campus.1
Investigations on these tragedies have likewise flagged a string of earlier
firearm-related incidents, including a student who accidentally shot himself after
bringing a gun to school and another who fired a .45-caliber pistol inside a Batangas
classroom, alongside stabbing incidents recorded within the same weeks.2 These
episodes show that Philippine schools now face threats that existing child protection
issuances were never designed to repel.

     Underlying these tragedies is a chronic shortage of trained security personnel.

        Beyond firearms, the PNP Women and Children Protection Center recorded
one thousand, two hundred seventy-five (1,275) school-related incidents involving
children in conflict with the law from 2022 to 2025, highlighting that violence,
bullying, and other safety concerns extend beyond isolated shootings. While DepEd
has responded through a nationwide School Safety Campaign, bag inspections, and
proposed 2027 funding for guards, counselors, CCTV systems, fences, and metal
detectors, these measures remain subject to administrative changes and budget
priorities. This bill institutionalizes such safeguards by establishing permanent
staffing, equipment, emergency hotline, and compliance requirements, ensuring that
learner safety remains protected regardless of changes in administration or funding
priorities.

       Parents send their children to school trusting that classrooms will nurture
them, not endanger them. Every week without a permanent, funded, and legally
mandated school safety framework is a week in which the State falls short of its
constitutional promise of safe and accessible quality education. This Representation
therefore pushes to accord this measure its immediate approval, so that no Filipino
family has to bury a child because protection arrived too late.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING PERMANENT SCHOOL SECURITY OFFICER POSITIONS IN
ALL PUBLIC AND PRIVATE ELEMENTARY SCHOOLS NATIONWIDE,
PROVIDING FOR A COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL SAFETY AND SECURITY
PROGRAM FOR SCHOOLS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Schools Security and
Safety Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels. The
State recognizes that the full realization of this constitutional mandate inherently
requires educational institutions to be safe, secure, and nurturing havens conducive
to learning and development.

       Towards this end, the State shall strengthen school security through the
creation of permanent plantilla positions for School Security Officers (SSOs) in all
public basic education institutions.

      SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private basic
education institutions under the Department of Education (DepEd).

       SEC. 4. Creation of Plantilla Positions for School Security Officers. – There are
hereby created permanent plantilla positions of School Security Officer (SSO) under
the DepEd for all public schools nationwide. SSOs shall be career government
personnel appointed in accordance with civil service laws, rules, and regulations
and shall receive all rights, privileges, benefits, and protections accorded to
government employees. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM), in
coordination with DepEd and the Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall create the
corresponding staffing pattern, salary grades, and funding requirements for SSO
positions necessary to implement this Act.



                                           1
       SEC. 5. Deployment of School Security Officers. – The deployment of SSOs shall
be as follows:

          a.​ Every public school shall be assigned at least one (1) School Security
       Officer;

          b.​ Additional School Security Officer positions shall be allocated
       according to the following minimum standards:​

                i.​ At least one (1) SSO for schools with up to one thousand (1,000)
              learners;

                ii.​ At least two (2) SSOs for schools with one thousand and one
              (1,001) to three thousand (3,000) learners; and

               iii.​ One (1) additional SSO for every additional two thousand
              (2,000) learners or a fraction thereof.

       The DepEd may assign additional SSOs to public schools located in high-risk
areas or those identified as requiring heightened security based on threat
assessments. The use of contractual, job-order, or outsourced security personnel
shall only serve as a supplementary measure and shall not substitute for permanent
SSO plantilla positions.

        SEC. 6. Qualification Standards, Duties, and Responsibilities of School Security
Officers. – The CSC, in coordination with DepEd and DBM, shall establish the
qualification standards, competency requirements, eligibility requirements, training
standards, duties, responsibilities, performance measures, and career advancement
system for SSOs created under this Act.

       SEC. 6. Establishment of Direct Emergency Hotlines. – To ensure immediate
response during crises, all schools are mandated to establish a dedicated direct
Emergency Hotline System. This system must have direct and prioritized routing to
the nearest Philippine National Police (PNP) station and local emergency medical
services. A localized communication protocol shall also be established for the
immediate notification of parents and guardians during school emergencies.

       SEC. 7. Preventive Measures and Safety Equipment. – The DepEd shall ensure
that all schools are equipped with necessary preventive security infrastructure. To
this end, the DepEd shall allocate resources for the following:

          a.​ The acquisition and utilization of metal detectors and other
       appropriate screening devices to regulate entry; ​

          b.​ The strategic installation of Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) cameras
       along school perimeters, entryways, and key common areas, in accordance
       with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012.​



                                           2
          c.​ The construction, structural repair, and fortification of school perimeter
       fences to prevent unauthorized intrusions.

       SEC. 9. Compliance for Private Schools. – Strict compliance with the security and
safety requirements provided in this Act shall be a mandatory condition for the
issuance, renewal, or maintenance of a private school’s DepEd permit or recognition.

       SEC. 10. Coordination with Local Government Units and National Agencies. – The
DepEd shall continuously coordinate with the PNP, the Department of the Interior
and Local Government (DILG), the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), the National
Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), the National Bureau of
Investigation (NBI), the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC),
local government units (LGUs), and other relevant agencies to conduct regular
school safety audits, share intelligence regarding physical and digital threats against
schools, and deploy increased police visibility during opening and dismissal hours
in high-risk zones.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the DBM, the CSC, the
DILG, and the PNP, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to carry out the
provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
