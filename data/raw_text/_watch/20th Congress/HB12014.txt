                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill, to be known as the “AI Para sa Tao Act”, provides a national law on
artificial intelligence (AI). It sets out the rights of persons affected by AI systems, the
duties of those who build and use them, and the support that the State owes to
Filipinos who want to work with the technology.

       Filipinos already deal with AI in ordinary transactions. A chatbot answers for
a bank or a government office, and software is used to screen job applicants and to
score borrowers. Programs can now copy a person’s face and voice well enough to
deceive that person’s own family, and generative AI systems have been trained on the
work of writers, musicians and artists who were never asked.

        Our laws on data privacy, cybercrime, intellectual property, labor and elections
reach parts of this. They were written before machines began to make or shape
decisions about people, and they answer only part of what a person turned down for
a job, a loan or a public benefit will want to know: whether a machine was involved,
on what basis it decided, and who can review the result.

       Executive Order No. 131, series of 2026, adopted the AI Governance
Framework for the Philippines and created the National Artificial Intelligence
Council. The Order is the right start, and this bill builds on it. An executive order,
however, can only organize the Executive branch. It cannot create rights that a citizen
may enforce against a private company, prescribe penalties, or commit public funds
beyond what the law already allows. This bill supplies them, and it keeps the Council
that the Order created.

        The Constitution supports this approach. Article II, Section 11 commits the
State to value the dignity of every human person, and Article XIV, Section 10 declares
science and technology essential for national development and progress. The bill is
written to serve both. The country should adopt AI widely, and no Filipino should be
left without recourse when it is used on him or her.
        The short title says whom the law is for. In Filipino, “tao” is both the person
and the people. The first guiding principle of the bill, taken from the Framework that
the Order adopted, is human centricity: AI shall serve people, uphold human rights
and human dignity, and remain under meaningful human oversight in decisions that
significantly affect persons.

       The bill defines an AI system in the words agreed by the member countries of
the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) in 2023,
which the AI Act of the European Union and the ASEAN Guide on AI Governance
and Ethics also draw on. Philippine rules will therefore speak the same language as
those of our partners.

       Chapter II declares the Filipino AI Bill of Rights. A person must be told when
he or she is dealing with an AI system. When a high-impact system is used to decide
on a job, a loan, admission to a school or a public benefit, the person may ask for an
explanation and for review by a competent human being, who must act within ten
working days. Complaints must be resolved within sixty days. The Chapter has a
separate section on children and on AI companions.

       Chapter III is where most Filipinos stand to gain. It makes government a first
adopter of AI in its own services, sets up an adoption program for micro, small and
medium enterprises (MSMEs), opens shared computing resources to researchers,
startups and MSMEs, and builds a Philippine Data and Language Commons so that
AI systems can work in Filipino and our other languages. It also provides for AI
literacy, research, and regulatory sandboxes.

       Chapter IV regulates according to risk. Scientific research and development
before a system reaches the market stays outside the bill unless it is tested on the
public, and uses that pose little risk are left free. A short list of practices is prohibited,
among them social scoring, sexual deepfakes made without consent, and weapons
that select human targets without meaningful human control. Only Congress may
change that list, and the Council must review it every year. Real-time biometric
identification by law enforcement in public places requires a judicial warrant, with a
narrow exception where a life or the rescue of a victim is at stake. Systems used to
make consequential decisions about people must be tested, kept under human
oversight, and notified to a National AI Register, and the bill says in so many words
that notification is not a license. Online platforms must take down a sexual deepfake
within twenty-four hours of notice from the person depicted. The use of AI in elections
stays under the Commission on Elections.

      Chapter V protects creators and performers. Every person has the right to
decide whether a digital replica of his or her voice or likeness may be made and used.
Providers of general-purpose and commercial generative AI must publish a summary
of the content used to train their models. Rightholders may reserve their works from
text and data mining outside scientific research, and are entitled to equitable
remuneration for the commercial use of their works in AI training. Traditional cultural
expressions may be used only with the free and prior informed consent of the
indigenous community concerned.

       Chapter VI deals with work. No one may be hired, disciplined or dismissed on
the output of an AI system alone. Workers must be informed in writing thirty days
before an AI system is used to manage them. An employer that expects AI to cost jobs
must notify the workers and the Department of Labor and Employment at least sixty
days ahead, and a grant program supports retraining.

        The bill does not create a new commission. Chapter VII keeps the Council
created by the Order, with the Executive Secretary as chair, places a Philippine
Artificial Intelligence Governance Office within the Department of Information and
Communications Technology, and leaves the National Privacy Commission, the
Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines, the Commission on Elections and the
other sectoral regulators in charge of their own fields.

       Enforcement under Chapter VIII is graduated. A first violation that is not
willful and has caused no serious harm is not fined if it is corrected within the period
given. Fines are lower for MSMEs. Imprisonment is reserved for knowing, willful or
intentional violations and for reckless conduct that results in death or serious harm.
The bans on sexual deepfakes and on deceiving voters about voting apply from the
day the law takes effect. The other duties follow in stages, with more time for MSMEs
and local governments. A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee will monitor
implementation, and the Council must report to Congress on the state of AI every
year.

      This measure draws on the bills on AI pending before the Committee on
Information and Communications Technology and on the consultations of its
Technical Working Group on Artificial Intelligence.

       AI will be used in this country with or without a law. With one, Filipinos will
have rights and remedies when it is used on them, and public support when they want
to build with it. In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

32          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Sections 11, 17, 18, and
33   24, and Article XIV, Sections 10 and 13 of the Constitution, the State recognizes science
34   and technology, including artificial intelligence (AI), as indispensable to national
35   development and to the dignity and welfare of the Filipino people. The State shall
36   promote the responsible development, adoption, and diffusion of AI across all sectors
37   of national life so that its benefits reach every Filipino, every enterprise, and every
38   region.

39         The State recognizes AI as a strategic national capability essential to
40   productivity, innovation, public-sector modernization, scientific advancement,
41   education, workforce development, and inclusive economic growth.

 6          human dignity, the primacy of the human person, and the rights

32          responsible use of AI; to enable innovation through a supportive and
33          adaptive environment for AI research, development, and deployment;

 8          SEC. 3. Guiding Principles. – The development, deployment, and use of AI in
 9   the Philippines shall be guided by the following principles, of which paragraphs (a)
10   to (f) correspond to the principles of the AI Governance Framework for the
11   Philippines. The principles shall guide the interpretation of this Act, rule-making, and
12   procurement. No sanction shall be imposed for failure to observe a principle except
13   through a specific duty under this Act:

14           (a) Human Centricity. AI shall serve people, uphold human rights and human
15   dignity, and remain under meaningful human oversight in decisions that significantly
16   affect persons;

17         (b) Fairness and Equity. AI shall be designed and used to prevent unjust bias and
18   to ensure inclusive outcomes, particularly for women, children, older persons,
19   persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, the poor, and other vulnerable and
20   marginalized sectors;

21           (c) Transparency and Explainability. Persons shall know when they interact with
22   AI or are subject to decisions made with AI, and shall be given explanations that are
23   clear, timely, and understandable to a layperson, and AI systems shall be documented
24   in a manner accessible to the competent authorities;

25           (d) Security and Safety. AI shall be secure, safe, and resilient throughout its
26   lifecycle, with risks assessed and managed before and after deployment;

27           (e) Accountability and Integrity. Clear human and organizational responsibility
28   shall attach to every stage of the AI lifecycle;

29         (f) Sustainable Development. AI shall advance the Philippine Development Plan
30   and the Sustainable Development Goals, balancing economic, social, and
31   environmental objectives, including the efficient use of energy and water;

32         (g) Privacy and Data Governance. AI shall respect privacy and the lawful, fair,
33   and secure processing of personal data in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173,
34   otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012";




                                                3
 1         (h) Robustness and Reliability. AI shall perform consistently with its intended
 2   purpose and shall be tested and monitored for accuracy and reliability;

 3          (i) Proportionate and Innovation-Enabling Governance. Obligations shall be
 4   proportionate to risk, technology-neutral, and sequenced with enabling support, so
 5   that low-risk uses remain free and Filipino enterprises, especially the MSMEs, can
 6   adopt AI with confidence;

 7          (j) Respect for Human Creativity and Culture. AI shall be developed and used with
 8   respect for human artistry, the intellectual property rights of creators, and the cultural
 9   heritage of the Filipino people, including the traditional cultural expressions of
10   indigenous cultural communities and indigenous peoples; and

12   based, technology-neutral, evidence-informed, and periodically reviewed to respond
13   to emerging technologies, scientific developments, and evolving societal needs while
14   maintaining legal certainty and protecting constitutional rights.

15          SEC. 4. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are:

16           (a) To promote the ethical and responsible development, adoption, and
17   utilization of AI and national competitiveness in a manner that upholds the privacy,
18   rights, safety, and dignity of all Filipinos;

19         (b) To accelerate the diffusion of AI across government, enterprises, especially
20   the MSMEs and startups, and every region of the country;

21         (c) To ensure that AI systems are designed and deployed to support human
22   autonomy, informed decision-making, and meaningful human oversight;

23          (d) To balance the promotion of AI with safety and the protection of the public
24   interest;

25          (e) To mitigate adverse labor impacts arising from AI adoption, to promote
26   decent and sustainable work and a humane working environment, and to provide
27   upskilling, reskilling, and just transitions for affected workers;

28         (f) To protect the human artistry, creativity, and rights of Filipino creators,
29   performers, and creative industries in works and performances affected by AI, and to
30   support their adoption of AI;

31          (g) To promote the use of AI for inclusive national development, strengthen
32   Philippine sovereign AI capabilities, enhance economic competitiveness, safeguard
33   national security, and respect human autonomy and fundamental rights;

10          SEC. 5. Scope and Application. – This Act applies to:

11             (a) The development, provision, deployment, distribution, importation,
12                 and use of AI systems and AI models in the Philippines. It applies
13                 whether the AI system or model originates in or outside the Philippines,
14                 where it is placed on the market, put into service, or used in the
15                 Philippines, or where its output is used in the Philippines and affects
16                 persons in the Philippines.

17             (b) Government entities and private persons alike, including in critical
18                 sectors such as health care, education, employment, finance, law
19                 enforcement, justice, elections, and media, and such other sectors as the
20                 Office may identify by rule applying the criteria in Section 28(3),
21                 particularly when such use involves high-impact AI systems. Nothing
22                 in this paragraph authorizes the regulation of news or opinion content.
23                 The duties that attach to high-impact AI systems apply to deployments
24                 by government entities on the same terms as to private deployments,
25                 subject to the transitory periods under Section 78.

26          This Act shall not apply to:

27             (a) AI systems or models developed and used solely for scientific research
28                 and development before being placed on the market or put into service,
29                 except where testing in real-world conditions may affect the rights or
30                 safety of persons;

31             (b) The use of AI by a natural person in the course of a purely personal, non-
32                 professional activity, except as provided in Sections 26, 35(5), 38, 39, 43,
33                 and 48(d); and

 7          (mm) Philippine National Standard refers to a standard adopted by the Bureau of

 1   shall not be used for text and data mining other than for the purposes under Section

30                    CHAPTER II. THE FILIPINO AI BILL OF RIGHTS

31           SEC. 7. The Filipino AI Bill of Rights. – Every natural person in the Philippines,
32   and every Filipino abroad with respect to AI systems used by government entities or,
33   subject to Section 5(6), by persons in the Philippines, shall enjoy the following rights
34   in relation to AI systems, which shall be known collectively as the Filipino AI Bill of
35   Rights or Ang mga Karapatan ng Bawat Pilipino sa Panahon ng AI:




                                                 13
 1          (a) Right to Safe and Effective AI. To be protected from AI systems that are unsafe
 2   or ineffective for their intended purpose, as secured by Chapter IV;

 3         (b) Right to Equal Treatment. To be free from algorithmic discrimination, as
 4   provided in Section 11;

 5          (c) Right to Privacy and Data Dignity. To have personal data processed by AI
 6   systems only in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, and to exercise the rights of
 7   data subjects under that law in relation to AI systems, as provided in Section 40;

 8          (d) Right to Know. To be informed when interacting with an AI system, when
 9   content is a deepfake, and when a high-impact AI system is used to make, or is a
10   substantial factor in making, a consequential decision about the person, as provided
11   in Sections 8 and 35;

12         (e) Right to an Explanation. To receive a meaningful explanation of a
13   consequential decision made with a high-impact AI system, as provided in Section 9;

14         (f) Right to Human Review and to Contest. To contest a consequential decision
15   made with a high-impact AI system and to obtain review by a competent natural
16   person with authority to change it, as provided in Section 10;

17         (g) Right to a Human Alternative. To be served by a natural person in the delivery
18   of essential public services and, where reasonably practicable, when a private
19   deployer uses a high-impact AI system, as provided in Section 10;

20          (h) Right to One's Own Voice and Likeness. To decide whether and how a digital
21   replica of one's voice, image, or likeness is created and used, as provided in Section
22   43;

23          (i) Rights of Creators and Performers. For authors, artists, and performers, to
24   control the use of their works and performances for the training of AI through
25   licensing and rights reservation, to credit and attribution, and to equitable
26   remuneration, as provided in Chapter V;

27           (j) Right to Fair Work. For workers, to a human decision-maker in decisions on
28   hiring, discipline, and dismissal, to transparency of algorithmic management, and to
29   a just transition, as provided in Chapter VI;

30          (k) Rights of Children. For children, to the special protection of the State against
31   AI systems that exploit, manipulate, or endanger them, as provided in Section 12;

32           (l) Right to AI Literacy and Inclusion. To have access to AI education and to the
33   benefits of AI regardless of location, language, disability, gender, age, or economic
34   status, through the programs under Chapter III; and


                                                 14
 1          (m) Right to a Remedy. To an accessible, timely, and effective remedy for any
 2   violation of these rights, as provided in Section 13 and Chapter VIII.

 3          The rights in this Chapter are in addition to, and shall not diminish, any right,
 4   protection, cause of action, or remedy under the Constitution and other laws.

 5           SEC. 8. Right to Know. – A deployer of an AI system that interacts directly with
 6   natural persons shall ensure that they are informed clearly, at the latest at the start of
 7   the first interaction, that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious
 8   to a reasonable person from the circumstances.

 9          A deployer that uses a high-impact AI system to make, or as a substantial factor
10   in making, a consequential decision about a natural person shall notify the person,
11   before or at the time the decision is made, of the use of the system, its purpose, the
12   nature of the decision, the identity and contact details of the deployer, and the rights
13   of the person under Sections 9 and 10.

14          Notices under this Section shall be in plain language, in Filipino or English or
15   in another language understood by the person, and in formats accessible to persons
16   with disabilities, including Filipino Sign Language under Republic Act No. 11106,
17   otherwise known as "The Filipino Sign Language Act". The Office shall issue model
18   notices within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act.

19            SEC. 9. Right to an Explanation. – A person who is the subject of a
20   consequential decision made with a high-impact AI system shall have the right to
21   receive from the deployer, upon request and free of charge, a meaningful explanation
22   of: (1) the role of the AI system in the decision; (2) the principal factors and data that
23   drove the decision; (3) a plain-language description of the logic involved; and (4) the
24   person's right to contest the decision.

25           The explanation shall be given within fifteen (15) working days from receipt of
26   the request, in a language understood by the person and in a format accessible to
27   persons with disabilities. The Office may prescribe a longer period, not exceeding
28   thirty (30) working days, for MSMEs.

29          This right may be limited only to the extent strictly necessary to protect trade
30   secrets, to prevent or detect fraud or crime, or to protect national security. Any
31   limitation shall be construed narrowly and shall not defeat the substance of the right.
32   Where an explanation is limited, the deployer shall give the full explanation, in
33   confidence, to the sectoral regulator concerned or to the Office upon request.

34         Where the deployer cannot give a meaningful explanation because of the
35   nature of the AI system, the person may require that the decision be set aside and
36   made anew by a qualified natural person without reliance on the output of the system.


                                                 15
 1          SEC. 10. Right to Human Review, to Contest, and to a Human Alternative. –
 2   A person who is the subject of a consequential decision made with a high-impact AI
 3   system may contest the decision and request human review. The deployer shall
 4   provide review by a competent natural person with authority to confirm, modify, or
 5   reverse the decision within ten (10) working days from receipt of a written request.
 6   The Office may prescribe a longer period, not exceeding thirty (30) working days, for
 7   MSMEs.

 8         Human involvement that merely ratifies an automated output without
 9   substantive review does not satisfy this Section. The burden of proving that human
10   review was provided within the prescribed period rests on the deployer. Failure to
11   provide it within that period shall be deemed a denial of the right.

12         In educational settings involving children, the parent or legal guardian may
13   invoke the right to human review of AI-based ranking, grading, or profiling, and the
14   educational institution shall provide review by a competent natural person within the
15   period under paragraph (1).

16          Every government entity that uses an AI system in the delivery of essential
17   public services shall maintain an accessible channel through which a person may be
18   served by a natural person at no additional cost. A private deployer of a high-impact
19   AI system shall offer a human alternative where reasonably practicable.

20          SEC. 11. Right to Equal Treatment. – No provider or deployer shall, through
21   the design, provision, or use of an AI system, subject any person to algorithmic
22   discrimination.

23          Providers and deployers of high-impact AI systems shall take proportionate
24   and continuing measures to prevent and mitigate algorithmic discrimination,
25   including the use of relevant and representative data, testing for disparate outcomes
26   before and after deployment, safeguards against proxies for protected characteristics,
27   and accessibility for persons with disabilities. A deployer that uses the system in
28   accordance with the instructions of the provider may rely on the testing of the
29   provider before deployment, and shall monitor outcomes in its own context of use.

30          A government entity that deploys a high-impact AI system shall publish, before
31   deployment and at least once a year thereafter, a plain-language summary of the
32   results of its testing for disparate outcomes, with personal data, trade secrets, and
33   privileged information redacted.

34          In a civil or administrative proceeding, where an affected person shows a
35   disparity in outcomes linked to a characteristic listed in the definition of algorithmic




                                                16
 1   discrimination in Section 6, the provider or deployer shall bear the burden of showing
 2   that the treatment was justified.

 3         Nothing in this Section shall prohibit measures, permitted by law, that are
 4   designed to remedy past discrimination or to promote the inclusion of disadvantaged
 5   groups.

 6          SEC. 12. Rights of Children and Safety of AI Companions. – Providers and
 7   deployers of AI systems intended for, or reasonably likely to be used by, children shall
 8   design and operate them in the best interests of the child, with privacy-protective
 9   default settings, age-appropriate design, and safeguards against manipulation,
10   exploitation, and harmful content.

11           A provider of an AI system designed to simulate an ongoing personal or
12   emotional relationship with its user shall, for every user, disclose clearly that the user
13   is interacting with an AI system and not with a human being, and maintain protocols
14   to detect expressions of self-harm or suicidal intent and to refer the user to appropriate
15   crisis services consistent with Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the "Mental
16   Health Act". Where the provider knows or has reason to know that a user is a child, it
17   shall in addition:

18             (a) Repeat the disclosure at the start of each session and at reasonable
19                 intervals during use;

20             (b) Prevent the system from producing sexual content with or for the child;
21                 and

22             (c) Provide tools that allow parents or legal guardians to set limits on use.

23          The use of AI to create, distribute, or facilitate online sexual abuse or
24   exploitation of children, or child sexual abuse or exploitation material, shall be
25   governed by Republic Act No. 11930, otherwise known as the "Anti-Online Sexual
26   Abuse or Exploitation of Children (OSAEC) and Anti-Child Sexual Abuse or
27   Exploitation Materials (CSAEM) Act", or its successor law, including its duties on the
28   removal of such material, and by Section 26 of this Act.

29           The Office, in coordination with the NPC, the Department of Social Welfare
30   and Development (DSWD), the DepEd, and the Council for the Welfare of Children,
31   shall, within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, issue rules implementing this
32   Section, including proportionate and privacy-protective measures to assure the age of
33   users.

34         SEC. 13. Enforcement of Rights. – Subject to Section 5(3), a person whose right
35   under this Chapter has been violated may file a complaint with the sectoral regulator


                                                17
 1   having jurisdiction over the provider or deployer or, where there is none, with the
 2   Office, through the single complaint portal established under Section 62(c). The
 3   regulator or the Office shall resolve the complaint within sixty (60) days, or within the
 4   shorter period provided in its charter. It may order the provider or deployer, including
 5   a government entity, to give the notice, explanation, human review, correction, or
 6   human alternative withheld, and may impose administrative fines under Section 68,
 7   subject to paragraph 3 of that Section.

 8           Independently of paragraph (1), a person aggrieved by a violation of Sections
 9   9, 10, 11, or 12 shall have a cause of action for damages under Articles 19, 20, 21, and
10   26 of Republic Act No. 386, otherwise known as the "Civil Code of the Philippines",
11   and shall be entitled to attorney's fees where the violation is willful. Exhaustion of the
12   remedy under paragraph (1) shall not be required.

13          In any civil or administrative proceeding, where a provider or deployer fails to
14   keep the logs or records required under this Act, it shall be presumed, until the
15   contrary is proved, that the AI system operated in the manner alleged by the
16   complainant.

17           A complaint may be filed by the affected person, by a representative, or, with
18   the consent of the affected persons, by a labor organization, consumer organization,
19   or civil society organization accredited by the Office. The Office shall, within one (1)
20   year from the effectivity of this Act, issue the criteria for accreditation, which shall
21   require legal personality, a non-profit purpose related to the rights protected by this
22   Act, and the absence of conflicts of interest.

23          Where the violation involves the processing of personal data, the NPC shall
24   exercise its jurisdiction under Republic Act No. 10173. The Office and the sectoral
25   regulators shall refer such matters to the NPC within five (5) days from receipt, and
26   shall coordinate with it to avoid duplicate proceedings and double penalties.

27         The Office shall publish standardized procedures for requesting explanations
28   and human review within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this
29   Act.

 4      CHAPTER IV. RISK-PROPORTIONATE GOVERNANCE OF AI SYSTEMS

 5         SEC. 25. Risk-Proportionate Approach. – AI systems shall be governed
 6   according to the risks posed by their use, as follows:

 7          (a) Practices incompatible with human dignity are prohibited under Section 26.
 8   The list of prohibited practices may be amended only by an Act of Congress: Provided,
 9   That the Council, in coordination with the Office and taking into account the advice
10   of the AI Advisory Panel under Section 60, shall assess at least once every year
11   whether any practice should be added to or removed from the list of prohibited
12   practices, and shall include its recommendations and the reasons therefor in the State
13   of AI Report under Section 76;

14          (b) High-impact AI systems under Sections 28 and 29 are subject to the duties
15   in Sections 8(2), 9, 10, 11(2), and 30 to 34;

16          (c) AI systems that interact with natural persons or generate synthetic content,
17   and emotion recognition and biometric categorization systems not prohibited under
18   Section 26, are subject to the transparency duties in Sections 8(1) and 35; and

19          (d) All other AI systems are subject only to the duties of general application
20   under this Act, including those under Sections 11(1), 12, 37, and 40 and Chapter V, to
21   the general principles of this Act, and to voluntary codes of conduct, which the Office
22   shall encourage and may recognize.

23         Classification shall be based on the intended purpose, reasonably foreseeable
24   use, and actual context of deployment of an AI system, and not solely on its
25   underlying technology, model architecture, or sector.

26         SEC. 26. Prohibited AI Practices. – It shall be unlawful for any person to place
27   on the market, put into service, or use an AI system for any of the following, or to
28   commit any of the acts under paragraph (i), whether or not the person uses an AI
29   system to commit them:

30             (a) Social scoring;

31             (b) The deployment of subliminal techniques beyond a person's
32                 consciousness, or of purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques,
33                 including deceptive design patterns, false representations, and coercive
34                 interface designs, with the object or effect of materially distorting the



                                               28
 1      behavior of a person or group by appreciably impairing their ability to
 2      make an informed decision, thereby causing them to take a decision they
 3      would not otherwise have taken, in a manner that causes or is

 5      lawful political messaging that do not bypass conscious deliberation are
 6      not covered;

 7   (c) The exploitation of the vulnerabilities of a person or group due to age,
 8       disability, cognitive impairment, bereavement, or a specific social or
 9       economic situation, including extreme financial distress, with the object
10       or effect of materially distorting their behavior in a manner that causes
11       or is reasonably likely to cause significant harm, including manipulative
12       digital design that targets children;

 4           SEC. 28. High-Impact AI Systems. – An AI system is a high-impact AI system
 5   if, in any of the following areas, it is intended or used to make, or to be a substantial
 6   factor in making, a consequential decision, or it is intended to be used as a safety
 7   component of critical infrastructure or of a product whose safety is regulated by law.
 8   An AI system described in subparagraph (a), (i), or (k) is a high-impact AI system
 9   whether or not it makes, or is a substantial factor in making, a consequential decision:

10             (a) Remote biometric identification not prohibited under Section 26, and
11                 biometric categorization not prohibited under that Section;

12             (b) Employment and work, including recruitment, selection, promotion,
13                 termination, task allocation, and performance evaluation;

14             (c) Financial services, including the assessment of creditworthiness, credit
15                 scoring, insurance pricing and underwriting, and decisions that deny or
16                 restrict financial services;

17             (d) Education and professional licensure, including admission, assessment,
18                 grading, proctoring, and licensure examinations;

19             (e) Access to essential public and private services, including social
20                 protection, public benefits, housing, utilities, emergency services, and
21                 child welfare assessments;

22             (f) Health care, including AI medical devices and clinical decision support;

23             (g) Critical infrastructure, including energy, water, transportation,
24                 telecommunications, and critical information infrastructure;

25             (h) Law enforcement, migration, border management,                  and    the
26                 administration of justice, to the extent permitted by law;

27             (i) Elections and democratic processes, including AI systems used by the
28                 COMELEC in the administration or conduct of elections, plebiscites,
29                 initiatives, referenda, or recalls;

30             (j) Disaster early warning and emergency response; and

31             (k) AI systems intended for, or reasonably likely to be used by, children that
32                 profile them or make decisions about them.

19                 and other subject matter to which they have lawful access, made by

24                 necessary for the research, including the verification of results. This
25                 exception shall not be excluded or restricted by contract. The IPOPHL
26                 shall, after consultation with the National Commission for Culture and
27                 the Arts, the National Library of the Philippines, and the Commission
28                 on Higher Education, define the institutions covered. For purposes of
29                 this paragraph, a research organization is a university, research institute,
30                 or other entity whose primary goal is to conduct scientific research on a
31                 not-for-profit basis or under a public-interest mission recognized by the
32                 State, and which is not under the decisive influence of a commercial
33                 enterprise that has preferential access to the results of the research.

34             (b) Other uses. The reproduction and extraction of works and other subject
35                 matter to which the user has lawful access, for the purpose of text and
36                 data mining, shall not constitute infringement unless the rightholder has
37                 made a rights reservation. A rights reservation shall be effective if made:


                                                49
 1                (1) through a machine-readable protocol recognized by the IPOPHL; (2)
 2                in metadata or electronic rights management information attached to the

 5                Related Rights. Associations, guilds, and collective management

20                protected expression in the output of an AI system; and (3) works
21                accessed by circumventing a technological measure, or obtained from a

32          SEC. 48. Human Authorship, Credit, and Attribution. – Copyright protection,
33   registration, moral rights, and attribution in relation to artificial intelligence systems
34   shall be governed by the following provisions:

35             (a) Human Authorship Requirement. – Only a natural person may be the
36                 author of a work. A work created with the assistance of an artificial
37                 intelligence system shall be protected to the extent that it embodies


                                                 53
 1      sufficient original human authorship, in accordance with the rules of the
 2      IPOPHL. Output generated solely by an artificial intelligence system,
 3      without sufficient original human authorship, shall not be protected by
 4      copyright. This paragraph does not affect the rules on the ownership and
 5      transfer of copyright under Sections 178 to 183 of Republic Act No. 8293,
 6      otherwise known as the "Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines,"
 7      as amended.

 8   (b) Disclosure of the Use of Artificial Intelligence. – An applicant for the
 9       registration or deposit of a work under Section 191 of Republic Act No.
10       8293, as amended, or for the recordation of an assignment, license, or
11       other transaction involving a work with the IPOPHL, shall disclose any
12       material reliance on a generative AI system in the creation of the work
13       and identify the portions so generated, in accordance with the rules of
14       the IPOPHL. A registration or recordation obtained through a false or
15       misleading disclosure may be cancelled by the IPOPHL after notice and
16       hearing.

17   (c) Moral Rights in AI Outputs. – The moral rights of authors and performers
18       under Republic Act No. 8293, as amended, apply to the output of an AI
19       system that reproduces or adapts their works or performances.

20   (d) Prohibition Against False Attribution and Misrepresentation. – No person
21       shall, without the consent of the author or performer concerned, market,
22       label, or present the output of an AI system as the work or performance
23       of an identified author or performer, or use the name or identity of an
24       author or performer to suggest that he or she created, performed, or
25       endorsed the output. A violation of this paragraph shall be subject to the
26       civil and administrative remedies available for the violation of the moral
27       rights of authors and performers under Republic Act No. 8293, as
28       amended, but shall not constitute a criminal offense under Section 217
29       of that Act, without prejudice to Section 43 of this Act. This paragraph
30       does not restrict truthful references to the name of an author or
31       performer, nor the creation of works in a style or genre.

32   (e) Preservation of Human Credits and Digital Replica Disclosure. – The
33       producer and the distributor of an audiovisual work, sound recording,
34       or game distributed to the public shall ensure that its credits do not omit

36       the use of an AI system, and that the credits identify any digital replica
37       used in place of a performer.

 5          SEC. 52. Human Judgment in Employment Decisions. – No employer shall hire,
 6   refuse to hire, discipline, demote, or dismiss a worker or job applicant through an
 7   automated decision or otherwise based solely on the output of an AI system. Such
 8   decisions shall be made by a natural person who has the authority and competence to
 9   assess the matter, and who shall consider the relevant facts and, where applicable, the
10   explanation of the worker.

12   which an AI system was a substantial factor shall have the rights under Sections 8, 9,
13   and 10 from the date Sections 8(2), 9, and 10 apply under Section 78(2)(c). The

19   procedural due process under Presidential Decree No. 442, as amended and
20   renumbered, otherwise known as the "Labor Code of the Philippines", and other laws.

21          SEC. 53. Transparency of Algorithmic Management. – An employer that uses
22   an AI system for algorithmic management shall inform the affected workers and their
23   representatives in writing, at least thirty (30) days before the system is used, of the
24   purpose of the system, the decisions it supports, the categories of data it uses, the role

27   decision-making and in a manner proportionate to their purpose, and shall not use
28   them to harass or intimidate workers, to monitor workers outside working time or in
29   private spaces, or to infer their emotions except as permitted under Section 26(f).

28          SEC. 67. Administrative Enforcement. – The competent authority to enforce
29   this Act shall be the sectoral regulator for matters within its jurisdiction, the NPC for
30   matters involving personal data, the IPOPHL for the matters assigned to it under
31   Chapter V, the COMELEC for elections, the DOE for Section 24(4), and the Office for
32   all other matters.

33          Enforcement shall be graduated. The competent authority shall, as the case may
34   warrant, issue guidance; issue a compliance notice with a period to cure; issue a
35   compliance order; impose administrative fines; suspend the use or availability of an
36   AI system; or refer the matter for criminal prosecution. In determining the sanction, it


                                                70
 1   shall consider the gravity and duration of the violation, the harm caused, the degree
 2   of culpability, the size and resources of the enterprise, cooperation and remediation,
 3   and previous violations. No administrative fine shall be imposed for a first violation
 4   that is not willful and has not caused serious harm unless the violator has failed to
 5   cure it within a period of not less than thirty (30) days from receipt of a compliance
 6   notice.

 7           A sectoral regulator acting under its charter may apply the sanctions provided
 8   in its charter in lieu of those under this Act. No person shall be subjected to more than
 9   one administrative fine for the same act under this Act and under the charter of a
10   sectoral regulator. The imposition of an administrative sanction shall be without
11   prejudice to criminal and civil liability.

12         No administrative action under this Act shall be commenced after five (5) years
13   from the commission of the violation or, where the violation was concealed, from its
14   discovery.

14           A public officer who commits an offense under this Act in the performance of
15   official functions shall suffer the maximum imprisonment and the maximum fine
16   prescribed for the offense and, as the court may determine, perpetual or temporary
17   absolute disqualification from holding public office. A public officer who fails to
18   perform the specific duties imposed on government entities under this Act shall be
19   administratively liable under civil service laws and rules.

20        SEC. 71. Civil Liability. – Nothing in this Act shall preclude civil actions for
21   damages under the Civil Code or other laws.

22          Providers, deployers, and other persons in the value chain of an AI system shall
23   be liable for damages in accordance with their respective roles and fault. A deployer
24   that uses a high-impact AI system contrary to the instructions of the provider, or that
25   substantially modifies it, shall bear the liability arising from such use or modification.

26           A stipulation that excludes or limits liability to affected persons for a violation
27   of this Act shall be void.

28          SEC. 72. Safe Harbor for Good-Faith Compliance. – Documented, good-faith
29   adherence to the applicable Philippine National Standards or to internationally
30   recognized standards identified by the Office shall be a mitigating circumstance in the
31   imposition of administrative fines. It shall be an affirmative defense against
32   administrative liability for a violation of Section 30 or 31 where the violation occurred
33   despite such adherence and the provider or deployer took prompt corrective action
34   upon learning of it. It shall not be a defense against a violation of Section 26, or against
35   willful misconduct or gross negligence.

 1          SEC. 73. Protection of Whistleblowers and Good-Faith Safety Testing. – No
 2   employer or principal shall dismiss, demote, suspend, terminate the engagement of,
 3   harass, or otherwise retaliate against an employee or contractor who, in good faith,
 4   reports to the Office, a sectoral regulator, or a law enforcement agency information on
 5   a violation of this Act or on a substantial and specific danger to public health or safety
 6   arising from an AI system or AI model.

 7          A contractual provision that prevents or penalizes such a report shall be void.

 8         The competent authority shall keep the identity of the whistleblower
 9   confidential.

10         An employer or principal that violates this Section shall be liable for
11   administrative fines under Section 68 and for damages.

12           A person who, in good faith, tests the safety or security of an AI system or AI
13   model within the scope of a published vulnerability-disclosure or safety-testing policy
14   of its provider, or of a testing arrangement with the Office in which the provider takes
15   part, and who promptly reports the findings to the provider or to the Office, shall not
16   be liable under Section 4(a)(1) of Republic Act No. 10175 for acts within that scope.
17   This paragraph shall not apply to access to personal data beyond what the test strictly
18   requires, to the disruption of services, or to the public disclosure of a vulnerability
19   before the provider has had a reasonable time to address it.

20          SEC. 74. Due Process and Appeals. – No sanction under this Act shall be
21   imposed without notice and an opportunity to be heard. A provisional order under
22   Section 29(3), or an order suspending the use or availability of an AI system that
23   presents an imminent and serious risk to life, health, or safety, may be issued before
24   hearing, but the party concerned shall be heard within ten (10) working days from its
25   issuance.

26         The Office and the sectoral regulators shall publish their rules of procedure
27   under this Act within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.

28         Final decisions of the Office may be appealed to the Court of Appeals under
29   Rule 43 of the Rules of Court. Decisions of sectoral regulators shall be appealable as
30   provided in their charters.

31         An appeal shall not stay a cease-and-desist order or a suspension order unless
32   the Court of Appeals so directs.

33         A party may move for the reconsideration of a final decision of the Office
34   within fifteen (15) days from receipt. The motion shall be resolved within sixty (60)
35   days.

12   regulators, review the implementing rules and regulations, conduct inquiries in aid of

15   regulations.

19   renewable by concurrent resolution of both chambers. Upon its expiration, its
20   functions under this Act, including the receipt of reports and rules, shall be exercised
21   by the committees concerned of each chamber.

22           SEC. 76. State of AI Report and Indicators. – The Council shall submit to the
23   President and to Congress, and shall publish, on or before the thirtieth (30th) day of
24   June of each year, a State of AI Report that covers, among others: progress on the
25   targets of the NAIS-PH; indicators of AI adoption by region, sector, and enterprise
26   size; the statistics of the National AI Register; serious incidents; complaints and their
27   outcomes; regulatory sandboxes; the effects of AI on labor markets, including
28   displacement and reskilling; its effects on the creative economy; the energy and water
29   use of AI infrastructure; international developments; and recommendations. The
30   Report shall draw on the digital monitoring and reporting platform maintained by the
31   DICT and on the quarterly reports of the Council to the President under Section 59(h).

32         The PSA and the lead agencies shall collect the data needed for the report,
33   disaggregated, where applicable, by sex, age, region, and disability.

12   this Act shall be resolved in favor of the protection of human dignity and of the rights
13   of affected persons and, in matters touching upon protected expression, in favor of the
14   freedoms guaranteed under Article III of the Constitution. Penal provisions of this Act
15   shall be strictly construed against the State and liberally in favor of the accused. This
16   Act shall be interpreted to promote national uniformity in the governance of AI, its
17   interoperability with international standards, and the avoidance of unnecessary
18   burdens on innovation.
