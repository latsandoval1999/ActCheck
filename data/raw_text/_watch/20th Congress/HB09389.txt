                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine horse racing industry has long formed part of the country’s
regulated gaming and sports sector. Beyond its entertainment value, the
industry supports employment, breeding, training, jockeying, racing
operations, and allied economic activities. It also contributes to government
revenues through taxes, fees, and regulatory charges imposed on racing
operations and betting transactions.

At present, documentary stamp tax is imposed on horse race tickets under
existing tax laws and the legislative franchises governing racing clubs.
However, the current rate places an additional cost on every ticket
transaction, which may affect bettor participation, reduce betting volume, and
limit the capacity of the industry to remain competitive. A more balanced tax
structure is therefore necessary to encourage wider participation in horse
racing while preserving the government’s revenue interests.

This bill seeks to amend Section 190 of Republic Act No. 8424, otherwise
known as the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended; Section
6 of Republic Act No. 7978; and Section 10(a)(1) of Republic Act No. 11649,
by lowering the documentary stamp tax imposed on horse racing tickets to
Five centavos (P0.05) for every One peso (P1.00), or fractional part thereof, of
the cost of each ticket.

The proposed measure also standardizes the applicable documentary stamp
tax rate on horse racing tickets across the relevant laws governing the
industry. By harmonizing the tax treatment of horse race tickets, the bill
promotes clarity, consistency, and ease of implementation for regulators,
operators, and stakeholders.

While the proposed rate is lower, the measure is intended to stimulate greater
transaction volume, encourage increased bettor participation, and support
the continued viability of the horse racing industry. A more competitive
payout structure may attract renewed public interest in horse racing and
encourage reinvestment in the sector, including in horse breeding, training,
race operations, and the organization of additional events.

At the same time, the bill recognizes the need to protect government revenues.
By encouraging higher participation and increasing the volume of taxable
transactions, the proposed adjustment seeks to maintain, and potentially
improve, revenue collection while giving the industry a more sustainable fiscal
environment.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     STANDARDIZING THE DOCUMENTARY STAMP TAX ON HORSE RACING
     TICKETS, AMENDING SECTION 190 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8424,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF
     1997, AS AMENDED, SECTION 6 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7978,
     AND SECTION 10(a)(1) OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11649, AND FOR
     OTHER PURPOSES

     of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

2    1997, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

4          Authorized Numbers Games - On each jai-alai, lotto, or other

8          additional tax of Ten centavos (P0.10) TWENTY CENTAVOS (P0.20) on

10         ON EACH HORSE RACING TICKET, THERE SHALL BE COLLECTED

2          SEC. 2. - The documentary stamp tax provisions under existing horse
3    racing franchise laws, including Section 6 of Republic Act No. 7978, as

5    and modified to conform with the rates prescribed under Section 190 of the
6    National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended by this Act.

 7          Accordingly, on each horse racing ticket issued pursuant to any existing
 8   horse racing franchise law, there shall be collected a documentary stamp tax
 9   of Five centavos (P0.05): Provided, That if the cost of the ticket exceeds One

12   all provisions of existing horse racing franchise laws imposing a different
13   documentary stamp tax rate on horse racing tickets, including special rates
14   for double forecast, quinella, trifecta, or other similar bets, are hereby deemed
15   amended, modified, or superseded accordingly.
