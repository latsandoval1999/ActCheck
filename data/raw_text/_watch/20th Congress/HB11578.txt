                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act (RA) No. 8050, otherwise known as the "Revised Optometry Law
of 1995," has served as the principal legal framework governing the regulation and
practice of optometry in the Philippines for more than three decades. Since its
enactment, significant advances in eye care, diagnostic technology, clinical education,
pharmaceutical science, digital health, and healthcare delivery have transformed the
practice of optometry both locally and internationally. These developments underscore
the need to modernize the law to ensure that Filipino optometrists are equipped to
meet the evolving eye health needs of the public while maintaining the highest
standards of professional competence, ethics, and patient safety.

        The proposed measure seeks to comprehensively update RA 8050 by
strengthening the legal and regulatory framework governing the profession. It expands
the clinical scope of optometric practice to reflect current standards of evidence-based
eye care; enhances educational, licensure, and continuing professional development
requirements; reinforces professional accountability and public protection; and
recognizes the appropriate integration of technological innovations into the delivery of
optometric services.

        Consistent with the State's policy under Section 15, Article II of the 1987
Constitution to protect and promote the right to health of the people, and in support of
RA 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care Act,” this measure
recognizes optometry as an integral component of the country's primary health care
system. It likewise promotes greater access to quality eye care services, particularly in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, through the responsible use of
digital health technologies and tele-optometry, subject to appropriate safeguards
prescribed by law.

     The bill also strengthens professional education and competency by updating
academic standards for the Doctor of Optometry program, reinforcing clinical training
requirements, and introducing mechanisms to ensure that applicants for licensure
possess the practical knowledge and clinical skills necessary for safe and effective
professional practice. It likewise enhances the authority of the Professional Regulatory
Board of Optometry to promote continuing competence, ethical practice, transparency,
and effective regulation of the profession.

       Recognizing the rapid advancement of diagnostic technologies and
pharmaceutical developments in eye care, the proposed measure provides a legal
framework for the regulated use of authorized diagnostic pharmaceutical agents and
other emerging technologies by duly qualified optometrists, subject to the supervision
of the Professional Regulation Commission, the Department of Health, the Food and
Drug Administration, and other appropriate government agencies. The bill likewise
strengthens safeguards against the unauthorized practice of optometry, unlawful
corporate interference with professional judgment, and other practices detrimental to
public welfare.

       Furthermore, this measure harmonizes RA 8050 with existing national policies
embodied in RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012), RA 8792 (Electronic Commerce
Act of 2000), RA 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012), and other applicable
laws governing public health, digital governance, and professional regulation. By
integrating these legal frameworks, the measure promotes innovation while ensuring
accountability, patient confidentiality, cybersecurity, and responsible professional
practice.

        Ultimately, this proposed legislation seeks not merely to update an existing law
but to establish a modern, responsive, and internationally competitive legal framework
that will enable the optometry profession to contribute more effectively to the promotion
of public health, prevention of avoidable blindness and visual impairment,
advancement of clinical excellence, and realization of universal access to quality eye
care services for every Filipino.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
    REGULATING AND MODERNIZING THE PRACTICE OF OPTOMETRY,
   REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8050, OTHERWISE
         KNOWN AS THE "REVISED OPTOMETRY LAW OF 1995"

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Title. — This Act shall be known and cited as the "Revised
Optometry Law of 2026."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of State Policy. —The State recognizes optometry as an
essential primary health care profession vital to safeguarding the health and physical
well-being of the citizenry; accordingly, it shall be the policy of the State to promote
optometric services as a regular component of the national health system by
integrating them into the Universal Health Care (UHC) framework, leveraging digital
health and community-based interventions to address the full spectrum of eye health
and vision care needs.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

        (a) Optometry refers to the science and art of examining the human eye,
analyzing the ocular function, prescribing, dispensing ophthalmic lenses, prisms,
contact lenses and their accessories and solutions, low vision devices, and similar
appliances and devices, conducting ocular exercises, vision training, orthoptics,
installing prosthetics, using authorized diagnostic pharmaceutical agents (DPA), and
other preventive or corrective measures or procedures for the aid, correction,
rehabilitation or relief of the human eye, or to attain maximum vision and comfort.

       (b) Optometrist refers to a person who has been certified by the Board of
Optometry and registered with the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) as
being qualified to practice optometry in the Philippines.

       (c) Optometrist in good standing refers to a registered optometrist who is
authorized to practice the profession in the Philippines by virtue of a valid certificate of
registration and a valid professional license issued by the PRC for the purpose.

        (d) Diagnostic pharmaceutical agents refer to specific topical medications,
diagnostic materials, advanced lubricating formulations, and specialized nutritional or
metabolic agents authorized for use, prescription, and administration by optometrists
to aid in the comprehensive examination, evaluation, and diagnosis of the human eye
and its adnexa; the immediate localized triage and infection prophylaxis of non-
invasive ocular emergencies; and the long-term management and prevention of ocular
surface and degenerative visual conditions.

      Until otherwise specified by the Board of Optometry, upon approval by the
Secretary of Health, upon the recommendation of the Food and Drug Administration
(FDA), the authorized formulary of these agents shall include:

       (1) Mydriatics and Cycloplegics - Including Phenylephrine Hydrochloride (2.5%),
Tropicamide (0.5% and 1.0%), Cyclopentolate Hydrochloride (0.5% and 1.0%), and
Atropine Sulfate (0.01%, 0.025%, 0.05%, and 1.0%) for specialized pediatric
assessment, cycloplegic refraction, and active myopia management;

       (2) Topical Anesthetics - Proparacaine Hydrochloride (0.5% ophthalmic drops)
or Tetracaine Hydrochloride (0.5% ophthalmic drops), Lidocaine Solution (1%)
authorized strictly for diagnostic procedures and the immediate, non-invasive
management of ocular emergencies;

       (3) Emergency Topical Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) -
Ketorolac Tromethamine (0.5% ophthalmic drops) or Diclofenac Sodium (0.1%
ophthalmic drops), authorized strictly for immediate emergency pain and inflammation
control resulting from minor ocular trauma or corneal abrasions.

       (4) Emergency Prophylactic Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics- Tobramycin (0.3%
ophthalmic solution/ointment) or Moxifloxacin Hydrochloride (0.5% ophthalmic
solution), authorized strictly for immediate, short-term prophylactic coverage to prevent
secondary bacterial keratitis following corneal epithelial trauma or superficial foreign
body removal.

       (5) Ocular Lubricants, Viscoelastic Agents, and Artificial Tear Substitutes refer
to ophthalmic lubricating formulations in liquid, emulsion, gel, or ointment forms—
including      Sodium     Hyaluronate,      Carboxymethylcellulose,       Hydroxypropyl
Methylcellulose, Polyvinyl Alcohol, Trehalose, and Glycerin (preserved or preservative-
free formulations), as well as biological tear substitutes including autologous serum
eye drops— authorized for the treatment, lubrication, stabilization, and rehabilitation of
the tear film and ocular surface in dry eye syndromes and corneal epitheliopathies.
      (6) Ocular Surface Stains and Ophthalmic Diagnostic Dyesrefer to Fluorescein
Sodium strips, Lissamine Green strips, and Rose Bengal strips or solutions for
advanced ocular surface, tear film breakup time, and corneal integrity evaluation.

       (7) Ocular Nutritional, Micronutrient, and Metabolic Agents refer to specialized
systemic and topical formulations, including high-dose macular carotenoids (Lutein,
Zeaxanthin, Meso-zeaxanthin), targeted antioxidant matrices (Vitamins C, E, Zinc, and
Copper in accordance with established age-related eye disease protocols), and
concentrated essential fatty acids (Omega-3 EPA/DHA), authorized for the prevention,
adjunctive care, and stabilization of progressive ocular surface diseases, corneal
dystrophies, and retinal degenerations.

       (e) Certificate of Registration refers to a document signed by the Commissioner
of the PRC and by all members of the Board of Optometry and bearing its seal, issued
to an applicant who has satisfied the requirements for the practice of optometry. It shall
serve as proof that the registrant is qualified to practice the profession in the
Philippines.

       (f) Professional license refers to a renewable card, issued by the PRC to a
registered optometrist, indicating that the person is competent and authorized to
practice optometry in the Philippines. Until the loss of its validity or its expiration, such
license shall serve as evidence that its bearer is an optometrist in good standing.

       (g) Code of Professional Ethics refers to a set of standards relating to the
conduct, integrity, and moral duties of optometrists, as prescribed by the duly
integrated and accredited national organization of optometrists and adopted and
promulgated by the Board of Optometry upon the approval by the PRC.

                              PRACTICE OF OPTOMETRY

       SEC. 4. Acts Constituting the Practice of Optometry. — Any of the following
acts shall constitute the practice of optometry:

        a) Examination of the Human Eye and Vision - The examination, assessment,
and evaluation of the human eye and visual system through the use of subjective and
objective procedures; approved pharmaceutical agents; diagnostic instruments,
imaging devices, and computerized technologies; tele-optometry platforms for remote
consultation and screening; visual function testing and ocular health assessment tools
for the purpose of determining, correcting, managing, and monitoring visual conditions,
ocular abnormalities, and related systemic manifestations affecting vision.

        (b) Prescription and Dispensing - The prescription, dispensing, fitting,
adjustment, and monitoring of ophthalmic lenses; contact lenses and related solutions;
prisms; devices; frames and optical accessories; and other approved vision-correcting
or vision-assistive devices for the correction or management of defects, deficiencies,
and abnormalities of vision.

      (c) Vision Therapy and Rehabilitation - The professional clinical practice of
assessing, diagnosing, managing, and rehabilitating visual and neuro-visual
dysfunctions, including low vision. This encompasses, but not limited to: vision
therapy, neuro-optometric, orthoptics and low vision rehabilitation; optometric
phototherapy (syntonics) and other recognized therapeutic, rehabilitative,
complimentary, and integrative clinical approaches. These modalities utilize manual,
optical, digital, computer-assisted, or other emerging technologies to optimize visual
function and patient’s quality of life.

        (d) Patient Counseling and Preventive Eye Care - The counseling and education
of patients regarding vision and eye health care; ocular hygiene; digital eye strain and
screen-related visual conditions; preventive eye care; occupational and environmental
eye safety; and compliance with prescribed vision management programs.

       (e) Telemedicine in Optometry - The delivery of optometric services through
telecommunications and information technology, including remote clinical
consultations, digital prescriptions, and electronic health record management, provided
that such practice complies with existing laws on patient data privacy, cybersecurity,
and professional standards of care.

      (f) Establishment of Clinics and Practice Facilities - The establishment,
management, and operation of offices, clinics, optical facilities, and similar places
where optometric services are offered.

       (g) Professional Fees - The collection of professional fees for optometric
services rendered, whether delivered through traditional in-person examination or
authorized telehealth modalities, in accordance with established professional
standards and guidelines, as mentioned in Paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (d), and (e) of this
section.

       SEC. 5. Prohibition Against the Unauthorized Practice of Optometry. —

       a) No person shall practice optometry or perform any act constituting the
practice of optometry without being duly licensed and registered under this Act and its
implementing rules and regulations: Provided, That this prohibition shall not apply to
duly registered physicians who have received post-graduate training in the diagnosis
and treatment of eye disease: Provided, however, That the examination of the human
eye by duly registered physicians in connection with the physical examination of
patients shall not be considered as practice of optometry: Provided, further, That public
health workers trained and involved in the government's blindness prevention program
may conduct only visual acuity tests and visual screening.

       b) No individual or entity shall operate tele-optometry services, computerized
eye examination systems, or automated vision screening services without supervision
or authorization by a licensed optometrist.

      c) Online sale of contact lenses, ophthalmic lenses, or low-vision devices, in the
absence of a verified prescription by a licensed optometrist, is strictly prohibited.

        d) Establishing and operating an optical clinic by any unlicensed optometrist is
strictly prohibited. No corporation shall open or operate an optical clinic unless a
majority of its capital stock or voting shares is owned by a registered optometrist.

       e) It shall be unlawful for any juridical entity to interfere with, influence, or
otherwise impede the independent professional judgment of a licensed optometrist.
Prohibited acts under this Section include, but are not limited to:
          i.   Clinical Interference - Dictating clinical outcomes or bypassing
               established clinical protocols as mandated by the Professional
               Regulatory Board of Optometry and existing regulations;

         ii.   Commercial Quotas - Establishing, imposing, or requiring a minimum
               quota for the prescription or sale of optical devices, ophthalmic lenses,
               or contact lenses; and

        iii.   Professional Autonomy - Meddling with the diagnostic and treatment
               decisions of the optometrist.

       f) No person, optical establishment, or legal entity shall employ, hire, or allow
any individual to practice optometry or perform clinical duties without a valid Certificate
of Registration and a Professional Identification Card from the Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC). An optometry degree alone does not authorize professional
practice and shall be liable for the penalties provided in Section 33 of this Act.

        g) Any licensed optometrist exercising supervisory or managerial authority who
knowingly allows, conspires in, or tolerates unauthorized practice of optometry or
unauthorized clinical activity under their supervision shall be liable under Section 26 of
this Act. This liability is in addition to any administrative, civil, or criminal charges under
the Revised Penal Code, the Consumer Act, and other applicable laws. This provision
shall not apply to individuals undergoing clinical training, an internship, or academic
activities authorized by a duly accredited and recognized educational institution of
optometry.

       SEC. 6. Disclosure of Authority and Credentials to Practice. —

       a)     Physical Display of Credentials. – Every person authorized to practice
optometry under this Act shall display their original Certificate of Registration and a
valid, current Professional Identification Card (PIC) issued by the Professional
Regulation Commission (PRC) in a conspicuous place within the primary clinic or office
where they practice. In the case of multiple practice locations, a certified true copy of
the said credentials shall be displayed in each secondary branch.

        b)     Digital Disclosure and Tele-Optometry. – Optometrists engaging in
digital consultations or tele-optometry, as permitted by the Department of Health
(DOH) and PRC guidelines, shall clearly display their PRC registration number, date
of expiration, and a verifiable digital link or QR code leading to the PRC’s official
verification portal on their website, mobile application, or digital billing statements.

        c)    Identification in Professional Correspondence. – The optometrist shall be
required to indicate their Professional License Number and Professional Tax Receipt
(PTR) number on all official prescriptions, clinical reports, and laboratory referrals
issued by them.

         d)    Public Access to Verification. – The Board of Optometry, in coordination
with the PRC, shall maintain an updated online registry accessible to the public to verify
the current standing and validity of an optometrist’s authority to practice. Failure to
update professional records or practicing with an expired license shall constitute a
violation of this Section and shall be subject to administrative penalties as provided in
Section 32 of this Act.
                              BOARD OF OPTOMETRY

      SEC. 7. Board of Optometry. — There is hereby created a Board of Optometry,
hereinafter referred to as the Board, composed of five (5) members who shall be
appointed by the President of the Philippines, upon the recommendation of the PRC,
from a list of ten (10) nominees, 50% of the nominees shall be recommended by the
Accredited Integrated Professional Organization (AIPO), and 50% from recognized
optometric organizations or societies. A qualified optometrist may also apply directly.

       Any vacancies should be announced to the aforementioned organization and
the public at least one hundred twenty (120) days prior to vacancy, and nominations
shall be submitted at least forty-five (45) days prior to vacancy.

       SEC. 8. Minimum Qualifications of Board Members. — Only persons
possessed with the following qualifications may be nominated and appointed to the
Board:

       a) A natural-born citizen of the Philippines;

       b) A duly registered optometrist of good standing;

       c) At least thirty-five (35) years of age at the time of appointment;

       d) Of good moral character; and

        e) In active practice of the profession for at least ten (10) years prior to
appointment: Provided, That a person who is a member of the faculty, whether full
time, part time, or lecturer of any school, college, or university where a regular course
in optometry is taught, or who has any pecuniary interest, directly or indirectly, in such
an institution must forego membership in said faculty or relinquish any interests in said
institution before or upon taking oath as a member of the Board: Provided, further, That
an officer of any optometric association is disqualified from being appointed to or
serving in the Board.

       At least two (2) Board members shall be DPA-certified to ensure
pharmacological expertise in Optometry not covered in the standard four-year
curriculum and to maintain operational redundancy, should a DPA-certified member
be unavailable.

       SEC. 9. Term of Office. —The members of the Board shall serve a single, non-
renewable term of five (5) years, effective from the date of their appointment and
continuing until their successors are duly appointed. To ensure continuity through a
staggered system, the initial appointees to the Board, which may include incumbent
members of the existing Optometry Board, shall serve the following fixed terms: the
Chairperson for five (5) years, and the four (4) other members for four (4), three (3),
two (2), and one (1) year, respectively. Consequently, the term of one member shall
expire annually. Thereafter, a new member shall be appointed each year for a full five-
year term. The member serving the final year of their term shall automatically assume
the position of Board Chairperson.

      Any vacancy arising before the expiration of a term shall be filled for the
unexpired portion of that term only. No incumbent shall serve in a holdover capacity
for more than sixty (60) days following the expiration of the incumbent’s term.
       SEC. 10. Functions and Powers of the Board. — The Board is vested with
authority to:

      a) Promulgate the rules and regulations for the practice of optometry, subject to
the approval of the PRC and in accordance with the provisions of this Act;

       b) Administer and enforce the rules and regulations specified in the preceding
section;

       c) Determine and prepare the contents of licensure examinations, adopt
measures to ensure its integrity, check and rate the examination papers and submit
the results thereof to the PRC;

       d) Formulate and oversee, in consultation and coordination with the PRC,
guidelines for the proper conduct of licensure examinations and the registration of
optometrists;

     e) Issue, suspend, revoke, and reissue certificates of registration to qualified
optometrists and renew their professional licenses;

       f) Impose reasonable requirements and conditions, such as attendance in
continuing education activities and programs, for the renewal of an optometrist's
license to practice and for the purpose of upgrading the professional competence of
optometrists;

       g) Maintain a registry of optometrists in good standing, accessible to the public.

     h) Adopt, promulgate, and disseminate the Code of Ethics for the practice of
optometry in the Philippines;

      i) Administer oaths in connection with the implementation of this Act. When
recognized Optometric Associations request the presence of Board Examiners to
administer oath for new optometrists, they should accede to request.

        j) Conduct hearings and investigations to resolve complaints against
practitioners of optometry for malpractice, unethical and unprofessional conduct, and
violations of this Act or its implementing rules and regulations, and, in connection
therewith, issue subpoena ad testificandum and subpoena duces tecum and punish
with contempt persons obstructing, impeding, and/or otherwise interfering with the
conduct of such proceedings;

       k) Conduct ocular inspections of establishments and/or premises where
optometric services are offered to ascertain that there are no violations of this Act or
its IRR, and that professional and ethical standards are complied with;

      l) Upon request, complaint, or petition from the professional organization and/or
person(s), to issue an order for injunction or termination of illegal practice of Optometry
by unqualified persons.

       m) Impose fines and penalties, after due process and in accordance with the
provisions of this Act, for causes provided for by law or by the rules and regulations
promulgated thereof;
       n) Evaluate periodically the status of optometric education and recommend
and/or adopt measures to upgrade and maintain its high standards;

       o) Update the list of topical diagnostic pharmaceutical agents, specifically
mydriatics, cycloplegics, miotics, anesthetics and diagnostic dyes, that may be used
by optometrists, subject to the approval of the Department of Health and the Bureau of
Food and Drugs; and

       p) Study the conditions affecting the practice of optometry in all parts of the
Philippines, and, whenever necessary, adopt such measures as may be deemed
proper for the maintenance of good ethics and high professional standards of
optometric practice.

       SEC. 11. Votes Required in Administrative Cases. — To render a decision
in an administrative case for unethical practice, malpractice, immorality, dishonesty,
and related charges affecting the professional fitness and integrity of an optometrist,
the vote of majority, three (3) members must be obtained: Provided, That in case
members are disqualified to vote by virtue of their relation to or interest in any of the
parties or voluntarily inhibit themselves from participating in the proceedings, the Board
shall designate, with the approval of the Commission, an optometrist in good standing
who is respected in the optometric community for their probity and integrity to sit in the
Board, participate in the proceedings, and vote on the case.

       SEC. 12. Annual Report. — The Board shall, at the end of each calendar year,
submit to the Commission a comprehensive report detailing its activities and
proceedings. The Commission may request additional information or data as deemed
necessary and practicable. Records of final Board Resolutions and annual reports shall
be available for public access, subject to existing laws on public records and data
privacy.

                       OPTOMETRIC BOARD EXAMINATIONS

       SEC. 13. Requirements for Examination of Applicants. — To be eligible to
take the licensure examination in optometry, the applicant must be:

       a) A Filipino citizen;

       b) Of good moral character and ethical conduct;

       c) A holder of a degree of Doctor of Optometry (O.D.) obtained from a school,
college, university, or institution duly accredited by the government after passing at
least a six-year course offering the following minimum basic subjects: mathematics,
general and organic chemistry, practical and mechanical optics, psychology, physics,
physiological optics, pharmacology, theoretic optics, practical optometry, sociology,
applied psychology, general anatomy, physiology, general histology, and embryology,
ocular anatomy and physiology, general pathology, ocular pathology, biochemistry,
microbiology, clinical optometry, theoretic optometry, contact lens practice, orthoptics
and vision training, neuro-optometry and visual fields, research methods, optometric
ethics and jurisprudence, optometric economics and practice management, hygiene,
sanitation and public health optometry and low vision and pediatric optometry.

      SEC. 14. Report of the Result of the Examination. — The Board shall
transmit electronically the ratings obtained by each candidate to the PRC within fifteen
(15) days after the last licensure examination day, unless extended for justifiable
reasons by the Commission. The PRC shall, within seven (7) working days, cause the
publication of the names of successful examinees, with their respective examinee
numbers and ratings, on its official website and in other official publications of general
publication.

              REGISTRATION AND LICENSURE OF OPTOMETRISTS

       SEC. 15. Oath of Optometrists. — All successful examinees shall be required
to take their professional oath before the Board or before any person authorized by law
to administer oaths, including local elective officials and notaries public, prior to the
issuance of the Certificate of Registration. The Board shall not require a second oath-
taking if the initial oath was administered by a legally authorized person. No examinee
shall be denied registration on the sole ground that the oath was not administered by
a Board member, provided the applicant meets the qualifications under Section 24
hereof.

       Sec. 16. Renewal of Professional Licenses. — All licenses shall be renewable
every five (5) years and shall be considered renewed only upon satisfying the
requirements imposed by the Board, such as attendance in continuing education
programs and payment of the corresponding fees as may be fixed by the Board in
accordance with the schedule of fees fixed by the PRC.

                             OPTOMETRIC EDUCATION

       SEC. 17. Technical Panel on Optometric Education. — To ensure the global
competitiveness of Filipino Optometrists and maintain the highest standards of the
profession, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), pursuant to Republic Act
No. 7722, shall maintain a Technical Panel on Optometric Education. The composition
of the Technical Panel on Optometric Education shall ensure diversity, independence,
and balanced representation within the optometry profession.

       Technical Panel shall be organized and maintained in active consultation with
the recognized Council of Deans in Optometry and the various Schools and Colleges
of Optometry. The panel shall be composed of members nominated by their respective
sectors, ensuring balanced representation of senior practitioners, specialists, and
academicians.

        The Technical Panel shall be composed of members nominated by their
respective sectors, and that no single professional organization shall constitute a
majority of the Technical Panel, ensuring a balanced representation of senior
practitioners, specialists, academicians in the field of Optometry, and a representative
from the Accredited Integrated Professional Organization (AIPO) of Optometrists.

       The Panel’s mandates shall include:

       a) Adaptive Curriculum Design- Developing and implementing a minimum
standard curriculum that incorporates Basic therapeutics, Minor in-clinic Procedures
(e.g. eyelash epilation, emergency foreign body removal, chemical burns), and the
application of Artificial Intelligence in Eye Care;
        b) Clinical Residency Programs- Establishing standards for post-graduate
clinical residencies in sub-specialties, including but not limited to Pediatric Optometry,
Medical Contact Lenses, and Glaucoma Management;

       c) Global Accreditation and Benchmarking- Aligning local institutional standards
with international benchmarking bodies to ensure reciprocity and professional
excellence; and

       d) Lifelong Learning Framework- Integrating micro-credentialing and
specialized certifications to ensure faculty and practitioners remain updated with rapid
technological shifts.

       SEC. 18. Competence in Pharmacology. — Persons who have been admitted
to the practice of the profession prior to the six-year curriculum must take a course in
pharmacology, as prescribed by the Commission on Higher Education, and pass a
special certification examination to be administered by the Board before they are
allowed to use specific diagnostic pharmaceutical agents.

                   PROFESSIONAL AND ETHICAL STANDARDS

        SEC. 19. Foreign Reciprocity. — No foreigner shall be admitted to the
optometric board examinations unless he proves in the manner prescribed by the
Rules of Court that by specific provision of law, the country of which he is a citizen,
subject, or national either admits Filipino citizens to the practice of optometry without
restriction or allows them to practice optometry after an examination on terms of strict
and absolute equality with citizens, subjects, or nationals of said country, including the
unconditional recognition of prerequisite degrees prescribed by the Commission on
Higher Education: Provided, however, That the Board, upon approval by the PRC, may
grant a special permit to a foreign optometrist to practice the profession in this country
whether or not reciprocity exists between his country and the Philippines and under
such conditions as may be determined by the Board, if such foreigner is internationally
known to be an outstanding expert in the profession or a well-known specialist in any
of its branches, and that his services will promote the advancement of the profession
in the Philippines. The Board is also hereby authorized to prescribe, upon approval by
the PRC, additional requirements or grant certain privileges to foreigners seeking
registration in the Philippines, if the same privileges are granted to, or some additional
requirements are required of citizens of the Philippines in acquiring the same certificate
in his country.

       SEC. 20. Professional Standards. — The Board shall set professional
standards for the practice of optometry in the Philippines and adopt such rules and
regulations as may be necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act, subject to the
approval of the PRC. Such standards, rules, and regulations shall take effect thirty (30)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or any newspaper of general circulation.

       SEC. 21. Integration of Optometrists. — In order for every optometrist to
contribute to the profession and to bear their share of professional responsibilities, all
optometrists are encouraged to join and be integrated into one national organization
that shall be recognized by the Board and the PRC. Every optometrist, upon
registration with the Board, may become a member of the integrated national
organization. Membership in the integrated National Organization shall be voluntary.
No registered Optometrist shall be denied any right, privilege, benefit or opportunity
granted under this Act.
       SEC. 22. Purposes.—The Board of Optometry, after consultation with the
Integrated National Organization, and all other accredited and PRC – recognized
professional organizations of Optometrists, shall promulgate the Code of Ethics for the
practice of the profession, define the professional responsibilities of its members,
ensure adherence to said professional ethics and responsibilities, improve the
standards of the profession, and enable all optometrists to discharge their public
responsibility more effectively.

       SEC. 23. Membership Fees and Dues. — All members shall be subject to the
payment of reasonable fees and dues for the effective discharge of the purposes of
their national organization. An optometrist shall receive the benefits and privileges
appurtenant to his membership only upon payment of the required membership fees
and dues.

       SEC. 24. Other Organizations. — The integration of optometrists into one
national organization shall not prohibit the creation of other associations of
optometrists organized by individual optometrists themselves. The other organization
shall be recognized by the Board and the PRC as one of the stakeholders of the
profession and shall at all times be accorded appropriate representation whenever
policies, implementing rules and regulations, accreditation and training programs,
professional standards, or other matters pertinent to the Optometry profession are
being formulated or reviewed. No single Optometric professional organization shall be
deemed the exclusive representative of the Optometry profession in consultations with
government agencies, unless otherwise provided by law. Likewise, no PRC
recognized professional organization shall be prohibited from participating in official
professional activities, including the Oath taking ceremonies of newly registered
Optometrists.

      SEC. 25. Code of Professional Ethics. — The Board shall adopt the
Professional Code of Ethics promulgated by the integrated national organization of
optometrists: Provided, That, such Code provides that it is the duty of an optometrist
to:

          a) Consider as his primary responsibility the visual welfare of the public;

          b) Promote the best possible standards of professional care;

       c) Ensure continuous professional advancement and verified proficiency in both
theoretical knowledge and clinical practice.

          d) Render his services with equal diligence to all, regardless of race, creed, or
status;

       e) Maintain the absolute confidentiality of patient records and privileged
communications, except when disclosure is required by law or necessary to protect
public health and safety, in accordance with existing data privacy standards;

          f) Refer for medical or other professional attention where the need is indicated.

       The Board shall also distribute the Code of Ethics of the Optometric Profession
in the Philippines to all new optometrists during their oathtaking. Any act or omission
which violates this Code shall be considered as unethical conduct and shall be
suspended or prohibited from the practice of optometry, in accordance with Section 26
of this Act.

                        PROHIBITED ACTS AND PENALTIES

       SEC. 26. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are prohibited:
        a)     Giving false or forged evidence of any kind to the Board or to any
member thereof for the purpose of obtaining a certificate of registration or a
professional license;

         b)     Assumption, use or advertisement by a person, not holding a valid
certificate of registration and a valid professional license in accordance with this Act,
of a description, in connection with the person’s name or title, tending to convey the
impression that the person is a duly registered optometrist;

         c)   Any form of advertisement in media outlets giving information and
impression to the public that the particular optometrist is better than the others, is
collecting lower professional fees or selling cheaper frames or lenses;

        d)     The use of diagnostic pharmaceutical agents by an untrained
optometrist, in violation of Section 18 hereof, whether or not such use results in injury
to the patient;

        e)   The use by any optometrist of pharmaceutical agents other than those
which the optometrist is authorized to use;

         f)    Engaging in unauthorized practice of optometry as defined in Section 5
of this Act; and

        g)    Violation by any person, whether natural or juridical, of any of the
provisions of this Act and the rules and regulations promulgated for its effective
implementation.

       SEC. 27. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), through
the Professional Regulatory Board of Optometry, in coordination with the Department
of Health (DOH), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA), and after consultation with the Accredited Integrated
Professional Organization (AIPO) of Optometrists, recognized professional
organizations, academic institutions, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 28. Transitory Provision. — Registered optometrists who, at the time of
the effectivity of this Act, are duly licensed under Republic Act No. 8050 shall continue
to be authorized to practice the profession under the terms of their existing certificates
of registration and professional identification cards.

       Optometrists who graduated under the previous curriculum and who seek
authority to use diagnostic pharmaceutical agents or perform additional clinical
functions authorized under this Act shall comply with the competency requirements,
certification, or other qualifications that may be prescribed by the Professional
Regulatory Board of Optometry, subject to the approval of the Professional Regulation
Commission and in accordance with the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

       Nothing in this Act shall impair vested rights acquired under Republic Act No.
8050, provided that all practitioners shall comply with the requirements of this Act upon
the renewal of their professional licenses.

        SEC. 29. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

       SEC. 30. Repealing Clause. — Republic Act No. 8050 is hereby amended
accordingly. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 31. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
