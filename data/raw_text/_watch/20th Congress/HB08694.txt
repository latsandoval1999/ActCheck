                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to grant female employees in both the public and private
sectors two (2) days of paid menstrual leave per month to address menstrual-
related discomfort and health conditions. The proposed benefit is non-
cumulative and non-convertible to cash, ensuring that it is used solely for its
intended purpose of supporting women’s health in the workplace.
      The measure provides that the availment of menstrual leave shall not
require medical certification for up to two (2) days per month, thereby reducing
unnecessary administrative burden on employees while preserving ease of
access to the benefit. It further clarifies that employers already providing
equivalent or greater menstrual leave benefits shall be deemed compliant with
the provisions of this Act.
      To safeguard workers, the bill explicitly prohibits any form of
discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or adverse employment action against
employees who avail of menstrual leave. It affirms that such availment shall
not be a ground for termination, demotion, reduction of benefits, or non-
renewal of employment contracts.
     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

       AN ACT GRANTING MENSTRUAL LEAVE TO FEMALE EMPLOYEES,
        PROVIDING BENEFITS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1              SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Menstrual Leave
 2      Act.”

 3              SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall protect working women
 4      by providing safe and healthful working conditions, taking into account their
 5      maternal functions, and such facilities and opportunities that will enhance their
 6      welfare and enable them to realize their full potential in the service of the nation. To
 7      this end, the State shall ensure that the unique health needs of women are recognized
 8      in the workplace and that female employees are provided with menstrual leave
 9      benefits to safeguard their health, productivity and dignity.
10              SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all female employees, except
11      pregnant and menopausal women in the public and private sectors, regardless of
12      employment status, who experience menstrual discomfort or health conditions
13      associated with menstruation.




                                                                                              1
 1             Employers granting a menstrual leave or its equivalent are not covered by
 2   this act.
 3             SEC. 4. Menstrual Leave Benefits. – Female employees shall be entitled to
 4   two (2) days of menstrual leave with pay per month, to address menstrual discomfort
 5   or health conditions associated with menstruation. Such leave shall be non-
 6   cumulative and non-convertible to cash.
 7             Employers shall not require medical certification unless the employee avails
 8   of more than two (2) consecutive days of menstrual leave.
 9             SEC. 5. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act shall be
10   construed to reduce any existing benefits of any form granted under existing laws,
11   decrees, executive orders, or any contract, agreement or policy between employer
12   and employee.
13             SEC. 6. Non-Discrimination for availment of menstrual leave. – No
14   employee shall be subjected to any discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or
15   adverse action for availing of menstrual leave.
16             SEC. 7. Security of Tenure. – Any female employee who avails of the
17   menstrual leave benefits provided under this Act shall be entitled to security of
18   tenure. The availment of such benefit shall not constitute a just or authorized cause
19   for termination of employment, non-renewal of contract, demotion, reduction of
20   wages or benefits, or any form of discrimination, retaliation, or adverse employment
21   action.
22             SEC. 8. Penalty. – Any person, corporation, trust, firm, partnership,
23   association or entity found violating this Act or the rules and regulations
24   promulgated thereunder shall be punished by a fine not exceeding Two Hundred
25   Thousand Pesos (PHP 200,000.00).
26             The proceeds of fines collected under this Act shall be remitted to a special
27   fund dedicated to programs advancing women's health, promoting workplace equity,
28   and strengthening labor rights education.
