EXPLANATORY NOTE

The mango is one of the Philippines' most important fruit crops and is recognized by law
as a high-value crop under Republic Act No. 7900, otherwise known as the "High-Value Crops
Development Act of 1995." The law recognizes mango as one of the country's high-value fruit
crops and provides a framework to strengthen production, processing, marketing, distribution,
research, training, and financing for high-value crops.

Despite the mango industry’s vital role, a wealth of untapped potential remains to boost
productivity, strengthen climate resilience, improve postharvest practices, innovate processifg and
product development, and expand market reach and global competitiveness. The Department of
Science and Technology’s latest profile highlights mango as the national fruit and underscofes the
urgent need for strategic research, technology advancement, knowledge sharing, capacity building,
and robust policy support to propel the industry forward.

Zamboanga City stands out among the country’s mango-producing regions for its rich yet
often overlooked mango resources and remarkable genetic diversity. The city’s mangoes, among
the most underrated in the Philippines, offer a delightful local variety with a unique blend of
sweetness and tang, a small seed, delicate skin, and an irresistible aroma. Grown across several
barangays, this mango is a hidden agricultural gem, brimming with promise for scientific
exploration, varietal discovery, propagation, innovative product development, branding, and
commercial success.

Beyond its prized mango varieties, Zamboanga City and the greater Zamboanga Pemiinsula
are also home to the indigenous baluno (Mangifera caesia Jack), a unique mango-family fruit
native to Mindanao and its neighboring regions. Recognized in scientific and forestry literature,
the baluno tree thrives in these areas, bearing edible fruit that ripens into a sweet and delightful
treat.

The true value of these local and indigenous mango treasures goes far beyond their eurrent
commercial output. Their rich genetics, distinctive flavors and aromas, adaptability to local
environments, deep cultural roots, and exciting possibilities for food innovation and value-added
products make them deserving of dedicated research, conservation, propagation, and thoughtful
commercialization.

The Philippine mango industry boasts a robust production base. According to the
Philippine Mango Industry Roadmap, the Zamboanga Peninsula ranks among the nation’s key

mango-producing regions, and local agricultural data show that Zamboanga City dedicates
considerable land and resources to thriving mango cultivation.

This proposed measure aims to launch the Philippine Mango Science, Innovation, and
Sustainable Development Program, creating a unified platform for research, innoyation,
conservation, production, processing, value enhancement, technology transfer, capacity building,
market expansion, and export promotion.

The proposed Act shall complement and strengthen existing national policies and
programs, particularly:

1. Republic Act No. 7900, or the "High-Value Crops Development Act of 1995," which
recognizes mango as a high-value crop and provides for financing, prodiiction,
processing, marketing, research, experimental stations, seed farms, and farmer training.

2. Republic Act No. 7844, or the "Export Development Act of 1994," declares export
development a national strategy and establishes mechanisms for export promotion,
research and development, technical assistance, quality standards, market
development, and strengthening the Export Development Council.

3. Republic Act No. 10848, which further provided for the implementation of the
Agricultural Competitiveness Enhancement Fund (ACEF) and authorized support for
agricultural productivity, production and postharvest machinery and equipment,
processing facilities, agricultural research and development, and programs a to
enhance the competitiveness of farmers and agricultural sectors in domestic and
international markets.

This measure complements, not replicates, existing laws. It will establish a targeted policy
framework that unites government agencies, research and academic institutions, farmers’ groups,
cooperatives, local governments, and private-sector partners in building a science-driven, globally
competitive Philippine mango industry.

Specifically, the bill champions research into mango genetics, varietal discovery and
profiling, propagation, climate resilience, integrated pest and disease control, sustainable
cultivation, advanced postharvest technologies, food safety, innovative processing and packaging,
product development, and market intelligence, and responsible development of locally important
mango varieties and indigenous mango-family resources, including the Zamboanga City local
mango varieties and baluno, while ensuring that commercialization does not compromise
biodiversity, ecological sustainability, or the rights and interests of local farming and indigenous
communities.

The measure likewise recognizes that true competitiveness goes beyond production.
Philippine mangoes need state-of-the-art postharvest facilities, efficient cold-chain systems,
cutting-edge processing technologies, rigorous quality standards, strong branding, traceability,
dynamic market development, and export-ready infrastructure. national program, the Philippines
can transform its mango resources from primarily raw agricultural commodities into high»value,
science-driven products capable of competing in domestic and international markets.

Passing this measure will empower farmers, spark agricultural innovation, boost rural
enterprises, safeguard precious plant genetics, create jobs, open new doors for food processing and
agribusiness, and drive the nation’s sustainable agricultural and export growth.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Mango Seience,

Innovation, and Sustainable Development Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

(a) Recognize mango as a strategic high-value agricultural commodity and promote the

sustainable development of the Philippine mango industry;

(b) Strengthen scientific research, technology development, innovation, and technology
transfer relating to mango production, processing, preservation, marketing, and
utilization;

(c) Increase the productivity, income, resilience, and competitiveness of Filipino mango

farmers, farm workers, cooperatives, and enterprises;
