                             AN ACT
    ESTABLISHING AN EXTENDED PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY (EPR) AND
    TAKE-BACK SYSTEM FOR HYBRID AND ELECTRIC VEHICLE BATTERIES,
     PROVIDING FOR THEIR PROPER DISPOSAL, AND REGULATING THE
        IMPORTATION AND END-OF-LIFE MANAGEMENT THEREOF

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid adoption of hybrid vehicles has delivered real benefits: lower fuel
consumption, reduced tailpipe emissions, and improved air quality outcomes in
congested urban centers.1 These gains, however, can be short-lived if policy focuses
only on cleaner vehicles on the road while ignoring what happens when these
technologies reach end-of-life.2

       Hybrid and electric vehicles rely on high-voltage traction batteries with finite
service lives, typically degrading after 8-10 years or 150,000-200,000 km.3 As used
hybrid vehicles are imported and circulate in the domestic market, the country
inevitably inherits the end-of-life problem: failing traction batteries that are discarded,
stored indefinitely, or handled by informal channels without clear standards,
authorized facilities, or a functioning recycling market.4 Without a lifecycle
management system, “green” mobility risks becoming a future toxic waste liability.5

       This bill therefore: (a) establishes an EPR and take-back obligation for traction
batteries used in hybrid and electric vehicles; (b) requires registration, reporting, and
compliance plans for producers, importers, distributors, and assemblers; (c) mandates
authorized collection points and environmentally sound interim storage and

                          AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING AN EXTENDED PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY (EPR) AND
 TAKE-BACK SYSTEM FOR HYBRID AND ELECTRIC VEHICLE BATTERIES,
  PROVIDING FOR THEIR PROPER DISPOSAL, AND REGULATING THE
     IMPORTATION AND END-OF-LIFE MANAGEMENT THEREOF

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Hybrid and EV
Battery Disposal and Take-Back Act”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to: (a) promote a
sustainable transition to low-emission mobility through lifecycle-based
environmental governance; (b) ensure the environmentally sound management of
end-of-life traction batteries, consistent with the mandate of the DENR to promulgate
rules on recycling and disposal of EVs and batteries under Republic Act No. 11697
(EVIDA); and (c) prevent the Philippines from becoming a repository of end-of-life
hazardous components by ensuring that importation and distribution are coupled
with enforceable take-back, recovery, and disposal systems.

        SEC. 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to: (a) traction batteries used to propel
hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), and battery
electric vehicles (BEVs), including but not limited to nickel-metal hydride (NiMH) and
lithium-ion batteries; (b) battery packs, modules, and cells intended for traction use,
whether new or used; (c) producers and obliged enterprises engaged in the
manufacture, assembly, importation, distribution, sale, servicing, collection,
transport, recycling, refurbishment, or disposal of covered batteries.

       SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

        (a) “End-of-life traction battery” means a traction battery that is no longer fit for
its intended propulsion use due to age, failure, degradation, damage, or obsolescence.




                                           Page 3 of 6
       (b) “Obliged enterprise” means any person or entity that manufactures,
assembles, imports, distributes, or sells HEVs, PHEVs, BEVs, or traction batteries in
the Philippines, including entities importing used vehicles for resale.

       (c) “Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)” refers to the environmental policy
approach requiring producers to be environmentally responsible throughout the life
cycle of a product, especially at the post-consumer or end-of-life stage.

       (d) “Producer” includes brand owners, manufacturers, and importers of
vehicles or traction batteries placed in the Philippine market.

       (e) “Second-life application” refers to repurposing a traction battery for non-
propulsion use, subject to safety, testing, labeling, and traceability requirements set
by the DENR and DOE.

      (f) “Environmentally sound management” refers to collection, handling, storage,
transport, recycling, treatment, and disposal conducted in a manner protective of
human health and the environment, consistent with applicable environmental
standards.

       SEC. 5. Lead Implementing Agencies. — The DENR shall be the lead agency for
environmental compliance, waste management standards, permitting, and
enforcement under this Act, consistent with its role to promulgate rules and
guidelines on the recycling and disposal of EVs and batteries.

      The DOE shall support the DENR in policy coordination related to EV industry
development and roadmap alignment.

       SEC. 6. Battery EPR Registration and Compliance Plan. —

       (a) All obliged enterprises shall register with the DENR prior to placing covered
vehicles or traction batteries into the Philippine market.

      (b) Within a period to be set in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR),
each obliged enterprise shall submit a Battery EPR Compliance Plan containing, at
minimum:

               (1) estimated annual volume and types of traction batteries placed in the
       market;
               (2) collection and take-back mechanisms, including designated
       collection points nationwide;
               (3) interim storage standards and transport arrangements using
       authorized service providers;
               (4) partnerships with duly accredited recyclers, treaters, or disposal
       facilities;



                                        Page 4 of 6
              (5) targets, timetable, and financing for recovery, recycling,
       refurbishment, and final disposal;
              (6) consumer information and labeling measures on safe return and
       handling; and
              (7) reporting and audit protocols.

       (c) The DENR shall evaluate, approve, conditionally approve, or require
       revision of the plan.

       SEC. 7. Mandatory Take-Back and Collection System. —

        (a) Obliged enterprises shall establish and finance a convenient take-back and
collection system for end-of-life traction batteries, including batteries removed during
servicing, replacement, or decommissioning of vehicles.
        (b) No person shall dispose of traction batteries through ordinary municipal
waste streams.
        (c) The DENR shall prescribe minimum standards for collection points,
including fire safety, spill containment, inventory logs, and emergency response
measures.

       SEC. 8. Minimum Recovery and Diversion Targets. —

      (a) The DENR, in consultation with DOE and industry stakeholders, shall set
progressive minimum annual recovery and diversion targets for obliged enterprises,
considering local capacity and safety.
      (b) Compliance may be achieved through:
             (1) recycling into recoverable materials through accredited facilities;
             (2) refurbishment for second-life applications, only if the battery passes
      safety and performance criteria and is traceable; and
             (3) final treatment and disposal for non-recyclable fractions in
      accordance with applicable standards.

        SEC. 9. Import Safeguards for Used Hybrid and Electric Vehicles. — (a) Any
importer of used HEVs, PHEVs, or BEVs for resale shall, as a condition for import
clearance, comply with DENR registration and submit proof of participation in an
approved Battery EPR Compliance Plan. (b) The DENR, in coordination with the
Bureau of Customs and relevant agencies, shall require documentation ensuring
traceability of the vehicle and its traction battery, including battery chemistry/type
and serial or identification data, subject to IRR. (c) The DENR may require an
environmental handling fee or EPR guarantee mechanism to ensure financing of end-
of-life management.

       SEC. 10. Standards on Recycling, Disposal, and Waste Management. — The DENR
shall promulgate detailed rules and guidelines on the recycling and disposal of
traction batteries. Such rules shall include guidelines for accurate characterization,
disposal, handling, and management of wastes involving EVs and batteries.

      SEC. 11. Prohibited Acts. — The following are prohibited:

        (a) placing covered traction batteries in the market without DENR registration
and an approved EPR Compliance Plan;
        (b) dumping, open storage without authorization, or abandonment of end-of-
life traction batteries;
        (c) dismantling or extracting battery components by unauthorized persons or
facilities;
        (d) misdeclaration or concealment of used traction batteries or used
hybrid/electric vehicles to evade EPR obligations; and
        (e) falsification of reports, recovery credits, or audit records.

       SEC. 12. Incentives and Market Development Measures. — The DOE and DENR,
in coordination with relevant agencies, may recommend fiscal and non-fiscal
incentives to accelerate the establishment of compliant recycling and treatment
capacity, consistent with national EV industry development objectives.

      SEC. 13. Penalties. — The IRR shall provide graduated administrative penalties,
without prejudice to applicable civil and criminal liabilities under existing
environmental laws, including:

      (a) fines per violation and per day of continuing violation;
      (b) suspension or revocation of registration;
      (c) cease and desist orders; and
      (d) blacklisting for repeated and willful non-compliance.

        SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DENR, in consultation with the DOE, DTI, DOTr, Bureau of Customs,
and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the IRR.

        SEC. 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

       SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.


      Approved.
