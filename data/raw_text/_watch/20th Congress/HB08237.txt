13                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The Philippines remains one of the most oil-import dependent economies in Asia.
16   Virtually all petroleum products consumed in the country—diesel, gasoline, kerosene,
17   and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG)—are sourced from international markets, making
18   the domestic economy highly vulnerable to geopolitical shocks affecting global oil
19   supply.
20
21   Recent events in the Middle East have once again demonstrated this vulnerability.
22   The ongoing armed conflict involving the United States, Israel, and Iran has triggered
23   severe disruptions in global energy markets. Military strikes and retaliatory actions
24   in the Persian Gulf have placed the Strait of Hormuz—one of the most critical oil
25   transit chokepoints in the world—at risk of closure or restricted passage. This
26   maritime corridor alone carries roughly twenty percent (20%) of the world’s oil
27   supply and a significant share of liquefied natural gas shipments.
28
29   As a result of the conflict and the threat to global oil transport routes, international
30   crude oil prices have already surged sharply, with analysts warning that prices may
31   climb toward or even exceed USD 100 per barrel should the crisis escalate further.
32   The disruption of tanker traffic and the suspension of shipping operations through
33   the Strait of Hormuz have intensified supply fears, causing immediate volatility in
34   global energy markets and raising fuel costs worldwide.
35
36   For countries like the Philippines, these developments have immediate and direct
37   consequences. Rising global oil prices translate into higher pump prices, increased
38   transportation costs, higher electricity rates, and inflationary pressures across
39   essential goods such as food and basic commodities. The burden is ultimately borne
40   by ordinary Filipino consumers, workers, farmers, and small businesses.
41
42   In such periods of global energy crisis, it is incumbent upon the State to adopt
43   responsive policy measures that mitigate the economic impact on the Filipino people.
44   One immediate intervention available to Congress is the removal of government-
45   imposed excise taxes on petroleum products.
46
47   At present, the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended by Republic Act No.
48   10963 or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law, imposes excise
49   taxes on petroleum products such as diesel, gasoline, kerosene, fuel oil, and LPG.
50   While these taxes were originally justified as part of broader fiscal reforms, they also




                                                1
 1   add a fixed cost component to fuel prices regardless of international market
 2   conditions.
 3
 4   During periods of extraordinary global price shocks, maintaining such taxes
 5   exacerbates the financial burden on consumers and industries that depend on fuel.
 6   By abolishing the excise tax on petroleum products, the government can immediately
 7   cushion the impact of rising international oil prices and help stabilize domestic fuel
 8   costs.
 9
10   This proposed measure therefore seeks to abolish the excise taxes imposed on diesel,
11   kerosene, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), fuel oil, and unleaded gasoline under
12   Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
13
14   This policy is not merely a fiscal adjustment. It is an economic relief measure designed
15   to protect Filipino households and sustain economic stability during a period of global
16   geopolitical uncertainty and energy market disruption.
17
18   In times of international crisis beyond our control, government policy must remain
19   responsive to the needs of the people. The abolition of excise taxes on petroleum
20   products is a necessary and timely step to shield the Filipino public from the full
21   impact of the ongoing global oil crisis.
22
23   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

13                              AN ACT
14   ABOLISHING THE EXCISE TAX IMPOSED ON DIESEL, KEROSENE,
15   LIQUEFIED PETROLEUM GAS, FUEL OIL AND UNLEADED GASOLINE
16   OIL, AMENDING SECTION 148 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL
17   REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED
18
19   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representative of the Philippines in
20   congress assembled:
21
22   Section 1. Abolition of Excise Tax.
23   The imposition of excise tax on diesel, kerosene, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), fuel
24   oil and unleaded gasoline oil enumerated under Section 148 of the National Internal
25   Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is hereby abolished.
26
27   Sec. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
28   Within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance shall,
29   upon the recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, promulgate the
30   rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.
31
32   Sec. 3. Separability Clause.
33   Should any provision or part of this Act be declared unconstitutional or invalid, the
34   other provisions and parts hereof, insofar as they are separable from the invalid ones,
35   shall remain in full force and effect.
36
37   Sec. 4. Repealing Clause.
38   The provisions on the imposition of excise taxes on diesel, kerosene, liquefied
39   petroleum gas (LPG), fuel oil and unleaded gasoline oil under Republic Act No. 10963
40   or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Law, BIR Revenue Regulation No.
41   2-2018, and other laws, executive orders, and decrees, insofar as they impose excise
42   tax on said petroleum products, are likewise repealed.
43   All other laws, decrees, orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts thereof which
44   are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
45
46   Sec. 5. Effectivity.
47   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette
48   or in a newspaper of general circulation.
