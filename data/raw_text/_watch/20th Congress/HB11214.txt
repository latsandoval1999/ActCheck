AUTHORITY (TESDA), AMENDING AND REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE

FOR OTHER PURPOSES
                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) plays a critical role
in preparing Filipinos for employment, entrepreneurship, and lifelong learning by providing
opportunities to acquire practical and industry-relevant skills. As the Philippine economy continues
to evolve, however, the nature of work, the technologies used in various industries, and the
competencies required of workers are likewise changing at an unprecedented pace. These
developments call for a modern, responsive, and forward-looking technical education and skills
development system capable of keeping pace with the needs of both Filipino workers and the
industries that employ them.

         The TESDA Modernization Act of 2026 is therefore necessary to strengthen the country's
technical education and skills development framework and ensure that it remains relevant to the
realities of a rapidly changing labor market. While the Philippines has made significant progress in
expanding access to technical-vocational education and training, continuing challenges remain in
ensuring that training programs are sufficiently responsive to emerging industries, technological
change, regional labor market needs, and the evolving expectations of employers. There is a need to
move beyond simply increasing access to training and to place greater emphasis on quality,
relevance, employability, mobility, and lifelong skills development.

        A modern technical education system must be closely connected to the actual world of work.
Employers and industries are not merely beneficiaries of a skilled workforce; they are essential
partners in identifying the competencies that workers need, shaping training standards, and
providing meaningful opportunities for practical learning. Strengthening collaboration among
government, industry, labor, educational institutions, and local governments will help ensure that
skills development is driven by actual economic and employment opportunities rather than by
training supply alone.

        The proposed measure also recognizes that Filipinos acquire knowledge and skills through
different pathways. Formal education is only one avenue for learning. Work experience,
apprenticeships, community-based training, enterprise-based learning, and other forms of skills
development can likewise produce valuable competencies. A more flexible and integrated system
can provide workers and learners with opportunities to have their skills recognized, upgraded, and
further developed, while allowing them to move more easily between technical-vocational education
and higher education.

        Equally important is the need to make skills development more accessible and responsive to
communities and regions. The country's labor market is diverse, with different localities having
distinct economic activities, industries, and workforce requirements. A strengthened partnership
with local governments and private-sector institutions can bring training opportunities closer to
communities and allow skills development programs to respond more effectively to local needs,
including those of marginalized and underserved learners.

        Modernization must likewise be supported by stronger institutional capacity, effective
quality assurance, adequate investment, research and innovation, competent trainers, and sustainable
financing. TESDA must be equipped to anticipate future workforce requirements, continuously
improve training systems, and support the development of internationally competitive Filipino
workers. At the same time, mechanisms for accountability and periodic assessment are essential to
ensure that reforms remain responsive to national development priorities.

       Ultimately, the proposed TESDA Modernization Act of 2026 seeks to ensure that technical
education and skills development remains a powerful instrument for inclusive economic growth,
decent employment, productivity, and national competitiveness. By strengthening the country's
capacity to develop a quality-assured and future-ready workforce, the measure will help Filipino
workers adapt to changing opportunities, enable industries to access the skills they require, and
contribute to a more resilient and competitive Philippine economy.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "TESDA Modernization Act of 2026."
 2   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to provide relevant,
 3   accessible, high-quality, and efficient technical education and skills development in support of the
 4   development of a quality-assured Filipino workforce, responsive to and in accordance with
 5   Philippine development goals and priorities. The State shall encourage the active participation of
 6   various concerned sectors, particularly private enterprises, as direct participants in and immediate
 7   beneficiaries of a trained and skilled workforce, in providing technical education and skills
 8   development opportunities.
 9   SEC. 3. Objectives. – It is the goal and objective of this Act to:
10      (a) Promote and strengthen the quality of technical education and skills development programs
11          to attain international competitiveness;
12      (b) Focus technical education and skills development on meeting the changing demands for
13          quality-assured workforce;
14      (c) Encourage innovation, continuous learning, growth-mindset, and collaboration by
15          disseminating the scientific and technical knowledge base of workforce development
16          programs;
17      (d) Recognize and encourage the complementary roles of public and private institutions in
18          technical education and skills development and training system; and
19      (e) Inculcate desirable values through the development of moral character with emphasis on
20          work ethic, self-discipline, self-reliance and nationalism.
21   SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
22      (a) "Apprentice" is a person undergoing training for an approved apprenticeable occupation
23          during an established period assured by an apprenticeship agreement;
24      (b) "Apprenticeable Occupation" is an occupation officially endorsed by a tripartite body and
25          approved for apprenticeship by the Authority;
26      (c) "Apprenticeship" refers to an enterprise-based education and training (EBET) program that
27          covers higher levels of competencies covered by National Certificates (NC) 3 and above.
28          Apprenticeship shall be for trainees at least eighteen (18) years old;
29      (d) "Apprenticeship Agreement" is a contract wherein a prospective employer binds himself to
30          train the apprentice who in turn accepts the terms of training for a recognized apprenticeable
31          occupation emphasizing the rights, duties and responsibilities of each party;
32   (e) "Applied Bachelor's Degrees" refer to a generally four-year degree program that combines
33       academic learning with a substantial proportion of practical or on-the-job experience,
34       targeted for specific career paths instead of academic or research-focused outcomes;
35   (f) “Associate Degrees" refers to post-secondary qualifications attained after completion of the
36       K to 12 basic education program, normally requiring a minimum of two years of study. It
37       equips learners with specialized skills for gainful employment in related professions, while
38       also providing the academic preparation necessary for progression to baccalaureate
39       programs;
40   (g) "Bachelor's Degree Program" refers to an undergraduate university degree that requires the
41       completion of a secondary-level education and involves 4 years of full-time study for most
42       programs, consisting of general education courses and specialized subjects focused on a
43       major degree;
44   (h) "Community-Based Technical Vocational Education and Training (CBTVET)" refers to
45       TVET programs intended to address specific needs of the community or local government
46       unit that may be delivered in an informal or formal setting, implemented with assistance and
47       coordination of the authority;
48   (i) "Community-Training and Employment Coordinators" refers to personnel of local
49       government units trained by the authority to plan, organize, manage and monitor community-
50       based training and enterprise development programs of local governments;
51   (j) "Competency-Based Training" refers to a system by which the learner acquires a
52       competency or a qualification based on demonstrated ability;
53   (k) "Digital Badges" serve as visual representations of micro-credentials, symbolizing skill
54       achievement and accomplishments;
55   (l) "Diploma" refers to a credential awarded after completing a compressed and technically-
56       focused program, specializing in a particular trade or vocational skill, and corresponds to a
57       level 5 qualification in the PQF framework;
58   (m) "Doctoral Degree" refers to the highest academic degree, earned by conducting significant
59       research and producing original work, typically in a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) or a
60       practice-based professional doctorate.
61   (n) "Dual System/Training" refers to a delivery system of quality technical and vocational
62       education which requires training to be carried out alternately in two venues: In school and
63       in the production plant. In school training provides the trainee the theoretical foundation,
64       basic training, guidance and human formation, while in-plant training develops his skills and
65       proficiency in actual work conditions as it continues to inculcate personal discipline and
66       work values;
67   (o) "Ladderized Education" refers to the harmonization of all education and training
68       mechanisms that allow students and workers to progress between technical-vocational and
69       higher education programs, or vice versa. It opens opportunities for career and educational
70       advancement to students and workers. It creates a seamless and borderless education and
71       training system that will allow transfers in terms of flexible entry and exit between technical-
72       vocational and higher education programs in the post-secondary school educational system;
73   (p) "Levy Grant System" refers to a legal contribution from participating employers who would
74       be beneficiaries of the program (often as a percentage of the payroll) which is subsequently
75       turned over or rebated to enterprises offering employee training programs;
76   (q) "Master's Degree" refers to a postgraduate academic program that provides advanced and
77       specialized knowledge and skills beyond a bachelor's degree;
78   (r) "Micro-credentials" refers to records of focused learning achievement that verifies what the
79       learner knows, understands, or can do. It meets the standards required by relevant quality
80       assurance frameworks and offers flexibility, making it stackable and targeted toward specific
81       competencies that can be assessed and verified;
82   (s) "Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF)" refers to the national policy that describes the
83       levels of educational qualifications and sets standards for learning outcomes across all
84       education and training sectors in the Philippines, namely basic education, technical-
 85      vocational education and training (TVET), higher education, and post-graduate scale of
 86      completion;
 87   (t) "Polytechnic College or Technological College" refers to a college that offers the following:
 88        (i)   At least three (3) or more programs that grant or award NC levels 4-5 in TESDA's
 89              National Competency Assessment scale, that provide pathways towards bachelor's
 90              degree programs offered by the same institution;
 91       (ii)   Three (3) or more ladderized and/or applied bachelor's degree programs; and
 92      (iii)   A highly specialized or technical industry on-the-job training program for all of its
 93              offered programs;
 94   (u) "Polytechnic Institute or Technological Institute" refers to post-secondary education
 95       institutions with a focused and technical industry-driven or curricula, that offer at least three
 96       (3) or more programs that grant or award National Certificates (NC) to qualified recipients,
 97       with a corresponding diploma or similar credential and an established on-the-job training
 98       program;
 99   (v) "Polytechnic University or Technological University" refers to a university that offers the
100       following:
101        (i)   All of the cited qualifications for a polytechnic or technological college;
102       (ii)   Three (3) or more master's or doctoral programs in applied fields, including but not
103              limited to highly technical or technologically-focused professions such as
104              engineering or other applied sciences; and
105      (iii)   An expansive and direct linkage of its degree programs to practical engagements with
106              applicable industries;
107   (w) "Private Enterprises" refers to an economic system under which property of all kinds can be
108       privately owned and in which individuals, alone or in association with another, can embark
109       on a business activity. This includes industrial, agricultural, or agro-industrial establishments
110       engaged in the production, manufacturing, processing, repacking or assembly of goods
111       including service-oriented enterprises;
112   (x) "Quality-assured Workforce" refers to those:
113        (i)   Who have acquired practical skills and knowledge through formal or non-formal
114              education and training equivalent to at least a secondary education but preferably a
115              post-secondary education with a corresponding associate degree or diploma, as
116              defined in this Act; or
117       (ii)   Skilled workers who have become highly competent in their trade or craft, as attested
118              by industry;
119   (y) "Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)" refers to the acknowledgement of a person's skills
120       and knowledge acquired through previous training, work or life experience, which may be
121       used to grant status, credit, or qualification. It is also defined as a process of identifying,
122       documenting, assessing, and certifying formal, non-formal and informal learning outcomes
123       against standards used in formal education and training. Thus, RPL provides an opportunity
124       for people to acquire qualifications or credits towards a qualification or exemptions (from
125       all or part of the curriculum, or even exemption from an academic prerequisite for entering
126       a formal study program) without going through a formal education or training program;
127   (z) "Skill" shall mean the acquired and practiced ability to carry out a task or job;
128   (aa)         "Skills Development" shall mean the process through which learners and workers are
129       systematically provided with learning opportunities to acquire or upgrade, or both, their
130       ability, knowledge and behavior pattern required as qualifications for a job or range of jobs
131       in a given occupational area;
132   (bb)      "Technical Education" shall refer to the education process designed at post-
133      secondary and lower tertiary levels, officially recognized as non- degree programs, including
134      associate degrees and diploma programs, aimed at preparing technicians, para-professionals
135          and other categories of skilled workers by providing them with a broad range of general
136          education, theoretical, scientific and technological studies, and related job skills training;
137      (cc)        "Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET)" refers to the education or
138          training process where it involves, in addition to general education, the study of technologies
139          and related sciences and acquisition of practical skills relating to occupations in various
140          sectors of economic life and social life, comprises formal (organized programs as part of the
141          school system) and non-formal (organized classes outside the school system) approaches;
142      (dd)      "Tertiary Education" refers to formal post-secondary programs offered by degree-
143         granting colleges, universities, and diplomas offered by TVIs. It shall encompass both higher
144         education and TVET;
145      (ee)        "Trainees" shall mean persons who are participants in a vocational, administrative,
146          or technical training program for the purpose of acquiring and developing job-related skills;
147      (ff) "Trainors/Trainers" shall mean persons who provide training to trainers aimed at developing
148           the latter's capacities for imparting attitudes, knowledge, skills, and behavior patterns,
149           required for specific jobs, tasks, occupations, or groups of related occupations;
150      (gg)       "Trade" shall mean any group of interrelated jobs or any occupation which is
151         traditionally or officially recognized as craft or artisan in nature requiring specific
152         qualifications that can be acquired through work experience and/or training;
153      (hh)       "TVET institute or TVI" refers to postsecondary education institutions that offer
154         short-term non-degree, diploma or certificate technical-vocational education and training
155         programs or courses;
156      (ii) "User-Led" or "Market-Driven Strategy" refers to a strategy which promotes strengthened
157           linkages between educational/training institutions and industry to ensure that appropriate
158           skills and knowledge are provided by the educational system; and
159      (jj) "Work-Based Learning" refers to learning that occurs in a real work environment and equips
160           individuals with the necessary skills to obtain, maintain, and progress in their jobs and
161           careers.

447   SEC. 16. Compliance with the Salaries Standardization Law. – The compensation and
448   emoluments of the officials and employees of the Authority shall be in accordance with the salary
449   standardization law and other applicable laws under the national compensation and classification
450   plan. All directors duly appointed by the Director General shall serve at the corresponding plantilla
451   position provided in the salary grade of their appointment, and shall not be subject to diminution in
452   the corresponding salary grade and benefits afforded to that position.
453   SEC. 17. Consultants and Technical Assistance, Publication and Research. – In pursuing its
454   objectives, the Authority is hereby authorized to set aside a portion of its appropriation for the hiring
455   of services of qualified consultants and private organizations for research work and publication in
456   the field of technical education and skills development. It shall avail itself of the services of other
457   agencies of the Government as may be required.
458   SEC. 18. Technical Education and Skills Development Committees. – The Authority shall
459   establish Technical Education and Skills Development Committees at the regional and local levels
460   to coordinate and monitor the delivery of all skills development activities by the public and private
461   sectors. These committees shall likewise serve as the Technical Education and Skills Development
462   Committees of the Regional and local development councils. The composition of the Technical
463   Education and Skills Development Committees shall be determined by the Director-General, subject
464   to the guidelines to be promulgated by the Authority.
465   SEC. 19. Skills Development Centers. – The Authority shall establish regional centers that shall
466   serve as exemplars for niche, higher-level qualifications, as well as ladderized offerings, relative to
467   the unique needs of the region, and in support of the local public and private providers in the area.
468   Said centers shall iterate new programs, provide training for trainers, lead the formulation of
469   responsive regional initiatives and programs vis-à-vis local labor market information, and other
470   similar initiatives. These centers shall be administered and operated under such rules and regulations
471   as may be established by the Authority in accordance with the National Technical Education and
472   Skills Development Plan.
473   SEC. 20. Establishment and Administration of National Trade Skills Standards. – There shall be
474   national occupational skills standards to be established by TESDA-accredited industry committees,
475   prioritizing those established and conducted by industries. The Authority shall develop and
476   implement a certification and accreditation program in which private industry groups and trade
477   associations are accredited to conduct approved trade tests, and the local government units to
478   promote such trade testing activities in their respective areas in accordance with the guidelines to be
479   set by the Authority. This prioritization shall aim to shorten the development cycle of competency
480   standards, competency assessment tools and training regulations, and ensure timely rollout of
481   higher-level qualifications, thereby aligning workforce competencies with emerging industry
482   requirements.
483   The Director General shall determine the occupational trades for mandatory certification.
484   All certificates relating to the national trade skills testing and certification system shall be issued by
485   the Authority through the TESDA Secretariat.
486   SEC. 21. Recognition of Alternative Assessment Systems. – The Authority shall duly recognize
487   and adapt industry-based and other systems of skills assessment, qualification and certification,
488   including but not limited to work-based learning, competency-based training, recognition of prior
489   learning, micro-credentials, and digital badges.
490   SEC. 22. Assistance to Employers and Organizations. – In consonance with RA No. 12063,
491   otherwise known as the "Enterprise-Based Education and Training Framework Act", the Authority
492   shall assist any employer or organization engaged in skills training schemes designed to attain the
493   objectives of relevant and accessible skills training provision.
494   SEC. 23. Industry Boards. – The Authority shall establish effective and efficient institutional
495   arrangements with industry boards and such other bodies or associations to provide direct
496   participation of employers and workers in the design and implementation of skills development
497   schemes, trade skills standardization and certification and such other functions in the fulfillment of
498   the Authority's objectives. These industry boards shall be formed in order to discuss broad-based
499   policies and direction of TESDA in determining the continuation, abolition and propagation of new
500   programs, skills training and other applicable proposals to meet industry needs.
501   To ensure coherence with the education system, said Boards shall work closely with the Department
502   of Education Bureau of Curriculum Development, the CHED Technical Panels, as well as the PRC
503   Professional Regulatory Boards to ensure and enable smooth learning transitions, well-designed
504   ladderized offerings, and alignment in curriculum and assessment.
505   SEC. 24. Incentives Schemes. – The Authority shall develop and administer appropriate incentive
506   schemes to encourage government and private industries and institutions to provide high-quality
507   technical education and skills development opportunities.
508   SEC. 25. Devolution of TESDA's Training Function to Local Governments. – In establishing the
509   delivery system provided for in the preceding Section, the Authority shall formulate, implement and
510   finance a specific plan to develop the capability of local government units to assume ultimately the
511   responsibility for effectively providing community-based technical education and skills
512   development opportunities: Provided, however, That there shall be formulated and implemented,
513   and effective and timely retraining of TESDA personnel that would be affected by the devolution to
514   ensure their being retained if the concerned local government units would not be able to absorb
515   them.
516   SEC. 26. Skills Olympics. – To promote quality skills development in the country and with the view
517   of participating in international skills competitions, the Authority, with the active participation of
518   private industries, shall organize and conduct annual National Skills Olympics. The Authority,
519   through the TESDA Secretariat, shall promulgate the necessary rules and guidelines for the effective
520   and efficient conduct of the Annual National Skills Olympics and for the country's participation in
521   international skills olympics.
522   SEC. 27. The TESDA Development Fund. – A TESDA Development Fund is hereby established,
523   to be managed/ administered by the Authority, the income from which shall be utilized exclusively
524   in awarding of grants and providing assistance to training institutions, industries, local government
525   units for upgrading their capabilities and to develop and implement training and training-related
526   activities. Furthermore, the TESDA Development Fund shall be reoriented to serve as the main
527   source of support for the operations of the industry TVET boards (ITBs), which is crucial for
528   ensuring the agency can effectively lead in developing a future-ready workforce and elevating the
529   quality and relevance of TVET across the country. The contribution to the fund shall be the
530   following:
531      (a) A one-time lump sum appropriation from the National Government;
532      (b) An annual contribution from the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration Fund;
533      (c) Donations, grants, endowments, and other bequests or gifts; and
534      (d) Any other income generated by the Authority.
535   The Board of Advisers, through its Secretariat, shall be the administrator of the fund, and as such,
536   shall formulate the necessary implementing guidelines for the management of the fund, subject to
537   the following:
538      (a) Unless otherwise stipulated by the private donor, only earnings of private contributions shall
539          be used; and
540       (b) No part of the seed capital of the fund, including earnings, thereof, shall be used to
541           underwrite expenses for administration.
542   The Board shall appoint a reputable government-accredited investment institution as fund manager,
543   subject to guidelines promulgated by the Board.
544   SEC. 28. Scholarship Grants. – Pursuant to the provisions of RA No. 11230, the Authority shall
545   adopt a system of allocation and funding of scholarship grants which shall be responsive to the
546   technical education and skills development needs of the different regions in the country. This system
547   shall include a systematic targeting mechanism to accurately identify and prioritize genuinely poor
548   but committed scholars in every region, ensuring guaranteed allocation under mandatory
549   appropriations and compliance with laws that expand TESDA's clientele.
550   Consistent with RA No. 10687 or ""Unified Student Financial Assistance System for Tertiary
551   Education (UniFAST) Act", the Authority shall work closely with the UNIFAST Secretariat in
552   conducting and/or commissioning reputable research organizations or universities, to undertake
553   regular impact evaluations for its scholarship grants at least every three (3) years, with the aim of
554   informing program design, subsidy amounts, and other needed interventions, in order to improve
555   efficiency and efficacy of programs. Said studies shall be submitted to the TESDA Board, to the
556   Department of Budget and Management, and to Congress.
557   SEC. 29. Automatic Review. – Every five (5) years, after the effectivity of this Act, an independent
558   review panel composed of three (3) persons appointed by the President shall review the performance
559   of the Authority and shall make recommendations, based on its findings, to the President and to both
560   Houses of Congress.
