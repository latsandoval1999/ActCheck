                THEREOF AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Every day, millions of Filipinos rely on the labor of vendors, transport workers,
home-based producers, and small-scale service providers operating in the informal
economy. Yet despite their indispensable contribution to economic life, many of these
workers remain outside the reach of labor protection and social security systems. Many
operate without formal recognition, job security, or access to social protection
programs. Women workers, in particular, face additional barriers due to persistent
gender inequality and the absence of adequate support services such as childcare and
reproductive health programs.

        The Constitution recognizes labor as a primary social and economic force and
mandates the State to promote social justice and protect the rights and welfare of
workers. 1 This includes extending protection and opportunities to sectors that have
historically been excluded from formal economic systems. It is therefore a
constitutional and social imperative to ensure that workers in the informal economy
are afforded rights, protections, and opportunities that will enable them to improve
their living conditions and contribute more productively to national development.

       This proposed measure seeks to establish a comprehensive framework that
affirms the fundamental rights of workers in the informal economy. These include
security of and in the workplace, safe and healthy working conditions, equal


1 Article II, Section 18, 1987 Philippine Constitution
remuneration for work of equal value, freedom from discrimination, and the right to
self-organization and participation in decision-making processes.

        The bill likewise seeks to strengthen the economic capacities of informal
workers by improving access to markets, financial services, training, technology, and
infrastructure support. It also provides safeguards against unjust eviction from
workplaces and arbitrary relocation of vending sites and transport terminals.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

10   Sec. 2. Declaration of P olicy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:
11      (a) Promote a just and dynamic social order that shall ensure the prosperity and
12          independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies
13          that provide adequate social services; promote decent, environmentally sound
14          and full employment; a rising standard of living; and an improved quality of life

17          non-discrimination, the right to self-organization, just and humane conditions
18          of work, access to social protection programs and services, access to justice,
19          security of and in the workplaces, and the right to represent their organizations
20          in a continuing process of consultation and social dialogue, with equitable
21          membership in relevant tripartite bodies; and

 5   Sec. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all workers and economic units – including
 6   enterprises, entrepreneurs and households – in the informal economy, in particular:
 7      (a) Those who own and operate economic units including:
 8              i. self-employed workers;
 9             ii. self-employed own-account workers;
10            iii. employers;
11           iv. members of cooperatives; and
12             v. members of other social and solidarity economy units;
13      (b) Home-based workers or contributing family workers, irrespective of whether
14          they work in economic units in the formal or informal economy;
15      (c) Employees holding informal jobs in or for formal enterprises, or for economic
16          units in the informal economy, including but not limited to those in contract
17          arrangement and/or in supply chains, or as non-regular paid domestic workers
18          employed by households;
19      (d) Workers in unrecognized or unregulated employment relationships, provided
20          that the work performed is not illegal;
21      (e) Volunteer; contractual; job orders; and other vulnerable workers in government
22          instrumentalities; and
23      (f) Agricultural workers or fisherfolk in unrecognized or unregulated agricultural or
24          farming endeavors and fishing activities.

26   Sec. 4. Definition of Term s. – For the purposes of this Act, the following terms are
27   defined as follows:
28
29      (a) Informal Economy refers to “all economic activities by workers and economic
30          units that are, in law or in practice, not covered or insufficiently covered by
31          formal arrangements.”
32      (b) Economic Units in the informal economy include but are not limited to:
33              a. Units that employ hired labor;
34              b. units that are owned by individuals working on their own account, either
35                 alone or with the help or contributing family workers;
36              c. cooperatives and other social and solidarity economy units; and
37              d. informal work that may be found across all sectors of the economy, in
38                 households, as well as public and private spaces including government
39                 instrumentalities and Government Owned and Controlled Corporations
40                 (GOCCs)
41      (c) Informal Economy Workers’ Organization refers to a group or groups of
42          informal workers, whether formally constituted or otherwise, that are organized
43          with the primary objective of promoting the rights and welfare of workers in
44          informal economy.
45      (d) Informal employment refers to an employment arrangement in the formal
46          or informal sectors or in households that exist based mostly on casual
 1       employment, kinship or personal and social relations with no formal guarantees
 2       on legal and social protection and employment benefits, and prevalent in
 3       economic units that are unregistered, unlicensed and unregulated.
 4   (e) Security of and in the Workplace refers to the right of every worker and
 5       informal economic unit to an enabling environment that guarantees and
 6       protects the spaces, facilities, and structures to undertake their work, including
 7       the right to feel safe in one’s own work space, legal security of tenure and
 8       freedom from discrimination, risk, danger, doubt, anxiety, or fear of being
 9       removed, evicted or prevented from working.
10   (f) Social Protection refers to policies and programs that seek to reduce poverty
11       and vulnerability to risks and enhance the social status of the marginalized by
12       promoting and protecting livelihood and employment, protecting against
13       hazards and sudden loss of income, and improving people’s capacity to manage
14       risk, as well as all other interventions that support communities, households,
15       and individuals, both women and men, and realizing their rights as citizens
16       through their full participation in decision-making affecting or may affect their
17       access to and control over resources necessary to maintain and sustain a
18       decent and secure life.
19
20       Social protection schemes shall include labor market interventions, social
21       insurance, social welfare, safety nets, disaster risk reduction and management
22       mechanisms, adaptive measures in the context of climate change, affordable
23       housing, and other provisions necessary for vulnerable groups to adequately
24       address and manage risks, vulnerabilities, and threats to workers’ livelihood
25       activities and overall quality of life.
26   (g) Social and Solidarity Economy Units refers to enterprises and
27       organizations including cooperatives as defined under Republic Act No. 9520,
28       mutual benefit societies, associations, foundations and social enterprises which
29       produce goods, services and knowledge that meet the needs of the community
30       they serve, through the pursuit of specific social and environmental objectives
31       and the fostering of solidarity.
32   (h) Volunteer, contractual, job orders, and other vulnerable workers in
33       government instrumentalities refers to workers in government
34       instrumentalities who do not occupy plantilla positions; receive salaries but not
35       benefits; are contracted out for specific periods of time; and/or who receive
36       allowances or honoraria. These include but are not limited to: [job order and
37       other contractual workers,] project staff, barangay health workers, barangay
38       tanod, barangay nutrition scholars, barangay daycare workers, barangay
39       volunteers and other vulnerable workers in government instrumentalities for
40       disasters, enumerators, among others.
41   (i) Social Protection Floor refers to a set of guarantees ensuring access to
42       essential health services, income support for children for education, health, and
43       nutrition, income assistance for economically active groups who do not have
44       sufficient earnings in case of emergencies, and financial support for elderly
45       population, and women informal workers in difficult and vulnerable
46       circumstances like single mothers, and women survivors of abuse and violence.

 4   Sec. 5. Workers in the informal economy shall have the same basic rights accorded
 5   to all workers, as enshrined in the Philippine Constitution, the Labor Code as amended,
 6   and relevant international human rights instruments and international labor standards.
 7   Accordingly, the state shall guarantee all workers in the informal economy the
 8   following rights:
 9
10      (a) Security of and in the workplace;
11      (b) Make a living through work freely chosen or accepted; and avail of technical
12          and vocational guidance and training programs;
13      (c) Just and favorable conditions of work, including provision for work-life balance
14          such as child-care and other facilities to reduce and redistribute family and
15          domestic responsibilities;
16      (d) A living wage and equal remuneration for work of equal value without
17          distinction of any kind, in particular for women who shall be guaranteed equal
18          wages for work similar or equivalent to those done by men;
19      (e) Equal opportunity for promotion, subject to no other considerations than
20          seniority and competence;
21      (f) Safe and healthy working conditions safeguarding general, occupational and
22          reproductive health;
23      (g) Basic services including but not limited to affordable medical care, reproductive
24          and other health services including modern family planning, low-cost housing,
25          water, sanitation, electricity and transport;
26      (h) Rest, leisure and reasonable limitation of working hours and periodic holidays
27          with pay, as well as remuneration for public holidays whenever applicable;
28      (i) Maternity and paternity benefits, as provided for by law;
29      (j) Equal access to education, skills training, and economic resources to develop
30          self-reliance, especially of children and young persons, without any
31          discrimination;
32      (k) Self-organization to collectively negotiate with government and other entities
33          in the promotion of their welfare and advancement of their interests, free from
34          any political interference;
35      (l) An adequate standard of living for workers and their families, adequate food,
36          clothing, and shelter, and the continuous improvement of such standard;
37      (m)         Freedom from any form of discrimination, violence, exploitation including
38          sexual exploitation, harassment, abuse and any form of inhumane treatment
39          which debases, degrades or demeans the intrinsic worth and dignity of the
40          worker as a human being;
41      (n) Accessible, affordable, and appropriate social protection as defined in Section
42          4 (f) of this Act;
43      (o) Equal treatment before the law;
44      (p) Substantial participation in policy and decision-making processes through their
45          organizations and the use of social dialogue, including access to information
 1          and other resources relevant to the promotion and protection of their rights
 2          and welfare;
 3      (q) Equal access to justice through appropriate mechanisms, including, but not
 4          limited to, alternative dispute resolution processes; and
 5      (r) Access to public procurement including advice and reserving quotas for informal
 6          economic units.
 7
 8   Sec. 6. Own-account workers as well as informal economic units, being the working
 9   poor’s primary instruments to address and overcome poverty, shall be accorded the
10   following preferential rights by the State:
11
12      (a) Infrastructure support such as farm to market roads, common, affordable and
13          secure workplaces and facilities, merchandising centers, farmers’ markets or
14          Bagsakan with proper storage facilities, and inventory bulk-buying centers;
15      (b) Policy support to promote and protect local and domestically-produced products
16          and services, and support in enhancing their participation in the value chain
17          process;
18      (c) Ease and facilitation of access to markets, including capacity-building to access
19          and maximize e-marketing facilities;
20      (d) Access to affordable, appropriate and adequate financial services, including,
21          among others, collateral-free and gender-responsive credit at low interest;
22      (e) Access to appropriate and adequate machinery, equipment, and other
23          technologies, with the end in view of increasing productivity, enhancing quality
24          of products and services, and growth;
25      (f) Protection from unjust dislocation from places where economic activities are
26          conducted with the “provision of relocation before demolition”;
27      (g) Measures against racketeering, extortion, and harassment, by both State and
28          non-State elements;
29      (h) Freedom from deprivation of property without valid cause and due process of
30          law; and,
31      (i) Affordable and customized social security and insurance programs.

33   Sec. 7. Rights of Legitimate Organizations of Workers in the Informal Economy
34   (WIE), Organizations of workers in informal employment, including unions,
35   cooperatives, social and solidarity economic units/organizations and mutual benefit
36   associations, shall have the right to:
37      (a) Freely function and act as the representatives of their members in policy and
38          decision-making processes, collective negotiations, and other similar bodies
39          and processes in the context of tripartism and multi-sector;
40      (b) Establish, join or affiliate with local unions, national federations or

42          be duly recognized, protected and supported by the national government;

45          their members;
 1      (d) Own property, real or personal, for the use and benefit of their organizations
 2          and members;
 3      (e) Sue and be sued under their registered names; and
 4      (f) Undertake all other activities, not contrary to law, designed to benefit their
 5          organizations and members.
 6
 7   Sec. 8. Organizing W orkers in I nform al Econom y. – Workers in informal
 8   economy shall be encouraged to organize themselves into self-help organizations,
 9   associations, federations, and confederations for their mutual benefit and protection,
10   and for other legitimate purposes. Special attention shall be given to organizing

39   Sec. 11. P olicy on Eviction and Dem olition. – Workers in the informal economy
40   or employment shall not be evicted from their homes and workplaces without legal
41   ground as provided for by R.A. 7279, otherwise known as the “Urban Development
42   and Housing Act of 1992”, and provision of livelihood opportunities, adequate water
43   and electricity availability, as well as decent conditions of living in accordance with
44   law. Logistical viability for the displaced shall be given priority in determining
45   resettlement areas, provided that, there shall be no demolition without ensuring
46   appropriate resettlement.

11   Sec. 13. P olicy on R elocation of Vending Sites. – Before any public market is
12   closed, sold or demolished, all market vendors shall first be relocated by the LGU to a
13   temporary or new public market. Relocation to a temporary market shall only be valid
14   until a new public market is constructed, and shall in no way be considered permanent.
15   Notice of the intention to close, sell, or demolish any public market shall be made to
16   all concerned vendors at least sixty (60) days before the actual transfer or relocation
17   to another market site. Within the sixty (60)-day period after issuance of notice, the
18   LGU shall conduct consultations with affected vendors on the selection of the
19   relocation site and implementation of the relocation.
20
21   Vendors, ambulant or otherwise, occupying public places not previously designated as
22   vending sites shall be provided with viable temporary sites by the LGU. Notice of
23   temporary transfer shall be given to the vendors at least fifteen (15) days before the
24   actual transfer. This shall likewise apply to vendors granted permits but whose
25   workplaces are withdrawn from the list of allowable vending sites. Any change in the
26   list of allowable vending sites shall only be done after consultations with affected
27   vendors.
28
29   In the event that a new public market is constructed in place of an old one, market
30   vendors with stalls displaced from their workplaces shall be assured of stall assignment
31   in the new market. Displaced ambulant vendors, shall, as much as practicable also be
32   given appropriate selling sites. To safeguard the livelihood of all affected vendors,
33   rental rates as a result of the redevelopment shall in no case exceed five per cent
34   (5%) above the original rate.
35
36   Sec. 14. P olicy on Relocation of Term inals. – Designated terminals for tricycles,
37   pedicabs and other similar modes of transportation shall not be arbitrarily relocated
38   without prior notice and consultation with small transport groups, legitimate
39   organizations of workers in the informal economy, affected community and other
40   relevant sectors. Relocation of terminals can only be affected through an Ordinance.
41   Provided further, that after the enactment of such ordinance, notice of intention to
42   relocate terminals shall be made to affected transport groups and community at least
43   sixty (60) days before the actual relocation.

21   Sec. 22. Applicability of the P rovisions of the Labor Code. – The provisions of
22   the Labor Code governing the employer-employee relationship, particularly on general
23   labor standards and occupational safety and health standards shall apply to all forms
24   of work arrangements in the informal economy.
25
26   The state shall adopt a developmental perspective in dealing with informal economic
27   units. The DOLE, in coordination with the IEDC, shall assess the capacity of these
28   enterprises to comply with general labor standards. Those incapable of meeting the
29   standards shall be assisted to upgrade their capacity to improve their businesses, as
30   well as the capacity to comply with relevant labor and social standards.
31
32   Sec. 23. P rioritization for Education and Training. – The DOLE shall conduct
33   adequate training and information campaigns to provide WIE and their employers the
34   full knowledge on applicable labor and occupational safety and health standards for
35   their subsequent compliance.
36
37   Sec. 24. P rohibited Acts Specific to W orkers in the I nform al Econom y. – The
38   following are deemed prohibited:
39
40      (a) Non-compliance with minimum labor standards and occupational health and
41          safety standards. The employment contract shall be in writing specifying the
42          terms and conditions of engagement, which must not be lower than the
43          minimum standards provided by law. However, the absence of a written
44          agreement does not mean the absence of an employment relationship.
45
 1         The contract shall be explained to the worker and attested to by an elected
 2         Barangay Official in the barangay where the worker is designated to work or
 3         by an IP leader/elder.
 4
 5      (b) Recruitment or Finders’ Fees. Regardless of whether the worker was sourced
 6          either through an employment agency or a third party, WIE shall neither be
 7          charged nor levied a recruitment fee or finders’ fee.
 8
 9      (c) Hazardous Work and Conditions in Formal Workplaces. Workers shall not be
10          engaged to do hazardous work, activity or undertaking, or be exposed to
11          hazardous working conditions in accordance with existing laws and the IRR of
12          this Act.
13
14      (d) Interference and Coercion. Any person is prohibited from committing any of the
15          following acts of interference or coercion:
16             i. Preventing any worker from upholding or exercising his/her rights;
17            ii. Preventing any worker from joining or assisting an organization for
18                 purposes not contrary to law;
19           iii. Preventing any worker from carrying out his/her duties or functions in
20                 an organization, or to penalize the same for any lawful action performed
21                 in that capacity;
22           iv. Harassing, threatening, coercing or intimidating any worker that results
23                 in preventing him or her from performing his or her duties and functions;
24            v. Transferring, penalizing or terminating the services of a worker without
25                 valid or legal ground; and
26           vi. Other acts that or threaten to diminish the independence and freedom
27                 of a workers’ organization to direct its own affairs.
28      (e) Non-compliance with Republic Act No. 7610, as amended by Republic Act No.
29          9231 in the employment of minors. In cases where minors are contracted or
30          hired to render work or services, the age should be at least fifteen (15) years
31          old and there is parental or legal guardian consent in the employment contract,
32          which is presented and attested to by any representative of the LGU or duly
33          elected Barangay official where the work is to be done and the local social
34          worker, and that the employers of these children should provide them with
35          access to at least elementary or secondary education, either through traditional
36          schooling or alternative learning systems.
37

 1   Sec. 26. Creation of the I nform al Econom y Developm ent Council (I EDC). -

 3   referred to as the IEDC, which shall be an attached agency of the National Economic
 4   and Development Authority (NEDA). The IEDC shall institute appropriate reforms
 5   within its structure, programming and other processes, including adjusting its human
 6   resource capabilities to ensure that WIE-focused programs are efficiently and

 8   committee shall be formed to coordinate policies and programs of various government
 9   agencies towards the transition of informal economic units to formal.
10   The IEDC shall be constituted within thirty (30) days after the approval of the IRR of
11   this Act.

15   Sec. 32. W orkers in I nform al Econom y Local Developm ent Unit (W I ELDU).
16   – The Workers in Informal Economy Local Development Unit (WIELDU) shall be
17   established in every city and municipality to perform the following functions, among
18   others, in consultation with the WIE and their organizations:
19
20      (a) prepare an overall development plan and work program that will address the
21          needs of the WIE. The barangay development plans shall be incorporated in
22          this overall development plan;
23      (b) encourage the organizing of informal economic units and WIE;
24      (c) develop and maintain a comprehensive, age, and sex-disaggregated database
25          of all WIE and economic units within the jurisdiction of the LGU that shall
26          include all relevant information as contained in the IRR of this Act. Provided
27          that, laws on privacy and confidentiality shall be fully complied with. Provided
28          further, that the database shall include information on payments collected from
29          WIE, organizations and economic units;
30      (d) establish an IE One-Stop Shop Center which shall handle all transactions and
31          processing of business permit applications within their respective jurisdiction
32          and worker’s registration; and,
33      (e) support MSME informal enterprise development towards transitioning to
34          formality.
35
36   In addition, the WIELDU may also perform other similar functions and undertakings
37   pursuant to the provisions of this Act. In the performance of WIELDU’s functions, the
38   principle of preferential treatment to the poorest and most vulnerable shall be
39   observed.
40
41   Sec. 33. Com position. – The Local Chief Executives (LCEs) shall be the primary
42   overseers of WIELDU’s operations. The IRR of this Act shall provide for WIELDU’s
43   composition provided, that, informal workers’ representatives shall form at least fifty
44   per cent (50%) of WIELDU’s membership. Provided further, that IE representatives
45   shall not be less than five (5), and provided finally, that at least forty per cent (40%)
46   of all WIELDO’s members are women. Other relevant guidelines on WIELDU’s
47   operations shall be included in the IRR of this Act.

 5   Sec. 38. P enal P rovisions. – To ensure compliance with the rights and obligations
 6   established under this Act, the following penalties shall be imposed upon any person,
 7   public official, juridical entity, or institution found to have committed violations thereof:
 8
 9      (a) Any person who shall willfully interfere with, restrain or coerce a worker in the
10          exercise of his or her rights or shall in any manner commit any act in violation
11          of any of the provisions of this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine
12          of not less than Twenty Thousand Pesos (PhP 20,000.00) or imprisonment of
13          one (1) to six (6) years or both fine and imprisonment at the discretion of the
14          Court.
15
16          In cases of juridical entities, its officers shall be held liable under this provision;
17
18      (b) Juridical entities found to be utilizing informal employment, whether entirely or
19          partially, in their operations shall have such operations suspended and/or their
20          licenses revoked upon any violation of the provisions of this Act;
21
22      (c) If the offender is a public official or an official of a GOCC, the Court, in addition
23          to the penalties provided in the preceding paragraph, may impose the
24          additional penalty or disqualification from public office;
25
26      (d) Any person who violates the provision of this Act on Policy on Confiscation of
27          Materials and Impounding of Vehicles shall be liable. Nothing herein shall
28          prohibit the aggrieved WIE from initiating a criminal or civil action against the
29          responsible person or officer; and
30
31      (e) Failure to implement the provisions of this Act on Relocation of Vending Sites
32          and Policy on Relocation of Terminals shall render the responsible official/s
33          administratively liable pursuant to Republic Act 7160 and other applicable laws
34          without prejudice to any civil or criminal case that may be filed against erring
35          officials. If a private institution or company is found to have violated any
36          provision of this Act, its business license shall be suspended or revoked at the
37          discretion of the Court.
