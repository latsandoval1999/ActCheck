                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Poverty continues to hold many Filipino families back from living stable and secure
lives. While government programs like Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps)
have helped ease daily struggles, many households still lack the means to build lasting
sources of income. To truly move families out of poverty, they need more than short-
term aid, they need real opportunities to earn, grow, and stand on their own.

The Sustainable Livelihood Program (SLP) of the Department of Social Welfare and
Development has been helping poor and vulnerable families start small businesses or
 ind jobs. It works closely with local governments while remaining guided by national
priorities. The Supreme Court has af irmed that the national government can carry
out programs like SLP, as long as there is proper coordination with local authorities
and communities.

At the same time, experience shows that programs involving public funds must be
handled with great care. Clear rules, transparency, and accountability are essential to
make sure assistance truly reaches those in need. Strengthening these safeguards will
protect public resources and build trust in the program.

This bill aims to make SLP a permanent and stronger program that helps families
achieve long-term stability. It will improve how bene iciaries are selected, how

support is delivered, and how results are measured. By working closely with
government agencies, local communities, and partners, this measure seeks to give
more Filipinos a fair chance at a better, more secure future.

respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “The Sustainable Livelihood
Program Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote livelihood
development, entrepreneurship, and inclusive growth as a means to reduce poverty
and vulnerability, consistent with sustainable development.

Towards this end, the State shall strengthen the capacity of poor and vulnerable
households, individuals, groups, and communities, particularly Pantawid Pamilyang
Pilipino Program (4Ps) households and those identi ied as poor under the National
Household      Targeting      System      for   Poverty       Reduction     (NHTS-
PR/Listahanan) and Republic Act No. 11291 (Magna Carta of the Poor) — to
access decent work, develop microenterprises, and build resilient livelihood assets
through a coordinated, transparent, and accountable government program.

SECTION 3. Coverage. — The Sustainable Livelihood Program (SLP) shall cover:

SECTION 5. The Sustainable Livelihood Program. — The SLP is hereby
institutionalized as a DSWD livelihood capability-building program for poor,
vulnerable, and marginalized households and communities. It shall provide
interventions under two tracks: microenterprise development and employment
facilitation, to enable program participants to access sustainable sources of income
and strengthen resilience against economic shocks.

The SLP shall prioritize capacity building, coaching and mentoring, and strengthening
of participant self-governance and linkages to credit, insurance, training, and markets,
consistent with safeguards and accountability mechanisms under this Act and its IRR.

SECTION 6. Tracks and Modalities of Assistance. — The SLP shall have two (2)
major tracks:

   (a) Microenterprise Development (MD) Track, which supports the
       establishment or strengthening of microenterprises; and
   (b) Employment Facilitation (EF) Track, which supports access to and retention
       of gainful employment.

The SLP may provide, subject to IRR and fund availability, the following modalities:

   (a) Under the MD Track: Seed Capital Fund (SCF), skills training and
       facilitation, and Cash-for-Building Livelihood Assets Fund (CBLAF);
   (b) Under the EF Track: Employment Assistance Fund (EAF) and skills training
       and facilitation.

The DSWD shall issue standards on the form (cash or in-kind), ceilings, safeguards,
and conditions of each modality, consistent with applicable auditing, procurement,
and inancial management rules.

SECTION 7. Eligibility Requirements. —

The following shall be eligible to participate, subject to screening, validation, and
prioritization rules:



                                                                            6|Page
   (a) Members of poor households identi ied by the NHTS-PR/Listahanan or its
       successor targeting system;
   (b) Current 4Ps bene iciaries;
   (c) Poor households or individuals identi ied under Republic Act No. 11291 and
       validated by appropriate mechanisms; and
   (d) Poor and vulnerable groups, including but not limited to Indigenous Peoples,
       persons with disabilities, and solo parents, subject to DSWD guidelines.

Minimum age requirements shall be as follows:

   (a) For the MD Track: at least sixteen (16) years old, with written consent of a
       parent or legal guardian when required by law;
   (b) For the EF Track: at least eighteen (18) years old at the time of employment,
       consistent with labor laws and standards.

Participation shall be subject to willingness to comply with program conditions and
accountability requirements under this Act and the IRR.

SECTION 8. Livelihood Sustainability Matrix. —

The DSWD shall adopt and maintain a Livelihood Sustainability Matrix as a results
framework to guide the graduated provision of assistance, capability building,
technical support, and organizational strengthening.

The Matrix shall include performance indicators covering, at a minimum:
   (a) Technical operations;
   (b) Financial strength;
   (c) Organizational development and management; and
   (d) Community impact.

Assessment of participants shall be conducted periodically, beginning not later than
the second (2nd) year of the participant’s engagement, to determine readiness for
additional interventions, graduation, or referral to other programs.

SECTION 9. Program Cycle and Implementation Stages. — The SLP shall operate
on a program cycle of up to ive (5) years per cohort or participant group, subject to
IRR, which may include the following stages:

   (a) Year 1 (Punla) – social preparation, resource mobilization, and initial project
       implementation;

                                                                          7|Page
   (b) Year 2 (Usbong) – monitoring, coaching and mentoring, and capability
       building, with possible product development support;
   (c) Year 3 (Sibol) – continuing monitoring and technical assistance, with possible
       support for formalization and accreditation where appropriate;
   (d) Year 4 (Yabong) – strengthening of market linkages, partnerships, and
       integration into value chains;
   (e) Year 5 (Ani) – graduation and transition planning, including possible
       graduation support subject to performance and evaluation.

The DSWD may re ine stage designations, sequencing, and speci ic grant
nomenclature in the IRR to ensure lexibility, responsiveness, and alignment with
evidence-based practice.

SECTION 10. Institutional Arrangement. — The DSWD shall be the lead agency for
coordinating, planning, implementing, and monitoring the SLP.

   (a) The SLP-NPMO shall provide overall policy direction, standards, and
       oversight;
   (b) The SLP-RPMO shall be responsible for regional implementation, supervision,
       and coordination with provincial and local partners.

The DSWD may designate implementing personnel, including community facilitators
and monitoring of icers, to ensure proper participant organization, capacity building,
validation of fund utilization, and regular monitoring, consistent with civil service
rules and operational guidelines.

SECTION 11. Functions of the SLP National Program Management Of ice (SLP-
NPMO). — The SLP-NPMO shall:

   (a)   Develop and update program policies, tools, and guidelines;
   (b)   Set performance standards and ensure compliance monitoring;
   (c)   Lead national program planning and coordination;
   (d)   Implement capacity building for national and regional program personnel;
   (e)   Manage the national program budget and issue standards for fund
         utilization;
   (f)   Conduct national monitoring and evaluation of outputs and outcomes; and
   (g)   Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out this Act.

SECTION 12. Functions of the SLP Regional Program Management Of ice (SLP-
RPMO). —

SECTION 13. Monitoring, Reporting, and Transparency. — The DSWD shall
regularly monitor SLP implementation to ensure attainment of program objectives.

The DSWD shall publish an annual SLP report covering the preceding iscal year,
which shall include, at a minimum:

   (a) Number of households and individuals served, disaggregated by region and
       track;
   (b) Physical and inancial accomplishments;
   (c) Outcome indicators and evaluation indings as available;
   (d) Key partnerships and capacity-building activities; and
   (e) Recommendations for program improvement.

The annual report shall be posted on the of icial DSWD website, subject to privacy and
con identiality requirements.

SECTION 14. Congressional Oversight Committee. —

A Congressional Oversight Committee on the Sustainable Livelihood Program,
hereafter referred to as the SLP Oversight Committee, is hereby created upon
effectivity of this Act.

It shall be composed of members designated by the Senate and the House of
Representatives from the committees having primary jurisdiction over poverty
alleviation, social services, and labor and employment, with the chairperson and co-
chairperson to be designated in accordance with the internal rules of Congress.

The Committee shall conduct periodic reviews on the implementation of this Act,
including the utilization of appropriations, program performance, and compliance

SECTION 15. Data Privacy and Con identiality. — The DSWD shall ensure that all
personal information collected, processed, and shared in the implementation of the
SLP shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) and its
implementing rules and regulations.

SECTION 16. Data Sharing. — Data sharing among government agencies for the
performance of a public function or provision of a public service shall be covered by
a data sharing agreement and shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173 and
applicable issuances.

Access to program information by stakeholders shall be subject to lawful disclosure
standards, security measures, and safeguards to protect personal data and sensitive
information.

SECTION 17. Convergence of Livelihood Programs and Services. — To strengthen
social protection and poverty alleviation outcomes, national government agencies,
local government units, the private sector, civil society, volunteer groups, and the
academe are encouraged to converge and complement the interventions under the
SLP.

Convergence may include co- inancing, resource mobilization, harmonization of
training and support services, shared market linkages, and coordinated referrals,
subject to applicable laws and agreements.

SECTION 18. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DSWD for the SLP. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within twelve (12)
months from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD Secretary shall, in coordination
with relevant government agencies and local government units, promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations to carry out the provisions of this Act.
