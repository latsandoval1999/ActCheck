                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and
   promote the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake
   appropriate health human resource development responsive to the country’s
   needs and problems (Article XIII, Section 12).

          Physical therapists play a vital role in the healthcare system by promoting
   mobility, functional independence, and overall quality of life among individuals
   affected by injury, illness, or disability. They provide rehabilitative care through
   therapeutic exercise, manual therapy, and other evidence-based interventions
   aimed at restoring physical function, preventing disability, and improving long-
   term health outcomes. Their services are essential in hospitals, rehabilitation
   centers, and community-based settings, particularly for patients recovering
   from surgery, trauma, stroke, and other debilitating conditions.

         As the demand for rehabilitation services continues to grow, driven by an
   aging population, the increasing prevalence of non-communicable diseases,
   and the long-term effects of injuries and illnesses, the role of physical therapists
   has become increasingly indispensable in ensuring continuity of care and
   reducing the burden on the healthcare system.

          Despite their critical contributions, physical therapists in government
   service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of compensation relative
   to the level of expertise, responsibility, and clinical demands of their profession.
   The current salary grade does not adequately reflect the complexity of their
   work nor provide sufficient incentive to retain skilled practitioners in the public
   sector.

           Moreover, the sustained global demand for physical therapists has
   contributed to the continued outmigration of Filipino health care professionals
   seeking better compensation and career opportunities abroad. This trend
   exacerbates workforce shortages and limits the capacity of public health
   facilities to provide adequate rehabilitation services.
      This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
government physical therapists from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15. The
proposed adjustment aims to ensure equitable compensation, improve
workforce retention, and align their remuneration with similarly situated
healthcare professionals.

       Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold
decent work standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the
delivery of accessible, comprehensive, and quality healthcare services for all
Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                               “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT PHYSICAL THERAPISTS FROM SALARY
            GRADE 11 TO SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Government
Physical Therapists' Salary Upgrading Act of 2026. "

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government physical therapists
in order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public health sector,
consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality healthcare
services to all Filipinos.

      SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Physical Therapists' Minimum
Salary Grade Level. - The minimum salary grade of government physical therapists
occupying entry-level positions (Physical Therapist I) is hereby upgraded from Salary
Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of physical therapists occupying higher positions shall be
correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all physical therapists employed
in public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and operated
by the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
