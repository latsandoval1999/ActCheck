                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A NATIONAL PALLIATIVE AND HOSPICE
CARE PROGRAM, ENSURING ACCESS TO INTEGRATED, EQUITABLE,
AND RIGHTS-BASED    PALLIATIVE    CARE SERVICES,   AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This proposed bill seeks to institutionalize a national Palliative and Hospice
Care Program, establish the rights of palliative care patients and other stakeholders,
outline the different palliative care services and essential medicines, promote
palliative care in educational institutions and workplaces, provide capacity-building
to providers of palliative care, conduct research towards the improvement of the
Program, and evaluate the implementation of such. The proposed bill likewise
ensures that efforts of different national government agencies involved and local
government units are harmonized.

       Palliative and hospice care plays a vital role in improving the quality of life
of patients with life-limiting illnesses by relieving pain, alleviating symptoms, and
addressing emotional, social, and spiritual needs. However, despite its recognition
as a human right and an essential component of universal health, access to palliative
and hospice care in the Philippines remains limited.


                                                                                         1
       According to a study in 2020, only about 10% of hospitals nationwide have
 dedicated palliative and hospice units. This leaves the majority of Filipinos without
 access to such services. Many institutions lack the facilities and essential medicines
 needed to provide adequate care.

        The lack of trained professionals further worsens this gap as palliative and
 hospice care are barely integrated into medical education. Thus, only a handful of
 specialists serve the country’s large and growing population. Many healthcare
 professionals also hesitate to refer patients to palliative care, perceiving it as “giving
 up.”

         At the same time, cultural values that emphasize family caregiving, while
 rooted in compassion, often prevent patients and families from seeking formal
 palliative care services. Moreover, there are misconceptions among families that
 equate palliative care services with euthanasia or exclusively end-of-life care.

         Therefore, this bill responds to these urgent challenges by institutionalizing a
 National Palliative and Hospice Care Program to ensure equitable, rights-based, and
 culturally sensitive access to services. It establishes the Philippine Council for
 Palliative Care to coordinate national efforts, strengthens training and education for
 healthcare providers, allocates dedicated resources to expand facilities and access to
 essential medicines, and embeds palliative care within the continuum of healthcare
 delivery. It also promotes public awareness campaigns to dispel misconceptions,
 encourage acceptance, and empower families to make informed choices.

        By passing this proposed measure, the State does not just fulfill its
 commitment to national and international laws to promote the health, wellbeing, and
 the overall quality of life of its citizens. It likewise asserts and affirms that every
 Filipino has the right to live and die with dignity, free from unnecessary pain and
 suffering.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  CHAPTER I
                               General Provisions


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Palliative Care Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State affirms the right of every Filipino
to receive palliative and hospice care services that uphold dignity, autonomy, and


                                                                                  1
compassion. In line with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the
International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), the International
Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), and other
international conventions ratified by the Republic of the Philippines, the State
commits to ensuring equitable access to quality palliative care for all, particularly
those with chronic life-limiting illness (CLLI), recognizing such access as a
fundamental human right.

Towards this end, it is the policy of the State to promote the well-being and quality
of life of people by ensuring that their right to palliative care is valued, promoted,
and protected.

To achieve this, the State shall pursue the following principles:

   a. An integrated and comprehensive approach that ensures continuity of care and
      addresses the diverse needs of individuals across all age groups and health
      conditions who require palliative and hospice services throughout the full
      continuum of care;
   b. A whole-of-system, whole-of-society, and whole-of-government approach in
      the development and implementation of essential palliative and hospice care
      programs made available to the public
   c. A model of care that respects and responds to the unique needs, values, and
      preferences of individuals and their families, ensuring that services are timely,
      affordable, culturally appropriate, and aligned with the person’s dignity and
      goals of care;
   d. A legal and service framework that upholds the dignity and choices of
      individuals receiving palliative care by ensuring their freedom from coercion



                                                                                      2
      and discrimination, protecting their autonomy, and supporting their active role
      in family, community, and civic life.

SECTION 3. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are as follows:

   a. Promote and protect the rights and freedoms of persons with palliative and
      hospice care needs, consistent with international human rights obligations;
   b. Establish a comprehensive, integrated, and responsive national palliative and
      hospice care system that addresses the needs of all population groups;
   c. Strengthen leadership, governance, and institutional coordination in the
      planning, delivery, and evaluation of palliative care programs and services;
   d. Build the capacity of the health workforce and community partners through
      sustained training, education, and support;
   e. Advance research, data systems, and evidence-informed practices in palliative
      and hospice care, including indigenous and community-based models;
   f. Embed palliative care within the basic health services, public health strategies,
      and educational, community, and workplace settings across all levels of
      government.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following
terms are hereby defined:

   a. Allied health professional refers to trained non-palliative specialist health
      professionals such as physicians, social workers, nurses, occupational
      therapists, recreational therapists, dietitians, among others;
   b. Carer refers to a person who provides regular support to a palliative patient,
      whether voluntarily or as part of a paid or professional role. A carer may be a
      family member, friend, or an individual formally designated by the patient or


                                                                                      3
   their family, regardless of whether a prior close personal relationship exists
   between them and the carer.
c. Chronic Life-Limiting Illness refers to any health condition that is inevitably
   fatal or which significantly shortens a person’s life expectancy, and/or causes
   serious health-related suffering. This includes advanced or metastatic cancers,
   endstage organ failures (such as end-stage renal, cardiac or liver disease),
   chronic progressive illnesses (such as late-stage chronic obstructive
   pulmonary disease        or   neurological disorders), AIDS         with    severe
   complications, degenerative conditions, congenital disorders, and other
   similar diseases or injuries where curative treatment is no longer effective or
   feasible.
d. Confidentiality refers to the obligation of all healthcare providers, institutions,
   and personnel involved in the delivery of palliative care services to protect the
   personal and medical information of patients from unauthorized access, use,
   or disclosure. This duty shall be exercised in accordance with the Data Privacy
   Act of 2012 (RA 10173), its Implementing Rules and Regulations, and other
   relevant issuances by the National Privacy Commission.
e. Continuum of Care is the coordinated and comprehensive delivery of health
   services that accompany a person’s health journey—health promotion, disease
   prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, supportive care, palliation,
   survivorship, end of life care, and bereavement support—ensuring continuity,
   responsiveness, and dignity at every stage.
f. Hospice Care refers to a component of palliative care which deals with
   persons who are not expected to survive more than six months. It focuses on
   ensuring peace, comfort, dignity, and holistic support—including medical,
   psychological, and spiritual care—delivered in accordance with the patient’s


                                                                                     4
   values, rights, and preferences.
g. Impairment or Temporary Loss of Decision-Making Capacity - A condition,
   as determined and documented by a licensed physician or mental health
   professional, in which an individual is unable to understand, deliberate, or
   communicate informed decisions about their care. For legal purposes, such
   assessment must be recorded in the patient’s medical record and, when
   applicable, must conform to relevant laws and institutional protocols
   governing substitute decision-making.
h. Informed Consent - A process in which a patient, who has decision-making
   capacity, voluntarily submits to a proposed treatment or intervention after
   receiving clear, comprehensible information on the nature and purpose of the
   intervention, its expected benefits and potential risks, and available
   alternatives (including the option of no treatment). Documentation of the
   patient’s voluntary decision shall be maintained in their medical record.
i. Life-sustaining Treatment refers to any medical intervention that serves to
   prolong life without curing the underlying condition, including but not limited
   to mechanical ventilation, dialysis, resuscitation, and artificial nutrition or
   hydration. Such treatment may be withheld or withdrawn in accordance with
   the patient’s wishes, advance directives, or applicable law and ethical
   standards.
j. Palliative Care is an approach that improves the quality of life of patients
   (adults and children) and their families facing problems associated with a life-
   threatening illness. It prevents and relieves serious health-related suffering
   through the early identification and impeccable assessment and treatment of
   pain and other issues—physical, psychosocial, or spiritual.
k. Palliative Care Health Facility is any establishment primarily engaged in


                                                                                  5
   delivering palliative care services, whether standalone or as part of a larger
   health institution, that is duly licensed or accredited by the Department of
   Health (DOH) or other regulatory bodies authorized by law.
l. Palliative Care Specialist is a physician who has completed formal fellowship
   training in Palliative and Hospice Medicine conducted or accredited by the
   Philippine Society of Hospice and Palliative Medicine (PSHPM) and has
   received certification in accordance with applicable rules and guidelines set
   by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) or its duly authorized
   agency.
m. Palliative Care Service Provider refers to a person, organization, or legally
   registered entity that is authorized to deliver palliative care services, including
   but not limited to licensed professionals, certified palliative care specialists,
   palliative care facilities, and accredited community-based service groups.
n. Palliative Care Services refers to a comprehensive range of evidence-based
   and rights-centered health interventions—including physical, psychosocial,
   and spiritual support—delivered across the continuum of care for individuals
   with chronic life-limiting illness.
o. Palliative Health Aide refers to a volunteer, advocate, or community-based
   worker duly certified to provide basic palliative and hospice care in the
   home/community-based or hospital/facility-based setting.
p. Palliative Patient refers to any person of any age diagnosed by a qualified
   health professional with a chronic life-limiting illness and who requires, is
   receiving, or has been referred for palliative or hospice care services.
q. Patient Navigation refers to the provision of individualized assistance to
   patients and families to help them overcome healthcare system barriers and
   facilitate timely access to quality medical and psychosocial care from pre-


                                                                                    6
   diagnosis through all phases of the illness experience
r. Pediatric Palliative Care refers to palliative care that is specifically adapted
   and provided to infants, children, and adolescents with life-limiting or life-
   threatening conditions. It addresses the unique medical, developmental,
   psychosocial, and spiritual needs of both the child and their family through a
   comprehensive, interdisciplinary approach. Care begins at diagnosis and
   continues throughout the illness trajectory, regardless of whether disease-
   directed treatment is ongoing. It is provided across all levels and settings of
   care and includes support for family caregivers and bereavement services.
s. Psychosocial support refers to the range of interventions, services, and
   assistance—beyond direct medical care—aimed at addressing the emotional,
   social, economic, cultural, and spiritual well-being of palliative patients and
   their families. It includes, but is not limited to, financial aid, counseling, grief
   and bereavement support, peer and family support, access to transient
   housing, transportation assistance, food and nutritional services, livelihood
   support, and spiritual care. Psychosocial support is designed to reduce
   distress, promote resilience, and maintain the dignity and quality of life for
   individuals with CLLI.
t. Serious Health-Related Suffering refers to suffering or distress associated
   with illness or injury of any kind and cannot be relieved without professional
   intervention and that compromises physical, social, spiritual, and/or emotional
   functioning.
u. Supportive Care refers to a set of services provided alongside curative or life-
   prolonging treatment to help patients manage the physical, emotional, and
   practical side effects of their illness or therapy. It includes symptom control,
   psychosocial support, nutritional care, rehabilitation, and interventions that


                                                                                     7
      promote comfort, well-being, and daily functioning throughout the illness
      trajectory
   v. Surrogate Decision Maker or Healthcare Proxy refers to a person legally
      designated to make care-related decisions on behalf of a palliative patient,
      either through a written advance directive made by the patient, a legal
      appointment by a court, or assignment under applicable law when the patient
      is no longer able to decide for themselves
   w. Transition care refers to the set of time-bound services and coordinated
      support provided to patients and their families when transferring between care
      settings (e.g., hospital to home), shifting approach to care (e.g. pediatric to
      adult), or shifting care goals (e.g., curative to palliative). These services aim
      to ensure safe, continuous, and well-informed care. They are delivered by the
      referring and receiving healthcare teams, in coordination with the patient,
      family, community providers, and Patient Navigators who help guide and
      support the transition process.




                                   CHAPTER II
              Rights of Palliative Patients and Other Stakeholders



SECTION 5. Rights of Palliative Patients – All persons receiving palliative or
hospice care services shall enjoy, on an equal and non-discriminatory basis, the
rights guaranteed under the 1987 Philippine Constitution, the Universal Declaration
of Human Rights, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR),
the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), the



                                                                                     8
Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223), the National Integrated Cancer Control Act
(RA 11215), relevant Department of Health issuances, and other international
conventions ratified by the Republic of the Philippines. These rights shall include,
but shall not be limited to, the following:

   a. Right to Non-Discrimination. The right to be free from all forms of
      discrimination, stigma, or marginalization based on age, sex, gender, health
      status, socioeconomic status, ethnicity, religion, disability, or any other
      personal condition;
   b. Right to Full Enjoyment of Civil, Political, Economic, Social, and
      Cultural Rights. The right to exercise inherent civil, political, economic,
      social, and cultural rights, including religious and educational rights, without
      discrimination, and with full respect for individual dignity, preferences, and
      values;
   c. Right to Palliative and Hospice Care Services. The right to receive
      palliative and hospice care services that respect dignity, autonomy, culture,
      and values, and to be provided with humane and compassionate care,
      especially at the end of life;
   d. Right to Quality and Equitable Care. The right to access quality, safe,
      affordable, and evidence-based palliative and hospice care, consistent with the
      highest attainable standard of health, across all levels of the health care
      system;
   e. Right to Coordinated and Comprehensive Services. The right to
      coordinated, comprehensive, and patient-centered services that include
      prevention, promotion, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, palliation, and
      end-of-life care, under a multidisciplinary plan of care;



                                                                                    9
f. Right to Appropriate Care Setting. The right to receive care in the least
   restrictive, most appropriate, and dignified setting, whether in a health facility,
   hospice, or the community;
g. Right to Information. The right to receive adequate, clear, and timely
   information regarding available palliative and hospice services, including
   options, risks, benefits, and the nature of care to be provided;
h. Right to Informed Consent. The right to informed consent, including the
   right to freely accept or refuse any proposed diagnostic or medical treatment
   procedure, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. Such consent or
   refusal shall be appropriately documented in the patient’s medical record.
i. Right to Participate in Care. The right to participate in the formulation,
   development, and periodic review of their individualized plan of care, and to
   designate a person of legal age to act as their representative for decision-
   making purposes when necessary;
j. Right to Appoint a Proxy. The right to appoint a surrogate decision-maker
   or legal representative by applicable law, in the event of impairment or loss
   of decision-making capacity;
k. Right to Privacy and Confidentiality. The right to confidentiality and
   privacy of all personal, medical, and health-related information, by the Data
   Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws. Information shall not be
   disclosed without prior consent, except in cases authorized by law.
l. Right to Psychosocial and Spiritual Support. The right to psychosocial,
   emotional, and spiritual support for themselves and their families throughout
   care and in the bereavement period;
m. Right to Participate in Policy-Making. The right to participate in palliative
   care policy-making, planning, service delivery, monitoring, and evaluation,


                                                                                   10
      subject to their voluntary capacity and interest;
   n. Right to Legal Assistance. The right to receive prompt legal support and
      representation. In cases where the patient cannot afford legal services, free
      legal assistance shall be provided by the Public Attorney’s Office or
      accredited legal aid institutions.
   o. Right to Know Rights and Responsibilities. The right to be informed of their
      rights and responsibilities under this Act within twenty-four (24) hours of
      admission to a health facility, in a manner and language that is comprehensible
      to the patient and family.

SECTION 6. Rights of Family Members, Carers and Legal Representatives. –
Family members, carers, and duly designated or appointed legal representatives of
palliative and hospice care patients shall enjoy the following rights, in accordance
with the provisions of this Act, the Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223), and other
applicable laws and issuances. These rights shall be exercised with full respect for
the patient’s autonomy and informed consent:

   a. Right to Participate in Care Planning. With the patient’s consent, the right
      to participate in the development and implementation of the patient’s
      individualized care plan.
   b. Right to Psychosocial and Spiritual Support. The right to receive
      psychosocial and spiritual support from health care providers, the DSWD, and
      other relevant agencies during caregiving and bereavement.
   c. Right to Caregiver Education and Respite Support. The right to receive
      caregiver training, guidance in home care, and access to respite services,
      where available.
   d. Right to Compassionate Workplace Accommodation. The right to use


                                                                                  11
      available leave benefits for caregiving, and to receive reasonable flexibility
      from employers. The State shall explore a dedicated Compassionate Care
      Leave.
   e. Right to Request for Discharge or Transfer. Family members, carers, or
      legal representatives may request the discharge or transfer of a patient to home
      or a more suitable health facility. This right may be exercised provided that:
        i.   They are fully informed by the care team of the risks, benefits, and
             implications of such decision;
       ii.   They execute a written waiver releasing the health care provider and
             facility from liability for foreseeable consequences;
      iii.   The decision does not endanger public health and safety, as determined
             by the attending team and applicable public health guidelines.
   f. Right to Participate in Palliative Policy and System Development. The
      right to engage, on a voluntary basis, in palliative care policy-making,
      program planning, and system evaluation.
   g. Right to Bereavement Support. The right to receive basic bereavement
      services, including counseling referrals and support group access, and to be
      informed of where to seek help.



                                  CHAPTER III
                 National Palliative and Hospice Care Program



SECTION 7. National Palliative and Hospice Care Program. – There is hereby
established a National Palliative and Hospice Care Program which shall serve as the
framework for all palliative care-related activities of the government. The program


                                                                                   12
shall have the following objectives:

   a. Decrease the overall burden of serious health-related suffering of all adult and
         children with CLLI;
   b. Uphold the rights, preferences, dignity, and comfort of palliative patients and
         their families;
   c. Develop national standards for palliative and hospice care services;
   d. Improve the capacity to provide quality palliative services throughout the
         country;
   e. Provide timely access to optimal and cost-effective palliative care services
         and medication;
   f. Make palliative, hospice, and grief and bereavement care more affordable and
         accessible to all Filipinos;
   g. Improve the experience of palliative care of patients and families and support
         their inclusion and/or reintegration to society.

SECTION 8. The Philippine Council for Palliative Care – There is hereby
established the Philippine Council for Palliative Care, hereinafter referred to as the
Council, as the principal policy-making, planning, coordinating, and advisory body
attached to the Department of Health (DOH). The Council, as a part of the DOH,
shall:

   a. Oversee the implementation of this Act and the National Palliative and
         Hospice Care Program;
   b. Uphold the rights and freedoms of persons with palliative, hospice, and
         psychosocial care needs; and
   c. Promote a rational, unified, and integrated service delivery network for



                                                                                   13
      palliative and hospice care responsive to Filipino health and cultural contexts.

SECTION 9. Composition. – The Council shall be composed of the following:

   a. Secretary of Health as the Chairperson in an ex officio capacity;
   b. A vice chairperson who shall be elected by the non-ex officio members, from
      among themselves and who shall serve for a term of three (3) years;
   c. Ex officio members consist of the following:
        i.   Secretary of DepED;
       ii.   Secretary of DOLE;
      iii.   Secretary of DSWD;
      iv.    Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government
             (DILG);
       v.    President and Chief Executive Officer of PhilHealth;
      vi.    Chairperson of CHR;
      vii.   Chairperson of CHED;
   d. One (1) representative from the academe/research involved in palliative and
      hospice care;
   e. Two (2) palliative care specialists; and
   f. Three (3) representatives from palliative care-focused nongovernment
      organizations (NGOs), patient organizations, and/or advocacy networks

The ex officio members of the Council may designate their permanent authorized
representatives. The Secretary of Health’s designated representative must have a
rank not lower than Undersecretary.

The non-ex officio members of the council must be citizens and residents of the
Philippines, of good moral character, of recognized probity and independence, and


                                                                                   14
have distinguished themselves professionally in public, private, civic, or academic
service in the field of palliative care. They shall be chosen from at least five (5)
persons recommended by the Secretary of Health, to be appointed by the President
for a term of three (3) years. In case a vacancy occurs in the Council, any person
chosen to fill the position vacated by a member of the Council shall only serve the
unexpired term of said member.

SECTION 10. Powers and Functions. – The Council shall exercise the following
powers and functions:

   a. Formulate, in coordination with the DOH and relevant stakeholders, a
      National Strategic Plan for Palliative Care that shall be periodically updated
      and shall include:
         i.   National goals and measurable targets for protecting the rights and
              dignity of patients with palliative and hospice care needs;
        ii.   A roadmap for developing and sustaining an integrated, rights-based
              palliative care service delivery network, including the development of
              human resources, infrastructure, and health information systems;
       iii.   A multi-year investment and resource mobilization plan with identified
              funding sources to support the implementation of this Act;
   b. Monitor and evaluate the implementation of this Act, the Strategic Plan, and
      the corresponding Rules and Regulations, including mid-term reviews and
      end-line impact assessments;
   c. Ensure effective inter-agency coordination and policy harmonization, and
      issue resolutions or recommendations to concerned government offices to
      guide program implementation, providing clear and comprehensive guidance
      to all involved.


                                                                                  15
   d. Strengthen collaboration among national agencies, local government units
      (LGUs), civil society, the private sector, and development partners in the
      promotion of equitable and high-quality palliative care, ensuring that all
      stakeholders are included and part of a larger, collective effort.
   e. Engage in technical cooperation with international agencies and institutions
      in the areas of policy, training, research, and innovation for palliative and
      hospice care;
   f. Facilitate joint planning and budget alignment across agencies to guarantee
      that appropriate allocations for palliative care are reflected in national and
      local budget proposals;
   g. Require the submission of relevant data and reports from government and
      private stakeholders to inform evidence-based policies and programs;
   h. Institutionalize and oversee a National Palliative Care Registry and
      monitoring system, in coordination with the DOH, for purposes of planning,
      policy-making, and quality assurance;
   i. Exercise such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the objectives
      of this Act.

SECTION 11. Creation of the DOH Palliative Care Division. – A Palliative Care
Division shall be established within the Disease Prevention and Control Bureau of
the Department of Health. This division, staffed by qualified palliative care
specialists, program managers, and support personnel with permanent appointments,
will be responsible for the technical and operational aspects of the National Palliative
and Hospice Care Program.

The Division shall:




                                                                                     16
   a. Serve as the lead technical and operational arm for the implementation of the
      National Palliative and Hospice Care Program and provide secretariat support
      to the Council;
   b. Coordinate with regional health offices, local governments, and stakeholders
      for effective service delivery. This coordination will involve regular
      communication, joint planning, and monitoring of service delivery to ensure
      that palliative and hospice care services are effectively reaching those in need;
   c. Ensure policy consistency and alignment with the Universal Health Care
      (UHC) Act, the DOH Manual of Operations for Palliative and Hospice Care,
      and international health commitments, including the UDHR, ICCPR,
      ICESCR, and WHO guidelines.


                                     CHAPTER IV
                              Palliative Care Services



SECTION 12. Quality of Palliative Care Services. – Palliative Care services
provided pursuant to this Act shall be:

   a. Evidence-based and aligned with internationally accepted clinical standards,
      including those of the World Health Organization and the Department of
      Health;
   b. Responsive to the clinical, gender, cultural, ethnic, and other specific needs of
      patients and their families;
   c. Delivered in the most appropriate and least restrictive setting possible;
   d. Age-appropriate and consistent with the principles of dignity and comfort
      across the lifespan;


                                                                                    17
   e. Provided by duly trained and accredited palliative care professionals,
      specialists, health aides, and workers, in a manner that ensures transparency,
      accountability, and adherence to the Department of Health’s Manual of
      Operations, Procedures, and Standards for the National Palliative and Hospice
      Care Program.

SECTION 13. Minimum Scope of Palliative and Hospice Care Services. –
Palliative and hospice care services provided pursuant to this Act shall, at a
minimum, include the following core components:

   a. Pain and Symptom Management - Relief from pain and other distressing
      symptoms through evidence-based pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic
      interventions. Patients shall have timely access to essential palliative
      medicines, including opioids, as listed in the Philippine National Formulary.
      The DOH, FDA, and PDEA shall ensure safe, legal, and appropriate
      availability and use at all levels of care.
   b. Psychosocial Support - Services to address emotional, social, and
      psychological needs of patients and families, including counseling, peer
      support, and social work assistance. Support for children, such as child life
      services, shall be provided when applicable.
   c. Spiritual Care - Spiritual support shall be offered in line with the patient’s
      beliefs, with access to faith leaders or spiritual advisers. Facilities shall
      accommodate spiritual practices when requested.
   d. Multidisciplinary Care - An interdisciplinary team shall deliver coordinated
      care that addresses medical, nursing, psychosocial, and spiritual needs.
      Palliative health aides and trained volunteers may support care delivery,
      especially at home.


                                                                                 18
e. Patient and Family Education - Patients and families shall be informed
   about the illness, care expectations, and basic caregiving skills. Education
   shall include medication use, comfort care, and recognizing signs of
   deterioration or death.
f. End-of-Life Care - Care during the terminal phase shall focus on comfort and
   dignity, avoid non-beneficial interventions, and respect patient wishes.
   Providers shall support families before, during, and after death, including
   postmortem guidance and bereavement initiation.
g. Respite Care - Short-term care shall be available, when feasible, to relieve
   family caregivers. Public hospitals, hospices, and community-based palliative
   care programs are encouraged to offer this when resources allow.
h. Advance Care Planning - Patients shall be supported in documenting their
   care preferences, including advance directives and appointment of proxies.
   Discussions shall be part of routine care.
i. Transition Care - Support shall be provided during transitions in care
   settings, phases, or populations to ensure continuity and safety for patients
   and families.
j. Home-Based Care - When appropriate, care may be provided at home
   through coordinated home visits, medication access, and caregiver support.
k. Grief and Bereavement Support - Families shall have access to basic
   bereavement support, including counseling referrals and information on grief
   resources.
l. Patient Navigation and Case Management - Navigators or case managers
   shall assist patients and families with service coordination, referrals, resource
   access, and continuity of care.




                                                                                 19
SECTION 14. Withholding or Withdrawing Life-Sustaining Treatment. – Any
person of legal age and sound mind may execute a directive directing the
withholding or withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment in a terminal condition or
permanent unconscious condition, given:

   a. Prior to withholding or withdrawing life-sustaining treatment, the diagnosis
      of a terminal condition by the attending physician or the diagnosis of a
      permanent unconscious state by two (2) physicians shall be entered in writing
      and made a permanent part of the patient’s medical records.
   b. The directive shall be signed by the declarer in the presence of two witnesses
      who:
        i.   Are not related to the declarer by blood or marriage;
       ii.   Would not be entitled to any portion of the estate of the declarer upon
             declarer’s death under any will of the declarer or codicil thereto then
             existing or, at the time of the directive, by operation of law then
             existing;
      iii.   Shall not be the attending physician;
      iv.    Shall not be an employee of the attending physician or a health facility
             in which the declarer is a patient; or
       v.    Have no claim against any portion of the estate of the declarer upon
             declarer’s death at the time of the execution of the directive
   c. The directive shall be duly notarized.
   d. The directive, or a copy thereof, shall be made part of the patient’s medical
      records retained by the attending physician, a copy of which shall be
      forwarded by the custodian of the records to the health facility when the
      withholding or withdrawal of life-support treatment is contemplated.



                                                                                  20
e. A directive may be revoked at any time by the declarer, without regard to
   declarer’s mental state or competency, by any of the following methods:
     i.   By being canceled, defaced, obliterated, burned, torn, or otherwise
          destroyed by the declarer or by some person in the declarer's presence
          and by the declarer’s direction.
    ii.   By a written revocation of the declarer expressing the declarer's intent
          to revoke, signed, and dated by the declarer. Such revocation shall
          become effective only upon communication to the attending physician
          by the declarer or by a person acting on behalf of the declarer. The
          attending physician shall record in the patient’s medical record the time
          and date when said physician received notification of the written
          revocation.
   iii.   By a verbal expression by the declarer of the declarer’s intent to revoke
          the directive. Such revocation shall become effective only upon
          communication to the attending physician by the declarer or by a person
          acting on behalf of the declarer. The attending physician shall record in
          the patient’s medical record the time, date, and place of the revocation
          and the time, date, and place, if different, of when said physician
          received notification of the revocation.
f. If the declarer becomes comatose or is rendered incapable of communicating
   with the attending physician, the directive shall remain in effect for the
   duration of the comatose condition or until such time as the declarer’s
   condition renders declarer able to communicate with the attending physician.
g. Any physician or health care provider acting under the direction of a
   physician, or health facility and its personnel who participate in good faith in
   the withholding or withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment from a qualified


                                                                                21
      patient in accordance with the requirements of this chapter, shall be immune
      from legal liability, including civil, criminal, or professional conduct
      sanctions, unless otherwise negligent.
   h. Any person who willfully conceals, cancels, defaces, obliterates, or damages
      the directive of another without such declarer’s consent is guilty of a gross
      misdemeanor.
   i. Any patient may appoint a surrogate decision maker or healthcare proxy
      through a written notarized document voluntarily signed by the patient in the
      presence of witnesses. Surrogate decision makers or healthcare proxies can
      make medical decisions on behalf of the individual in instances of inability to
      communicate, loss of decision-making capacity, or unconsciousness.
   j. If a qualified patient capable of making health care decisions indicates that he
      or she wishes to die at home, the patient shall be discharged as soon as
      reasonably possible. The health care provider or facility has an obligation to
      explain the medical risks of an immediate discharge to the qualified patient.
      If the provider or facility complies with the obligation to explain the medical
      risks of an immediate discharge to a qualified patient, there shall be no civil
      or criminal liability for claims arising from such discharge.
   k. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to condone, authorize, or approve mercy
      killing or physician-assisted suicide, or to permit any affirmative or deliberate
      act or omission to end life other than to permit the natural process of dying.

SECTION 15. Community-Based Palliative Care Services – Primary-level
palliative care services shall be integrated into the basic health services at the city,
municipal, and barangay levels. Standards of care shall conform to the National
Palliative and Hospice Care Program, the Department of Health’s Manual of



                                                                                     22
Operations, Procedures, and Standards for the National Palliative and Hospice Care
Program, and relevant issuances.

SECTION 16. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). – Each LGU, in
partnership with other institutions and community stakeholders, shall develop and
implement a community-responsive palliative care program that is inclusive and
considerate of all individuals, in accordance with the national guidelines set by the
Philippine Council for Palliative Care. This inclusive approach ensures that all
individuals, regardless of their background or circumstances, are considered in the
development and implementation of the palliative care program. LGUs shall
coordinate with relevant government agencies, private healthcare institutions, and
civil society to support this inclusive and culturally appropriate implementation.




SECTION 17. The Role of the Department of Health (DOH). – The DOH, in close
coordination with LGUs and other stakeholders, plays a crucial role in establishing
and supporting the operation of community-based palliative care facilities in
provinces, cities, and clusters of municipalities. This is based on population health
needs and service accessibility, demonstrating the government's commitment to
providing accessible care.

Each community-based palliative care facility shall have:

   a. An adequate physical infrastructure, including patient care rooms and
      administrative offices;
   b. A core team composed of a palliative care specialist, palliative care providers,
      palliative health aides, allied professionals, support staff, and community



                                                                                     23
      volunteers;
   c. A substantial amount of essential medical supplies, equipment, and
      medications appropriate for the facility’s service level; and
   d. A system that adheres to the Department.

LGUs, through their health offices, are required to submit quarterly reports to the
DOH and the Philippine Council for Palliative Care in accordance with the
requirements of the Palliative Care Registry and in compliance with the Data Privacy
Act of 2012.

SECTION 18. Palliative and Hospice Services in Regional, Provincial, and
Tertiary Hospitals. – All public and private regional, provincial, and tertiary
hospitals shall integrate the following palliative and hospice services to be provided
by a multidisciplinary team under the supervision of a palliative medicine specialist:

   a. Inpatient care in designated palliative or hospice wards for patients
      experiencing acute symptoms;
   b. Partial hospital care for those with moderate symptoms or psychosocial
      challenges;
   c. Outpatient services coordinated with community-based programs;
   d. Transition or home-based care for patients with special or long-term needs;
   e. Coordination with relevant programs addressing comorbidities, including
      substance use disorders and mental health conditions;
   f. An integrated referral system to public and private providers of palliative and
      hospice care;
   g. Hospital-based Palliative Care Registry that must record the personal
      identification of palliative patients, type of CLLI, management received and



                                                                                   24
       its results and other data that the DOH may prescribe. The information shall
       be treated with utmost confidentiality and shall not be released to third parties,
       in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 or the “Data Privacy Act of
       2012.” Submission of the Palliative Care registry data to the DOH shall be a
       requirement for the renewal of a license to operate of a hospital providing
       palliative and hospice care services.

SECTION 19. Referral Networks and Continuity. – Each province or region shall
establish a referral network for palliative and hospice care, ensuring coordinated
transitions across providers, facilities, and care settings. The DOH shall integrate
palliative care into Service Delivery Networks under the Universal Health Care
framework, with each network designating at least one palliative care specialist to
accept referrals and support other facilities. Continuity of essential medicines,
particularly for pain management, shall be ensured upon discharge through adequate
supply or valid prescriptions redeemable at community pharmacies.




SECTION 20. Duties and Responsibilities of Palliative Care Facilities. – Palliative
Care facilities shall:

   a. Ensure compliance with existing standards for licensure by the DOH and other
       relevant agencies;
   b. Establish internal policies to minimize involuntary and restrictive care
       practices;
   c. Provide all patients with accessible information regarding their rights under
       this Act;
   d. Ensure transparent communication of each patient’s individualized care plan;


                                                                                      25
   e. Obtain informed consent prior to any procedure, except in emergencies or
      when the patient has impaired decision-making capacity;
   f. Maintain detailed clinical records of treatments administered;
   g. Ensure legal representatives are designated in accordance with this Act,
      honoring patient preferences and safeguarding autonomy.

SECTION 21. Pediatric Palliative and Hospice Care. – The State shall ensure
access to age-appropriate palliative care for infants, children, and adolescents with
life-limiting or life-threatening conditions. In addition to the general provisions of
this Act, the following shall apply:

   a. Tertiary hospitals with pediatric services shall establish or designate pediatric
      palliative care teams to provide coordinated, family-centered care from
      diagnosis.
   b. The DOH, with relevant professional societies, shall develop pediatric-
      specific     standards    covering   pain   assessment,   medication     dosing,
      communication, and family support.
   c. LGUs and the DOH shall integrate pediatric palliative care into home and
      community programs and ensure access to child-appropriate medicines and
      educational continuity.
   d. Parents, guardians, and siblings shall receive counseling, decision-making
      guidance, and bereavement support as part of routine care.
   e. Providers shall prioritize pain relief, use age-appropriate tools, and ensure
      access to pediatric formulations, including opioids, with proper safeguards.
   f. Palliative care training shall be included in pediatric medical education and
      supported through continuing professional development programs.
   g. Hospice care for children shall be responsive and family-centered, with


                                                                                    26
      facilities staffed and equipped to meet pediatric needs.
   h. In cases of neglect or refusal of essential care, the DOH and Department of
      Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall act to uphold the child’s best
      interests and right to relief from serious health-related suffering.

Education and Promotion of Palliative Care in Educational Institutions and in
                                   the Workplace



SECTION 22. Public Awareness. – The DOH, in partnership with LGUs, other
institutions, media, and civil society, shall implement a nationwide information
campaign promoting awareness and understanding of palliative and hospice care.
The campaign shall include:

   a. Health education on the components and benefits of palliative care;
   b. Information on patient rights, advance care planning, and available services;
      and
   c. Promotion of community support, spiritual care, and psychosocial well-being.




SECTION 23. Encouraging the Integration of Palliative Care into the
Educational System. – The State shall recognize the importance of building
palliative care awareness, promoting the values of compassion, and imparting
knowledge of supportive care among students and health professionals. To this end:

   a. The Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education


                                                                                27
      (CHED), and Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
      (TESDA) shall incorporate age-appropriate and culturally sensitive content
      related to palliative and hospice care into relevant curricula and training
      programs, consistent with their institutional mandates and subject to existing
      education standards and development processes.
   b. Institutions offering medical and allied health courses shall include palliative
      and hospice care as essential components of undergraduate and postgraduate
      training, in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH) and
      professional societies.

SECTION 24. Promotion of Palliative Care in Educational Institutions. –
Educational institutions, such as schools, colleges, universities, and technical and
vocational institutions shall:

   a. Develop programs and information campaigns to raise awareness on palliative
      care, reduce stigma associated with life-limiting conditions, and promote
      supportive care values among students, teachers, and staff;
   b. Facilitate linkages with local health offices, professional associations, and
      palliative care organizations to provide appropriate support, referrals, and
      services for students and staff in need of palliative care or psychosocial
      support; and
   c. Adopt anti-discrimination      measures to ensure safe, inclusive,          and
      compassionate environments for people with CLLI.

SECTION 25. Palliative Care Promotion and Policies in the Workplace. – The
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Civil Service Commission
(CSC), and TESDA, in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), shall



                                                                                   28
develop guidelines and provide technical support to employers, workers’
organizations, and unions to:

   a. Promote awareness of palliative care and related health conditions through
      workplace communication initiatives, health and wellness programs, and
      continuing education;
   b. Encourage employers to mainstream supportive care and psychosocial
      assistance for workers living with CLLI and their families;
   c. Integrate, where feasible, appropriate palliative care services into workplace
      clinics or referral arrangements with accredited providers;
   d. Establish mechanisms that reduce stigma and discrimination in the workplace
      related to life-limiting conditions and promote compassion-based inclusion;
   e. Encourage employers to develop and implement internal policies that support
      workers and their families affected by CLLI, in collaboration with the DOLE,
      CSC, or other applicable regulatory agencies; and
   f. Promote meaningful family involvement in palliative and end-of-life care by
      encouraging the provision of short-term compassionate leave for immediate
      family members, without risk to their employment or benefits.

SECTION 26. Community-Based Palliative Care Education and Promotion. –
The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in partnership with
the DOH, LGUs, palliative care networks, and patient support organizations, shall
lead public awareness campaigns at the community level to:

   a. Promote understanding of palliative and hospice care, including the value of
      compassion, advance care planning, spiritual care, and family-centered care;
   b. Provide age-appropriate, gender-sensitive, and culturally relevant education



                                                                                 29
      on palliative care, especially for community leaders, barangay health workers,
      and out-of-school youth; and
   c. Develop Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials and
      conduct training workshops to strengthen local understanding and
      participation in palliative care.




                                   CHAPTER VI
                 Capacity Building, Research, and Development



SECTION 27. Capacity Building and Training of Palliative Care Specialists. –
The Department of Health, to ensure the delivery of quality, evidence-based
palliative care services, in coordination with the Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC) and recognized specialty societies in palliative care accredited
by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) shall:

   a. Establish and institutionalize national standards for palliative care specialists,
      including qualifications, scopes of practice, certification process, and
      continuing professional development in accordance with Republic Act No.
      10912 or the Continuing Professional Development Act of 2016;
   b. Designate a single national accrediting body for palliative medicine training
      programs and certification of palliative care specialists, based on defined
      criteria including nationwide representation, expertise, and alignment with
      DOH policies;
   c. Promote the recognition of palliative medicine as a medical and allied health
      subspecialty, and support the development of accredited training programs


                                                                                     30
       through partnerships with academic institutions and specialty boards;
   d. Ensure that palliative care specialists are equitably deployed across various
       levels of care, including regional hospitals, cancer centers, and community
       settings, especially in underserved areas; and
   e. Maintain a national registry of certified palliative care specialists to support
       workforce planning and resource distribution

SECTION 28. Capacity Building and Training of Palliative Care Providers. – The
DOH, as the leading authority in healthcare, in collaboration with other government
or private institutions, health facilities, professional societies, and other relevant
stakeholders, shall take the lead in developing and implementing standardized,
evidence-based training programs for palliative care providers, specialists, health
aides, and workers. These programs shall emphasize community- and public health-
oriented palliative care, promoting gender-sensitive, culturally appropriate, and
human rights-based approaches to care delivery.

The DOH shall ensure that all service providers engaged in the provision of palliative
and hospice care undergo continuing education, professional reorientation, and
periodic competency assessments.

SECTION 29. Capacity Building of Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) and
Palliative Health Aides (PHA). – The DOH and the TESDA shall lead the charge
in developing training modules and disseminating relevant information to LGUs.
This collaborative effort aims to educate and empower BHWs and community-based
volunteers, underscoring the shared responsibility in delivering community-based
palliative care.

The LGUs, with technical assistance from the DOH, the TESDA, and accredited


                                                                                   31
palliative and hospice care organizations, shall be primarily responsible for training
and continuing the development of BHWs and volunteers in community-based
palliative care delivery. The DOH shall likewise provide necessary assistance in the
form of supplies, equipment, and resource materials.

SECTION 30. Research and Development on Palliative and Hospice Care. – The
DOH, in partnership with research institutions, academic organizations, medical and
allied health associations, and civil society organizations, shall promote and support
research on palliative and hospice care.

Such research aims to generate evidence-based, culturally relevant, and inclusive
models of palliative care delivery, including the documentation of indigenous and
traditional practices that align with palliative care goals.

All research shall observe the highest ethical standards, including but not limited to:

   a. Securing free and informed consent from participants;
   b. Prohibiting undue inducement or coercion in participant recruitment;
   c. Avoiding research activities that are harmful or pose undue risks; and
   d. Requiring approval from an accredited institutional review board or
      independent ethics committee, in accordance with national regulations.



SECTION 31. National Center in Palliative Care. – There shall be established a
National Center in Palliative Care under the control and supervision of the DOH, to
be headed by an Executive Director. Other comprehensive specialty palliative
centers shall be designated in strategic locations across the country. The National
Center in Palliative Care shall have the following purposes and objectives:



                                                                                    32
   a. Promote, encourage, and engage in scientific research on palliative care;
   b. Encourage and undertake the training of palliative care service providers,
      palliative health aides, allied health professionals, patients, and families on
      palliative care and related activities;
   c. Assist communities, universities, hospitals, and research institutions in
      training, research, and capacity-building in palliative care and related
      activities;
   d. House the National Palliative Care Registry and its Registry Committee; and
   e. Publish annual reports from the National Palliative Care Registry including
      an evaluation of the state of palliative and hospice care in the country. These
      reports shall be submitted to the Council, the Department of Health, the Global
      Atlas of Palliative Care and other similar international bodies reporting on
      global palliative care services.

SECTION 32. National Palliative Care Registry. – The DOH in collaboration with
relevant stakeholders shall establish a national palliative care registry and
monitoring system. The registry shall cover all forms of palliative care illnesses
among adults and children and serve as a guide in policy development of the
National Palliative and Hospice Care Program.

              Duties and Responsibilities of Government Agencies



SECTION 33. Duties and Responsibilities of the Department of Health (DOH). –
To achieve the policy and objectives of this Act, the DOH shall:


                                                                                  33
a. Formulate and implement a comprehensive National Palliative and Hospice
   Care Program, in coordination with relevant government agencies and
   stakeholders. This pivotal role includes a public awareness campaign on

   reduce stigma and misinformation. The DOH will be responsible for

c. Integrate palliative care indicators into the National Health Information

d. Promote and support research and innovation, including partnerships with

f. Coordinate with the PhilHealth to develop and expand insurance packages

   illness and aligned with UHC principles;
g. Prohibit any form of coerced or unpaid labor within palliative care settings,

h. Provide psychosocial, spiritual, and practical support services to families,

  l. Facilitate human rights and palliative care training for health professionals in

SECTION 34. Duties and Responsibilities of the Commission on Human Rights

  b. Inspect palliative care health facilities to ensure that palliative patients therein

                                Essential Medicines

SECTION 44. Essential Palliative Medicines. – The Department of Health (DOH),
in coordination with the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA), the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA), and other relevant agencies, shall ensure the
availability, affordability, accessibility, and appropriate use of essential medicines
for palliative and hospice care. These medicines shall be included in the Philippine
National Formulary and made available at all levels of the public health system,
especially in underserved and geographically isolated areas.

SECTION 45. Implementation of Evidence-based Reforms. – The DOH shall
adopt and implement evidence-based reforms to:

   a. Facilitate early and uninterrupted access to essential palliative medicines,
      including opioids and controlled substances for pain and symptom
      management;
   b. Develop and promote compassionate use protocols, emergency dispensing
      mechanisms, and simplified documentation for patients with CLLI;
   c. Implement training programs for healthcare providers and pharmacists on the
      safe and ethical prescription and administration of palliative medicines;
   d. Strengthen the supply chain, inventory, and cold-chain management systems
      to prevent stockouts and wastage;
   e. Ensure that innovative medicines and emerging palliative health technologies
      are evaluated for inclusion into the formulary based on therapeutic value, cost-
      effectiveness, and ethical standards.

SECTION 46. Establishment of a Rights-sensitive Regulatory Framework. – The
PDEA and FDA shall establish a dedicated, streamlined, and rights-sensitive
regulatory framework for the licensing, procurement, prescription, distribution, and



                                                                                   42
post-marketing surveillance of essential palliative medicines, consistent with the
UHC Act, the Dangerous Drugs Act (RA 9165), and international guidelines of the
World Health Organization (WHO).



                                   CHAPTER IX
                     Affordable Palliative and Hospice Care




SECTION 47. Establishment of a Palliative Care Trust Fund. – There is hereby
established a Palliative Care Trust Fund. The DOH and the Council shall manage
the Fund in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules and
regulations and shall make a quarterly report to the Office of the President and
Congress on the disbursement of the Fund.

This fund is intended to ensure financial support for palliative care programs,
services, and medications, particularly for the palliative and hospice care services
provided to indigent and economically disadvantaged patients who may not be able
to afford care or whose PhilHealth benefits are insufficient. The Palliative Care Trust
Fund shall consist of:

   a. An annual appropriations from the national government as provided in the
      General Appropriations Act (an initial seed amount is to be appropriated under
      Section 13 of this Act);
   b. A portion of the sin tax revenues or universal health care funds, as may be
      determined by law or by the Department of Finance in consultation with DOH;
   c. Donations, grants, endowments, or contributions from domestic or foreign



                                                                                    43
       donors; and
   d. Income or interest derived from the investment of the Palliative Care Trust
       Fund as permitted by law will accrue to the fund to be used for the same
       purposes.

SECTION 48. Donations. – The DOH may solicit and receive donations which
shall form part of the Fund and such donations shall be exempt from income or
donor’s tax and all other taxes, fees, and charges imposed by the government.
Likewise, fund raising activities may be conducted by the Council and the proceeds
of which shall accrue to the Fund and shall be exempt from any and all taxes.

Receipts from donations, whether in cash or in kind, shall be accounted for in the
books of the donee government agency in accordance with accounting and auditing
rules and regulations. The receipts from cash donations and proceeds from sale of
donated commodities shall be deposited with the National Treasury and recorded as
a special account in the General Fund and shall be available to the implementing
agency. The cash value of the donation shall be deemed automatically appropriated
for the purpose specified by the donor. Donations within a term not exceeding one
(1) year shall be treated as trust receipts.

The donee-agency concerned shall submit the quarterly reports of all donations
received, whether in cash or in kind, and expenditures or disbursements thereon with
electronic signature to the DBM, through the Unified Reporting System, and to the
Speaker of the House of Representatives, the President of the Senate of the
Philippines, the House Committee on Appropriations, the Senate Committee on
Finance and the Commission on Audit, by positing such reports on the donee-agency
concerned websites for a period of three (3) years. The head of the donee-agency



                                                                                 44
concerned shall send written notice to the said offices when said reports have been
posted on its website which shall be considered the date of submission.

SECTION 49. PhilHealth Benefits for Palliative and Hospice Care. – The
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation shall expand its benefit packages to include
palliative and hospice care in both adults and children and in all settings of care,
including home-based care. It shall also develop innovative benefits such as support
for community-based models of care. The development or expansion of any
PhilHealth benefit shall go through a proper, transparent and standardized
prioritization setting process, such as the Health Technology Assessment and
actuarial feasibility study, to avoid inequitable allocation of funds for health care
services.

The Palliative Care Trust Fund and PhilHealth benefits shall be made available in
public and private DOH-licenced palliative care facilities. DOH, the Council, and
PhilHealth shall prescribe, in consultation with stakeholders, the coverage rates and
applicable rules on options to charge co-payment for services rendered. Processes to
avail of such funding shall be streamlined to ensure timely provision of palliative
and hospice care.

SECTION 50. Social Protection Mechanisms. – The DOH, in collaboration with
the Social Security System (SSS), Government Service Insurance System (GSIS),
Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), DOLE, DSWD, PhilHealth, and
LGUs, shall develop appropriate and easily accessible social protection mechanisms
for palliative care patients, their families, and carers. It shall aim to encourage the
underprivileged and marginalized palliative care patient to undergo the necessary
care.



                                                                                    45
A Palliative Care Policy shall be established in every workplace. It shall form part
of the employee benefits in the formal sector covering all palliative and hospice care
services.

The Insurance Commission shall mandate the Health Maintenance Organizations
(HMOs) to cover palliative and hospice care services for both adults and children,
including home-based palliative care.

The palliative care-related absences from work of member employees and primary
carers and voluntary members shall be covered and compensated by the Sickness
Benefits of the SSS and Disability Benefits of the GSIS.

The employees in the informal sector shall be prioritized in the palliative and hospice
care packages of PhilHealth while employees in the formal sector shall be offered
cost-sharing PhilHealth benefit packages.




                                    CHAPTER X
                  Monitoring, Participation, and Accountability




SECTION 51. Monitoring and Evaluation Framework. – The Department of
Health (DOH), through the Philippine Council for Palliative Care, shall establish a
comprehensive Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Framework. This framework, of
utmost importance, will assess the implementation and impact of the National
Palliative and Hospice Care Program. This task cannot be achieved without the
active participation of all stakeholders.



                                                                                    46
The framework shall be based on evidence-based indicators covering access, equity,
quality of care, financial protection, and patient and family satisfaction. It shall be
aligned with the goals of the Universal Health Care Act, WHO guidelines, and
international human rights instruments such as the UDHR, ICCPR, and ICESCR.




SECTION 52. Multi-Stakeholder Engagement. – The DOH shall develop a formal
engagement mechanism that includes all stakeholders. This includes, but is not
limited to, civil society organizations, patient and family groups, academic
institutions, faith-based groups, health professionals, and private sector partners.
Your involvement is crucial, and your perspectives are valued in the formulation of
policies, delivery of services, training, research, and community-based initiatives.

Stakeholders shall be consulted in the formulation of policies through regular
meetings, surveys, and public forums. Their input will be considered and integrated
into the policy development process, ensuring that palliative care remains people-
centered, inclusive, and culturally relevant.




SECTION 53. Community Participation and Grievance Mechanism. – LGUs, in
coordination with DOH and community organizations, shall establish participatory
platforms that allow palliative care beneficiaries and their families to provide
feedback, raise concerns, and co-create solutions to improve services.

A grievance redress system shall be in place to ensure that all complaints related to
access, quality, or abuse in palliative care services are addressed promptly and
appropriately.


                                                                                       47
SECTION 54. Transparency and Public Access. – All reports, strategic plans, audit
findings, and evaluation outcomes related to the implementation of this Act shall be
published on the official DOH website and disseminated through public health
information platforms. This commitment to transparency and citizen awareness is a
testament to our dedication to keeping you informed about the progress and
challenges in palliative care.




SECTION 55. Legislative Oversight and Sunset Review. – A Joint Congressional
Oversight Committee on Palliative and Hospice Care shall be created to oversee the
full and faithful implementation of this Act. This committee, composed of members
from both houses of Congress, will be responsible for reviewing the progress of the
Act's implementation,      identifying any issues or challenges, and making
recommendations for improvement.

Every five (5) years, the Act shall be subject to a legislative sunset review to
determine its relevance, responsiveness, and effectiveness. The review shall be
based on the findings of the M&E Framework and stakeholder consultations.
Recommendations for policy refinements or amendments shall be submitted to
Congress.

                                 Miscellaneous Provisions




                                                                                 48
SECTION 56. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Health (DOH), specifically to cover:

   a. Maintenance and other operating expenses (MOOE) of the National Palliative
      and Hospice Care Program, a crucial component of this Act, including human
      resources, training, procurement of essential medicines, support for palliative
      care facilities, and public awareness campaigns.
   b. Capital outlay for the development of community-based and facility-based
      palliative care services in selected DOH-retained hospitals; and
   c. Formulation and operationalization of the National Strategic Plan for
      Palliative Care, as provided under this Act.

For subsequent years, funding requirements for implementing this Act shall be
determined based on the Philippine Council for Palliative Care's multi-year
investment plan. They shall be included in the annual budget proposal of the DOH
and other implementing agencies. Such amounts shall be reflected in the National
Expenditure Program (NEP) and incorporated into the General Appropriations Act
(GAA).

SECTION 57. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Health, as the key
figure, in consultation with key implementing agencies, including the DSWD,
DILG, and other relevant agencies and stakeholders representing palliative care
patients, professionals, and service providers, shall promulgate the necessary
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) to carry out the provisions of this Act.

The DOH shall ensure that the IRR reflects the principles of universal healthcare,


                                                                                    49
participatory governance, gender responsiveness, cultural sensitivity, and the right
to health and human dignity, in alignment with existing laws and the Philippines'
international obligations under the UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, and the World Health
Organization frameworks, underscoring our commitment to these principles.

SECTION 58. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the remainder of the
Act or any provision not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect, and
the court's decision shall not affect the validity of the Act as a whole.

SECTION 59. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
administrative regulations, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 60. Effectivity. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative
regulations, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
