                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Every child deserves the opportunity to reach his or her full potential. Yet
many Filipino children with developmental delays, disabilities, and other special
needs remain undiagnosed or receive intervention only after critical
developmental milestones have been missed, particularly in underserved and
geographically isolated communities.

      While Republic Act No. 11650 or the Inclusive Education Act strengthened
the State's commitment to inclusive learning, gaps remain in the early detection,
assessment, referral, and intervention systems needed to support children with
special needs. Families continue to face barriers such as limited access to
specialists, high therapy costs, and inadequate community-based support
services.

       This measure seeks to establish a comprehensive and affordable
framework for the early detection and intervention of children with special needs
through accessible screening, assessment, intervention, parent support,
workforce development, and stronger coordination among health, education, and
social welfare agencies. It also strengthens community-based support systems,
enhances Special Education (SPED) Centers, and promotes accountability
through monitoring and oversight mechanisms.

     By ensuring timely intervention and support, the State can help children
with special needs achieve better developmental, educational, and social
outcomes, while ensuring that no child is left behind.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      PROVIDING   A   COMPREHENSIVE    AND    AFFORDABLE
      FRAMEWORK FOR EARLY DETECTION AND INTERVENTION
      SERVICES FOR CHILDREN WITH SPECIAL NEEDS”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Early Detection
and Intervention Services for Children with Special Needs Act of 2026."

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect
and promote the rights of children with special needs to equal opportunities in
education, healthcare, and social integration. The State shall establish a
comprehensive framework for early detection, assessment, and intervention,
ensuring that no child is left behind, regardless of geographic, economic, and
social barriers. Towards this end, the State shall support and empower children
with special needs and their families by giving them better lifelong outcomes
through accessible and affordable early detection and intervention services.

      SECTION 3. Objectives. - This Act seeks to promote the rights and
developmental potential of all Filipino children, particularly those with or at risk
of developmental delays and disabilities, by establishing a comprehensive and
inclusive framework for early detection and intervention. Specifically, it aims to:

      (a) Establish a unified and integrated National Program for the early
          screening, identification, diagnosis, detection, and monitoring of
          developmental delays, disabilities, and disorders in children. The
          National Program shall be led by the Department of Health (DOH), in
          close coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd), the
          Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), and Local
          Government Units (LGUs).
      (b) Ensure the provision of free or subsidized intervention services,
          including but not limited to occupational, physical, speech-language,
          psychological, and educational therapies, accessible across all regions,
          including geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

      (c) Build and sustain a competent and adequately trained workforce in
          the fields of health, education, and social services through continuing
          professional development programs focused on early childhood
          disability identification and intervention.

      (d) Promote public awareness and advocacy to empower parents,
          caregivers, and communities with knowledge on child development,
          early warning signs, available services, and to reduce stigma against
          children with disabilities.

      (e) Provide scholarship grants to deserving Filipino students pursuing
          degree programs in professions relevant to early childhood
          development, disability identification, and intervention, with a
          corresponding return service obligation in public or accredited
          facilities.

      (f) Strengthen inter-agency coordination and governance mechanisms
          among the DOH, DepEd, DSWD, LGUs, and other relevant entities to
          ensure the effective, inclusive, and rights-based delivery of services to
          children with developmental needs.

      (g) Strengthen coordination among health, education, and social welfare
          systems to ensure continuity of services from early childhood through
          basic education.

      (h) Establish community-based and family-centered support mechanisms
          for the early detection, assessment, referral, intervention, and
          monitoring of children with special needs, and ensure the active
          participation of parents and caregivers in individualized intervention
          and educational planning.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - All children from birth up to eighteen (18) years
of age shall be entitled to services under this Act, with priority given to children
from birth up to eight (8) years of age. Priority shall be given to children who are
suspected of, or diagnosed with, developmental delays, disabilities, or
neurodevelopmental disorders including, but not limited to the following:

      (a) Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) - A developmental disorder typically
      appearing before age three, characterized by challenges in communication,
      social interaction, and repetitive behaviors, often co-occurring with
      intellectual disability or epilepsy.
(b) Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)- A neurodevelopmental
disorder marked by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and
impulsivity inappropriate for the child's age.

(c) Behavioral Disorders - Emotional or behavioral issues that disrupt
learning, social functioning, or classroom environments, requiring
behavioral support or intervention.

(d) Cerebral Palsy (CP) - A group of non-progressive neurological disorders
affecting movement, posture, and coordination, often caused by brain
damage before, during, or shortly after birth.

(e) Down Syndrome - A genetic condition caused by the presence of an
extra chromosome, leading to developmental delays, intellectual disability,
and characteristic physical features.

(f) Global Developmental Delay (GDD) - A condition diagnosed in children
under five years old who show significant delays in two or more
developmental domains: motor skills, speech and language, cognitive
development, social interaction, or activities of daily living.

(g) Intellectual Disability - A condition marked by significant limitations in
intellectual functioning and adaptive behavior, affecting conceptual,
social, and practical skills.

(h) Specific Learning Disability (SLD) – refers to a neurodevelopmental
disorder that affects one or more of the basic psychological processes
involved in understanding or using spoken or written language, which may
manifest in difficulties in listening, thinking, speaking, reading, writing,
spelling, or performing mathematical calculations, despite otherwise
adequate intelligence and educational opportunities. This includes
conditions such as dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia, developmental
aphasia, and other related learning disorders.

(i) Visual Impairment - Includes both blindness and low vision. Blindness
refers to a visual capacity of 20/200 or less in the better eye with
maximum correction; low vision involves limited vision that allows reading
only under special conditions.

(j) Hearing Impairment - Includes deafness (non-functional hearing) and
hard-of-hearing conditions (partially functional hearing possibly with
hearing aids).
      (k) Orthopedic or Physical Disabilities- Conditions affecting the
      musculoskeletal system that hinder mobility or motor functioning,
      whether temporary or permanent.

      (l) Speech or Communication Disorders - Conditions that impair the ability
      to communicate, including stuttering articulation issues, language
      impairment, or voice disorders that negatively impact learning.

      (m) Multiple Disabilities - The presence of two or more disabling conditions
      (e.g. deaf-blindness, autism with intellectual disability) requiring
      combined support services.

      (n) Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) - An acquired brain injury caused by
      external physical force, resulting in cognitive, behavioral, or physical
      impairments that adversely affect learning and functioning. TBI excludes
      congenital or degenerative brain conditions and birth-related trauma.

       This Act shall also grant priority to those who reside in disaster-prone
areas and Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDA), such as
areas that are isolated due to distance, inaccessibility to transportation, and
weather conditions, unserved and underserved communities and other areas
identified to have high incidences of poverty, those persons belonging to the
vulnerable sector, communities in or recovering from situation of crisis or armed
conflict and recognized as such by a government body.

      SECTION 5. National Program for Early Detection and Intervention.
- The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department of
Education (DepEd), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
and other relevant agencies, shall jointly develop, establish, and implement a
comprehensive and integrated National Program for Early Detection and
Intervention. The Program shall aim to identify, support, and monitor Filipino
children with developmental concerns. It shall ensure the following key
components:

      (a) Early and Accessible Screening, Diagnosis, and Intervention
          Services - A standardized system shall be developed and deployed
          nationwide to provide routine developmental screening at barangay
          health centers, daycare centers, and other community-based settings,
          with appropriate referral pathways for diagnostic and intervention
          services. Services shall be culturally sensitive, family-centered, and
          financially accessible.

      (b) Trained and Competent Workforce in Early Intervention and
          Child Development - The Program shall support the capacity building
          of healthcare and education professionals, including but not limited to
          special education teachers, developmental pediatricians, therapists,
          social workers, allied health personnel and barangay health workers.
          Training shall focus on early childhood development, disability
          inclusion, and evidence-based intervention strategies.

      (c) Public Awareness and Advocacy Campaign. - A nationwide
          information and advocacy campaign shall be implemented to educate
          parents, caregivers, and communities on early childhood development,
          the importance of timely screening and intervention, and to reduce
          stigma and discrimination against children with disabilities.

      (d) Monitoring, Evaluation, and Data Management. - A robust
          monitoring and evaluation framework shall be established to track the
          implementation and outcomes of the Program. This shall include the
          collection and analysis of age-disaggregated and disability-specific
          data, establishment of benchmarks, and the use of performance
          indicators to assess impact and inform policy adjustments.

      (e) Integration of screening, assessment, intervention, and referral
          services with Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs), SPED
          Centers, and public schools.

      (f) Establishment of referral pathways between hospitals, schools,
          ILRCs, Special Education (SPED) Centers, and local government
          units.

      (g) Parent and caregiver education and support services.

      The implementing agencies shall issue joint guidelines within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act to operationalize the National Program and
harmonization with existing government initiatives on child development,
disability rights, and inclusive education.

      SECTION 6. Mandatory Early Childhood Developmental Screening
and Assessment.- All government health centers and public hospitals shall be
mandated to conduct regular developmental screening and assessments for
infants and children from birth up to eight (8) years of age as part of their basic
health services.

      Developmental screening may likewise be conducted in child development
centers, day care centers, public schools, and Inclusive Learning Resource
Centers, subject to standards jointly issued by the DOH and DepEd.

      Such screening shall follow evidence-based clinical guidelines to be issued
by the DOH, and shall be performed by trained health professionals or barangay
health workers (BHWs) under supervision.
       The mandatory early childhood developmental screening and assessment
shall be free of charge.

      SECTION 7. Mandatory PhilHealth Coverage. - The Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) shall provide mandatory coverage for
medically necessary occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech therapy,
and other related therapeutic interventions for children with special needs. Such
coverage shall include, but not be limited to, assessment, diagnosis, treatment,
and rehabilitation services rendered by duly licensed and accredited
professionals. PhilHealth is hereby directed to formulate and implement
appropriate benefit packages and reimbursement schemes to ensure accessible,
affordable, and quality therapeutic care for children with special needs. The
DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth and other relevant agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement this
provision.

       SECTION 8. Scholarship Grants in Early Childhood Disability
Identification and Intervention. - To ensure the sustained availability of
qualified professionals in the field of early childhood disability identification and
intervention, scholarship grants shall hereby be provided to deserving Filipino
students who wish to pursue degree programs in medicine, psychology,
occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech-language pathology, special
needs education, social work, and other allied health or social science disciplines
relevant to the early detection and management of childhood disabilities, subject
to guidelines to be issued by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in
coordination with the DOH, the Department of Education (DepEd), and other
concerned agencies.

       Scholars shall be required to render return service in government
institutions or accredited facilities providing services to children with special
needs, for a period equivalent to the number of years they benefited from the
scholarship, under terms to be defined in the implementing rules and regulations
of this Act.

       SECTION 9. Establishment of Early Childhood Disability
Identification and Intervention Service Facilities in Government Hospitals.
- To ensure the accessibility and availability of early childhood disability
identification and intervention services across all regions of the country, the
following shall be mandated:

(a) Level 3 Government Hospitals. - All Level 3 government hospitals under the
supervision of the DOH shall establish, operate, and maintain an Early
Childhood Development and Inclusion Center dedicated to the comprehensive
evaluation, diagnosis, and intervention of developmental delays, disabilities, and
disorders in children. These Centers shall be multidisciplinary in nature and
staffed by qualified professionals, including but not limited to developmental
pediatricians, psychologists, speech-language pathologists, occupational
therapists, physical therapists, and social workers. Services shall include
developmental screening, comprehensive assessments, individualized therapy
programs, caregiver support, and coordination with community-based services.

      (b) Level 1 and Level 2 Government Hospitals. - All Level 1 and Level 2
government hospitals shall establish at least a Special Needs Unit or Office
within their premises to provide initial developmental screening and referral
services for children suspected of having developmental delays or disabilities.
These Units or Offices shall be manned by appropriately trained healthcare
personnel and equipped to conduct preliminary assessments and facilitate
timely referrals to higher-level hospitals or specialized centers, as may be
necessary.

      The DOH shall issue the necessary standards, protocols, and capacity-
building programs to ensure the effective implementation of this provision and
shall prioritize the allocation of resources to geographically isolated,
disadvantaged, and remote areas.

       Implementation shall be undertaken in phases within five (5) years from
the effectivity of this Act.

      SECTION 10. Enhancement and Improvement of Existing Special
Education (SPED) Centers. - The DepEd shall ensure the enhancement,
upgrading, and continuous improvement of all existing SPED Centers in public
schools and institutions. This shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

       (a) Upgrading of facilities and learning environments to meet inclusive and
child-friendly standards, with appropriate assistive technologies, instructional
materials, and accessible infrastructure.

      (b)Provision of adequate and qualified SPED teachers, para-teachers, and
allied professionals through targeted recruitment, training, and capacity-
building programs.

      (c) Allocation of sufficient budget and resources for the maintenance and
operational requirements of SPED Centers, including the procurement of
developmental tools, therapy aids, and learning equipment tailored for children
with disabilities.

     (d) Integration of inclusive and culturally responsive pedagogical
approaches and individualized education programs (IEPs) in SPED curricula.

      (e) Strengthening of partnerships with local government units,
nongovernmental organizations, and the private sector to support the delivery of
specialized services and community-based interventions.
     DepEd shall conduct an annual assessment of all public SPED Centers to
monitor compliance, identify resource gaps, and ensure the effective
implementation of this provision.

     The enhancement of SPED Centers shall complement the establishment
and operation of Inclusive Learning Resource Centers under Republic Act No.
11650.

      SECTION 11. Lead Implementing Agencies. - The DOH, in collaboration
with the DepEd, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, the
DSWD, and the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), shall jointly
implement this program through Regional and Local Health Units and Special
Education (SPED) Centers.

     SECTION 12. Local Government Involvement. - The LGUs shall integrate
the program into their local development plan, ensuring barangay-level
implementation, and designate focal persons for monitoring and reporting.

     SECTION 13. Appropriations. - The amount deemed necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be charged against appropriations of the
concerned agencies under the current General Appropriations Act (GAA).
Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
GAA.

      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DILG, DepEd, and DSWD, in
consultation with relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary IRR.

      SECTION 15. Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting. - The DOH, in
coordination with DepEd, DSWD, NCDA, DILG, and LGUs, shall establish a
monitoring and evaluation system. An annual report shall be submitted to
Congress containing:

     (a) Number of children screened;
     (b) Number assessed and diagnosed;
     (c) Intervention services provided;
     (d) Geographic coverage;
     (e) Budget utilization; and
     (f) Policy recommendations.

     SECTION 16. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee. - A Joint
Congressional Oversight Committee is hereby created to review and monitor the
implementation of this Act.

      The Committee shall conduct a comprehensive review every three (3) years
and recommend appropriate legislative or administrative measures.
      SECTION 17. Data Privacy. - All personal information collected pursuant
to this Act shall be processed in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173,
otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

      SECTION 18. Separability Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
and rules inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 19. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with or
contrary to the provisions of this Act are repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SECTION 20. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or at least two national newspapers of
general circulation.

      Approved.
