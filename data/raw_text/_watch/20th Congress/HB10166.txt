                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippine Constitution recognizes labor as a primary social economic force
and mandates the State to protect the rights of workers while promoting full
employment, productivity, and the participation of private enterprises in national
development. As digital technologies continue to reshape the workplace, employment
policies must likewise evolve to encourage innovation, improve competitiveness, and
provide flexible work arrangements that benefit both employers and employees
without compromising labor standards.

     Republic Act No. 11534, otherwise known as the Corporate Recovery and Tax
Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act, provides fiscal incentives to registered
business enterprises operating within economic zones and freeports. However, its
requirement that registered activities be conducted exclusively within these
designated areas has created uncertainty regarding the adoption of alternative work
arrangements such as telecommuting, hybrid work, compressed workweeks, and
other flexible work schemes. Although temporary administrative measures were
adopted during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, the absence of a clear statutory
framework continues to create regulatory uncertainty for investors and enterprises
seeking to modernize their operations while maintaining their incentive eligibility.

      The rapid advancement of digital infrastructure and information technology has
demonstrated that many business processes, particularly in information technology,
business process outsourcing, research and development, engineering, finance, and
other knowledge-based industries, can be performed efficiently outside traditional
office settings. Allowing registered enterprises to adopt alternative work arrangements
enhances operational resilience, reduces business costs, improves employee
productivity and work-life balance, expands employment opportunities for persons in
geographically distant areas, and strengthens the Philippines' competitiveness as an
investment destination in an increasingly digital global economy.

     This measure seeks to amend Section 309 of the National Internal Revenue
Code, as amended by the CREATE Act, by expressly allowing registered business
enterprises under Investment Promotion Agencies to adopt telecommuting, work-

from-home, hybrid work, flexible work arrangements, compressed workweeks, and
other lawful alternative work arrangements without forfeiting their entitlement to fiscal
incentives. At the same time, the bill ensures that such arrangements remain voluntary,
mutually agreed upon, and fully compliant with Republic Act No. 11165, otherwise
known as the Telecommuting Act, as well as existing labor laws, occupational safety
standards, data privacy requirements, cybersecurity safeguards, and other statutory
rights afforded to workers.

      The proposed measure likewise empowers Investment Promotion Agencies to
issue reasonable implementation and monitoring guidelines that balance regulatory
oversight with operational flexibility. By institutionalizing alternative work
arrangements within the country's investment incentives framework, the bill promotes
business continuity, encourages technological innovation, attracts higher-value
investments, supports environmental sustainability through reduced commuting, and
enables Philippine enterprises to remain competitive in the rapidly evolving global
digital economy.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
