                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE



The Philippines, an archipelagic nation, relies heavily on its “blue economy,” with the
fisheries sector contributing approximately 1.3% to 1.5% of the National Gross Domestic
Product (Philippine Statistics Authority, 2023).1 However, there exists a profound socio-
economic paradox: the very individuals who secure the nation’s primary protein source—
our fisherfolk and fishworkers—consistently register the highest poverty incidence
among all basic sectors at 30.6% (PSA, 2023).2 This systemic impoverishment is not

For decades, fisherfolk have been marginalized through misclassification. In both
municipal and commercial operations, they are often labeled as “commission-based
partners” or “independent contractors.” This nomenclature is a legal fiction designed to
circumvent the mandatory obligations of the Labor Code. In reality, these workers operate
under a state of total subordination to Fish Traders and Vessel Owners.

Judicial Basis: The “Control Test” and Economic Subordination

Philippine Jurisprudence is clear on the determination of an employer-employee
relationship. Under the “four-fold test” affirmed in landmark cases such as Chavez v.
NLRC (G.R. No. 148253) and Pauig v. NLRC, the elements of employment are: (1) selection
and engagement; (2) payment of wages; (3) power of dismissal; and (4) the power of
control.3

In the fishing industry, Fish Traders exercise absolute control by dictating the timing of
voyages, specifying gear requirements, and enforcing exclusivity in the sale of the catch.
This level of operational and economic direction is the hallmark of employment. Yet, by
denying this status, “Traders” evade their responsibilities to provide social security,
medical benefits, and security of tenure.

International and Statistical Imperatives

The International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention No. 188 (Work in Fishing
Convention, 2007)4 identifies the industry as highly susceptible to forced labor and debt
bondage. This is largely due to the informal nature of hiring and the “share-based” system,
which often operates as a predatory credit mechanism, leaving workers in a perpetual
cycle of debt to their traders. 5

Constitutional Mandate

Article XIII, Section 3 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to afford
full protection to labor—local and overseas, organized and unorganized—and guarantee
security of tenure and humane conditions of work. The current unregulated state of the
fisheries labor market is a direct affront to this constitutional guarantee. 6

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Security of Tenure for
 2   Fisherfolk and Fishworkers Act” or "Batas Alon".
 3
 4   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the cardinal policy of the State to afford full
 5   protection to labor—local and overseas, organized and unorganized—and to promote
 6   full employment and equality of employment opportunities for all. Recognizing the
 7   fishing industry as a vital component of the national economy and a high-risk maritime
 8   endeavor, the State hereby declares the following:
 9       1. Guarantee of Labor Rights: The State shall guarantee the rights of all fisherfolk
10          and fishworkers to Security of Tenure, humane conditions of work, and a living
11          wage. The State shall actively prevent the misclassification of these workers as
12          "independent contractors" or "partners" when the elements of an employer-
13          employee relationship are present.
14       2. Social Justice in Fisheries: Pursuant to Article XIII, Section 3 of the Constitution,
15          the State shall regulate the relations between workers and employers,
 1         recognizing the inherent imbalance of power in the "share-based" system and the
 2         "Takay" (advances) tradition.
 3      3. Adherence to International Standards: The State shall align its domestic labor
 4         standards with the International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention No. 188
 5         (Work in Fishing Convention, 2007), ensuring that fishers have written articles of
 6         agreement, health protection, and social security.
 7      4. Economic Empowerment and Financial Inclusion: The State recognizes that
 8         Economic Independence is the bedrock of labor rights. Therefore, it shall provide
 9         mandatory mechanisms for fisherfolk to access formal credit, micro-financing,
10         and the right to self-organization through cooperatives, thereby eliminating debt
11         bondage and dependence on usurious informal credit.
12      5. Protection of Municipal and Commercial Workers: The State shall ensure that
13         these protections extend to all workers across the spectrum of the industry, from
14         small-scale municipal fishing to large-scale commercial deep-sea operations,
15         without distinction as to the size of the vessel or the method of compensation.

16   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For the implementation of this Act, the following
17   terms are defined and expounded:

18      1. Fisherfolk – Refers to individuals, naturally born or naturalized citizens of the
19         Philippines, directly or personally engaged in catching fish or other aquatic
20         products and related activities. This includes those in municipal waters or
21         commercial waters. Under this Act, their status is elevated from "marginalized
22         laborers" to Regular Employees when they operate under the control of a Fish
23         Trader or Vessel Owner.
24      2. Fishworker – Refers to any person, whether skilled or unskilled, regularly
25         employed or engaged in any capacity on board a fishing vessel or in the hauling,
26         sorting, and primary processing of fish at the point of landing. This includes
27         "crew members," "buseros," and "batilyos." They are hereby recognized as
28         Regular Employees entitled to all benefits under the Labor Code.
29      3. Fish Trader (Consignee/Buyer-Lender) – Refers to any person, partnership, or
30         corporation who provides capital, equipment, fuel, or credit to fisherfolk. A Fish
31         Trader is considered an Employer under this Act if they impose the condition of
32         Exclusive Sale of Catch or exercise Operational Control over fishing schedules.
33      4. Security of Tenure – The constitutionally guaranteed right of fisherfolk and
34         fishworkers to continued employment. They shall not be terminated or
35         "grounded" except for Just Causes or Authorized Causes as provided under the
36         Labor Code, and only after observance of Due Process.
37      5. Control Test – The legal standard used to establish an employer-employee
38         relationship. It exists when the entity for whom the services are performed
39         reserves the right to control not only the result but also the means and methods
40         used to achieve it.
41      6. Articles of Agreement – The written contract of employment between the
42         fishworker and the vessel owner/trader, registered with the DOLE and BFAR. This
43         document serves as the primary Proof of Employment.
 1   SECTION 4. The Employer-Employee Relationship and the Multi-Factor Control Test. –
 2   An Employer-Employee Relationship shall be legally presumed to exist between a Fish
 3   Trader or Vessel Owner (Employer) and a Fisherfolk or Fishworker (Employee) when the
 4   Employer exercises any of the following forms of control over the means, methods, and
 5   economic viability of the work:

 6      1. Exclusivity and Market Control: When the ﬁsherfolk or ﬁshworker is contractually
 7         or practically restricted from selling, disposing of, or trading their catch to any
 8         person or entity other than the designated Fish Trader or Vessel Owner. Any
 9         "Right of First Refusal" or "Exclusive Buying Agreement" imposed by the Trader
10         shall be prima facie evidence of an employment bond.
11      2. Operational Direction and Superior Supervision: When the Trader or Vessel
12         Owner exercises the power to dictate, directly or indirectly:

13                a) The schedule of departure and arrival;
14                b) The duration of the ﬁshing expedition;
15                c) The speciﬁc ﬁshing grounds or geographical limits to be exploited; and
16                d) The speciﬁc gear, technology, or methods to be utilized during the
17                   voyage.

18      3. Economic Dependency and Financial Subordination: When the ﬁsherfolk are
19         ﬁnancially dependent on the Trader or Vessel Owner for "Pre-departure
20         Advances" (Takay or Ubos), fuel, ice, provisions, or equipment, the cost of which
21         is unilaterally deducted from the gross value of the catch. The act of "ﬁnancing"
22         the voyage combined with the power to manage the partition of the catch
23         proceeds constitutes the "Payment of Wages" element of the four-fold test.
24      4. The Power of Selection and Dismissal: When the Trader or Vessel Owner retains
25         the authority to engage the crew (Selection) or to "ground" a ﬁsherfolk by refusing
26         them access to the vessel or the necessary capital for a voyage (Dismissal).

27   SECTION 5. Non-Waiver of Rights. – Any contract, memorandum of agreement, or
28   verbal "gentleman's agreement" declaring the ﬁsherfolk as an "independent contractor,"
29   "partner," or "joint-venture associate" for the purpose of avoiding the obligations under
30   this Act is hereby declared void ab initio for being contrary to public policy.
31   SECTION 6. Classification and Tenure of Fishworkers. – To ensure that the fluid nature
32   of maritime work does not result in the subversion of labor rights, the following
33   classifications and rules on tenure are hereby established:
34      1. Large-Scale or Commercial Vessel Workers (Above three (3) Gross Tons):
35                a) Status: All fishworkers engaged on vessels with a capacity exceeding
36                   three (3) gross tons are hereby recognized as Regular Employees from
37                   the very first day of engagement or the commencement of the voyage.
 1                b) Rationale: The capital-intensive and highly organized nature of
 2                   commercial fishing implies a permanent need for labor, thus negating
 3                   any "probationary" requirement.
 4      2. Small-Scale or Municipal Vessel Workers (Three (3) Gross Tons or Less):
 5                a) Status: Fishworkers on small-scale vessels shall be recognized as
 6                   Regular Employees upon reaching an Aggregate Service of six (6)
 7                   months with the same Fish Trader or Vessel Owner.
 8                b) Rule on Continuity: The six-month period need not be continuous. For
 9                   this Act, "Aggregate Service" shall mean the total cumulative duration
10                   of all voyages, tasks, or engagements rendered for the same Employer
11                   within a twenty-four (24) month period.
12                c) Presumptive Regularity: If the nature of the work is "necessary and
13                   desirable" to the usual business of the Trader or Owner (which is the
14                   catching and selling of fish), the worker is presumed a regular
15                   employee unless the employer can prove a specific "Project-Based" or
16                   "Seasonal" contract that complies with DOLE standards.
17      3. Protection Against "End-of-Contract" (Endo) Schemes:
18                a) Any deliberate interruption of service (e.g., "grounding" a worker for a
19                   week to break the continuity of the six-month period) shall be
20                   considered a prohibited act of Labor-Only Contracting.
21                b) The transition from a "Probationary" or "Casual" status to "Regular"
22                   status shall be automatic by operation of law upon reaching the
23                   aggregate threshold, without the need for a new contract.
24   SECTION 7. Security of Tenure. – Regular Fisherfolk and Fishworkers shall enjoy the
25   right to stay in their jobs. They shall not be terminated, replaced, or denied a spot on a
26   voyage except for Just Causes (e.g., gross negligence, theft of catch, or serious
27   misconduct) or Authorized Causes (e.g., decommissioning of the vessel), subject to the
28   "Two-Notice Rule" and a formal administrative hearing.

29   SECTION 8. Comprehensive Employment Beneﬁts and Statutory Floor. – All ﬁsherfolk
30   and ﬁshworkers recognized as regular employees under this Act shall be entitled to the
31   following beneﬁts, which shall be considered the "Statutory Floor." Any agreement
32   providing for beneﬁts less than those mandated herein shall be unenforceable:

33      1. Guaranteed Wage and the "Share-or-Wage" Rule:

34                a) The Floor: In no case shall the total compensation of a ﬁsherfolk or
35                   ﬁshworker be lower than the prevailing National Minimum Wage for
36                   the actual days worked.
37                b) The Share: If the agreed "share" from the catch exceeds the National
38                   Minimum Wage, the ﬁsherfolk shall receive the higher amount.
39                c) Catch Failure: In the event of a "zero catch" or "low yield," the Employer
40                   (Fish Trader/Vessel Owner) is still obligated to pay the worker the
 1                   equivalent of the minimum wage for the duration of the voyage,
 2                   recognizing that the worker’s time and labor were placed at the
 3                   employer's disposal.

 4      2. Mandatory Social Protection:

 5                a) Compulsory Coverage: Employers must register all ﬁsherfolk or
 6                   ﬁshworkers with the Social Security System (SSS), PhilHealth, and
 7                   Pag-IBIG Fund from the date of engagement.
 8                b) Premium Sharing: Premiums shall be shared in accordance with
 9                   existing laws. The Employer is strictly prohibited from deducting the
10                   "Employer’s Share" of the premiums from the ﬁsherfolk or ﬁshworker’s
11                   share of the catch.
12                c) Proof of Payment: Employers are required to provide ﬁshers with
13                   monthly copies of contribution remittals.

14      3. Maritime Hazard Pay:

15                a) Recognizing the high-risk nature of work at sea, including exposure to
16                   unpredictable weather, piracy, and physical injury, all ﬁsherfolk or
17                   ﬁshworker shall receive Hazard Pay equivalent to at least 20% of their
18                   basic pay or share.
19                b) This shall be paid on top of the basic wage and shall not be integrated
20                   into the minimum wage calculation.

21      4. 13th Month Pay and Bonuses:

22                a) All ﬁsherfolk or ﬁshworker are entitled to 13th Month Pay regardless of
23                   the nature of their compensation.
24                b) Calculation: The 13th Month Pay shall be 1/12 of the total basic
25                   earnings (including shares but excluding overtime and hazard pay)
26                   earned by the worker within a calendar year.

27      5. Service Incentive Leave (SIL) and Overtime:

28                a) SIL: Five (5) days of leave with pay for every year of service, which may
29                   be commuted to its cash equivalent if unused.
30                b) Night Shift Differential: A minimum of 10% of the basic wage for work
31                   performed between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM, common in nocturnal
32                   ﬁshing operations.

33   SECTION 9. Prohibition on Illegal Deductions. – The "Takay" or "Ubos" (advances)
34   system shall be strictly regulated. Employers are prohibited from deducting the
35   following from the worker's share:
 1         a) Vessel depreciation or maintenance costs;
 2         b) Business taxes or trader licensing fees;
 3         c) Interest rates on pre-departure advances exceeding the legal limit set by the
 4            Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).

 5   SECTION 10. Financial Inclusion and Mandatory Credit Access for Fisherfolk. – To
 6   break the cycle of debt bondage and usurious lending, the State hereby mandates the
 7   full integration of ﬁsherfolk and ﬁshworkers into the formal ﬁnancial system.

 8      1. Legal Validity of Proof of Employment and Income:

 9                a) Equivalent to Payslip: The Articles of Agreement (duly registered with
10                   DOLE) and the Consolidated Catch Share Ledger (a record of the
11                   worker’s earnings) shall be recognized as oﬃcial and suﬃcient
12                   evidence of "Regular Income" and "Stable Employment."
13                b) Mandatory Acceptance: No Government Financial Institution (GFI),
14                   private bank, or micro-ﬁnance institution shall deny a loan application
15                   solely on the basis that the applicant lacks a traditional corporate ITR
16                   (Income Tax Return) or a ﬁxed-salary payslip, provided the applicant
17                   presents the DOLE-certiﬁed Articles of Agreement.
18                c) DOLE Certiﬁcation: Upon request, the DOLE shall issue a "Certiﬁcate
19                   of Regular Employment for Fisherfolk" within three (3) working days,
20                   free of charge, to facilitate these transactions.

21      2. The "Kasama sa Laot" Special Credit Window:

22            a. Mandatory Allocation: The Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP) and the
23               Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) are hereby mandated to
24               allocate a speciﬁc portion of their loan portfolio to the "Kasama sa Laot"
25               Special Credit Window.
26            b. Terms and Conditions: Loans under this window shall feature:
27                    i. Non-Collateralized Options: For micro-loans intended for gear
28                       replacement or emergency medical funds;
29                   ii. Preferential Interest: Rates not exceeding the lowest prime rate
30                       offered to agricultural cooperatives;
31                  iii. Flexible Repayment: Payment schedules aligned with the "ﬁshing
32                       season" (e.g., grace periods during closed seasons or months of
33                       extreme weather/monsoons).

34      3. Priority in Government Subsidies and Social Safety Nets:

35            a. Automatic Inclusion: Registered ﬁsherfolk or ﬁshworker under this Act
36               shall be automatically enrolled in the Registry System for Basic Sectors in
37               Agriculture (RSBSA).
 1             b. Preferred Beneﬁciary Status: They shall be granted "Tier 1" priority in the
 2                distribution of:
 3                     i. Fuel Subsidies: Direct-to-consumer fuel cards during periods of oil
 4                        price hikes;
 5                    ii. Climate Disaster Relief: Immediate cash assistance and gear
 6                        replacement grants following typhoons or maritime disasters;
 7                   iii. Scholarship Programs: Priority for their dependents in TESDA or
 8                        DA-BFAR educational grants.

 9   SECTION 11. Anti-Discrimination in Financial Services. – Any ﬁnancial institution that
10   refuses to process a loan application from a ﬁsherfolk or ﬁshworker despite the
11   presentation of the documents required under Section 7.1 shall be reported to the
12   Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) for administrative sanction and may be subject to a
13   ﬁne of Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 50,000.00) per occurrence.
14   SECTION 12. Right to Self-Organization and the Formation of Labor-Managed
15   Fisherfolk Cooperatives. – The State recognizes the right of ﬁsherfolk and ﬁshworkers
16   to organize into cooperatives as a primary vehicle for economic independence,
17   collective bargaining, and ﬁnancial self-suﬃciency.

18      1. Mandate for Labor-Managed Cooperatives:

19                a) Organizational Autonomy: Fisherfolk and ﬁshworkers shall have the
20                   absolute right to form, join, or assist in the formation of Labor-
21                   Managed Cooperatives (LMCs). These LMCs shall be owned and
22                   controlled exclusively by the workers themselves.
23                b) Collective Marketing and Bargaining: These cooperatives are hereby
24                   empowered to act as the "Consignee of Record," allowing them to sell
25                   catch directly to commercial markets, exporters, or government
26                   agencies, effectively bypassing predatory middle-men or usurious
27                   traders.
28                c) Supply Chain Management: LMCs shall be authorized to engage in the
29                   bulk procurement and tax-free importation of fuel, ice, ﬁshing gear, and
30                   engine parts for the exclusive use of their members, ensuring that the
31                   cost of "pre-departure provisions" remains at wholesale prices.

32      2. Internal Micro-lending and Debt Redemption:

33                a) Credit Revolving Fund: LMCs are authorized to establish an internal
34                   credit system to provide members with low-interest emergency loans
35                   and "voyage ﬁnancing," intended speciﬁcally to replace the "Takay" or
36                   "Ubos" system provided by traders.
37                b) Debt Consolidation: The cooperative may act as a legal intermediary to
38                   negotiate the settlement or restructuring of existing predatory debts
 1                    owed by its members to Fish Traders, ensuring a clean transition into
 2                    the formal employment system.

 3      3. CDA "Express Lane" and Technical Support:

 4                 a) Fast-Track Registration: The Cooperative Development Authority
 5                    (CDA) shall establish a "Fisherfolk Express Lane" for the registration of
 6                    LMCs under this Act. The registration process shall be completed
 7                    within ﬁfteen (15) working days from the submission of requirements.
 8                 b) Waiver of Fees: All registration fees and initial ﬁling costs for
 9                    Fisherfolk Cooperatives under this Act are hereby waived for the ﬁrst
10                    three (3) years of the Act’s effectivity.
11                 c) Mandatory Training: The CDA, in coordination with DOLE and BFAR,
12                    shall provide free mandatory training on Financial Literacy, Cooperative
13                    Management, and Labor Rights to all founding members.

14   SECTION 13. Non-Interference and Anti-Union Busting. – It shall be considered an
15   Unfair Labor Practice for any Fish Trader or Vessel Owner to interfere with, restrain, or
16   coerce ﬁsherfolk in the exercise of their right to form or join a cooperative. Any trader
17   who "grounds" or dismisses a worker for joining a cooperative shall be liable for illegal
18   dismissal and subject to the penalties provided in Section 10 of this Act.

27                 b) Adjudication: Through the National Labor Relations Commission

30                    disputes arising from this Act.
31                 c) Registration: Shall maintain the oﬃcial "Master Registry of Fisherfolk
32                    Employees" based on the submitted Articles of Agreement.

33      2. Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR):

34                 a) Vessel Licensing Linkage: Shall make the issuance or renewal of

 1                b) Monitoring: Shall monitor catch records and exclusivity agreements to

 4                c) Logistics: Provide technical data on ﬁshing seasons to the LBP/DBP to

12      4. Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP) & Development Bank of the Philippines

14                a) Financial Integration: Shall establish a digital "Fisherfolk Payroll

17                b) Fund Management: Manage the "Alon Guarantee Fund," which shall

20      5. Philippine Coast Guard (PCG):

21                a) Safety-Labor Veriﬁcation: During "Pre-Departure Inspections," the PCG

25   SECTION 15. The "Batas Alon" One-Stop Shop. – Within one (1) year of the effectivity of
26   this Act, the IAOC shall establish "One-Stop Shops" in major ﬁshing ports (such as
27   General Santos, Navotas, Lucena, and Zamboanga) to provide simultaneous registration
28   for DOLE, SSS, PhilHealth, and CDA services.

29   SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) and the Debt-to-Wages
30   Transition Framework. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the
31   DOLE, in mandatory consultation with BFAR, CDA, the Land Bank of the Philippines, and
32   accredited National Fisherfolk Federations, shall promulgate the IRR. The IRR shall
33   include, but not be limited to, the following specific pillars:

34      1. The "Debt-to-Wages" Conversion Scheme:
 1                 a) Audit of Advances: All existing "advances" or utang claimed by
 2                    Traders/Owners against Fisherfolk must be liquidated and audited.
 3                    Only documented and verified professional advances (e.g., gear, fuel,
 4                    and food provisions) shall be recognized.
 5                 b) Interest Ceiling: Any interest rate on existing debt exceeding the legal
 6                    ceiling set by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) for non-
 7                    collateralized loans is hereby declared void. The principal shall be
 8                    recalculated using the legal interest rate.
 9                 c) Non-Predatory Repayment: The IRR shall mandate that repayments for
10                    old debts be made through salary deductions which shall not exceed
11                    twenty percent (20%) of the worker’s take-home pay (after mandatory
12                    SSS/PhilHealth deductions).
13                 d) Moratorium: A six-month moratorium on debt repayment shall be
14                    enforced during the first year of the Act to allow the Fisherfolk to
15                    stabilize their household income under the new wage system.

16      2. Standardized Maritime Employment Contract:

17                 a) The IRR shall provide a template for the Articles of Agreement which
18                    must be written in the local dialect understood by the Fisherfolk. It
19                    must explicitly state the "Statutory Floor" of wages and the prohibition
20                    of "commission-only" pay.

21      3. Transition Period and Compliance Amnesty:

22                 a) A one-year transition period is hereby granted to Fish Traders and
23                    Vessel Owners to formalize their workforce.
24                 b) During this period, Traders who voluntarily register their Fisherfolk as
25                    Regular Employees and settle SSS/PhilHealth back-payments shall be
26                    exempt from the administrative fines provided in Section 11, provided
27                    they adhere to the non-predatory repayment plans.

28      4. The "Alon" Monitoring System:

29                 a) The IRR shall establish a digital "Catch-to-Cash" monitoring system
30                    where every landing of fish is recorded alongside the distribution of
31                    shares, ensuring that the "Minimum Wage Floor" (as defined in Section
32                    6) is met regardless of the catch volume.

35   of the substantive rights granted under this Act. The provisions on the Employer-
36   Employee relationship (Section 4) and Security of Tenure (Section 5) shall be self-
37   executing upon the Act’s effectivity.
 1   SECTION 18. Administrative and Criminal Penalties. – Any person, partnership,
 2   association, or corporation found in violation of the provisions of this Act, particularly
 3   on the misclassification of employees, withholding of benefits, or interference with
 4   cooperative formation, shall be subject to the following penalties:

 5      1. Administrative Fines (Per Worker):

 6                 a) First Offense: A fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 50,000.00) per
 7                    affected fisherfolk or fishworker.
 8                 b) Second Offense: A fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php
 9                    100,000.00) per affected worker and a suspension of the Business
10                    Permit and/or Commercial Fishing Vessel License (CFVL) for a period
11                    of six (6) months.
12                 c) Third and Subsequent Offenses: A fine of Five Hundred Thousand
13                    Pesos (Php 500,000.00) per affected worker.

14      2. The "Blacklist" and Permanent Revocation:

15                 a) In cases of the third offense, or where the violation involves Labor-
16                    Only Contracting or Debt Bondage, the Department of Agriculture-
17                    BFAR shall, upon recommendation of DOLE, order the Permanent
18                    Cancellation of the Commercial Fishing Vessel License (CFVL), Fish
19                    Trader’s Permit, and all other permits to operate in Philippine waters.
20                 b) The violating entity and its principal officers shall be permanently
21                    blacklisted from applying for any new fishing-related licenses or
22                    government agricultural subsidies.

23      3. Criminal Liability for Corporate Officers:

24                 a) If the offender is a corporation, trust, firm, or association, the penalty
25                    shall be imposed upon the responsible officers, including the
26                    President, General Manager, or the Managing Partner, if the violation is
27                    committed with their knowledge or gross negligence.
28                 b) In cases involving the "grounding" of a fisherfolk as a form of
29                    retaliation (Union Busting or Cooperative Interference), the responsible
30                    officer shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of not less than one (1)
31                    year but not more than three (3) years, at the discretion of the court.

32      4. Automatic Restitution:

33                 a) The payment of fines to the government shall be separate and distinct
34                    from the mandatory restitution of unpaid wages, shares, and social
35                    security contributions to the affected fisherfolk.
