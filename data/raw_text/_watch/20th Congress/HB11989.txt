                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XV of the 1987 Constitution recognizes the Filipino family as the
foundation of the nation and directs the State to strengthen its solidarity. Article II,
Section 12 recognizes the natural and primary right and duty of parents in rearing
the youth. Families cannot fulfill this duty if the people holding them together are
struggling without help.

       Parents and guardians are usually the first to notice when a child is in
distress, and the ones the child relies on during recovery. Yet they are rarely asked
how they are coping. Mothers and fathers working long hours, solo parents raising
children alone, grandparents caring for the children of overseas workers, and
family members caring for a child with a disability or a mental health condition often
carry heavy emotional burdens. When those burdens are left unattended, the
whole household is affected.

      The demand is visible in the calls received by the National Center for Mental
Health crisis hotline, where family problems are among the most common reasons
people call.1

     The law already provides two platforms that reach parents in every
community. Republic Act No. 11908, the Parent Effectiveness Service Program
Act, requires parent effectiveness sessions to be held in every barangay. Republic
Act No. 11861, the Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act, requires a Solo Parents
Office in every province and city and a Solo Parents Division in every municipality.
Neither law, however, provides mental health services for the parents themselves,
and the Parent Effectiveness Service Program has been reported as not yet fully
operational in many localities.2 At the same time, Republic Act No. 12080 is now

       This bill does not create a new bureaucracy. It builds mental health support
into the platforms that already reach families. It:

      1. Establishes a National Parent and Guardian Mental Health Program, led
         jointly by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
         and the Department of Health (DOH);

      2. Adds a mandatory parental mental health and self-care module to the
         Parent Effectiveness Service Program, with screening and referral at
         every session cycle;

      3. Requires the Solo Parents Offices and Divisions to provide psychosocial
         support and referral, and entitles solo parents to free counseling;

      4. Provides free counseling sessions to parents, guardians and family
         caregivers through local government health and social welfare offices,
         linked to PhilHealth mental health benefits;

      5. Requires mental health screening of mothers and fathers during prenatal
         and well-child visits in public health facilities;

      6. Ensures that when a child is referred for mental health care, whether by
         a school care center, a hospital or a crisis line, the parent or guardian is
         offered guidance and support as well;

      7. Organizes parent support groups in barangays and respite care for
         family caregivers of persons with severe disabilities or mental health
         conditions; and

      8. Extends these services to the families of overseas Filipino workers,
         including the grandparents and relatives who raise their children.

       This measure complements House Resolution No. 1259, which I filed to
strengthen mental health and psychosocial programs for children and adolescents,
because supporting a child means supporting the adults who care for that child. As
Mayor of Marikina, I saw that barangay-based family programs work best when
they are close to home and part of everyday services. This bill applies that
approach nationwide.

 9            PROVIDING MENTAL HEALTH ASSISTANCE TO PARENTS,

14          Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
15   Congress assembled:

16          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Mental Health
17   Assistance for Parents and Guardians Act.”

18          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the Filipino family as
19   the foundation of the nation and the natural and primary right and duty of parents in
20   the rearing of the youth. Consistent with Article II, Sections 12 and 15, and Article XV
21   of the Constitution, and Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental
22   Health Act,” the State shall protect and promote the mental health of parents,
23   guardians, solo parents and family caregivers, and shall integrate mental health
24   support into the programs and services that reach families in every community.

25          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

26           (a)   Family caregiver refers to a family member or relative who, without
27                 compensation, provides regular care to a child, an older person, a
28                 person with disability, or a person with a chronic illness or mental health
29                 condition;

30           (b)   Guardian refers to a person who has legal custody of a child, or who
31                 exercises substitute parental authority or actual custody and care of a



                                                1
 1                 child, including grandparents and relatives caring for the children of
 2                 overseas Filipino workers;

 3           (c)   Parent refers to a biological or adoptive father or mother;

 4           (d)   Parent Effectiveness Service (PES) Program refers to the program
 5                 established under Republic Act No. 11908, otherwise known as the
 6                 “Parent Effectiveness Service Program Act”;

 7           (e)   Psychosocial support refers to services that help individuals and families
 8                 cope with stress and adversity, including psychological first aid,
 9                 counseling, peer support and case management; and

10           (f)   Solo parent refers to a person defined as such under Republic Act No.
11                 8972, as amended by Republic Act No. 11861, otherwise known as the
12                 “Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act.”

13         SEC. 4. National Parent and Guardian Mental Health Program. – The
14   Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and the Department of
15   Health (DOH) shall jointly establish and implement a National Parent and Guardian
16   Mental Health Program (Program), in coordination with the Department of Education
17   (DepEd), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the
18   Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration
19   (OWWA), the Early Childhood Care and Development Council, the National Council
20   on Disability Affairs, and local government units (LGUs). The DSWD shall be
21   responsible for psychosocial support and family services, and the DOH for clinical
22   standards, screening tools, treatment and referral. The Program shall include the
23   services provided under Sections 5 to 13 of this Act.

24         SEC. 5. Mental Health Module in the Parent Effectiveness Service
25   Program. – The PES Program shall include a mandatory module on parental mental
26   health and self-care, to be developed by the DSWD and the DOH, covering stress
27   management, recognition of signs of anxiety, depression and burnout in oneself and
28   in one’s child, positive coping, and how and where to seek help. At least once in every
29   session cycle, PES participants shall be offered voluntary screening using a validated
30   tool prescribed by the DOH, and those who need further help shall be referred to the



                                                2
 1   city or municipal health office or an accredited provider. PES facilitators shall be
 2   trained in psychological first aid and referral.

 3          SEC. 6. Psychosocial Support for Solo Parents. – The Solo Parents Office
 4   or Solo Parents Division established under Republic Act No. 11861 shall provide
 5   psychosocial support to registered solo parents, including information on mental health
 6   services, psychological first aid, facilitation of peer support groups and referral for
 7   counseling. Every registered solo parent shall be entitled to free counseling under
 8   Section 7 of this Act.

 9          SEC. 7. Free Counseling for Parents, Guardians and Family Caregivers. –
10   Every city and municipality, through its health office and social welfare and
11   development office, shall provide parents, guardians, solo parents and family
12   caregivers with not less than six (6) free counseling sessions per year with a licensed
13   mental health professional or a registered social worker trained in counseling, upon
14   request or referral. Sessions may be conducted in person or through telehealth. Where
15   further care is needed, the person shall be assisted in availing of benefits under the
16   National Health Insurance Program. Priority shall be given to indigent families, solo
17   parents, parents and guardians of children with disabilities or mental health conditions,
18   and families affected by disaster, violence or the death of a family member.

19          SEC. 8. Perinatal and Early Parenthood Screening. – Public health facilities
20   shall offer mental health screening, using DOH-prescribed tools, to mothers during
21   prenatal and postnatal visits, and to mothers and fathers during well-child and
22   immunization visits within the first two (2) years of a child’s life. Persons identified as
23   needing further assessment shall be referred for care. This Section shall be
24   implemented in coordination with existing maternal, newborn and child health
25   programs.

26          SEC. 9. Support When a Child Is Referred for Mental Health Care. –
27   Whenever a child is referred for mental health assessment or care by a school care
28   center established under Republic Act No. 12080, a health facility, a crisis hotline or a
29   social worker, the referring office or the receiving provider shall, subject to the best
30   interest of the child and the rules on confidentiality, offer the parent or guardian: (a)
31   information on the child’s condition and how to support the child at home; (b)
32   psychological first aid and referral for the parent’s or guardian’s own counseling; and


                                                  3
 1   (c) linkage to a parent support group. This Section shall not apply where the parent or
 2   guardian is the alleged source of harm to the child, in which case the rules on child
 3   protection shall govern.

 4          SEC. 10. Barangay Parent Support Groups. – Every barangay, in
 5   coordination with the city or municipal social welfare and development office and
 6   health office, shall organize at least one (1) parent and guardian support group
 7   facilitated by a trained facilitator. LGUs shall also organize or support specialized
 8   groups for parents and guardians of children with disabilities, children with mental
 9   health conditions, children in conflict with the law, children who are victims of abuse
10   or bullying, and families bereaved by suicide. Accredited civil society and faith-based
11   organizations may facilitate support groups in partnership with the LGU.

12          SEC. 11. Respite Care for Family Caregivers. – The DSWD, in coordination
13   with LGUs, shall develop community-based respite care services that provide
14   temporary relief to family caregivers of persons with severe disabilities or severe
15   mental health conditions, through day care arrangements, trained home-care
16   volunteers or short-term placement in accredited facilities. Family caregivers shall be
17   included in the psychosocial support and training programs of the National Council on
18   Disability Affairs and LGU persons with disability affairs offices.

19          SEC. 12. Families of Overseas Filipino Workers. – The DMW and OWWA,
20   in coordination with the DSWD and LGUs, shall extend the services under this Act to
21   the spouses, guardians and family caregivers of the children of overseas Filipino
22   workers, including through the family welfare programs of OWWA, and shall include
23   parental mental health in pre-departure and post-arrival orientation programs.

24          SEC. 13. Employed Parents and Caregivers. – The Department of Labor and
25   Employment and the Civil Service Commission shall ensure that the workplace mental
26   health policies and programs required under Republic Act No. 11036 address the
27   needs of employees who are parents, solo parents, guardians or family caregivers,
28   including information on the services under this Act and access to flexible work
29   arrangements under existing laws and regulations.

30          SEC. 14. Training of Frontline Workers. – The DSWD and the DOH shall
31   train PES facilitators, child development workers, barangay health workers, social



                                                 4
 1   workers and solo parent office personnel in psychological first aid, recognition of
 2   parental distress, and referral pathways. Training shall be provided free of charge and
 3   integrated into existing capacity-building programs.

 4          SEC. 15. Information Campaign. – The DSWD and the DOH, in coordination
 5   with the Philippine Information Agency and LGUs, shall conduct a continuing
 6   information campaign on parental mental health and the services available under this
 7   Act, using materials in Filipino and local languages and through digital, broadcast and
 8   community channels.

 9          SEC. 16. Confidentiality. – All information obtained in the course of providing
10   services under this Act shall be confidential and processed in accordance with
11   Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and
12   Republic Act No. 11036, without prejudice to mandatory reporting of child abuse under
13   existing laws. Information on a parent’s or guardian’s mental health shall not, by itself,
14   be used as a basis to deny any benefit or service under existing laws.

15          SEC. 17. Role of Local Government Units. – LGUs shall integrate the
16   Program into their Local Investment Plan for Health, local social welfare and
17   development plans, and the annual plans of their Solo Parents Offices or Divisions.
18   Barangays may use their allocation for the protection of children for parent support
19   groups and related activities under this Act, subject to existing rules.

20          SEC. 18. Monitoring and Reporting. – The DSWD and the DOH shall jointly
21   submit to Congress an annual report on the implementation of this Act, including the
22   number of parents, guardians and family caregivers screened, counseled and referred,
23   disaggregated by sex, age, region and city or municipality, and the number of
24   barangays with functioning parent support groups.

25          SEC. 19. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
26   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
27   DSWD and the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included
28   in their budgets in the annual General Appropriations Act. LGUs shall include in their
29   annual budgets the amounts necessary for the services under Sections 7 and 10 of
30   this Act. Services covered by the National Health Insurance Program shall be charged
31   against the funds of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation.
