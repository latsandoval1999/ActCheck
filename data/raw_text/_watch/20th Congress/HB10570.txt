                               AN ACT
       PROVIDING FOR THE ORGANIZATION OF COOPERATIVES FOR FRY
         GATHERERS, SMALL SCALE FISHERMEN AND FISHFARMERS


                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Recent developments have highlighted the continuing constraints of the
local fisheries sector in meeting the growing demand of an increasing population.
In 2024, fish and other seafood products remained among the key contributors
to food inflation, reflecting persistent supply-side challenges.1

       On the production side, the sector continues to rely heavily on millions of
small-scale fisherfolk dispersed across coastal communities. According to the
Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), municipal or small-scale
fisheries account for a significant share of total fisheries employment, yet these
fisherfolk remain among the poorest sectors in the country. Recent data show
that poverty incidence among fisherfolk remains among the highest across basic
sectors, estimated at around 30% or more in recent years.2

       Despite their vital role in ensuring food security, many small-scale
fishermen, fry gatherers, and fish farmers continue to operate under constrained
conditions. Limited access to modern fishing gear, reliance on non-motorized or
aging bancas, and inadequate post-harvest facilities—such as cold storage and
ice plants—restrict both the volume and value of their catch. Moreover, climate

       At the same time, while the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA)
reports a growing number of registered cooperatives nationwide, participation of
fisherfolk in well-organized, adequately supported cooperatives remains limited,
particularly in remote coastal areas. This gap reduces their ability to pool
resources, access financing, improve bargaining power, and establish stable
market linkages.

      Empowering these marginalized fisherfolk must therefore be a priority of
the national government. This bill seeks to promote and support the organization
and strengthening of cooperatives among fry gatherers, small-scale fishermen,
and fish farmers. Through cooperative structures, members can consolidate
resources, access credit and government support, and invest in essential assets
such as motorized bancas, improved fishing gear, and post-harvest facilities.

      By fostering stronger cooperatives, the State can help enhance
productivity, reduce post-harvest losses, stabilize incomes, and improve the
overall resilience of the fisheries sector. Ultimately, this measure aims to
contribute to food security, inclusive economic growth, and poverty reduction in
coastal communities.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
   PROVIDING FOR THE ORGANIZATION OF COOPERATIVES FOR FRY
     GATHERERS, SMALL SCALE FISHERMEN AND FISHFARMERS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Small Scale
Fishfarmers Cooperatives Act”.
SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy - It is the policy of the State to promote the
growth of cooperatives among fry gatherers, small-scale fishermen, and small
fish farmers to enable them to receive an equitable share in the benefits delivered
from the fishing industry. Such cooperatives shall receive the support and
assistance of the government.

SECTION 3. Definition – For purposes of the Act, the following definitions shall
apply.

   a) Fry gatherer- shall refer to an individual, usually self-employed, who is
      engaged in the catching, gathering or collecting of fish, utilizing for the
      purpose fishing boats of three gross tons or less or using gear without the
      use of such boats.

   b) Small scale fishermen – shall refer to an individual, either self-employed
      or employed by others, who is engaged in fishing, utilizing for the purpose
      fishing boats of three gross tons or less or using gear without the use of
      such boats.

   c) Small fish farmer – shall refer to an individual who owns not more than
      one of fishpond or leases not more than three hectares of fishponds or
      owns not more than one-half hectare of fish pens.
   d) Cooperative – shall mean an association of fry gatherers, small farmers
      organized and registered pursuant to Presidential Decree Numbered One
      Hundred Seventy-Five, as amended.

   e) Organization of Cooperative – fry gatherers, small-scale fishermen and
      small farmers shall be organized as cooperatives and be registered under
      Republic Act No. 6939 under the supervision of the Cooperative
      Development Authority.

SECTION 4. Cooperative Development Programs. The Bureau of Fisheries and
Aquatic Resources of the Department of Agriculture and the Cooperative
Development Authority under the Office of the President shall initiate and
develop programs to promote, encourage, and assist the organization of such
cooperatives.

SECTION 5. Privileges of Cooperatives – In addition to the privileges granted
to cooperatives under Presidential Decree Numbered One Hundred Seventy-Five,
as further amended by Republic Act Numbered Six Thousand Nine Hundred
Thirty-Nine, fishery cooperatives organized under this Act shall have the
following fishery privileges:

   a) Availment of the privilege of constructing and operating oyster culture
      beds; gathering fish fry in municipal waters; operating fish fry in
      municipal water operating fishing boats of three gross or less in municipal
      waters, or fishing in municipal waters with nets, traps or other fishing
      gear not prohibited by Republic Act No. 8500, as provided in Section
      Twenty-Nine of Presidential Decree Numbered Seven Hundred Four,
      subject to the conditions imposed thereof;

   b) Availment of training programs, measures and studies conducted by the
      Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources and other government
      agencies and instructions; and

   c) Availment of technical assistance provided by government agencies and
      private institutions.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations – The Cooperative
Development Authority shall, in coordination with the Bureau of Fisheries and
Resources, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations or parts thereof contrary to, or inconsistent with, this Act are hereby
modified or repealed accordingly.
SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions of this Act not otherwise affected
shall remain valid and subsisting.
SECTION 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following
its complete publication in any national newspaper of general circulation or in
the Official Gazette.
