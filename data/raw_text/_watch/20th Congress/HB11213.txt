                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


Education is the great equalizer of our nation. It is the promise that every Filipino child, regardless
of background, can rise to a life of dignity and opportunity. Yet for too long, this promise has been
weakened by a fragmented system where the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and the Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC) operate in silos, with overlapping mandates and misaligned standards. The result
has been confusion for learners, inefficiency for institutions, and lost opportunities for workers and
professionals who strive to compete in an increasingly globalized world.

This measure, the Philippine Professional Standards and Quality Assurance System Act of 2025,
seeks to transform that reality. By establishing the Board of Curriculum Standards and the Skills
and Standards Harmonization Councils, the Act envisions a unified system where education,
training, and professional regulation are seamlessly integrated. It is a vision of a country where every
student’s effort, every worker’s skill, and every professional’s qualification is recognized, valued,
and aligned with both national priorities and international benchmarks.

Through this Philippine Professional Standards and Quality Assurance System, CHED shall be
empowered to harmonize higher education programs with the needs of industry and the standards
of professional practice. TESDA shall continue to strengthen technical-vocational training, opening
pathways for lifelong learning and workforce mobility. Together, these institutions will no longer
work in isolation, but in unity, building bridges instead of barriers for Filipino learners and
professionals.

This proposed measure to strengthen the foundations upon which parents may entrust their
children’s future, workers may claim dignity in their labor, and professionals may uphold excellence
and integrity in the global arena. This proposed measure affirms that the State must not only provide
access to learning, but must also guarantee that such access leads to empowerment, productivity,
and progress for every Filipino.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Professional Standards and
 2   Quality Assurance System Act of 2025"
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to promote the seamless integration and
 4   harmonization of tertiary education, technical-vocational training, and professional regulation in
 5   order to develop a globally competitive, highly skilled, and qualified workforce responsive to the
 6   evolving demands of the national and global labor markets. Toward this end, the State shall ensure
 7   effective coordination and collaboration among the Commission on Higher Education, the Technical
 8   Education and Skills Development Authority, and the Professional Regulation Commission in the
 9   regular review and updating of curricula, qualifications, and standards.
10   The State shall likewise strengthen and institutionalize a unified qualifications framework
11   encompassing all levels and sectors of education and training, and ensure that licensure,
12   certification, and other regulatory requirements are aligned with competency-based learning
13   outcomes. Such harmonization shall promote the efficient use of public and institutional resources,
14   eliminate redundancies across education and training systems, and support lifelong learning, credit
15   transfer, and career mobility for all Filipino learners and professionals
16   SEC. 3. Definitions. - For purposes of this Act:
17          a. "CHED" - refers to the Commission on Higher Education, as identified in Republic Act
18          No. 7722;
19          b. "EWDG" - refers to the Education and Workforce Development Group which was
20          established by virtue of Administrative Order No. 36 Series of 2025;
21          c. "Enterprise-Based Education and Training Program" - shall refer to a Technical
22          Vocational Education and Training delivered by an enterprise, including programs that are
23          developed and recommended by recognized industry boards, which may be stand-alone or
24          linked with a Technical Vocational Institution;
25          d. "Micro-credentials" - shall refer to short, focused, and modular learning programs
26          formally recognized by educational and training institutions in the Philippines, which certify
27          the acquisition of specific competencies, skills, or knowledge aligned with industry or
28          academic standards;
29          e. "Mutual Recognition Agreements" - shall refer to formal arrangements jointly
30          developed by the relevant government agencies and/or other education stakeholders which
31          establish shared principles and procedures for the recognition of qualifications,
32          competencies, and assessment outcomes. These agreements shall be executed with the aim
33          of reducing redundancy and promoting the harmonization and mutual acceptance of
34          assessment and examinations across agencies, thereby facilitating learner mobility,
35          professional practice, and skills portability within and across sectors;
36          f. "Tables of Specifications" - shall refer to a structured, competency-based framework
37          used by the Professional Regulation Commission, and its Professional Regulatory Boards,
38          to guide the development, content validation, and alignment of licensure examinations with
39          the prescribed syllabi, learning outcomes, and professional standards.
40          g. "TESDA" - refers to the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, as
41          identified in Republic Act 7796;
42          h. Philippine Qualifications Framework" ("PQF") - is a national policy which describes
43          the levels of educational qualifications and sets the standards for qualification outcomes. It
44          is a quality assured national system for the development, recognition and award of
45          qualifications based on standards of knowledge, skills and values acquired in different ways
46          and methods by learners and workers in a certain country;
47          i. "PRC" - refers to the Professional Regulation Commission under Republic Act No. 8981;
48          j. "Professional Regulatory Boards" ("PRB") - shall refer to the boards under the PRC
49          which were created by their respective enabling laws and exercise administrative, quasi-
50          legislative, and quasi-judicial powers over their respective professions;
51          k. "Program" - means any course of study leading to a post-secondary certificate, diploma,
52          or degree;
53          l. "Competency Standard" - refers to a nationally recognized specification of expected
54          learning outcomes for each qualification level, serving as the benchmark for the contextual
55          application of knowledge and skills;
56          m. "Recognition of Prior Learning" ("RPL") - refers to the formal process by which an
57          individual's previously acquired knowledge, skills, competencies, and learning outcomes,
58          which may either be gained through formal, non-formal, or informal learning, are assessed
59          and validated against established standards or qualifications.
60          n. "Skills and Standards Harmonization Councils" ("SSHCs") - refers to the multi-
61          sectoral advisory and technical bodies established by virtue of this Act which are mandated
62          to support the alignment and coherence of technical-vocational and higher education
63          programs with industry needs, licensure requirements, and national and international
64          qualification frameworks.
65          o. "Technical Vocational Education and Training" ("TVET") - refers to the education
66          or training process which involves, in addition to general education, the study of technologies
67          and related sciences and acquisition of practical skills relating to occupations in various
68          sectors of economic and social life;
69          p. "Technical Vocational Institution" ("TVI") - refers to an institution, whether public or
70          private, offering TVET programs). This shall include TESDA Technical Institutions, Public
71          and Private Technical Vocational Institutes, and Higher Education Institutions, State
72          Universities and Colleges and Universities, Training Centers and enterprises offering TVET
73          programs;
74          q. "Training Regulation" ("TRs") - refers to a TESDA-promulgated document that serves
75          as basis for which competency-based curriculum and instructional materials and competency
76          assessment tools are developed TRs represent qualifications and define the competency
77          standards for a national qualifications and how such qualification can be gained, assessed
78          and be given recognition.
79   SEC. 4. Board of Program Standards. A Board of Program Standards ("Board") is hereby created
80   and shall be composed of the following members:
81          a) CHED Chairperson, PRC Chairperson and TESDA Director General as Co-Chairpersons
82          of the Board; and
83          b) The Secretary of the Department of Education ("DepEd"), Secretary of Department of
84          Labor and Employment ("DOLE"), and Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning
85          and Development as members of the Board.
86   SEC. 5. Duties and Responsibilities of the Board of Program Standards. The Board shall have the
87   following duties and responsibilities:
 88          a) Develop and promulgate a unified set of policies, qualifications profiles, and program
 89          standards for all higher-education and technical-vocational fields, in consultation with the
 90          relevant SSHCs which may include, but is not limited to, appropriate requirements for
 91          faculty/trainers, facilities and other matters relevant to its duties under this Act, cognizant of

 93          b) Review and harmonize curricula of CHED and TESDA programs to eliminate
 94          redundancies and align with licensure requirements when necessary and/or applicable;
 95          c) Coordinate with SSHCs and other relevant agencies and stakeholders in conducting a
 96          regular review of the tertiary education programs, regulations, and assessments at least every
 97          five (5) years;
 98          d) Jointly undertake review and conduct international benchmarking of qualifications both
 99          in relation to developments in tertiary education and in the concerned professions;
100          e) Issue joint regulations on ladderized pathways and credit recognition, including
101          continuing professional development, between TVET and higher education degree
102          programs;
103          f) Develop a system or mechanism that shall ensure the conduct of a regular review, update,
104          and/or enhancement of curricula and/or licensure syllabi for licensed professions, taking into
105          consideration developments and needs of respective industries and sectors;
106          g) Advise and propose to the EWDG, Congress and other relevant bodies and government
107          agencies the necessary reforms to enhance the country's professional accreditation systems
108          and standards;
109          h) Initiate a transition from input-based to outcomes-based recognition in coordination with
110          concerned agencies, as well as external quality assurance agencies;
111          i) Regularly commission independent surveys and studies to assess
112          performance of graduates, in partnership with the Philippine Statistics Authority ("PSA"),
113          the Philippine Institute for Development Studies ("PIDS"), and other partner universities and
114          institutions to inform their work of program, policy, and assessment review; and
115          j) Undertake any and all similar duties, functions or responsibilities in relation to its mandate
116          under this Act, or its Implementing Rules and Regulations.
117   In the performance of its duties and responsibilities, the Board's decisions/resolutions may be
118   discussed and made during the regular meetings of the EWDG, consistent with Administrative Order
119   No. 36 Series of 2025, or any similar body agreed upon by its members.
120   No action may be taken by the Board unless a quorum is first established. A quorum shall be present
121   when at least a majority of the members are present provided that at least one (1) of the Chairpersons
122   is present. In the event that any member of the Board is unable to attend, he/she may designate a
123   duly authorized representative to attend and act on his/her behalf, and such representative shall be
124   counted to constitute a quorum and may vote as if the member is personally present.

174   SEC. 8. Skills and Standards Harmonization Councils. Skills and Standards Harmonization
175   Councils ("SSHC") are hereby created which will be tasked in reviewing, developing and/or
176   updating, and harmonizing the curricula of disciplines and/or sector skills and aligning the same
177   with the relevant licensing requirements.
178   An SSHC shall be composed of the following members:
179          a) Three (3) members from Higher Education Institutions ("HEIs"), considering
180          representation of public and private institutions;
181          b) Two (2) members from private Technical Vocation Institutions ("TVIs"), preferably
182          representing those with experience and track record in offering a range of qualifications (i.e.,
183          PQF Levels 1 to 5);
184          c) One (1) member from TESDA Technology Institutions ("TTIs");
185          d) Three (3) industry experts relative to the discipline and/or sector skill governed by the
186          SSHC;
187          e) At least (3) members of the PRB relative to the profession governed by the SSHC, when
188          applicable.
189   The Board, in coordination with the Secretariat, shall develop and issue the guidelines detailing the
190   qualifications and the manner of selection of each member of the SSHCs.
191   If the Board finds that there is a lack of experts for a specific discipline and/or sector skill governed
192   by an SSHC, the Board shall have the discretion to determine and adjust the maximum number of
193   members, the membership distribution, and the qualifications for membership. In line with this, if a
194   law is enacted which establishes a new PRB governing a particular discipline and/or sector skill, the
195   Board shall have the discretion to either integrate the members of the new PRB to existing SSHCs
196   or initiate the formation of a new SSHC to govern the discipline and/or sector skill covered by the
197   new PRB.
198   SEC. 9. Duties and Responsibilities of SSHCs. The SSHCs shall have the following duties and
199   responsibilities:
200          a) Facilitate regular consultations with representatives from industry, academe, training
201          institutions, professional boards, and relevant stakeholders to gather insights on sector-
202          specific competencies, trends, and evolving labor market demands;
203          b) Submit recommendations to the Board on qualification profiles, faculty/trainer
204          requirements, facilities, and other instructional methodologies to guide the development and
205          review of unified program standards across higher education and technical-vocational
206          programs;
207          c) Analyze and recommend revisions to eliminate duplication and misalignment in CHED
208          and TESDA curricula to ensure coherence between TVET and degree programs in relation
209          to licensure requirements;
210          d) Propose sector/discipline specific models for ladderized education pathways and credit
211          recognition, ensuring portability of skills and learning outcomes across education levels and
212          institutions;
213          e) Provide technical and sectoral input into review and updating of licensure examination
214          syllabi to reflect current industry standards, emerging technologies, and professional
215          practices;
216          f) Collaborate with institutions and agencies in collecting, validating, and interpreting data
217          on the employability of graduates, skills gaps, licensure exam performance, and institutional
218          effectiveness within their respective sectors;
219          g) Assist in undertaking or supporting international benchmarking of qualifications, program
220          outcomes, and standards within their discipline or sector to align national systems with
221          global best practices;
222          h) Advocate for and assist in the transition to outcomes-based education and recognition
223          frameworks within their disciplines by developing tools, standards, and best practices for
224          measuring learning outcomes and professional competencies;
225          i) Identify capacity gaps in curriculum design, standards development, and quality assurance
226          processes within their sector and support capacity-building initiatives for educators,
227          regulators, and institutions;
228          j) Advise the Board and relevant agencies on needed reforms in quality assurance processes,
229          curriculum design, or skills recognition in light of technological, economic, or global trends
230          affecting their sector;
231          k) Participate in or initiate research studies and evaluations relevant to program and licensure
232          outcomes, in collaboration with the PSA, PIDS, academic institutions, and industry partners.
233          1) Act as technical working groups or resource panels to support the Board in formulating
234          joint regulations, reviewing curricula, developing national standards, and undertaking policy
235          reforms;
236          m) Monitor the implementation of reforms in their respective sectors and regularly submit
237          feedback reports to the Board, highlighting successes, challenges, and recommendations;
238          n) Ensure that all sector-specific inputs and recommendations are aligned with the
239          descriptors, levels, and standards of the Philippine Qualifications Framework and other
240          international referencing initiative; and
241          o) Perform such other functions that may be necessary to assist the Board to perform its
242          duties and responsibilities under this Act or its implementing rules and regulations.
243   SEC. 10. Duties and Responsibilities of CHED, TESDA and PRC. - For purposes of this Act, the
244   agencies shall have the following duties and responsibilities:
245          A. CHED
246                  In the event that the Board cannot decide on matters relating to curricula and
247                  programs in higher education, CHED shall have final authority to:
248                  a) Design, develop, and/or enhance programs and sample curricula through its
249                  issuance of policies, standards, and guidelines ("PSGs");
250                  b) Prescribe faculty academic and licensure qualifications provided that in courses
251                  requiring licensure and/or certification, such qualifications shall be subject to the
252                  minimum standards set by relevant laws, rules and/or regulations;
253                  c) Conduct institutional approval and program audits, including the issuance of
254                  Certificates of Program Compliance ("COPC") for Public Higher Education
255                  Institutions ("HEl") and Government Recognition for Private HEIs;
256                  d) Harmonize and streamline the recommendations of the PRC (or the PRB) and
257                  TESDA into coherent and actionable education policies for higher education. In cases
258                  wherein the members of the Board cannot decide on the recommendations for
259                  curricula and programs, CHED shall mediate and resolve such inconsistencies, and
260                  enforce the unified policy direction in accordance with national education and
261                  workforce development goals. In these instances, PRC (through its PRBs) and
262                  TESDA must consult and align with CHED on its final decision on matters involving
263                  curricula development in higher education.
264          B. TESDA
265   For technical-vocational education programs, including associate degrees and diploma programs,
266   TESDA shall retain jurisdiction over TRs, recognition and certification of TVIs, as well as technical
267   program alignment and ladderization. In this regard, TESDA shall have, among others, the following
268   duties and responsibilities:
269                  a) For TVET programs aligned with PRC-regulated examinations, TESDA and PRC
270                  shall coordinate and collaborate to harmonize TESDA'S National Certification
271                  standards with the PRC'S licensure examinations as well as the standards for the
272                  practice of profession and sub-profession;
273                  b) Harmonize TRs with applicable CHED PSGs and PRB licensure requirements,
274                  consistent with the intents of Republic Act No. 10647;
275                  c) Ensure matched exit points from CHED diploma courses with TESDA
276                  certifications and promote stackable credentials under the PQF;
277                  d) Consult with CHED, PRC, the relevant SSHCs and other education stakeholders
278                  in the Philippines for the preparation and execution of Mutual Recognition
279                  Agreements geared towards the reduction and optimization of assessments and/or
280                  exams across agencies;
281                  e) Collaborate with PRC, the Board, and/or the relevant SSHCs in designing and
282                  piloting Enterprise-Based Education Training programs and industry-based training
283                  models to address skills shortages;
284                  f) Promote the use of Micro-credentials and Recognition of Prior Learning ("RPL")
285                  as alternative pathways to professional qualification;
286                  g) Integrate TESDA qualification and certification data into the shared data system
287                  with PRC to ensure seamless pathways for lifelong learning, professional
288                  progression, and regulatory impact assessment; and
289                  h) Consult the Board prior to issuing, modifying, or phasing out TRs, involving
290                  ladderized pathways.
291          C. PRC and Professional Regulatory Boards
292   The PRC shall have final authority on all licensure examination and professional inputs. To this end,
293   the PRC shall have, among others, the following duties and responsibilities:
294                  a) All concomitant duties and functions under Republic Act No. 8981;
295                  b) Examine and prescribe licensure examination syllabi and Tables Of
296                  Specifications, competency clusters, and professional standards, which shall
297                  integrate the required knowledge, skills and/or
298                  competencies and shall be in accordance with their enabling laws, taking into account
299                  industry and labor market needs/requirements;
300                  c) Through its respective PRBs, provide advice on curriculum content, faculty
301                  competencies, and facilities standards, for CHED'S consideration in the development
302                  and implementation of curricula and programs;
303                  d) Through its respective PRBs, inspect and monitor HEIs, independently or jointly
304                  with CHED and/or the relevant SSHCs. The PRBs' findings that relate to the matters
305                  enumerated in Section 6(A) hereof shall be advisory in nature and shall, at all times,
306                  be immediately endorsed to the Board for appropriate action; and
307                  e) In close coordination with CHED and TESDA, empower PRBs to assess, update
308                  and reconcile statutory requirements for the practice and admission of professions or
309                  licensure under their respective functions into timely, practical and progressive
310                  standards for the improvement of the respective professions under their collective
311                  authority.
312   SEC. 11. Teacher Professionalization Exemption. Pursuant to the provisions of Republic Act No.
313   11713 or the Excellence in Teacher Education Act, the Board shall not exercise authority over the
314   regulation of the teacher education curriculum and the teaching profession.
315   SEC. 12. Institutional Quality Monitoring and Transparency. CHED, TESDA and PRC shall share
316   data under a unified reporting system. Following each exam, the agencies shall jointly and
317   proactively publish the performance of institutions to inform prospective applicants, graduates, their
318   parents, and the general public, of the standing of TVIs and HEIs, to help inform decision making
319   students. To this end, the Board may recommend corrective measures, such as program suspension
320   or discontinuation, for institutions with persistently low performance, as well as initiate program
321   supports to improve quality of institutions and performance of graduates.
322   SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The Board shall, within ninety (90) days after the
323   effectivity of this Act, promulgate rules necessary for its implementation, including guidelines for
324   joint curriculum development, data sharing protocols, and PQF level assignments. The Board shall
325   have rule-making power in coordination with the CHED Commission En Banc, the TESDA Board,
326   and the PRC Commission En Banc.
327   SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. All laws or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
328   or modified, including provisions of Republic Act No. 7722, Republic Act No. 7796, Republic Act
329   No. 8981, and other applicable professional practice laws governed by PRBs as may be necessary
330   to implement this Act's coordination mechanisms.
331   SEC. 15. Separability Clause. If any part of this Act is held unconstitutional or invalid, the
332   remaining parts of provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
333   SEC. 16. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
334   Gazette or a national newspaper.
