                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 13, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution recognizes the
vital role of the youth in nation-building and directs the State to promote and
protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being.
Complementing this constitutional mandate, Section 3, Article XIV requires the
State to make quality education accessible and responsive to national
development by equipping citizens with the competencies necessary for
productive participation in the economy. In fulfillment of these constitutional
principles, this measure seeks to establish the Youth Employment and Enterprise
Fund, a dedicated national financing mechanism that provides grant-based
financial assistance and integrated enterprise development support to qualified
young entrepreneurs. The proposed measure recognizes entrepreneurship as a
strategic pathway toward employment generation, innovation, and inclusive
economic growth by enabling young Filipinos to become job creators rather than
job seekers.

       Recent studies indicate that young entrepreneurs continue to face
significant barriers in establishing and sustaining businesses, particularly in
accessing financial resources and business support services. In the Philippines,
the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) found that gaps in
financial inclusion, limited access to formal financial services, and inadequate
financial capability continue to constrain entrepreneurial activities, particularly
among aspiring entrepreneurs and underserved sectors.1 Similarly, the
Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) reported that
young entrepreneurs are disproportionately affected by financing constraints,
limited business networks, and insufficient entrepreneurial support systems,
thereby restricting business creation and long-term enterprise growth.2

       Studies demonstrate that access to financing alone is insufficient to ensure
enterprise sustainability. The OECD concluded that youth entrepreneurship
programs are significantly more effective when financial assistance is
complemented by mentoring, entrepreneurship training, business incubation,
market linkage, and continuous business development support.3 These findings
are reinforced by the International Labour Organization, which emphasized that
integrated entrepreneurship support programs contribute to improved
employment outcomes, enterprise survival, and long-term economic participation
among young people.4 Collectively, these studies underscore the importance of
adopting a comprehensive and coordinated approach to youth entrepreneurship
rather than relying solely on credit or financing interventions.

       Recognizing these empirical findings and existing policy gaps, this
proposed legislation institutionalizes a coordinated national framework for youth
entrepreneurship through the establishment of the Youth Employment and
Enterprise Fund. Integrating grant-based financial assistance with mentoring,
enterprise incubation, business development services, digital entrepreneurship
support, market linkage, and performance monitoring under a unified
implementation mechanism, the measure seeks to strengthen enterprise
sustainability, expand employment opportunities, stimulate innovation, and
enhance the competitiveness of youth-led enterprises. Ultimately, the proposed
measure invests in the entrepreneurial potential of Filipino youth as a catalyst for
inclusive, resilient, and sustainable national economic development. In view of
the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:
      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Youth
Employment and Enterprise Fund Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to recognize and strengthen the vital role of the youth in nation-building by
promoting their productive economic participation, entrepreneurship, self-
employment, innovation, and sustainable livelihood opportunities.

      The State recognizes the contribution of youth-led enterprises to
employment generation, innovation, local economic development, and inclusive
and sustainable economic growth, as well as the distinct barriers that young
Filipinos may face in accessing capital, business networks, technical assistance,
markets, and enterprise development opportunities.
      Towards this end, the State shall establish a dedicated national financing
and enterprise development mechanism to provide qualified young entrepreneurs
with financial assistance and integrated support for the establishment,
sustainability, and growth of viable youth-led enterprises.

      The implementation of this Act shall be complementary to and harmonized
with existing national and local government programs on youth development,
employment, entrepreneurship, skills development, and micro, small, and
medium enterprise development.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Youth shall refer to persons fifteen (15) to thirty (30) years of age,
      consistent with Republic Act No. 8044, otherwise known as the “Youth in
      Nation-Building Act,” as amended, and other applicable laws;

      (b) Qualified Young Entrepreneur shall refer to a Filipino citizen who is at
      least eighteen (18) but not more than thirty (30) years of age and who is
      engaged in, or is in the process of establishing, a micro, small, or medium
      enterprise, and who meets the eligibility requirements provided under this
      Act and its implementing rules and regulations;

      (c) Youth-Led Enterprise shall refer to an enterprise owned or controlled
      by one or more Qualified Young Entrepreneurs, subject to the ownership
      or control requirements prescribed under this Act and its implementing
      rules and regulations; and

      (d) Enterprise Development Support shall refer to mentoring, enterprise
      incubation, business development and technical assistance, market linkage,
      digitalization, innovation assistance, business coaching, and other services


                                                                                2
      intended to support the establishment, sustainability, and growth of youth-
      led enterprises.

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the Youth Employment and Enterprise Fund.
– There is hereby established the Youth Employment and Enterprise Fund,
hereinafter referred to as the Fund, which shall provide financial assistance and
enterprise development support to Qualified Young Entrepreneurs in accordance
with the provisions of this Act.

      The Fund shall primarily provide grant-based financing to support youth-
led enterprises, particularly during the early stages of business development.

      The Fund shall complement and, where appropriate, leverage existing
government financing and enterprise development programs promoting youth
entrepreneurship and employment, and shall be implemented in coordination with
concerned government agencies to minimize duplication and maximize the
efficient use of available public resources.

      SEC. 5. Integrated Enterprise Development Support System. – The Fund
shall operate under an integrated enterprise development support system to ensure
the long-term sustainability and competitiveness of supported enterprises. Such
support shall include:

      (a) Direct grant-based financing;

      (b) Mentoring and enterprise incubation;

      (c) Business development and technical assistance services;

      (d) Monitoring and evaluation of funded enterprises; and




                                                                                 3
      (e) Long-term sustainability support, including business coaching, market
      linkages, digitalization, innovation assistance, and enterprise growth
      support.

      SEC. 6. Qualified Beneficiaries. – The financial assistance and enterprise
development programs under this Act shall be made available to Qualified Young
Entrepreneurs as defined in Section 3 of this Act, subject to eligibility, evaluation,
prioritization, availability of appropriations, and other requirements prescribed
under this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

      Qualification under this Act shall not be construed as an automatic
entitlement to financial assistance.

      Priority shall be given to:

      (a) Disadvantaged youth, including out-of-school youth, rural youth,
      Indigenous Peoples, persons with disabilities, and young women
      entrepreneurs; and

      (b) Youth-led start-ups, social enterprises, and innovation-driven
      enterprises, particularly those in the early stages of business development.

      SEC. 7. Inter-Agency Coordination and Implementation. – The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall serve as the lead implementing
agency of this Act and shall chair an Inter-Agency Coordinating Council
composed of representatives from the Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Department of Education
(DepEd), and such other government agencies as may be necessary.

      The Council shall ensure the effective coordination, implementation,
monitoring, and harmonization of programs and services under this Act to
                                                                                    4
provide unified access to financial assistance and enterprise development support
for Qualified Young Entrepreneurs.

      SEC. 8. Performance Monitoring and Evaluation Framework. – The
Inter-Agency Coordinating Council shall establish a performance monitoring and
evaluation framework to assess the implementation of this Act and the
effectiveness of government investments in youth entrepreneurship and
employment generation.

      The monitoring and evaluation framework shall include mechanisms for
the proper utilization, accountability, and reporting of financial assistance,
consistent with existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and government
financial management laws, rules, and regulations.

      The Council shall submit an annual report to Congress on the
implementation of this Act, including the utilization of the Fund, the number of
beneficiaries assisted, enterprises established or expanded, jobs generated, and
such other performance indicators as may be necessary.

      SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Trade and Industry and other participating agencies.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      The release and utilization of appropriations shall be subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, auditing, and government financial management laws,
rules, and regulations.

      SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Trade and Industry, in

consultation with the Department of Labor and Employment, the Technical

      SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
