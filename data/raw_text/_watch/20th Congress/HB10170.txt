                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution affirms that labor is a primary social economic force. Regarded as the cornerstone
of national development, the spirit of labor shall afford workers with full protection of their rights and
interests. However, while such comprehensive protection remains to be the mandate of the state, such
should still be taken in cognizance with the rights of the employers.

The aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic eventually led to initiatives by the government to
institutionalize the transition of the government to e-governance which is applicable to all government
offices and agencies including local government units and government-owned or controlled corporations.
This is also simultaneous with the intent of Republic Act No. 11967 or the Internet Transactions Act
(ITA) of 2023 which further regulated e-commerce by protecting both online consumers and merchants,
ensuring secure internet transactions, and promoting the growth of digital commerce. Hence, it becomes
of primordial importance to enunciate a middle ground in terms of digital innovation, both on the part of
the private entities whose majority are employers and the employees whose rights are enforced by the
government.

At present, the Department of Labor and Employment quarterly monitors its labor force through the
Philippine Statistics Authority’s (PSA’s) Labor Force Survey. This is in addition to Department of Labor and
Employment’s (DOLE’s) Labor Advisory No. 03 Series of 25 2024 which required all private establishments to
submit an annual report on the verified itemized listing of their labor component, specifying the names of
their rank-and-file employees, including learners, apprentices, and workers with disabilities who were hired
under the terms prescribed in the employment contracts, and their corresponding salaries and wages
through registration in the Department of Labor and Employment's Online Compliance Portal. DOLE
has also been incorporating digital reforms, one of which is the Management Information System (MIS)
which includes real-time reporting of inspection results which can generate various reports on
compliance, violations, and other labor-related data of employers.

It is in this regard that this measure seeks to establish a centralized information system which would cater
to all labor-related information, primarily to the regional and field offices and attached agencies of
DOLE. Granted, however, that the proposed measure will not be in contrary with laws regarding
protection of personal privacy, national security, records retention, access for persons with disabilities, and
other relevant laws.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this Bill is earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE NATIONAL REGISTRY OF WORKERS,
ESTABLISHING THE EMPLOYMENT MANAGEMENT INFORMATION
SYSTEM FOR THE PURPOSE​

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:​


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Employment Management
Information System Act of 2025. "

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Section 18, Article II of the 1987 Constitution affirms
that labor is a primary social economic force. Thus, labor is regarded as the cornerstone of
national development, and workers shall be afforded with full protection of their rights and
interests. In addition, while comprehensive protection shall be the mandate of the state, such
should still be balanced with the rights of the employers.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to attain the following objectives:

    a. Provide an up-to-date database of employees, including information on union and
       worker’s association across the country;
    b. Foster information sharing between jobseekers and potential employers;
    c. Allow the employers to manage a secure and independent platform that creates linkages
       in the employee’s job history;
    d. Provide data for employees on employment opportunities that are readily available in a
       particular sector or industry and for purposes of real-time employment rate monitoring;
    e. Provide a secure, transparent, and independent modern web application that may be
       accessed anytime by the public through a multi-platform support or cross-platform
       support devices which can be linked to existing systems in the Department of Labor and
       Employment (DOLE) in order to monitor compliance of employers to labor laws and
       regulations;
    f. Streamline training and seminars in the management of the operations; and
    g. Ensure that all forms of assistance given to registered unions and other qualified
       individuals and organizations are monitored.

       SEC. 4. Employment Management Information System. – All employers are hereby
mandated to establish an Employment Management Information System (EMIS), hereinafter
referred to as Information System. The EMIS shall serve as a dynamic online computer database

where employers shall exercise due diligence in filing basic employment information of the
employees, without prejudice to the employees’ right to privacy. The EMIS shall merge and
synchronize employment data from different sources into a cohesive database designed to
facilitate linkages from employers. The EMIS shall also be integrated with the existing systems of
the DOLE.

      The Information System shall have the following features and contents:

   a.​ Nation-wide identification of employees as well as qualified union and workers
       associations;
   b.​ Employment demographics, including age group, gender, ethnic group, income, seminars
       and trainings attended, and assistance received;
   c.​ Selected reports presented in both tabular and graphical form;
   d.​ Registry of employees' employment information without prejudice to the employees’
       personal information and sensitive personal information;
   e.​ Search engine which allows prospective employers to navigate the jobseekers; and
   f.​ Customizable dashboard which is accessible only to registered employers whose due
       diligence are required in filing all of their employee’s profile, including supporting
       documents and proof of employee’s consent in case of termination, end of contract or
       term of employment, or resignation;
   g.​ Personalized dashboard which is accessible only to registered employees who are granted
       the option to file resignation and leave, among others through the said medium; and
   h.​ Repository of real-time reporting of inspection results which can generate various
       reports on compliance, violations, and other labor-related data of employers.

        SEC. 5. Registration. – All employers are required to administer and facilitate the
registration of all employees upon the commencement of the employment. Failure to do so
would constitute negligence and appropriate sanctions.

       SEC. 6. Functions and Responsibilities of the Department of Labor and Employment.
– The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall exercise the following functions
and responsibilities:

   a.​ The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall gather, encode, and
       consolidate all pertinent data on the profile and demographics of all employees per
       region;
   b.​ Establish employer - jobseeker linkages and generate the following information from
       employers:

          i.​ Name and addresses of establishments
         ii.​ Demographics as gender and age of workers
       iii.​ Workers work status
        iv.​ Whether the establishment is organized or unorganized
         v.​ Classification of establishments (Small, medium and large)
      vi.​ Different industries
      vii.​ Compliance and violations made on labor standards; and

   c.​ Serve as the web administrator of nationwide employment to establish a geographic
       integration system map.

     SEC. 7. Functions and Responsibilities of the Regional Offices of the Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE). – The regional office of the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) shall have the following functions and responsibilities:

    a.​ Integrate the data provided by the provinces and highly urbanized cities in the existing
        information systems of the DOLE such as the Management Information System (MIS)
        which is a serves as a central repository for information collected from labor inspectors
        and other sources;
    b.​ Validate the data from the DOLE’s attached agencies and field offices which are acting as
        the system administrator at the regional, provincial, and city level and provide real-time
        report to the central office of the DOLE; and
    c.​ Endorse all forms of assistance given to registered unions and other qualified individuals
        and organizations generated by the EMIS.

     SEC. 8. Functions and Responsibilities of the Central Office of the Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE). – The central office of the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) shall have the following functions and responsibilities:

    a.​ Allocate funding for the integration of the Employment Management Information
        System (EMIS) with existing information systems;
    b.​ Coordinate with the Department of Information and Communications Technology
        (DICT) to upgrade, expand, and integrate its information systems;
    c.​ Ensure that the implementation, appropriate verification processes and information
        safeguards are in place pursuant to existing laws and regulations, in coordination with the
        DOLE’s attached agencies, DICT, Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Bureau of
        Internal Revenue (BIR), National Privacy Commission (NPC), Department of the
        Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the Department of Science and
        Technology (DOST);
    d.​ Approve the prioritized interventions by the regional and field offices and allocate
        corresponding funding; and
    e.​ Collaborate with other agencies for the creation of employment linkages in the domestic
        and international markets;

       SEC. 9. Duties of the Secretary. – The Secretary of the DOLE shall monitor and ensure
the compliance of all regional and field offices as provided for under this Act and shall, as soon
as practicable, establish a centralized information system that shall integrate and link all existing
employment information systems in the country.

       SEC. 10. Reports. – The Secretary of the DOLE shall prepare and submit to the
President and both Houses of Congress an annual report on the implementation of the program
which shall contain the list of accomplishments and recommendations for improvements.

        SEC. 11. Penalties. – Any person, corporation, trust, firm, partnership, association, or
entity violating any provision of this Act shall be punished by a fine of not less than One
hundred thousand pesos (P50,000.00) nor more than Two hundred Fifty thousand pesos
(P500,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than one year nor more than two years, or both at the
discretion of the Court: Provided, That if the violation is committed by a corporation, trust or
firm, association or any other entity, the penalty of imprisonment shall be imposed upon the
entity's responsible officers including the president, vice president, chief executive officer, general
manager, managing director or partner.

         The employer concerned shall be ordered to pay an amount equivalent to double the
damages to the employee caused by their failure to exercise due diligence in the information
system: Provided, That the payment of indemnity shall not absolve the employer from the criminal
liability imposable under this Act..

       SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Employment shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The Secretary of Employment shall include in the
Department's programs the establishment of the integrated and centralized Employment
Management Information System (EMIS), the funding of which shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force
and effect.

       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
