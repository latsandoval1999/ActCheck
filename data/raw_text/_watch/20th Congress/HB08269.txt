                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The State prioritizes the welfare of vulnerable sectors, including persons
with disabilities, as enshrined in Article XIII, Section 11 of the 1987 Philippine
Constitution. It reaffirms its commitment to protect and promote the rights and
dignity of all individuals, particularly those who face discrimination and social
barriers.

      People with Down syndrome continue to encounter stigma, limited
opportunities, and misconceptions about their abilities. Raising public
awareness is essential to promote understanding, eliminate discrimination, and
foster a more inclusive society. Through proper education and advocacy,
communities can better appreciate the potential, talents, and contributions of
individuals with Down syndrome.

       While Proclamation No. 157 already declares February as National Down
Syndrome Consciousness Month, there remains a need to institutionalize a
specific day dedicated to strengthening advocacy efforts and reinforcing
inclusivity nationwide.

     This bill seeks to declare February 6 of every year as the “National Day of
Awareness for Persons with Down Syndrome” to enhance public understanding,
promote equal opportunities, and encourage respect for diversity in schools,
workplaces, and communities.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Day of
Awareness for Persons with Down Syndrome Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to prioritize
the welfare of the underprivileged, the sick, the elderly, persons with disabilities,
women, and children, as enshrined in Article XIII, Section 11 of the Philippine
Constitution. Additionally, the Republic of the Philippines reaffirms its
commitment to the principles of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of
The Child, which recognizes the right of children with disabilities to special care,
education, and training to help them achieve dignified and self-reliant lives while
fostering their social integration.

The State acknowledges the importance of raising awareness about Down
syndrome to eliminate societal barriers, promote inclusivity, and foster a
supportive environment for individuals with Down syndrome. To this end, the
State shall take appropriate measures to
13 uphold their rights to equal opportunities and protection from discrimination,
harassment, and violence.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – February 6 of every year is hereby declared
as the “National Day of Awareness for Persons with Down Syndrome”. This
annual observance aims to:

      1. Enhance public understanding of Down syndrome, including its causes,
      challenges, and the potential of individuals with the condition;

      2. Promote the rights, dignity, and well-being of persons with Down
      syndrome; and

      3. Encourage inclusivity and respect for diversity within communities,
      schools, workplaces, and other sectors of society.

SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
Health shall, in collaboration with the Department of Education, the Department
of Labor and Employment, the Department of Social Welfare and Development,
and other relevant government agencies and non-governmental organizations,
lead the implementation of programs and activities in observance of the “National
Day of Awareness for Persons with Down Syndrome”.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, ordinances, other issuances or parts
thereof, which are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
deemed repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
