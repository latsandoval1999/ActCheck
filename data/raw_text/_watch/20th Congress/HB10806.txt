                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


        For many Filipinos today, the first place they encounter election news is no longer television,
radio or newspapers. It is Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, X, messaging applications and other online
platforms.

       The problem is that not everything that looks real online is real.

        This is no longer simply a matter of “fake news.” Artificial intelligence can now create
convincing videos, photographs and audio recordings of people saying or doing things that never
happened. Fake accounts and automated bots can make a political message appear more popular than
it actually is. Political advertisements can be precisely targeted to particular groups using personal
information. Paid influencers can promote political messages without making it clear that they are
being compensated.

        Recent evidence shows that this is a serious concern among Filipinos. The Reuters Institute's
Digital News Report 2025 reported that 67% of Filipinos expressed concern about online
misinformation and disinformation, the highest level recorded in the Philippine data at that time. The
report also noted that social media remain a major source of news for Filipinos amid an increasingly
fragmented and politically polarized information environment.

         The problem is not limited to fabricated stories. In 2025, an investigation reported extensive
use of fake social media accounts in Philippine political discourse, with researchers estimating that
as much as 45% of election-related online discussion in a particular period was driven by inauthentic
accounts. Such operations can create the illusion that a candidate or political position enjoys
widespread public support when, in reality, much of the apparent engagement may have been
artificially generated.

       At the same time, any law addressing online misinformation must be careful not to silence
legitimate political speech.
       Filipinos have the right to criticize candidates, express political opinions, share commentary,
make jokes, create satire and parody, and engage in political debate—even when others disagree with
them. An honest mistake should not automatically become an election offense simply because it was
posted online.

        This measure therefore draws an important line between ordinary political expression and
deliberate digital manipulation.

       It distinguishes misinformation from disinformation and recognizes that satire, parody,
opinion and legitimate editing are different from deliberately deceptive content. It specifically
prohibits the use of deepfakes to impersonate candidates or election officials when intended to
deceive voters.

        The bill likewise addresses the use of bots, fake accounts and coordinated inauthentic behavior
designed to artificially influence electoral discourse. It regulates political microtargeting, particularly
the use of sensitive personal information, while allowing legitimate digital campaigning.

        The measure also promotes transparency. Filipinos should be able to know who paid for a
political advertisement and when a social media influencer is being paid or otherwise compensated
to promote political content. Digital platforms are likewise required to maintain records of political
advertising so that campaign spending and digital influence operations can be properly monitored.

       Digital campaign spending should not be invisible simply because it happens online.
Payments for social media advertisements, digital influencers, online campaign services, content
production and other digital activities should be properly reported as campaign expenditures.

       The bill also makes candidates and campaign organizations accountable when they knowingly
authorize, finance or coordinate unlawful digital operations, while making clear that a candidate
should not automatically be liable for every post, comment, share or reaction made independently by
an ordinary supporter.

        Finally, the measure gives the Commission on Elections the authority to issue implementing
rules and regulations, with consultation among technology platforms, media organizations, civil
society, political parties, digital experts and other election stakeholders.

       The objective of this measure is simple: technology should help Filipinos make informed
choices, not create an artificial picture of what Filipinos believe.

        The Internet has become an important part of Philippine democracy. This bill does not seek
to take political discussion away from Filipinos. Instead, it seeks to make digital campaigning more
transparent, accountable and fair—so that when Filipinos cast their votes, their choices are influenced
by genuine political discourse and not by hidden digital manipulation.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE FAIR USE OF SOCIAL MEDIA, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
AND INTERNET TECHNOLOGY IN ELECTIONS, REGULATING DIGITAL ELECTION
          PROPAGANDA AND PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

        SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fair Use of Social Media,
Artificial Intelligence and Internet Technology in Elections Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure that the use of social
media, artificial intelligence and internet technology in elections promotes informed political
participation and does not undermine the conduct of free, orderly, honest, peaceful and credible
elections.

The State recognizes the importance of digital platforms as venues for political expression, campaign
communication, voter education and public discourse. At the same time, the State shall prevent the
deliberate use of digital technologies to deceptively manipulate electoral discourse, conceal the source
of political propaganda, artificially influence public perception, unlawfully exploit personal
information, or otherwise interfere with the electoral process.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit legitimate political opinion, commentary, satire,
parody, journalism or lawful editing of digital content.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Artificial Intelligence or AI refers to a machine-based system capable of generating or modifying
text, images, audio, video or other digital content;

(b) AI-generated content refers to content substantially created through the use of an artificial
intelligence system;

(c) AI-manipulated content refers to authentic content that has been materially altered through artificial
intelligence;

(d) Bot refers to an automated software program capable of interacting with a digital platform without
direct human operation;
(e) Coordinated inauthentic behavior refers to the coordinated use of multiple accounts, persons,
automated systems or other mechanisms to conceal the true identity, source, affiliation or level of
support for political content and thereby artificially influence electoral discourse;

(f) Deepfake refers to synthetic or manipulated audio, video, image or other digital content generated
or materially altered through artificial intelligence or similar technology to realistically depict a person
as having said or done something that the person did not actually say or do;

(g) Disinformation refers to false or materially misleading information that is knowingly created,
fabricated, altered or disseminated with the intent to deceive and influence electoral choice;

(h) Misinformation refers to false or inaccurate information disseminated without the intent to deceive;

(i) Microtargeting refers to the use of personal data, inferred characteristics, behavioral information or
other individualized information to direct political advertising or election propaganda to a narrowly
defined individual or group;

(j) Political advertisement refers to paid or sponsored communication disseminated through social
media, websites, online platforms or other internet-based technology for the purpose of promoting or
opposing a candidate, political party or party-list organization;

(k) Political influencer refers to a person who, whether for monetary compensation, consideration or
other material benefit, creates, publishes, endorses or distributes political content through a social
media platform or other digital medium;

(l) Political microtargeting refers to the use of microtargeting for the dissemination of political
advertisements or election propaganda;

(m) Sensitive personal information shall have the same meaning as provided under Republic Act No.
10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”;

(n) Satire refers to the use of humor, irony, exaggeration or ridicule to comment upon a person, event,
policy or political issue;

(o) Opinion refers to a statement expressing a person's belief, judgment, interpretation or view
concerning a political candidate, party, issue or matter of public concern;

(p) Parody refers to an imitation or transformation of a person, work, statement, event or political
communication for purposes of humor, criticism, commentary or artistic expression; and

(q) Legitimate editing refers to ordinary editing, cropping, enhancement, translation, captioning,
subtitling or other alteration of digital content that does not falsely represent a person as having said
or done something that did not occur.

      SEC. 4. Distinction Between Misinformation, Disinformation and Protected Expression.
— For purposes of this Act, the following distinctions shall apply:

(a) Misinformation, by itself, shall not constitute a violation of this Act when there is no intent to
deceive or materially manipulate electoral discourse;

(b) Disinformation shall refer to the knowing creation or dissemination of false or materially
misleading information with the intent to deceive and influence electoral choice, subject to the specific
prohibited acts under this Act;
(c) Satire and parody shall not be considered disinformation solely because they contain fictional,
exaggerated or altered representations, provided that they are not presented as authentic statements or
events for the purpose of deceiving voters;

(d) Opinion and commentary shall not be considered disinformation merely because they are disputed,
inaccurate, unfavorable or critical of a candidate, political party or public official;

(e) Legitimate editing, including cropping, enhancement, translation, captioning, subtitling and similar
alterations, shall not constitute prohibited manipulation unless it materially changes the substance of
the content in a manner intended to deceive voters; and

(f) The determination of a violation shall consider the nature of the content, the circumstances of its
creation or dissemination, the representation made to the public, and the intent of the person
responsible.

       SEC. 5. Prohibition on Deceptive Deepfakes. — No person shall knowingly create, publish,
disseminate, sponsor or cause the dissemination of a deepfake during the election period that:

(a) falsely depicts a candidate as saying or doing something that the candidate did not say or do;

(b) falsely depicts a candidate's withdrawal, endorsement, admission, confession or other material act
concerning the election;

(c) falsely depicts a COMELEC official, election officer or other person acting in an official election
capacity as making a statement or issuing an instruction that such person did not make or issue; or

(d) otherwise impersonates a candidate or election official through synthetic media, when done with
the intent to deceive voters or materially influence electoral choice.

The use of artificial intelligence for satire, parody, commentary, legitimate campaign production or
other lawful purposes shall not constitute a violation of this Section in the absence of the intent
contemplated herein.

       SEC. 6. Disclosure of AI-Generated Political Content. — Any candidate, political party,
campaign organization, political advertiser or political influencer who disseminates election
propaganda that is substantially generated or materially altered through artificial intelligence shall
disclose such use in a clear and reasonably conspicuous manner.

The Commission on Elections (COMELEC) shall prescribe the appropriate form and manner of
disclosure, taking into consideration the nature and format of the digital content.

The absence of an AI disclosure shall not, by itself, constitute proof that the content is a deepfake or
disinformation.

      SEC. 7. Prohibition on Bots, Fake Accounts and Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior. —
No person, candidate, political party, campaign organization or person acting on their behalf shall
knowingly:

(a) create or operate a fake account for the purpose of concealing the true source or identity of political
communication;

(b) use bots or automated accounts to impersonate genuine users for the purpose of artificially
influencing electoral discourse;
(c) purchase or employ fake followers, artificial engagement, fabricated reactions or similar deceptive
mechanisms to create a false appearance of public support for or opposition to a candidate or political
position;

(d) organize or participate in coordinated inauthentic behavior intended to artificially amplify, suppress
or manipulate political content; or

(e) knowingly finance, authorize or direct another person or entity to undertake any of the acts
prohibited under this Section.

Nothing herein shall prohibit automated tools used for legitimate purposes, including content
scheduling, accessibility, translation, moderation, customer service or campaign administration,
provided that such use does not involve deceptive impersonation or artificial manipulation of electoral
discourse.

      SEC. 8. Political Microtargeting. — Political advertisements may be targeted to geographic,
demographic or other lawful audience categories, subject to existing election and data privacy laws.

Political microtargeting shall not be conducted using:

(a) sensitive personal information as defined under Republic Act No. 10173, except when expressly
permitted by law;

(b) personal information obtained through unlawful or fraudulent means;

(c) personal information obtained without the required lawful basis;

(d) personal information relating to minors for purposes of political advertising; or

(e) information specifically processed for the purpose of exploiting a person's sensitive personal
circumstances or vulnerabilities to influence electoral choice.

Political advertisers shall disclose, in the manner prescribed by COMELEC, the general categories of
data or audience characteristics used to target political advertisements.

The disclosure shall not require the publication of the personal information of individual voters.

       SEC. 9. Transparency in Political Advertisements. — All paid political advertisements
disseminated through social media, websites, online platforms or other internet-based services shall
contain a clear and reasonably conspicuous disclosure identifying:

(a) the candidate, political party, party-list organization or other political entity promoted or opposed;

(b) the person or entity that paid for or authorized the advertisement; and

(c) when applicable, the fact that the advertisement contains AI-generated or materially AI-
manipulated content.

No person shall knowingly conceal the actual sponsor or source of a paid political advertisement
through the use of a fictitious identity or deceptive intermediary.

       SEC. 10. Transparency for Political Influencers. — A political influencer who receives
money, compensation, consideration or other material benefit in exchange for creating, publishing,
endorsing or distributing political content shall clearly disclose such relationship in the content.
The disclosure shall be readily visible or audible to the audience and shall not be concealed in a manner
reasonably calculated to prevent the audience from recognizing the commercial or political nature of
the communication.

Payments or other consideration received by political influencers for election-related content shall be
properly documented and reported as campaign expenditures in accordance with this Act and existing
election laws.

        SEC. 11. Records of Political Advertising by Platforms. — Social media platforms, online
advertising services and other internet platforms that provide paid political advertising services in the
Philippines shall maintain records of political advertisements placed through their services during the
election period.

Such records shall include, to the extent applicable:

(a) the content of the advertisement;

(b) the identity of the person or entity that paid for or authorized the advertisement;

(c) the amount paid;

(d) the period during which the advertisement was disseminated;

(e) the candidate, political party or political organization promoted or opposed;

(f) the general geographic or demographic audience targeted; and

(g) such other information as may be reasonably required by COMELEC for purposes of enforcing
election laws.

The records shall be retained for such period as may be prescribed by COMELEC, taking into
consideration data privacy, cybersecurity, legitimate business interests and other applicable laws.

COMELEC may require the submission or inspection of such records through lawful procedures.

       SEC. 12. Digital Election Advertising Repository. — COMELEC shall establish or designate
a publicly accessible digital repository containing information on political advertisements submitted
or reported pursuant to this Act.

The repository may include the advertisement, sponsor, amount spent, period of dissemination,
candidate or political organization concerned, and general audience category targeted.

The repository shall not contain unnecessary personal information of individual voters or users.

        SEC. 13. Digital Election Expenditures. — All expenditures incurred for digital election
propaganda shall be considered election expenditures and shall be reported in accordance with the
Omnibus Election Code, Republic Act No. 9006, otherwise known as the “Fair Election Act,” and
other applicable laws and regulations.

Digital election expenditures shall include, but shall not be limited to:

(a) social media and online advertising;

(b) search-engine advertising;
(c) payments to political influencers and digital content creators;

(d) digital campaign consultants;

(e) website development and maintenance;

(f) livestreaming and e-rally production;

(g) digital content production;

(h) artificial intelligence services used for campaign purposes;

(i) account management and digital campaign services; and

(j) other substantially similar digital services incurred for election campaign purposes.

Candidates, political parties and campaign organizations shall maintain complete and accurate records
of digital election expenditures and shall include the same in their applicable campaign-finance reports.

       SEC. 14. Accountability of Candidates and Campaign Organizations for Coordinated
Digital Operations. — A candidate, political party, coalition or campaign organization shall be
accountable for a prohibited digital operation undertaken on its behalf when it is established that the
candidate, political party, coalition or campaign organization:

(a) directly authorized or directed the operation;

(b) knowingly financed the operation;

(c) knowingly coordinated with the persons or entities undertaking the operation; or

(d) knowingly caused, assisted or materially participated in the operation.

A candidate, political party, coalition or campaign organization shall not be held liable solely because
an ordinary supporter, volunteer or private individual independently posts, shares, comments upon or
reacts to political content.

Neither shall a candidate or campaign organization be automatically liable for an unlawful act merely
because the person who committed the act publicly expressed support for such candidate.

       SEC. 15. Contractors and Digital Campaign Service Providers. — A person, advertising
agency, digital consultant, influencer, content creator, technology service provider or other contractor
engaged by a candidate, political party or campaign organization shall be subject to this Act when such
person knowingly participates in a prohibited digital election operation.

Contracts for digital campaign services shall identify, when applicable, the nature of the service and
the amount of compensation paid.

Payments for services covered by this Section shall be reported as campaign expenditures in
accordance with existing election laws.

        SEC. 16. No Automatic Liability for Platforms. — A social media platform or internet
service provider shall not be criminally liable solely because a user has posted, shared or disseminated
content through its service.
Nothing in this Act shall relieve a platform or service provider from complying with lawful
requirements concerning the maintenance and production of political advertising records under this
Act or from liability for its own unlawful acts.

      SEC. 17. Reporting of Digital Campaign Expenditures. —The statements of contributions
and expenditures required under existing election laws shall separately identify digital election
expenditures.

The report shall, where applicable, identify:

(a) the platform or service provider;

(b) the nature of the digital service;

(c) the amount paid;

(d) the date or period of the expenditure; and

(e) the candidate, political party or campaign organization on whose behalf the expenditure was
incurred.

COMELEC shall prescribe standardized reporting forms for digital campaign expenditures.

       SEC. 18. Enforcement by COMELEC. — COMELEC shall have authority to monitor
compliance with this Act and investigate alleged violations thereof in accordance with law.

In carrying out its functions, COMELEC may require candidates, political parties, campaign
organizations and covered digital platforms to submit records or information reasonably necessary to
determine compliance with this Act, subject to applicable constitutional and statutory protections.

        SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, COMELEC shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective
implementation.

Before the promulgation of such rules and regulations, COMELEC shall conduct consultations with
relevant stakeholders, including:

(a) social media and technology platforms;

(b) political parties and candidates;

(c) media organizations and journalists;

(d) civil society organizations;

(e) digital rights and technology experts;

(f) data privacy experts;

(g) election-law practitioners and experts; and

(h) other election stakeholders as COMELEC may deem appropriate.
The implementing rules shall, insofar as practicable, take into account technological developments and
shall avoid imposing requirements that unnecessarily restrict legitimate political expression or lawful
use of digital technology.

        SEC. 20. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the validity of the remaining provisions shall not be affected.

        SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or parts
thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified
accordingly.

        SEC. 22. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
