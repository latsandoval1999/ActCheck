                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The proposed bill addresses a critical gap in the Philippine healthcare
system by treating mental health care not as an expensive luxury, but as a
fundamental human right. While existing legislation recognizes the
importance of mental well-being, the prohibitive costs of psychiatric
evaluation, psychological services, neurological services, and long-term
treatment prevent millions of Filipinos from seeking essential care.

       By explicitly mandating the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth) to develop and implement a comprehensive benefit package
within one year of its effectivity, this bill removes the financial barriers that
have historically marginalized individuals with mental health conditions.
This measure requires coverage for all mental health disorders, extending
specific protections to minors, and incorporating emergency interventions,
continuing care, and evidence-based services alongside the World Health
Organization’s Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP).

      Furthermore, this bill strengthens the legal and financial frameworks
necessary to sustain nationwide mental healthcare. It aligns national policy
with international human rights standards and with Republic Act No.
11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,” while explicitly
recognizing individuals with mental health conditions under the protections
of the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons (RA 7277) to curb systemic stigma
and discrimination.

      Rather than creating a new budgetary burden, the Act utilizes
PhilHealth’s existing revenue structure, funding the package through a
combination of direct contributor premiums and government subsidies for
indirect contributors. PhilHealth shall undertake the necessary actuarial
and financial assessment to ensure that the benefit package is sustainable
and appropriately integrated into its existing benefit structure.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                          “AN ACT
      EXPANDING PHILHEALTH COVERAGE TO                     INCLUDE
      BENEFIT PACKAGE FOR ALL MENTAL                        HEALTH
      DISORDERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. -        This Act shall be known as the
“Comprehensive Mental Health Benefit Act”.

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State affirms the basic right of
all Filipinos to mental health as well as the fundamental rights of people who
require health services.

      The State commits itself to promoting the well-being of people by
ensuring that mental health is valued, promoted and protected; mental
health conditions are treated and prevented; timely, affordable, high quality,
and culturally appropriate mental health care is made available to the
public; mental health services are free from coercion and accountable to the
service users; and persons affected by mental health conditions are able to
exercise the full range of human rights, and participate fully in society and
at work free from stigmatization and discrimination.

      The State shall comply strictly with its obligations under the United
Nations Declaration of Human Rights, the Convention on the Rights of
Persons with Disabilities, and all other relevant international and regional
human rights conventions and declarations.

      The applicability of Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, otherwise
known as the “Magna Carta for Disabled Persons,” to persons with mental
health conditions, as defined herein, is expressly recognized.

      The State shall likewise ensure that the implementation of this Act is
consistent with Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental
Health Act” and other applicable laws protecting the rights and welfare of
persons with mental health conditions.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following
terms are defined as follows:

      a) “Mental health” refers to a state of well-being in which the
         individual realizes one’s own abilities and potentials, copes
         adequately with the normal stresses of life, displays resilience in
         the face of extreme life events, works productively and fruitfully,
         and is able to make a positive contribution to the community;

      b) “Mental health condition” refers to a neurologic or psychiatric
         condition characterized by the existence of a recognizable,
         clinically-significant disturbance in an individual’s cognition,
         emotional regulation, or behavior that reflects a genetic or
         acquired dysfunction in the neurological, psychosocial, or
         developmental process underlying mental functioning. The
         determination of neurologic and psychiatric conditions shall be
         based on scientifically-accepted medical nomenclature and best
         available scientific and medical evidence;

      c) “Mental disorders” refer to conditions that affect a person’s
         thinking, feelings, mood, and behavior. These may be occasional
         or chronic;

      d) “Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP)” refers to the
         World Health Organization (WHO) program aimed at scaling up
         services for mental, neurological, and substance use disorders
         for countries especially with low- and middle-income.

      Section 4. Mental Health Benefit Package. - Within one (1) year from
the effectivity of this Act, the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth),
in consultation with the Department of Health (DOH), and key stakeholders such as
professional societies, patient organizations, and health facilities, shall develop and
implement a comprehensive mental health benefit package for Filipinos with mental
health conditions or mental health disorders.

      This benefit package shall cover all mental health disorders and shall
include, as appropriate, outpatient and inpatient care, psychiatric and
psychological consultations, psychotherapy and other evidence-based
mental health interventions, emergency services, neurological services,
diagnostic and assessment services, prescribed medicines, follow-up care,
continuing treatment, rehabilitation and the Mental Health Gap Action
Programme (mhGAP).

      A minor suffering from mental health disorders shall likewise be
covered by the mental health benefit package, with age-appropriate and
evidence-based assessment, treatment, and continuing care.

     The implementation of the benefit package shall be consistent with
Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,” and

      PhilHealth shall ensure continuity of care for beneficiaries requiring
sustained mental health treatment, subject to evidence-based clinical
standards and applicable PhilHealth policies.

      Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Secretary of
the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth and in consultation with key
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to
implement the provisions of this Act.

      Section 6. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to implement this
Act shall be charged against the total revenue of PhilHealth, composed of premium
contributions from direct contributors and premium subsidies of the government
for the indirect contributors.

        PhilHealth shall undertake the necessary actuarial and financial assessment
to ensure the sustainability of the mental health benefit package and shall provide
for its appropriate integration into its existing benefit structure.

      Section 7. Separability Clause. - If any portion or provision of this
Act is subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions
hereof which are not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      Section 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, ordinances,
administrative orders, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are
inconsistent with this Act are repealed or amended accordingly.

       Section 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of
national circulation.
