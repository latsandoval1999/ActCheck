                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The 1987 Constitution commands the State to value the dignity of every
human person and guarantee full respect for human rights; to protect the
physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of the youth; and to
defend the right of children to special protection from all forms of neglect, abuse,
cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions prejudicial to their development.
      Despite these mandates, the Philippines has no national system for
keeping track of persons convicted of sexual offenses once they have served their
sentences. A person convicted of raping or exploiting a child may be released,
move to a new community, and seek work in a school, a child development
center, or a youth program without anyone being the wiser. Proposals to
establish a sex offender registry have been filed in Congress, yet none has been
enacted.
     This bill establishes a National Sex Offender Registry under the
Department of Justice. Convicted sex offenders must register before release,
update their information, verify it in person; and basic information on them is
made available to the public through a website. These measures help make our
communities safer and more secure.
      A registry that is accurate, proportionate, and lawful is a registry that will
withstand judicial scrutiny and earn the trust of the communities it is meant to
protect.
      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is urgently sought.

                               AN ACT
         ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY,
       PROVIDING SAFEGUARDS FOR SURVIVORS, CHILDREN, AND
        REGISTRANTS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR


       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Sex Offender
Registry of the Philippines Act”.

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II of the
Constitution, the State values the dignity of every human person, guarantees full
respect for human rights, and shall defend the right of children to special
protection from all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and other
conditions prejudicial to their development. Towards this end, it is hereby
declared the policy of the state to protection persons, especially women and
children, from sexual abuse and exploitation by keeping communities and law
enforcement agencies informed of the presence of convicted sex offenders.

       The State shall uphold measures for public safety proportionate to the risk
posed, and consistent with due process, the right to privacy, and the
rehabilitation and reintegration of persons who have served their sentences.
Further, the State upholds the right of survivors of sexual violence to privacy
and confidentiality, and ensure that no measure under this Act shall cause their
re-victimization.

      SECTION 3. Definitions of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:
      (a) National Sex Offender Registry or NSOR refers to the national
          database established under this Act, which shall contain the names
          and other pertinent details of sex offenders who reside, work, study, or
          sojourn in the Philippines;
      (b) Local Registration Office refers to the office in each city or
          municipality that are designated offices where offenders may register,
          update, and verify their information;
      (c) Public Registry Website refers to the publicly accessible online
          platform maintained under Section 8 of this Act; and
      (d) Sex Offender refers to any person convicted by final judgement of any
          of the following offenses, whether consummated, frustrated, or
          attempted:
             a. Rape, including rape by sexual assault, as defined by the Revised
                 Penal Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 8353 or the “Anti-
                 Rape Law of 1997,” and Republic Act No. 11648;
             b. Crime against chastity, except adultery and concubinage under
                 the Revised Penal Code;
             c. Child prostitution and other sexual abuse under Section 5 of
                 Republic Act No. 7610, or the “Special Protection of Children
                 Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act,” as
                 amended;
             d. Online harassment or exploitation of children as defined by
                 Republic Act No. 11930 0r the “Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or
                 Exploitation of Children (OSAEC) and Anti-Child Sexual Abuse
                 or Exploitation Materials (CSAEM) Act;”
             e. Trafficking in persons for purposes of prostitution, pornography,
                 sexual exploitation, or sex tourism;
             f. Sexual violence in relation to Republic Act No. 9626 or the “Anti-
                 Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004;” and
             g. Offenses under the laws of a foreign country that are
                 substantially equivalent to any of the foregoing, as determined by
                 the Department of Justice (DOJ): Provided, That the conviction
                 was rendered in proceedings that observed the basic
                 requirements of due process.

       SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Sex Offender Registry. – There
is hereby established a National Sex Offender Registry, which shall be created
and maintained by the DOJ. The NSOR shall allow the immediate sharing of
information among law enforcement agencies in the Philippines and, subject to
existing treaties and agreements and to Section 5(e) of this Act, with foreign
jurisdictions.

       The NSOR shall contain the following information on each registrant, in
addition to such other information as may be deemed necessary for proper
registration and identification:
      Full name and aliases;
         (a) Date of birth, sex, citizenship, and physical description;
         (b) Permanent and present residential addresses;
         (c) Name and address of employer and school, if any;
         (d) Fingerprints and DNA profile;
         (e) Recent photograph;
         (f) The sex offense of conviction, the convicting court, and the dates of
             conviction and of release;
         (g) Complete criminal history; and
         (h) Tier under Section 7 of this Act.

       The NSOR shall not contain the name, address, or any other information
that identifies the survivor.
      Every registrant shall be notified in writing of his or her inclusion in the
NSOR, tier, duties under this Act, and right to seek correction, reclassification,
or removal through the grievance and petition mechanism under Section 9 of
this Act.

       SECTION 5. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to sex offenders convicted
by final judgment after its effectivity, and to sex offenders who, upon its
effectivity, are serving sentence or are on parole, probation, or other conditional
release: This Act shall likewise apply to persons convicted of sex offenses by
foreign courts who reside, work, or study in the Philippines, or who stay in the
country.

       No person shall be included in the NSOR for an offense committed when
he or she was a child. The records of children in conflict with the law shall remain
confidential pursuant to Republic Act No. 9344, or the “Juvenile Justice and
Welfare Act of 2006”, as amended. Any record included in violation of this
paragraph shall be immediately expunged.

       Where the public disclosure of a registrant’s information would directly or
indirectly reveal the identity of the survivor, such as when the registrant is a
parent, relative, guardian, or household member of the survivor, the registrant
shall be registered, but his or her information shall be withheld from the Public
Registry Website and shall be accessible only under Section 8(a) and (c) of this
Act. The DOJ shall make this determination upon registration, or upon request
of the survivor, the survivor’s parent or guardian, or the prosecutor.
      The collection, processing, storage, sharing, and disposal of information in
the NSOR shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, or the “Data Privacy Act of
2012”, and the issuances of the National Privacy Commission (NPC). Towards
this end:
      (a) The DOJ shall act as the personal information controller of the NSOR,
          designate a data protection officer, and conduct a privacy impact
          assessment before the NSOR becomes operational and periodically
          thereafter;
      (b) Only information necessary for the purposes of this Act shall be
          collected, and it shall be used solely for those purposes;
      (c) Fingerprints, DNA profiles, and other biometric or sensitive personal
          information shall never be publicly disclosed, and shall be accessed
          only by authorized personnel for identification and law enforcement
          purposes;
      (d) All access to the NSOR shall be logged and regularly audited; and
      (e) The DOJ shall adopt organizational, physical, and technical security
          measures to protect the NSOR against unauthorized access, alteration,
          disclosure, and loss, and shall notify the NPC and affected persons of
          any personal data breach in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173.

      SECTION 6. Duties of the Offender. – The sex offender shall:

      Register with the Local Registration Office of each city or municipality
where he or she resides, is employed, or studies: prior to release from
imprisonment; within ten (10) days from the finality of judgment, if not
imprisoned; or within ten (10) days from arrival in the Philippines, for those
covered under Section 5(b) of this Act;
      (a) Provide true, complete, and accurate information as required under
          Section 4 of this Act;
      (b) Update his or her registration within ten (30) days from any change of
          name, residence, employment, or school;
      (c) Appear in person before the Local Registration Office to verify his or
          her information at the frequency required for his or her tier under
          Section 7 of this Act;
      (d) Notify the Local Registration Office prior to any travel outside the
          Philippines, in the manner and within the period prescribed in the
          implementing rules and regulations; and
      (e) Submit to the taking of a recent photograph and fingerprints and, if
          none has yet been taken, a DNA sample, when required for registration
          or verification.

       SECTION 7. Duration of Inclusion in the Registry. – Registrants shall be
classified into tiers based on the offense of conviction and shall remain in the
NSOR and verify their information in person for a set amount of time in
correspondence with the level of their offense.

      SECTION 8. Public-Access. –
(a) Law Enforcement Access. – The complete NSOR record shall be
    accessible to the DOJ, the Philippine National Police (PNP), the
    National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), the Bureau of Immigration (BI),
    the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor), the Bureau of Jail Management
    and Penology (BJMP), the Parole and Probation Administration (PPA),
    and the courts, on a need-to-know basis, for the prevention,
    investigation, and prosecution of crimes and the supervision of
    registrants.
(b) Public Registry Website. – The DOJ shall maintain a Public Registry
    Website, accessible to the general public free of charge, which shall
    display only the following information on registrants: name and
    aliases, recent photograph, the sex offense of conviction, tier, and the
    city or municipality and barangay of residence. The general public
    shall not have access to any other information in the NSOR. The
    website shall carry a prominent warning against the misuse of its
    information and the penalties therefor under Section 10 of this Act.
(c) Background Verification. – Schools, child development centers,
    residential care facilities, and other public or private institutions
    whose personnel work with or have regular access to children, persons
    with disabilities, or other vulnerable persons, may verify with the DOJ,
    with notice to the person concerned, whether an applicant or
    personnel member is a registrant of any tier. The result of such
    verification shall be used solely to determine fitness for positions
    involving such access.
(d) Community Notification. – Upon the registration or change of residence
    of a registrant, the Local Registration Office shall notify the punong
    barangay of the barangay of residence, and the schools and child
    development centers therein, in accordance with the protocols set in
    the implementing rules and regulations.
(e) Local Registries. – Provinces and cities shall maintain online registries
    that are linked to, and draw solely from, the Public Registry Website,
    and that display no information beyond what is allowed under
    paragraph (b) of this Section.
(f) Exclusions. – The following shall not be publicly disclosed: information
    on information withheld under Section 5(d) of this Act or suspended
    under Section 9 of this Act; any information that identifies a survivor;
    and the exact address, employer, school, fingerprints, DNA profile, and
    other sensitive personal information of any registrant.

SECTION 10. Grievance and Petition Mechanism. –
  (a) Any registrant, or any person who claims to have been erroneously
      included in the NSOR, may petition the DOJ for the correction of
      information, reclassification, or removal from the NSOR on any of
      the following grounds:
  (b) A survivor, or the survivor’s parent, guardian, or authorized
      representative, may request that the registrant’s information be
      withheld from the Public Registry Website under Section 5(d) of this
      Act.
  (c) The DOJ shall resolve the petition or request within thirty (30) days
      from its filing. Upon a prima facie showing of any of the grounds
      under paragraph (a)(2), (4), or (5) of this Section, the registrant’s
      information shall be suspended from the Public Registry Website
      within forty-eight (48) hours from filing, pending resolution. The
      decision of the DOJ may be appealed to the Secretary of Justice
      within fifteen (15) days from receipt, whose decision may be
      appealed to the Court of Appeals in accordance with the Rules of
      Court. No filing fee shall be charged, and indigent petitioners may
      avail themselves of free legal assistance from the Public Attorney’s
      Office.
  (d) No petition for early removal shall be granted without giving the
      survivor, where he or she can be located, notice and an opportunity
      to be heard, in a manner that protects his or her identity and
      privacy.
  (e) Any person aggrieved by the unlawful access, use, or disclosure of
      NSOR information may file a complaint with the DOJ, without
      prejudice to the filing of a complaint with the NPC and of the
      appropriate civil, criminal, or administrative actions.

SECTION 11. Penalties. –

     (a) A registrant who knowingly and willfully fails to register, update,
         or verify his or her registration, or who knowingly provides false
         information, shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of one (1)
         year to five (5) years and a fine of not less than Ten thousand
         pesos (₱10,000.00) but not more than Fifty thousand pesos
         (₱50,000.00).
     (b) A registrant who, while unregistered or with outdated registration
         in violation of this Act, commits another sex offense shall suffer
         the penalty of imprisonment of five (5) years to ten (10) years and
         a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00), in addition to the
         penalty imposed for the sex offense committed.
     (c) Any person who uses NSOR information to harass, threaten,
         intimidate, extort from, or inflict harm upon a registrant or any
         member of his or her family, who uses the result of a background
         verification for any purpose other than that allowed under
         Section 8(c) of this Act, or who publicly discloses information
         identifying a survivor, shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of
         six (6) months to two (2) years and a fine of not less than Fifty
                thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred
                thousand pesos (₱200,000.00).
            (d) Any public officer or employee who, without authority, accesses,
                alters, discloses, or uses NSOR information, or who through
                gross negligence fails to observe the safeguards under this Act,
                shall suffer the penalty under the preceding paragraph and the
                accessory penalty of dismissal from the service and perpetual
                disqualification from holding public office.
       The penalties under this Section are without prejudice to higher penalties
under Republic Act No. 10173, the Revised Penal Code, and other applicable
laws. If the offender is a juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed upon its
officers responsible for the violation. If the offender is a foreign national, he or
she shall be deported after serving the sentence and paying the fine.

      SECTION 12. Implementing Agencies. – The DOJ shall be the lead
implementing agency of this Act. It shall designate or organize, within its existing
structure, an office to manage the NSOR, and shall create and maintain the
database, computer system, and software of the NSOR, which shall be shared
with local government units (LGUs) for the operation of a uniform registry and
websites. The following agencies shall support the DOJ:
      (a) The PNP, through its city and municipal police stations shall serve as
          Local Registration Offices, and shall conduct verification and
          enforcement;
      (b) The NBI, which shall provide identification, forensic, and DNA support,
          and facilitate information exchange with foreign counterparts;
      (c) The BuCor and BJMP, which shall inform persons deprived of liberty of
          their duties under this Act and facilitate their registration prior to
          release; and the PPA, with respect to registrants on probation or parole;
      (d) The BI and the Department of Foreign Affairs, with respect to foreign
          registrants, the arrival of persons with foreign convictions for sex
          offenses, and international information-sharing;
      (e) The Department of Information and Communications Technology,
          which shall provide technical assistance in the development and
          security of the NSOR;
      (f) The NPC, which shall provide guidance on and monitor compliance with
          Republic Act No. 10173;
      (g) The Department of Social Welfare and Development, the Council for the
          Welfare of Children, and the Philippine Commission on Women, which
          shall assist in developing survivor-centered and child-sensitive
          protocols; and
      (h) The Department of the Interior and Local Government and the LGUs,
          whereby provinces and cities shall maintain local online registries
          under Section 8(e) of this Act, cities and municipalities shall support
         the Local Registration Offices, and barangays shall receive community
         notifications under Section 8(d) of this Act.
     The DOJ shall coordinate with the Supreme Court for the timely
transmittal of final judgments of conviction for sex offenses.

       SECTION 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
of the DOJ. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
LGUs may appropriate from their own funds the amounts necessary to carry out
their duties under this Act.

      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOJ, in coordination with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, PNP, NBI, BI, Department of
Information and Communications Technology, NPC, Department of Social
Welfare and Development, Council for the Welfare of Children, and Philippine
Commission on Women, and in consultation with the Commission on Human
Rights and civil society organizations working on women’s and children’s rights,
survivor welfare, and human rights, as well as International and National non-
government organizations operating in the Philippines, Civil Society
Organizations, and rights groups shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary for the implementation of this Act.

       SECTION 15. Public Awareness Campaign. – The DOJ, in coordination
with the Philippine Information Agency, Department of the Interior and Local
Government, PNP, Department of Education, Department of Social Welfare and
Development, Philippine Commission on Women, Council for the Welfare of
Children, and LGUs, shall conduct a continuing information and education
campaign on: (a) the existence of the NSOR and the proper use of the Public
Registry Website; (b) the limits on the use of registry information and the
penalties for its misuse; and (c) the services available to survivors of sexual
violence and the means of reporting sex offenses. The campaign shall use
Filipino, English, and the major local languages, shall be made available in
formats accessible to persons with disabilities, and shall be survivor-centered
and free of content that stigmatizes or incites hostility against any person.
       SECTION 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SECTION 18. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
