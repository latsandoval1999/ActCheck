                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The housing sector is a cornerstone of national development, yet millions of
Filipino families continue to struggle with inadequate or unaffordable housing.
According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), the country’s housing need
for 2023–2028 is estimated at 3.7 million units, reflecting both existing shortages and
projected household formation. Despite government efforts under the Pambansang
Pabahay para sa Pilipino (4PH) Program, only about 575,693 units were produced or
financed between July 2022 and June 2026, representing just over half of the
administration’s recalibrated target of 1.13 million units.

       The affordability crisis is most acute in Metro Manila, where the median
condominium price is 19.8 times the median annual household income of $8,957
(approx. ₱500,000). Townhouses are even further out of reach at 33.4 times annual
income, while median monthly rent exceeds the typical household’s monthly
earnings by 41 percent. Independent studies estimate that the private sector
produces only 128,000 housing units annually, against demand of 478,000 units,
leaving a shortfall of about 350,000 units per year.

       This bill seeks to provide tax incentives to developers of economic and
medium-cost housing projects, thereby reducing fiscal barriers and encouraging
greater private sector participation. By lowering development costs, these incentives
will enable developers to offer more affordable housing units, directly benefiting
low- and middle-income families who are often excluded from high-end
developments and underserved by socialized housing programs.

      The measure aligns with the government’s agenda of inclusive growth and
poverty reduction, while also addressing the structural imbalance between housing
demand and supply. Beyond meeting shelter needs, expanded housing development
will generate employment, stimulate allied industries such as construction and
materials supply, and contribute to sustainable urbanization.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought
to ensure that economic and medium-cost housing becomes a viable and attainable
option for millions of Filipino families, thereby strengthening the nation’s social and
economic foundations.

    AN ACT PROVIDING FOR TAX INCENTIVES ON DEVELOPMENT OF
             ECONOMIC AND MEDIUM-COST HOUSING

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. - This act shall be known as the “BAHAY NI JUAN ACT".

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared a policy of the state to address
the housing demand, encourage private participation, and provide incentives to
needed investments for mass housing, taking into consideration the following:
   a. Housing is a key industry in the economy due to its strong linkages to other
      industries;
   b. Government has limited resources that it can allocate for mass housing;
   c. Current housing supply is insufficient to meet housing demand;
   d. The cost of housing production is constantly increasing.

Sec. 3. Incentives for Mass Housing. - Enterprises engaged in the development of
economic and medium-cost housing and its related activities duly registered with
the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) shall be
entitled to the same incentives granted to enterprises registered with the Board of
Investments pursuant to Executive Order No. 226, otherwise known as the
"Omnibus Investment Code of 1987".

   Such incentives shall include:
   a. Income tax holiday;
   b. Exempt from the coverage of the expanded value-added tax law;
   c. Duty-free importation on imported capital equipment and imported raw
      materials
      For this purpose, the industry shall be included in the annual Investment
      Priorities Plan (IPP).
Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. - For the purpose of this Act, the terms below shall be
construed to mean, except where explicitly indicated or where the context clearly
indicates otherwise, as follows:
   a.​ Economic Housing. Residential subdivision and condominium units that
       conform with the development standards of Batas Pambansa Blg. 220 (BP 220)
       and are sold to the public within the current maximum selling price of PHP
       1,700,000.00 as prescribed by the DHSUD per Housing and Land Use
       Regulatory Board (HLURB) Memorandum Circular No. 09, Series of 2015.
   b.​ Medium-Cost Housing. Residential subdivision and condominium units that
       conform with the development standards of Presidential Decree No. 957 (BP
       220) and are sold to the public within the current maximum selling price of
       PHP 4,000,000.00 as prescribed by the DHSUD per HLURB Memorandum
       Circular No. 04, Series of 2006.
   c.​ Related Activities. The domestic manufacturing industries, which have a
       direct bearing on the long-term viability of the housing industry.

Sec. 5. Price Ceiling Adjustment. — The price ceiling for economic and
medium-cost housing shall be jointly determined by the DHSUD and the National
Economic Development Authority (NEDA); provided, that every three (3) years
thereafter, the price ceiling shall be adjusted to its present value using the Consumer
Price Index (CPI) as published by the National Statistics Office (NSO); provided,
further, that such adjustment shall be published through a Memorandum Circular to
be issued not later than 31 March of each year.

A copy of such guidelines, rules, and regulations shall be furnished to the Congress
of the Philippines.

Sec. 7. Repealing Clause. - All other laws, decrees, rules, regulations, and other
administrative issuances or provisions thereof that are contrary to or inconsistent
herewith are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Sec. 8. Separability Clause. — If, for any reason, any section or provision of this Act
is held unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof shall not
be affected thereby.

Sec. 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
