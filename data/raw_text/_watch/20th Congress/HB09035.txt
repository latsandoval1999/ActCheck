                          EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The 1987 Constitution mandates the State to promote social justice,
ensure transparency and accountability in public service, and adopt policies
that enhance efficiency in government operations. It likewise recognizes the
vital role of technology in national development and the duty of the State to
promote a more equitable distribution of opportunities and services.

     In this light, the herein proposed Digital Payments Act seeks to
modernize the way government collects revenues and disburses public
funds through the institutionalization of safe and efficient digital payments.
This measure ensures that public financial transactions are conducted in a
manner that is transparent, traceable, and responsive to the needs of the
people.

      Once enacted, the benefits of this bill for our people are immediate and
practical. Digital payments eliminate the need to physically line up in
government offices, lower transaction costs, and enable payments for taxes,
fees, and permits anytime and anywhere. For small businesses and local
merchants, the integration of digital payment capability streamlines
compliance with local regulations while expanding their access to formal
financial services. By empowering local government units to support small
and micro-merchants in adopting digital systems, the bill promotes financial
inclusion and reduces the risks associated with handling cash.

       This policy is a necessary step toward building a modern, citizen-
centered government. By prioritizing safety, interoperability, and digital
literacy, this measure enhances efficiency while reinforcing public
accountability. We thus aim to institutionalize routine financial transactions
into faster, safer, and more transparent processes, ensuring that government
services are delivered with greater convenience, integrity, and reliability for
every Filipino.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

16                            AN ACT
17        PROMOTING THE ADOPTION OF DIGITAL PAYMENTS FOR
18       FINANCIAL TRANSACTIONS OF THE GOVERNMENT AND ALL
19                          MERCHANTS

20         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
21   Philippines in Congress assembled:

22        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known and cited as the
23   "Promotion of Digital Payments Act."

24         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes the vital role of
25   information and communications technology in nation-building. The State
26   also recognizes the need of promoting ease of doing business and efficient
27   delivery of goods and services to the general public. Towards this end, the
28   State shall promote financial inclusion, optimize the use of technology and
29   innovative payment systems for financial transactions, and promote the
30   electronic conveyance of payment for various transactions with the
31   government, to and among business entities and merchants, and among the
32   general public.
33         SEC. 3. Objective. - This Act aims to facilitate transactions,
34   arrangements, or exchanges of goods and services by promoting the
35   universal use of safe and efficient digital payments in financial transactions
36   of the government and the general public.

37        SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

38        (a) Access device refers to any card, plate, code, account number,
39        electronic serial number, personal identification number, or other
40        telecommunications service, equipment or instrumental identifier, or
41        other means of account access that can be used to obtain money,
42        goods, services, or any other thing of value or to initiate a transfer of
43        funds other than a transfer originated solely by paper instrument;

44        (b) Digital payments refer to monetary payment transactions between
45        two (2) parties through a digital payment instrument in which both the
46        payer and the payee use an electronic channel;

47        (c) Merchant refers to a person or entity engaged in buying and selling
48        merchandise, purchasing goods and services, skills, or expertise, and
49        leasing of goods and services. It also includes credit-granting entities,
50        lending institutions, pawnshops, remittance companies and other
51        money service business, except financial institutions supervised by the
52        Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), which shall be governed by specific
53        BSP regulations and requirements on digital services;

54        (d) Payment system refers to the set of payment instruments,
55        processes, procedures and participants that ensures the circulation of
56        money or movement of funds which comply with the requirements set
57        forth under Republic Act No. 11127, otherwise known as "The National
58        Payment Systems Act"; and

59        (e) Payment service provider (PSP) refers to an entity that provides
60        payment services to end-users, such as consumers, merchants, and
61        billers, including government institutions.

62         SEC. 5. Use of Digital Payments. - All national government agencies
63   (NGAs), government-owned or -controlled corporations (GOCCs), and local
64   government units (LGUs) are hereby mandated to utilize safe and efficient
65   electronic or digital means of receiving payment for taxes, fees, tolls,
66   imposts, and other revenues and for the payment of goods, services, and
67   other 9 disbursements. To this end, government entities may be allowed to
 68   include in their respective budgets the amounts that will cover the cost of
 69   establishing and maintaining the infrastructure, system and process
 70   adjustments as well as transaction fees that they may shoulder in connection
 71   with the implementation of digital payments, including merchant discount
 72   rate, processing fees, cash out fees, administration fees: Provided, That the
 73   release of said budget may be conditioned on the actual adoption of digital
 74   payments by the concerned government entities.

 75         To facilitate compliance with this Act, NGAs, GOCCs, and LGUs shall
 76   adopt account-based disbursements whereby target recipients directly
 77   receive government payments into their transaction accounts. These
 78   government entities may create their respective digital payment technical
 79   support and maintenance service units which shall be responsible for
 80   troubleshooting and maintaining coordination with the PSP partner for
 81   technical and other concerns within the agency. They shall also be
 82   responsible for ensuring the proper transition of their respective agency's
 83   payments and disbursement procedures and policies to digital payments.
 84   The creation of the technical support and maintenance service units shall be
 85   without prejudice to any technical support arrangement between the agency
 86   and the PSP partner pursuant to a contract or agreement: Provided, That,
 87   the contract or agreement shall be subject to the applicable regulations of
 88   the BSP.

 89        The BSP shall accelerate the adoption by all PSPs of the national quick
 90   response (QR) code standard to hasten the interoperability of QR-driven
 91   payment services and eliminate the need for merchants and clients to
 92   maintain several accounts.

 93         The Government Procurement Policy Board shall, in coordination with
 94   the BSP, issue guidelines to be observed when procuring the services of a
 95   PSP that provide digital payment: Provided, That, government entities shall
 96   avail only of interoperable digital payment solutions.

 97        Notwithstanding any law to the contrary, a partner PSP of a
 98   government entity shall not be limited to government financial institutions.

 99         SEC. 6. Digital Payment Capability of Merchants. - To accelerate
100   the adoption of digital payment, LGUs shall, by ordinance, require merchants
101   within their localities to establish or outsource arrangements or mechanisms
102   that would enable them to receive payments from clients and make
103   payments to creditors and suppliers in digital form as a pre-requisite for the
104   approval or renewal of their business permits. No new or renewal of business
105   permit shall be approved unless the merchant concerned show to the
106   satisfaction of the LGU that a functional digital payment system accessible
107   by mobile phone or other access devices is installed or provided by a duly
108   registered PSP in the merchant partner's place of business.

109          The LGUs shall ensure that merchants in their jurisdictions have
110   access to appropriate digital payment solutions and have the capacity to
111   effectively use the same, with due consideration to small and micro-
112   merchants, including market vendors, tricycle operators and food stalls. The
113   LGU shall extend assistance to small and micro-merchants to facilitate their
114   adoption of digital transaction capability. The BSP, Department of Trade and
115   Industry (DTI), Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the
116   Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall
117   also facilitate measures to provide capacity building for the NGAs, GOCCs,
118   LGUs, and merchants on the use of digital payments.

119         SEC. 7. Promotion of Digital Payment Transactions. - To optimize
120   the benefits of this technological innovation, scale up financial inclusion, and
121   promote sustainability, NGAs, GOCCs, and LGUs shall prioritize the use of
122   safe and efficient digital payment in their financial transactions.

123         The NGAs, GOCCs, and LGUs shall also explore the feasibility of
124   adopting a comprehensive incentive framework for selected financial
125   transactions to encourage the availment of digital payments. Moreover,
126   LGUs may impose reduced fees or grant other incentives for merchants
127   providing efficient digital payment systems.

128         The BSP shall, in coordination with relevant stakeholders, promote
129   measures on financial and digital literacy, and consumer protection to
130   strengthen the public's trust in digital payment transactions.

131         The Department of Science and Technology and the DICT shall
132   implement measures to further enhance the availability and cost of internet
133   connection to support the government's program on the digitalization of
134   financial transactions.

135          SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The BSP shall, in
136   coordination with the Department of Budget and Management, DTI, DICT,
137   and DILG, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the provisions
138   of this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.
139         SEC. 9. Separability Clause. - If for any reason, any part or provision
140   of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or
141   provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

142          SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive
143   orders, rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions
144   of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

145         SEC. 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
146   its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of national circulation.
