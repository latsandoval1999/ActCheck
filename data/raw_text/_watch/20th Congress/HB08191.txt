  EXPANDING THE ELECTRICITY LIFELINE RATE TO MINIMUM WAGE
 EARNERS AND INSTITUTIONALIZING AUTOMATIC ENROLLMENT FOR

  SECTION 73 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER

                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Article XIII, Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to
promote social justice in all phases of national development by reducing social, economic, and
political inequalities and removing inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and power for the
common good. Access to electricity is indispensable to human dignity, public health, education,
safety, and economic participation. It is for this reason that Section 73 of Republic Act No.
9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), established
the lifeline rate as a socialized pricing mechanism for marginalized electricity consumers.

         Republic Act No. 11552 strengthened the lifeline rate framework and provided a longer-
term policy basis for extending this social protection mechanism. Yet despite the existence of
the lifeline rate program, the country has experienced a persistent implementation gap.
Government reports have shown that while around 4.5 million households were identified as
eligible beneficiaries (primarily through the 4Ps registry), only a small fraction were actually
registered with distribution utilities, meaning that many intended beneficiaries did not receive the
discount in practice.1 This highlights a structural problem: where the benefit depends on
beneficiary-initiated registration or documentation, the poorest households—especially those in
remote areas—are the most likely to be excluded.

        Recent Executive reforms in early 2026 validated the need to shift away from manual,
beneficiary-driven processes. Through Joint Resolution No. 1, Series of 2026 (and related
implementing measures), the Department of Energy (DOE), the Department of Social Welfare
and Development (DSWD), and the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) moved toward
automatic registration of qualified 4Ps beneficiaries for the lifeline program, subject to data
matching with distribution utilities, with manual processes retained only as a fallback for
substantial mismatches.2 In the same reform period, government issuances also pointed to a
uniform national lifeline consumption threshold of 0–50 kWh, with qualified beneficiaries
within this level entitled to a 100% discount on applicable electricity charges, while allowing
existing higher-threshold structures to continue for higher consumption levels where already
approved.3 These reforms are important progress, but as executive issuances, they remain
vulnerable to policy reversal, uneven implementation, or shifting administrative priorities.

          This bill—part of the broader Ginhawa K.I.T. (Kuryente–Internet–Trabaho)
reform package—seeks to make electricity bill relief both more durable and more inclusive.
First, it institutionalizes the “automatic, not opt-in” principle by requiring distribution utilities to
apply the lifeline rate automatically to qualified marginalized end-users, subject to secure
eligibility matching, account tagging, and anti-leakage safeguards to be defined in the
implementing rules and regulations. It anchors identification of qualified marginalized end-users
on the DSWD’s official poverty targeting registry, derived from or validated using data under the
Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) pursuant to Republic Act No. 11315, with the
most recent NHTS-PR/Listahanan and other officially adopted DSWD registries available
during the transition period.4

        Second—and as the bill’s major expansion—this measure extends meaningful monthly
relief to all Minimum Wage Earners by granting them a uniform fifty percent (50%)
discount on applicable electricity charges for their household’s principal residence.
Millions of Filipino workers receive minimum wage and still struggle with rising cost-of-living
pressures. Reducing their monthly electricity burden increases take-home resources available for
food, transport, education, health, and basic household needs—supporting productivity,
improving well-being, and strengthening household spending in the local economy. This
recognizes a reality often felt by ordinary families: many households who are not classified as 4Ps
beneficiaries still struggle with rising cost-of-living pressures despite being employed. Recent
government reporting on wage orders indicates that minimum wage adjustments affect millions
of workers, underscoring the scale of households that may be economically vulnerable even
while working.5

        Finally, this measure strengthens accountability by requiring robust guardrails: one-
account safeguards, principal-residence limitations, verification standards, periodic revalidation,
clear grievance and correction mechanisms, and penalties for misrepresentation. These
safeguards ensure that relief is targeted, auditable, and protected from abuse—while remaining
accessible to the very families the law intends to help.

         In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Ginhawa sa Kuryente Act."

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall promote a just and dynamic social order
thereby uplifting its citizens and marginalized sectors from poverty through policies that provide
adequate social services, promote full employment, a rising standard of living, and an improved
quality of life for all.

          The State recognizes that access to electricity is indispensable to human dignity, public
health, education, and economic participation, and shall therefore ensure that these essential
utilities are available, reliable, and affordable to every Filipino household, particularly the poor
and vulnerable.

       Consistent with the constitutional commitment to social justice and equality, the State
shall pursue measures that reduce social and economic inequities by diffusing access to
opportunities and modern services, especially in rural and underprivileged communities.

        Section 3. Definition of terms. – As used in this Act:

             (a) “Lifeline Rate” refers to the socialized pricing mechanism for electricity
                 consumers under Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9136, as amended, in the form
                 of discounts determined by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) after due
                 notice and hearing.

             (b) “Qualified Marginalized End-Users” refer to households qualified under
                 Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9136, as amended.

             (c) “Minimum Wage Earner (MWE)” refers to a worker whose wage rate is at or
                 below the applicable statutory minimum wage under wage orders issued by the
                 Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards (RTWPBs), or such lawful
                 successor wage-setting issuances, as may be applicable.

             (d) “Principal Residence” refers to the dwelling where the beneficiary household
                 ordinarily resides and which corresponds to a single residential electricity service
                 account actually used for household consumption, as further defined in the
                 implementing rules and regulations.

             (e) “Automatic Application” refers to the process by which lifeline rate discounts
                 are applied to eligible service accounts through secure eligibility matching and
                 account tagging, without requiring the beneficiary to file an individual
                 application, except in cases of failed matching, disputed eligibility, or other
                 exception cases as may be provided in the implementing rules and regulations.
           (f) “DSWD Official Poverty Targeting Registry” refers to the official household
               registry maintained or issued by the Department of Social Welfare and
               Development (DSWD) for identifying beneficiaries of poverty-targeted social
               protection programs, derived from or validated using data under the Community-
               Based Monitoring System (CBMS) pursuant to Republic Act No. 11315 and
               related issuances, and, during transition, the most recent National Household
               Targeting System for Poverty Reduction (NHTS-PR) or Listahanan and such
               other DSWD registries officially adopted for social protection targeting.

           (g) “Minimum Necessary Data Set” refers to the least amount of information
               required to implement eligibility matching and account tagging for automatic
               application, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012),
               its implementing rules, and applicable issuances.

           (h) “Anti-Leakage Safeguards” refer to controls and procedures designed to
               prevent improper inclusion, duplicate availment, fraud, or non-residential use of
               benefits under this Act, as further detailed in the implementing rules and
               regulations.

       Section 5. Guardrails and Safeguards for Minimum Wage Earner Discount. – To strengthen
program integrity and prevent leakage for the MWE discount under Section 73(c), the
implementing rules and regulations shall include, at a minimum, the following safeguards:

        (a) One Household–One Account Rule. – Availment shall be limited to one (1)
residential service account corresponding to the household’s principal residence.

        (b) Principal Residence and Residential Use. – Availment shall be limited to
residential service accounts and shall not apply to commercial, industrial, or mixed-use accounts,
except as may be allowed under strict criteria to cover home-based arrangements that are
essentially residential, as may be defined in the IRR.

        (c) Non-Transferability. – The benefit shall be non-transferable and shall not be traded,
assigned, or used to cover an account other than that approved for the household’s principal
residence.

       (d) Verification Standards. – Verification shall be designed to be simple and accessible,
while being auditable. The IRR may provide for employer certification, basic payroll or payslip
proof, or other official verification sources consistent with law, with special provisions for
workers without regular documentation.

        (e) Periodic Revalidation. – Eligibility shall be time-bound and subject to periodic
revalidation to confirm continuing MWE status, as may be provided in the IRR.

       (f) Anti-Fraud and Penalties. – The IRR shall include penalties and disqualification
rules for misrepresentation, fraud, or manipulation, without prejudice to applicable civil,
administrative, or criminal liabilities under existing laws.

        (g) Grievance and Correction. – A clear process shall be established to address denial,
exclusion, account-tagging disputes, and correction of records, with response timelines and
escalation paths.

      Section 6. Inter-Agency Committee. – To ensure the proper, efficient, and timely
implementation of the provisions of this Act, there is hereby created an Inter-Agency
Monitoring and Oversight Committee on Electricity Bill Relief and Automatic Lifeline
Application (hereinafter referred to as the “Committee”), which shall be composed of the
following:

           (a) The Secretary of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
               – Chairperson

           (b) The Chairperson of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) – Co-
               Chairperson

           (c) The Secretary of the Department of Energy (DOE) – Co-Chairperson

           (d) The Chairperson of the National Privacy Commission (NPC) – Member

           (e) A representative from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) - Member

           (f) A representative from the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) –
               Member

           (g) A representative from the National Wages and Productivity Commission
               (NWPC) – Member

           (h) A representative from the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) –
               Member

           (i) One (1) representative from an accredited national organization of distribution
               utilities – Member

           (j) Two (2) representatives from civil society organizations – Members

The Committee shall have the following functions:

           (1) Monitor and evaluate the implementation of automatic lifeline application for
               qualified marginalized end-users, including coverage, match rates, exclusion
               errors, correction outcomes, and anti-leakage controls;

           (2) Monitor and evaluate the implementation of the Minimum Wage Earner
               electricity discount, including verification mechanisms, uptake, duplication risks,
               fraud patterns, and corrective actions;

           (3) Oversee compliance of implementing agencies, service providers, and regulatory
               bodies with the provisions of this Act and its implementing rules and
               regulations;

           (4) Ensure accurate and timely data sharing among implementing entities, in
               accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) and
               issuances of the NPC, including adoption of a Minimum Necessary Data Set;

           (5) Recommend amendments to policies, procedures, and regulatory rules to
               improve implementation, prevent leakage, and strengthen inclusion;
            (6) Establish and oversee a grievance redress and correction mechanism, in
                coordination with the DSWD and the ERC, to address complaints regarding
                exclusion, incorrect billing, verification disputes, or non-compliance by service
                providers;

            (7) Conduct or cause the conduct of periodic audits, either directly or through
                designated agencies, to verify compliance and accuracy of benefit application;
                and

            (8) Submit an annual report to Congress on the status of implementation, including
                the number of beneficiary households or accounts served per utility, geographic
                distribution, fiscal/financial impact indicators, inclusion and leakage metrics, and
                issues encountered.

        The Committee shall convene within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act and
shall meet at least quarterly or as often as may be necessary. The Committee shall be provided
with sufficient technical and administrative support from the member agencies. The initial
funding necessary for the operationalization of the Committee shall be charged against the
current appropriations of the member agencies, and shall be included in their respective budgets
in the succeeding years.

        Section 7. Data Privacy. – All processing and transfer of data under this Act, including
the Minimum Necessary Data Set and any verification-related information, shall strictly adhere
to Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012), its implementing rules, and applicable
issuances of the National Privacy Commission.

      Section 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the DOE, DSWD, DOLE, NWPC, PSA, NPC, and
the National Electrification Administration (NEA), shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of the amended lifeline rate, including the MWE
discount under Section 73(c), ensuring harmonization with existing lifeline program frameworks
and relevant Executive issuances.

unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
force and effect.
