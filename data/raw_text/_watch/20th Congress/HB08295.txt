                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to promote and institutionalize a secure, efficient and universally
accessible digital or electronic system for the conveyance of payments in all
transactions, arrangements or exchanges of goods and services involving the
government and the public. This provision explains that the Local Government Units
(LGUs) are mandated to promote the adoption of digital payments at the local level,
and that, through ordinances, merchants will be able to receive their payments
digitally.

        A 2023 report by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas showed that only 56% of
Filipinos have formal bank accounts, while the other 76% have internet access and
own mobile phones. Notably, digital payments serve as a pivotal technological
innovation that can enhance productivity and reduce transaction costs. Available data
indicates that, as early as 2022, approximately 40% of payments were already
conducted online. This preference is largely driven by convenience and efficiency of
digital platforms for routine financial activities such as bills payments, fund transfers
to family, friends and account deposits.

       Studies indicate that the use of electronic payment systems has significantly
increased in recent years. This shift has been further accelerated by the COVID-19
pandemic, which underscored the need to minimize unnecessary physical contact.
During the pandemic, there was a rampant use of contactless payment methods such
as QR-based transactions, online banking transactions and the use of mobile wallets
which enabled consumers to pay for essential goods and services without exchanging
cash or physically touching point-of-sale devices. In light of these matters, the use of
contactless payments during the pandemic is evidence that the economy can still
prosper while introducing a shift toward a digitally enabled payment ecosystem. It can
be noted that through this move, the move toward digital transactions promotes
transparency, curbs opportunities for corruption and strengthens mechanisms for fund
monitoring and accountability.
       Given these advantages, the enactment of the Digital Payments Act becomes
essential in advancing a more inclusive, efficient and future-ready Philippine economy.
This measure likewise aligns with national priorities to accelerate digital
transformation, stimulate economic development and enhance the delivery of public
services.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
        PROMOTING THE ADOPTION OF DIGITAL PAYMENTS FOR FINANCIAL
           TRANSACTIONS OF THE GOVERNMENT AND ALL MERCHANTS

           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title: - This Act shall be known and cited as the “Digital
 2   Payments Act.”
 3
 4          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of information
 5   and communications technology in nation-building. The State also recognizes the need
 6   of promoting ease of doing business and efficient delivery of goods and services to
 7   the general public. Towards this end, the State shall promote financial inclusion,
 8   optimize the use of technology and innovative payment systems for financial
 9   transactions, and promote the electronic conveyance of payment for various
10   transactions with the government, to and among business entities and merchants, and
11   among the general public.
12
13           Sec 3. Objective. – This Act aims to facilitate transactions, arrangements or
14   exchanges of goods and services by promoting the universal use of safe and efficient
15   digital payments in financial transactions of the government and the general public.
16
17          Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
18
19           (a) Access device refers to any card, plate, code, account number, electronic
20   serial number, personal identification number, or other telecommunications service,
21   equipment or instrumental identifier, or other means of account access that can be
22   used to obtain money, goods, services, or any other thing of value or to initiate a
23   transfer of funds other than a transfer originated solely by paper instrument;
24
25          (b) Digital payments refer to monetary payment transactions between two (2)
26   parties through a digital payment instrument in which both the payer and the payee
27   use an electronic channel;
                                                                                             1
 1
 2           (c) Merchant refers to a person or entity engaged in buying and selling
 3   merchandise, purchasing goods and services, skills, or expertise, and leasing of goods
 4   and services. It also includes credit-granting entities, lending institutions, pawnshops,
 5   remittance companies and other money service business, except financial institutions
 6   supervised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), which shall be governed by
 7   specific BSP regulations and requirements on digital services;
 8
 9         (d) Payment system refers to the set of payment instruments, processes,
10   procedures and participants that ensures the circulation of money or movement of
11   funds which comply with the requirements set forth under Republic Act No. 11127,
12   otherwise known as “The National Payment Systems Act”; and
13
14          (e) Payment service provider (PSP) refers to an entity that provides payment
15   services to end-users, such as consumers, merchants, and billers, including
16   government institutions.
17
18           Sec. 5. Use of Digital Payments. – All national government agencies (NGAs),
19   government-owned or-controlled corporations (GOCCs), and local government units
20   (LGUs) are hereby mandated to utilize safe and efficient electronic or digital means of
21   receiving payment for taxes, fees, tolls, imposts, and other revenues and for the
22   payment of goods, services, and other disbursements. To this end, government
23   entities may be allowed to include in their respective budgets the amounts that will
24   cover the cost of establishing and maintaining the infrastructure, system and process
25   adjustments as well as transaction fees that they may shoulder in connection with the
26   implementation of digital payments, including merchant discount rate, processing
27   fees, cash out fees, administration fees: Provided, That the release of said budget
28   may be conditioned on the actual adoption of digital payments by the concerned
29   government entities.
30
31          To facilitate compliance with this Act, NGAs, GOCCs, and LGUs shall adopt
32   account-based disbursements whereby target recipients directly receive government
33   payments into their transaction accounts. These government entities may create their
34   respective digital payment technical support and maintenance service units which shall
35   be responsible for troubleshooting and maintaining coordination with the PSP partner
36   for technical and other concerns within the agency. They shall also be responsible for
37   ensuring the proper transition of their respective agency’s payments and disbursement
38   procedures and policies to digital payments. The creation of the technical support and
39   maintenance service units shall be without prejudice to any technical support
40   arrangement between the agency and the PSP partner pursuant to a contract or
41   agreement: Provided, That, the contract or agreement shall be subject to the
42   applicable regulations of the BSP.
43
44          The BSP shall accelerate the adoption by all PSPs of the national quick response
45   (QR) code standard to hasten the interoperability of QR-driven payment services and
46   eliminate the need for merchants and clients to maintain several accounts.

 1           The Government Procurement Policy Board shall, in coordination with the BSP,

 9           Sec. 6. Digital Payment Capability of Merchants. - To accelerate the adoption
10   of digital payment, LGUs shall, by ordinance, require merchants within their localities
11   to establish or outsource arrangements or mechanisms that would enable them to
12   receive payments from clients and make payments to creditors and suppliers in digital
13   form as a pre-requisite for the approval or renewal of their business permits. No new
14   or renewal of business permit shall be approved unless the merchant concerned shows
15   to the satisfaction of the LGU that a functional digital payment system accessible by
16   mobile phone or other access devices is installed or provided by a duly registered PSP
17   in the merchant partner’s place of business.
18
19         The LGUs shall ensure that merchants in their jurisdictions have access to
20   appropriate digital payment solutions and have the capacity to effectively use the
21   same, with due consideration to small and micro-merchants, including market
22   vendors, tricycle operators and food stalls. The LGU shall extend assistance to small
23   and micro-merchants to facilitate their adoption of digital transaction capability. The
24   BSP, Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Interior and Local
25   Government (DILG) and the Department of Information and Communications
26   Technology (DICT) shall also facilitate measures to provide capacity building for the
27   NGAs, GOCCs, LGUs, and merchants on the use of digital payments.
28
29           Sec. 7. Promotion of Digital Payment Transactions. – To optimize the benefits
30   of this technological innovation, scale up financial inclusion, and promote
31   sustainability, NGAs, GOCCs and LGUs shall prioritize the use of safe and efficient
32   digital payment in their financial transactions.
33
34          The NGAs, GOCCs, and LGUs shall also explore the feasibility of adopting a
35   comprehensive incentive framework for selected financial transactions to encourage
36   the availability of digital payments. Moreover, LGUs may impose reduced fees or grant
37   other incentives for merchants providing efficient digital payment systems.
38
39           The BSP shall, in coordination with relevant stakeholders, promote measures
40   on financial and digital literacy and consumer protection to strengthen the public’s
41   trust in digital payment transactions.
42
43         The Department of Science and Technology and the DICT shall implement
44   measures to further enhance the availability and cost of internet connection to support
45   the government’s program on the digitalization of financial transactions.
