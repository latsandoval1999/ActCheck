                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “Carry each other’s burdens, and in this way, you will fulfill the law of Christ.”
     — Galatians 6:2

     “Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in the power
     of your hand to do so.” – Proverbs 3:27

       Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution explicitly establishes that the
protection and promotion of health, and access to health care is fundamentally a
human right, to wit: “[T]he State shall protect and promote the right to health of the
people and instill health consciousness among them.” In the same vein, Article III,
Section 20 stipulates that "[N]o person shall be imprisoned for debt or non-payment of
a poll tax.” These key Constitutional provisions are the guiding principles on the
enactment of Republic Act No. 9439, otherwise known as the Anti-Hospital Detention
Law, which prohibits the unlawful detention of patients on account of unpaid medical
obligations. However, gaps in the coverage and enforcement mechanisms of the law
continue to leave patients and their families vulnerable, particularly those who are
financially incapacitated and those confronted with the release of medical documents,
personal belongings, or the remains of a deceased loved one.

     This measure seeks to strengthen the protections under Republic Act No. 9439
by expressly covering hospitals, medical clinics, and other similar healthcare facilities,
and by prohibiting the detention not only of patients but also of their relatives,
representatives, caregivers, and, in appropriate cases, newborn children. It further
provides a clearer definition of detention to include acts that effectively prevent
discharge or release, such as withholding documents necessary for discharge, benefit
claims, or interment, refusing the medically appropriate removal of medical devices,
withholding personal belongings, or refusing to release the remains of a deceased
patient on account of unpaid obligations.

      The bill also establishes safeguards for patients who are financially incapable of
settling their obligations. While allowing the execution of secured promissory notes

                                             1
where appropriate, it provides alternative mechanisms such as guarantees from
government agencies and assignments of benefits or proceeds. Indigent patients
endorsed through the hospital's medical social services may be exempted from the
requirement of a promissory note upon submission of an appropriate guarantee from
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) or the Department of
Health (DOH). These mechanisms seek to balance the legitimate financial interests of
healthcare facilities with the patient's right to timely discharge and access to essential
documents and benefits.

       Recognizing that vulnerable patients may require assistance beyond the
immediate prohibition against detention, the measure mandates DOH to facilitate
access to government medical assistance programs and establish a grievance and
redress mechanism for unlawful detention. It likewise requires healthcare facilities to
adopt standard discharge protocols, provide transparency in healthcare costs and
billing, prominently post patients' rights, and refer indigent or financially incapacitated
patients to medical social service offices. These provisions strengthen accountability
and ensure that the prohibition against hospital detention is translated into an
accessible and enforceable patient protection system.

      To ensure meaningful enforcement, the bill imposes higher penalties when
unlawful detention is committed pursuant to an established institutional policy or
management instruction. Repeated violations under such policy may likewise result in
the revocation of the health facility's license to operate. The measure further provides
a tax deduction for certain unpaid balances guaranteed to facilitate the discharge of
financially incapacitated patients, thereby helping mitigate the financial burden on
healthcare facilities while preserving the patient's right to timely release.

      By amending and strengthening Republic Act No. 9439, this measure seeks to
ensure that financial hardship does not become a barrier to a patient's liberty, dignity,
or access to necessary medical and legal documents. It establishes clearer
responsibilities for healthcare institutions and government agencies while providing
practical mechanisms for the recovery or settlement of legitimate medical obligations
without resorting to the detention of patients or their families.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
