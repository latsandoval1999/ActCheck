                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This proposed measure seeks to amend Section 3 of Republic Act No. 9167 or
“An Act Creating The Film Development Council Of The Philippines (FDCP), Defining
Its Powers And Functions, Appropriating Funds Therefor, And For Other Purposes” by
inserting a new subsection that establishes a Classic Filipino Film Restoration Program.
The proposed program shall authorize the FDCP to assess and identify classic Filipino
films in need of restoration and to undertake their restoration, digitization and long-
term preservation.

         Republic Act No. 9167, which created the FDCP, mandates the agency to
promote and develop the Philippine film industry. While the FDCP has undertaken
initiatives related to film promotion and development, there remains a need to
expressly empower and mandate the Council to systematically address the
preservation, restoration and digitization of classic Filipino films as part of its core
functions.

       Despite increasing awareness of the importance of film preservation, the
Philippines continues to face significant challenges in this area, most notably the lack
of sustainable funding. In practice, private institutions are often better positioned to
undertake restoration initiatives, as ownership of film rights allows them to
commercially distribute restored works and recover associated costs.

       A notable example is the ABS-CBN Film Restoration initiative, which successfully
undertook the scanning, enhancement, restoration and remastering of more than two
hundred forty (240) motion pictures, including films dating as far back as 1939.
Through this effort, culturally and historically significant Filipino films were
reintroduced to contemporary audiences, underscoring the value and impact of
systematic restoration programs.
       Furthermore, the bill provides that restored films shall be made accessible to
the public through cinematheques, museums, educational institutions, and digital
platforms, subject to existing copyright laws. This ensures that restored works are not
only preserved but are also utilized for education, research, and cultural appreciation,
thereby strengthening national identity and promoting film literacy among present and
future generations.

      Ultimately, this proposed amendment recognizes classic Filipino films as
important cultural assets and underscores the State’s role in preserving and making
them accessible as part of the nation’s cultural legacy.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
