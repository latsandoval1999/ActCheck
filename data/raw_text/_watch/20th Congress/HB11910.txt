                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to face recurring spikes and instability in food
prices caused by supply chain disruptions, climate-related disasters, import
dependency, hoarding, profiteering, transportation inefficiencies, and market
speculation. These fluctuations disproportionately burden Filipino consumers,
especially low-income households, while simultaneously harming farmers and
fisherfolk who often receive low farmgate prices despite soaring retail prices in
urban markets.

       While existing agencies conduct price monitoring and implement isolated
interventions, there remains no permanent inter-agency mechanism with sufficient
authority, funding, and coordination powers dedicated to stabilizing agri-food prices
from production to retail distribution. There is likewise no institutionalized
emergency intervention fund capable of rapidly responding to supply shocks, sudden
price surges, or agricultural market disruptions.

       This proposed measure seeks to establish the Agri-Food Price Stabilization
Council (AFPSC), an inter-agency body mandated to comprehensively monitor,
analyze, and manage agri-food price movements nationwide. The measure further
creates an Emergency Market Intervention Fund that may be utilized for strategic
buffer stocking, direct procurement from farmers and fisherfolk, transport and
logistics subsidies, market interventions, temporary consumer support mechanisms,
and other stabilization measures during periods of abnormal price volatility or food
supply emergencies.

      The bill also strengthens transparency, accountability, and coordination
among key agencies including the Department of Agriculture, Department of Trade
and Industry, Department of Finance, National Economic and Development
Authority, Philippine Competition Commission, and local government units to
ensure timely and science-based interventions in the agri-food sector.

      Ultimately, this measure seeks to protect both producers and consumers,
promote fair and efficient food markets, reduce excessive price volatility, enhance

      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy.        It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to ensure the availability, accessibility, affordability, safety, and adequacy of food

             with existing competition and consumer protection laws;

                    (c) Strengthen national and local capacities to anticipate,
             monitor, and respond to disruptions in food production, distribution,
             logistics, and marketing arising from natural disasters, climate change,
             armed conflict, public health emergencies, economic shocks, and other
             force majeure events;

                    (d) Institutionalize a science-based, data-driven, and coordinated
             national system for agri-food price monitoring, forecasting, risk
             assessment, and market intervention;

             and retail prices by improving market linkages, logistics efficiency,
             storage infrastructure, and supply chain transparency;

                    (f) Encourage direct participation of farmers, fisherfolk,
             cooperatives, and producers' organizations in agricultural markets;

                    (g) Ensure that government interventions in agri-food markets
             are temporary, targeted, transparent, proportionate, and consistent with
             existing laws; and

                    (h) Promote the long-term resilience, competitiveness, and

                    (c). Retail Price refers to the final selling price of agri-food
             products to consumers;

                    (d). Price Volatility refers to abnormal or excessive fluctuations
             in food prices over a specific period;

             distribution during market disruptions;

                   (f). Buffer Stocking refers to the strategic procurement and
             storage of essential food commodities for stabilization purposes.

                                   ARTICLE III
             EMERGENCY MARKET INTERVENTION FUND

      SEC 7. Creation of the Emergency Market Intervention Fund.                There is
hereby established a special fund to be known as the Emergency Market Intervention
Fund, hereinafter referred to as the Fund, which shall be used exclusively to finance
urgent, temporary, and targeted interventions necessary to prevent, mitigate, or



                                                                                      6
address severe agri-food supply disruptions, abnormal price volatility, market
failures, natural disasters, public health emergencies, climate-related events, and
other extraordinary circumstances affecting food availability, accessibility, and
affordability. The Fund shall support government measures aimed at stabilizing the
supply and prices of essential agri-food commodities, protecting the welfare of
farmers, fisherfolk, consumers, and other stakeholders, and strengthening national
food security and market resilience.

      SEC 8. Uses of the Fund.         The Fund shall be used exclusively for the
following purposes:

            (a) Procurement and maintenance of strategic buffer stocks of essential
            agri-food commodities;

            (b) Direct purchase of agricultural and fishery products from farmers,
            fisherfolk, and their cooperatives during periods of oversupply,
            depressed farmgate prices, or market disruptions;

            (c) Emergency logistics support, including transportation, cold-chain
            facilities, warehousing, and distribution services;

            (d) Targeted food assistance programs and consumer support measures
            during severe supply shortages and extraordinary price increases;

            (e) Market operations designed to stabilize prices and ensure food
            availability;

            (f) Development and maintenance of digital price monitoring,
            forecasting, and market intelligence systems;




                                                                                 7
             (g) Climate adaptation, disaster response, and emergency recovery
             interventions affecting food production and supply chains;

             (h) Capacity-building programs for local governments and stakeholders
             on market monitoring and food security; and

             (i) Such other stabilization measures as may be approved by the Council
             consistent with the objectives of this Act.

      No portion of the Fund shall be used for salaries, bonuses, allowances,
administrative overhead unrelated to intervention activities, or purposes inconsistent
with this Act.

SEC 9. Fund Administration.        The Fund shall be administered by the Council
through a dedicated Secretariat under the Department of Agriculture (DA), which
shall be responsible for the management, allocation, disbursement, monitoring, and
reporting of Fund utilization in accordance with this Act and applicable budgeting,
accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations. The Secretariat shall maintain
complete and accurate records of all transactions and ensure that all expenditures are
transparent, accountable, and used solely for the purposes authorized under this Act,
subject to the examination and audit jurisdiction of the Commission on Audit
(COA).

`     SEC 10. Sources of the Fund.       The Fund shall be sourced from the annual
appropriation under the General Appropriations Act, excess tariff revenues from
agricultural imports, grants, donations, and official development assistance
consistent with existing laws and other lawful sources as may be determined by the
Council.

                                   ARTICLE IV
                  PRICE MONITORING, TRANSPARENCY,
                         AND MARKET INTERVENTION

      SEC 11. National Agri-Food Price Monitoring System.           The Council shall
establish a centralized and publicly accessible digital platform containing updated
farmgate, wholesale, and retail prices of essential commodities across all regions of
the country. It is to support evidence-based policymaking, market transparency,
price stabilization, and informed decision-making by government agencies,
producers, traders, consumers, and other stakeholders.

      SEC 12. Mandatory Data Submission.          All relevant government agencies,
local government units, public markets, trading centers, food terminals, importers,
exporters, wholesalers, processors, logistics providers, retailers, and other entities
identified by the Council shall submit accurate, timely, and verifiable market
information as may be required for monitoring, forecasting, and policy formulation.
The Council shall ensure that the collection, storage, processing, and disclosure of
data comply with applicable laws on data privacy, confidentiality, and cybersecurity.



      Failure to submit required data, submission of false or misleading
information, or deliberate obstruction of monitoring activities shall constitute a
violation of this Act.

      SEC 13. Early Warning and Market Alert Mechanism. The Council shall
establish and operate an integrated Early Warning and Market Alert Mechanism that
utilizes price trends, supply and demand forecasts, inventory levels, weather and
climate information, disaster risk assessments, and other relevant indicators. It shall



                                                                                     9
promptly issue advisories and recommend appropriate interventions to concerned
government agencies, local government units, and stakeholders.

      SEC 14. Emergency Market Intervention Authority. Upon a determination
by the Council, supported by evidence-based assessment, that severe or abnormal
price volatility, food supply disruption, or market failure exists or is imminent, the
Council may authorize or recommend the implementation of one or more of the
following interventions, subject to existing laws and regulations:

             (a) Release of government-held buffer stocks;

             (b) Strategic procurement and emergency purchases;

             (c) Temporary transportation, logistics, storage, and distribution
             support;

             (d) Direct farmer-to-consumer market operations;

             (e) Localized food distribution programs in coordination with local
             government units;

             (f) Temporary importation recommendations when domestic supply
             shortages have been verified and after consultation with affected
             stakeholders and relevant agencies;

             (g) Emergency food assistance programs for vulnerable populations;
             and

             (h) Other temporary and targeted measures necessary to stabilize prices
             and ensure food availability.

      All interventions shall be proportionate, time-bound, transparent, and subject
to periodic review by the Council.

                                     ARTICLE V
                  SAFEGUARDS AND ACCOUNTABILITY

      SEC 15. Transparency and Public Disclosure.            All interventions, fund
disbursements, procurement activities, contracts, grants, subsidies, market
operations, buffer stock transactions, and other actions undertaken pursuant to this
Act shall be subject to full transparency and public accountability. The Council shall
establish and maintain a publicly accessible online transparency portal containing
timely and updated information on approved interventions, utilization of funds,
procurement notices and awards, suppliers and beneficiaries, inventory levels of
government-held buffer stocks, price monitoring reports, audit findings, and other
relevant documents, subject to existing laws on data privacy, national security, and
confidentiality of commercially sensitive information. The Council shall likewise
publish periodic reports on the implementation and outcomes of all market
stabilization measures to ensure informed public participation, independent
monitoring, and effective oversight.

      SEC 16. Anti-Corruption Safeguards. All procurement, contracting, fund
utilization, disbursement, and financial transactions undertaken under this Act shall
strictly comply with the provisions of Republic Act No. 9184, as amended, otherwise
known as the Government Procurement Reform Act, its implementing rules and
regulations, and all applicable auditing, budgeting, financial management, anti-graft,
anti-corruption, and accountability laws. The Council shall adopt internal control
mechanisms, risk management systems, independent monitoring procedures, and




                                                                                   11
safeguards against fraud, collusion, conflict of interest, overpricing, misuse of funds,
and other irregularities.

      Any public officer, employee, contractor, supplier, or private individual found
to have participated in the misappropriation, diversion, misuse, or unlawful
utilization of funds or resources under this Act shall be subject to administrative,
civil, and criminal liability in accordance with existing laws.

      SEC 17. Congressional Oversight.            The appropriate committees of the
Senate and House of Representatives shall exercise continuing oversight over the
implementation of this Act. The Council shall submit an annual report to Congress
on agri-food market conditions, interventions undertaken, fund utilization, and
recommendations, and shall provide such information and records as may be
necessary for the conduct of oversight and policy review.

             (b) Willfully refusing or unjustifiably failing to submit information,

             appropriate authority;

amended), the Philippine Competition Act (Republic Act No. 10667), and other
applicable statutes.
