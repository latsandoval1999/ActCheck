27                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
28
29          The pandemic has brought to the fore a major weakness in the country’s system
30   of mass transportation. Various studies found that 72% of Filipinos found it difficult to
31   go to work when the COVID-19 pandemic hit and the lockdowns were implemented
32   and even afterwards—and majority of them are ordinary workers and employees in the
33   private and public sectors. These are those who do not own cars or even motorcycles,
34   who cannot afford to ride in taxis or book ride-hailing services. Due to the long-time
35   stoppage of the operations of buses, jeepney, FX vans, and the MRT and LRT, 87% of
36   Filipinos in 2020 have hoped that the government would accord the public
37   transportation system a higher priority, rather than private vehicles.
38
39          According to the Move as One Coalition and UP School of Urban and Regional
40   Planning, 70% of road users in Metro Manila rely on public transportation but they are
41   only able to use 22% of the road space. On the other hand, nearly a third of private
42   vehicles (30%)—just a small minority—of those on the road use private vehicles, and
43   they are given rein over almost 80% of the road space. It is estimated that there is not
44   much difference in other parts of the country.
45




                                                                                                           1
 1          Otherwise put, majority of Filipinos are commuters, but they are crammed into
 2   a very small portion of the roads. No wonder, sardinas-like public utility jeepneys,
 3   buses, and vans as well as the MRT and LRT trains are common. Lines or clusters of
 4   people waiting for rides, oftentimes spilling over into the roads, are the average sight
 5   everyday.
 6
 7          Move as One continued with a forecast: Even if all projects under the ambitious
 8   and much-touted Build, Build, Build Program were completed, there will be a “transport
 9   supply shortage” come 2030. Therefore, there is an urgent need for “sustainable
10   mobility”—and one of the first measures towards that is laying down policies,
11   mechanisms, and infrastructure for a safe, healthful, budget-friendly, and fast mode of
12   transportation: bicycling.
13
14          Many have discovered and rediscovered bicycling in the past few years. In just
15   the education sector, many teachers and education support personnel “padyak their way
16   to school,” deliver modules, and do their various tasks. But they do not feel that the
17   appropriate recognition is accorded to cyclists and the bicycle as road users just like
18   everyone else, especially private vehicles. In fact, the Metropolitan Manila
19   Development Authority is finding ways to either narrow or remove the bicycle lanes,
20   or share it with motorcycles. As their complaint goes, “Tao din kami, pwedeng gumamit
21   ng kalsada. Gulong din kami, may karapatang umarangkada nang ligtas sa daan.”
22
23          This Bill aims to introduce the Philippine Bicycle Act to serve as the “unang
24   padyak,” the first ride towards a more mass-based system of transportation, in which
25   the respect and recognition to cyclists and the protection for their rights can be seen not
26   just in words but also in the policies, even in the infrastructure or the physical
27   components of the system. May this proposal give way for policies from the concerned
28   agencies, as well as other laws for safe cycling.
29
30           We aim, beginning with the Philippine Bicycle Act, to get more bikes out in the
31   streets, to make the roads safer for cyclists, and to promote a more health-friendly,
32   environment-friendly, and sustainable mode of transport.
33
34          This Bill is borne out of consolidated proposals in the 18th Congress for the above
35   mentioned objectives, and incorporated various inputs from bicycling advocates such
36   as Move As One. Among the salient provisions are the institutionalization of the bicycle
37   as a regular mode of transportation, the establishment of protected bike lanes in all
38   primary and secondary roads for the exclusive use of cyclists, a “hierarchy of road
39   users,” and the introduction of the rights of cyclists, such as the right to own and use
40   bicycles, parity of rights with drivers of motorized vehicles, and the right to be protected
41   from street harassment.
42
43           Also incorporated is the proposal of ACT Teachers and the Makabayan bloc in
44   the bill for Minimum Overtaking of Cyclists Distance Act which obliges all drivers of
45   motor vehicles to maintain a minimum overtaking distance of 1.5 meter from cyclists.
46   This particular bill was approved on third reading during the 17th Congress.

 3   Program under the Department of Transportation, and the granting of incentives such
 4   as tax reliefs and benefits, including a ₱2,500 tax deduction for employees using
 5   bicycles, and the observance of the World Car-Free Day and National Car-Free Day.
 6
 7          The Committee on Transportation gave this Bill a green light during the 18 th
 8   Congress. Hopefully, this 20th Congress will set the wheels in motion for safer cycling,
 9   make it less expensive and prohibitive for the ordinary Filipino, and raise the status of
10   cyclists as road users equal to all other road users. Approval of this Bill is therefore
11   earnestly sought.

28
29   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “The Philippine Bicycle Act.”
30
31   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – In conformity with the provisions of the Constitution
32   to promote the general welfare and social justice in all phases of national development,
33   protect and advance the right of the Filipino people to a balanced and healthful ecology
34   in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature, and to conserve and develop the
35   patrimony of the nation, the State shall:
36
37       a) Prioritize the welfare and safety of the commuting public, especially pedestrians,
38          cyclists, women, elderly, persons with disabilities, and children;
39
40       b) Promote a shift towards a safe, collective, efficient, non-congestive, non-
41          pollutive, and healthful locomotion and transportation system that includes the
42          use of bicycles;
43
44       c) Ensure that the needs and safety of all road users, particularly the pedestrians
45          and cyclists, are fully integrated into the planning, design, operation, and
46          maintenance of the country’s transportation system and infrastructure; and




                                                                                                           1
 1
 2      d) Establish its plans and programs in conformity with the overall national land use
 3         plan as well as the local comprehensive land use plans.
 4
 5   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the term:
 6
 7      a) Bicycle or bike refers to a non-motorized vehicle consisting of a light frame
 8         mounted on two wheels, one behind the other, and having a seat, handlebars for
 9         steering, brakes and two pedals;
10
11      b) Bicycle lane or bikeway refers to any longitudinal strips designated for the
12         passage of a bicycle or other personal mobility device;
13
14      c) Cyclist or PMD user refers to a person who drives or operates a bicycle or any
15         other PMD, respectively;
16
17      d) Motor vehicle refers to any vehicle driven by a motor or engine and operates on
18         a road or highway;
19
20      e) Personal Mobility Devices (PMD) refers to devices upon which any person may
21         ride and is propelled either solely or assisted by human power, which shall
22         include bicycles, personal assistive mobility devices, scooters, skateboards, and
23         the like; Provided, that for purposes of this act, a PMD shall not weigh more than
24         100 kilograms;
25
26      f) Road refers to a facility used for the movement of vehicles, pedestrians, and
27         other conveyances; and
28
29      g) Slow street refers to a street or road wherein the minimum width prescribed for
30         bicycle lanes may not be feasible, and where speed limits at low levels shall be
31         required among motor vehicles to allow for safe mixing with bicycle traffic.
32
33   SEC. 4. Coverage. – For purposes of this Act, provisions pertaining to bicycles and
34   cyclists shall likewise apply to personal mobility devices and their users.
35
36   SEC. 5. Inclusion of Bicycle as Regular Mode of Transportation in the National
37   Transportation Plan. – Within six (6) months from the issuance of rules and regulations
38   implementing the provisions of this Act, the Department of Transportation (DOTr), in
39   coordination with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and
40   Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev), shall come up with a
41   National Bike Program (Program). The Program shall provide a roadmap for national
42   and local bicycle transportation system to be adhered to at all levels throughout the
43   country.
44
45        The Program shall include the plans and strategies for the construction, provision
46   and maintenance of bicycle lanes, parking areas and other support facilities. It shall




                                                                                           2
 1   also provide for the standards for bicycles and regulations for the exercise and
 2   protection of rights of cyclists.
 3
 4   SEC. 6. Local Bicycle Office. – A Local Bicycle Office (LBO) is hereby created in
 5   every city or municipality and shall have the following powers, functions and
 6   responsibilities:
 7
 8      a) Manage the construction and maintenance of bicycle lanes and road signages,
 9         including the identification of bicycle networks within the jurisdiction of the
10         locality;
11
12      b) Manage the bike park or parks;
13
14      c) Ensure that bicycle lanes are free of any obstruction that can compromise the
15         safety of cyclists and pedestrians;
16
17      d) Enforce the establishment of bicycle parking and other related facilities;
18
19      e) Encourage partnership with local and foreign organizations to facilitate easy
20         access to bicycles; and
21
22      f) Conduct education and information drives on the benefits of using bicycles in
23         coordination with the Land Transportation Office (LTO) and the Health
24         Promotion Bureau of the Department of Health in order to promote the usage of
25         the same.
26
27         The staffing pattern of the LBO shall be determined by the city or the
28   municipality concerned.
29
30   SEC. 7. Designation of Bicycle Lanes. – There shall be designated bicycle lanes in all
31   primary and secondary roads for the exclusive use of cyclists.
32
33           The bike lanes shall be separated by physical barriers and shall be clearly
34   identified with signs or pavement markings. In the planning, configuration, and
35   establishment of bicycle lanes, the national and local governments shall thoroughly
36   consider existing traffic levels and behaviors of the particular road or street, adequate
37   safety buffers to protect cyclists from parked and moving vehicles, and enforcement to
38   prohibit motorized vehicle encroachment and double parking. In cases where the
39   installation of a physical barrier is not feasible due to limited space, the lane for bicycles
40   shall be identified through striping, signage, and pavement markings with reflectorized
41   paint. Existing car lanes shall be converted to bicycle lanes whenever necessary.
42
43           The national government and all local governments shall ensure that slow streets
44   are likewise present wherever bicycle lanes are not feasible. For this purpose, the LBO
45   and LTO shall evaluate and recommend candidate roads and streets and facilitate their
46   transition into slow streets within thirty days after effectivity of this Act.

 1
 2          Designation of bicycle lanes shall in no way take away or diminish the right of
 3   cyclists to use public roads outside of said lanes, provided that cyclists observe
 4   applicable traffic rules and regulations.
 5
 6   SEC. 8. Bicycle Parking Facilities. – All public places, government offices, schools,
 7   major business establishments, including malls, banks, restaurants, hospitals, and the
 8   like, are hereby required to provide bicycle racks for bicycle parking.
 9
10          Bicycle parking should be secure, visible, accessible, and segregated from
11   pedestrians and motor vehicles.
12
13   SEC. 9. Other Related Bicycle Facilities. – In addition to the bike lanes and bike
14   parking, the following related facilities shall be provided to ensure that cyclists and their
15   bicycles can travel safely and to lessen if not avoid collision incidents with pedestrians
16   and other vehicles:
17
18      a) Sidewalk improvements;
19      b) Traffic calming and speed reduction improvements;
20      c) Pedestrian and bicycle crossing improvements;
21      d) Traffic signage pertaining to bicycles;
22      e) Off-street pedestrian and bicycle facilities; and
23      f) Traffic diversion improvements.
24
25   SEC. 10. Bike Parks. – Each city or municipality, through the LBO and the City or
26   Municipal Engineer’s Office, shall create and establish a bicycle park where facilities
27   such as but not limited to bikeways, bicycle parking areas and similar infrastructure are
28   available. The bicycle parks shall be funded by the local government unit (LGU)
29   concerned by allocating thirty percent (30%) of their respective revenues raised from
30   the miscellaneous activities like parking and license fees.
31
32   SEC. 11. Rights of Cyclists. – All cyclists shall enjoy:
33
34      a) the right to own a bicycle and use the same subject to such limitations as may be
35         provided by law;
36
37      b) the right to ride on any public road or street;
38
39      c) the rights granted to drivers of motorized vehicles, except those that by their
40         nature are inapplicable; and
41
42      d) the right to be protected from street harassment or any other conduct, whether
43         from public agents or private persons, that violates Republic Act 11313 or the
44         Safe Spaces Act and other applicable laws and regulations.
45




                                                                                                4
 1   SEC. 12. Equal Right to Use The Road; Hierarchy of Road Users. All roads and
 2   streets being shared spaces, every road user shall have the equal right to use the road
 3   along with other users.
 4
 5         Pursuant to the principles of protecting the most vulnerable road users and
 6   promoting sustainable modes of transport, the following hierarchy of road users shall
 7   be observed:
 8
 9          1) Pedestrians and persons with disabilities;
10          2) Cyclists and other users of PMDs;
11          3) Public transport
12          4) Motorcyclists;
13          5) General traffic.
14
15           All government agencies shall likewise consider this hierarchy in road
16   infrastructure planning, land use planning, formulation of rules and regulations on road
17   use, and other related activities.
18
19   SEC. 13. Obligations and Responsibilities of Cyclists. – Every cyclist and PMD user
20   must comply with the following duties and obligations:
21
22      a) Obedience to the applicable traffic rules and traffic signs and to drive responsibly
23         with extreme care and caution at all times;
24
25      b) At all times and before mounting to drive a bicycle, ensuring that the same is
26         fully equipped and in good running condition; and
27
28      c) While riding a bicycle, ensure that he or she is equipped with basic protective
29         gear. This may include pads, helmet and other accessories such as reflectors and
30         lightings. For this purpose, the national government, local governments, and
31         employers are highly encouraged to provide protective gear for the general
32         public and their employees, respectively.
33
34   SEC. 14. Limitations on the Use of Bicycles. – The use of bicycles and other PMDS
35   shall be subject to the following limitations and such other limitations as may be
36   provided in the National Bike Program:
37
38      a) No bicycle shall carry more persons than it was designed to carry except when
39         another regular seat or trailer towed by the bicycle is present;
40
41      b) Persons riding a bicycle shall not cling to another bicycle or vehicle while in the
42         designated bikeway; and
43
44      c) No person shall operate a bicycle or any other PMD at a speed greater than what
45         is reasonable and prudent under the prevailing conditions. Cyclists and other
46         PMD users shall observe the speed limits at all times.

 2   SEC. 15. Yielding Right-Of-Way. – Motorists shall yield the right of way to cyclists.
 3   Every motorist who is about to enter or cross a roadway shall yield the right of way to
 4   cyclists.
 5
 6   SEC. 16. Overtaking of Motor Vehicles. – In the case of bicycle lanes not secured by
 7   physical barriers, the driver of a motor vehicle overtaking and passing a bicycle that is
 8   travelling in the same direction shall maintain a minimum overtaking distance of one
 9   and a half (1.5) meters between any part of the motor vehicle he or she is driving and
10   any part of the bicycle or its cyclist.
11
12   SEC. 17. Prohibition on the Use of Bicycle Lanes by Motor Vehicles, Exceptions. –
13   Whenever a bicycle lane or bikeway has been established on a road, no motor vehicle
14   shall be driven on the bikeway, except when necessary to enter or leave such road or to
15   make a turn.
16
17   SEC. 18. “Bike to Work” Program. – The DOTr shall establish a program that gives
18   incentives to private and government offices that encourage their employees to ride their
19   bicycles to work by providing bicycle facilities, such as lockers and showers, within
20   their establishments.
21
22   SEC. 19. Incentives. – In order to encourage the use of bicycle as a regular mode of
23   transportation, this Act grants the following tax reliefs and benefits:
24
25      a) Employees who shall regularly and mainly use bicycles as means of transport to
26         and from their places of work shall be entitled to an annual tax deduction of Ten
27         Thousand Five Hundred Pesos (₱10,000.00) from their gross income. The said
28         tax deduction shall be reviewed and updated every three years by the DOTr;
29
30      b) Expenses incurred by employers or schools in providing their employees or
31         students by way of benefits, bicycles, related equipment and bike-friendly
32         facilities shall be deductible in full from their gross income;
33
34      c) Expenses incurred by companies, business establishments, and other institutions
35         by reason of their bike-friendly programs and facilities such as secure parking
36         spaces, showers, and repair stations shall be deductible in full from their gross
37         income; and
38
39      d) Bicycles granted by employers to its managerial and supervisory employees in
40         compliance with this Act shall not be taxable as fringe benefits as provided under
41         Republic Act 8424, as amended.
42
43          For purposes of this Act, the qualified persons, companies, institutions desiring
44   to avail of the benefits of this Act shall substantiate their claim for incentives with
45   sufficient evidence, such as logbook entries, official receipts, affidavits or other
46   adequate records.

 2   SEC. 20. Financing. – The DOTr, in coordination with the Department of Environment
 3   and Natural Resources (DENR), shall explore the possibility of accessing a growing
 4   range of global environment funds in order to be able to assist in the funding of
 5   sustainable public transport systems and less polluting energy supplies.
 6
 7   SEC. 21. Grants. – In carrying out the provisions of this Act, the Secretary of DOTr
 8   may extend grants to local government units and non-governmental organizations that
 9   the Secretary may determine suitably equipped and organized to carry out the objectives
10   and requirements of this Act.
11
12   SEC. 22. Observance of the Carless Day. – To promote the use of bicycles, the country
13   shall participate in the annual celebration of World Car-Free Day every second Sunday
14   of September and observe the National Car-Free Day every second Sunday of April.
15
16         The Secretary of DOTr is hereby mandated to determine the primary and
17   secondary roads which shall be carless during these annual celebrations.
18
19   SEC. 23. Penalties. – The following penalties shall be imposed upon the persons who
20   act in violation of this Act:
21
22      a) Any person who violates any provision of this Act shall suffer a penalty of fine
23         in an amount not less than Five Thousand Pesos (₱5,000.00) nor more than Ten
24         Thousand Pesos (₱10,000.00). If such violation causes damage or injury to
25         person or property, then the appropriate provisions of the Civil Code or the
26         Revised Penal Code (RPC) shall apply. When the offender is a corporation, the
27         officers responsible for the violation shall be meted with the penalties herein
28         provided.
29
30      b) Any public works official who approves the construction of main roads or
31         highways without the required bicycle lanes in their designs shall be punished
32         by a fine of not less than ten thousand pesos (₱10,000.00) nor more than Thirty
33         Thousand Pesos (₱30,000.00) or suspension, or both, if found guilty in an
34         appropriate administrative investigation. The same penalty applies to a public
35         work official who is duty-bound to mark the existing main roads and highways
36         but refuses or fails to do so.
37
38      c) Any person who shall violate Section 17 of this Act shall be penalized with:
39           i) A fine of Five Thousand Pesos (₱5,000.00) for the first offense;
40          ii) A fine of Ten Thousand Pesos (₱10,000.00) for the second offense;
41         iii) A fine of Fifteen Thousand Pesos (₱15,000.00) and suspension of driver’s
42               license for three (3) months for the third offense; and
43         iv) A fine of Twenty Thousand Pesos (₱20,000.00) and revocation of driver’s
44               license for the fourth offense:
