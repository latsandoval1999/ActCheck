                        AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL BARANGAY HEALTH EQUITY INDEX,
MANDATING THE DEVELOPMENT OF A HEALTH EQUITY
SCORECARD FOR EVERY BARANGAY TO IDENTIFY DISPARITIES
AND GUIDE TARGETED INTERVENTIONS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution recognizes health as a fundamental right of every Filipino
and mandates the State to adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health
development. In the current health landscape, landmark to this obligation is the
enactment of Republic Act No. 11223, or otherwise known as the “Universal Health
Care Act” which further institutionalizes a system that guarantees equitable access
to quality and affordable health care for all Filipinos1. Despite these commitments,
significant health disparities persist across communities, particularly at the barangay
level.

       Many barangays continue to experience unequal access to health services due
to variations in socioeconomic conditions, geographic isolation, infrastructure gaps,

and uneven distribution of health personnel and resources. These disparities
contribute to differences in health outcomes such as maternal and child mortality,
disease burden, nutrition status, and access to preventive services2. However, current
national and local planning mechanisms often lack a standardized, granular tool that
systematically measures and compares these inequalities across barangays.

       This proposed measure seeks to address this gap through the establishment of
a Barangay Health Equity Index (BHEI) and a corresponding Barangay Health
Equity Scorecard for every barangay in the country. The BHEI will serve as a
composite, data-driven index that measures key indicators of health status, disease
burden, access to services, and the social determinants of health. Meanwhile, the
Scorecard will function as an annual performance and monitoring tool that identifies
priority health gaps, vulnerabilities, and necessary interventions at the barangay
level.

       By institutionalizing a standardized equity-based measurement system, this
measure will enable the government to adopt more evidence-driven health planning,
targeted interventions, and responsive resource allocation. The BHEI and Scorecard
will guide national agencies and local government units (LGUs) in prioritizing
communities with the greatest health needs, strengthening primary care networks,
improving service delivery, and ensuring that health investments are directed where
they are most needed.

       Moreover, the proposed system will promote transparency and accountability
in local health governance. The public availability of barangay-level health
scorecards will empower communities to participate in monitoring health outcomes
and encourage stronger coordination among the Department of Health, LGUs, and
other relevant stakeholders.

       Ultimately, the establishment of a Barangay Health Equity Index will provide
the national government and local governments with a powerful policy instrument
to systematically identify and reduce health inequities across the country. Through
better data, more strategic planning, and targeted public health action, this measure
aims to strengthen the implementation of Universal Health Care and ensure that no
community is left behind in the pursuit of health equity.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Health
Equity Index Act”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and ensure equitable



                                                                                  1
access to quality health services, in accordance with Article II, Section 15 of the
1987 Constitution, and Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the Universal
Health Care Act.


      The State recognizes that health disparities across barangays persist due to
differences in socioeconomic conditions, service availability, disease burden, and
geographic constraints. To ensure equity-based planning, evidence-driven resource
allocation, and targeted public health interventions, there is a need to develop a
standardized index that measures the health status, vulnerabilities, and service gaps
at the barangay level.


      In line with this policy, the State shall establish a Barangay Health Equity
Index (BHEI) and corresponding Health Equity Scorecard for every barangay,
serving as the national foundation for health planning, budgeting, monitoring, and
policy reforms.


      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
      (a) Barangay Health Equity Index (BHEI) refers to a composite, data-driven
      index measuring a barangay’s health status, service access, disease burden,
      and social determinants of health.
      (b) Barangay Health Equity Scorecard refers to the annual performance report
      reflecting a barangay’s BHEI rating, gaps, health risks, service utilization, and
      recommended interventions.
      (c) Health Disparity refers to preventable differences in health outcomes,
      disease prevalence, service access, and health risks between population
      groups or communities.


                                                                                     2
      (d) Social Determinants of Health (SDH) include non-medical factors such as
      income, education, nutrition, housing, sanitation, and geographic accessibility
      that influence health outcomes.


      SECTION 4. Creation of the Barangay Health Equity Index (BHEI). – The
Department of Health (DOH), through the Health Policy Development and Planning
Bureau (HPDPB), the Epidemiology Bureau, and the Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth), shall jointly develop the Barangay Health Equity Index,
which shall include, but not be limited to, the following indicators:


      (a) Maternal, neonatal, and child health outcomes;
      (b) Incidence of communicable and non-communicable diseases
      (c) Access to primary care services and health personnel;
      (d) Nutrition, sanitation, and environmental health indicators;
      (e) Immunization coverage and preventive service uptake;
      (f) Disease surveillance responsiveness;
      (g) Geographic and socioeconomic vulnerabilities;
      (h) Emergency and disaster-health risk factors; and
      (i) Any other metrics determined by DOH.


      Provided that, The BHEI shall be computed annually and shall serve as the
baseline assessment tool for local health systems.


      SECTION 5. Barangay Health Equity Scorecard. –
      (a) Annual Preparation and Issuance – Every barangay shall maintain an
          Annual Barangay Health Equity Scorecard, which shall be generated and



                                                                                   3
   validated by the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the
   City or Municipal Health Office (CMHO/MHO), and with the
   participation of the Barangay Health Committee. The Scorecard shall serve
   as the official instrument for assessing and monitoring the barangay’s
   health status, disparities, and system performance.

(b) Contents of the Scorecard. — The Scorecard shall contain, at a
    minimum, the following components:
         i. The barangay’s annual Barangay Health Equity Index (BHEI)
         rating, including sub-indices for service access, disease burden,
         social determinants of health, and health system capacity;
         ii. Identification of priority health gaps and vulnerabilities, including
         high-risk populations, disease clusters, and geographic or
         socioeconomic barriers;
         iii. A list of targeted interventions and priority actions required to
         address the identified disparities, aligned with DOH standards and
         the Universal Health Care (UHC) framework;
         iv.. A performance assessment of the barangay’s health service
         delivery, including preventive services, primary care utilization,
         emergency response, health promotion, and community
         mobilization;
         v. A record of health financing, budget utilization, and resource
         allocation, including compliance with national and local health
         budget mandates;
         vi. A review of health human resources at the barangay level,
         including the availability and training status of Barangay Health
         Workers (BHWs), volunteers, and assigned primary care staff;
         vii. Community feedback and citizen satisfaction metrics, as
         gathered through DOH or LGU mechanisms; and
         viii. Recommendations for improvement, including technical,
         financial, and programmatic measures.

(c) Integration into Local Health Planning — The Scorecard shall be
    incorporated into the Local Investment Plan for Health (LIPH), the Annual


                                                                                4
         Operational Plan (AOP), and other local development and sectoral plans.
         LGUs shall use the Scorecard as a primary basis for determining annual
         priorities, budget allocation, and performance targets in accordance with
         UHC.

      (d) Transparency and Public Access. — The Scorecard shall be made
          publicly accessible through barangay posting, online publication by the
          LGU, and submission to the DOH for inclusion in the National Health
          Equity Database.


      SECTION 6. Duties of the Department of Health (DOH). – Under this Act,
the Department of Health (DOH) shall;

      (a) Develop, operationalize, and maintain a National Barangay Health Equity
         Index (BHEI) database, including standardized indicators, digital tools,
         and secure data systems covering all barangays nationwide;
      (b) Issue detailed guidelines, methodologies, and standards for index
         development, measurement, validation, and periodic updating, consistent
         with the Universal Health Care Act and global best practices;
      (c) Provide technical assistance and capacity-building to LGUs, including
         training on data collection, Scorecard preparation, analysis, planning, and
         utilization of BHEI results for policy and budgeting purposes;
      (d) Monitor and evaluate LGU compliance, including accuracy of data
         submitted, utilization of Scorecards, and progress in addressing health
         disparities;
      (e) Publish an Annual National Health Equity Report, which shall include
         national, regional, provincial, city/municipal, and barangay-level equity
         analyses, trends, and comparative performance results;


                                                                                  5
      (f) Recommend policy, regulatory, programmatic, and budgetary adjustments
         to national agencies and LGUs based on BHEI findings and disparities
         identified;
      (g) Coordinate with PhilHealth, Department of the Interior and Local
         Government (DILG), Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), and other
         relevant agencies to harmonize data standards, avoid duplication, and
         ensure coherence of national health information systems; and
      (h) Submit an annual report to Congress, detailing national and local
         performance, disparities addressed, and policy recommendations.

      SECTION 7. Duties of Local Government Units (LGUs) – The Local
Government Units (LGUs), through the Provincial, City or Municipal Health Offices
and Barangay Local Government Units, shall;


      (a). Collect, consolidate, and submit complete and accurate health data to the
      DOH within prescribed timelines, using standardized tools and reporting
      mechanisms;
      (b) Utilize the BHEI and Scorecard as the primary evidence base in preparing
      local development plans, investment plans for health, annual budgets, and
      program implementation strategies;
      (c) Prioritize funding, programs, personnel, and interventions for barangays
      with low BHEI ratings or classified as high-risk or high-vulnerability
      communities;
      (d) Ensure barangay-level dissemination and access to the Scorecard for
      transparency, accountability, and community engagement;




                                                                                  6
      (e) Mobilize Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) and community
      organizations in data collection, monitoring, and implementation of
      interventions identified in the Scorecard;
      (f) Integrate BHEI findings into local disaster risk reduction and emergency
      health response plans, particularly for vulnerable barangays; and
      (g) Comply with DOH reporting, validation, and audit requirements as part of
      national health equity monitoring.


      SECTION 8. Integration with Universal Health Care (UHC). — The BHEI
and the Barangay Health Equity Scorecard shall serve as mandatory reference
instruments for national and local UHC implementation. Specifically, they shall be
used in:


      (a). Primary Care Provider Network (PCPN) planning and configuration,
      including service catchments, facility placement, and referral pathways;
      (b). PhilHealth benefit distribution, capitation, and performance-based
      payments, ensuring that funding reflects equity needs and disparities among
      barangays;
      (c). DOH and LGU resource allocation, including grants, health facility
      enhancement, commodities distribution, and deployment of medical and
      health personnel;
      (d). Prioritization and deployment of health human resources, including
      doctors to the barrios, nurses, midwives, and other cadres needed to address
      barangay-level disparities; and




                                                                                 7
(e). Local health investment prioritization, including facility development,
equipment procurement, community programs, and health promotion
activities, consistent with the UHC framework.


SECTION 9: Sanction and Penalties. –
(a). Administrative Liability of Public Officials – Any barangay, municipal,
city, or provincial official, as well as any public officer of the Department of
Health (DOH), who, without justifiable cause, fails, refuses, or neglects to
comply with the data submission, reporting, planning integration, or
implementation requirements under this Act shall be administratively liable
in accordance with Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991),
Republic Act No. 6713 (Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public
Officials and Employees), and relevant Civil Service rules. Administrative
sanctions may include:
      i. Written reprimand for first offense;
      ii. Suspension of not less than thirty (30) days but not more than one
      (1) year for second offense; and
      iii. Dismissal from service, including accessory penalties of perpetual
      disqualification from public office, forfeiture of benefits, and
      cancellation of eligibility, for third or subsequent offenses.


(b). Criminal Liability for Falsification or Deliberate Misreporting – Any
public official or employee who knowingly falsifies, manipulates, conceals,
or materially alters health data, BHEI indicators, or Barangay Health Equity
Scorecards shall be liable under Articles 171 and 172 of the Revised Penal




                                                                               8
   Code, punishable by prision correccional to prision mayor, and shall likewise
   be subject to administrative dismissal.


(c). Liability for Obstruction. — Any person who knowingly obstructs, delays,
or prevents the lawful collection of health data, validation of indicators, or
implementation of BHEI interventions shall, after due process, be liable to:
   i.    An administrative fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos
         (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
         (₱200,000.00); and
   ii.   Suspension or revocation of accreditation if the offender is a private
         health provider or contracted entity.


(d) Penalties for Misuse of Funds. — Any public official who misappropriates,
   diverts, or illegally uses funds intended for BHEI-related programs,
   monitoring activities, or interventions shall be held liable under Articles 217
   and 220 of the Revised Penal Code, Republic Act No. 3019 (Anti-Graft and
   Corrupt Practices Act), and other applicable laws, without prejudice to
   administrative sanctions.


(e) Penalties for Non-Posting and Non-Disclosure. — Failure of any local
   government unit to publicly post the annual Barangay Health Equity
   Scorecard as required under this Act shall constitute simple neglect of duty,
   punishable under Civil Service rules. Repeated or willful non-posting shall
   constitute grave misconduct.

      SECTION 10. Whistleblower Protection. — Any public officer, employee,
health worker, or private individual who, in good faith, reports violations of this Act,
including falsification of data, obstruction of monitoring activities, misuse of funds,
or non-compliance with mandatory reporting, shall be granted full protection against
retaliatory actions such as harassment, demotion, suspension, removal, threats, or
discrimination. Whistleblowers shall be entitled to confidentiality, immunity from
administrative or disciplinary proceedings arising from their disclosures, and
immediate reinstatement with full restitution of benefits in cases of retaliatory acts.


      The DOH and the DILG shall establish a secure, confidential reporting
mechanism, including electronic and written channels, for whistleblower


                                                                                     10
submissions. Retaliation against whistleblowers shall constitute grave misconduct
and shall be punishable by dismissal from service, without prejudice to criminal
prosecution under existing laws.


      SECTION 11. Reporting and Congressional Oversight.              – A Joint
Congressional Oversight Committee on Health Equity (JCOCHE) is hereby created
to oversee the implementation of this Act. The Committee shall be composed of five
(5) Members each from the Senate and the House of Representatives.


      The Department of Health (DOH) shall submit to Congress annual Health
Equity Implementation Report containing national and regional BHEI trends,
compliance levels of LGUs, status of barangays with persistent low scores, fund
utilization reports and recommended policy adjustments.


      SECTION 12. Audit Mechanism. – The Commission on Audit (COA) shall
conduct an annual audit of all funds used for BHEI generation, data management,
capacity-building, and related interventions, and shall issue recommendations to
strengthen financial integrity and prevent misuse.


      Provided that, all audit findings, BHEI scores, barangay scorecards, and
performance ratings shall be made publicly accessible via a DOH-managed online
portal.


      SECTION 13. Funding. – The initial amount necessary for the development
and implementation of the BHEI shall be charged to the current appropriations of

DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be required shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act (GAA).


      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with DILG,
PhilHealth, PSA, and LGU leagues, shall promulgate the IRR.


      SECTION 15. Separability Clause. If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


      SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, letters of instruction, proclamations or administrative regulations that are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or
modified accordingly.


      SECTION 17. Effectivity. – Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the
implementing rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
