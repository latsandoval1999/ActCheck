EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Healthy Food Marketing Environment Actis proposed to
institutionalize a robust regulatory framework safeguarding public health-—-most
notably among Filipino children—from the aggressive commercialization of
obesogenic products.

The overarching philosophy of this legislation is rooted in the timeless
health adage that “prevention is better than cure.” In the context of modern public
health, treating chronic illnesses like cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and
cancers places a crushing financial and emotional burden on families and the
national healthcare infrastructure, with non-communicable diseases (NCDs)
accounting for roughly 68% to 70% of total deaths in the Philippines.

By shifting the focus upstream toward preventative policy, the State can
effectively intervene before lifestyle-related diseases develop. This preventative
stance is given urgency by recent data from the Department of Science and
Technology—Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI), which reveals
that around 44.5% of Filipino adults aged 20 to 59 are classified as overweight
or obese, reflecting a steady escalation in metabolic risk factors that demand
immediate systemic correction.

Food labeling serves as an indispensable pillar of this preventative strategy
by bridging the information gap between commercial food manufacturers and
everyday consumers. Traditional nutrition facts panels are frequently printed in
small text and complex jargon that discourage casual reading or require
specialized nutritional literacy to interpret.

Front-of-package warning labels (FOPWL) solve this by translating
complex data into immediate, easily understood visual cues—such as
prominent black octagons with white edges bearing warnings like “High in
Sugar” or “High in Sodium”—that cover at least 5% of the main package face.

===== [page 2] =====
Empowered with this immediately cognizable information, citizens and parents
can instantly recognize nutritional hazards and make healthier purchasing
choices at a glance. Furthermore, backed by the Pan American Health
Organization (PAHO) Nutrient Profile Model as an interim standard for maximum
nutrient thresholds, the legislation ensures that regulatory enforcement is
grounded in rigorous, evidence-based science.

In alignment with international commitments, this legislative measure
directly supports United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG 3: Good
Health and Well-being), which aims to ensure healthy lives and promote well-
being for all ages, specifically by targeting the reduction of premature mortality
from non-communicable diseases through prevention and treatment.

Ultimately, by combining clear front-of-package transparency with strict
limitations on child-directed marketing in schools, public spaces, and digital
platforms, this measure establishes a protective environment that stops litestyle
diseases before they start, safeguarding the well-being of future generations of
Filipinos.

ARTICLE I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Healthy Food Marketing
Environment Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of all Filipinos and instill health consciousness
among them. It is likewise the policy of the State to protect and promote the right
of every child to the enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of health and
to defend their right to proper care and nutrition, which includes the right
to healthy environments.

The State recognizes the need to enable all Filipinos to make informed food
choices and avoid food and beverages that are harmful to one's health in line
with its policy to protect consumers from trade malpractices and substandard or
hazardous food products.

The State also recognizes the urgent need to protect children from
obesogenic food environments that promote the sale and marketing of unhealthy
food and beverages, such as foods high in saturated and trans fat, sodium, and
sugar.

ARTICLE II
FRONT-OF-PACKAGE WARNING LABELS

SEC. 6. Mandatory Front-of-Package Warning Labels. — No person shail sell,
manufacture, import, export, or distribute pre-packaged food without a front-of-
package warning label as prescribed in this Act, when the final composition of
the product contains nutrients of concern that exceed the maximum values
prescribed under Section 8 of this Act.

SEC. 7. Form, Size, Placement, and Characteristics of the Warning
Label. — As prescribed by the FDA, the packaging shall display a warning label
when the final composition of the pre-packaged food product contains critical
nutrients or calories in excess of the maximum values under Section 8 of this
Act. In accordance with the following guidelines, the packaging shall include:

a) One (1) warning labelfor each nutrient of concern in excess of
prescribed values, namely:

"HIGH IN SUGAR';

e "HIGH IN SODIUM’;

"HIGH IN SATURATED FAT";

"HIGH IN TOTAL FAT";

b) the following warning label if the product's calories is in excess of the
prescribed vaiues:
e "HIGH IN CALORIES’;

c) the following warning label if the product contains sweeteners:
e "CONTAINS SWEETENERS, NOT RECOMMENDED FOR CHILDREN’;

d) a warning label and shall have the form of black octagons with a white edge
and capital letters;

e) a warning label on which the size is not less than five percent (5%) of the total
area of the main face of the packaging;
Warning labels shall:

a) form part of the packaging and its full visibility and integrity must not
be susceptible to being damaged, concealed, obstructed or changed by any
package design, feature, or mechanism, or covered by any other markings;

b) be located on the top right edge of the product's main face. If the shape
of the product is round or cylindrical, it must be on the top center of the
main face. For products in which the main face of the packaging is equal
to or less than ten (10) square centimeters, it will contain a single octagon
that will show inside of it how many nutrients of concern above the

===== [page 10] =====
thresholds the product has. The warning label shall be 15% of the total
area of the front of the package;

c) Be placed adjacent to each other on the product's main face if the food
or beverage has more than one (1) warning label;

d) Be included in the outer packaging, as well as each individual package
inside for packages with multiple products.

The provisions established in this Section are applied in addition to the
existing rules and regulations governing the labeling of pre-packaged food
distributed in the Philippines. In addition, the FDA is encouraged to release
guidelines pertaining to the placement of products at the point of sale.

SEC. 8. Maximum Values of Nutrients of Concern for Pre-packaged Food. —
The Department of Health (DOH) shall develop or adopt a nutrient profile model
(NPM) to determine the maximum values of energy, sugar, sodium, saturated fat,
total fat, and other nutrients for pre-packaged food. Pending the development of
a local NPM, the Pan American Health Organization NPM shall be used as the
basis for such maximum values.

SEC. 9. Prohibition against Misleading Claims and _ Child-Directed
Marketing in the Packaging. — It is absolutely prohibited for all pre-packaged
food required to have a warning label under Section 6 and 7 of this Act to contain:

a) Complementary nutritional information;

b) Logotypes or phrases stating the sponsorship or endorsements of
scientific societies or civil associations;

c) Any other health and nutrition claim; and

d) Any advertisement or promotional feature that qualifies as child-
directed marketing as defined in this Act.

SEC. 10. Misbranding. — A violation under Sections 6, 7, or 9 of this Act shall
render the product misbranded under RA 9711. Pursuant to Section 11 of
Republic Act No. 3720 or the "Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act" (RA 3720), as
amended by Republic Act No. 9711 (RA 9711), the following acts are prohibited:

a) The manufacture, importation, exportation, sale, offering for sale,
distribution, transfer, non-consumer use, promotion, advertising, or

sponsorship of any food that is adulterated, unregistered or misbranded;

b} The adulteration or misbranding of any food.

===== [page 11] =====
SEC. 11. Prohibition against Concealment or Obstruction of Warning
Labels. — Pre-packaged food products required to have a warning label under
Section 6 and 7 shall be displayed with the warning labels in full view for the
consumers in retail stores. The concealment or obstruction of the warning labels
in retail stores, whether partial or whole, is prohibited.

Any concealment or obstruction of view of warning labels herein shall be
considered as a deceptive act or practice under Republic Act No. 7394 or the
“Consumer Act of the Philippines" (RA 7394) and is considered a prohibited act
therein.

SEC. 12. Exemptions. — Pre-packaged food for special therapeutic dietary uses
that are formulated for a particular physiological condition or as partial
replacement for meals, and culinary ingredients which typically consist of only
one nutrient in high purity (such as cooking oils, table sugars, common table
salt, etc.) shall follow their respective labeling requirements and shall be
exempted from the obligation to incorporate a nutritional warning label.
Breastmilk substitutes shall likewise be exempted from this Act and shall follow
the guidelines promulgated by Executive Order No. 51, series of 1986 (the
Philippine Milk Code), its implementing rules and regulations and other
pertinent regulations.

ARTICLE III
RESTRICTIONS ON THE MARKETING OF PRE-PACKAGED FOOD
PRODUCTS REQUIRED TO HAVE A WARNING LABEL

SEC. 13. Minimizing the Power of Child-Directed Marketing. — It is
absolutely prohibited to employ child-directed marketing or to initiate, produce,
publish, broadcast, or disseminate any marketing that employs child-directed
marketing techniques for all pre-packaged food required to have a warning label
under Section 6 of this Act. This prohibition includes, but is not limited to, such
child-directed marketing in the following settings:

a) Outdoor environments (such as billboards);

b) Retail environments (such as vending machines and in points-of-sale),
especially child-directed marketing within the direct line of sight of
children in groceries, supermarkets, and retail stores engaged in the sale
of pre-packaged food products in small-scale and commercial quantities;

c) Public spaces (such as public transportation terminals and _ utility
vehicles); and

d) Digital media and e-commerce platforms, such as, but not limited to,
social media platforms and mobile application platforms.

===== [page 12] =====
All persons who sell, manufacture, distribute, or import pre- packaged food
that are required to have a warning label herein are likewise prohibited from
engaging in sponsorship as defined in this Act. This prohibition shall also apply
to any person who receives or facilitates such sponsorship, including but not
limited to, the media or event organizer, venue owner, team owner, sportsperson,
celebrity, artist, or other performer.

The DOH is hereby authorized to identify and regularly publish a schedule
of forms, methods, and means of marketing that are considered child-directed
marketing under this Act, and other similar and analogous appeals to child-
directed marketing techniques for the guidance of relevant stakeholders.

SEC. 14. Reducing Exposure of Children to the Marketing of Pre-packaged
Food Products required to have a Warning Label.— It is absolutely
prohibited to market pre-packaged food products required to have a warning
label under Section 6 of this Act in the following settings:

a) In and within 250 meters from any point of the perimeter of child-
centered settings;

b) Between 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. for all programs, commercials, and
films shown in television with a rating other than R-18 by the Movie and
Television Review and Classification Board (MTRCB); and

c) Between 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. for all programs and commercials
broadcast on radio.

SEC. 20. Local Implementation. — LGUs, through appropriate issuances such
as ordinances or executive orders, shall establish mechanisms to enforce the
provisions under Sections 6, 7, 9, 13, and 14(a) of this Act, as applicable, within
their jurisdictions and shall impose penalties for violations thereof. For this
purpose, local health boards (LHBs) and local councils for the protection of
children (LCPCs) shall regularly monitor the compliance of LGUs with the
provisions of this Act.

All LGUs are enjoined to form Local Task Forces for Healthy Food
Marketing Environment to implement the provisions of this Act, and shall
mobilize local law enforcement to apprehend violators of this Act and to institute
the appropriate criminal proceedings therefor. Such task forces shall be led by a
local office with expertise on childhood nutrition and/or health promotion, and
shall include youth and civil society representation, which representation shall
strictly comply with Sections 24 and 25 of this Act. The LTF shall submit reports
to the NTF of any prohibited marketing herein denied, removed, disabled, or
acted upon, as well as complaints received from individuals or organizations,
annually beginning from the implementation of the prohibitions of this Act.

LGUs may mobilize the Special Health Fund under Republic Act No. 11223
or the "Universal Healthcare Act", among other resources available, to implement
local projects, programs, and activities consistent with this Act.

SEC. 21. Role of the Ad Standards Council and Other Entities. — The Ad
Standards Council (ASC) shall revise its standards, guidelines, codes of ethics,
and other rules and regulations in compliance with the provisions of this Act, no
later than one (1) year from its effectivity. Without prejudice to other provisions
of this Act. all entities involved in the marketing chain, whether analog or digital,
including digital media or e-commerce platforms shall:

a} Monitor and remove or disable any prohibited marketing upon notice by
the NTF that a violation of this Act may have occurred;

b) Establish an effective process and mechanism to receive and act on
complaints from individuals and organizations for violations of this Act as
may be required by NTF, and report to the NTF actions taken on said
complaints; and

c) Preserve relevant documents, websites, webpages, upon request of the
NTF.

SEC. 22. Inspection Powers and Record Keeping. — The FDA through its
registered agents shall have the power to inspect the premises and records of
manufacturers and distributors to determine compliance with the provisions of
Sections 6, 7, 9, or 10 of this Act. The FDA shall develop guidelines to prescribe
procedures and requirements for record-keeping.

SEC. 23. Compliance by Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). —
The DTI, in coordination with other relevant government agencies and
stakeholders, shall develop and implement programs to assist micro, small, and
medium-sized food business operators of pre-packaged food covered by this Act.

SEC. 24. Policy against Industry Interference. — Government, including
institutions and instrumentalities of the State at the national and sub-national
levels and to persons working in government or engaged by government to work
on its behalf, shall ensure that policy setting and implementation of laws that
ultimately aim to promote the right to health of all Filipinos, especially children,
are protected from the commercial and other vested interests of the food and
beverage industry.

SEC. 25. Declaration against Conflict of Interest. — All representatives of
government agencies and organizations, including civil society, non-government,
grassroots, and international organizations, that take any part, directly or
indirectly, in the setting and implementing of policies and measures that are part
of the strategic framework of measures to reduce overweight and obesity shall
be required to fully accomplish a signed declaration under oath prepared by the
DOH that states, among others, that they are not affiliated with the food and
beverage industry, and that they do not act on such industry's behalf or
interests. The signed and notarized copy of such declarations shall be made
available to the public primarily via the DOH website and other means to readily
access such information.

SEC. 26. Multisectoral Coordination and Civil Society Participation for
Enforcement, Monitoring and Surveillance. — The State recognizes the role
of civil society organizations and the private sector in promoting public health.
The NTF members in Section 18 herein may engage civil society organizations
and the private sector, as long as they are not affiliated or related with the food
and beverage industry and have signed a declaration against conflict of interest
described in Section 25, to provide technical assistance in implementing and
monitoring the provisions of this Act. The DOH shall develop and implement
programs to enable citizen participation in compliance with this Act.

Any citizen or civil society, non-government, and grassroots organization
is hereby authorized to file an appropriate civil, criminal, or administrative action
in the proper courts or administrative body against any public officer who
willfully or grossly neglects the performance of a responsibility specifically

enjoined by this Act and its implementing rules and regulations, or against any
member of the food and beverage industry, including persons working on their

and shall exempt the plaintiff from the filing of an injunction bond for the

The DOH shall develop and publicize mechanisms for filing anonymous

encourage citizen participation in the compliance monitoring and surveillance of
this Act.

SEC. 27. International Cooperation and Provision on Technical and
Financial Assistance. — The State recognizes that international and regional
cooperation is an important component of regulating cross-border marketing of
pre-packaged food products required to have a warning label.

For this purpose, NTF members may seek and receive technical and/or
financial assistance from international organizations, except those that belong
to or are affiliated with the food and beverage industry as defined herein, for the
purpose of implementing the provisions or pursuing the objectives of this Act,
subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations: Provided, That Sections 24 and
25 of this Act are strictly complied with.

SEC. 29. Other Corrective Actions. — In addition to the fines and penalties in
Section 28, the following corrective actions, if not initiated by the offenders
themselves, shall be imposed by the appropriate authorities to the offenders:

a) Seizure and condemnation, destruction, recall, and/or appropriate disposition
of pre-packaged food products that are considered misbranded under Section 9
of this Act, or items that constitute prohibited marketing under Sections 9 (d),
13 or 14 of this Act;

b) The issuance of a Cease-and-Desist Order (CDO) which shall specify that the
trader or retailer and/or their agents shall cease and desist from concealment or
obstruction of view of warning labels under Section 11 of this Act and that they
shall be required to submit a report of compliance therewith within a reasonable
time;

c) A submission of a voluntary assurance of discontinuance of concealment or
obstruction of warning labels under Section 11 of this Act;

d) Issuance of take-down orders against prohibited advertising, promotion, and
sponsorship content under Sections 13 or 14 of this Act;

e) Suspension, revocation, or cancellation of applicable licenses, registrations,
and permits to operate for business establishments and operators or drivers of
public utility vehicles; including the submission of a recommendation to the LGU
concerned, through proper channels, for the cancellation or suspension of
licenses, registrations, and permits;

f) The withholding of any permit, license, authority, or registration pending from
the appropriate agency or entity;

g) Disapproval of an application for the issuance or renewal of a Certificate of
Product Registration (CPR) by the FDA, including suspension, revocation, or

cancellation of an existing CPR, for pre-packaged food products that are
considered misbranded under Section 9 of this Act;

h) Closure of the establishment by LGUs having jurisdiction over premises where
a violation of this Act has been committed; and

i) Censure.

SEC. 30. Liability of Juridical and Other Persons. — Should a violation of
this Act be committed by a juridical person, the Chair of the Board, the

===== [page 22] =====
President, the General Manager, or the partners and/or the persons directly
responsible therefor shall be penalized.

If the offender is an alien, they shall be deported after service of sentence
and/or payment of fine without further deportation proceedings and shall be
permanently barred from re-entering the Philippines.

In case a violation is committed by, or in the interest of, a foreign juridical
person duly licensed to engage in business in the Philippines, such license to
engage in business in the Philippines shall be immediately revoked.

SEC. 31. Liability of Government Officials and Employees. — Public officials
and employees who violate Section 24 or 25 of this Act, or who deliberately or
knowingly fail to fulfill their responsibilities under this Act, shall be subject to
administrative disciplinary action pursuant to relevant laws, rules, and
regulations.

ARTICLE VI
FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 32. Healthy Food Marketing Environment Trust Fund. — A Healthy
Food Marketing Environment Trust Fund is hereby created. All fines collected
pursuant to the enforcement of this Act shall be used exclusively for its
implementation and to fund other measures to promote a healthy food marketing
environment. The DOH shall be responsible for the management and control of
the Trust Fund.

SEC. 33. Compliance Monitoring. — Not later than three (3) years after the
date of the effectivity of this Act, and annually thereafter, the DOH shall submit
to the President of the Philippines and to both Houses of Congress a monitoring
report on the compliance of the food and beverage industry with this Act and all
related laws, rules, regulations, and ordinances. All reports required under this
Act shall be made available to the public primarily via the DOH website and other
means to readily access such information.

The DOH is hereby authorized to report to Congress any instance of delay
or non-compliance by the national agencies and LGUs herein of the timelines in
implementing this Act. The DOH is also authorized to demand accountability
from such agencies or LGUs for failure to fulfill its duties under this Act, without
prejudice to other remedies or penalties and sanctions that are available or may
be imposed by law for such delay or non-compliance.

SEC. 34. Impact Assessment. — The DOH shall conduct an impact assessment

every three (3) years to evaluate the effectiveness of the enforcement of this Act,
health, behavioral, and social outcomes, and other matters relevant to

20

===== [page 23] =====
implementation of this Act. The DOH shall include in its budget the conduct of
the assessment.

SEC. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).— The DOH, in
consultation with the members of the NTF in Section 18, shall promulgate such
rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act
within six (6) months from the date of publication of this Act. The IRR shall cover,
among others, the phases of implementation: Provided, That non-issuance of the
IRR shall not prevent the implementing agencies from enforcing this Act upon
its effectivity.

SEC. 36. Transitory Provision.— Existing industries, businesses,
manufacturers, distributors, and other stakeholders affected by the
implementation of this Act shall be given six (6) months from the effectivity of
this Act to comply with its requirements.

SEC. 37. Appropriations.— The amount necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriations
of the concerned national government agencies. Thereafter, such funds as may
be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in
the budgets of the concerned national government agencies under the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 38. Separability Clause. — If any portion or provision of this Act is
subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which
are not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 39. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative
order, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with
this Act are repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 40. Effectivity.— This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national
circulation.
