                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to mandate all correctional, custodial, and detention facilities
to implement a comprehensive Contraband Detection and Control System (CDCS),
and to impose clear and stringent penalties for the possession, introduction, or
facilitation of contraband within such facilities.

        The State bears the constitutional duty to maintain peace and order, protect
life and property, and promote the general welfare. This responsibility extends to
correctional institutions, where the safety of personnel, visitors, and persons
deprived of liberty (PDLs) must be ensured at all times. While PDLs remain entitled
to the protection of their fundamental rights, these rights are subject to reasonable
restrictions necessary to uphold institutional security and public safety.

        The persistent smuggling of firearms, explosives, dangerous drugs, electronic
communication devices, and other prohibited items into jails and prisons has
exposed serious vulnerabilities in existing control mechanisms. These contraband
items have been used to orchestrate criminal activities within facilities, coordinate
illegal operations outside prison walls, intimidate other inmates, and plan escapes.
The presence of such items undermines discipline, compromises the integrity of the
justice system, and poses grave risks to national security and public order.

       To address these concerns, the bill requires all government agencies and local
government units operating correctional, custodial, and detention facilities to
establish and implement a standardized CDCS. The system shall utilize modern
technologies such as metal detectors, X-ray scanners, and K9 units, in addition to
traditional search procedures, to effectively detect and prevent the entry and
proliferation of contraband.

       The bill imposes substantial penalties for introducing, conveying, possessing,
or attempting to bring contraband into facilities, with heavier penalties for highly
dangerous items such as firearms, explosives, and illegal drugs. Public officials or
employees found guilty of violations are subject to additional penalties, including
perpetual disqualification from holding public office.
       By institutionalizing stronger detection systems, clarifying prohibited acts,
and prescribing appropriate sanctions, this measure aims to fortify correctional
security while upholding the rule of law.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
MANDATING ALL CORRECTIONAL, CUSTODIAL, AND DETENTION
FACILITIES TO IMPLEMENT A CONTRABAND DETECTION AND
CONTROL SYSTEM, AND PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR POSSESSING,
INTRODUCING, OR  AIDING   AND     ABETTING THE ENTRY OF
CONTRABAND INSIDE SUCH FACILITIES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Contraband Detection
Control Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby the declared policy of the State to
maintain peace and order, to protect the life, liberty and property of its citizens, and
to promote the general welfare for the enjoyment by all Filipinos of the blessings of
democracy. The State also adheres to the policy that persons deprived of liberty are
entitled to the exercise and protection of fundamental rights under the Constitution,
subject to such restrictions and limitations in the interest of national security, public
order, public safety, or public health.

        To this end, the State shall implement measures to strictly monitor and
regulate the entry of persons and things in correctional, custodial, and detention
facilities, to prevent the entry of contraband inside such facilities, and to provide
sanctions for violations of rules and regulations regarding the entry of contraband in
these facilities.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

           a. Contraband or prohibited item refers to any object, thing or article that
       is prohibited by law, rules or regulations to be obtained or possessed inside
       correctional, custodial, and detention facilities, or that could endanger the
       safety or security of persons inside such facilities or to the public. This shall
       include:

       SEC. 4. Establishment and Implementation of Contraband Detection and Control
System. — All government agencies and local government units that operate and
maintain correctional, custodial, or detention facilities shall establish and implement
a Contraband Detection and Control System (CDCS) to prevent the entry and
proliferation of contraband inside such facilities. The CDCS shall include the use of
modern technology, devices, or units such as handheld and walk-through metal
detectors, X-ray scanners, and K9 units, that would assist in the effective
implementation of this Act.

        All personnel in charge of the entry to correctional, custodial or detention
facilities shall conduct an effective contraband detection and control procedure
using CDCS technologies and devices, in addition to the traditional methods of
searching any person, including their personal effects and belongings, entering such
facilities.
​

before or during the actual visit, accomplish a registration form which shall contain
the following information, and present the following proof of identity:

         a.​ Full name and complete address of the person entering the facility;

         b.​ The relationship to the person to be visited;

        SEC. 6. Search and Seizure Operations. — The government agency or local
government unit operating and maintaining a correctional, custodial or detention
facility shall conduct regular and random search and seizure operations in all areas
inside such facilities, including the immediate area surrounding the perimeter
thereof. All contraband seized during such operations shall be duly inventoried,
recorded and disposed of in accordance with existing laws, rules and regulations.

      SEC. 7. Prohibited Acts. — Any person who commits any of the following
prohibited acts shall be punished in accordance with the provisions of this Act:

          a.​ Introduce, convey, or attempt to introduce or convey, any contraband
      into a correctional, custodial or detention facility;

          b.​ Facilitate, assist, or abet in the introduction or conveyance, or in the
      attempt thereof, of contraband into a correctional, custodial or detention
      facility;

         c.​ Possess, obtain, or attempt to possess or obtain, contraband while
      being confined or detained, or while serving sentence in a correctional,
      custodial or detention facility; and

         d.​ Failure to register prior to entering a correctional, custodial or
      detention facility as required in this Act.

       SEC. 8. Penalties. — The following penalties shall be imposed on persons who
violate the provisions of this Act, without prejudice to any liability for violation of
any provision of the Revised Penal Code, as amended, or other special laws:

          a.​ Any person who shall commit any of the prohibited acts under Section
      7 (a) of this Act shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of twenty (20) years
      and one (1) day up to forty (40) years and a fine of not less than Five million
      pesos (₱5,000,000.00) but not more than Ten million pesos (₱10,000,000.00), if
      the contraband involved is any of those mentioned in paragraphs (1), (2) and
      (3) of Section 3(a) of this Act.

             If the contraband involved is other than those abovementioned, the
      penalty shall be imprisonment of six (6) years and one (1) day up to twelve
      (12) years, and a fine of not less than One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00) but
      not more than Five million pesos (₱5,000,000.00).

      service of the sentence imposed by the court with respect to the possession of
      the prohibited object or contraband.

          b.​ Any person who shall commit any of the prohibited acts under Section

      (6) years and one (1) day up to twelve (12) years, and a fine of not less than
      One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00) but not more than Five million pesos
      (₱5,000,000.00).

             The penalty of imprisonment shall be served consecutively after the
      service of the sentence imposed by the court, if any, with respect to the
      possession of the prohibited object or contraband and shall also be in addition
      to the original penalty for which the PDL is serving or will be sentenced to
      serve.

          d.​ Any person who enters a correctional, custodial or detention facility
      without complying with the registration requirements under Section 5 of this
      Act, or who provides any false information or submits a falsified ID or
      document, shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of one (1) day up to five
      (5) days, or a fine of One thousand pesos (₱1,000.00), or both.

         e.​ In addition to the penalties provided in paragraphs (a) – (c) of this
      Section, the additional penalty of perpetual absolute disqualification from
      holding public office and the forfeiture of all retirement benefits and accrued
      leave credits shall be imposed if the offender is a public official or employee.

        SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretaries of the Department of Justice and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, in coordination with the Director
General of the Bureau of Corrections, the Chiefs of the Philippine National Police
and the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology, shall issue the necessary rules and
regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.
