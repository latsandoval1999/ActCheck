                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This measure to amend Section 24 of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997 seeks to decrease individual income tax rates by increasing the tax-exemption
threshold to PhP500,000 and reducing rates across various income brackets.


        Republic Act (RA) No. 10963, or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion
(TRAIN) Law, was signed in December 2017. It significantly reformed the country’s
income tax structure and granted exemptions to individuals with an annual taxable
income of ₱250,000 and below. This legislation played a crucial role in promoting tax
relief, particularly for Filipino workers. However, since its implementation, the
country has faced several economic challenges.


        At present, the anticipated rise in the cost of basic commodities and essential
utilities—driven by increasing fuel prices due to tensions in the Middle East—will
inevitably reduce the purchasing power of working Filipinos. The current income tax
exemption threshold of ₱250,000, as provided by law, may no longer be sufficient to
achieve its intended purpose of easing the economic burden on low- and middle-
income earners. In light of these developments, it is evident that there is a need to
amend existing tax laws.


        Furthermore, based on data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA),
prices have increased significantly since 2018. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) has
risen from 100 in 2018 to approximately 127 as of mid-2025, indicating an average
inflation rate of about 3.5% per year. In real terms, this means that the ₱90,000
threshold set in 2018 now has the purchasing power of only around ₱71,000.
Consequently, the intended relief provided by the exemption has been eroded by
inflation, diminishing both its value and its impact on employees’ financial well-being.


       Thus, this measure seeks to amend Section 24 of the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997 by decreasing individual income tax rates, increasing the tax-exemption
threshold to ₱500,000, and reducing rates across various income brackets. By raising
the income tax exemption threshold, workers will be able to retain a larger portion of
their earnings, thereby enhancing their purchasing power and providing much-
needed financial relief. At the same time, higher take-home pay is expected to translate
into increased consumer spending, which will help stimulate economic activity
among individuals and households. Moreover, adjusting the tax brackets will
promote social justice by ensuring a more equitable and responsive national tax
system that reflects current economic conditions and provides greater protection for
vulnerable sectors.


       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. — The State shall uphold a just and dynamic
social order that promotes national prosperity and independence, and seeks to free the
people from poverty through equitable economic and fiscal measures. In accordance with
Section 9, Article II of the 1987 Constitution, the State recognizes the need to ensure fairness
in the tax system and to uphold the principles of equity and social justice in the treatment of
income earned by Filipino workers.
