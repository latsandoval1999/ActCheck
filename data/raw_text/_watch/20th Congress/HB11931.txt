                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy are a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth, a sustained increase in the amount of goods
and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people, and an
expanding productivity as the key to raising the quality of life for all, especially
the underprivileged. Complementing this mandate, Section 5 of Article XIII of
the 1987 Constitution recognizes the rights of farmers, farmworkers,
cooperatives, and independent farmers' organizations to participate in the
planning, organization, and management of agricultural development and directs
the State to provide them with support through appropriate technology and
research, adequate financial, production, marketing, and other support services.
In furtherance of these constitutional policies, this measure seeks to establish a
dedicated fiscal and non-fiscal incentive program for accredited agri-tourism
enterprises to encourage private investments, strengthen rural entrepreneurship,
promote agricultural diversification, and accelerate sustainable countryside
development.
       Agri-tourism has increasingly been recognized as an effective strategy for
integrating agriculture and tourism to generate additional farm income, create
employment opportunities, stimulate local enterprise development, and promote
environmental sustainability. International studies have shown that institutional
support, investment incentives, and government interventions significantly
influence the establishment and long-term sustainability of agri-tourism
enterprises, particularly in rural communities where agriculture remains the
primary source of livelihood.1 Likewise, the United Nations World Tourism
Organization emphasizes that rural tourism contributes to inclusive economic
growth by expanding livelihood opportunities, encouraging private investments,
preserving cultural heritage, and improving the resilience of local economies.2

       Recognizing the importance of the sector, Congress enacted Republic Act
No. 10816, otherwise known as the Farm Tourism Development Act of 2016,
which institutionalized the national framework for the development and
promotion of farm tourism in the Philippines. However, while the law provides
for accreditation, strategic planning, institutional coordination, infrastructure
support, and promotional programs, it does not establish a dedicated incentive
mechanism specifically designed to encourage sustained investments in
accredited agri-tourism enterprises. Local studies support the need for stronger
investment interventions. The Philippine Institute for Development Studies found
that inadequate infrastructure, limited investment support, and weak institutional
coordination continue to constrain the growth of agroecotourism enterprises
despite their significant potential to generate rural employment and diversify
farmers' incomes.3 Similarly, a study conducted in Central Luzon identified
financial constraints, bureaucratic processes, and limited government support as
persistent barriers to the expansion and sustainability of agritourism enterprises.4

       Recognizing these policy gaps, this proposed legislation institutionalizes a
dedicated fiscal and non-fiscal incentive framework exclusively for accredited
agri-tourism enterprises through investment incentives, streamlined government
transactions, technical assistance, capability-building programs, market
promotion, and strengthened monitoring mechanisms. By complementing rather

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Agri-Tourism
Enterprise Incentives Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote investment, innovation, competitiveness, and sustainable growth
in the agri-tourism sector as a means of generating rural employment, enhancing
agricultural productivity, diversifying farm-based enterprises, and stimulating
local economic development. Towards this end, the State shall provide dedicated
fiscal and non-fiscal incentives to accredited agri-tourism enterprises in order to
encourage private sector participation and sustainable investments in rural
communities.
        SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply exclusively to the
establishment, administration, and implementation of fiscal and non-fiscal
incentives for accredited agri-tourism enterprises.

        This Act shall complement, and shall not diminish or amend, the provisions
of Republic Act No. 10816, otherwise known as the "Farm Tourism Development
Act of 2016." Nothing herein shall be construed as creating a separate farm
tourism regulatory framework or accreditation system inconsistent with existing
laws.

        SEC. 4. Eligibility and Accreditation. - Only agri-tourism enterprises duly
accredited by the Department of Tourism (DOT), in coordination with the
Department of Agriculture (DA), pursuant to Republic Act No. 10816 and its
implementing rules and regulations, shall qualify for incentives under this Act.

        The DOT and the DA shall prescribe the standards for eligibility, including
operational, environmental, sustainability, safety, and quality requirements.

        SEC. 5. Fiscal Incentive Program. - Accredited agri-tourism enterprises
shall be eligible to avail themselves of fiscal incentives consistent with existing
national investment and tax incentive laws. Subject to applicable laws, rules, and
regulations, qualified enterprises may be granted the following:

        (a) Income tax incentives under existing investment incentive laws;

        (b) Duty exemptions or preferential customs treatment for imported capital
        equipment, machinery, and agricultural implements directly necessary for
        accredited agri-tourism operations;

        (c) Tax deductions or tax credits for qualified investments in
        modernization, environmental sustainability, innovation, accessibility

      improvements, and agricultural diversification, as may be determined
      under the implementing rules and regulations; and

      (d) Such other fiscal incentives as may subsequently be authorized by law.

      The grant of fiscal incentives shall be subject to the requirements and
approval processes prescribed under existing national laws governing fiscal
incentives.

      SEC. 6. Non-Fiscal Incentives. - Accredited agri-tourism enterprises shall
likewise be entitled to the following non-fiscal incentives:

      (a) Priority and expedited processing of permits, licenses, certifications,
      and other regulatory requirements by concerned national government
      agencies and local government units;

      (b) Access to government-funded technical assistance, extension services,
      capability-building programs, and specialized training in agriculture,
      tourism, entrepreneurship, environmental sustainability, and digital
      innovation;

      (c) Inclusion in national and international tourism promotion programs,
      investment missions, trade fairs, exhibitions, and marketing campaigns
      conducted by the DOT, DA, DTI, and other concerned agencies;

      (d) Assistance in establishing market linkages, business networking, and
      access to financing programs administered by government financial
      institutions; and

      (e) Priority consideration in appropriate government programs supporting
      infrastructure, innovation, and enterprise development, subject to existing
      laws and available appropriations.

      (f) Accredited agri-tourism enterprises may avail of fiscal incentives only
      in accordance with existing investment incentive laws, subject to the
      applicable approval requirements of the appropriate Investment Promotion
      Agency (IPA), the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB), where
      applicable, and other relevant laws, rules, and regulations.

      Nothing in this proposed measure shall be construed as granting tax
incentives beyond those authorized under existing laws governing fiscal
incentives and investments.

      SEC. 7. Administration and Monitoring. - The Department of Tourism
shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act, in coordination with the
Department of Agriculture, the Department of Trade and Industry, the Bureau of
Internal Revenue, the Bureau of Customs, and other concerned government
agencies.

      The DOT shall establish a monitoring and evaluation system to assess the
implementation and effectiveness of the incentive program, including its impact
on:

      (a) Investments generated;

      (b) Employment created;

      (c) Rural enterprise development;

      (d) Agricultural diversification;

      (e) Tourist arrivals; and

      (f) Local economic development.

      SEC. 8. Annual Report to Congress. - Within ninety (90) days after the
close of every fiscal year, the Department of Tourism shall submit to the Senate
                                                                                4
and the House of Representatives an Annual Report on the implementation of this
Act, including:

      (a) Number of accredited beneficiaries;

      (b) Incentives granted;

      (c) Investments generated;

      (d) Employment created;

      (e) Economic and tourism impact;

      (f) Compliance status of beneficiaries; and

      (g) Recommendations for policy improvements.

      SEC. 9. Suspension or Revocation of Incentives. - The incentives granted
under this Act may be suspended or revoked upon due notice and hearing
whenever an accredited enterprise:

      (a) Violates the conditions of its accreditation;

      (b) Obtains incentives through fraud, misrepresentation, or falsification;

      (c) Uses the incentives for purposes other than those authorized under this
      Act;

      (d) Commits serious violations of environmental, labor, tax, or tourism
      laws; or

      (e) Fails to comply with the reporting and monitoring requirements
      prescribed by the implementing agencies.

      The revocation of incentives shall be without prejudice to any civil,
criminal, or administrative liability under existing laws.

      SEC. 10. Periodic Review. - The Department of Tourism, in coordination
with the Department of Agriculture and other concerned agencies, shall conduct
a comprehensive review of the implementation and effectiveness of this Act every
five (5) years and submit recommendations to Congress on the continuation,
modification, or enhancement of the incentive program.

      SEC. 11. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Tourism and other concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums
as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Tourism, in coordination with
the Department of Agriculture, the Department of Trade and Industry, the Bureau
of Internal Revenue, the Bureau of Customs, and other concerned government
agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

      SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
administrative rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
