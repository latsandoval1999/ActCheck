                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       For many students, an internship is their introduction to the professional world
as it offers an opportunity to apply classroom theories, build confidence, and gain
practical skills that will shape their future careers. Done well, internships can open
doors to lifelong opportunities.

        However, this is not always the case in the Philippines. Many students report
being assigned tasks that have little to do with their course, receiving minimal
supervision, or working full-time without any allowance, even when they contribute
meaningful work to the host institution.1 With no clear national standards, students
become vulnerable to unfair treatment and prevent institutions from maximizing the
full learning potential of these programs.

      Current laws and policies meant to support youth employment also leave a
major gap.

       These gaps have led to two pressing problems: (1) Students are required to
render uncompensated work without proper safeguards; and (2) Pre-licensure
internships lack clear protections and standards, even though they are performing
essential professional duties.

       This Bill, the “National Higher Education Internship Act”, responds to the
State’s duty to support the development of the youth. It introduces uniform standards
that protect students, elevate the quality of training, and strengthen partnerships
between HEIs and host institutions. When students are required to perform
productive work as part of their education or as a condition for professional licensure,
they deserve fair compensation, appropriate supervision, and a safe and supportive
learning environment. This is especially crucial for students from low-income
families, for whom unpaid internships can be a significant financial burden and a
barrier to completing their degree.

       We believe internships should truly serve their purpose: meaningful learning,
real industry exposure, and better employment outcomes for Filipino graduates.

      In light of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this Bill is respectfully
requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Higher
Education Internship Framework Act”.

        SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to
recognize the vital role of the youth in nation-building and to promote their holistic
development through meaningful experiential learning opportunities. Towards this
end, the State shall ensure that all internship programs in Higher Education
Institutions (HEIs), including those required for professional licensure, are
implemented through a collaborative partnership between the HEIs and Host
Institutions to ensure a fair, structured, and safe environment that enhances learning
while preventing exploitation.

        The State shall establish a national framework that sets minimum standards
and safeguards for all HEI internship programs, balancing the educational and
developmental needs of students with the operational realities of participating host
institutions.

      SEC 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act:

         a) Intern refers to a student enrolled in an accredited higher educational
institution (HEI) who undergoes a structured and supervised practical training



                                          3
program in a host institution as part of the academic requirements of their course or
as a prerequisite for a professional licensure.

      b) Host Institution refers to any government agency, local government unit,
government-owned or-controlled corporation, or duly-authorized private enterprise or
organization that accepts interns for purposes of training and exposure.

      c) Program Plan refers to a written schedule or document detailing the
educational objectives, specific key learning tasks, duration of engagement, and the
name and contact information of the designated Internship Supervisor.

       d) Internship Coordinator refers to the duly designated school representative
responsible for coordinating with the Host Institution, monitoring the Intern’s
progress, and ensuring the protection of the Intern’s welfare during the internship
period.

       e) Work-Required Internship a mandatory requirement for graduation,
including those required for professional licensure, and where the Intern performs
tasks that are directly related to the Host Institution’s operations or services;

        f) National Training Directory refers to a government-endorsed platform that
lists accredited internship opportunities available to students, parents, and schools.

       g) Certificate of Program Compliance refers to the official document issued by
the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) certifying that a Host Institutions’
internship program meets all minimum standards under this Act.

       SEC 4. Minimum Standards for Internship Programs. – All Host Institutions
shall comply with the following minimum standards in the conduct of internship
programs:

        a) Formal Agreement and Program Plan. – Prior to commencement, the Host
Institution shall execute a written Internship Agreement with the Intern and the
Educational Institution, specifying the start and end dates, expected hours of
engagement, any agreed-upon stipend or benefits; and the approved Program Plan.
      b) Compensation for Work-Required Internships. – An Intern undergoing a
Work-Required Internship shall receive a stipend or allowance equivalent to not less
than seventy-five percent (75%) of the prevailing minimum wage, for the region
where the internship is conducted, computed based on actual attendance and hours
worked. This requirement shall not apply to internships designed purely for
observation, shadowing, or where the primary benefit is demonstrably to the student,
provided that such nature is clearly stated in the Agreement and approved by the
HEI.
       c) Access to Resources. – The Host Institution shall provide the Intern with
access to the tools, facilities, and resources necessary to accomplish the tasks
stated in the Program Plan.
      d) Safety and Liability Coverage. – The Host Institution shall provide adequate
coverage against liability for accidents, injuries, or illness sustained by the Intern in

                                           4
the performance of approved internship activities, including authorized travel
between official work sites.
       e) Grievance and Protection Mechanism. – The Host Institution, in
coordination with the Educational Institution, shall establish a confidential,
accessible, and non-retaliatory grievance mechanism through which interns may
report mistreatment, harassment, unsafe working conditions, or violations of this Act.

      SEC 5. Duties and Responsibilities of Educational Institutions. –
Educational institutions shall:

      a) Designate a qualified Internship Coordinator;
      b) Conduct a mandatory pre-deployment seminar, in coordination with the
Host Institutions, to prepare students for the training environment and clarify work
expectations, workplace rules, and supervision arrangements;
     c) Ensure that internships remain aligned with the educational objectives and
academic requirements of the course;
       d) Be prohibited from collecting any fees unrelated to the internship program,
requiring interns to shoulder the cost of accompanying school personnel, or pay
school fees for facilities or services they will not use while on internship.

        SEC 6. Incentives for Compliance. – To encourage compliance, Host
Institutions shall be granted monetary and non-monetary incentives by the Bureau of
Internal Revenue (BIR), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), and the Civil Service Commission (CSC). Such
incentives may include, which may include:

        a) Tax deductions on stipends paid to interns, upon presentation of a
Certificate of Program Compliance;
       b) Access to government-subsidized technical assistance to improve program
quality and compliance readiness;
      c) Awards and recognitions for exemplary internship programs;
      d) Inclusion in the National Training Directory; and
      e) Priority invitation to job fairs and recruitment events.

        SEC 7. Oversight and Accountability. – The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall exercise oversight over internship programs involving
currently enrolled students. For internships conducted in private host institutions,
CHED shall coordinate with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), while
for internships conducted in public host institutions, CHED shall coordinate with the
Civil Service Commission (CSC).
      For post-graduate or pre-licensure internships involving graduates, oversight
and regulatory authority shall rest with the appropriate Professional Regulatory



                                            5
Board under the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), in coordination with
DOLE or CSC, as applicable.
      Host Institutions found to have violated the provisions of this Act or its
implementing rules may be blacklisted or suspended from participating in internship
programs, subject to the administrative action, as appropriate.

       SEC 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the CHED, DOLE, CSC, PRC, and DTI shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement the
provisions of this Act.

       SEC 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision or part not otherwise
affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

       SEC 10. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.

        SEC 11.        Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
