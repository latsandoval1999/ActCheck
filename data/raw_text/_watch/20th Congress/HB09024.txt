                              AN ACT
        PROMOTING TRADITIONAL FOUNDATIONAL LEARNING IN
       KINDERGARTEN TO GRADE 6, PROHIBITING LEARNER USE OF
    PERSONAL DIGITAL DEVICES DURING CLASS HOURS, AND LIMITING
    SCREEN-BASED INSTRUCTION AS THE DEFAULT MODE OF TEACHING

                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The early and middle elementary grades, Kindergarten to Grade 6, are critical
years for building and consolidating the foundations of reading comprehension,
writing, handwriting fluency, numeracy, study habits, and sustained attention.1 These
competencies require focused, repeated, and tactile learning experiences that are best
developed through structured, print-based, handwriting-based, and developmentally
appropriate instruction especially in the primary grades while progressively
strengthening higher-order comprehension and problem-solving through the
elementary years.2

       While digital tools can support learning when used deliberately, the increasing
presence of screens and learner-owned gadgets in the classroom may contribute to
distraction and may crowd out time devoted to essential literacy and numeracy skills.3
Excessive or unregulated screen exposure during these formative years risks
undermining the development of core competencies and classroom focus.4

       Empirical studies and national assessments likewise reveal that many Filipino
learners struggle with reading and mathematics skills in the elementary levels,
reinforcing the urgent need for strengthened interventions and protected learning
time across Kindergarten to Grade 6.5

        This bill adopts a “screen-light” approach in Kindergarten to Grade 6 through
two targeted rules: (1) it prohibits learner use of personal digital devices during class
hours within school premises; and (2) it prohibits screen-based instruction as the
default mode of teaching in Kindergarten to Grade 6. The measure is not a total
rejection of technology. It allows limited exceptions for assistive technology, medical
and safety needs, emergencies, and brief, teacher-directed screen use when reasonably
necessary to achieve a specific competency.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. —This Act shall be known as the “Going Back to
Traditional Education Act of 2026”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. —It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the right of all citizens to quality basic education and to make such education
accessible to all.

        Pursuant to the State’s authority to exercise reasonable supervision and
regulation of all educational institutions, the State shall ensure that learners in
Kindergarten to Grade 6 develop strong foundational skills, particularly reading and
literacy, writing and handwriting fluency, numeracy, and sustained attention,
through age-appropriate, print-based and handwriting-based learning, while
preventing avoidable digital distraction and excessive or poorly managed screen
exposure during instruction.

       SEC. 3. Coverage. —This Act shall apply to all basic education schools and
learning centers, whether public or private, offering Kindergarten to Grade 6,
including alternative learning system (ALS) learning centers offering equivalent
learning levels, subject to the reasonable supervision and regulation of the
Department of Education (DepEd).

       SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. —As used in this Act:

       (a) “Kindergarten to Grade 6” refers to Kindergarten, Grades 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.




                                           Page 3 of 7
       (b) “Personal Digital Device (PDD)” refers to learner-owned smartphones,
tablets, smartwatches with messaging or notification capability, handheld gaming
devices, and other similar gadgets capable of screen interaction, excluding medically
necessary devices.

       (c) “Class Hours” refers to the official daily period of instruction and school-
supervised learning activities, including remediation and school-supervised co-
curricular learning time.

        (d) “Screen-Based Instruction (SBI)” refers to teaching-learning activities where
a learner’s primary engagement with content or tasks is through an electronic screen,
including but not limited to watching or reading lessons on a device, answering
activities on applications, or completing digital worksheets, whether online or offline.

       (e) “Non-Screen Instruction (NSI)” refers to teaching-learning activities not
requiring learner screen use, including but not limited to printed books or modules,
handwriting-based tasks, pen-and-paper exercises, teacher-led discussion,
manipulative learning aids, skills-based activities, and experiential learning.

     (f) “Assistive Technology” refers to devices and applications used as reasonable
accommodations to support learners with disabilities or special needs.

       (g) “Screen-Light Learning Plan (SLLP)” refers to a school-level plan
implementing this Act’s requirements and DepEd minimum standards, consistent
with school-based management and shared governance principles.

       SEC. 5. Prohibition on Learner Use of Personal Digital Devices During Class Hours
(Kindergarten to Grade 6). —In Kindergarten to Grade 6, learners shall be prohibited
from using Personal Digital Devices during Class Hours within school premises.

       The prohibition shall not apply to:

      (1) Assistive Technology required as a reasonable accommodation for learners
with disabilities or special needs;

      (2) medical or safety-related use as certified by school health personnel or as
reasonably required under school safety protocols; and

      (3) emergency communications, subject to school rules designed to prevent
disruption.

       DepEd shall prescribe minimum standards for the safekeeping, storage,
retrieval, and parent or guardian coordination for Personal Digital Devices brought to
school, consistent with child protection and due process in discipline.

       SEC. 6. Prohibition on Screen-Based Instruction as the Default Mode in Kindergarten
to Grade 6; Limited Exceptions. —In Kindergarten to Grade 6, Screen-Based Instruction
shall be prohibited as the default mode of teaching for the delivery of core
competencies, particularly in reading and literacy, writing and handwriting, and
numeracy.

      Screen-Based Instruction may be used only when all of the following are
present:

       (1) the SBI is brief, targeted, and teacher-directed;

       (2) the SBI is reasonably necessary to achieve a specific learning competency
that cannot be effectively achieved through NSI under the circumstances; and

       (3) the SBI complies with age-appropriate limits and safeguards prescribed in
the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), including required breaks and
attention and distraction safeguards.

       DepEd shall establish minimum weekly protected time for:

       (1) print-based reading; and

      (2) handwriting-based learning activities, including pen-and-paper exercises
and writing practice appropriate to grade level.

       SEC. 7. School-Level Implementation Through a Screen-Light Learning Plan. —Each
basic education school offering Kindergarten to Grade 6, whether public or private,
shall adopt a Screen-Light Learning Plan consistent with DepEd minimum standards.

       The SLLP shall cover at minimum:

       (1) classroom enforcement protocols for Sections 5 and 6;

       (2) procedures for device storage and safekeeping;

       (3) parent or guardian communication and partnership measures;

       (4) inclusion safeguards for learners requiring Assistive Technology; and

      (5) internal monitoring measures, including remediation supports for literacy
and writing.

       Private schools may comply through equivalent measures that meet or exceed
the objectives and minimum standards under this Act and its IRR, as reflected in their
SLLP.

       SEC. 8. Nature and Limits of DepEd Authority Over Private Schools. —In
implementing this Act with respect to private basic education schools, DepEd shall
exercise only reasonable supervision and regulation to set minimum standards and
ensure compliance.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize DepEd to control the
internal governance of private schools, including the management of their personnel,
internal administration, and other matters beyond ensuring compliance with
minimum standards under this Act and its IRR.

       SEC. 9. Monitoring, Compliance, and Corrective Action Plans (Administrative
Model). —
       (a) Monitoring. — DepEd shall develop a monitoring framework for
implementation of this Act that minimizes teacher administrative burden and focuses
on learning outcomes and learner welfare.

      (b) Compliance Support. — Where DepEd finds noncompliance, it shall
primarily provide technical assistance and require the school to submit a time-bound
Corrective Action Plan consistent with this Act.

      (c) Due Process. — DepEd shall ensure notice and opportunity to be heard
before issuing final findings of noncompliance and before requiring a Corrective
Action Plan, consistent with administrative due process.

       SEC. 10. Teacher Support and Learning Resources. —DepEd shall prioritize:

      (a) the provision or facilitation of printed learning resources, including
textbooks, workbooks, and learning modules for Kindergarten to Grade 6;

       (b) teacher training on literacy, handwriting instruction, print-based strategies,
and classroom management consistent with this Act; and

      (c) research and monitoring tools to measure reading, writing, and numeracy
outcomes in Kindergarten to Grade 6.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. —Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from the effectivity of this Act, DepEd shall promulgate the IRR, which shall:

       (a) set age-appropriate parameters, safeguards, and permitted exception types
for Section 6;

       (b) prescribe minimum standards for Screen-Light Learning Plans under
Section 7;

       (c) provide guidance on device storage and safekeeping under Section 5; and

      (d) establish an administrative monitoring and corrective action framework
under Section 9.

      SEC. 12. Appropriations. —Such sums as may be necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act. DepEd may realign existing appropriations, subject to applicable budgeting,
accounting, and auditing rules.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. —If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. —All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. —This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
