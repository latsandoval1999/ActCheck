                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

Infrastructure development remains one of the government’s primary drivers
of economic growth, regional connectivity, and public service delivery.
However, planning inefficiencies, overlapping projects, lack of spatial data
coordination, and weak monitoring systems often lead to delays, cost
overruns, and underutilized infrastructure.

Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide a powerful tool for planning,
implementing, and monitoring infrastructure projects by enabling accurate
mapping, spatial analysis, and real-time visualization of project data.
Integrating GIS into infrastructure programming enhances transparency,
improves decision-making, strengthens disaster resilience planning, and
ensures that public investments are strategically located and efficiently
maintained.

Mandating GIS integration in infrastructure projects funded through the
Annual General Appropriations Act will modernize infrastructure governance,
reduce duplication of projects, improve inter-agency coordination, and
promote evidence-based development planning. The public will also benefit
from greater transparency, as infrastructure projects become easier to track
and evaluate.

Infrastructure projects funded by public money will not only be built, but also
properly documented, monitored, and integrated into a national spatial
planning network. Thus, the establishment of the National Spatial Registry.

More importantly, this proposed measure is about maximizing the value of
public funds. When infrastructure planning is informed by accurate data and
mapping, projects are more strategic, sustainable, and responsive to the
needs of the public.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

        AN ACT MANDATING THE INTEGRATION OF GEOGRAPHIC
           INFORMATION SYSTEMS (GIS) IN ALL GOVERNMENT
      INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS UNDER THE ANNUAL GENERAL
    APPROPRIATIONS ACT, ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL SPATIAL
       INFRASTRUCTURE REGISTRY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                                THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Smart
Infrastructure and GIS Integration Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote transparency, efficiency, sustainability, and evidence-based
planning in public infrastructure development. The State recognizes the
importance of geospatial data and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) in
improving project planning, monitoring, disaster resilience, and asset
management.

       To ensure accountability and optimized allocation of public funds, all
infrastructure projects funded under the Annual General Appropriations Act
shall incorporate GIS-based mapping and monitoring components.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For the purpose of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

   1. Geographic Information System (GIS): A conceptualized framework that
      provides the ability to capture and analyze spatial and geographic data.

   2. Infrastructure Projects: Shall include, but not be limited to, roads,
      bridges, school buildings, hospitals, water systems, flood control
      structures, and digital connectivity assets funded by the National
      Government.

   3. Implementing Agencies: refers to any national government agency,
      GOCC, State Universities and Colleges, or Local Government unit
      implementing infrastructure projects funded under the Annual General
      Appropriations Act.

   4. Geo-tagging: The process of adding geographical identification
      metadata to various media, such as photographs or videos.

      SEC. 4. Mandatory GIS Integration. -          All government agencies,
including Government-Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs) and
Local Government Units (LGUs) implementing infrastructure projects
appearing in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA), are hereby
mandated to submit GIS-compliant data for every project. No project shall be
released for bidding without a verified spatial coordinate profile.

      SEC. 5. The National Infrastructure Spatial Registry (NISR). There is
hereby created a centralized database to be managed by the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the
Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and the National
Economic and Development Authority (NEDA). This registry shall be:

   1. Accessible to the Commission on Audit (COA) for real-time monitoring.

   2. Open to the public via a web-based dashboard (subject to national
      security protocols).

      SEC. 6. Funding. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the DICT and DPWH. Thereafter, it shall be incorporated in
the annual budget of the implementing agencies and may be charged to
project management or administrative costs, subject to budgeting,
accounting, and auditing rules and regulations

     SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within sixty (60)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, DPWH, and DBM shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of the Act or the provisions not
otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
