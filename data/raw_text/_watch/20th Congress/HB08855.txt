                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Philippines faces the dual challenge of ensuring energy security while
transitioning to a sustainable and low-carbon future. Continued dependence on imported
petroleum exposes the economy to global price volatility and supply risks, even as the State
advances renewable and green energy development in line with climate commitments. While
existing laws, including Republic Act Nos. 9136 (EPIRA), 9513 (Renewable Energy Act),
11285 (Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act), and 11534 (CREATE Act), provide a
framework for energy development and investment incentives, there is a need to further
strengthen and harmonize these incentives to attract large-scale investments in both
petroleum refining and renewable energy infrastructure.

        This measure seeks to enhance domestic refining capacity to improve energy security,
while accelerating investments in renewable and green energy to meet sustainability goals. It
establishes a competitive, predictable, and performance-based fiscal regime aligned with
CREATE, streamlines regulatory processes, and ensures strict compliance with
environmental and safety standards.

       Ultimately, this bill aims to secure a stable, affordable, and environmentally
responsible energy system for the Filipino people. In view of the foregoing, the immediate
passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
     INCENTIVIZING INVESTMENTS IN PETROLEUM REFINERY AND
  RENEWABLE AND GREEN ENERGY PROJECTS, PROVIDING FISCAL AND
         NON-FISCAL INCENTIVES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

       SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Energy Investment
Incentives Act of 2026.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to: (a)
Ensure energy security and self-sufficiency; (b) Promote sustainable and environmentally
sound energy development; (c) Encourage private sector participation in both conventional
and renewable energy; (d) Align fiscal incentives with Republic Act No. 11534 (CREATE
Act); (e) Support the transition to low-carbon energy systems consistent with national and
international commitments.


       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:

(a) Petroleum Refinery Plant – Any facility engaged in processing crude oil into petroleum
products.
(b) Renewable Energy – Energy derived from sources such as solar, wind, hydro, geothermal,
and biomass pursuant to Republic Act No. 9513.
(c) Green Energy Projects – Projects that reduce emissions, improve efficiency, or support
sustainability.
(d) Investor – Any person or entity investing in covered projects.
(e) Operator – Any entity responsible for managing covered facilities.


       SEC. 4. Coverage. This Act shall apply to:

       (a) Petroleum refinery plants undertaking establishment, expansion, or modernization;
       (b) Renewable and green energy generation, storage, and distribution;

       (c) Energy-related infrastructure including transmission, fuel logistics, and storage.



       SEC. 5. Fiscal Incentives. Registered business enterprises (RBEs) under this Act
shall be entitled to performance-based, time-bound, and targeted fiscal incentives, consistent
with Republic Act No. 11534 (CREATE Act), and subject to approval by the Board of
Investments (BOI) and/or the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB):

(a) Income Tax Holiday (ITH)

   ● Up to six (6) years for renewable and green energy projects;
   ● Up to four (4) years for petroleum refinery establishment, modernization, or
     expansion;
   ● An additional one (1) to two (2) years may be granted for projects located in less
     developed areas or utilizing advanced, low-emission technologies.

(b) Special Corporate Income Tax (SCIT) or Enhanced Deductions

   ● RBEs may avail of either: (i) SCIT at a rate of five percent (5%) on gross income
     earned, in lieu of all national and local taxes; or (ii) Enhanced deductions regime, as
     provided under CREATE.
   ● The availment period shall be consistent with CREATE and subject to performance
     metrics.

(c) Duty-Free Importation

   ● Exemption from customs duties and VAT on capital equipment, machinery, and spare
     parts directly used in registered activities, for a period of up to five (5) years.

(d) Net Operating Loss Carry-Over (NOLCO)

   ● Losses may be carried over for the next five (5) consecutive taxable years.

(e) Accelerated Depreciation

   ● Accelerated depreciation of capital assets, particularly for renewable and green energy
     systems, subject to existing tax rules.

(f) Zero-Rated VAT

   ● On local purchases of goods and services directly attributable to registered projects,
     subject to compliance with existing VAT laws.

(g) Green Incentive Bonus

   ● Renewable and green energy projects meeting higher efficiency or emission reduction
     standards may be granted additional non-monetary incentives or extended ITH within
     limits set by the FIRB.
         SEC. 6. Non-Fiscal Incentives.

       (a) Streamlined permitting through the Energy Virtual One-Stop Shop (EVOSS)
pursuant to Republic Act No. 11234;

      (b) Priority grid connection for renewable energy through the National Grid
Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP);

      (c) Access to government financing through Development Bank of the Philippines
(DBP) and Land Bank of the Philippines (LANDBANK);

         (d) Technical support from the Department of Energy (DOE).



         SEC. 7. Additional Incentives for Green Energy. Projects meeting high
sustainability standards shall be eligible for:

         (a) Additional two (2) years ITH;

         (b) Access to carbon markets in coordination with the Climate Change Commission
(CCC);

     (c) Grants and R&D support through the Department of Science and Technology
(DOST).



         SEC. 8. Environmental and Safety Compliance. All projects shall comply with: (a)
Republic Act No. 8749 (Clean Air Act); (b) Republic Act No. 9275 (Clean Water Act); (c)
Republic Act No. 6969 (Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act);
(d) Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) requirements under the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR).

Petroleum refinery projects shall adopt best available technologies to minimize emissions.

         SEC. 9. Clawback for Non-Compliance. A clawback mechanism shall be
determined and implemented by the appropriate implementing agencies, including but not
limited to the partial or full recovery of fiscal incentives previously granted, suspension or
cancellation of remaining incentive entitlements, and the imposition of administrative fines
and penalties, without prejudice to other civil or criminal liabilities under existing laws. Such
clawback shall apply in cases of non-compliance or violation of environmental and safety
laws, rules, and regulations, including failure to secure or comply with Environmental
Compliance Certificates (ECCs), breach of safety standards resulting in significant incidents,
accidents, or public harm, misrepresentation or falsification of compliance reports, and
repeated or willful non-compliance as determined by the Department of Environment and
Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Energy (DOE), or other relevant regulatory
bodies. Clawback actions shall be imposed only after due notice and hearing, and shall be
proportionate to the severity and impact of the violation.



       SEC. 10. Implementing Agencies. The following agencies shall implement this Act:

(a) Department of Energy (DOE) – Lead agency for policy, planning, and project
endorsement;

(b) Board of Investments (BOI) / Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) – Incentives
approval and administration;

(c) Department of Finance (DOF) / Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) / Bureau of Customs
(BOC) – Tax and duty administration;

(d) Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) – Environmental
compliance;

(e) Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) – Regulatory oversight;

(f) National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) – Grid connection;

(g) Climate Change Commission (CCC) – Climate policy alignment;

(h) Department of Science and Technology (DOST) – Research and innovation support;

(i) Local Government Units (LGUs) – Local permits and compliance.



       SEC. 11. Investment Promotion. The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in
coordination with the DOE, shall promote investments through international and domestic
campaigns.

        SEC. 12. Sunset Review. Within five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act, and
every five (5) years thereafter, the FIRB, in coordination with the DOE, National Economic
and Development Authority (NEDA), and DOF, shall conduct a comprehensive review of the
implementation and impact of the incentives provided under this Act. The review shall
assess, among others, the effectiveness of the incentives in attracting investments, generating
employment, enhancing energy security, and advancing environmental sustainability.

Based on the findings, the FIRB shall recommend to Congress the continuation, modification,
or termination of specific incentives to ensure fiscal sustainability, policy coherence, and
alignment with national development goals.

        SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DOE, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall promulgate the IRR.

       SEC. 14. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder shall not be affected.
       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, or issuances inconsistent with this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 16. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in
the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
