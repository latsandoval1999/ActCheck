                             AN ACT
 PROHIBITING THE TRADE OF GOODS PRODUCED WHOLLY OR IN PART WITH
         FORCED LABOR AND PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR


                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

        On June 2, 2026, the Office of the United States Trade Representative found that the Philippines
had failed to impose and effectively enforce an express prohibition on the importation of goods produced
wholly or in part through forced labor.

        On July 23, 2026, the United States consequently imposed an additional tariff of twelve and one-
half percent (12.5%) on products of the Philippines, subject to specified exemptions. The tariff took effect
on July 24, 2026.

        The Executive Branch has issued a Joint Administrative Order establishing an inter-agency
mechanism for investigating imported goods suspected of having been produced through forced labor.
However, an administrative issuance is not a substitute for an unequivocal and permanent statutory
prohibition.

        This bill expressly prohibits the importation of goods produced wholly or in part through forced
labor, authorizes the Bureau of Customs to exclude and forfeit such goods, and provides corresponding
penalties. It also requires permanent inter-agency coordination for its effective implementation.

        Its immediate enactment will protect workers and Filipino enterprises from unfair competition and
provide a clear legal basis for seeking the modification or termination of the additional United States tariffs
imposed on Philippine products.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Forced Labor Goods Trade
Prohibition Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to afford full
protection to labor and protect Filipino enterprises against unfair foreign competition and trade
practices. In pursuance thereof, the State shall prohibit the importation of goods produced wholly
or in part with forced labor and ensure the effective enforcement of such prohibition.

       SEC. 3. Prohibition. – Goods mined, produced, manufactured, harvested, processed, or
assembled wholly or in part with forced labor shall not be entitled to entry into the Philippines,
and their importation is hereby prohibited.

       For purposes of this Act, forced labor means all work or service exacted from any person
under the menace of any penalty and for which the person has not offered himself or herself
voluntarily.

         The prohibition shall include goods containing any raw material, intermediate good,
component, part, or other input produced with forced labor, regardless of the country of origin,
shipment, processing, or substantial transformation. It shall apply regardless of the customs
procedure to which the goods are declared, including consumption, warehousing, processing,
transit, transshipment, admission into a free zone, or re-exportation.

        SEC. 4. Enforcement. – Where there are reasonable grounds to believe that imported goods
were produced wholly or in part with forced labor, the Bureau of Customs may withhold their
release and require the importer to submit relevant supply-chain, origin, production, and labor-
compliance records within fifteen (15) working days.
       If the importer fails to establish by clear and convincing evidence that the goods were not
produced wholly or in part with forced labor, the Bureau shall deny their entry. The goods shall be
reexported within thirty (30) days at the importer’s expense. Failure to reexport the goods within
such period shall cause their seizure, forfeiture, and destruction in accordance with this Act and
applicable customs laws.

        A permanent inter-agency mechanism is hereby established to coordinate the investigation
and enforcement of the prohibition under this Act. Its composition and procedures shall be
prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.

        SEC. 5. Seizure, Forfeiture, and Penalties. – Goods imported or sought to be imported in
violation of this Act shall be considered goods of prohibited importation and shall be subject to
seizure and forfeiture under Section 1113(f) of Republic Act No. 10863, otherwise known as the
“Customs Modernization and Tariff Act.” Goods finally forfeited shall be destroyed and shall not
be sold, donated, or otherwise released into Philippine commerce.

         Any person who knowingly imports, brings into the Philippines, or assists in importing
goods prohibited under this Act, or who receives, conceals, buys, sells, transports, or otherwise
facilitates the disposition of such goods after importation with knowledge of their unlawful
importation, shall be penalized under Section 1401 of the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act.

       Any person who knowingly submits a false or fraudulent statement, certification, or
document to secure the entry or release of goods covered by this Act shall be penalized under
Section 1403, in relation to Section 1401, of the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act.

        SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within thirty (30) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon recommendation of the Commissioner of Customs and
in consultation with the Department of Trade and Industry, Department of Labor and Employment,
Department of Foreign Affairs, economic zone authorities, and other concerned agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations.

        SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect.

        SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, rules, regulations, and issuances inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
