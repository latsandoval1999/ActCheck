EXPLANATORY NOTE

Occupational therapy and speech-language therapy are essential rehabilitation
services that help individuals develop, regain, or maintain the functional, communication,
and daily living skills necessary for independent and meaningful participation in society.

For many indigent Filipino families, the cost of these services remains a significant
barrier to obtaining appropriate and continuous treatment. Children with developmental
delays, autism spectrum disorder, cerebral palsy, and communication disorders may
require sustained therapeutic interventions to support their development and participation
in school and community life. Likewise, persons with disabilities and individuals
recovering from stroke, traumatic brain injury, or other serious medical conditions may
require occupational therapy or speech-language therapy to improve their functional
independence and quality of life.

Financial hardship and limited access to qualified professionals and rehabilitation
facilities prevent many individuals from receiving the services they need. These barriers
are particularly significant in underserved communities and areas where rehabilitation
resources are limited.

This proposed measure seeks to establish a National Free Occupational and Speech-
Language Therapy Program for Indigents, to be created, administered, and implemented
by the Department of Health in all public hospitals nationwide.

The proposed measure establishes two alternative means of determining indigency:
inclusion in the latest applicable official Listahanan of the Department of Social Welfare
and Development, or formal assessment and endorsement by the Local Social Welfare and
Development Office of the beneficiary's city or municipality of residence.

By recognizing local social welfare endorsements as an alternative basis for
eligibility, the measure seeks to accommodate indigent individuals who may not appear in
Listahanan but whose financial circumstances have been assessed and verified by the
appropriate local social welfare office.

The measure further requires public hospitals to establish appropriate service units
or referral arrangements, provides for the development of qualified rehabilitation

personnel, and requires coordination among national government agencies and local
government units to ensure equitable access to services.

Through this proposed legislation, the State shall take steps to remove financial
barriers to essential rehabilitation services, promote functional independence and

communication, support families and caregivers, and protect the dignity and well-being of
indigent Filipinos.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT

ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL PROGRAM PROVIDING FREE OCCUPATIONAL
THERAPY AND SPEECH-LANGUAGE THERAPY SERVICES TO INDIGENT
INDIVIDUALS, INCLUDING THOSE IDENTIFIED THROUGH THE LATEST
LISTAHANAN OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL WELFARE AND
DEVELOPMENT AND THOSE FORMALLY ENDORSED BY LOCAL SOCIAL
WELFARE AND DEVELOPMENT OFFICES, MANDATING ITS IMPLEMENTATION
IN ALL PUBLIC HOSPITALS NATIONWIDE, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Free Occupational and

Speech-Language Therapy for Indigents Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health of every Filipino and ensure that essential health and rehabilitation
services are accessible, affordable, and responsive to the needs of indigent individuals and other

vulnerable sectors of society.

The State recognizes that occupational therapy and speech-language therapy are essential
rehabilitation services that help individuals develop, regain, or maintain the functional,
communication, cognitive, sensory, and daily living skills necessary for independent and

meaningful participation in society.

The State further recognizes that the high cost and limited availability of these services
prevent many indigent individuals from obtaining necessary and continuous therapeutic
interventions, particularly children with developmental delays, persons with disabilities, and

individuals recovering from illness, injury, stroke, or other medical conditions.

Accordingly, the State shall establish a nationwide program providing free occupational
therapy and speech-language therapy services to qualified indigent individuals through al! public
hospitals, with the Department of Health (DOH) serving as the lead implementing agency in
coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DS WD), local government

units (LGUs), and other concerned agencies.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:

(a) Indigent Individual refers to a person who lacks sufficient income or financial
resources to afford necessary occupational therapy or speech-language therapy

services and who qualifies under either of the following categories:

(1) An individual belonging to a household identified as poor in the latest
applicable official Listahanan of the DSWD, or in the officially designated
successor to Listahanan should the DSWD replace or formally discontinue the

system; or

(2) An individual who has been assessed and formally endorsed as indigent by the
Local Social Welfare and Development Office (LSWDO) of the city or
municipality where the individual resides, in accordance with applicable social

welfare assessment and verification procedures.

(b) Occupational Therapy refers to a client-centered health and rehabilitation service that
promotes health and well-being by enabling individuals to participate in activities of

daily living, education, work, play, leisure, and community life.

(c) Speech-Language Therapy refers to the assessment, treatment, and management of

speech, language, communication, cognitive-communication, voice, fluency, and

SEC. 4. Creation and Implementation of the Program. — The DOH shall create,
establish, administer, and implement a nationwide program to provide free occupational therapy
and speech-language therapy services to qualified indigent individuals, hereinatter referred to as

the Program.

The DOH shall serve as the lead implementing agency and shall formulate the policies,
standards, guidelines, service delivery mechanisms, staffing requirements, referral systems, and

monitoring procedures necessary for the effective implementation of the Program.

The Program shall be implemented in all public hospitals nationwide, including hospitals

operated by the national government, LGUs, and other government entities.

The DOH shall ensure the progressive availability of appropriate therapy services in all

public hospitals through the establishment or designation of therapy units. employment or

Public hospitals that lack the necessary personnel, equipment, or facilities shall comply

The absence of an in-house occupational therapist or speech-language pathologist shall not,

SEC. 5. Identification and Qualification of Beneficiaries. — An individual shal! qualify

for coverage under the Program upon satisfaction of the following requirements:
(a) The individual qualifies as indigent under Section 3(a) of this Act; and

(b) The individual has been assessed by a qualified health professional as requiring

occupational therapy, speech-language therapy, or both.

For purposes of establishing indigency, either of the following shall be accepted as an

alternative basis for eligibility:

(1) Verification that the individual belongs to a household included in the latest applicable

official Listahanan of the DSWD, or in its officially designated successor; or

(2) A written endorsement, indigency certification, or other official document issued by
the LSWDO of the city or municipality where the individual resides, confirming that
the individual has been assessed and determined to be indigent under applicable social

welfare assessment procedures.

Inclusion in Listahanan and formal endorsement by the LSWDO shall constitute separate
and alternative means of establishing indigency. An individual shall not be required to satisfy both

requirements.

The absence of an individual's name from Listahanan shall not, by itself, be a ground for
denying eligibility when the individual has been formally assessed and endorsed as indigent by the

appropriate LSWDO in accordance with this Act.

The DOH shall coordinate with the DSWD and concerned LSWDOs to establish uniform
procedures for beneficiary verification, documentation, and referral. Such procedures shall
minimize unnecessary documentary requirements and prevent unreasonable delays in the delivery

of services.

All beneficiaries shall undergo the appropriate clinical assessment by a qualified health

professional to determine their need for occupational therapy, speech-language therapy, or both.

SEC. 6. Coverage of Free Services. — Qualified beneficiaries shall be entitled to the

following services without professional fees or charges:
(a) Initial assessment, evaluation, and development of an individualized therapy plan;

(b) Occupational therapy interventions to improve or maintain activities of daily living,
fine and gross motor skills, sensory processing, functional mobility, adaptive skills,

self-care, and participation in educational, vocational, and community activities:

(c) Speech-language therapy interventions for speech. language, communication,
cognitive-communication, voice, fluency, and swallowing disorders, as clinically

appropriate;

(d) Individualized therapy sessions, progress monitoring, reassessment, and modification

of therapy plans;
(e) Caregiver education, family guidance, and home-based therapeutic recommendations;

(f) Referral to appropriate medical, developmental, educational, psychological, or

rehabilitation services when necessary; and

(g) Other related services that the DOH determines necessary for the effective delivery of

occupational therapy and speech-language therapy.

The frequency, duration, and number of therapy sessions shall be determined by qualified
professionals according to the beneficiary's clinical needs, established standards of care, and

applicable clinical guidelines.

Necessary supplies, assistive devices, and ancillary services may be covered in accordance

with the implementing rules and regulations, clinical necessity, and available appropriations.

No qualified beneficiary shall be charged professional fees for services covered by the

Program.

SEC. 7. Mandatory Implementation in All Public Hospitals Nationwide. — A\\ public

hospitals shall implement the Program in accordance with the standards and guidelines established

by the DOH.
For this purpose, every public hospital shall:
(a) Designate a focal person or unit responsible for implementing the Program;

(b) Establish procedures for beneficiary screening, verification of indigency, clinical

assessment, scheduling, and referral;

(c) Employ, engage, or otherwise secure the services of qualified occupational therapists
and speech-language pathologists, as appropriate to the facility's service capacity and

patient needs;
(d) Establish referral arrangements for services that cannot be delivered within the hospital;

(e) Display and disseminate information regarding the availability of free services and the

procedures for accessing the Program;

(f) Maintain records of beneficiaries served, therapy sessions provided, and resources

utilized, subject to applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws; and

(g) Submit periodic implementation reports to the DOH.

All public hospitals shall recognize valid indigency endorsements or certifications issued
by the LSWDO of the beneficiary's city or municipality of residence as an alternative to inclusion

in Listahanan.

No qualified beneficiary shall be denied access to the Program solely because the
beneficiary is not listed in Listahanan, provided that the required LSWDO endorsement and

clinical eligibility requirements have been satisfied.

The DOH shall prescribe minimum service standards and implementation timelines and
shall provide the technical and administrative assistance necessary to ensure nationwide access to

the Program.

SEC. 8. Personnel, Facilities, and Capacity Development. — The DOH shall develop a
national workforce and facility development plan to ensure the adequate availability of

occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists in public hospitals.
For this purpose, the DOH may:

(a) Provide assistance for the establishment or improvement of rehabilitation units and

therapy facilities;
(b) Allocate or augment qualified personnel in underserved public hospitals;

(c) Conduct training and continuing professional development activities consistent with

applicable professional standards;
(d) Provide necessary equipment and therapeutic materials; and

(e) Enter into service agreements with qualified government and accredited private
institutions to expand access to therapy services, particularly in geographically isolated

and disadvantaged areas.

All services shall be delivered by duly qualified professionals in accordance with

applicable Philippine laws, licensing requirements, and professional standards.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this

Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act following its effectivity.

Thereafter, the funds necessary for the continued implementation and expansion of the
Program shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act, subject to applicable

budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

The DOH may accept grants, donations, and other lawful forms of assistance for the
Program, subject to existing laws and government regulations, provided that such assistance shall
not compromise the equitable delivery of services or the independence and integrity of the

Program.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the DSWD, the Department of the
Interior and Local Government, the Department of Budget and Management, LGUs, relevant
professional regulatory bodies, professional organizations, and representatives of persons with
disabilities and beneficiary groups, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations

necessary to carry out this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall include eligibility criteria, acceptable proof
of indigency, procedures for accepting LSWDO endorsements, clinical assessment standards,
service delivery requirements, implementation timelines for public hospitals, workforce and
facility requirements, monitoring procedures, and safeguards against fraud and misuse of public

funds.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional

or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
administrative rules and regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this

Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation, in accordance with

applicable law.
