                             AN ACT
PROVIDING HAZARD PAY, NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE, AND GROUP
ACCIDENT INSURANCE TO ACCREDITED DISASTER RESPONSE RESCUERS AND
VOLUNTEERS ENGAGED IN AUTHORIZED DISASTER RESPONSE AND
EMERGENCY OPERATIONS DURING FORMALLY DECLARED STATES OF
CALAMITY, EMERGENCY, OR IMMINENT DISASTER, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

Disasters and emergencies often place communities in sudden danger, from floods,
landslides, typhoons, fires, and other hazards. In these critical moments, accredited
rescuers and disaster volunteers step forward to save lives, evacuate families, and
assist communities in distress. Their work often involves great personal risk, long
hours, and difficult conditions.

Yet despite the dangers they face, many volunteers do not always receive clear and
timely support for their welfare and protection while performing these vital duties.

The Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010 (Republic Act
No. 10121) recognizes the important role of disaster volunteers and provides that
those who suffer injury or death while performing disaster response duties are
entitled to benefits and insurance protection. More recently, Republic Act No. 12287
strengthened the country’s disaster preparedness system by allowing the
government to act early through the declaration of a State of Imminent Disaster.




                                                                            1 | Page
However, in practice, gaps remain in ensuring that rescuers and volunteers
consistently receive hazard pay, insurance, and other benefits while responding to
officially declared disasters and emergencies.

This bill seeks to strengthen the protection and welfare of accredited rescuers and
disaster volunteers. It provides hazard pay during periods of actual deployment in
officially declared disasters, ensures access to national health insurance coverage,
and guarantees group accident insurance for volunteers who may suffer injury,
disability, or death while performing rescue and disaster response operations. It also
establishes clear procedures and documentation requirements so that benefits can
be given promptly while remaining transparent and compliant with government
auditing rules.

By recognizing the sacrifices of those who risk their lives to save others, this measure
supports and strengthens the country’s disaster response system. Providing clear and
reliable protection for rescuers and volunteers will encourage more citizens to serve
their communities during times of crisis.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Rescuers and Volunteers
Protection and Benefits Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the welfare of individuals who, as part of organized disaster risk reduction
and management (DRRM) operations, expose themselves to hazards and life-
threatening conditions to save lives and protect communities. The State shall ensure
that accredited rescuers and disaster volunteers engaged in authorized disaster
response and emergency operations during formally declared states of calamity,
emergency, or imminent disaster are provided with hazard pay, national health
insurance coverage, and group accident insurance, subject to transparent standards,
proper accreditation, and accountability in the use of public funds.




                                                                              3 | Page
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Accredited Rescuers refer to individuals who are (i) employed, appointed,
       detailed, or officially designated by a government agency or local government
       unit (LGU), or (ii) members of organized response teams of duly accredited
       civil society organizations (CSOs) or non-government organizations (NGOs),
       and who are mobilized to conduct search, rescue, retrieval, medical first
       response, evacuation, relief distribution, incident management support, or
       other emergency response functions, and who are accredited/registered
       pursuant to Section 4 of this Act.

   (b) Disaster Volunteers refer to individuals or organized volunteer groups
       mobilized to augment government or private sector personnel for DRRM
       programs and activities, who are accredited/registered through the Local
       Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Offices (LDRRMOs) for inclusion in
       the database of community disaster volunteers maintained by the Office of
       Civil Defense (OCD), consistent with Republic Act No. 10121.

   (c) Authorized Operation refers to an emergency or disaster operation officially
       activated or directed by the NDRRMC/OCD or the appropriate Regional/Local
       DRRM Council or LDRRMO, conducted only during the effectivity of a formal
       declaration of any of the following by the competent authority: (i) State of
       Calamity; (ii) State of Emergency; or (iii) State of Imminent Disaster under
       Republic Act No. 12287.

   (d) Actual Deployment refers to the period during which an accredited rescuer
       or disaster volunteer is physically present at, or assigned to, the incident site,
       staging area, evacuation site, operations center, or other officially designated
       area of operations, performing duties under an Authorized Operation.

   (e) Hazard Pay refers to additional compensation granted under this Act for
       performing hazardous duties and enduring physical hardships only during
       periods of actual exposure to danger or occupational risk in the course of an
       Authorized Operation, as certified in accordance with this Act and its
       implementing rules and regulations (IRR).

   (f) Certification of Exposure and Service refers to the written certification
       issued by the Incident Commander or authorized supervising official, attesting
       to (i) the existence of an Authorized Operation and the declaration relied upon,
       (ii) the accredited rescuer’s or disaster volunteer’s actual deployment dates

                                                                               4 | Page
      and hours, (iii) duties performed, and (iv) the nature/level of risk exposure,
      supported by the documentary requirements under this Act and the IRR.

   (g) National Health Insurance Coverage refers to coverage under the National
       Health Insurance Program (NHIP) administered by PhilHealth, subject to its
       benefit packages and rules.

   (h) Group Accident Insurance refers to insurance coverage procured or funded
       under this Act providing benefits for accidental death, dismemberment, and
       permanent disability, and such other accident-related benefits as may be
       provided in the IRR, covering accredited rescuers and disaster volunteers
       during Authorized Operations and for injuries sustained therein.

SECTION 4. Coverage and Accreditation Requirement. —

   (a) Accreditation/Registration. — Only accredited rescuers and disaster
       volunteers who are duly listed and certified by the appropriate LDRRMO/OCD
       database or the head of the mobilizing agency, as may be provided in the IRR,
       shall be entitled to benefits under this Act.

   (b) Alignment with RA 10121. — Accreditation and registration mechanisms shall
       be aligned with Section 13 of Republic Act No. 10121, including submission of
       volunteer lists to the OCD through the LDRRMOs for accreditation and
       inclusion in the database of community disaster volunteers.

SECTION 5. Hazard Pay. —

   (a) Entitlement. No Blanket Grant.— Accredited rescuers and disaster volunteers
       who render actual service in an Authorized Operation shall be entitled to
       hazard pay only for the period of Actual Deployment and actual exposure to
       hazard, subject to Certification of Exposure and Service and compliance with
       documentary requirements. No blanket, across-the-board, or sweeping grant
       of hazard pay shall be made without showing that the qualifications and
       conditions under this Act and the IRR have been met.

   (b) Rate for Government Personnel. — For accredited rescuers who receive regular
       compensation from government, hazard pay shall be computed at a rate of not
       less than twenty percent (20%) of the monthly basic salary (or its equivalent)
       for the period of actual exposure, and shall be pro-rated when the deployment


                                                                            5 | Page
      covers less than a full month, in accordance with standards to be prescribed in
      the IRR based on risk level, nature of assignment, and place of deployment.

   (c) Rate for Volunteers. — For disaster volunteers, hazard pay shall be in the form
       of a daily hazard allowance corresponding to actual days of deployment and
       exposure. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM), in consultation
       with the DILG and OCD, shall prescribe standardized daily rates and risk tiers
       under the IRR.

   (d) Required Certification. — Payment of hazard pay shall require Certification of
       Exposure and Service issued by: (i) the Incident Commander; or (ii) the head
       of the mobilizing agency/LDRRMO, or a duly authorized representative,
       stating the formal declaration relied upon (State of Calamity/State of
       Emergency/State of Imminent Disaster), the authority activating the
       operation, the inclusive dates and hours of actual deployment, the duties
       performed, and the assessed level of risk exposure.

   (e) Minimum Documentary Requirements. — No disbursement of hazard pay shall
       be made without the following minimum documents, in addition to those
       required under existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules: (i) a copy
       of the formal declaration of State of Calamity, State of Emergency, or State of
       Imminent Disaster; (ii) written activation order, mission order, deployment
       order, or equivalent instruction issued by competent authority; (iii)
       attendance log, duty roster, or equivalent record showing Actual Deployment;
       and (iv) Certification of Exposure and Service.

   (f) Implementing Standards on “Actual Exposure.” — The IRR shall define objective
       indicators of “actual exposure,” adopt risk tiers, and specify reasonable
       minimum exposure thresholds to ensure that hazard pay is granted only to
       those who meet the statutory conditions.

SECTION 6. National Health Insurance and Group Accident Insurance. —

   (a) NHIP Enrollment of Volunteers. — All disaster volunteers covered by this Act
       shall be enrolled in the NHIP. Premium contributions for qualified disaster
       volunteers without employer-shouldered premium shall be subsidized by the
       National Government, subject to guidelines under the IRR and PhilHealth
       rules.



                                                                             6 | Page
   (b) Group Accident Insurance. — Accredited rescuers and disaster volunteers
       covered by this Act shall be provided Group Accident Insurance for the
       duration of their Authorized Operation and for such reasonable period
       thereafter as may be necessary to cover injuries suffered during the
       Authorized Operation, as may be provided in the IRR.

   (c) Compensatory Benefits. Consistency with RA 10121. — Any disaster volunteer
       who incurs death or injury while engaged in DRRM activities defined under
       Republic Act No. 10121 shall be entitled to compensatory benefits and
       individual personnel accident insurance as may be defined under the
       guidelines.

   (d) Non-Diminution. Coordination. — Benefits under this Act shall be without
       prejudice to any existing benefits, insurance, or entitlements under other laws,
       rules, or valid contracts, subject to coordination and avoidance of double
       recovery as may be provided in the IRR.

SECTION 7. Funding and Appropriations. —

   (a) National Funding. — The amount necessary for the initial and continuing
       implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
       Appropriations Act (GAA).

   (b) Local Funding. — LGUs are authorized to allocate funds from their Local
       Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF) for allowable
       expenses under this Act, including hazard pay for local deployments and
       premiums/insurance costs for accredited rescuers and disaster volunteers
       within their jurisdiction, subject to budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), the Office of Civil Defense (OCD), the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM), and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), in consultation
with accredited volunteer organizations and concerned stakeholders, shall
promulgate the IRR.

The non-promulgation of the IRR shall not prevent the immediate implementation of
this Act upon its effectivity, and the concerned agencies shall issue interim guidelines
to operationalize accreditation/registration, enrollment, insurance procurement, and
benefit processing.

SECTION 9. Accountability. — Any public official who, without lawful justification,
unjustifiably delays, refuses, or fails to process or release benefits under this Act
despite complete documentation and availability of funds shall be subject to
appropriate administrative liability under existing civil service rules and regulations,
and to such other liabilities as may be applicable under existing laws.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
