                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Section 11, Article XIII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that the state
shall adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development that makes
essential goods, health, and other social services available to all people at affordable cost,
with the priority for the needs of the underprivileged, the sick, the elderly, women, and
children.

        In our culture, our elders are traditionally revered as the pillars of the family and
keepers of cultural wisdom. But despite our high regard for our senior citizens, the reality
is that a large number of senior citizens are living below the poverty line and are
vulnerable to chronic and debilitating illnesses such as cardiovascular diseases, diabetes,
and cancer. In 2025, the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
recorded1 around 4 million indigent senior citizens as beneficiaries under its Social
Pension Program.

         The government has established various social protection measures, which
include the RA No. 9994 or the Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010, the Universal Health
Care Act, and PhilHealth coverage for Senior Citizens. However, many indigent senior
citizens still seek medical attention only after illnesses have progressed to advanced
stages. In these trying times, treatment has become more costly, more invasive, and less
likely to achieve favorable outcomes. This pattern does not only diminish the quality of
life of our senior citizens but also puts a substantive financial burden on their families.

      Currently, most indigent seniors only visit a hospital when a disease has reached
an advanced, painful, and often terminal stage. By providing these comprehensive

        Furthermore, providing free comprehensive screenings is a highly cost-effective
strategy for the Philippine public healthcare system. Critics might argue that funding
executive check-ups for millions of poor seniors would strain the national budget.
However, we can view this as an investment. Treating advanced-stage illnesses, stroke
rehabilitation, and prolonged intensive care hospitalizations place a massive financial
burden on public tertiary hospitals. By investing a modest amount in early diagnostics,
the government can save billions of pesos in long-term tertiary treatment costs, allowing
public health resources to be distributed more efficiently.

       While Republic Act No. 9994 provides discounts on medicines and medical
services, these deductions are often meaningless to indigent seniors who have zero
disposable income. For someone choosing between a meal and a diagnostic test, survival
will always take precedence over prevention. Free “executive check-ups” bridge this
economic divide, ensuring that quality healthcare is a right enjoyed by all, rather than a
luxury reserved for the affluent.

       By establishing a unified, zero-cost framework for senior citizens with an annual
comprehensive geriatric health assessment, and integrating the mandates of the
Department of Health (DOH), PhilHealth, and local government units (LGUs), this
measure will bridge systemic healthcare gaps, mitigate out-of-pocket medical expenses,
and safeguard the dignity and well-being of Filipino senior citizens.

        A nation that cares for its elderly reflects its moral character. Indigent senior
citizens have spent their youth contributing to the country’s economy, raising the next
generation of workers, and paying indirect taxes. In their twilight years, they deserve to
live with dignity, free from the constant fear of a sudden, catastrophic medical debt.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT ESTABLISHING A FREE ANNUAL COMPREHENSIVE
GERIATRIC HEALTH ASSESSMENT PROGRAM FOR INDIGENT SENIOR
   CITIZENS, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “LIBRENG ‘EXECUTIVE
CHECK-UP’ PARA SA INDIGENT SENIOR CITIZENS ACT,” PROVIDING
         FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Libreng
‘Executive Check-Up’ para sa Indigent Senior Citizens Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
them. Pursuant to Article II, Section 15, Article XIII, Sections 11 and 14 of the 1987
Constitution, the State shall adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health
development and endeavor to make quality health services available and accessible to all,
particularly vulnerable and disadvantaged sectors, including indigent senior citizens.
        The State recognizes that senior citizens deserve dignity, proper healthcare, and
preventive medical intervention to ensure healthy, productive, and independent living.
Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize a comprehensive health assessment
program to promote effective disease detection, reduce avoidable hospitalization, prevent
catastrophic medical expenditures, and improve the quality of life of indigent senior
citizens.
       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:

     a) Ensure equitable access to comprehensive healthcare services for indigent senior
     citizens;


                                                                                  Page 3 of 8
     b) Promote effective detection and management of illnesses commonly affecting
     elderly Filipinos;

     c) Reduce morbidity, disability, and premature mortality among senior citizens;

     d) Complement and strengthen the implementation of Republic Act No. 9994 or the
     Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010, Republic Act No. 10645, Republic Act No.
     11223 or the Universal Health Care Act, and other relevant health laws;

     e) Minimize out-of-pocket healthcare expenses among indigent senior citizens; and

     f) Strengthen inter-agency coordination in the delivery of geriatric healthcare
     services.


     SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
       a) Indigent Senior Citizen refers to any Filipino citizen aged sixty (60) years old
          and above who is classified as poor, near-poor, vulnerable, or indigent under
          the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Community-Based Monitoring
          System (CBMS) or National Household Targeting System for Poverty
          Reduction (NHTS-PR), recipient of social pension benefits, beneficiary of the
          Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps), or otherwise certified by the Local
          Social Welfare and Development Office (LSWDO) as indigent, and as
          otherwise defined in Section 6 of this Act;

       b) Executive Check-Up, for the purpose of this Act, refers to a Comprehensive
          Geriatric Health Assessment, consisting of physician consultation, laboratory
          diagnostics, disease-risk screening, imaging procedures, geriatric evaluation,
          and referral services intended for the early detection, prevention, and
          management of diseases affecting senior citizens; and

       c) Accredited Health Facility refers to Department of Health hospitals, local
          government hospitals, rural health units (RHUs), barangay health stations
          (BHS), public health facilities, and accredited private health facilities
          authorized to implement this Act.
        SEC. 5. Establishment of the Free Annual “Executive Check-Up” Program. –
There is hereby established a Free Annual “Executive Check-Up” Program or the Annual
Comprehensive Geriatric Health Assessment for Indigent Senior Citizens, hereinafter
referred to as the Program, to be administered by the Department of Health (DOH), in
coordination with the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), Department
of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of the Interior and Local



                                                                                Page 4 of 8
Government (DILG), National Commission of Senior Citizens (NCSC), Local
Government Units (LGUs), and other relevant agencies.
        Qualified beneficiaries shall be entitled to one (1) free annual executive check-up
per calendar year.
       SEC. 6. Coverage and Eligibility. – All Filipino indigent senior citizens, classified
as poor, near-poor, vulnerable, or indigent identified under the implementation of Republic
Act (RA) No. 11315 or the Community-Based Monitoring System implemented by the
Philippine Statistics Authority in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), and certified as indigent by the Local Social Welfare and
Development Office (LSWDO), as mandated under RA No. 11223 otherwise known as the
Universal Health Care Act, shall be entitled to the benefits provided under this Act.
        Senior citizens shall be entitled to one (1) free comprehensive health assessment
subject to the availability of PhilHealth funds. No qualified senior citizen shall be denied
access to the Program due to lack of complete documentation, subject to subsequent
validation.
      SEC. 7. Free “Executive Check-Up” Package. – The Program shall provide a
Comprehensive Geriatric Health Assessment, including but not limited to:
       A) Basic Medical Evaluation:
          1. Medical history review;
          2. Comprehensive physical examination;
          3. Blood pressure monitoring;
          4. Nutritional assessment;
          5. Medication review; and
          6. Risk assessment for chronic illnesses.


       B) Mandatory Diagnostic and Laboratory Services
          1. Complete Blood Count (CBC);
          2. Urinalysis;
          3. Fasting Blood Sugar or HbA1c;
          4. Lipid Profile;
          5. Blood Chemistry, including kidney function tests;
          6. Electrocardiogram (ECG);
          7. Chest X-ray, where medically indicated;



                                                                                  Page 5 of 8
           8. Vision and hearing screening; and
           9. Other laboratory examinations as determined by the DOH.
       C) Preventive Disease Screening
           1. Hypertension and cardiovascular disease screening;

           2. Diabetes screening;
           3. Kidney disease screening;
           4. Cancer risk screening subject to age and medical guidelines;
           5. Osteoporosis and mobility assessment, where available;
           6. Mental health and depression screening; and
           7. Cognitive impairment and dementia screening.
       D) Consultation and Referral Services
           1. Physician consultation;
           2. Referral to specialists when medically necessary;
           3. Telemedicine consultation where available; and
           4. Referral to PhilHealth Konsulta providers, government medical assistance
           programs, and medicine access programs.
       The DOH shall periodically review and update the check-up package based on
disease prevalence, public health needs, and advancements in geriatric care.
        SEC. 8. Schedule of Free Executive Check-Up. – To promote health, identify
risks, and ensure effective and accurate diagnosis, PhilHealth shall establish a system of
availing the services under this Act, ensuring accessibility to senior citizens in availing the
free Comprehensive Geriatric Health Assessment.
       SEC. 9. Duties and Responsibilities of the Department of Health. – The
Department of Health (DOH) shall lead the implementation of this Act and develop
standards, protocols, and clinical guidelines. The DOH shall also ensure adequate
availability of diagnostic services in public healthcare facilities and shall conduct public
awareness campaigns. The Office of the Health Secretary shall submit annual
accomplishment and implementation reports to Congress.


       SEC. 10. Duties and Responsibilities of PhilHealth. – PhilHealth shall prioritize
diagnostic benefit packages for senior citizens, integrate Program services with PhilHealth
Konsulta, to serve as the primary mechanism for reimbursement of the cost incurred in
rendering the services indicated in this Act. Also, PhilHealth shall establish a streamlined
system of processing claims to ensure ease of access for the beneficiaries.



                                                                                     Page 6 of 8
       No qualified indigent senior citizen shall incur out-of-pocket expenses for services
covered under this Act.

        SEC. 11. Duties and Responsibilities of the National Commission of Senior
Citizens (NCSC), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and Local
Government Units (LGUs). – The NCSC, in coordination with the DSWD and PSA, shall
identify, verify, and maintain the registry of qualified beneficiaries.

       Further, LGUs shall assist potential beneficiaries in registration and enrollment,
conduct outreach and mobile medical missions, facilitate assistance for vulnerable
beneficiaries in terms of mobility and transportation where practicable, and ensure
accessibility for bedridden and mobility-impaired senior citizens.

        SEC. 12. Mobile Executive Check-Up Services. – The DOH, in partnership with
LGUs, shall deploy mobile medical teams and “executive check-up” caravans to
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas to ensure that indigent senior citizens have
access to preventive healthcare services.

        Priority shall be given to bedridden senior citizens, persons with disabilities, and
elderly individuals residing in remote communities.

         SEC. 13. Data Sharing and Monitoring. – The DOH, PhilHealth, DSWD, and
LGUs shall establish an integrated information system for monitoring beneficiaries,
utilization of services, medical records, and program effectiveness, subject to Republic Act
No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

       SEC. 14. Prohibited Acts. – Sec. 10 of RA No. 9994 or the Expanded Senior
Citizens Act of 2010 states that refusal to honor a senior citizen's card or violates
any provision of the Act has a penalty of imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years
and a fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos to One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php50,000.00
to Php100,000.) for a first offense, escalating to One Hundred Thousand Pesos to
Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php100,000.00 to Php200,000.00) for subsequent
offenses.

       Section 38 of RA No. 1122 penalizes healthcare providers/PhilHealth-
contracted facilities for unethical acts or abuse of authority against patients;
violations can carry imprisonment of six (6) months and one (1) day up to six (6)
years, on top of contract-based sanctions (suspension/termination of PhilHealth
accreditation).

      Further, falsification of medical records or beneficiary information and
misappropriation or misuse of government funds intended for the Program shall
be penalized under existing laws.

        SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and PhilHealth.
Thereafter, the amount necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

        SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with PhilHealth, DSWD, Department
of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), National Council for Senior Citizens
(NCSC), and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR) necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not otherwise affected shall remain in
full force and effect.

        SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

        SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
