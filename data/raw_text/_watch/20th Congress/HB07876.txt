                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution mandates the State to promote social justice in all phases of
national development and to reduce social, economic, and political inequalities. Article
II, Section 9 directs the State to promote a just and dynamic social order that frees the
people from poverty, while Article XIII obliges the State to ensure that the benefits of
development are equitably shared. These constitutional principles require policies that
address structural disparities that leave certain communities and workers at a persistent
disadvantage.

        Local government units are central to the realization of these mandates. Provincial
capital towns, in particular, serve as centers of governance, commerce, and public service
delivery. Yet currently, twenty eight (28) provinces have no component city, independent
component city, or highly urbanized city within their jurisdiction. 1 These capital towns
are therefore expected to perform city-level functions while remaining municipalities,
operating under limited fiscal and institutional capacity.

       Where a provincial capital municipality is classified as first-class by the
Department of Finance and is certified by the Bureau of Local Government Finance to be
less than fifty percent (50%) dependent on the National Tax Allotment (NTA), it has

demonstrated fiscal self-reliance through the effective exercise of the extensive revenue-
raising powers granted to local government units under the Local Government Code,
including sustained collection efforts and the generation of income from both tax
revenues and non-tax receipts. The continued classification of such a locality as a
municipality under these circumstances results in a structural misalignment between its
proven economic capacity and the scope of its institutional authority.

       This structural gap has broader developmental implications. Without city status,
economically capable capital municipalities are unable to fully maximize their fiscal
powers, expand revenue bases, or access enhanced resource allocations available to cities.
The absence of a city within a province limits its ability to establish a strong urban growth
center that can attract investments, stimulate local industries, generate employment, and
deliver higher-level public services. Consequently, economic activity and labor
opportunities tend to concentrate in established metropolitan areas, perpetuating
regional disparities and migration pressures.

       By providing for the conversion of provincial capital municipalities that meet
clearly defined fiscal criteria, specifically first-class income classification and less than
fifty percent (50%) dependency on the NTA, this measure adopts an objective and
performance-based approach to cityhood. It recognizes demonstrated economic viability
as a basis for institutional upgrading, thereby ensuring that only fiscally capable and self-
sustaining municipalities qualify.

       Cities receive a greater share of national tax allotments2 and possess stronger
capacity to stimulate local economies and deliver social services. Hence, when an entire
province lacks a city, its working population is deprived of these developmental
advantages. This inequity undermines the constitutional commitment to balanced
regional development and weakens the State's duty to protect labor.

       This measure seeks to correct this disparity by recognizing the central role of
provincial capital towns in local and countryside development. By enabling their
conversion into component cities under defined conditions, the State aligns institutional
capacity with actual governance responsibilities. This policy strengthens local economies,
expands fiscal space for public services, and promotes employment growth closer to
where people live.

  AN ACT CONVERTING THE CAPITAL TOWN OF PROVINCES WITH LESS
THAN FIFTY PERCENT (50%) DEPENDENCY IN NATIONAL TAX ALLOTMENT
INTO A COMPONENT CITY, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 450 OF
      REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "LOCAL
GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991," AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9009, AS
           FURTHER AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11683

  Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Conversion. Section 450 of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the
"Local Government Code of 1991," as amended by Republic Act No. 9009, as further
amended by Republic Act No. 11683, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 450. Requisites for Creation. - (a) A municipality or a cluster of barangays
      may be converted into a component city if it has a locally generated average annual
      income, as certified by the Department of Finance, of at least One hundred million
      pesos (P100,000,000.00) for the last two (2) consecutive years based on 2000
      constant prices, and if it has either of the following requisites:

      “(i) a contiguous territory of at least territory of at least one hundred (100) square
      kilometers, as certified by the Land Management Bureau; or

      "(ii) a population of not less than one hundred fifty thousand (150,000) inhabitants,
      as certified by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA).

                                                                                               Page 4 of 6
"Provided, That the creation thereof shall not reduce the land area, population and
income of the original unit or units at the time of said creation to less than the
minimum requirements prescribed herein.

"A municipality or a cluster of barangays with a locally generated average annual
income, as certified by the Department of Finance, of at least Four hundred million
pesos (P400,000,000.00) for the last two (2) consecutive years based on 2012
constant prices may also be converted into a component city if it has either a
population of not less than one hundred thousand (100,000) inhabitants as certified
by the PSA or a contiguous territory of at least one hundred (100) square
kilometers, as certified by the Land Management Bureau; Provided, That three (3)
years after the effectivity of this Act and every three (3) years thereafter. the
threshold amount of Four hundred million pesos (P400,000,000.00) shall be
increased by five percent (5%).

"THE REQUIREMENT ON INCOME, POPULATION, AND LAND AREA
PRESCRIBED HEREIN SHALL NOT APPLY IF A MUNICIPALITY TO BE
CONVERTED INTO A CITY IS:

“(I) THE CAPITAL TOWN OF ANY PROVINCE;

"(II) A FIRST CLASS MUNICIPALITY AS CERTIFIED BY THE DEPARTMENT
OF FINANCE (DOF), IN ACCORDANCE WITH REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11964,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE 'AUTOMATIC INCOME CLASSIFICATION OF
LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS ACT’; AND

“(III) LESS THAN FIFTY PERCENT (50%) DEPENDENT ON THE NATIONAL
TAX ALLOTMENT (NTA) BASED ON THE AVERAGE OF THE LAST THREE (3)
CONSECUTIVE YEARS, AS CERTIFIED BY THE THE BUREAU OF LOCAL
GOVERNMENT FINANCE (BLGF).

"(b) The territorial jurisdiction of a newly-created city shall be properly identified
by metes and bounds. The requirement on land area shall not apply where the city
proposed to be created is composed of one (1) or more islands. The territory need
not be contiguous if it comprises two (2) or more islands.

“(c) The average annual income shall include the income accruing to the general
fund, exclusive of specific funds, transfers, and non-recurring income.

"(d) In order to minimize the effect of conversion, the newly converted cities shall,
upon enactment of this Act, bring their respective allotment derived from the
national taxes as municipalities to the allotment of cities: Provided, That said
portability shall be effective for a period of three (3) years from conversion into
cityhood."

SECTION 2. Repealing Clause. - All other laws, decrees, executive orders,
implementing rules and regulations, issuances, or any part thereof inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 3. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect. Approved,

SECTION 4. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication, in English and Filipino, in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2)
newspapers of general circulation.
