                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Filipino economy is driven primarily by domestic consumption. According
to the Asian Development Bank (ADB), household final consumption expenditure —
the spending of Filipino families on goods and services — accounts for over 70% of
the country’s gross domestic product, making consumer spending the largest engine
of economic growth and resilience in the Philippines. In an economy where every
Filipino contributes to and relies on market transactions, strengthening the legal
foundations that protect consumers is not only sound policy, but a fundamental duty
of the State.
        Consumer protection is essential to ensure that market transactions are fair,
transparent, and safe. As markets evolve — with digital commerce, cross-border
purchases, and complex product offerings now the norm — existing laws must be
updated to meet contemporary challenges. This bill, entitled the Enhanced Consumer
Act, modernizes and fortifies the legal framework for consumer protection to reflect the
realities of today’s marketplace and to safeguard the rights of every Filipino as a
consumer.
        This measure affirms, in clear and unequivocal terms, the fundamental rights
of consumers—among them the right to safety, truthful information, fair treatment, and
effective redress—while also promoting responsible and informed consumer behavior.
It expands the State’s ability to prevent and penalize deceptive and abusive practices,
including pyramid and Ponzi schemes, fraudulent sales promotions, and misleading
advertising that preys on trust and financial insecurity.
       To promote transparency and fairness, the bill requires that consumers be
given clear, accessible transaction documents—whether printed or electronic—at no
additional cost. It strengthens labeling rules to ensure that essential information,
including expiration dates and instructions, is easy to read and understand. It also
improves oversight of imported goods to help protect Filipino consumers from



                                                                                      1
counterfeit, unsafe, or substandard products entering the market. Recognizing the rise
of digital commerce, the measure updates rules governing promotions and marketing
to ensure that consumers are not misled by false promises or manipulative tactics.
       At its core, this bill is about protecting dignity. It recognizes that every peso
spent by a Filipino family represents hours of labor, sacrifice, and hope for a better life.
In an economy where consumer spending is the backbone of growth, empowering
consumers with stronger rights, clearer protections, and meaningful remedies benefits
every Filipino. Whether buying food, medicines, electronics, or services, consumers
stand to gain from a robust framework that promotes fairness, transparency, and
accountability in the marketplace.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

        SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within 90 days after the
effectivity of this Act, the DTI, in coordination with the implementing agencies under
this Act, other relevant government agencies and stakeholders, promulgate the rules
and regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 18. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

        SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, proclamations or administrative regulations that are inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 20. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette, or in a newspaper of general circulation.
