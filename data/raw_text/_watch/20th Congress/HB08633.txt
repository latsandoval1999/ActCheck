                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish a comprehensive and responsive Civil Registration
and Vital Statistics (CRVS) system that ensures the accurate, timely, and secure
registration of births, deaths, marriages, and other changes in civil status of all
Filipinos while safeguarding the integrity of civil registry documents. A reliable
CRVS system is fundamental to governance to generate accurate demographic data
for planning, target social protection programs, and uphold every individual’s legal
identity.

       The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that approximately 3.7
million Filipinos, which comprises 3.4% of the population, remain without birth
registration1. A significant portion of this consists of children aged zero to fourteen
(0 - 14) years old. It exposes them to risks of exclusion from basic services such as
education, healthcare, and social welfare. The country also faces serious integrity
issues in civil registration. Previous reports indicate the proliferation of fraudulent
and irregular civil registry documents, particularly through delayed registration
processes2. PSA disclosed that millions of late birth registrations filed in recent years
are subject to verification, while thousands of suspicious records have already been
flagged, blocked, or referred for legal action. This includes high-profile cases3 that
revealed systemic vulnerabilities in identity documentation which undermines
public trust and poses risks to national security and public administration.

       Furthermore, this proposed measure aligns with Philippine’s commitments to
international human rights instruments. This includes the Universal Declaration of
Human Rights which recognizes the right of every person to legal identity, and the

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A COMPREHENSIVE AND RESPONSIVE CIVIL
REGISTRATION AND VITAL STATISTICS SYSTEM, PROVIDING FUNDS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Civil Registration and
Vital Statistics (CRVS) Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
deploy a comprehensive, responsive, and fraud resistant Civil Registration and Vital
Statistics (CRVS) system, consistent with Republic Act (RA) No. 10173, otherwise
known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, and RA No. 11055, or the “Philippine
Identification System Act”, to ensure that the people’s right to privacy, confidentiality,
and other basic rights are at all times upheld and protected.

       It is also declared the policy of the State to reaffirm its commitment to
international human rights by adhering to the principles embodied in the Universal
Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), including the right of every individual to be
recognized as a person before the law under Article VI. Consistent with the United
Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UN CRC), the State recognizes every
child’s right to nationality and registration immediately after birth and the
preservation of their identity, as provided in Articles VII and VIII of the convention.

       The State further upholds the right to be protected from discrimination based
on race, sex, color, language, religion, or national origin as per Article XXIV of the
International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), the right to nationality
for persons with disabilities under Article XVIII of the convention on the Rights of
Persons with Disabilities (CRPD), and the right of adolescents to birth registration as
defined in Article VIII of the UN CRC General Comment (GC) 20.

      Further, the State shall ensure that children in street situations are proactively
supported in obtaining legal identity documents through free, accessible, and


                                            1
expeditious birth registration according to Article XV of the UN CRC GC 21. It shall
further guarantee that all Filipino migrant workers are recognized as persons before
the law under Article XXIV of the International Convention on the Protection of the
Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of their Families (ICMW) within the
CRVS framework.

        SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all acts, events, and judicial and
administrative issuances affecting the civil status and vital statistics of persons in the
Philippines and all Filipino citizens abroad, including all children with at least one
(1) Filipino parent.

        SEC. 9. Free Registration of Vital Civil Registry Events. – The registration of
births, deaths and marriage is a national priority. Therefore, no fees shall be imposed
or collected in the registration of these events, whether or not these are registered on
time. All supporting documents as prerequisite to birth registration shall also be
free.

       While the first copy of any civil registration certificate is free, the CGR shall
prescribe a minimal fee for the issuance of all succeeding copies. Additionally, a
standard fee shall be collected for the registration of registrable judicial and
administrative issuances, orders, and legal instruments.

        SEC. 10. Registration and Certification of Birth. – The declaration of the
physician, midwife, nurse, birth attendant, finder or in default thereof, the
declaration of either or both parents or any person who has knowledge of the facts
of birth of the newborn child shall be sufficient for the registration of birth in the
civil register.

       The duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth (COLB) containing the above
declaration shall be submitted for registration to the Civil Registrar's Office in the
city or municipality where the birth occurred within thirty (30) calendar days from
date of birth by the aforementioned persons, declaring the following when available:

          1.​ ﻿Full name of the child,

          2.​ Sex;

          3.​ Date of birth,

          4.​ Place of birth or where found;

          6.​ Citizenship of the mother;

          7.​ Name of father;

          9.​ Date and place of marriage of parents; and

          10.​﻿Such other data as may be identified in the implementing rules and
       regulations of this Act.

       Birth and other civil registry records of persons when any or some of the
following information is absent/missing shall have equal acceptability and shall be
respected before all government, public, private, commercial, and business

          a.​ ﻿usual middle initial or middle name;

          b.​ ﻿citizenship, if stateless;

          c.​ ﻿names of parents, if unknown;

          d.​ ﻿date of marriage, if inapplicable;

          e.​ ﻿when an individual has only one birth name; or

          f.​ ﻿any analogous circumstances.

       All regulatory bodies such as, but not limited to, the Securities and Exchange
Commission (SEC) and Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, shall adhere to this law, and
adopt the necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.

       The fetus is considered born if it is alive at the time it is completely delivered
from the mother’s womb and shall be considered as live birth and shall be registered
in the Register of Births. However, if the fetus had an intra-uterine life of less than
seven months, it is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four (24) hours after its
complete delivery from the maternal womb.

       All cases of birth on board a vessel or airplane en route to the Philippines
shall be recorded in the civil register of the city or municipality where the mother
habitually resides if either of the parents is a citizen of the Philippines, or both are.
When the parents are both foreigners but not residents of the Philippines, the birth
may be recorded in any civil registry office in the Philippines, if they so desire.

     Abandoned children or foundlings whose parents, guardians or relatives are
unknown, or children committed to an orphanage or a child-caring institution with

       All LCR/PSAs in partnership with LGUs shall provide mobile birth

       SEC. 11. Delayed Registration of Birth. – A person, regardless of age, who has
not been registered with the Civil Registrar's Office at the place of birth or where
found shall apply for delayed registration within the prescribed period of thirty (30)
calendar days upon discovering that no entry of birth has been recorded in the
Register of Births.

       For delayed registration, resorting to out-of-town registration of birth may be
allowed, where the place of application for registration is other than the place of
birth of the person seeking to be registered. The LCR and PSA shall issue a COLB
free of charge regardless of delay. A person who has not been registered before the
office of the C/MCR and PSA where he or she was born within the prescribed
period of thirty (30) calendar days shall apply on the basis of his or her nearest kin.
The informant and affiant must show authentic proof of the identity of the person to
be registered, the facts of his birth, his parentage and other relevant data.

       In the implementation of this act, the LCR/PSA should conduct regular
review to assess the accessibility of registration and design and implement
programmatic interventions to address gaps in CRVS, with PSA as the central office
for data management and standards.

      The record of a person's birth shall be kept strictly confidential, and any
treatment thereof shall be fully compliant with the RA No. 10173, also known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012”.

       SEC. 12. Culturally-Sensitive Registration System to be known as the Indigenous

(BARMM), Philippine Association of Civil Registrars, National Commission on

and leaders Of IPs shall establish an Indigenous Peoples Civil Registration System
(IPCS) that is sensitive and appropriate to the unique cultural practices and
identification systems of IPs; Provided, That the IPCRS shall be harmonized with
existing laws and systems on civil registry.

       In recording the birth of IPs delivered in the traditional customs and practices
of the indigenous community, the report as to the circumstances of the birth by any
of the parents or by the tribal doctor or tribal midwife who attended to the delivery

shall be conclusive upon the Local Civil Registrar without need for further proof and

       The IPCRS shall take into account the various ethnic affiliations of the IPs in

instead of issuing separate forms for the IPCRS, shall cause the amendment of the

      In the absence of any identifying document, the self-ascription or ascription

        SEC. 13. Mandatory Continuing Education and Training Program. – To ensure
that the efficient and effective delivery of civil registration services meet the demand
for quality civil registrars who shall perform civil registration functions, the Civil
Registrar General together with LGUs, shall conduct continuing education and
training program for Civil Registrars, Consul Generals, District Recorders, and
Circuit Registrars and their staff, in collaboration with relevant partners in
government, academe, industry, and non-governmental organizations. Such training
programs shall be initiated, conducted based on Training Needs Analysis, and
evaluated regularly throughout the year to ensure constant upgrading of skills of the
Civil Registrars, Consul Generals, District Recorders, and Circuit Registrars, and
their staff.

       SEC. 14. Barangay Civil Registration System. – The Civil Registrar General shall
establish a systematic civil registration procedure at the barangay level, to be called
as the Barangay Civil Registration System (BCRS), where elected and appointed
barangay officials shall be mandated to assist in the facilitation of civil registration
within their area or jurisdiction, subject to the provision of this Act.

       The Barangay Captain shall submit to the Civil Registrar's Office of their city
or municipality the names of the Barangay Secretary as Ex-Officio Barangay Civil
Registration Officer (BCRO) and another deputized barangay official as Barangay
Civil Registration Assistant (BRA) who shall assist in the registration of all vital
events of every individual within their area of jurisdiction.

       The CRO shall engage the barangays to support in the implementation of the
law, including the provision of fiscal and budgetary support.

       Proper networking, interconnection, and collaboration between the barangay
and its respective Civil Registrar's Office shall be ensured in order to attain
mandatory civil registration of all born Filipinos. The PSA, LGUs, a national
professional organization of local civil registrars duly registered with the SEC, and
other accredited government or private institutions shall conduct regular training

and capacity building programs to upgrade the knowledge and skills in civil
registration functions of all partners in the barangay.

       SEC. 15. Free Delayed Registration Program. – LGUs are mandated to implement
a free delayed registration program for persons whose births have not been
registered and certified due to such factors as poverty, distance, or lack of literacy.
The Sangguniang Barangay shall assist in the implementation of the program
through the Barangay Civil Registration System.

       The OCRG, the DILG, and the NCIP shall jointly prepare a Free Delayed
Registration Program for Indigenous Peoples (FDRIP) which shall be implemented
by the LGUs and the NCIP regional or local offices in the localities concerned.

       SEC. 16. Strict Confidentiality of Birth Records. – The record of a person's birth
shall be kept confidential and no information relating thereto shall be issued except
upon request of the following:

          a.​ ﻿The concerned person themself, or any person authorized by them;

          b.​ Spouse;

           c.​ ﻿Any or both the parents, direct descendants, or the guardian, or
       institution legally in-charge in case of a minor;

          d.​ The Court or appropriate public official whenever absolutely necessary
       in administrative, judicial, or other official proceedings to determine the
       identity of the child's parents or other circumstances surrounding the birth;
       and

          e.​ ﻿The nearest kin in case of a person's death.

      SEC. 17. Registration and Certification of Death. – No human body shall be
buried or cremated unless the proper death certificate has been presented and
recorded in the Civil Registrar's Office.

concerned, any member of the family of the deceased or any person having
knowledge of the death shall immediately report the same to the local health
authorities who shall issue a death certificate and shall order the same to be
recorded before the Civil Registrar's Office of the city or municipality where the
death occurred.

        The death certificate which shall be issued by the attending physician of the
deceased or, in his default, by the authorized health officer, shall indicate the
following data, which shall be furnished by the person reporting the death to the

          10.​Full names of the deceased's parents; Cause of death; and

          11.​Such other relevant data that may be required.

       The surname to be entered in the registry of death and the Death Certificate
shall be the surname used prior to their death. In case the deceased is a legally
married woman, informants shall register the death using the surname she chose as
a legally married person.

       The registration of death shall be made within thirty (30) calendar days from
the time and date of death before the Civil Registrar's Office of the city or
municipality where the death occurred, or the Philippine Consulate Office in the
foreign country where the death happened.

       In the absence of the city or municipal health officer, or their authorized
representative in the place of registration, or when it is a non-working day and the
health officer or their authorized representative is not expected to be in the office,
the death shall be reported by the nearest kin of the deceased or by any person
having knowledge of the death within forty-eight (48) hours after its occurrence, to
the mayor or to the vice mayor or any member of the Sangguniang Bayan, or to the
municipal secretary, who shall issue and sign the medical certification of the
Certificate of Death for burial, cremation, and registration purposes.

       When the death is subjected to a medico-legal examination, or the cause of
death is under investigation by the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), or other
concerned government office and the body of the deceased is subjected to autopsy
or examination of the medico-legal officers, and no record of death has been
registered, the head of the NBI or other investigative agency or the duly authorized
representative shall cause the registration of such death through the City or
Municipal Health Officer of the city or municipality where the death occurred. The




                                          14
medico-legal officer shall accomplish and sign the medical certification portion of
the certificate of death, subject to the review of the City or Municipal Health Officer.

       SEC. 18. Delayed Registration of Death. – A deceased person's facts of death that
have not been within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of death shall be
allowed delayed registration before the Civil Registrar's Office at the place of death.
In any case, out-of-town registration of death may be allowed.

       SEC. 19. Registration Of the Application for Marriage License. – An application
for a marriage license shall be filed by the contracting parties before the Civil
Registrar's Office of the city or municipality where one of the parties resides.

      SEC. 20. Registration of Marriage. – All marriages shall be registered by the
solemnizing officer before the Civil Registrar's Office of the place where they were
solemnized.

          a.​ In case of marriages solemnized while in transit:

                i.​ En route to, or within the Philippines, the marriage shall be
              registered at the place of residence of either party; or

                ii.​ Leaving the Philippines, the marriage shall be registered at the
              consulate of the Philippines in the place of destination of the parties, or
              in the absence thereof, to the nearest Philippine consulate from the
              place of destination.

         b. In cases of registration of traditional marriages and dissolution of
       marriages among and between IPs.

       The report of marriage among and between IPs solemnized in accordance
with customary laws, rites, traditions, and by the person duly authorized to perform
the ritual or ceremony and by the parties to the marriage shall be conclusive upon
the LCR or PSA without need for further proof. For this purpose, the NCIP shall
submit to the PSA a list of IPs by each indigenous community to solemnize marriage
according to its customary laws or practices. The PSA shall issue a Certificate of
Registration of Authority to Solemnize Marriage (CRASM) to such IPs for free.

        In the case of indigenous communities whose traditional marriage ceremony
does not require the presence of a solemnizing officer, as determined by the NCIP,
the report by the contracting parties shall be conclusive upon the LCR/PSA without
the need for further proof except personal confirmation, orally or in writing, by any
of the parents or by a tribal leader or elder of the indigenous community to which
any of the spouses belong. For this purpose, the NCIP shall furnish the PSA and the
LCR with a list of indigenous communities falling under this paragraph; Provided,
That pending the submission of such list. The oral or written confirmation of the
tribal leader or elder shall be conclusive upon the LCR/PSA.

       The dissolution of marriage Of IPs following customary indigenous laws and
practices shall be directly recorded with the LCR/PSA without need of court
intervention; Provided, That such facts and information shall be confirmed orally or
in writing by a tribal leader or elder of the indigenous community to which any of
the spouses belongs.

       All marriages shall be registered within thirty (30) days from the date of the
marriage. Marriages registered after the stipulated period shall be considered late
registration and an administrative fee shall be imposed on the solemnizing officer, in
no shall double or multiple registration of marriages be except for cases provided by
law.

       SEC. 21. Multiple Registration. – In cases of multiple registrations of birth,
marriage or death, the first duly registered document shall prevail, unless the Court
orders otherwise. For multiple marriages by and between the same parties, the first
marriage shall prevail, and not the first registered marriage of such parties

       SEC. 22. Registration Of Solemnizing Officers. – All solemnizing officers shall be
registered with the Office of the CRG, except those who are not to be so registered as
provided for by law or existing rules. The Mayor, with the assistance of the Civil
Registrar's Office, shall determine documents pertaining to the existence of religious
sects.

       SEC. 23. Registration of Court Orders. – In case of court issuance or order
concerning the status of a person, the Clerk of Court shall advise the successful
petitioner to register such document before the Civil Registrar's Office which has
jurisdiction on the issuing court.

       In cases of legal separation, absolute nullity of void marriages and annulment
of voidable marriages, the prevailing party shall cause the registration of the decree
in the Civil Registrar's Office where the marriage was registered, the Civil Registrar's
Office where the family court is situated, and in the PSA. Such party shall report to
the Court compliance with this requirement within thirty (30) days from receipt of
the copy of the decree.

       In case a person other than the petitioner shall apply to register a court order
before the Civil Registrar's Office, the LCR has the responsibility to verify the
veracity of the court issuance or order, otherwise, registration thereof shall be
refused.

       The Civil Register Office where the event was registered shall forward a true
copy of the order to the Civil Registrar's Office where the vital event affected was
originally registered. The Civil Registrar's Office where the Vital event was
originally registered shall make proper annotations in the document and in the
applicable registry book. The Civil Registrar's Office which has jurisdiction over the
original registry shall send a certified true copy of the annotated document and the
registered court order to the CRG.

      Each corrected document shall be reported to the Office of the CRG during
the usual reporting month. All court orders shall be registered within thirty (30)
days after its finality, otherwise it shall be considered as delayed registration. All
court orders shall be affected through annotations in the civil registry document
except in an adoption case when the child shall be entitled to the issuance of an
amended COLB.

        SEC. 24. Registration of a Child born Abroad to at least one Filipino. – The parents
shall cause the registration of the birth of their child to the Philippine Consulate
which in tum shall issue a Report of Birth within thirty (30) calendar days from date
of birth of a Filipino child declaring the following when available:

           1.​ Full name of the child;

           2.​ Sex;

           3.​ ﻿Date of birth;

           4.​ ﻿Place of birth or where found;

           5.​ ﻿Name of mother;

           6.​ ﻿Citizenship of the mother;

           7.​ ﻿Name of father;

           8.​ ﻿Citizenship of the father;

           9.​ ﻿Date and place of marriage of parents; and

          10.​﻿Such other data as may be required in the implementing rules and
       regulations of this Act.

       SEC. 25. Registration of Persons without Known Parents. – All without known
parents, guardians or relatives, or a person committed in an orphanage or charitable
institution with unknown facts of birth and parentage, shall be reported by the
finder to the Barangay Chairperson or any member of the Barangay Council for the
Protection of Children (BCPC) of the place where the person was found, or to the
Philippine National Police Women and Children's Protection Desk (PNP-WCPD),
whichever is nearer or convenient to the finder, stating the facts and circumstances
surrounding the finding of when the report is duly noted.

       The finder shall bring the child to the nearest DSWD office in the region, for
proper screening, assessment. and evaluation by an authorized social worker within
fifteen (15) calendar days from the date of finding or commitment of the child, the
finder shall execute a sworn statement before the Barangay Captain stating the facts
and circumstances surrounding the finding of the person.

        In case of foundlings found in Philippine Consulates, embassies and
territories, the relevant Philippine Consulate shall be governed by RA No. 11767,
otherwise known as the “Foundling Recognition and Protection Act”, the finder or

from the date of entrustment to the institution.

      Any report made after the thirty (30) day shall be considered late registration,

        Should the foundling become adopted, the Local Civil Registrar shall stamp

likewise be stamped “cancelled” with the annotation of the issuance of a new birth

Local Civil shall record, register, and issue a new certificate of birth which shall not
bear any notation that it is a new or amended issue, as stated in Sec. 12 of RA No.
11222, otherwise known as the “Simulated Birth Rectification Act”.

       SEC. 26. Registration of Adoption. — The decree of adoption shall be in the civil
register within thirty (30) days after the date of issue of the final judgment of the
court. After registration of the decree of adoption, the Civil Registrar's Office where
the birth of the adopted was registered shall immediately issue an amended COLB.

       Such COLB of an adopted child shall not have any mark or annotation or any
indication of the facts of adoption. It shall have the same entries as those appearing
in the original records of birth, except for the following items of information:

          a) Name of the adopted. – The first name of the adopted child shall be the
       same as in the original COLB unless the order of adoption carried an order
       changing it to another name. The middle name of the adopted child shall be
       the same as the middle name of the adopter, except when the husband and
       the wife jointly adopted the child, in which case, the middle name of the
       adopted shall be the surname of the adopting mother.

          The surname of the adopted child shall be that of the adopter, If the
       husband and wife jointly adopt the child, the adopted child shall use the
       surname of the adopting father.

           b) Names of parents. – The names of the natural parents of the adopted
       child shall be substituted by the names of the adopting parents, to be
       indicated in the appropriate spaces in the COLB as the or mother, as the case
       maybe; and

           c) Other information about the adopting parents such as citizenship and
       religion shall be indicated in the appropriate spaces of the amended
       certificate of live birth.

      The birth order of the adopted and other information about the adopted shall
follow the information given by the adopting parents, unless the court order states
otherwise. In cases where legally married husband and wife jointly adopt the child,
the date and place of marriage of the adopting parents shall be entered in the
appropriate spaces of the amended certificate of live birth of the adopted child.

        In all cases of adoption, no amendment or changes shall be affected in the
civil registry books where the original birth records were duly registered, except for
the marginal annotation by virtue of the adoption decree.

      In case the adopted child has no known parents, the basis for the issuance of
the amended COLB shall be the court order.

       SEC. 27. Registration Of Presumptive Death. – A judicial order or decree
declaring a person presumably dead for purposes of remarriage shall be registered
in the office of the local civil registrar of the place where the marriage was
solemnized. It shall be annotated and recorded. The registration shall be annotated
in the marriage register as well as in the marriage certificate.

       SEC. 28. Registration of Foreign Court Orders Decided Abroad. – All foreign court
orders involving status of persons residing in the Philippines shall be registered
before the Civil Registrar's Office where the affected document is registered, These
foreign court orders, to be legal and binding in Philippine jurisdiction, must first be
recognized by the appropriate embassy of such country here in the Philippines or
Regional Trial Court which has the lawful jurisdiction over such case.

      SEC. 29. Registration Of Legal Instruments. – As a general rule, all legal
instruments Shall be registered in the Civil registry of the place where they were
executed except for the following:

         a.​ Affidavit of Reappearance. – where the parties to the subsequent
       marriage are residing;

          b.​ Marriage Settlement. – where the marriage is recorded; or

         c.​ Admission of Paternity, Acknowledgment, Legitimation, Voluntary
       Emancipation of Minor, Artificial Insemination. – where the birth of a child
       was recorded.

       SEC. 30. Registration of Affidavit of Reappearance. – A sworn statement of the
facts and circumstances of reappearance of the absentee spouse shall be in the Civil
Registry Office where marriage of the present spouse was recorded, at the instance
of any interested person, with due notice to the spouses of the subsequent marriage

and without prejudice to the fact of reappearance being judicially determined in
cases where such a fact is disputed.

       SEC. 31. Registration of Affidavit of Acknowledgment or Affidavit of Admission of
Paternity. – It shall be the duty of parents or parents who executed the affidavit of
acknowledgment to send the original copy to the Civil Registrar's Office where the
entries on the birth of the child were recorded for registration in the Register of
Legal Instruments and for proper annotation in the Register of Births.

       SEC. 32. Registration Of Repatriation. – The instrument of repatriation and the
oath of allegiance to the Constitution and the Government of the Philippines. Shall
be filed in the Civil Registrar's Office of the place where the instrument was
executed; Provided, That if the Philippine citizenship is reacquired by naturalization,
the order of the court or any mandated (omnibus) authority granting citizenship
shall be recorded in the Register of Court Order.

       SEC. 33. Registration of Muslim Filipinos, Indigenous Cultural Communities
(ICCS) or Indigenous Peoples (IPs), and Children in Need of Protection (CNSP). – Muslim
Filipinos, ICCs or IPS, and CNSP, by reason of their religious, and indigenous
peculiarities, and vulnerabilities, shall be registered as follows:

          a.​ ﻿Muslim. – Civil registration of Muslim shall be governed by PD No.
       1083, Order No. 157, s. 1994, otherwise known as the “Civil Registration System
       for Muslim Filipinos”, and its implementing rules and regulations.

         b.​ Indigenous Peoples. – Civil registration of indigenous cultural
       communities or indigenous shall be governed by RA No. 8371, otherwise
       known as the “Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997”, its implementing rules
       and regulations.

         c.​ Children in Need of Special Protection. – Civil registration of CNSP
       shall be by the provisions of this Act, special laws, and implementing rules
       and regulations, in coordination with the Department of Welfare and
       Development, and other concerned agencies.

       SEC. 34. Civil Registry Documents as Public Documents. – The registry books
making up the civil register, the office files in hard copies and its corresponding
digital files of the Civil Registrar's Offices, certification or certified copies from these
files and all other documents relating thereto shall considered public and shall be
prima facie evidence of the truth of the facts therein contained. These public
documents, except those documents which are to be kept strictly confidential as
provided for under this Act, and other applicable laws shall be to the public during
office hours and shall be kept in a secured place which shall be furnished to the civil
registrar at the expenses of the funds of the Civil Registrar's Office, Philippine
Consulate Office, or the Sharia court concerned.




                                             20
       The civil registrar shall not, under any circumstances, permit any document
entrusted to his/her care to be removed from his/her office, except upon lawful
order of the court, in which case the proper receipt shall be secured.

       SEC. 35. Digital or Electronic Reporting of Births, Deaths, and Marriages. – There
shall be a compulsory reporting of all occurrences of births, deaths, and marriages
from all Barangays in the Philippines. As Barangay Registration Agents, all
Barangay Secretaries, or their representatives, are hereby mandated to report these
events using appropriate and secure electronic devices and systems to the LCR/PSA
and other government agencies mandated by law to monitor the status of
registration of vital civil registry events and shall assist the subsequent registration
thereof. Continuing skills training relevant to digital or electronic reporting of births,
deaths, and marriages of Barangay Registration Agents shall be provided by the
PSA, I-GU, PACR, and other accredited government and private institutions and
organizations. This training shall equip Barangay Registration Agents with the
knowledge and skills to properly implement digital/electronic civil registration.

       SEC. 36. Development of National Civil Registry Database Software. – The
National Government through the National Computer Center, in close coordination
with the PSA, the PACR, and the DILG shall develop official software responsive to
the National Civil Registry Database (NCRD) implementation. Participation and
assistance of international and local private organizations having parallel objectives
on the full development of digital civil registration may be sought in the
development of this software. Among other features, this software shall include the
highest degree of data security to ensure the integrity and confidentiality of its
database.

        SEC. 37. Digitization of all Civil Registry. – All registered documents in the
LCR/PSA shall be digitized. In addition to the usual reports and forwarding of the
printed hard copy files, digitized files shall also be submitted to the PSA as back up
files of the NCRD, for digital archiving purposes.

       SEC. 38. False Statements. – Any person who shall knowingly make false
statements in the furnished forms and present the same for entry in the Civil
Registers shall in accordance with law unless it was done for the best interest of the
child.

       SEC. 39. Digitalization of Linkages and Interconnection of all Participating
LCR/PSA. – All capable LCR/PSA shall be interconnected to facilitate retrieval,
online certifications, and for local government revenue generation using LGU-linked
NCRD digital files. Participating LCR/PSA are hereby to issue certifications from
NCRD for, and on behalf of other LCR/PSA subject to pertinent laws, rules or
regulations on the confidentiality of birth records.

      SEC. 40. Penal Provisions. – Persons in authority who knowingly fail to
perform their duties in relation to this Act shall be held liable in accordance to law.

       Any solemnizing officer without being authorized by the Civil Registrar or
who, upon solemnizing marriage, refuses to exhibit his authority to solemnize
marriage when called upon to do so by the parties or parents, grandparents,
guardians, or persons having charge of the parties; or any bishop or officer, priest,
minister, or imam of any church, religion or sect, the regulations and practices
whereof require bans or publication previous to the solemnization of marriage that
is subsequently declared illegal. or any solemnizing officer in violation of this Act,
shall punished with imprisonment of not than six (6) months but not to three (3)

       The attending physician, midwife, nurse, birth attendant, hospital
administrator in-charge to cause the registration of live births but knowingly fails or
withholds the delivery of copies of the certificate/s of live birth to the city or
municipal civil registrar for registration, or any person who caused the failure of
delivery, shall penalized by imprisonment of not more than six (6) months and a fine
of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00), or both, upon the discretion of
the Court.

      SEC. 41. Withholding of Documents due to Non-Payment of Hospital Fees. – In no
case shall failure to settle hospital bills, in full or in part, be a ground for
non-delivery or withholding of the certificate of live birth or certificate of death or
documents required in the issuance of the said certificate.

        SEC. 42. Use of Fees and Income Collected. – All fees that are remitted to the PSA
by the National Treasury, and all income that the PSA is allowed to retain under law
shall, any provision of law to the contrary notwithstanding, be used primarily for
operating expenses including capital outlay, upgrading of its current facilities,
acquisition of office and establishment of satellite offices in the provinces, and other
activities or services the PSA in the of its mandate needs.

       SEC. 43. Transitory Provisions. – The PSA shall carry out the reorganization of
the civil registrars in such a manner that personnel of such office absorbed by the
PSA shall continue to their duties and responsibilities in a holdover capacity so as
not to unduly delay the production of statistics from ongoing censuses, surveys, and
of administrative records.

       SEC. 44. Appropriations. – There shall be an initial appropriation of One
Hundred Fifty Million Pesos (Php 150,000,000.00) in the General Appropriations Act,
which will be in the PSA's budget for the initial development of software and the
digitization of the Civil Registrar's Office files.

       SEC. 45. Mandatory Review. – This Act shall undergo a mandatory review on
its provisions every five (5) years to make the law more responsive to the needs of
the times. The review shall be made by Congress, which shall conduct public
hearings and record relevant testimonies of functionaries in the civil registration
system, which shall be the basis of any amendment or of this Act.

        SEC. 46. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days

       SEC. 47. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act which shall
be held to be invalid or unconstitutional, other parts or provisions hereof which are
not affected thereby shall continue to be in force and effect.

       SEC. 48. Repealing Clause. – Act No. 3753, or the “Law on Registry of Civil
Status” is hereby repealed. Sec. 453, 454, and 479 of RA No. 7160, or the “Local
Government of 1991”, are also hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees, ordinances,
administrative order, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 49. Construction. – In the interpretation of this law, the best interest of the
child shall prevail.

       SEC. 50. Retroactivity. – This Act Shall have retroactive effect insofar as it does
not prejudice or impair vested or rights.

       SEC. 51. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national
circulation.
