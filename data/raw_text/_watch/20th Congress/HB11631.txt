                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The Philippines is geographically situated in the Pacific Ring of Fire and the
Pacific Typhoon Belt, making it one of the most vulnerable countries in the world
to natural disasters, particularly typhoons and torrential monsoon rains. An
average of twenty (20) tropical cyclones enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility
(PAR) annually, bringing massive rainfall that frequently results in devastating
floods.

While the national government, through the Department of Public Works and
Highways (DPWH) and the Metro Manila Development Authority (MMDA),
implements macro-level flood control projects, these efforts are often undermined
by inadequate, poorly maintained, or entirely absent drainage networks at the
local and barangay levels. Rapid urbanization, improper solid waste disposal,
informal settlements along waterways, and the effects of climate change have
further exacerbated urban and rural flooding.

Currently, flood management is highly fragmented. Local Government Units
(LGUs) often lack the technical expertise, dedicated personnel, and financial
resources to develop and maintain a scientifically sound local drainage master
plan.

This bill seeks to address this critical gap by institutionalizing a Comprehensive
Local Flood and Drainage Management System. It mandates the creation of a
Local Flood and Drainage Management Office (LFDMO) within every city and
municipality, tasked specifically with the formulation, implementation, and
maintenance of a 10-year Local Flood and Drainage Master Plan. Furthermore, it
establishes a funding mechanism and ensures strict coordination with the
DPWH, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) to ensure that local
drainage systems seamlessly connect to national flood control infrastructures.

By empowering LGUs and institutionalizing accountability in local drainage
management, we can mitigate the devastating impacts of flooding on lives,
properties, and the local economy.

     In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE LOCAL FLOOD AND
DRAINAGE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM, MANDATING THE FORMULATION OF
LOCAL FLOOD MASTER PLANS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND
                     FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Local Flood and
Drainage Management Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the right of
the people to a balanced and healthful ecology and to protect life and property
from the devastating effects of natural and man-made disasters. Towards this
end, the State shall ensure the establishment of a comprehensive, localized, and
sustainable flood and drainage management system in every city and
municipality to mitigate flooding, ensure public safety, and promote resilient
infrastructure in the face of climate change.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For the purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean: (a) LGU refers to Local Government Units, specifically cities and
municipalities. (b) Drainage System refers to the network of pipes, canals,
culverts, waterways, and pumping stations designed to collect and convey surface
runoff or groundwater away from streets, public spaces, and residential areas. (c)
Flood Control Infrastructure refers to dams, dikes, levees, retention basins, and
similar structures designed to regulate water levels and prevent inundation. (d)
Master Plan refers to the 10-year Local Flood and Drainage Master Plan mandated
under this Act.

SEC. 4. Creation of the Local Flood and Drainage Management Office (LFDMO). —
There is hereby created a Local Flood and Drainage Management Office (LFDMO)
in every city and municipality. For 1st to 3rd class cities and municipalities, the
LFDMO shall be a distinct department headed by a licensed Civil or Sanitary
Engineer. For 4th to 6th class municipalities, the LFDMO may be integrated as a
specialized division under the existing Local Engineering Office.

The LFDMO shall have the following duties and functions: (a) Conduct regular
topographic, hydrologic, and hydraulic assessments of the LGU’s territorial
jurisdiction; (b) Design, construct, and maintain the local drainage and flood
control network; (c) Ensure the regular clearing, desilting, and declogging of all
local drainage canals, esteros, and waterways; (d) Coordinate with the solid waste
management office to prevent garbage-induced clogging of waterways; (e) Issue
drainage clearances for all new public and private infrastructure and real estate
developments.

SEC. 5. Formulation of the Local Flood and Drainage Master Plan. — Within one
(1) year from the effectivity of this Act, every LFDMO shall formulate a
comprehensive 10-year Local Flood and Drainage Master Plan, which shall be
updated every three (3) years.

The Master Plan shall include, but not be limited to: (a) Detailed mapping of the
existing drainage network and natural waterways; (b) Identification of flood-prone
areas, hazard zones, and drainage bottlenecks; (c) Design specifications for
upgrading capacity to accommodate climate change-induced rainfall projections;
(d) Integration of nature-based solutions, such as permeable pavements, urban
wetlands, and rainwater harvesting facilities; (e) A multi-year investment and
implementation schedule.

SEC. 6. Inter-Agency Coordination and Alignment. — The Local Master Plan must
be submitted to and approved by the DPWH District Engineering Office and the
DENR to ensure that local drainage systems are seamlessly integrated with
national flood control projects and natural river systems. The DPWH shall provide
technical assistance and capacity-building programs to LFDMO personnel.

SEC. 7. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are hereby prohibited: (a) Dumping
of solid waste, toxic materials, or debris into drainage canals, esteros, and
waterways; (b) Construction of informal settlements, commercial structures, or
any permanent extension of private property over drainage canals and natural
waterways; (c) Paving over designated natural easements or obstructing the flow
of water without a clearance from the LFDMO; (d) Issuance of building permits
by the Local Building Official without the required drainage clearance from the
LFDMO.

SEC. 8. Penalties. — Any person or entity found violating Section 7 of this Act
shall be penalized with a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00)
but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), or imprisonment
of not less than six (6) months but not more than six (6) years, or both, at the
discretion of the court. If the offense is committed by a corporation, partnership,
or other juridical entity, the chief executive officer, president, or general manager
shall be held liable. Public officials who fail to enforce the provisions of this Act
shall be subject to administrative sanctions under existing civil service laws.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DPWH and
the DILG. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation at the national level shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

For the LGUs, the funding for the creation of the LFDMO, the formulation of the
Master Plan, and the implementation of local drainage projects shall be sourced
from their National Tax Allotment (NTA) and at least twenty percent (20%) of their
Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF).

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, DPWH, DENR, and the League of Cities and
Municipalities of the Philippines shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, issuances,
rules, and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
