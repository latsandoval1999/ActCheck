                            EXPLANATORY NOTE
      Section 5(5), Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution commands that the
State “shall assign the highest budgetary priority to education and ensure
that teaching will attract and retain its rightful share of the best available
talents through adequate remuneration.”1 Of all the professions in the public
service, teaching alone is singled out in the Constitution for a guarantee of
adequate remuneration. That guarantee remains unfulfilled.
      A Teacher I presently earns Thirty Thousand Twenty-Four Pesos (PhP
30,024.00) monthly,2 below the family living wage of Thirty-Six Thousand
Five Hundred Ten Pesos (PhP 36,510.00) estimated for a household of five.3
Some eight hundred three thousand two hundred seventy-two (803,272)
personnel occupy Teacher I to Teacher III positions. While officials in Salary
Grades 31 to 33 receive between Two Hundred Ninety-Three Thousand One
Hundred Ninety-One Pesos (PhP 293,191.00) and Four Hundred
Thirty-Eight Thousand Eight Hundred Forty-Four Pesos (PhP 438,844.00)
monthly, teachers were granted increases of only 4.95% to 5.6%. Many take
on part-time work or leave for posts abroad.
     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING A MINIMUM BASIC MONTHLY SALARY OF
      FIFTY THOUSAND PESOS (P50,000.00) FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL
          TEACHERS AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Tamang
Sahod para sa Guro Act.”
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to assign the highest budgetary priority to education and to ensure
that teaching attracts and retains its rightful share of the best available
talents through adequate remuneration.
       SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all persons occupying
teaching positions in public elementary and secondary schools, including
those performing supervisory or administrative functions therein, as
contemplated under Republic Act No. 4670, otherwise known as the “Magna
Carta for Public School Teachers,” but excluding those in the professorial
staff of state universities and colleges. As used herein, basic monthly salary
refers to the compensation corresponding to the salary grade and step of the
position, exclusive of allowances, bonuses, honoraria and other
emoluments.
       SEC. 4. Minimum Basic Monthly Salary. – No public school teacher
shall receive a basic monthly salary lower than Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP
50,000.00), or the rate corresponding to the salary grade and step of the
position under Republic Act No. 11466, otherwise known as the “Salary
Standardization Law of 2019,” as amended, whichever is higher.
       SEC. 5. Preservation of Salary Differentials. – The Department of
Budget and Management shall adjust the basic monthly salaries of public
school teachers occupying positions higher than the entry-level teaching
position so as to maintain the percentage differentials between successive
salary grades and steps existing immediately prior to the effectivity of this

Act: Provided, That in no case shall the basic monthly salary of a higher
position be equal to or lower than that of a position subordinate to it.​
​
​      SEC. 6. Implementation Schedule. – The difference between the
basic monthly salary received by a public school teacher immediately prior
to the effectivity of this Act and the salary to which the teacher becomes
entitled under Sections 4 and 5 hereof shall be granted in three (3) equal
annual tranches, the first to take effect on the first day of January of the
year following the effectivity of this Act, and the second and third on the first
day of January of each year thereafter.​
​
​     SEC. 7. Annual Adjustment. – Beginning the year following the third
tranche, the rates under Sections 4 and 5 shall be adjusted annually in
accordance with the annual change in the consumer price index published
by the Philippine Statistics Authority as certification of the Department of
Budget and Management.​
​
​      SEC. 8. Relation to the Salary Standardization Law. – The rates
prescribed herein shall be observed notwithstanding the salary schedule
under Republic Act No. 11466, as amended, and subsequent issuances
adjusting the compensation of civilian government personnel, in recognition
of the distinct constitutional mandate.​
​
​     SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Budget and
Management, in consultation with the Department of Education, the Civil
Service Commission and duly accredited organizations of public school
teachers, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to carry out
the provisions hereof.​
​
​    SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the Department of Education. ​
​
​      SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby
shall continue to be in full force and effect.​
​
​     SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.​
​
​     SEC. 13. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.​

      Approved.
