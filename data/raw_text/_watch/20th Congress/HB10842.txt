                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Microinsurance Mutual Benefit Associations, or Mi-MBAs, play a vital role in
providing affordable, accessible, and community-based risk protection to low-
income, vulnerable, underserved, and informal-sector Filipinos. They serve members
who are often excluded from, or unable to afford, conventional insurance products,
including microentrepreneurs, farmers, fisherfolk, workers, cooperative members,
microfinance clients, and families dependent on small livelihood activities.

        Unlike ordinary commercial insurance providers, Mi-MBAs are member-
based and non-profit in character. They operate on the principles of mutual aid,
pooled contributions, democratic participation, and community-based risk sharing.
Through small and affordable contributions, members are able to obtain basic
protection against death, sickness, accident, calamity, fire, flood, crop loss, livestock
loss, livelihood disruption, business interruption, and other risks that may severely
affect low-income households.

       Despite their importance, Mi-MBAs continue to face legal, tax, and
operational limitations that affect their ability to provide broader and more
responsive protection to their members. There is a need to clarify the tax treatment of
member contributions, premiums, dues, assessments, claims expense, benefit funds,
guaranty funds, reserves, member equity value, investment income, revenue-
generating activities, and related documents. There is likewise a need to rationalize
the tax burden on non-life microinsurance products so that transaction costs do not
unduly reduce the benefits intended for low-income members.

       The need for non-life microinsurance has become more urgent in light of the
recurring risks faced by low-income communities. Many Mi- MBA members do not
only need protection against death or life-related contingencies. They also need
     protection against re, typhoon, ood, earthquake, crop loss, livestock loss, damage
     to residential homes used for livelihood, loss of tools and equipment, business
     inventory loss, livelihood asset loss, and microenterprise interruption. These risks
     are often too small for ordinary insurance markets, but too devastating for poor
     households and microentrepreneurs to bear on their own.

            This bill therefore seeks to strengthen Mi-MBAs by allowing quali ed
     associations, subject to the approval and supervision of the Insurance Commission,
     to provide limited non-life microinsurance products to their members. The authority
     granted is not intended to convert Mi-MBAs into full commercial non-life insurance
     companies. Rather, it is limited to member-based, low-value, microinsurance-speci c
     products, subject to actuarial review, reserve requirements, separate accounting, risk
     management standards, reinsurance, catastrophe protection, and other safeguards
     prescribed by the Insurance Commission.

            The bill also recognizes that Mi-MBAs may not always be legally, technically,
     or nancially capable of developing or underwriting all products needed by their
     members. For this reason, it authorizes Mi-MBAs to facilitate access to Micro-
     Products and micro-risk protection products issued by duly licensed or regulated
     providers, such as commercial insurers, non-life insurers, pre-need companies,
     health maintenance organizations, takaful operators, and other authorized risk-
     protection providers. This authority is limited to facilitation, administration,
     servicing, collection, member education, and claims assistance, and does not
     authorize Mi-MBAs to act as underwriters or issuers of products beyond their
     license.

           This measure also supports the development of Micro-Products, including
     Microinsurance and Micro Takaful, as inclusive and affordable protection
     mechanisms for low-income and informal-sector members. These products are
     intended to expand access to basic protection while remaining subject to the rules
     and supervision of the Insurance Commission and other appropriate regulators.

             To ensure sustainability, the bill authorizes Mi-MBAs to engage in regulated
     investments and revenue-generating activities, provided that such activities are
     incidental, complementary, or supportive of their mutual bene t and microinsurance
     purposes. Any net income from these activities must be used exclusively for
     member-bene t purposes, including payment or enhancement of bene ts, reduction
     or stabilization of member contributions, strengthening of reserves and member
     equity value funds, improvement of claims processing, digitalization, nancial
     literacy, disaster preparedness, calamity assistance, educational scholarships, civil
     documentation assistance, and other member-support programs.

           The bill likewise promotes the digital transformation of Mi-MBAs. It
     encourages the adoption of secured, safe, accessible, and member-friendly digital

     certi cate issuance, claims ling and payment, member engagement, regulatory
     reporting, tracking of member equity value, and product monitoring. This will make
     Mi-MBA operations more ef cient, transparent, responsive, and accessible to
     members.

            At the same time, the bill strengthens member protection. It requires clear
     disclosure of bene ts, exclusions, fees, charges, claims requirements, and the identity

     timely claims processing, written explanation for denied claims, internal grievance
     mechanisms, annual or periodic reporting, internal audit, risk management, data
     privacy safeguards, and member education, consistent with the Bill of Rights of
     Policyholders and applicable issuances of the Insurance Commission.

            The bill does not remove or diminish the authority of the Insurance
     Commission. On the contrary, it reinforces the Commission’s supervisory, visitorial,
     regulatory, and enforcement powers over Mi-MBAs, their products, funds,
     investments, partnerships, facilitated products, revenue- generating activities,
     governance, and member-protection mechanisms. It also contains safeguards against
     unauthorized insurance activity, tax avoidance, private inurement, misuse of
     member funds, false representation, commingling of funds, and circumvention of
     applicable laws.

            In view of the foregoing, this bill seeks to establish a clearer, fairer, and more
     responsive legal framework for Mi-MBAs as non-stock, non-pro t, member-based
     institutions serving low-income and underserved communities. By rationalizing
     their tax treatment, expanding their authority to provide and facilitate micro-risk
     protection products, strengthening their sustainability, and ensuring strict regulatory
     supervision, this measure will help advance social protection, nancial inclusion,
     and community resilience.

           In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
     sought.

                           AN ACT
STRENGTHENING MICROINSURANCE MUTUAL BENEFIT ASSOCIATIONS
  AS INSTRUMENTS OF SOCIAL PROTECTION, FINANCIAL INCLUSION,
 AND COMMUNITY-BASED RISK PROTECTION, RATIONALIZING THEIR
    TAX TREATMENT, EXPANDING THEIR AUTHORITY TO PROVIDE
 MICROINSURANCE AND MICRO-RISK PROTECTION PRODUCTS, AND
                     FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Microinsurance Mutual
Benefit Association Strengthening Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote social justice, financial inclusion, community-based risk protection, and
affordable access to microinsurance, mutual benefit, and inclusive risk-protection
services, particularly for low-income, vulnerable, underserved, and informal-sector
members.

     Toward this end, the State shall recognize Microinsurance Mutual Benefit
Associations as partners in delivering affordable, member-based, and community-
based social protection. The State shall likewise promote the sustainability, good
governance, transparency, financial soundness, responsible growth, and digital
transformation of Microinsurance Mutual Benefit Associations, subject to the
supervision and regulation of the Insurance Commission.

SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act shall have the following objectives:

     SEC. 5. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply only to duly licensed and
     authorized Microinsurance Mutual Bene t Associations, including their approved
     microinsurance, Micro-Product, Micro Takaful, investment, revenue-generating,
     digitalization, partnership, and member-service activities.

            Regular Mutual Bene t Associations that are not licensed or authorized by the
     Insurance Commission as Microinsurance Mutual Bene t Associations shall not be
     entitled to the special authorities, tax treatment, incentives, privileges, or regulatory
     treatment granted under this Act, unless they subsequently qualify and are duly
     authorized as Microinsurance Mutual Bene t Associations under rules prescribed by
     the Insurance Commission.

     Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authorizing any person, entity, association,
     or organization to operate as a Mi-MBA, insurer, insurance provider, takaful
     operator, insurance intermediary, microinsurance provider, or Micro-Product
     provider without the required license, authority, accreditation, or approval from the
     Insurance Commission or other appropriate regulator.

     SEC. 6. Recognition of Mi-MBAs as Social Protection Partners. – Duly licensed Mi-
     MBAs are hereby recognized as community-based social protection institutions and
     partners of the State in advancing nancial inclusion, disaster resilience, livelihood
     protection, and affordable risk protection for their members.

            National government agencies, local government units, cooperatives,
     micro nance institutions, workers’ associations, farmers’ and sherfolk
     organizations, transport groups, homeowners’ associations, people’s organizations,
     rural banks, organized community groups, and other similar institutions may,
     subject to applicable laws and regulations, partner with duly licensed Mi-MBAs for
     the delivery, promotion, administration, or servicing of approved mutual bene t,
     microinsurance, Micro-Product, and member-protection programs.

     SEC. 7. Tax Treatment of Member Contributions, Premiums, Dues, and
     Assessments. – Member contributions, premiums, dues, assessments, and similar
     amounts collected by a duly licensed Mi-MBA for approved mutual bene t,
     microinsurance, Micro-Product, or member- protection products shall not be treated
     as ordinary taxable income of the Mi-MBA, provided that such amounts are held,
     reserved, invested, or applied exclusively for the payment of bene ts, claims, claims
     expense, reserves, member equity value, refunds, member services, reasonable and
     necessary operating expenses directly connected with the carrying out of its
     approved business, or other lawful purposes directly connected with the protection
     and welfare of members.

SEC. 8. Preferential Premium Tax on Non-Life Microinsurance. – Premiums,
contributions, dues, assessments, and similar payments collected by a duly licensed
Mi-MBA for approved non-life microinsurance products issued to its members shall
be subject to a preferential premium tax rate of two percent (2%) of gross premiums,
contributions, dues, or assessments collected.

       The same preferential premium tax rate may apply to approved non-life
microinsurance products facilitated by a duly licensed Mi-MBA for its members
through a duly licensed non-life insurance company or other authorized partner
risk-protection provider, subject to the rules and conditions to be jointly prescribed
by the Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and Insurance
Commission.

       The tax imposed under this section shall be in lieu of value-added tax,
percentage tax, and other national business taxes imposed on such premiums,
contributions, dues, or assessments.

       Service fees, administrative fees, commissions, or other facilitation income
received by a Mi-MBA in connection with facilitated micro-risk protection products
shall be separately accounted for and shall be subject to the tax treatment prescribed
by the Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and Insurance
Commission, taking into account the non-stock, non-pro t, and member-bene t
character of Mi-MBAs.

      The Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and Insurance
Commission shall issue the necessary rules to implement this section, including the
manner of payment, reporting, remittance, audit, veri cation, classi cation of
covered products, and safeguards against abuse.


SEC. 9. Local Tax Treatment. – Local government units may extend local tax, fee, or
charge incentives to duly licensed Mi-MBAs, consistent with the Local Government
Code and other applicable laws, in recognition of their role as community-based
social protection institutions.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as automatically exempting Mi-MBAs
from local taxes, fees, charges, permits, or regulatory requirements, unless expressly
provided by law or ordinance.

     SEC. 11. Tax Treatment of Claims Expense, Bene t Funds, Guaranty Funds,
     Reserves, Member Equity Value, and Investment Income. – Claims expense, bene t
     funds, guaranty funds, reserve funds, member equity value funds, and other funds
     required by law, regulation, actuarial determination, or membership certi cate to be
     maintained by a Mi-MBA shall not be treated as taxable income when such funds are
     held, maintained, accumulated, credited, or applied exclusively for the bene t of
     members or to comply with prudential, reserve, solvency, equity value, or member-
     protection requirements.

            Investment income derived from the lawful investment of such funds shall
     likewise be exempt from income tax, provided that the same is used exclusively for
     any of the following purposes:

             a) Payment of bene ts, claims, or claims expense;
             b) Strengthening of reserves guaranty funds, bene t funds, or member equity
                value funds;
             c) Crediting of interest or earnings to refundable member equity value, where
                required by law, regulation, actuarial determination, or rules of the
                Insurance Commission;
             d) Enhancement of member bene ts;
             e) Reduction or stabilization of member contributions;
             f) Improvement of claims processing, digital systems, and member services;
             g) Member education, nancial literacy, and disaster preparedness programs;
             h) Disaster-response, emergency assistance, or calamity-related member
                support;
             i) Research and development related to product innovation, market
                development, actuarial studies, nancial reporting standards, and member
                protection; or
             j) Such other member-bene t purposes as may be approved by the Insurance
                Commission.

non-life microinsurance products to its members.

      The non-life microinsurance products that may be approved under this Act
include, but are not limited to:

     a) Fire microinsurance;
     b) Calamity, typhoon, ood, earthquake, and disaster microinsurance;
     c) Crop and agricultural microinsurance;
     d) Livestock microinsurance;
     e) Residential house microinsurance, especially where the residence is also
        used for livelihood or microenterprise activities;
     f) Small property protection;
     g) Livelihood asset protection;
     h) Business inventory protection;
     i) Business equipment, tools, assets, and small machinery protection;
     j) Personal accident microinsurance;
     k) Equipment, tool, tricycle, motorcycle, banca, or small livelihood vehicle
        protection;
     l) Microenterprise interruption or livelihood disruption protection;
     m) Funeral, emergency, and disaster-related protection products, subject to
        applicable regulation; and
     n) Other non-life microinsurance products that may be approved by the
        Insurance Commission.

     The authority granted under this section shall be limited to approved
microinsurance products issued to members of the Mi-MBA and shall not be
construed as a general authority to engage in commercial non-life insurance

Insurance Commission and other concerned regulators, facilitate access by its
members to Micro-Products or micro-risk protection products issued by duly
licensed partner risk-protection providers.

Takaful, health protection, pre-need, personal accident, calamity, property,
livelihood, agricultural, funeral, emergency, or other member-protection products
that the Mi-MBA is not authorized or able to develop or issue on its own.
