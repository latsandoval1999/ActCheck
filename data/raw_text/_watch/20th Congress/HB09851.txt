                         AN ACT
     DEFINING THE CRIME OF SUBORNATION OF PERJURY,
    AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE ACT NO. 3815, OTHERWISE
     KNOWN AS “THE REVISED PENAL CODE,” AS AMENDED

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The administration of justice depends on the truthfulness of testimony and
evidence presented before courts, quasi-judicial bodies, and other tribunals.
When witnesses are induced, coerced, or persuaded to give false testimony or
execute false affidavits, the integrity of judicial proceedings is undermined,
innocent persons may be wrongfully prosecuted or convicted, and public
confidence in the legal system is eroded.

In Hondrade v. Office of the Ombudsman (Visayas),1 the Supreme Court
discussed the offense of subornation of perjury and defined it as the act of
knowingly and willfully procuring another person to swear falsely. The Court
explained that subornation of perjury is presently treated as perjury, with the


1 G.R. No. 226526, 7 December 2021.
offender punished as a principal by inducement under the provisions of the
Revised Penal Code. There is currently no distinct felony that specifically
punishes subornation of perjury under the Revised Penal Code.

This legislative gap fails to adequately recognize the unique and grave nature
of inducing another person to commit perjury. The individual who
orchestrates, procures, or pressures another to provide false testimony is the
principal architect of the deception and poses a direct threat to the
administration of justice. By expressly defining and penalizing subornation of
perjury as a separate crime, the State strengthens its ability to deter witness
manipulation, protect the integrity of judicial proceedings, and hold
accountable those who seek to corrupt the truth-finding process.

The need for stronger accountability is even more compelling when the
offender is a public officer. Public officials occupy positions of authority and
influence and are expected to uphold the law and safeguard the fair
administration of justice. When a public officer uses the powers or prestige of
public office to induce another to testify falsely, the resulting harm extends
beyond a particular case and undermines public trust in government
institutions. For this reason, the bill imposes a higher penalty when
subornation of perjury is committed by a public officer. This is consistent with
Article XI, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution, which declares that: "Public
office is a public trust. Public officers and employees must at all times be
accountable to the people, serve them with utmost responsibility, integrity,
loyalty, and efficiency, act with patriotism and justice, and lead modest lives."

By clearly defining and penalizing subornation of perjury, this bill seeks to
safeguard the integrity of judicial proceedings, promote truth and
accountability, and strengthen public confidence in the administration of
justice.

                              AN ACT
          DEFINING THE CRIME OF SUBORNATION OF PERJURY,
         AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE ACT NO. 3815, OTHERWISE
          KNOWN AS “THE REVISED PENAL CODE,” AS AMENDED


     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:

1    Section 1. A new article denominated as Article 184-A is hereby added to Act
2    No. 3815, otherwise known as the Revised Penal Code, as amended, to read as
3    follows:

4        “Art. 184-A. Subornation of Perjury. – Whoever procures, coerces, or
5        otherwise induces another to commit any perjury shall be punished
6        as guilty of false testimony, and shall suffer the respective penalties
7        provided in this Section, as if they themselves had given the false
8        testimony: Provided, that if the person responsible for the
9        commission of this felony is a public officer or employee, or if the
10       testimony results in the institution of a criminal action against
1        another, the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum period:
2        Provided, further, that the offender who is a public officer or employee
3        shall also suffer perpetual absolute disqualification from holding any
4        appointive or elective position in the government or in any agency,
5        entity, or instrumentality thereof.”

6    Section. 2 Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, regulations,
7    and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are
8    hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

9    Section. 3. Effectivity. – This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
10   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

11   Approved,
