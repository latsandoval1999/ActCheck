 AN ACT MANDATING COASTAL CITIES AND MUNICIPALITIES TO PROVIDE
 FREE EMERGENCY COMMUNICATION AND SAFETY GEAR TO MUNICIPAL
 FISHERFOLK, ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MARITIME SAFETY NETWORK,
              AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Municipal fisherfolk constitute a vital productive force in the national economy,
anchoring rural livelihoods and national food security while facing severe structural
vulnerabilities, maritime risks, and a persistent lack of basic safety infrastructure
within municipal waters. While the 1987 Constitution explicitly mandates the State to
protect marginal fishers and advance social justice, small-scale producers remain
exposed to sudden weather shifts, engine failures, and collision hazards without
direct access to emergency response networks monitored by maritime authorities.

        The “Fisherfolk Safety at Sea Act” translates this constitutional obligation into
a concrete operational framework by mandating coastal local government units to
equip registered municipal fisherfolk with free waterproof two-way emergency radios,
life vests, and emergency SOS distress beacons. Designed to respect the
operational independence and daily realities of small-scale fishing without imposing
restrictive bureaucracy, the bill mandates permanent equipment custody under
Property Acknowledgement Receipts, eliminates burdensome clearance fees,
guarantees automatic qualification through the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry, and
secures direct funding through local disaster risk reduction resources supported by
civil society collaboration.

       By strengthening local search-and-rescue capabilities and safeguarding the
working conditions of small producers at sea, this measure advances a practical,
rights-based policy that protects both the lives and the economic viability of our
coastal communities.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Fisherfolk Safety
at Sea Act."

       SECTION 2. Declaration Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to
promote social justice, safeguard human life at sea, enhance coastal disaster risk
reduction, and protect the welfare of small-scale municipal fisherfolk. To this end, the
State shall establish a comprehensive maritime safety framework that guarantees
the permanent provision of emergency communication gear, personal safety
apparatus, and disaster protection mechanisms to municipal fishers without imposing
burdensome bureaucratic procedures or administrative fees.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall be defined as follows:

          (a) Emergency Communication Gear (ECG). Refers to portable,
              waterproof, shock-resistant communication devices designed for
             maritime distress signaling, including but not limited to Very High
             Frequency (VHF) marine hand-held radios, emergency position-
             indicating radio beacons (EPIRBs), personal locator beacons (PLBs),
             or automated SOS distress transponders.

          (b) Municipal Fisherfolk. Refers to natural persons engaged directly or
              indirectly in municipal fishing and related activities as defined under
              Republic Act No. 8550, as amended by Republic Act No. 10654, who
              are registered in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry (FishR) of their
              respective Local Government Units (LGUs).

          (c) Municipal Waters. Includes not only streams, lakes, inland bodies of
              water and tidal waters within the municipality, but also marine waters
              included between two lines drawn perpendicular to the general
              coastline from any point where the boundary lines of the municipality
              touch the sea at low tide and a line parallel with the general coastline
              including offshore islands and fifteen (15) kilometers therefrom.

          (d) Personal Flotation Device (PFD). Refers to high-buoyancy, life-
              preserving vests or jackets rated for open-sea survival and compliant
              with standards set by the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) and the
              Department of Trade and Industry - Bureau of Philippine Standards
              (DTI-BPS).

          (e) Force Majeure Loss. Refers to the destruction, washing away, or loss
              of safety gear occurring as a direct result of maritime accidents,
              adverse weather conditions, typhoons, sea surge, vessel capsizing, or
              severe engine breakdown at sea, beyond the human control of the
              fisherfolk.

       SECTION 4. Free Safety and Emergency Communication Gear. - All
coastal cities and municipalities shall provide registered municipal fisherfolk with
basic safety gear free of charge, including:

          (a) Waterproof Two-Way Emergency Radios preset to emergency
              frequencies monitored by the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), Bureau of
              Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), and Local Disaster Risk
              Reduction and Management Office (LDRRMO);

          (b) Emergency SOS Distress Beacons or Location Signalers for instant
              emergency broadcast;

          (c) Standard Life Vests and Basic Maritime Safety Kits; and
          (d) Waterproof Emergency Manual containing basic sea survival rules and
              PCG hotline numbers.

      SECTION 5. Permanent Issuance and Operational Mechanics. –

          (a) Mode of Transfer and Custody. Safety equipment provided under this
              Act shall be granted directly to registered municipal fisherfolk under a
              Socialized Equipment Grant Agreement and Stewardship Receipt.
              Custody shall remain permanently with the beneficiary, subject to
              annual physical validation during vessel re-registration.

          (b) Prohibition of Daily Return Systems. Local Government Units (LGUs)
              shall not impose daily check-out or check-in requirements that disrupt
              the departure schedules or fishing operations of municipal fisherfolk.

          (c) Annual Inspection. LGUs, through their local agricultural or disaster
              offices, shall inspect the operational condition of assigned safety gear
              once a year during annual vessel registration renewals or routine
              dockside visits.

      SECTION 6. Distribution - To ensure immediate, hassle-free access and
prevent bureaucratic delays:

          (a) Automatic Qualification. All fisherfolk duly registered under the
              Municipal Fisherfolk Registry (FishR) shall automatically qualify for free
              safety equipment without submitting additional application forms.
          (b) Prohibition on Extra Clearances. No LGU or national agency shall
              require barangay clearances, police clearances, tax clearances, or any
              fees as a condition for receiving safety gear under this Act.

          (c) Dockside and On-Site Distribution. LGUs and BFAR shall conduct
              equipment distribution directly at coastal barangay halls, fish landing
              sites, and local ports to avoid disrupting daily fishing schedules.

        SECTION 7. No Penalty for Accidental Loss. – No municipal fisherfolk shall
be held civilly, criminally, or administratively liable, nor shall they be subjected to
fines, replacement penalties, or disqualification from public assistance programs for
the loss, damage, or destruction of issued safety gear if such loss occurred due to a
sea emergency, vessel capsizing, adverse weather, or force majeure.

      Upon submission of an Affidavit of Loss certified by the Barangay Chairperson
or Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Officer (LDRRMO), the
concerned LGU Property Officer shall be automatically granted relief from property
accountability in accordance with COA rules and regulations, and replacement
equipment shall be issued subject to fund availability.


       SECTION 8. Capacity Building and Maritime Safety Orientation. No safety
kit shall be issued without the beneficiary completing a basic Maritime Emergency
and Safety Orientation. The DA-BFAR, in joint partnership with the PCG and coastal
LGUs, shall conduct mandatory, practical, non-fee training modules covering:

          (a) Basic marine VHF radio operation and emergency MAYDAY distress
              protocols;

          (b) Proper wearing and maintenance of Personal Flotation Devices;

          (c) Basic sea survival, capsizing protocols, and localized search-and-
              rescue signaling; and

          (d) Interpretation of weather advisories issued by PAGASA and PCG sea
              travel advisories.

      SECTION 9. Emergency Communication and Coordination Network.

          (a) Systemic Protocol. All coastal LGUs, through their Local Disaster Risk
              Reduction and Management Offices (LDRRMOs), shall establish a
              direct, real-time communication link with the Philippine Coast Guard
              (PCG), the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), the
              Philippine Navy, and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
              Management                      Council                   (NDRRMC).

          (b) Rescue Standards. The network shall maintain 24/7 readiness for real-
              time weather alerts, distress signal monitoring, joint search-and-rescue
              protocols, and regular safety training for local fishers.

          (c) Frequency Allocation and Standard Specifications. The National
              Telecommunications Commission (NTC), in coordination with the PCG
              and DA-BFAR, shall allocate dedicated, non-licensed emergency radio
              frequencies for municipal fisherfolk and establish simplified type-
              approval specifications for low-cost, waterproof maritime emergency
              communication devices within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this
              Act.

       SECTION 10. Fisherfolk Safety Registry. Each coastal city and municipality
shall maintain an updated, confidential database of registered municipal fisherfolk,
their assigned vessels, and issued safety equipment. This database shall be fully
integrated into the existing Municipal Fisherfolk Registry (FishR) System.

       SECTION 11. Funding and National Financial Assistance. — The primary
cost for the procurement and maintenance of safety gear mandated under this Act
shall be charged against the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund
(LDRRMF) or the Twenty Percent (20%) Development Fund of the concerned
coastal city or municipality.

       Provided, That for Fourth (4th), Fifth (5th), and Sixth (6th) class coastal
municipalities, a National Fisherfolk Safety Subsidization Program is hereby
established, funded under the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) through the
budget of the Department of Agriculture - Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic
Resources (DA-BFAR) and the Local Government Support Fund (LGSF)
administered by the Department of the Budget and Management (DBM), to cover up
to seventy-five percent (75%) of the procurement cost of the equipment mandated
herein.

       Donations, grants, or equipment contributions made by private entities, non-
governmental organizations, or international donor agencies to DA-BFAR or coastal
LGUs for the specific purpose of this Act shall be fully deductible from the donor's
gross income pursuant to Section 34(H) of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended.

       SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), as the lead agency, in consultation with the Department of
Agriculture–Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DA-BFAR), the Philippine
Coast Guard (PCG), the League of Municipalities of the Philippines (LMP), and
national fisherfolk associations, shall promulgate the national Implementing Rules
and Regulations (IRR). Within sixty (60) days from the publication of the national IRR,
all covered coastal cities and municipalities shall issue the necessary local executive
orders, administrative guidelines, or municipal ordinances to operationalize the
provisions of this Act within their respective jurisdictions.

      SECTION 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

       SECTION 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
      Approved,




BRIEFER: Fisherfolk Safety at Sea Act

The proposed bill establishes a statutory framework that mandates coastal local
government units (LGUs) to furnish registered municipal fisherfolk with free,
standardized emergency communication equipment and survival gear.
Municipal fishers face high occupational mortality rates, exposure to sudden extreme
weather events, capsizing hazards, and mechanical breakdowns within municipal
waters. Unlike commercial fishing vessels regulated under maritime safety regimes,
small-scale municipal fishers lack access to real-time distress signaling systems
monitored by national maritime security and disaster response networks.

STATUTORY PROVISIONS ANGLE:
  1. Free Safety and Emergency Communication Gear: Small fishers often rely on
     personal cellphones that lose cellular coverage at sea or malfunction when
     wet. Requiring preset, non-licensed marine VHF frequencies ensures instant
     connectivity to coast guard units and local disaster offices without requiring
     individual radio licenses or recurring subscriptions. Multi-agency specification
     (DA-BFAR, PCG, DTI-BPS) prevents LGUs from procuring sub-standard,
     non-survivable gear.
  2. Permanent Issuance and Operational Mechanics: Fishing trips usually happen
     on nocturnal tides, irregular hours, and localized landing sites, daily retrieval
     systems might create major operational bottlenecks (if everyday ang abutan
      at pagsasauli ng mga equipment). Granting permanent personal custody
      balances property control with operational realities.
   3. Administrative Redtape: Fee collection, policy checks, tax clearances, serve
      as major barriers that exclude the poorest fishers. By linking eligibility
      exclusively to FishR, the bill eliminatesbureaucratic delays.
   4. Protection against penalties and COA relief mechanics: Under standard
      government property rules, lost government assets trigger stringent financial
      liability and administrative surcharges against property officers and custodians.
      Without an explicit statutory exemption for force majeure losses, LGU
      Property Officers would be reluctant to distribute gear, or would force fishers
      to pay for lost items after capsizing incidents. This section removes
      administrative liability while maintaining auditability.
      Instead, we shall grant full civil, criminal, and administrative immunity to
      fisherfolk for accidental gear loss due to sea emergencies, weather, or force
      majeure. The bill will also allow a streamlined COA relief mechanism: a
      simplified Affidavit of Loss certified by the Barangay Chairperson or LDRRMO
      will also automatically relieve the LGU Property Officer from property
      accountability under COA rules.
   5. Training before the hand out of the equipment is necessary for the
      beneficiaries to be conducted and in coordination with concerned agencies
      and organizations.
   6. Funding:
          a. Primary: LDRRMF or 20% ng Development fund of the LGU
          b. National Subsidy Program: GAA fund via DA-BFAR and the DBM
               administered LGSF to subsidize up to 75% of the procurement costs
               for 4th, 5th, and 6th class coastal municipalities.
          c. donations or grants from private entities or NGOs are declared 100%
               deductible from gross income
   7. DILG will be the lead agency so LGUs will immediately produce the necessary
      documents and memo for the implementation of the bill. (but in tight
      coordination with DA-BFAR, PCG, LMP, and fisherfolk organizations).
