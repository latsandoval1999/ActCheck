                           AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL PREVENTIVE HEALTH PROMOTION FUND,
     STRENGTHENING POPULATION-BASED PREVENTIVE HEALTH
             PROGRAMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes that the health of the Filipino people is a fundamental
right and a cornerstone of national development. While significant strides have
been made through the enactment of Republic Act No. 11223, the Philippine
health system continues to face persistent challenges arising from the growing
burden of preventable diseases and the insufficient prioritization of preventive
health interventions.

      Recent data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) underscore the
urgency of strengthening preventive health care. As of 2025, the leading causes
of death in the Philippines remain non-communicable diseases, with ischemic
heart diseases accounting for 19.7% of total deaths, followed by neoplasms at
11.4%, and cerebrovascular diseases at 9.8%. Diabetes mellitus likewise ranks
among the top causes of mortality. These conditions are largely preventable and
manageable through early detection, sustained health promotion, and timely
intervention.

      Complementing these findings, the Department of Health (DOH), through
its National Objectives for Health 2023–2028, highlights the continuing burden
of preventable health risks, including a premature mortality rate attributable to
non-communicable diseases at 4.6 per 1,000 population, as well as persistent
public health concerns such as child stunting at 27% and maternal mortality at
144 per 100,000 live births. These indicators demonstrate that despite existing
programs, significant gaps remain in prevention, early intervention, and health
promotion across the life course.

       The predominance of preventable diseases has resulted in increasing
health care costs, overburdened hospitals, and avoidable loss of life. The current
health financing structure continues to favor curative and hospital-based
services, while investments in population-based preventive programs remain
fragmented and insufficiently funded. Although preventive services are
recognized under the Universal Health Care framework, there is no dedicated
and sustained funding mechanism that ensures the systematic implementation
of large-scale health promotion and disease prevention programs nationwide.

      International and local evidence consistently demonstrates that investing
in preventive health care yields substantial social and economic benefits.
Prevention reduces the incidence and severity of disease, lowers long-term health
expenditures, improves productivity, and enhances overall quality of life.
Strengthening preventive health programs is therefore essential not only for
improving health outcomes but also for ensuring the sustainability of the
country’s health system.

       This measure seeks to address these gaps by establishing a National
Preventive Health Promotion Fund that will provide a stable and dedicated
source of financing for population-based preventive health programs. The
proposed Fund will support nationwide health promotion campaigns,
community-based screening and early detection initiatives, lifestyle modification
programs, and other evidence-based interventions aimed at reducing health
risks and preventing disease. It likewise strengthens the capacity of local
government units and community health systems to deliver preventive services
effectively and equitably.

      Importantly, this bill is designed to complement, and not duplicate, the
provisions of Republic Act No. 11223. While the Universal Health Care Act
provides the overarching framework for health system governance and service
delivery, this measure focuses specifically on ensuring that preventive health
programs are adequately financed, implemented, and sustained at the
population level.
       By institutionalizing this proposed measure, the “Preventive Measures in
Healthcare Act”, a dedicated preventive health fund, the State takes a decisive
step toward shifting the health system from a predominantly curative model to
one that prioritizes prevention and wellness. Such a shift is critical to reducing
the burden of disease, improving the health of all Filipinos, and securing a more
resilient and sustainable health care system for future generations.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL PREVENTIVE HEALTH PROMOTION FUND,
     STRENGTHENING POPULATION-BASED PREVENTIVE HEALTH
             PROGRAMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Preventive Measures
in Healthcare Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote and protect the right to health of the people by strengthening
population-based preventive health programs, reducing the incidence of
preventable diseases, and fostering a culture of health and wellness.

Consistent with Republic Act No. 11223, the State shall prioritize health
promotion, disease prevention, and early detection through adequately funded,
evidence-based, and community-driven interventions.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Preventive Health Programs refer to population-based interventions
      aimed at reducing health risks, preventing disease, and promoting healthy
      behaviors, including but not limited to health education, vaccination
      campaigns, screening initiatives, and community-based wellness
      programs;
      (b) Fund refers to the National Preventive Health Promotion Fund
      established under this Act;

      (c) Population-Based Services refer to interventions directed at entire
      populations or communities, as distinguished from individual-based
      clinical services under existing health laws.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Preventive Health Promotion
Fund. – There is hereby established a National Preventive Health Promotion
Fund, hereinafter referred to as the “Fund,” which shall be used exclusively to
finance population-based preventive health programs nationwide.

The Fund shall be separate and distinct from other health funds under existing
laws and shall be used solely for purposes authorized under this Act.

SECTION 5. Sources of Fund. The Fund shall be sourced from:

      (a) A designated percentage of revenues collected from excise taxes on
      tobacco, alcohol, and other products as may be provided by law;
      (b) Annual appropriations under the General Appropriations Act;
      (c) Grants, donations, and assistance from local and international
      partners; and
      (d) Other lawful sources as may be determined by the Department of
      Budget and Management (DBM).

SECTION 6. Authorized Uses of the Fund.– The Fund shall be utilized
exclusively for population-based preventive health programs, including:

      (a) Nationwide health promotion and behavior change campaigns;
      (b) Community-based screening and early detection initiatives for priority
      diseases;
      (c) Nutrition, physical activity, and lifestyle modification programs;
      (d) Preventive interventions targeting non-communicable and
      communicable diseases;
      (e) Capacity-building for local government units and community health
      workers; and
      (f) Monitoring, evaluation, and research on preventive health outcomes.

Provided, That the Fund shall not be used for curative, hospital-based, or
individual clinical services already covered under existing health financing
mechanisms.
All individuals shall be automatically enrolled in a preventive health care
schedule appropriate to their age, sex, and risk profile, to be determined by the
Department of Health (DOH). Primary care providers shall ensure that
empaneled individuals are regularly informed, scheduled, and reminded of
recommended preventive services, including periodic health risk assessments
and screenings.

SECTION 7. Implementing Agency and Governance – The Department of
Health (DOH) shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act.

A Preventive Health Promotion Council is hereby created, to be chaired by the
Secretary of Health, with representatives from PhilHealth, DBM, Department of
Education (DepEd), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
and other relevant agencies, to:

      (a) Set national priorities for preventive health programs;
      (b)    Approve    allocation    and      utilization   of     the    Fund;
      (c) Ensure alignment with national health strategies and existing laws; and
      (d) Monitor program implementation and outcomes.

SECTION 8. Preventive Health Care Participation and Incentive
Mechanisms. – The State shall promote universal participation in preventive
health services through a system of automatic enrollment, active engagement,
and the provision of appropriate incentives.

PhilHealth, in coordination with the DOH, shall develop and implement benefit
enhancement mechanisms to encourage compliance with recommended
preventive care schedules. Such mechanisms may include, but are not limited
to:

      (a) Expanded benefit packages and reduced co-payments for individuals
      who regularly avail of preventive services;
      (b) Priority access to select health services and programs;
      (c) Integration of preventive compliance indicators in benefit eligibility
      enhancements, provided that no individual shall be denied access to
      essential health services on the sole ground of non-compliance.

Local government units (LGUs) and primary care providers shall adopt
community-based strategies, including reminders, outreach programs, and
mobile health services, to improve participation in preventive health programs,
particularly among vulnerable and underserved populations.
SECTION 8. Role of Local Government Units. Local government units (LGUs)
shall implement preventive health programs funded under this Act in accordance
with national guidelines issued by the Department of Health (DOH).

The DOH shall provide technical assistance to LGUs and establish performance-
based incentive mechanisms to encourage the achievement of national
preventive health targets.

LGUs shall likewise establish and maintain a monitoring system for preventive
health services within their respective jurisdictions. Such systems shall track
population coverage and compliance with recommended health assessments and
screenings, which shall serve as bases for the grant of performance-based
incentives.

SECTION 9. Coordination with Existing Health Systems. All programs
funded under this Act shall be implemented in coordination with existing health
systems and mechanisms established under Republic Act No. 11223.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to modify, amend, or supersede provisions
relating to individual-based health services, health care provider networks, or
PhilHealth benefit packages, which shall continue to be governed by existing
laws.

SECTION 10. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Transparency. The DOH shall
establish a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system to assess the
effectiveness, efficiency, and equity of programs funded under this Act.

An annual report shall be submitted to Congress and made publicly accessible,
detailing fund utilization, program outcomes, and performance of implementing
units.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with
concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
implementation.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act shall
be held unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
