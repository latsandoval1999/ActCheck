Introduced by: Hon. Keith Micah “Atty. Mike” D.L. Tan

AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION OF LOCAL HOG RAISERS, AND ENSURING FARMGATE
PRICE TRANSPARENCY, FAIR TRADING PRACTICES, AND IMPORTATION SAFEGUARDS

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Agriculture is the backbone of the Philippine economy and the essential foundation of
national food security. The domestic swine industry, among the economy's vital subsectors,
sustains millions of rural livelihoods, ranging from small and backyard raisers to commercial
farm enterprises and allied agricultural businesses across the country. However, local hog
raisers currently face a severe economic crisis that threatens their survival and the long-term
stability of the national food supply. The Philippine livestock sector is severely impacted by
continuously depressed farmgate prices alongside escalating production costs. Reports! indicate
liveweight hog farmgate prices dropping to between PhP 120 and PhP 160 per kilogram in
several regions, significantly below average production costs, inflicting cumulative daily losses
on local raisers and forcing many small and backyard farmers to reduce production or exit the
industry altogether.

This bill seeks to establish transparent, market-driven mechanisms to safeguard local hog
raisers without authorizing price fixing. Furthermore, the bill standardizes trading operations
by mandating liveweight transactions using calibrated weighing instruments and mandatory
transaction receipts stating recorded liveweights, agreed prices per kilogram, and payment
terms. By promoting fair trading practices, curbing unfair trading practices, and enhancing
market transparency, this bill seeks to protect the rights and welfare of local hog raisers,
safeguards consumer welfare, and fosters a balanced, competitive, and self-reliant domestic
swine industry.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

ARTICLE |
GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Local Hog Industry Protection,
Farmgate Price Transparency, and Fair Trading Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — lt is the policy of the State to promote comprehensive
rural development, provide support to agriculture through adequate financial, production,
marketing, and other support services, promote the preferential use of locally produced goods,
prohibit combinations in restraint of trade and unfair competition, and protect the rights and
welfare of farmers, local hog raisers, and agricultural producers consistent with the Constitution.
t is also the policy of the State to ensure food security, foster a self-reliant and independent
national economy, and maintain a balanced policy environment that safeguards consumer
welfare without unduly compromising the competitiveness, recovery, and growth of the
domestic swine industry. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the fixing of the
purchase or selling price of any animal or animal product.

SEC. 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to farmgate transactions in swine, livestock
marketing chains, meat importation policies, and government food procurement programs
throughout the country.

ARTICLE II
LIVESTOCK TRANSACTION REPORTING AND FARMGATE PRICE TRANSPARENCY

SEC. 4. Livestock Transaction Reporting System. — The Bureau of Animal Industry (BAI)
shall establish and maintain a Livestock Transaction Reporting System. Every buyer covered by
the implementing rules shall report the date, location, volume, price, and terms of payment of
each farmgate transaction, without disclosing the identity of the selling raiser without the
raiser’s written consent. Reported information shall be published only in aggregate form and
shall not be used as the sole basis for any tax assessment or admitted in evidence against the
reporting party, except in a proceeding for violation of this Act.

===== [page 3] =====
SEC. 5. Reference Farmgate Price and Market Monitoring. — The Department of
Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), the Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), and the BAI, shall compute and publish weekly a reference farmgate
price for hogs, at the national and regional levels, derived from data collected under Section 4.

The reference price shall be published on official websites and disseminated to local
agriculture offices and accredited raisers’ associations, and shall be clearly identified as an
adininistrative indicator distinct from official statistical series.

SEC. 6. Standard Weighing and Receipts. — All farmgate transactions shall be on a
liveweight basis using weighing instruments calibrated and sealed in accordance with existing
law. Every buyer shall issue the selling raiser, upon delivery, a receipt stating the number of
heads, recorded liveweight, agreed price per kilogram, total consideration, and agreed date of
payment.

ARTICLE Il
PROHIBITED UNFAIR TRADING PRACTICES

SEC. 7. Prohibited Unfair Trading Practices. — It shall be unlawful for any buyer, in a
farmgate transaction with a small livestock raiser or accredited association, to:

(a) Pay later than fifteen (15) days from delivery, absent a prior written agreement
providing otherwise;

(b) Unilaterally deduct from the agreed price or recorded weight after delivery without a
documented and disclosed basis;

{c} Refuse to issue the receipt required under Section 6 or to execute a written supply
agreement upon the raiser’s request;

(dc) Refuse without justifiable cause, and on unreasonably short notice, to accept delivery
of animals already accepted for purchase;

(e) Retaliate against a raiser or association for a complaint filed or information given
under this Act; or

(f) Collude with other buyers to fix, depress, or allocate the purchase price of hogs, or to
allocate raisers or areas of purchase among themselves, without prejudice to the
application of the Philippine Competition Act.

ARTICLE IV
IMPORTATION SAFEGUARDS AND REGULATORY REVIEW

SEC. 8. Comprehensive Review and Impact Assessment of Importation Policies. —- Whenever
pork importation is utilized to address domestic supply shortages or stabilize market prices, the
Department of Agriculture, in consultation with the Tariff Commission, hog raisers’ associations,
and relevant stakeholders, shall first conduct a comprehensive impact assessment to ensure that
such importation does not depress local farmgate prices, reduce profitability, or undermine the
recovery and growth of the local swine industry.

===== [page 4] =====
SEC. 9. Quantitative Import Safeguards and Monitoring. — The Tariff Commission and the
Department of Agriculture shall institute quantitative triggers and monitoring mechanisms. If
the volume of imported pork threatens to cause or causes serious injury to local hog raisers,
appropriate safeguard duties or temporary volume restrictions shall be recommended and
enforced in accordance with international trade agreements and domestic laws.

ARTICLE V
INSTITUTIONAL SUPPORT AND LOCAL SOURCING PREFERENCE

SEC. 10. Local Sourcing Preference. — Meat and meat products procured for any
vovernment food distribution program shall be sourced from domestic producers, preferably
small livestock raisers and accredited associations, through negotiated procurement under
existing procurement law. Not less than fifty percent (50%) of the total volume procured for
such programs in any calendar year shail be of domestic origin, phased in at not less than thirty
percent (30%) in the first year and forty percent (40%) in the second year of effectivity.

Imported meat may be allocated to such a program only upon a written certification by
the Secretary of Agriculture, published and valid for not more than six (6} months, that domestic
supply is insufficient.

SEC. 11. Industry Recovery and Development Programs. — The Department of Agriculture,
along with the Bureau of Animal Industry and other relevant agencies, shall prioritize funding
and implementation for programs aimed at:

{a) Enhancing disease biosecurity, prevention, and control measures (particularly against
African Swine Fever) to safely rebuild the local swine population;

(b} Providing accessible credit facilities, production subsidies, and capacity-building
programs for small and medium local hog raisers; and

(c) Upgrading local slaughterhouses, cold chain logistics, and processing facilities to boost
the competitiveness of domestic pork.

ARTICLE VI
ADJUDICATION, PENALTIES, AND MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

SEC. 12. Adjudication. — A complaint for violation of Section 7 may be filed with the BAI
by the aggrieved raiser, an accredited association on the raiser's behalf, or the DA motu proprio.

The BAI shall resolve the complaint within sixty (60) days from filing of the answer, with
notice and opportunity to be heard, and its decision shall be appealable to the Secretary of
Agriculture and thereafter in accordance with the Rules of Court.

The BAI shall offer mediation at the provincial level, not exceeding thirty (30) days, before
adjudication.

SEC. 13. Penalties. — After notice and hearing, the BAI shall impose upon an erring buyer:

(a} A fine of Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP 10,000.00) to One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP
100,000.00) for failure to report, or for late reporting, under Section 4;

===== [page 5] =====
(b) A fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000.00) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP
500,000.00} for false reporting or violation of Section 6;

(c) A fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP 100,000.00) to One Million Pesos (PhP
1,000,000.00), plus restitution to the aggrieved raiser, for violation of Section 7; and

(d) A second violation shall double the fine; a third shall carry suspension of registration
for up to one (1) year; and a fourth shall carry revocation.

These sanctions are without prejudice to criminal liability under existing laws.

SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the Secretary of Agriculture, in consultation with the Department of Trade and
Industry, the Philippine Competition Commission, accredited associations, and consumer
groups, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations, including reporting
thresholds, forms, aggregation parameters, and the schedule of fines. Failure to promulgate the
rules within this period shall not suspend the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DA, the BAI, and other concerned
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shal] remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

modified accordingly.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
