                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE


Under Republic Act No. 9406, the Public Attorney’s Office (PAO) is mandated to independently
provide legal representation, assistance, and counseling to indigent persons in criminal, civil, labor,
administrative, and other quasi-judicial cases.


In the performance of this mandate, public attorneys are often required to handle cases involving
serious and violent offenses and to represent persons who may be associated with dangerous
criminal organizations, drug syndicates, or armed groups. They may likewise be assigned to remote,
isolated, or conflict-affected areas, where they are exposed to threats of violence, retaliation,
infectious diseases, and other substantial risks arising from the nature and circumstances of their
duties.


Republic Act No. 9406 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations provide for special allowances
funded from a Special Trust Fund consisting principally of success fees collected after successful
litigation. Those special allowances, however, are distinct from hazard pay because they are not
specifically tied to the actual performance of dangerous assignments or exposure to hazardous
conditions.


The proposed measure seeks to grant a monthly hazard pay of Five Thousand Pesos ((₱5,000.00)
to public attorneys who, by reason of the nature, location, or circumstances of their assignments,
are exposed to substantial risks to their safety, health, or security. The measure recognizes that the
hazards faced by public attorneys are not merely incidental to the practice of law but may arise

other high-risk cases.

                                                  AN ACT
        GRANTING HAZARD PAY TO PUBLIC ATTORNEYS EXPOSED TO
     HAZARDOUS SITUATIONS AND PERFORMING HAZARDOUS DUTIES
 RELATED TO THEIR OFFICIAL FUNCTIONS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                                               THEREFOR


     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:


        SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Public Attorneys Hazard
Pay Act.”


        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect the life,
security, health, and welfare of public attorneys who perform essential functions under hazardous
conditions. The State recognizes the indispensable role of public attorneys in providing free legal
services to indigent persons and ensuring access to justice. To ensure the continued delivery of
effective and independent legal assistance, the State shall provide qualified public attorneys with
hazard pay as they perform hazardous duties or are exposed to hazardous conditions in the course
of their official functions.


        SECTION 3. Coverage. - All duly appointed and incumbent PAO lawyers, as defined
under Republic Act No. 9406 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations, who are actually



                                                                                                              3
performing the hazardous duties or are exposed to the hazardous situations described in Section
4 of this Act shall be entitled to receive hazard pay.
        PAO officials who have direct supervision over PAO lawyers may likewise be entitled to
hazard pay when they are required, as part of their official functions, to directly supervise,
authorize, review, approve, coordinate or otherwise participate in hazardous duties or assignments
covered in this Act.


        The hazard pay shall be granted only upon certification by the Secretary of Justice or a duly
authorized representative that the covered PAO lawyer or official actually performed the
hazardous duty or was exposed to the hazardous situation during the applicable period.


        SECTION 4. Hazard Pay- All covered PAO lawyers and officials shall be entitled to a
monthly hazard pay of Five Thousand Pesos (₱5,000.00), in addition to their regular
compensation, while performing hazardous duties or while assigned or exposed to hazardous
conditions, including:
        (a) Handling or providing legal representation, assistance, or counseling in criminal or
other cases involving serious, violent, organized, or high-risk offenses defined and penalized under
Act No. 3815, otherwise known as “The Revised Penal Code”;
        (b) Representing or assisting clients who are charged with, accused of, or investigated for
offenses involving organized crime, dangerous drugs, terrorism, firearms, trafficking, money
laundering, corruption, or similar serious offenses;
        (c) Appearing in custodial investigations, inquests, hearings, or other proceedings
involving violent, armed, or high-risk accused, detainees, or convicted persons;
        (d) Performing official travel, field work, client interviews, detention-facility visits,
investigations, or court appearances in conflict-affected, strife-torn, isolated, distressed, or disaster
or emergency-affected areas;
        (e) Performing official duties in correctional, detention, custodial, police, military, or other
facilities where there is a substantial risk of violence, infectious disease, hostage-taking, riot, escape,
or similar danger; and
        (f) Assigned to other hazardous duties and circumstances analogous to the foregoing, as
determined by the Secretary of Justice.


        SECTION. 5. Tax Exemption and Non-Diminution of Benefits. - The hazard pay
 provided in this Act shall form part of the exclusions from taxable gross income in accordance

with Section 32 (b) (7) (e) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended: Provided,
That nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish, in any manner, any benefit granted to
duly appointed public attorneys under existing laws, rules and regulations, local ordinances, and
other issuances.


      SECTION. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Budget and Management and the Secretary of Justice shall
jointly promulgate rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.


      SECTION 7. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation of the Department of Justice.
Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      SECTION. 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in force and
effect.


      SECTION. 9. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
amended, repealed or modified accordingly.


      SECTION 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.


      Approved.
