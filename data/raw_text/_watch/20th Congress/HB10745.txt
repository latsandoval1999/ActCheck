14                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
15
16   The Philippines continues to face the challenge of ensuring an electricity
17   supply that is affordable, reliable, secure, and resilient. Advances in solar
18   photovoltaic generation and battery energy storage technologies now provide
19   households, businesses, health facilities, educational institutions,
20   government establishments, and other electricity end-users with greater
21   opportunities to generate and store electricity primarily for their own use.
22
23   Behind-the-meter (BTM) solar generation and battery energy storage systems
24   (BESS) can enable consumers to reduce their dependence on electricity
25   purchased from the distribution system, manage peak electricity
26   consumption, improve energy resilience, and make greater use of electricity
27   generated within their premises. Battery storage is particularly valuable
28   because it allows electricity generated or purchased during periods of lower
29   demand to be stored for use during periods of higher demand or interruptions
30   in grid supply.
31
32   However, the adoption of these technologies should not be hindered by
33   unnecessary or disproportionate regulatory requirements. At the same time,
34   simplification must be accompanied by appropriate safeguards to protect
35   consumers, electrical workers, distribution utilities, and the reliability and
36   safety of the electric power system.
37
38   The Sariling Kuryente Act therefore establishes a proportionate and risk-
 1   based framework for BTM solar generation and BESS. It provides simplified
 2   regulatory treatment for standardized, low-risk systems and streamlines
 3   procedures for qualified zero-export systems, while preserving necessary
 4   technical, electrical, structural, fire, environmental, and distribution-system
 5   safety requirements.
 6
 7   The measure also strengthens consumer protection by requiring covered solar
 8   and storage equipment to comply with applicable Philippine National
 9   Standards and conformity-assessment requirements. It requires the
10   installation and commissioning of covered systems to be undertaken by duly
11   accredited    installers and    establishes    appropriate   accountability
12   requirements, including mandatory liability insurance or other financial
13   security, to protect consumers from unsafe installations, defective
14   workmanship, and unqualified or unscrupulous service providers.
15
16   The bill further protects the ability of end-users to install BTM generation and
17   energy storage systems on property they own or exclusively control, while
18   allowing reasonable restrictions necessary for structural safety, public safety,
19   and the protection of common property.
20
21   The bill also prioritizes energy storage as a means of increasing self-
22   consumption, reducing peak demand, and supporting distribution-system
23   reliability and resilience. It likewise recognizes the need to account for the
24   adoption of BTM systems in electricity planning while preventing
25   unreasonable cost shifting to consumers who do not have such systems. It
26   also authorizes the development of appropriate financing programs,
27   incentives, and targeted financial assistance for qualified end-users adopting
28   BESS and integrated solar-plus-storage systems, with priority given to
29   measures that support energy storage, reduce or shift peak demand, improve
30   electricity reliability and resilience, and assist low-income or underserved
31   consumers, subject to the availability of funds and applicable laws.
32
33   By reducing unnecessary regulatory barriers while maintaining appropriate
34   safety, technical, environmental, and consumer-protection safeguards, the
35   Sariling Kuryente Act seeks to make consumer-owned solar generation and
36   energy storage simpler, faster, safer, and more accountable.
37
38   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

14                            AN ACT
15   PROMOTING BEHIND-THE-METER SOLAR ENERGY GENERATION AND
16   BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEMS FOR OWN-USE, ESTABLISHING
17   A   STREAMLINED  REGULATORY,     SAFETY,   AND  CONSUMER
18   PROTECTION FRAMEWORK, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC
19   ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "ELECTRIC POWER
20   INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001," AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
21
22   Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Republic
23   of the Philippines in Congress assembled:
24

25        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Sariling
26   Kuryente Act."

27         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
28   State to:

29      (a) Promote the adoption of behind-the-meter (BTM) solar energy
30          generation systems, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and
31          integrated solar-plus-storage systems for own-use by households and
32          other electricity end-users;
33      (b) Simplify, harmonize, and expedite the permitting, registration,
34          notification, inspection, commissioning, and operation of qualified BTM
35          systems while maintaining necessary safety and reliability
 1       requirements;
 2   (c) Promote battery energy storage to increase self-consumption, reduce
 3       and shift peak electricity demand, improve consumer resilience, and
 4       support the reliability, stability, and efficient operation of the electric
 5       power system;
 6   (d) Protect consumers and electrical workers from unsafe installations,
 7       hazardous back-feeding, substandard equipment, and unqualified or
 8       fraudulent suppliers and installers;
 9   (e) Protect the right of electricity end-users to install qualified BTM systems
10       on property they own or exclusively control, subject to reasonable
11       requirements for structural safety, public safety, and the protection of
12       common property; and
13   (f) Establish proportionate regulatory requirements for BTM systems
14       based on their characteristics and potential impact on the distribution
15       system.

16      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

17   (a) Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) refers to equipment or
18       technologies capable of storing electrical energy for subsequent use,
19       including batteries, battery management systems, inverters, and
20       associated control systems.
21   (b) Behind-the-Meter (BTM) System refers to an electricity generation
22       system, energy storage system, or integrated generation and storage
23       system located on the end-user's side of the applicable point of
24       connection with the distribution system.
25   (c) Micro-Solar System (MSS) refers to a standardized small-scale solar
26       generation system intended for own-use, consisting of:
27       (1) photovoltaic (PV) modules with a total direct current (DC) nameplate
28           capacity not exceeding one thousand watts (1,000 W) or one kilowatt
29           (1 kW);
30       (2) a factory-set, grid-tied micro-inverter with a maximum alternating
31           current (AC) output of eight hundred watts (800 W) or zero point
32           eight kilowatts (0.8 kW), equipped with an automatic anti-islanding
33           disconnection safety mechanism; and
34       (3) a standard connection designed for connection through a standard
35           household wall outlet or circuit in accordance with the Philippine
36           Electrical Code (PEC) and applicable safety standards.
37   (d) Own-Use refers to the primary utilization of the generated energy by the
38       owner, occupant, or user of the residential, commercial, or industrial
39       property where the facility is installed, intended solely for own-use
40       consumption without the export of electricity to the distribution system.
41   (e) Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export System (SGF ZESS) refers to
 1          solar PV installations of any capacity that are intended solely for own-
 2          use and are not for the export of electricity to the distribution system,
 3          and are equipped with appropriate control, protection, and isolation
 4          mechanisms designed and configured to prevent the export of electricity
 5          to the distribution system.
 6      (f) Accredited Supplier refers to any person or entity accredited under
 7          this Act that sells, supplies, imports, distributes, designs, installs,
 8          commissions, maintains, repairs, or provides related services for BTM
 9          solar energy generation systems, BESS, or associated equipment. An
10          Accredited Supplier shall maintain its own qualified team of installers
11          and shall be directly accountable for the entire supply, installation,
12          commissioning, warranty, and after-sales service of the BTM System.

13         SEC. 4. Scope. — This Act shall apply to behind-the-meter solar energy
14   generation systems, battery energy storage systems, and integrated solar-
15   plus-storage systems installed primarily for the own-use of electricity end-
16   users, subject to applicable laws, rules, regulations, and technical standards.

17   Systems capable of exporting electricity to the distribution system shall
18   remain subject to applicable laws and regulations governing interconnection
19   and authorized electricity export.

20        SEC. 5. Amendment to Republic Act No. 9136. — Section 6 of
21   Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the “Electric Power Industry
22   Reform Act of 2001,” is hereby amended by adding the following paragraphs:

23   “An electricity end-user that installs, owns, or operates a qualified
24   behind-the-meter electricity generation or energy storage system
25   primarily for own-use shall not, solely by reason thereof, be considered
26   a generation company or be subject to regulatory requirements
27   applicable exclusively to commercial generation or participation in the
28   electricity market, provided that such system complies with applicable
29   safety, technical, and other requirements under existing laws and
30   regulations.”

31         SEC. 6. Regulatory Framework. – The DOE, in coordination with the
32   ERC, DTI, DILG, and other concerned government agencies within their
33   respective mandates, shall establish a proportionate and risk-based
34   regulatory framework for BTM Systems. The DOE shall lead the accreditation
35   framework for Suppliers under this Act, in coordination with the DTI and
36   DILG.

37   The framework shall provide simplified procedures for standardized, low-risk
38   systems, including qualified micro-solar and zero-export systems, while
 1   providing appropriate technical requirements for systems whose
 2   characteristics or configuration may materially affect the safety, operation, or
 3   reliability of the distribution system.

 4   The specific technical standards, system classifications, thresholds,
 5   documentary requirements, accreditation requirements, and procedures shall
 6   be prescribed pursuant to this Act and applicable laws.

 7          SEC. 7. Simplified Treatment of Micro-Solar Systems. — Qualified
 8   Micro-Solar Systems (MSS) within the capacity limits prescribed in this Act
 9   shall be subject to simplified regulatory treatment, subject to applicable safety
10   and technical requirements.

11   For qualified MSS installed for own-use, the accreditation of the Supplier and
12   the required Notice of Installation shall, subject to applicable safety and
13   technical standards, be in lieu of additional local permits, clearances, or
14   approvals required solely for the installation of the system.

15          The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the applicable
16   notification, accreditation, inspection, registration, commissioning, and other
17   procedures for qualified MSS, including the requirements that may be
18   simplified or dispensed with for standardized, low-risk systems.

19          SEC. 8. Distribution Utility Requirements and Charges. – A
20   distribution utility shall not impose an interconnection, technical
21   assessment, meter replacement, study, fee, or other requirement solely by
22   reason of the installation of a qualified non-exporting BTM System, except
23   when such requirement is authorized by law or applicable ERC rules and is
24   reasonably necessary to address a documented and demonstrated safety,
25   technical, billing, or reliability concern.

26   Any authorized requirement or charge shall be reasonable, transparent,
27   proportionate, and uniformly and non-discriminatorily applied.

28         SEC. 9. Service Capacity. – The approved service capacity of an end-
29   user shall not be reduced solely because of the installation of a qualified BTM
30   System.

31   Any modification of service capacity, transformer allocation, or service
32   connection shall be based on a demonstrated technical or safety requirement
33   and shall comply with applicable ERC rules.

34          SEC. 10. Protection Against Unreasonable Restrictions. – No
35   homeowners' association, subdivision developer, property manager, or similar
36   entity shall unreasonably prohibit or effectively prevent the installation of a
 1   qualified BTM solar energy generation system, BESS, or integrated solar-plus-
 2   storage system on property owned or exclusively controlled by an end-user.

 3   Reasonable requirements or restrictions may be imposed only when necessary
 4   to address structural safety, electrical safety, fire safety, public safety, or the
 5   protection, integrity, or proper use of common property. Such requirements
 6   or restrictions shall not be applied in an unreasonable, arbitrary, or
 7   discriminatory manner so as to effectively prohibit the installation or
 8   operation of a qualified BTM System.

 9   The DOE, in coordination with the Department of Human Settlements and
10   Urban Development (DHSUD), Department of the Interior and Local
11   Government (DILG), Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), and other concerned
12   agencies within their respective mandates, shall develop appropriate
13   guidelines or model rules for the implementation of this Section.

14         SEC. 11. Safety and Technical Requirements. – All BTM Systems
15   shall comply with applicable electrical, structural, fire, occupational,
16   environmental, product-safety, and distribution-system safety requirements.

17   Every BTM System electrically connected to the distribution system shall
18   incorporate appropriate anti-islanding, export-control, isolation, or equivalent
19   protective mechanisms to prevent unintended energization or back-feeding of
20   the distribution system.

21   Where required by applicable technical standards, the BTM System shall be
22   equipped with an accessible and lockable manual disconnect, double-throw
23   switch, or equivalent means of physical isolation.

24   BTM Systems shall be appropriately labeled and identified to enable
25   distribution utility personnel, electrical workers, emergency responders, and
26   other authorized persons to safely identify and isolate the system.

27   The specific protection, equipment, testing, commissioning, maintenance,
28   labeling, and other technical requirements shall be prescribed pursuant to
29   applicable laws, rules, regulations, codes, and technical standards.

30         SEC. 12. Equipment Standards and Certification. – Solar
31   photovoltaic (PV) modules, inverters, batteries, BESS equipment, protection
32   devices, and other covered equipment shall comply with applicable Philippine
33   National Standards and mandatory product-safety and conformity-
34   assessment requirements.

35   The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), through the Bureau of Philippine
36   Standards (BPS), shall administer applicable product standards and
 1   conformity-assessment requirements for covered equipment, in coordination
 2   with the DOE and other concerned agencies within their respective mandates.

 3   No person shall knowingly sell, distribute, import, install, or represent as
 4   compliant covered equipment that does not comply with applicable mandatory
 5   standards.

 6         SEC. 13. Accreditation and Accountability of Suppliers. – No
 7   person or entity shall undertake the supply, design, installation,
 8   commissioning, maintenance, repair, or other regulated installation services
 9   for BTM Systems covered by this Act unless duly accredited as a Supplier
10   under this Act or otherwise authorized under applicable law.

11   No person or entity shall represent itself as an Accredited Supplier, use the
12   accreditation of another Supplier, or undertake installation through an
13   unauthorized or unregistered installation team.

14   The DOE, in coordination with the DTI, DILG, ERC, and other concerned
15   agencies within their respective mandates, shall establish and maintain an
16   accreditation system for Suppliers of BTM solar generation and BESS systems
17   and associated equipment.

18   Accreditation shall be granted only to Suppliers that demonstrate the required
19   business registration, technical competency, financial capacity, equipment
20   compliance, and other qualifications prescribed under this Act and its
21   implementing rules and regulations.

22   An Accredited Supplier shall maintain its own qualified team of installers and
23   shall be directly and fully accountable for the entire supply, installation,
24   testing, commissioning, warranty, maintenance, and after-sales service of the
25   BTM System.

26   The DOE, in coordination with the DTI and DILG, shall maintain a publicly
27   accessible registry of Accredited Suppliers and their authorized installation
28   teams.

29         SEC. 14. Prevention of Fly-by-Night Suppliers and Substandard
30   Installations. – No person or entity shall advertise, offer, represent, or
31   provide BTM System supply or installation services as an Accredited Supplier
32   unless duly accredited under this Act.

33   An Accredited Supplier shall ensure that all BTM Systems supplied, installed,
34   tested, or commissioned by it comply with applicable Philippine National
35   Standards, the Philippine Electrical Code, fire and safety requirements, and
36   other applicable technical standards.
 1   An Accredited Supplier shall be responsible for correcting, at its own expense,
 2   any defective or substandard installation attributable to its acts or omissions,
 3   without prejudice to applicable warranty, insurance, civil, or administrative
 4   remedies.

 5   The DOE, in coordination with the DTI, DILG, ERC, and other concerned
 6   agencies, shall establish measures to prevent fly-by-night suppliers,
 7   unauthorized operators, misrepresentation of accreditation, fraudulent or
 8   misleading representations, and substandard installations.

 9         The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe appropriate
10   requirements for supplier verification, installation records, warranties,
11   consumer complaints, corrective measures, and other mechanisms necessary
12   to protect consumers and ensure the quality and safety of BTM Systems.

13          SEC. 15. Continuity of Supplier Obligations. – An Accredited
14   Supplier that intends to cease, suspend, or discontinue its supply or
15   installation activities for BTM Systems shall notify the DOE and other
16   concerned government agencies within the period prescribed by the
17   implementing rules and regulations.

18   Such notification shall include measures for the orderly disposition of its
19   outstanding obligations, including warranties, maintenance, after-sales
20   service, and pending consumer complaints.

21   The cessation, suspension, or discontinuance of business shall not extinguish
22   the Supplier's obligations arising from BTM Systems previously supplied,
23   installed, or commissioned, including applicable warranty and consumer
24   protection obligations.

25   The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the notification
26   period, transition requirements, and procedures for the continued servicing
27   of systems previously supplied or installed by a Supplier that ceases
28   operations.

29          SEC. 16. Penalties and Administrative Sanctions. – Any Accredited
30   Supplier that violates this Act, its implementing rules and regulations,
31   applicable safety and technical standards, or the conditions of its
32   accreditation shall be subject to administrative penalties, without prejudice
33   to civil or criminal liability under existing laws.

34      (a) First Offense. – A fine in an amount to be prescribed under the
35          implementing rules and regulations and suspension of accreditation for
36          a period to be prescribed by the DOE;
37      (b) Second Offense. – A higher fine as may be prescribed under the
 1          implementing rules and regulations and suspension of accreditation for
 2          a longer period;
 3      (c) Third Offense. – Revocation of accreditation and disqualification from
 4          re-accreditation for a period to be prescribed by the DOE.

 5   The penalties shall be imposed only after notice and opportunity to be heard
 6   and in accordance with due process. The implementing rules and regulations
 7   shall establish the schedule of fines, periods of suspension, grounds for
 8   revocation, procedures for investigation and adjudication, and rules for
 9   determining whether violations are separate offenses or continuing violations.

10         SEC. 17. System Planning and Consumer Protection. – The DOE
11   and ERC shall, within their respective mandates, ensure that the adoption of
12   BTM generation and energy storage is appropriately considered in electricity
13   demand forecasting, distribution planning, and power supply planning,
14   consistent with existing contractual and regulatory obligations.

15   Nothing in this Act shall authorize the modification, impairment, or avoidance
16   of valid existing contracts or obligations. The appropriate government
17   agencies shall take into account the effects of BTM adoption on system costs
18   and shall ensure, consistent with applicable law, that the implementation of
19   this Act does not result in unreasonable or unjustified cost shifting to
20   electricity consumers who do not have BTM systems.

21          SEC. 18. Environmental Compliance and End-of-Life Management.
22   – The installation, operation, maintenance, decommissioning, removal,
23   collection, transport, recycling, treatment, and disposal of solar photovoltaic
24   modules, batteries, BESS, and related equipment shall be undertaken in
25   compliance with applicable environmental laws, rules, regulations, and
26   standards.

27   The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), in
28   coordination with the DOE and other concerned agencies within their
29   respective mandates, shall prescribe appropriate environmental and end-of-
30   life management requirements for covered equipment, consistent with
31   existing law.

32         SEC. 19. Financing and Government Support. — The DOE, in
33   coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
34   Department of Finance (DOF), and other concerned agencies within their
35   respective mandates, shall establish financing programs, targeted financial
36   assistance, incentives, or other forms of support for qualified end-users
37   adopting battery energy storage systems or integrated solar-plus-storage
38   systems for own-use.
 1   Priority shall be given to measures that promote energy storage, reduce or
 2   shift peak electricity demand, improve electricity reliability and resilience, and
 3   support low-income or underserved consumers, subject to the availability of
 4   funds and applicable laws.

 5   No subsidy or financial assistance under this section shall be charged to
 6   electricity consumers through distribution utility rates or charges unless
 7   otherwise authorized by law.

 8         SEC. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
 9   days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOE, in coordination with the ERC,
10   DTI, DENR, DILG, DPWH, DHSUD, BFP, and other concerned agencies within
11   their respective mandates, shall promulgate the rules necessary for the
12   implementation of this Act.

13          SEC. 21. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is
14   declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby
15   shall remain in full force and effect.

16         SEC. 22. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders,
17   rules, regulations, circulars, orders, and other issuances, or parts thereof,
18   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
19   modified accordingly.

20         SEC. 23. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
21   its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

22   Approved.
