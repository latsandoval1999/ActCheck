                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 7610, Republic Act No. 10175, and Republic Act No.
11930 penalize the abuse and exploitation of children online after it has
occurred. No existing Philippine statute regulates the conditions that produce
the encounter. A platform is under no legal obligation to determine whether its
user is a child, to apply protective default settings to that child's account, or to
withhold engagement-maximizing recommendation systems from a minor. This
bill addresses that specific gap and does not extend beyond it.
       This bill imposes safety-by-design obligations on every account a
platform knows or ought reasonably to know belongs to a minor, independently
of the minimum age requirement. The obligations therefore apply to children
who evade the age requirement, and to children who migrate to smaller
services with weaker moderation and no Philippine presence. It places the
obligation and the penalty on the covered platform. No penalty attaches to a
child or to a parent, and parental consent cannot be used to waive the
minimum age. This conforms the bill to Republic Act No. 9344, which prohibits
the imposition of penalties on minors for status offences, and follows the
Australian scheme in force since 10 December 2025.
       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                      AN ACT
     IMPOSING SAFETY-BY-DESIGN AND AGE ASSURANCE DUTIES ON
     SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORM PROVIDERS WITH RESPECT TO CHILD
 USERS, ESTABLISHING A MINIMUM AGE FOR SOCIAL MEDIA ACCOUNTS,
     PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Social Media
Safety for Children Act”.
       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote
and protect the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual and social well-being of
the youth, and to defend the right of children to special protection from all
forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation and other conditions prejudicial to
their development.
       SEC. 3. Definitions. — For purposes of this Act:
  (a) “Child” or “minor” refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age.

  (b) “Covered social media platform” refers to an online service, whether or
       not established in the Philippines, that satisfies all of the following:
      (i) a sole or significant purpose of the service is to enable online social
      interaction among two (2) or more end-users;
      (ii) the service allows end-users to link to, follow, or otherwise interact
      with some or all other end-users; and
       (iii) the service allows end-users to post, upload or share user-generated
       content. The term does not include an excluded service under Section 8.
  (c)​ “Platform provider” refers to the natural or juridical person that owns,
       operates or controls a covered social media platform and determines the
       purposes and means of processing user data for it.
  (d)​ “Age assurance” refers to any technical or procedural means of
       establishing, with reasonable confidence, whether a user is above or
       below a specified age, including age estimation, age inference,
       account-based signals, parental attestation and other privacy-preserving
       methods that do not require the submission of an identity document.
  (e)​ “Verifiable parental consent” refers to consent given by a parent or, in
       the parent’s absence, by the guardian or the person exercising substitute
       parental authority under the Family Code, obtained through a means
       reasonably designed to confirm that the person giving consent is in fact
       such parent, guardian or person, and which may be withdrawn at any
       time.
  (f)​ “Recommender system” refers to a fully or partially automated system
       that determines, ranks or prioritizes the content presented to a user,
       including content the user has not sought or subscribed to.
  (g)​ “Reasonable steps” refers to objectively appropriate, effective and
       proportionate measures, assessed with regard to the nature and size of
       the platform, the risks it poses to children, the effectiveness and
       proportionality of available measures, their privacy impact, and the need
       to avoid unduly impairing lawful communication, education and
       participation.
       SEC. 4. Duty of Care; Safety-by-Design. — With respect to every account
that a platform provider knows, or ought reasonably to know, is held by a
minor, and irrespective of whether Section 5 is engaged, the provider shall:
  (a)​ apply the highest available privacy and safety settings by default,
       including limiting profile visibility and discoverability and restricting
       geolocation sharing;
  (b)​ refrain from behavioral or targeted advertising to the minor, and from the
       sale or disclosure of the minor’s personal data for advertising purposes;
  (c)​ refrain from deploying recommender systems optimized for engagement
       duration or session frequency, and disable autoplay, infinite scroll,
       streak mechanics and comparable retention features by default;
  (d)​ take reasonable steps to prevent the recommendation to the minor of
       content depicting or promoting suicide, self-harm, disordered eating,
       sexual exploitation or gratuitous violence;
  (e)​ suppress non-essential notifications between ten o’clock in the evening
       and six o’clock in the morning;
  (f)​ restrict unsolicited contact directed at the minor by adult accounts not
       connected to the minor, and restrict the discoverability of the minor’s
       account by such accounts;
  (g)​ refrain from collecting biometric or sensitive personal information from
       the minor except as permitted by Section 6 and only for the period there
       allowed;
  (h)​ provide the minor, and where applicable the parent or guardian, with
       accessible tools to review account activity, adjust privacy settings,
       manage contacts, and set time and usage limits, without requiring the
       disclosure of personal data beyond what is necessary; and
  (i)​ maintain the reporting and referral mechanisms required by Section 9.
No user shall be penalized, and no service shall be withheld, solely for declining
to enable an optional tool made available under paragraph (h).
       SEC. 5. Minimum Age; Parental Consent Pathway. — No platform
provider shall permit a person below sixteen (16) years of age to create, hold or
maintain an account on a covered social media platform, except as provided in
this Section.
       A person who is at least thirteen (13) but below sixteen (16) years of age
may create and hold such an account upon verifiable parental consent. The
platform provider shall:
       (a) re-confirm the consent at intervals of not more than twelve (12)
       months;
       (b) provide the consenting parent or guardian with the tools described in
       Section 4(h); and
       (c) upon withdrawal of consent, deactivate the account within
       seventy-two (72) hours, preserving the user’s content for a reasonable
       period to permit retrieval.
       Nothing in this Section shall be construed to prohibit the viewing of
publicly available content where no account registration or log-in is required,
nor to restrict access to educational, governmental, emergency, health or child
protection services.
       Verifiable parental consent shall not be a defence to a failure to comply
with Section 4.
       SEC. 6. Age Assurance and Data Minimization. — Platform providers
shall take reasonable steps, through age assurance applied at account creation
and periodically thereafter, to prevent persons below the applicable minimum
age from creating, holding or maintaining accounts. Age assurance shall
employ the least intrusive method reasonably capable of achieving the purpose.
A platform provider shall not:
  (a)​ retain any identity document, or any image or copy thereof, beyond the
       completion of verification;
  (b)​ use data collected for age assurance for any other purpose, including
       advertising, profiling, product research or the training of models; or
  (c)​ transfer such data outside the platform except to a third-party age
       assurance provider bound by equivalent obligations, or pursuant to a
       lawful order.
       A platform provider shall not require government-issued identification, or
any government or government-accredited digital identity system, as the sole
means of age assurance. Where such identification is offered as an option, at
least one reasonable alternative shall be made available. No person shall be
denied access to a covered social media platform solely for declining to submit
a government-issued identity document where an alternative method is
reasonably available.
       The National Privacy Commission shall exercise concurrent jurisdiction
over violations of this Section.
       SEC. 7. Designation of Covered Platforms. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, and at intervals of not more than twelve (12) months
thereafter, the Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT), in consultation with the National Telecommunications Commission
(NTC), the National Privacy Commission and the Council for the Welfare of
Children, shall publish a list of covered social media platforms and of excluded
services, together with the criteria applied and the reasons for each
designation.
       A platform provider shall assess its own coverage under Section 3(b) and
shall not be excused from compliance by reason only of non-inclusion in the
published list: Provided, That no penalty shall attach for any period prior to
publication or prior to actual notice of coverage.
       A platform provider may petition the DICT for a determination of
coverage, which shall be resolved within sixty (60) days, and may seek review
in accordance with the Rules of Court.
       SEC. 8. Excluded Services. — A service shall be excluded from Section 5
where its primary function is direct, limited-scope utility rather than
general-purpose public social networking, and where it does not primarily rely
on engagement-optimizing design that materially increases risk to children.
Excluded services include:
  (a)​ private interpersonal communications services, where communications
       are primarily one-to-one or within closed groups and are not publicly
       discoverable by default;
  (b)​ electronic mail;
  (c)​ enterprise and workplace collaboration services;
  (d)​ educational and learning management services, including
       school-authorized platforms;
  (e)​ health, mental health, counselling, crisis line and safety-support
       services, including child protection reporting channels;
  (f)​ government digital services and official citizen service portals;
  (g)​ interactive gaming services without general-purpose social networking
       features; and
  (h)​ such other services as the DICT may classify as excluded under Section
       7, upon a documented finding that the service is low-risk and necessary
       to a child’s access to essential communications, education, health, safety
       or public services.
       Exclusion from Section 5 shall not exempt a service from Section 4 with
respect to accounts held by minors.
       SEC. 9. Reporting, Referral and Crisis Pathways. — Every platform
provider shall maintain an accessible, child-comprehensible in-platform
mechanism by which a child, parent, guardian, teacher or third party may
report grooming, sexual exploitation, bullying, harassment, and content
depicting or promoting suicide or self-harm, and shall acknowledge and act
upon such reports within a reasonable period prescribed in the implementing
rules.
       Reports involving child sexual abuse or exploitation material shall be
transmitted in accordance with Republic Act No. 11930.
       Platform providers shall make available to child users, in plain language
and in Filipino and English, referral information for child protection and
mental health services, including the school-based services established under
Republic Act No. 12080 and the crisis lines maintained by the Department of
Health.
        SEC. 10. Non-Liability of Children, Parents and Guardians. — No
criminal, civil or administrative liability, and no penalty, fine or disability of
any kind, shall attach to a child, parent, guardian or person exercising
substitute parental authority for any act or omission arising from this Act.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the imposition of any
penalty upon a minor.
        SEC. 11. Transparency and Risk Assessment. — Every platform provider
shall, at intervals prescribed by the DICT and in a form that does not disclose
personal data:
   (a)​ publish a plain-language description of how content is selected, ranked
        and recommended to accounts held by minors, and of the default
        settings applied under Section 4;
   (b)​ conduct and submit a risk assessment identifying foreseeable risks to
        child users arising from the design and operation of the platform, and
        the measures adopted to mitigate them; and
        SEC. 12. Erroneous Age Classification. — Every platform provider shall
maintain an accessible mechanism by which an account holder, or a parent or
guardian, may seek the restoration of an account restricted or deactivated on
the ground of erroneous age classification, and shall resolve such a request
within ten (10) days.
        Denial shall be reviewable by the DICT, which shall resolve the matter
within thirty (30) days. Nothing in this Section shall require the submission of
a government-issued identity document.
        SEC. 13. Enforcement and Penalties. — The DICT shall enforce this Act.
        Upon determination of a violation after notice and hearing, the DICT
shall issue a corrective order specifying the required remedial action and a
reasonable period for compliance.
        A platform provider that fails to comply with a corrective order shall be
liable to an administrative fine of not less than One million pesos (PhP
1,000,000.00) nor more than Fifty million pesos (PhP 50,000,000.00) for each
violation, or two percent (2%) of its gross revenues derived from Philippine
users for the preceding taxable year, whichever is higher, and a daily fine of
One hundred thousand pesos (PhP 100,000.00) for each day of continuing
violation.
        In determining the fine, the DICT shall consider the nature, gravity,
duration and frequency of the violation, the number of affected child users, and
the steps taken by the provider to remedy it.
       For repeated or willful violations, and only after the remedies in this
Section have been exhausted, the DICT may recommend to the appropriate
authority the restriction or suspension of the platform’s operations in the
Philippines. Any measure directed to internet service providers shall be
targeted to the specific service or account concerned, shall be time-bound,
shall employ the least restrictive technical means, and shall minimize collateral
blocking of lawful content.
       Any order of the DICT under this Section shall be subject to review in
accordance with the Rules of Court.
       SEC. 14. Digital Citizenship and Media Literacy. — The Department of
Education, in coordination with the DICT and the Council for the Welfare of
Children, shall integrate age-appropriate instruction in digital citizenship and
media and information literacy into the basic education curriculum, covering
critical evaluation of online information, awareness of algorithmic influence,
recognition and reporting of grooming, exploitation and bullying, and healthy
patterns of use.
       The Commission on Higher Education and the Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority shall promote comparable programs within their
respective mandates.
       SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in consultation with the National
Privacy Commission, the NTC, the Council for the Welfare of Children, the
Department of Social Welfare and Development, the Department of Education,
the Department of Health, the Commission on Human Rights, and duly
recognized child rights and youth organizations, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations.
       SEC. 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue in full force and effect.
       SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modified accordingly.
       SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
