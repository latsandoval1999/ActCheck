                        AN ACT
ALLOWING,    UNDER    EXCEPTIONAL    CONDITIONS,  THE
APPOINTMENT AND GRANT OF ACADEMIC TENURE TO
QUALIFIED FOREIGN NATIONALS IN TEACHING AND RESEARCH
POSITIONS IN STATE UNIVERSITIES AND COLLEGES, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The proposed Foreign Academic Faculty Tenure Act affirms as a
controlling constitutional principle that government service is primarily reserved
to Filipino citizens, a rule rooted in national sovereignty, democratic
accountability, and public trust. At the same time, it recognizes that public higher
education plays a strategic role in scientific advancement, economic productivity,
and national development. This measure therefore establishes a narrowly tailored
and strictly exceptional framework under which foreign nationals may be
appointed to tenured teaching or research positions in State Universities and
Colleges (SUCs), only when their expertise is demonstrably unavailable or
insufficient within the Filipino academic community. The bill underscores that
SUCs exist principally to cultivate Filipino human capital and leadership, and that
any engagement of foreign faculty must be instrumental, time-bound, and
capacity-building in nature—never a substitute for sustained investment in
Filipino faculty development.

       To safeguard constitutional fidelity, the Act limits its application
exclusively to teaching and research roles within SUCs and expressly excludes
all administrative, executive, managerial, governance, and policy-determining
positions, which shall at all times remain reserved to Filipino citizens. It clarifies
that such academic posts, by their professional and non-sovereign character, are
not deemed public offices for purposes of the citizenship requirement, while
preserving the public character of SUCs and compliance with all other laws.
Foreign appointments are permitted only upon a rigorous showing of exceptional
necessity and internationally distinguished merit, subject to approval, periodic
renewal-of-necessity review, non-displacement safeguards, and strict
prohibitions against administrative authority. The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) is vested with primary oversight, regulatory, and enforcement
authority, in coordination with the Civil Service Commission, to ensure uniform
standards, transparency, and constitutional compliance, including the imposition
of graduated sanctions for violations.

        Ultimately, this bill seeks to reconcile the constitutional primacy of
Filipino citizenship in public service with the strategic need, in rare and clearly
justified circumstances, to access globally distinguished expertise for the
advancement of national academic standards and priority disciplines. It
institutionalizes stringent tests of necessity, continuing qualification review,
knowledge-transfer commitments, and a five-year sunset review to prevent
dependency and ensure that any foreign engagement directly strengthens Filipino
faculty capability and institutional self-sufficiency. By embedding strict
safeguards, accountability mechanisms, and legislative oversight, the measure
ensures that foreign academic tenure remains exceptional, limited, and aligned
with the long-term objective of building a self-reliant, world-class Filipino higher
education system. The passage of this Act is thus earnestly sought to uphold
constitutional principles while prudently advancing national academic
excellence.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Foreign Academic Faculty
Tenure Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

   (a) Uphold, as a controlling constitutional principle, that government service
      is primarily reserved to Filipino citizens, such principle being essential to
      the preservation of national sovereignty, democratic accountability, and
   public trust, and to permit any departure therefrom only upon a showing
   of compelling state interest, under circumstances that are exceptional,
   indispensable, and narrowly confined, and in a manner that does not dilute
   the general rule of Filipino primacy in public service;


(b) Recognize education, particularly public higher education, as a vital
   instrument of national development, scientific and technological
   advancement, economic productivity, social mobility, and cultural
   enrichment, and as a strategic public function through which the State
   cultivates human capital, strengthens national competitiveness, and
   secures long-term societal resilience;


(c) Affirm that State Universities and Colleges exist primarily to develop
   Filipino human capital, Filipino scholarship, and Filipino academic
   leadership, and that their foremost obligation is to educate Filipino
   students, build indigenous research capacity, and nurture Filipino faculty
   who will sustain and lead the nation’s higher education system;


(d) Allow the engagement of foreign nationals in public higher education
   solely in exceptional circumstances where their expertise is demonstrably
   unavailable, insufficient, or not yet developed within the Filipino academic
   community, and where such expertise is clearly necessary to attain
   academic standards, disciplinary competence, or national development
   objectives that cannot reasonably be achieved through Filipino resources
   alone;


(e) Ensure that any foreign participation in State Universities and Colleges is
   strictly instrumental, time-bound in purpose, and capacity-building in
   effect, designed to facilitate knowledge transfer, faculty mentoring,

                                                                             2
      collaborative research, and curriculum enhancement, and is never
      construed or utilized as a substitute for sustained, deliberate, and
      systematic    investment    in   Filipino    faculty   recruitment,   training,
      advancement, and leadership development; and


   (f) Require that any exception to citizenship requirements in government
      service be narrowly tailored to achieve a compelling public purpose,
      employ the least restrictive means consistent with that purpose, be
      implemented with full transparency and accountability, and be subject to
      robust constitutional, statutory, and institutional safeguards, including
      oversight, periodic review, and legislative evaluation, to ensure continuing
      fidelity to both the letter and spirit of the Constitution.


SEC. 3. Scope and Application — This Act shall apply exclusively and strictly
to the following:

   (a) State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) as defined under Republic Act No.
       8292, otherwise known as the “Higher Education Modernization Act of
       1997,” including only those institutions that are created by law, funded
       wholly or substantially by the national government, and vested with the
       mandate to provide public higher education; and


   (b) Teaching and research faculty positions, limited to instructional,
       professorial, clinical teaching, laboratory-based, or research-oriented
       academic posts, whose primary functions consist of classroom instruction,
       scholarly research, curriculum development, academic mentoring, or
       knowledge production, and which do not entail, whether directly or
       indirectly, administrative authority, decision-making power, budgetary
       control, personnel supervision, or policy influence.

                                                                                   3
For the avoidance of doubt, administrative, executive, managerial, fiduciary,
supervisory, governance, and policy-determining positions, including but not
limited to offices involving institutional leadership, budgetary control, personnel
management, regulatory authority, or strategic decision-making, are expressly
and absolutely excluded from the coverage of this Act and shall, in all cases,
remain reserved to Filipino citizens in accordance with the Constitution and
existing laws.


SEC. 4. Exceptional Nature of Foreign Faculty Appointments — Appointments
of foreign nationals under this Act shall be exceptional in character and shall not
form part of the ordinary or regular staffing complement of any State University
or College.

No SUC shall rely on foreign faculty as a routine, permanent, or substitute staffing
solution. The burden of demonstrating exceptional necessity shall at all times rest
with the appointing institution.


SEC. 5. Authority to Appoint Foreign Nationals to Tenured Teaching Positions
— Notwithstanding existing laws, rules, or regulations, a State University or
College may appoint a foreign national to a tenured teaching or research position
only upon a clear showing of exceptional academic necessity, subject to the
safeguards provided herein.

Provided, That such appointment is confined exclusively to teaching, research,
mentoring, and scholarly functions; Provided, further, That no such appointment
shall be construed as conferring eligibility for administrative, governance, or
policy-making roles; Provided, finally, That such appointment shall be approved
by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED).


                                                                                  4
SEC. 6. Teaching Positions Not Deemed Public Office — Teaching and research
positions occupied by foreign nationals pursuant to this Act shall not be deemed
public offices within the contemplation of the constitutional rule requiring
citizenship for government service, as such positions are, by their nature and
function,   non-sovereign,   non-political,   and   non-discretionary,   and   are
characterized by the following:

   (a) The functions performed are confined to academic instruction, scholarly
      inquiry, research activity, curriculum development, and the dissemination
      of knowledge, and do not include the exercise of sovereign authority,
      coercive governmental power, or any function that binds the State or the
      public through command, regulation, or enforcement;


   (b) The duties attached to such positions are limited to academic and scholarly
      judgment and exclude the formulation of public policy, the implementation
      or enforcement of laws or regulations, or participation in governmental
      decision-making processes that determine public rights, obligations, or
      institutional direction beyond purely academic matters;


   (c) The positions are professional, technical, and academic in nature, requiring
      specialized expertise, scholarly competence, and disciplinary judgment,
      and are assessed primarily through academic standards, peer review,
      research output, teaching effectiveness, and scholarly contribution, rather
      than through political accountability or governmental authority; and


   (d) The authority exercised arises from academic freedom, collegial
      governance, peer evaluation, and institutional academic processes, and is
      subject to ethical norms and professional standards, rather than to political
      control, partisan influence, or the exercise of political power.

                                                                                 5
The classification of such teaching positions as non-public offices shall apply
solely for purposes of constitutional interpretation under this Act, and shall not
be construed to diminish the public character of State Universities and Colleges
or to relax any other legal, immigration, labor, or regulatory requirements
applicable to foreign nationals under existing laws.


Section 7. Nature and Limits of Academic Tenure —

   a. Definition — Academic tenure granted under this Act shall mean the
      conferment of security of academic employment based on merit,
      demonstrated competence, sustained performance, and the protection of
      academic freedom essential to teaching, research, and scholarly work. Such
      tenure is granted solely to preserve continuity of instruction, intellectual
      independence, and scholarly integrity within the academic community, and
      shall not be construed as a guarantee of employment beyond the legitimate
      scope of academic functions authorized under this Act. Provided, That
      academic tenure granted pursuant to this Act shall not be deemed civil
      service tenure nor constitute a permanent appointment within the meaning
      of civil service laws, rules, or regulations, Provided, further, That it shall
      not confer civil service eligibility, plantilla permanence, or any vested right
      to   appointment,    promotion,    succession,    or   designation    to   any
      administrative or executive position within a State University or College.


   b. Monitoring and Evaluation — Such academic tenure shall at all times
      remain subject to periodic academic evaluation, compliance with
      institutional performance standards, adherence to ethical norms and
      professional conduct, and accountability mechanisms prescribed by the
      institution and the Commission on Higher Education, Provided, That
      nothing herein shall be construed to impair the authority of State
                                                                                   6
      Universities and Colleges to enforce academic standards, impose
      disciplinary sanctions for cause, or terminate appointments in accordance
      with due process and applicable academic, institutional, and regulatory
      requirements.

SEC. 8. Exceptional Qualifications Required of Foreign Tenured Faculty — A
foreign national may be appointed under this Act only upon a clear, convincing,
and documented showing of exceptional merit, and only if he or she satisfies all
of the following qualifications:

   (a) Possesses internationally distinguished academic credentials, including a
       terminal degree from a globally recognized institution of higher learning,
       or equivalent professional standing duly recognized in the relevant
       discipline, supported by evidence of international reputation, peer
       recognition, or leadership in the field, and demonstrating a level of
       expertise substantially exceeding ordinary academic or professional
       competence;


   (b) Demonstrates sustained and exceptional scholarly, scientific, or
       professional output of international caliber, as evidenced by high-impact
       peer-reviewed publications, patents, copyrighted works, significant
       technological or scientific innovations, or other achievements recognized
       by reputable international bodies, professional associations, or academic
       institutions, reflecting a continuing record of excellence rather than
       isolated accomplishment;


   (c) Be appointed to a discipline, specialization, or field of study where there
       exists a documented and persistent shortage of qualified Filipino faculty,
       or where the field is newly emerging, highly technical, interdisciplinary,
       or rapidly evolving in nature, such that local academic capacity is
                                                                                7
       demonstrably insufficient or not yet adequately developed within a
       reasonable period of time;


   (d) Brings expertise that is demonstrably and strategically aligned with
       national development priorities, workforce requirements, research and
       innovation agendas, or sectoral needs, as identified or endorsed by the
       Commission on Higher Education, including priorities related to science
       and technology, health, engineering, digital transformation, sustainability,
       or other areas of national significance; and


   (e) Commits contractually, as a condition of appointment and continued
       tenure, to faculty mentoring, joint research and publication, curriculum
       development,    instructional   capacity-building,   or   other   structured
       knowledge-transfer activities designed to strengthen Filipino faculty
       competence, institutional capability, and long-term academic self-
       sufficiency.


SEC. 9. Test of Exceptional Necessity — No appointment under this Act shall be
approved unless the appointing State University or College, with the prior
concurrence of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), establishes
through clear, documented, and contemporaneous evidence that all of the
following conditions are fully satisfied:

   (a) The academic, instructional, or research need sought to be addressed
       cannot be adequately met by existing Filipino faculty, despite
       demonstrable and good-faith efforts to recruit, retain, retrain, or develop
       qualified Filipino personnel within a reasonable period of time, including
       the utilization of faculty development programs, scholarships, fellowships,
       or other capacity-building measures;

                                                                                 8
   (b) The foreign national’s expertise is clearly superior, unique, or
      indispensable to the academic program, research agenda, or scholarly
      activity concerned, as shown by credentials, experience, or scholarly
      output that substantially exceed those reasonably available within the local
      academic community, and which are essential to achieving clearly defined
      academic or institutional objectives;


   (c) The anticipated academic, research, and institutional benefits of the
      appointment substantially outweigh any risk of displacement of Filipino
      faculty, erosion of local academic opportunities, or long-term institutional
      dependency on foreign expertise, taking into account both immediate
      outcomes and long-term effects on Filipino faculty development and
      succession; and


   (d) The appointment is reasonably expected to result in measurable, verifiable,
      and time-bound improvements in academic quality, research productivity,
      instructional effectiveness, or Filipino faculty capability, including
      outcomes such as enhanced curricula, increased research output,
      strengthened mentoring systems, or demonstrable progress in developing
      Filipino expertise in the relevant field.


Failure to satisfy any of the foregoing conditions, whether at the time of initial
appointment or upon subsequent review, shall bar the approval or continuation of
the appointment under this Act, without prejudice to the application of other
safeguards and oversight mechanisms provided herein.


SEC. 10. Renewal-of-Necessity and Continuing Qualification Review —

Appointments of foreign nationals under this Act shall be subject to a periodic
renewalof-necessity review, as a condition for the continuation of academic
                                                                                9
tenure, to ensure that the exceptional circumstances justifying the appointment
remain present and compelling.

Such renewal-of-necessity review shall be conducted at intervals to be determined
by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), but in no case less frequent
than once every five (5) years, and shall require a clear, documented, and
contemporaneous showing that the foreign national continues to satisfy the
qualifications and conditions prescribed under this Act, including the exceptional-
merit criteria enumerated herein.

The renewal-of-necessity review shall assess compliance with contractual
commitments on faculty mentoring, joint research, curriculum development, and
structured knowledge transfer, and shall evaluate whether measurable progress
has been achieved in strengthening Filipino faculty competence, institutional
capability, and long-term academic self-sufficiency.

Where the appointing institution or CHED determines that the exceptional
necessity for the appointment no longer exists, that the qualifications required
under this Act are no longer satisfied, or that adequate Filipino expertise has
become reasonably available, the continuation of academic tenure shall be denied
or withdrawn in accordance with due process, without prejudice to the authority
of the institution to transition academic responsibilities to qualified Filipino
faculty.

Provided, That the burden of demonstrating continuing necessity and compliance
with the requirements of this Act shall at all times rest with the appointing
institution and the foreign national concerned; Provided, finally, That any doubt
arising from the renewal-ofnecessity review shall be resolved in favor of Filipino
faculty primacy and the strict, exceptional application of this Act.


                                                                                10
SEC. 11. Prohibition on Administrative, and Governance Functions — Foreign
nationals appointed under this Act are absolutely and unequivocally prohibited
from engaging in, assuming, or exercising any administrative, executive,
governance, or representative function within a State University or College, and
shall, in all cases, be confined to academic teaching and research roles only.
Accordingly, no foreign national appointed pursuant to this Act shall:

   (a) Hold, occupy, assume, or be designated to positions such as university
      president, vice-president, chancellor, dean, director, department chair,
      program head, or any equivalent or analogous post, whether permanent,
      acting, interim, concurrent, or in a holdover capacity, regardless of the
      manner of appointment or designation;


   (b) Exercise, directly or indirectly, any authority or influence over institutional
      budgets, financial planning, procurement processes, allocation or
      disbursement of public funds, personnel appointments, promotions,
      discipline,   or   other   matters    involving    institutional   governance,
      management, or administration;


   (c) Vote in, participate as a decision-maker in, or otherwise exercise
      determinative influence within governing boards, councils, committees, or
      similar bodies vested with policy-making, regulatory, approval, or
      decision-making authority, including bodies that determine academic
      policies beyond purely instructional or scholarly matters; or


   (d) Represent the State University or College in official dealings,
      communications, negotiations, or engagements with national government
      agencies, local government units, constitutional bodies, regulatory



                                                                                   11
       authorities, foreign governments, international organizations, or inter-
       agency bodies, whether in an official, advisory, or spokesperson capacity.


Any act performed in violation of this section shall be void and of no legal effect,
without prejudice to the immediate cessation of the prohibited activity and the
imposition of appropriate administrative or legal sanctions as may be provided
under existing laws and regulations.


SEC 12. Priority for Filipino Faculty and Non-Displacement Safeguards —
Appointments under this Act shall be permitted only upon the issuance of a
formal, written certification by the appointing State University or College, with
the concurrence of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), affirming that
all of the following conditions have been fully satisfied:

   (a) No qualified Filipino faculty member is displaced, demoted, or removed
       from an existing position, nor unjustly bypassed, overlooked, or denied
       appointment, promotion, or assignment, as a result of the engagement of a
       foreign national, and that reasonable, good-faith efforts have been
       undertaken to recruit, retain, or develop Filipino faculty for the position
       concerned;


   (b) Parallel and proportionate investments in Filipino faculty development,
       including scholarships, fellowships, training programs, research funding,
       and other capacity-building initiatives, are maintained or strengthened, and
       that the engagement of a foreign national does not result in the reduction,
       diversion, or substitution of resources intended for the advancement of
       Filipino academic personnel; and




                                                                                 12
   (c) The engagement of foreign faculty is demonstrably designed to and does
       directly contribute to the long-term development of Filipino academic
       leadership, research capability, and institutional self-sufficiency, including
       through structured mentoring, collaborative research, curriculum
       enhancement, and the preparation of qualified Filipino successors in the
       relevant discipline or field.


Failure to secure or maintain the certification required under this section shall bar
the approval, continuation, or renewal of the appointment under this Act, without
prejudice to the exercise of oversight and corrective authority by the Commission
on Higher Education.


SEC. 13. CHED Oversight and Regulation — The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall exercise primary regulatory, supervisory, and oversight
authority over the implementation of this Act, and for this purpose shall:

   (a) Issue detailed, clear, and comprehensive guidelines governing eligibility,
       appointment, academic tenure standards, performance evaluation,
       renewal-of-necessity review, and termination of appointments of foreign
       nationals, including the establishment of uniform criteria, documentary
       requirements, timelines, and procedures to ensure consistent application
       across all State Universities and Colleges;
   (b) Approve or disapprove institutional justifications for the appointment and
       continued engagement of foreign faculty, based on a rigorous assessment
       of academic necessity, exceptional merit, alignment with national
       development priorities, and compliance with all substantive and
       procedural safeguards prescribed under this Act and its implementing rules
       and regulations;


                                                                                  13
   (c) Establish, maintain, and regularly update a centralized national registry of
        foreign nationals appointed to tenured teaching or research positions under
        this Act, containing relevant information on qualifications, fields of
        specialization, duration of appointment, renewal status, and compliance
        with knowledge-transfer and mentoring commitments, subject to
        applicable data privacy laws;


   (d) Monitor, audit, and evaluate compliance by State Universities and
        Colleges with all safeguards, limitations, prohibitions, and conditions
        imposed under this Act, including the authority to conduct inspections,
        require the submission of reports and supporting documents, issue
        corrective   directives,   disallow   non-compliant   appointments,    and
        recommend appropriate administrative action against erring institutions or
        officials; and


   (e) Submit to Congress an annual report on the implementation of this Act,
        detailing the number and distribution of foreign faculty appointments,
        disciplines covered, outcomes achieved, impacts on academic quality and
        research productivity, progress in Filipino faculty development, identified
        challenges, and any legislative or policy recommendations necessary to
        ensure the continued exceptional, limited, and constitutionally compliant
        application of this measure.


SEC. 14. CHED Enforcement Powers — In order to ensure faithful, uniform,
and constitutionally compliant implementation of this Act, the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) is hereby vested with enforcement and corrective
authority, without prejudice to the powers of other oversight bodies under existing
laws.

                                                                                14
For this purpose, CHED is authorized to impose graduated sanctions,
proportionate to the nature, gravity, frequency, and intent of the violation,
including the following:

   (a) The issuance of written notices of non-compliance, directives for
      corrective action, or formal warnings, requiring the State University or
      College concerned to remedy identified deficiencies within a specified
      period;


   (b) The suspension, disallowance, or revocation of approval for a foreign
      faculty appointment or renewal thereof, where such appointment is found
      to be inconsistent with the requirements, safeguards, or limitations
      prescribed under this Act or its implementing rules and regulations;


   (c) The suspension of authority to make further appointments under this Act
      for a specified period, where an institution is found to have engaged in
      repeated, willful, or systemic violations, including circumvention of
      prohibitions or misrepresentation of academic necessity;


   (d) The recommendation to appropriate authorities of administrative sanctions
      against responsible officials, including but not limited to reprimand,
      suspension, or other disciplinary action, in accordance with civil service,
      anti-graft, and accountability laws; and


   (e) The referral of matters involving fraud, misrepresentation, bad faith, or

      other actionable conduct to the appropriate investigative, prosecutorial, or
      oversight bodies.


In the exercise of its enforcement powers, CHED shall observe due process,
including notice and opportunity to be heard, and shall ensure that sanctions
                                                                               15
imposed are graduated, proportionate, and corrective, rather than punitive, except
where the circumstances warrant otherwise.


SEC. 15. Coordination with the Civil Service Commission — To ensure
consistency with constitutional principles governing public employment and to
prevent circumvention of citizenship and civil service rules, the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) shall coordinate closely with the Civil Service
Commission (CSC) in the implementation of this Act.

Such coordination shall include, but not be limited to:

   (a) The joint development or harmonization of guidelines, advisories, or

       protocols to clearly delineate academic teaching and research positions
       covered by this Act from civil service positions requiring Filipino
       citizenship;


   (b) The exchange of information, records, and findings relevant to

       appointments, reclassifications, plantilla designations, or personnel actions
       involving foreign nationals in State Universities and Colleges;


   (c) The conduct of joint or coordinated reviews, audits, or compliance checks,

       particularly in cases involving alleged circumvention, misclassification of
       positions, or unauthorized exercise of administrative or governance
       functions; and


   (d) The formulation of coordinated recommendations for corrective action or

       sanctions, consistent with the respective mandates of CHED and the CSC.


Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish the constitutional authority of
the Civil Service Commission over the civil service, nor to impair the regulatory
                                                                                 16
authority of CHED over higher education institutions. In case of ambiguity or
conflict in classification, the presumption shall favor the applicability of civil
service rules, unless the position clearly and unequivocally falls within the limited
academic exception established under this Act.


SEC. 16. Sunset Review — Five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act, the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall conduct a comprehensive,
evidence-based, and institution-wide review of the implementation of this Act,
for the purpose of assessing the continuing necessity, effectiveness, and
constitutional compliance of allowing the exceptional appointment of foreign
nationals to tenured teaching and research positions in State Universities and
Colleges.

Such review shall include, but shall not be limited to, an evaluation of the
academic, institutional, and systemic impacts of the Act, including its effect on
academic quality, research productivity, knowledge transfer, and the
development, retention, and advancement of Filipino faculty and academic
leadership, as well as an assessment of whether the exceptional circumstances
justifying such appointments continue to exist.

The review shall further examine the adequacy and effectiveness of safeguards,
limitations, enforcement mechanisms, and inter-agency coordination measures
established under this Act, including compliance by State Universities and
Colleges with non-displacement, non-circumvention, and renewal-of-necessity
requirements.

Upon completion of the review, CHED shall submit to Congress a comprehensive
report containing its findings, conclusions, and specific recommendations,
including whether the authority granted under this Act should be continued,

                                                                                  17
modified, further restricted, or allowed to lapse, in whole or in part, in order to
remain consistent with constitutional principles and the long-term objective of
strengthening Filipino academic capacity.


SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in
consultation with the Civil Service Commission (CSC) and State Universities and
Colleges, as well as other relevant government agencies as may be necessary,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) necessary to carry
out the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 18. Separability Clause — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 19. Repealing Clause — All laws, rules, regulations, and issuances
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 20. Effectivity — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
