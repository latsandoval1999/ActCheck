                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine Normal University (PNU) occupies a unique and singular position in the
Philippine higher education system. It is the only institution designated by law as the
National Center for Teacher Education, and the only public higher education institution
in the country whose academic mandate is exclusively devoted to teaching and allied
education professions. Unlike comprehensive universities with multiple disciplinary
mandates, PNU’s sole and enduring mission is the preparation, development, and
advancement of teachers, teacher educators, and education leaders across all levels and
contexts of Philippine education. As a multi-campus national university system, PNU
carries out this mandate through its flagship campus in Manila and its constituent
campuses in Isabela, Quezon, Negros Occidental, and Agusan del Sur, enabling the
University to respond to the diverse educational, cultural, linguistic, environmental, and
socio-economic realities of Philippine education across the archipelago.

Through this coordinated network of regional and thematic hubs, PNU strengthens the
country’s capacity for context-responsive teacher education, research, innovation,
extension, and policy support. This system-wide structure places the University in a
unique position to respond to the systemic challenges facing Philippine education. Since
the quality of education is fundamentally tied to the strength and coherence of teacher
education, persistent issues in teacher preparation point to the need for coordinated,
long-term, and nationally anchored responses.

The quality of Philippine education depends on the strength, coherence, and
sustainability of its teacher education system. National assessments and policy reviews
over time have consistently identified persistent challenges in Philippine education,
particularly in teacher preparation. These include uneven quality of teacher education
across institutions and regions, weak integration of research into teaching practice,
misalignment between pre-service education and classroom realities, and limited
institutional capacity to sustain innovation and reform in teacher education over time.
These challenges are systemic in nature and require coordinated, long-term responses.

Recent international large-scale assessments further highlight the urgency of these
reforms. The Philippines has consistently ranked among the lowest-performing countries
in major global and regional assessments, including the Programme for International
Student Assessment (PISA), Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study
(TIMSS), and Southeast Asia Primary Learning Metrics (SEA-PLM). In the 2022 PISA, the
Philippines ranked 77th out of 81 countries. In the 2019 TIMSS, it placed last among 58
participating countries in both mathematics and science. While modest gains were seen
in the 2024 SEA-PLM, the country still remained near the bottom among participating
ASEAN nations. These results point to continuing gaps in foundational learning and
highlight the important role of teacher quality in improving student performance and
overall education outcomes.

In response, the State has implemented major reforms aimed at strengthening the
teaching profession and improving governance in teacher education. Republic Act No.
9647 designated the Philippine Normal University (PNU) as the National Center for
Teacher Education (NCTE), while Republic Act No. 11713 institutionalized the Teacher
Education Council (TEC) to promote policy coordination and coherence across education
agencies. These reforms have been reinforced by the Enhanced Basic Education Act, the
adoption of professional standards for teachers and school leaders, licensure and
regulatory systems administered by the Professional Regulation Commission, and quality
assurance mechanisms under the Commission on Higher Education. Together, these
measures have strengthened the regulatory, standards-setting, and accountability
frameworks that govern teacher education and professional development in the country.

While these reforms have established a more coherent policy and regulatory
environment, they have also revealed a critical institutional gap. Existing laws primarily
address coordination, regulation, standards, and oversight, but they do not provide a
dedicated national academic institution with the mandate, authority, and continuity to
support sustained system-wide improvement in teacher education. In particular, the
NCTE framework under Republic Act No. 9647 positions PNU primarily as a coordinating
and advisory body and does not sufficiently equip it to undertake long-term research
engagement, reform piloting, professional learning, and institutional capacity
development at scale in partnership with Teacher Education Institutions nationwide.

To address these gaps, this measure seeks to revise the Charter of the Philippine Normal
University and formally designate it as the National University for Teacher Education
(NUTE). The proposed charter revision preserves the University’s institutional continuity
while strengthening its legal, academic, and organizational foundations to carry out an
expanded national mandate in teacher education and education sciences. More
importantly, the designation of PNU as a national university represents a substantive
institutional reform. It aims to strengthen the State’s long-term capacity to lead,
coordinate, and sustain teacher education reforms across administrations, regions, and
diverse educational contexts. Through this measure, PNU is positioned not only as a
center of excellence, but also as a national institution with the mandate, authority, and
capacity to advance innovation, research, policy development, and standards-setting for
the continuous improvement of teacher education in the Philippines.

As the country’s only institution exclusively mandated for teacher education, the
Philippine Normal University (PNU) operates within a field that is broad, complex, and
multidisciplinary. Teacher education supports the country’s basic education system by
preparing teachers across different subject areas, learner needs, and socio-cultural
contexts. National assessments, including findings from the Second Congressional
Commission on Education (EDCOM II), have identified continuing shortages and uneven
teacher preparation in key areas such as mathematics, science, technology, technical-
vocational and livelihood education, values education, special education, inclusive
education, and other emerging fields.

Alongside strengthening academic and professional preparation, teacher education must
continue to uphold values formation as a central component of the profession. Teachers
are expected not only to demonstrate competence in instruction and content knowledge,
but also to embody ethical leadership, social responsibility, and commitment to nation-
building, consistent with nationally recognized professional standards for teachers and
education leaders.

Given the Philippine context as an archipelagic and disaster-prone nation, teacher
education must prepare educators not only in pedagogy and content knowledge, but
also in resilience, emergency preparedness, and responsible technology use. Teachers
must be equipped with practical competencies such as basic life support, first aid, water
safety, survival skills, disaster response, and financial literacy to help safeguard learners
and respond effectively during emergencies and community disruptions. At the same
time, while artificial intelligence and digital technologies offer important opportunities for
innovation in teaching and learning, their integration must remain balanced and
purposeful. Teacher education must continue to strengthen foundational literacy, reading
comprehension, critical thinking, and writing skills, ensuring that technology supports
rather than replaces human judgment, creativity, and intellectual discipline.

Addressing persistent gaps in basic education requires a steady and sustained supply of
teachers with strong disciplinary preparation and professional competence across a wide
range of specializations. This demand calls for a teacher education system anchored by
a national institution with the capacity, disciplinary breadth, and long-term mandate to
respond to both existing shortages and emerging educational priorities. As a core
responsibility of the State, teacher education requires sustained institutional focus,
strategic investment, and coordinated national leadership. Unlike comprehensive
universities that distribute resources and attention across multiple academic fields, a
dedicated National University for Teacher Education ensures that teacher preparation,
teacher development, and education sciences remain central institutional priorities.

The Philippine Normal University is uniquely positioned to fulfill this role. PNU operates
through a system of specialized campuses that collectively provide one of the broadest
ranges of teacher education expertise in the country. Each campus functions as a regional
and thematic hub responsive to local development needs and national education
priorities, forming an integrated network of complementary strengths. Declaring PNU as
the National University for Teacher Education will strengthen its capacity to lead national
reforms, expand specialization areas, support and partner with other Teacher Education
Institutions, and contribute to a more adequate, balanced, and future-ready supply of
competent teachers across disciplines and regions.

Institutionally, PNU’s quality and maturity are well established. It is a SUC Level III
university, ISO 9001:2015 certified, and a Philippine Quality Award (PQA) recipient. It
maintains 110 Level IV accredited programs and is recognized by CHED and the Teacher
Education Council as a Center of Excellence in Teacher Education. Since 2009, PNU has
produced 149 topnotchers in the Licensure Examination for Teachers, demonstrating
sustained academic excellence.

Internationally, PNU continues to build a strong reputation for innovation and social
responsibility, including recognition in global rankings and leadership in regional teacher
education networks.

Declaring the Philippine Normal University as the National University for Teacher
Education (NUTE) is not merely an institutional recognition but a strategic national
reform. As NUTE, PNU will:
    a. lead national research and innovation in teacher education and lifelong learning;
    b. strengthen academic capacity and quality assurance across Teacher Education
       Institutions;
    c. provide policy advice and technical expertise to national education bodies;
    d. expand equitable access to high-quality teacher education, particularly for
       underserved learners; and
    e. enhance the resilience, inclusivity, and global competitiveness of the teaching
       profession.

To support these objectives, the proposed measure introduces key structural and policy
reforms, including a mandatory collaboration framework with education agencies, the
establishment of laboratory schools for clinical practice, strengthened faculty
development mechanisms, integration of values formation and life skills in teacher
education, and a balanced framework for the use of artificial intelligence in teaching and
learning. The proposed measure likewise recognizes the importance of participatory and
transparent governance. The proposed measure likewise promotes participatory,
transparent, and sector-responsive governance mechanisms within the University,
consistent with its national mandate and public character.

This measure aligns with the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028, AmBisyon Natin
2040, and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4 on Quality Education. It
affirms the country’s commitment to ensuring that every Filipino learner is taught by a
competent, ethical, resilient, and future-ready teacher.

As it marks its 125th year of service and 15th year as the National Center for Teacher
Education, the Philippine Normal University stands ready to fulfill this expanded mandate
as the National University for Teacher Education (NUTE), leading systemic transformation
in teacher education and advancing excellence, inclusion, and innovation in Philippine
education.

For these reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly recommended.

35   SEC. 5. Role of PNU. — As NUTE, PNU shall serve as the State’s academic, research, and
36   leadership institution for teacher education, guiding the continuous improvement of the
37   teacher education system through innovation, scholarship, and capacity development.
38
39   SEC. 5.1. Nature and Scope of the Mandate. – The exercise of PNU’s mandate as the
40   NUTE shall be academic, research-based, developmental, and model-setting in nature. In
41   carrying out this mandate, PNU shall develop academic standards, research-based
42   benchmarks, and model frameworks; lead the formulation and piloting of innovations and
43   reforms; and provide policy advice and technical expertise, all without prejudice to and
44   subject to the statutory regulatory, accreditation, and licensure powers of the CHED, TEC,
45   the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Technical Education and Skills
46   Development Authority (TESDA), and other relevant government agencies under existing
47   laws.
48       a) Provide evidence-based policy advice, research-informed recommendations, and
49          technical support to the Department of Education (DepEd), CHED, TEC, PRC,
50          TESDA, and other relevant agencies for teacher education policy, innovation, and
51          system improvement; and serve as the principal academic adviser to the TEC in
52          the formulation and implementation of teacher education policies and programs.
53       b) Lead in the formulation, piloting, and scaling of national reforms in teacher
54          education, training, and curriculum innovation. Integrate artificial intelligence (AI),
 1         digital tools, and adaptive technologies in teacher preparation and professional
 2         development to ensure inclusion of underserved and remote regions. Collaborate
 3         with national agencies and TEIs in co-designing research-based, inclusive, and
 4         future-ready curricula.
 5      c) Serve as a research-intensive university advancing teacher education and
 6         education sciences, emphasizing teacher quality, pre-service and graduate
 7         education, lifelong learning, and continuing professional development for teachers
 8         and education leaders.
 9      d) Set standards and benchmarks for transformative, inclusive, innovative, and
10         sustainable teacher education and education sciences across undergraduate, post-
11         baccalaureate, and graduate levels.
12      e) Serve as the international hub for collaborative teacher education, global mobility,
13         and multilateral academic partnerships, promoting international benchmarking and
14         alignment with global and regional standards, particularly within ASEAN and the
15         Asia-Pacific, in accordance with the relevant rules and guidelines on international
16         linkages.
17      f) Lead the creation, utilization, and dissemination of research with policy
18         implications to teacher education and education sciences through its national
19         research units — the Research Institute for Teacher Quality (RITQ), the Institute
20         for Educational Policy Research and Development (IEPRD), the Research Institute
21         for Lifelong Learning (RILL), and the Research Institute for Teacher Welfare and
22         Well-Being (RITWW), and other similar units as may be approved by the Board of
23         Regents (BOR).
24      g) Lead the development and implementation of research-based and alternative
25         pedagogies and assessment models in teacher education and lifelong learning. The
26         University may establish, upon BOR approval, specialized centers to advance
27         lifelong learning and continuing education.
28      h) Upon request of Congress or its committees, provide technical assistance and
29         evidence-based inputs in the formulation, review, or evaluation of legislative
30         measures concerning teacher education, teacher training, continuing professional
31         development, and lifelong learning.
32      i) Serve as the lead academic partner of the Department of Economy, Planning and
33         Development (DEPDev), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Department of
34         Education (DepEd), Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), Technical
35         Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), Department of Science and
36         Technology (DOST), and other national agencies.

38   SEC. 5.2. Balanced Integration of Artificial Intelligence and Traditional Pedagogy. — In
39   pursuing the integration of artificial intelligence (AI), digital tools, and adaptive
40   technologies in teacher education, the University shall adopt a balanced framework that
41   safeguards foundational literacy, critical thinking, and intellectual independence. The
42   University shall ensure that the use of AI enhances, and does not replace, human
43   cognition, creativity, and professional judgment.
44
45   In furtherance of this framework, the University shall:
46       a) Ensure that pre-service teachers demonstrate proficiency in foundational skills,
47          including reading comprehension, analytical writing, critical thinking, and problem-
48          solving without reliance on AI tools, prior to and alongside the integration of such
49          technologies;
50       b) Develop and implement pedagogical guidelines that clearly distinguish AI as a tool
51          for learning support and augmentation, and not as a substitute for cognitive effort,
52          emphasizing that AI shall complement but not replace human reasoning, reflection,
53          and authorship;
54       c) Require coursework on AI literacy for all pre-service teachers, including ethical and
 1         responsible use, data privacy, misinformation and bias, verification of AI-generated
 2         outputs, and the cognitive implications of AI dependency, consistent with national
 3         frameworks on responsible AI use;
 4      d) Mandate that teacher education programs include explicit instruction and practice
 5         in traditional and evidence-based pedagogical approaches, including close reading,
 6         writing without AI assistance, classroom discourse, and other methods that
 7         strengthen comprehension, memory, and higher-order thinking skills;
 8      e) Establish and support research initiatives within the Research Institute for Lifelong
 9         Learning (RILL) and other relevant research units to study the impact of AI and
10         digital technologies on teacher cognition, student learning outcomes, and
11         foundational skills, and to generate evidence-based recommendations for policy
12         and practice. Such research shall include the study of cognitive, pedagogical, and
13         developmental impacts of artificial intelligence on learners and teachers, and shall
14         inform national policy and curriculum standards. And;
15      f) Collaborate with the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher
16         Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
17         (TESDA), and the Teacher Education Council (TEC) in the development of national
18         guidelines on the balanced and responsible integration of AI in teacher education
19         and learning environments.

21   SEC. 5.3. Mandatory Collaboration Framework for Addressing Education Sector
22   Challenges. — To ensure that the University’s mandate translates into measurable
23   improvements in the basic education, higher education, and technical-vocational
24   education and training sectors, the University shall establish and maintain a structured
25   collaboration framework with the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher
26   Education, the Teacher Education Council, the Technical Education and Skills
27   Development Authority, and other relevant agencies. Under this framework, the following
28   mechanisms shall be implemented:
29
30      a) Annual Education Sector Work Plan. — The University, in consultation with the
31         Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education, and the Technical
32         Education and Skills Development Authority, shall prepare and submit to the
33         Teacher Education Council an annual education sector work plan identifying priority
34         challenges in teacher education and basic education, including but not limited to
35         learning outcomes in international assessments, teacher shortages in critical
36         subject areas, technical-vocational education, and disaster-related education
37         disruptions. The work plan shall outline the University’s proposed research, policy
38         recommendations, technical assistance, and capacity-building interventions to
39         address these concerns and shall be made publicly available.
40      b) Regular Policy Advisory with Formal Response Requirement. — The University shall
41         issue a formal policy advisory to the Teacher Education Council at least once every
42         two years, drawing from its research institutes. The University may issue advisories
43         more frequently as it deems necessary or upon request of the Teacher Education
44         Council. The Teacher Education Council shall respond in writing within sixty days
45         of receipt of an advisory, indicating which recommendations have been adopted,
46         which are under consideration, and the reasons for any non-adoption. Advisories
47         pertaining to technical-vocational education shall be shared with the Technical
48         Education and Skills Development Authority for its information and appropriate
49         action.
50      c) Education Sector Crisis Response Protocol. — The University, in coordination with
51         the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education, and the
52         Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, shall develop a crisis
53         response protocol that enables the rapid mobilization of the University’s research,
54         training, and technical expertise in response to urgent education challenges,
 1         including but not limited to learning recovery following natural disasters, public
 2         health emergencies, or identified declines in national or international learning
 3         assessments.
 4      d) Longitudinal Impact Assessment. — The University shall establish a comprehensive
 5         Longitudinal Teacher Impact and Tracer System to systematically track the
 6         performance of its graduates in the classroom including, where feasible, the
 7         learning outcomes of the students taught by PNU-trained teachers and, for
 8         technical-vocational graduates, the performance outcomes as may be relevant to
 9         TESDA competency standards. Findings shall be disaggregated by campus,
10         program, and region, and shall be used to inform the continuous improvement of
11         the University’s teacher education programs. A report on these findings shall be
12         submitted to the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education,
13         the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, and Congress at least
14         once every three years. This system shall function as the University’s official tracer
15         study mechanism and feedback loop for continuous program improvement and
16         national policy development.

54   SEC. 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within ninety (90) days after the
 1   effectivity of this Act, the Board, in consultation with the Commission on Higher Education
 2   (CHED), Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and other relevant oversight
 3   agencies, shall formulate the rules and regulations to fully implement the provisions of
 4   this Act. The implementing rules and regulations shall include the following:
 5       a) Provision for the Philippine Normal University (PNU) modernization program,
 6           including its corresponding program budget to CHED and DBM;
 7       b) Provision for additional plantilla positions, including teaching faculty, research
 8           faculty, technical, support-to-technical, and key administrative positions, for the
 9           existing constituent campuses — PNU Manila, PNU South Luzon, PNU North Luzon,
10           PNU Visayas, and PNU Mindanao — together with their respective extension
11           campuses, and the creation of dedicated research faculty items necessary for the
12           effective operation of the University’s research institutes and research hubs, with
13           corresponding augmentation of personnel services allocation from DBM, in order
14           to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of the administration, research, and
15           management of the University, towards the attainment of its mandate as the
16           national teacher education university;
17       c) Provision that the funding requirements for the establishment, operation, and
18           sustainability of the research institutes, research hubs, and related research
19           coordination mechanisms created under this Act shall be explicitly reflected in the
20           annual budget of the Philippine Normal University, subject to existing budgeting,
21           accounting, and auditing rules and regulations; and
22       d) The implementing rules and regulations shall outline the engagement of
23           stakeholders such as CHED, the Department of Education (DepEd), the
24           Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Technical Education and Skills
25           Development Authority (TESDA), and DBM, as well as regional councils, through
26           Technical Working Groups, with PNU advocating for shared governance, grounded
27           legal frameworks, and legislative alignment with existing education laws.
28       e) The establishment of a clear, transparent, and consultative mechanism for the
29           selection of private sector representatives to the Board of Regents, with
30           participation from relevant stakeholders, including students and sectoral
31           representatives.

33   SEC. 41. Independent External Evaluation. — The Philippine Normal University, as the
34   National University for Teacher Education (NUTE), shall undergo an independent external
35   evaluation at least once every five (5) years.
36
37   The evaluation shall assess the University’s performance in fulfilling its national mandate,
38   including teacher education quality, research impact, policy influence, collaboration
39   effectiveness, and contribution to national learning outcomes.
40
41   The evaluation shall be conducted by an independent panel composed of recognized
42   experts in teacher education, educational policy, and institutional governance, which may
43   include international experts to ensure global benchmarking.
44
45   The results of the evaluation shall be submitted to Congress, the Commission on Higher
46   Education (CHED), and the Teacher Education Council (TEC), and shall be made publicly
47   accessible.
48
49   The University shall utilize the findings to inform institutional reforms, strategic planning,
50   and continuous improvement of teacher education programs.
