                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Flooding and soil erosion remain among the most persistent
environmental and disaster risks in the Philippines, resulting in loss of lives,
damage to infrastructure, and disruption of livelihoods. These risks are further
intensified by deforestation, land degradation, and the increasing impacts of
climate change.

      While existing laws such as Republic Act No. 10121 and Republic Act No.
9729 provide comprehensive frameworks for disaster risk reduction and climate
change adaptation, there remains a need to strengthen the integration of
ecosystem-based and nature-based solutions within these systems. Current
approaches to flood control have largely relied on structural or “gray”
infrastructure, which, while necessary, may be complemented by more
sustainable and cost-effective natural interventions.

       This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize Natural Flood
Management (NFM) and erosion control strategies as complementary approaches
to existing flood control systems. It promotes the restoration and protection of
watersheds, wetlands, mangroves, and river systems to naturally regulate water
flow, reduce flood risks, and prevent soil erosion.

      The bill establishes a National Program on Natural Flood Management and
Erosion Control as a convergence mechanism among key government agencies,
without creating new bureaucratic structures. It ensures alignment with existing
national policies and reinforces the role of local government units, consistent
with Republic Act No. 7160, in integrating these strategies into local development
and disaster risk reduction plans.

      By strengthening ecosystem-based approaches, this measure aims to
enhance community resilience, promote environmental sustainability, and
provide long-term, cost-efficient solutions to flooding and land degradation.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                             AN ACT
      PROVIDING FOR NATURAL FLOOD MANAGEMENT AND EROSION
      CONTROL MECHANISMS, STRENGTHENING ECOSYSTEM-BASED
      APPROACHES    TO  DISASTER    REDUCTION, AND   FOR
      OTHER PURPOSES


      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Natural Flood
Management and Erosion Control Act."

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to safeguard lives, property, and the environment by strengthening
ecosystem-based approaches to disaster risk reduction and climate change
adaptation. Toward this end, the State shall integrate natural flood management
and erosion control measures into national and local planning, consistent with
the frameworks established under Republic Act No. 10121 otherwise known as
the “Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010” and
Republic Act No. 9729 or the “Climate Change Act of 2009”.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

   (a) Natural Flood Management (NFM) refers to the use of natural processes,
       land use, and ecosystem restoration to reduce flood risk, including
       reforestation, wetland restoration, riverbank stabilization, and watershed
       management.

   (b) Erosion Control refers to measures that prevent or reduce soil erosion and
       sedimentation through vegetative, structural, and natural engineering
       methods.

   (c) Watershed Management refers to the coordinated conservation and
       sustainable use of soil, water, and vegetation resources within a watershed
       to regulate water flow and reduce disaster risk.
   (d) Ecosystem-based Adaptation refers to the use of biodiversity and
       ecosystem services as part of a broader strategy to adapt to climate change
       impacts.

      SECTION 4. National Program on Natural Flood Management and
Erosion Control. There is hereby established a National Program on Natural
Flood Management and Erosion Control (NP-NFMEC), which shall serve as a
convergence and coordination mechanism to strengthen ecosystem-based
approaches within existing disaster risk reduction and climate change
adaptation frameworks.

       The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) shall act
as the lead coordinating agency, in collaboration with the Department of Public
Works and Highways (DPWH), Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of
the Interior and Local Government (DILG), National Irrigation Administration
(NIA), Climate Change Commission (CCC), and the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC).

The Program shall:

   (a) Identify and prioritize watersheds, river basins, and coastal areas
       vulnerable to flooding and erosion, consistent with national risk
       assessments under existing DRRM and climate frameworks;

   (b) Develop and implement ecosystem-based interventions such as
       reforestation, mangrove rehabilitation, riverbank stabilization, and
       restoration of wetlands and floodplains, in coordination with existing
       DENR and DA programs;

   (c) Promote the adoption of bioengineering and nature-based solutions to
       complement structural flood control measures implemented by DPWH;


   (d) Integrate natural flood management strategies into national infrastructure
       planning, without diminishing the mandate of DPWH over structural flood
       control systems;


   (e) Formulate a National Natural Flood Management Framework, which shall
       guide agencies and local government units (LGUs) in planning and
       implementation subject to coordination with the Climate Change
       Commission and integration into existing national plans; and
   (f) Strengthen community participation and capacity-building initiatives
       consistent with community-based disaster risk reduction approaches
       under existing laws.

      SECTION 5. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). Consistent with
the autonomy granted under Republic Act No. 7160, LGUs shall:
   (a) Integrate natural flood management and erosion control strategies into
       their Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), Local Climate Change
       Action Plans (LCCAPs), and Local Disaster Risk Reduction and
       Management Plans (LDRRMPs), in accordance with national guidelines
       and consistent with existing land use and development planning
       standards;

   (b) Implement community-based monitoring and maintenance of watershed,
       river, and coastal ecosystems, consistent with their existing environmental
       and Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM) functions;

   (c) Allocate resources for NFM and erosion control initiatives from available
       local funds, including but not limited to their Local Disaster Risk
       Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF), subject to existing
       budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules; and

   (d) Coordinate with national government agencies for technical assistance,
       capacity-building, and program alignment.

       SECTION 6. Incentives for Natural Flood Management. To encourage
participation in natural flood management initiatives:

      (a) Farmers, fisherfolk, and cooperatives engaged in agroforestry,
          riverbank stabilization, and erosion control activities may access
          existing crop insurance and support programs of the Philippine Crop
          Insurance Corporation (PCIC) and the Department of Agriculture;

      (b) Private sector entities participating in NFM-related projects may qualify
          for incentives under Republic Act No. 10771, subject to existing laws,
          rules, and regulations;

      (c) LGUs demonstrating exemplary implementation of NFM initiatives may
          be prioritized for recognition and support under existing performance-
          based incentive systems, including the Seal of Good Local Governance.

       SECTION 7. Funding. The initial funding for the implementation of this
Act shall be sourced from the existing appropriations of the DENR, DPWH, DA,
and other participating agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       LGUs may access financing support from existing sources, including the
People’s Survival Fund under Republic Act No. 10174, the Local Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Fund, and other climate financing mechanisms. The
concerned agencies shall incorporate the Program into their respective annual
and multi-year investment plans.

      SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DENR, as lead agency, in coordination
with DPWH, DA, DILG, CCC, NDRRMC, and other concerned agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act.

      SECTION 9. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. The DENR, in
coordination with concerned agencies, shall establish a monitoring and
evaluation system for the Program. An annual report on the implementation of
this Act, including performance indicators and outcomes, shall be submitted to
Congress.

       SECTION 10. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 12. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved.
