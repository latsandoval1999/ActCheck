                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

          Access to basic healthcare remains a significant challenge for Filipinos
   living in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) and other
   underserved communities, where distance, inadequate health facilities, and
   shortages of healthcare personnel can prevent timely access to essential
   medical services.

           This measure seeks to establish a Mobile Rural Health Clinic Program
   under the Department of Health (DOH) to provide regular, accessible, and
   community-based healthcare services in areas where permanent health
   facilities are limited or difficult to reach. The Program shall provide primary
   and preventive care, diagnostics, medicines, maternal and child health
   services, mental health services, emergency care, health education, and
   referral assistance, as appropriate to the needs of the communities served.

         The measure likewise strengthens coordination among the DOH, local
   government units, PhilHealth, and other concerned agencies to ensure
   continuity of care and integration with existing health systems, while
   providing appropriate standards for mobile clinic operations, personnel,
   equipment, and digital health systems.

          By bringing essential healthcare services closer to underserved
   Filipinos, this measure seeks to advance equitable access to healthcare and
   strengthen the delivery of primary health services in communities that need
   them most.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
   earnestly sought.

                              “AN ACT
         MANDATING THE DEPLOYMENT OF MOBILE HEALTH CLINICS
         TO GEOGRAPHICALLY ISOLATED AND DISADVANTAGED
         AREAS (GIDAs) AND OTHER UNDERSERVED COMMUNITIES,
         STRENGTHENING THE CAPACITY OF THE DEPARTMENT OF
         HEALTH TO PROVIDE REGULAR MOBILE HEALTH SERVICES,
         APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
         PURPOSES”

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
   in Congress assembled:

          SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Mobile Health
   Clinic Program Act.”

         SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of
   the State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and ensure
   equitable, accessible, affordable, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos,
   particularly those residing in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas
   (GIDAs) and underserved communities.

          Toward this end, the State shall strengthen community-based and primary
   healthcare systems through the establishment of mobile health services capable
   of delivering preventive, promotive, curative, diagnostic, emergency, and referral
   healthcare services, consistent with Republic Act No. 11223, or the “Universal
   Health Care Act,” and other existing public health laws, policies, and programs.

        SECTION 3. Creation of the Mobile Rural Clinic Program. - There is
   hereby created a “Mobile Health Clinic Program” under the Department of Health
   (DOH) to deploy mobile health clinics and medical outreach units to underserved
   communities, particularly Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas
   (GIDAs).

         The Program shall serve as a supplemental and community-based
   healthcare delivery mechanism intended to bridge healthcare service gaps in
   areas with limited or inadequate access to permanent healthcare facilities.

       The Program shall complement, and shall not diminish or replace, existing
public health facilities, programs, and services of the national government and
local government units (LGUs).

     For purposes of this Act, “GIDAs” shall refer to areas classified as such
under existing DOH policies, guidelines, and criteria.

      SECTION 4. Objectives. - The Program shall pursue the following
objectives:

      a) Ensure equitable access to primary healthcare, emergency services,
         and preventive and promotive health interventions in underserved
         communities;

      b) Provide regular and appropriate medical, dental, maternal and child
         health, nutrition, immunization, mental health, and preventive care
         services;

      c) Strengthen healthcare service delivery in rural and underserved
         communities through mobile diagnostic, laboratory, pharmacy, and
         telemedicine services;

      d) Support early disease detection, referral systems, and continuity of care
         for vulnerable populations;

      e) Enhance disaster preparedness and emergency medical response
         capacity in remote and disaster-prone communities; and

      f) Promote health education, sanitation, disease          prevention, and
         community health awareness.

      SECTION 5. Coverage and Priority Areas. - Priority deployment under
the Program shall be given to:

      a) Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs);

      b) Indigenous Peoples (IP) communities;

      c) Disaster-affected,   conflict-affected,   and   medically   underserved
         communities; and

      d) Areas identified by the DOH and local government units as having
         limited healthcare access or shortages of healthcare personnel or
         facilities.

     The DOH shall periodically review and update deployment priorities based
on public health indicators, accessibility, healthcare service gaps, population
needs, and other relevant factors.

                                                                             3
      SECTION 6. Composition and Operation. - The Program shall be
implemented by the DOH in coordination with concerned national government
agencies, local government units, and public and private healthcare institutions,
including, but not limited to:

      a) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

      b) Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth);

      c) Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD);

      d) Local Government Units (LGUs);

      e) Provincial, City, and Municipal Health Offices; and

      f) Government hospitals and accredited healthcare institutions.

      The participation of government personnel and healthcare workers in the
Program shall be subject to existing laws, civil service rules, staffing
requirements, compensation policies, professional regulations, and other
applicable rules and regulations.

      Each mobile clinic shall meet such minimum equipment, facility, safety,
and operational standards as may be prescribed by the DOH, taking into account
the healthcare needs and conditions of the areas to be served, and may include:

      1.     Primary care and emergency medical equipment;

      2.     Basic laboratory and diagnostic facilities;

      3.     Essential medicines, vaccines, and medical supplies;

      4.     Telemedicine and digital health systems;

      5.     Electronic medical record capabilities; and

      6.   Appropriate     transportation,    communication,   and    emergency
response equipment.

      SECTION 7. Powers and Functions of the Department of Health.- The
DOH shall:

      a) Procure, maintain, operate, or otherwise provide for the deployment
         and operation of mobile health clinics and related equipment, subject
         to existing procurement, budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws,
         rules, and regulations;

      b) Deploy interdisciplinary healthcare teams composed of physicians,
         nurses, midwives, dentists, medical technologists, nutritionists, mental
                                                                            4
         health personnel, and other healthcare workers as necessary, subject
         to existing laws, staffing requirements, civil service rules, and
         applicable professional regulations;

      c) Develop standards, operational protocols, deployment schedules, and
         service guidelines for the Program;

      d) Ensure integration of the Program with existing primary healthcare
         systems, referral networks, applicable PhilHealth benefits, and
         Universal Health Care mechanisms;

      e) Establish and maintain interoperable digital health information
         systems consistent with Republic Act No. 10173, or the “Data Privacy
         Act of 2012,” Republic Act No. 11223, or the “Universal Health Care
         Act,” and other applicable laws, rules, and regulations;

      f) Coordinate with LGUs and local health boards in identifying priority
         areas and ensuring continuity of care for patients requiring further
         treatment;

      g) Provide training and capacity-building programs for healthcare workers
         involved in the Program;

      h) Conduct public health education and awareness campaigns in
         beneficiary communities; and

      i) Maintain appropriate monitoring and reporting systems for the
         implementation of the Program and provide such reports as may be
         required under this Act.

      SECTION 8. Responsibilities of Local Government Units. - LGUs,
consistent with their respective powers, functions, and responsibilities under
existing laws, shall:

   a) Assist in identifying priority communities and target beneficiaries;
   b) Provide logistical, transportation, security, and operational support when
      appropriate and subject to available resources;
   c) Coordinate community mobilization, scheduling, and follow-up care
      services;
   d) Facilitate patient referrals and integration with local healthcare systems;
   e) Support health promotion, sanitation, and community awareness
      activities; and
   f) Maintain coordination with barangay health workers and local health
      boards.

       LGUs may provide counterpart funding, facilities, personnel, equipment,
or logistical assistance for the implementation of the Program, subject to existing
laws, applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules, and the availability
of funds and other resources.
                                                                              5
       SECTION 9. Referral System and Continuity of Care. - Patients requiring
specialized treatment, hospitalization, long-term management, or emergency
intervention shall be referred to appropriate healthcare facilities through existing
referral systems.

       The DOH shall coordinate with PhilHealth and other concerned agencies
to facilitate the access of qualified patients to applicable healthcare financing
mechanisms and benefit packages under Republic Act No. 11223, or the
“Universal Health Care Act,” and other applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

      SECTION   10. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual appropriations of the
DOH under the General Appropriations Act.

      The Program may likewise be supported by PhilHealth reimbursements
and other lawful funding sources, including grants, donations, and development
assistance from domestic or foreign sources, subject to existing laws, rules, and
regulations.

      Funds appropriated for other programs shall not be realigned or utilized
for the Program except in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting,
auditing, and appropriations laws, rules, and regulations.

      SECTION 11. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Transparency. - The DOH
shall establish and maintain a monitoring and evaluation framework for the
Program and shall submit an annual report to Congress and the Office of the
President containing:

      a) Number of communities and individuals served;
      b) Frequency and geographic coverage of deployments;
      c) Health services delivered and relevant health outcomes, subject to
         applicable data privacy laws;
      d) Budget utilization and operational status of mobile clinics; and
      e) Challenges, recommendations, and impact assessments.

       Periodic independent evaluations may be conducted to assess the
effectiveness, efficiency, and sustainability of the Program.

      SECTION 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with concerned
                                                                          6
national government agencies, LGUs, healthcare institutions, and other relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

       Such rules and regulations shall include standards for clinic operation
and equipment, personnel deployment, service frequency, beneficiary
prioritization, referral mechanisms, data management, monitoring and
evaluation, and coordination with LGUs.

      SECTION 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved.
