                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

          Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) serve as the backbone of community-based
health care in the Philippines. As frontline health volunteers, they assist in delivering essential
primary health care services, promote health awareness, monitor community health
conditions, and support government health programs at the grassroots level.

         According to data from the Department of Health, there are approximately
270,000 Barangay Health Workers nationwide, deployed across thousands of
barangays and serving as the most accessible health workers for many Filipino
families. In many communities, especially in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas, BHWs are often the first, and sometimes the only, health
personnel available to assist residents.

          Despite their indispensable role, most BHWs continue to serve primarily as
volunteers and receive minimal or irregular honoraria, limited benefits, and
insufficient institutional support. Existing legislation, particularly Republic Act No.
7883, recognizes their contributions but has proven inadequate in addressing the
evolving needs of the health sector and the demands placed upon BHWs. The
implementation of Republic Act No. 11223 further highlights the need to strengthen
the country’s primary health care workforce. A responsive and effective health care
system requires well-trained, adequately supported, and properly compensated
community health workers who serve as the bridge between health facilities and local
communities.

          This proposed Magna Carta of Barangay Health Workers seeks to
institutionalize stronger support and protection for BHWs by providing a
comprehensive framework for their registration, certification, training, and
professional development. The measure also provides for the creation of plantilla
positions in local government units, the establishment of minimum salary grades, and


                                                                                                 1
the granting of appropriate incentives and benefits, including honoraria, allowances,
insurance coverage, and career advancement opportunities.

        Moreover, the bill establishes a National Barangay Health Worker
Information System to strengthen policy planning and program implementation,
while also providing mechanisms to ensure security of tenure, protection from
discrimination, and representation in local health governance.

         By strengthening and professionalizing the Barangay Health Worker sector,
the State affirms its commitment to improving primary health care and achieving
universal health care for all Filipinos.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the “Magna
 2   Carta of Barangay Health Workers Act”.

 3           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is declared the policy of the State to protect
 4   and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
 5   them.
 6           The State recognizes the importance of primary healthcare in attaining
 7   universal, with the concerted efforts of the local government units, the communities,
 8   and volunteers.
 9           The State likewise recognizes the vital role of Barangay Health Workers
10   (BHWs), who are volunteers, and the need to provide them with security of tenure,
11   adequate benefits and just compensation, consistent with the principle of equal pay
12   for equal work and work of equal value.
13           SEC. 3. Definition. - The term “Barangay Health Worker” refers to a person
14   who has undergone training programs under any accredited government and non-
15   government organization and who renders primary health care services in the
16   community after having been accredited to function as such by the local health board

 1   in accordance with the guidelines promulgated by the Department of Health (DOH)
 2   and appointed by the city or municipal mayor, as the case may be.
 3          SEC. 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all BHWs, whether as volunteers or
 4   employees of the government, and all LGUs and national government agencies that
 5   mobilize BHWs to expand the delivery of primary health care services to
 6   communities, families, and individuals.

11          SEC. 5. Roles and Responsibilities of Barangay Health Workers. - BHWs shall
12   serve as frontliners in supporting the delivery of primary health care in their
13   respective community. They shall form part of the community health teams of the
14   barangay health station, health center, or any other primary health care facility of the
15   municipal/city health office established to provide primary health care services to a
16   barangay or a part of it, or a cluster of barangays.
17          For this purpose, BHWs shall perform the following roles:
18          1) Assist the health facilities in providing primary health care services to the
19             members of the community;
20          2) Provide knowledge and skills to community members in the prevention
21             and management of simple illnesses;
22          3) Organize and mobilize the community towards self-reliance; and
23          4) Assist in the development and maintenance of a community health profile,
24             and collect health data and information of families and individuals in the
25             community.
26          The head of the barangay health station, health center, or any other primary
27   health care facility of the municipal/city health office shall exercise day-to-day direct
28   control and supervision over the BHWs.
29          SEC. 6. Number of Barangay Health Workers. - The Department of Health
30   (DOH) shall issue the guidelines as to the ideal number of Barangay Health Worker-

 8          SEC. 7. Registration. - In addition to qualifications to be set by the
 9   municipal/city health board, an applicant must meet the following requirements to
10   qualify as a BHW:
11          1) At least eighteen (18) years of age at the date of the filing the application for
12             registration;
13          2) Physically and mentally fit; and
14          3) With basic BHW orientation and training as prescribed by the DOH,
15             completed in the same year of application for registration.
16          Eligible applicants shall register with the local health board in the municipality
17   or city where they reside and intend to render service and shall be given appropriate
18   proof of said registration.
19          It shall be the duty of the municipal/city health offices, in cooperation with the
20   provincial health offices and the DOH, to provide interested BHW applicants with
21   basic orientation and training.
22          SEC. 9. National BHW Information System. - A National BHW Information
23   System shall (NBIS) be established and maintained by the DOH, in partnership with
24   the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), to assist the
25   national government and LGUs in formulating and implementing policies and
26   programs for the organization and development of BHWs.
27          The NBIS m shall be the sole source of real-time data and information on BHWs
28   nationwide. It shall contain the list of all BHWs at the barangay, municipal, city, and
29   provincial levels, their socio-demographic profiles, qualifications, areas of
30   assignment, and other information that the DOH may deem necessary.

 7         SEC. 10. Certification of BHWs. - To ensure the health and welfare of the

16         1) Completed at least two (2) years of continuous service in the locality;
17         2) Completed the training on the roles, duties, and responsibilities of a BHW,
18         3) Provided satisfactory performance as evaluated by the health care
19             professional supervising the concerned BHW immediately before the filing
20             of the application for certification; and
21         4) Completed the courses of the certification component of the Education and
22             Training Program, provided in Section 11 of this Act.

27         It shall be the duty of the municipal/city governments, in cooperation with the
28   provincial government and the DOH, to provide the applicant BHWs the necessary
29   support and opportunities for certification.

17          SEC. 15. Qualifications for Appointment. - To be eligible for appointment to a

27   shall be required for appointment to Salary Grade 1 positions under this Act. The CSC

29          SEC. 16. Security of Tenure. - BHWs appointed to plantilla positions under this

12                                        CHAPTER III
13                              INCENTIVES AND BENEFITS
14          SEC. 18. Monthly Honoraria for Non-Plantilla BHWs. - Pending the creation
15   of plantilla positions for BHWs, those who are actively and regularly performing their
16   duties shall be entitled to monthly honoraria in the amount of not less than Three
17   Thousand Pesos (Php 3,000.00) for registered BHWs and not less than Five Thousand
18   Pesos (Php 5,000.00) for certified BHWs, subject to adjustment based on the prevailing
19   market value: Provided, That no diminution of monthly honoraria can be made on
20   BHWs already receiving more than the herein prescribed minimum amounts:
21   Provided, further, That the DOH, in coordination with the DILG and the DBM, shall
22   review and adjust the minimum honoraria every three (3) years.
23          SEC. 19. Transportation Allowance/Expense Reimbursements. - Registered and
24   certified BHWs shall be provided by the barangay with transportation allowance or
25   to reimbursement of actual transportation expense for the performance of their
26   functions, as may be authorized by law or regulation: Provided, That the barangay
27   may, instead, provide applicable forms of transportation to enable the BHWs to
28   provide the needed primary health care service: Provided, further. That the
29   municipality or city, through their health offices, may shoulder such allowance or
30   expense: Provided, finally. That official travel, such as seminars, conferences, and
31   transport of patients outside of the barangay or locality, shall be shouldered by the


                                                                                             9
 1   barangay, municipality, city, or province that authorized such official travel and shall
 2   not be deducted from the honoraria of the BHWs.
 3          SEC. 20. Subsistence Allowance. - The concerned municipality or city shall
 4   provide BHWs who render service in unserved or underserved barangays where they
 5   are not residents to make their services available at any and all times, with subsistence
 6   allowance equivalent to the meals taken during their duty, which shall be computed
 7   following prevailing circumstances. In no case shall such subsistence allowance be less
 8   than One hundred pesos (P 100.00) per day.
 9          SEC. 21. Hazard Allowance. - BHWs who, in the exercise of their duties, are
10   exposed to situations, conditions, or factors in the work environment or place where
11   foreseeable but unavoidable danger or risks to their health, life, or on their person
12   exist, including assignment to communities in or recovering from a situation of armed
13   conflict, as validated by proper authorities, shall be paid a hazard allowance by the
14   concerned municipality or city in an amount to be determined by its local health board
15   and the local peace and order council, which shall in no case be less than One thousand
16   pesos (P 1,000.00) per month, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations;
17          SEC. 22. Insurance Coverage. - Insurance coverage and benefit packages from
18   the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), suited to their needs and unique
19   circumstances, shall be borne by the concerned municipality or city. For this purpose,
20   the GSIS shall design such appropriate insurance packages;
21          SEC. 23. Health Emergency Allowance During Public Health Emergency. -
22   Volunteer BHWs who are part of the National BHW Information System and are
23   assigned to health facilities and barangay health emergency response teams during
24   public health emergencies shall be entitled to the Health Emergency Allowance (HEA)
25   and other benefits prescribed in Republic Act No. 11712. The national government
26   shall provide funds for the payment of the HEA and other benefits.
27          SEC. 24. Cash Gift. - The BHWs shall be entitled to a cash gift not less than the
28   minimum monthly honoraria, to be given every December.
29          SEC. 25. One-time Dedicated Service Recognition Incentive. - A BHW who
30   withdraws as a registered or certified BHW but has continuously and satisfactorily
31   served for at least fifteen (15) years shall, in recognition of his/her loyalty, dedication,


                                                                                              10
 1   and service, be entitled to a one-time incentive of not less than Ten thousand pesos
 2   (P10,000.00), which shall be borne by the municipality or city concerned. The national
 3   and provincial governments shall assist in case of non-availability of funds from the
 4   concerned municipality or component city.
 5         SEC. 26. Education and Career Advancement - The DOH, in coordination with
 6   the Department of Education, CHED, TESDA, CSC, other concerned national
 7   government agencies, non-government organizations, and LCDs, shall provide
 8   information on, and opportunities for, education and career advancement for BHWs,

10              1) Educational programs that credit the years of primary health care
11                 service of the BHW and the number of training hours gained during

14                 institutions with step-ladder curricula, as provided under Republic Act
15                 No. 10968, otherwise known as the "PQF Act", and Republic Act No.

20                 scholarship and financial grants, among others;
21              3) Scholarship grant or financial assistance to one (1) child of a BHW who

24              4) Special training programs, such as traditional medicine, disaster
25                 preparedness, and other programs that address emergent community
26                 health problems and issues.

28   shall be immediately provided by the Public Attorney's Office to a BHW in cases of

 4                                        CHAPTER IV
 5                    PROHIBITIONS, PROTECTIONS, AND RIGHTS
 6          SEC. 31. Discrimination Prohibited. - Discrimination against any BHW by
 7   reason of gender, civil status, creed, religious or political beliefs, and ethnic groupings
 8   in the exercise of their functions and responsibilities is hereby prohibited.
 9          SEC. 32. Protection of BHWs. - The municipal, city, provincial governments
10   and the DILG shall establish a grievance mechanism as a means of processing
11   complaints of BHWs against alleged acts of discrimination and unjust removal from
12   the service. A BHW may appeal their grievances to the DILG.
13          SEC. 33. Right to Seif-organization. - A BHW shall have the right to freely
14   form, join or assist organizations to obtain redress of their grievances through peaceful
15   concerted activities, in a manner not contrary to law, and with utmost regard to service
16   to patients and the continuous operation of barangay health services in the interest of
17   public health, safety, or survival of patients.
18          SEC. 34. Representation in the Local Health Board and Primary Health Care
19   15 Provider Network. - The president of the municipal or city association of BHWs
20   shall be a member of the municipal or city local health board. The presidents of the
21   association of BHWs of each component city and municipality shall elect from among
22   themselves their representative to the provincial health board: Provided, That, the
23   BHW representatives to the health boards shall not be allowed to vote on the
24   registration, accreditation and disciplinary or removal complaints of BHWs.
25          The BHWs shall also form part of the healthcare provider network and shall
26   participate in the implementation of healthcare services and programs.
27          SEC. 35. Continuous Capacity Building for BHWs. - The DOH shall conduct
28   continuous capacity building for BHWs to enhance and upgrade their knowledge and
29   skills, including training programs conducted online and in major Philippine dialects.
30   The DOH shall, in coordination with TESDA and other concerned agencies shall also
31   assist the LGUs in the development of education modules or materials that highlight


                                                                                             13
 1   the collective experiences and learnings of BHWs and the use of traditional and
 2   complementary medicine.
 3          The LGUs shall endeavor to establish their own training centers for their BHWs
 4   and other health workers in coordination with TESDA, NGOs, and other agencies
 5   concerned.
 6          SEC. 36. Prohibition on Participation in Political Activities. - To maintain
 7   their integrity and impartiality, and eliminate the possibility of conflicts with their
 8   catchment households, BHWs are prohibited from participating in partisan political
 9   activities. Any BHW who files an application as a candidate in any political position
10   is hereby deemed removed from the list of registered BHWs or resigned from
11   government service, as the case may be. Participation in political activities shall serve
12   as basis for the local health board to remove BHWs from the list of registered BHWs.

26          The review shall include, but not be limited to:
27          1) The adequacy of the salary grade and benefits of BHWs;
28          2) The effectiveness of the plantilla integration process;
29          3) Equity in allocation of national subsidies; and
30          4) The overall impact of the Act on community health outcomes.
