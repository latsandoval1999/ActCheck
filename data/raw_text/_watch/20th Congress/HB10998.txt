                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE
      Article XVI, Section 7 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State
“shall provide immediate and adequate care, benefits, and other forms of
assistance to war veterans and veterans of military campaigns, their surviving
spouses and orphans,” and directs that “[f]unds shall be provided therefor.”1
That obligation does not end at the veteran's death. A dignified place of
interment is the last benefit the Republic can confer on those who bore its
arms, and at present the Republic confers it unevenly.
       The Libingan ng mga Bayani in Taguig City remains the principal
national resting place for Filipino military veterans. It is also, for most of them,
unreachable. Republic Act No. 6948, as amended, furnishes a burial flag and
burial assistance now fixed at Twenty Thousand Pesos (PhP 20,000.00).2 That
sum defrays funeral expenses. It does not purchase a burial lot, which in most
provinces now costs several times that amount, and it does not secure
perpetual care. The result is a benefit structure that pays for the rites but not
for the ground.
       This bill takes the other road. Rather than build, the State buys,
acquiring through competitive public procurement and in consolidated volume
perpetual interment rights in memorial parks that already exist, that are
already developed, and that are already subject to regulation and contractual
perpetual care obligations. The economic case is not that the arrangement is
free. It is that a single, fixed, present payment extinguishes an open-ended
future liability: the cost of upkeep is prepaid into a perpetual care fund held in
escrow, the land is never bought, no civil works are undertaken, and a plot
becomes available to a grieving family in days rather than in budget cycles. The
proponent reports a developed lot in Nueva Vizcaya, inclusive of perpetual care,

       Three safeguards distinguish this measure from a simple purchase order,
and the Committee's attention is respectfully invited to them. First, a memorial
lot in this jurisdiction is ordinarily conveyed not as titled land but as a right of
interment under contract.5 A statute that assumed the Republic would receive
a certificate of title for each plot would fail in the registry. The bill therefore
secures the State's interest as a perpetual, irrevocable, and registered
encumbrance annotated on the certificate of title of the memorial park itself, so
that the veteran's resting place survives the sale, foreclosure, or insolvency of
the developer. Second, perpetual care funds must be held in escrow with a
government financial institution and pass automatically to a successor
administrator upon default, rather than remaining an unsecured promise.
Third, every plot is inalienable, exempt from execution, and traceable in a
national registry, and its commercial resale is penalized.
     In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL VETERANS BURIAL ASSURANCE PROGRAM
 THROUGH THE ACQUISITION OF PERPETUAL INTERMENT RIGHTS IN
   ACCREDITED MEMORIAL PARKS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
 REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6948, AS AMENDED, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                           THEREFOR
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SEC. 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Veterans Burial
Dignity Act.”
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to provide
immediate and adequate care, benefits, and other forms of assistance to war
veterans and veterans of military campaigns, their surviving spouses and
orphans. The State recognizes that a dignified and accessible place of
interment, with assured perpetual care and full military honors, is an integral
part of that assistance, and that this benefit shall not be defeated by the
distance between a veteran's home and the national military cemetery.
      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
      (a) Veteran refers to a person qualified as such under Republic Act No.
           6948, as amended, and registered with the Philippine Veterans
           Affairs Office (PVAO);
      (b) Interment Right refers to the perpetual and irrevocable right,
           acquired by the Republic under this Act, to inter human remains
           in a specified and physically delineated plot, to erect a marker
           thereon, and to have the same visited by the public; and
      (c) Accredited Memorial Park refers to a privately owned memorial park
           or cemetery accredited for participation in the Program by the
           Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
           (DHSUD).​
       SEC. 4. The Program; Coverage. — There is hereby established, as a
mandatory welfare operation of the PVAO, a National Veterans Burial
Assurance Program, under which the Republic shall acquire and hold in
advance of need, and allocate without cost upon death, perpetual Interment
Rights in Accredited Memorial Parks distributed among all regions of the
country. The benefit shall be available to every Veteran and to the surviving
legal spouse of a Veteran, who may be interred in the same or an adjoining
plot. Not more than one (1) plot shall be allocated for each Veteran, exclusive
of the spousal accommodation herein provided.​
​
​     SEC. 5. Nature of the Interest Acquired; Registration. — The
Republic shall acquire under this Act a statutory perpetual interment right,
which is hereby declared a real right constituting a registrable encumbrance
upon the land, enforceable against the owner of the Accredited Memorial Park
and its successors and assigns, and not merely a contractual privilege. Every
Interment Right shall be registered and annotated on the certificate of title
covering the Accredited Memorial Park, and shall for that purpose be deemed
a registrable interest under Presidential Decree No. 1529, otherwise known as
the Property Registration Decree. ​
​
​      The annotation shall expressly provide that the Interment Right runs
with the land and subsists notwithstanding the sale, assignment, mortgage,
foreclosure, dissolution, rehabilitation, or insolvency of the owner or operator.
The Land Registration Authority shall prescribe the form of the annotation
and shall direct the Registries of Deeds to give it due course. Where the laws
on land registration permit the issuance of a separate certificate of title over a
plot, the PVAO shall cause such title to be issued in the name of the Republic
of the Philippines. No disbursement shall be made for any plot until the
annotation required herein has been effected and verified.​
​
​     SEC. 6. Acquisition; Accreditation. — The PVAO shall be the
procuring entity and shall acquire Interment Rights through its Bids and
Awards Committee, in accordance with Republic Act No. 12009, otherwise
known as the New Government Procurement Act, and consolidated by region
or by such other grouping as will secure the greatest volume advantage.
Interment Rights shall be acquired only from Accredited Memorial Parks. ​
​
​      The DHSUD shall accredit and periodically review the memorial parks
eligible to participate, applying its rules governing memorial parks and
cemeteries and, where applicable, Presidential Decree No. 957. Revocation of
accreditation shall in no case impair or extinguish an Interment Right already
acquired by the Republic.​
​
​     SEC. 7. Perpetual Care Fund. — The perpetual care fund
corresponding to plots acquired under this Act shall be maintained in escrow
with a government financial institution, separate from the operating funds of
the memorial park operator and beyond the reach of its creditors. Upon the
default, abandonment, dissolution, or insolvency of the operator,
administration of the fund and responsibility for maintenance shall pass to a
successor administrator designated by the PVAO, which may be another
accredited operator, a court-appointed administrator, or, with its written
consent, the local government unit concerned.​
​
​      SEC. 8. Inalienability; Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — Interment
Rights acquired under this Act are property of the Republic. They shall be
inalienable and non-transferable, exempt from levy, execution, attachment,
and encumbrance by private persons, and, except for allocation to a qualified
beneficiary under Section 10, shall not be sold, leased, assigned, or otherwise
disposed of. A plot that is unused, declined, or revoked shall revert to the
available inventory. The sale or offer for sale of a plot acquired under this Act,
the falsification of an allocation document issued hereunder, and the
interment of a person known to be unqualified, shall be punishable by prisión
correccional and a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP
100,000.00) nor more than One Million Pesos (PhP 1,000,000.00), without
prejudice to liability under the Revised Penal Code and other laws.​
​
​     SEC. 9. Distribution Among Provinces. — Plots shall be distributed
among the regions and provinces on the basis of the registered veteran
population, the distance to the nearest national or public veterans cemetery,
and the absence of an existing public burial facility affording military honors.
Provinces farthest from such a cemetery and with the largest unserved
veteran populations shall be given priority in the initial phase of acquisition.​
​
​     SEC. 10. Allocation Upon Death; Markers and Honors. — Upon the
death of a Veteran, or upon the subsequent death of the qualified surviving
spouse, the next of kin may apply for an allocation with the PVAO or with the
accredited veterans district or post having jurisdiction. Upon verification of
the veteran's registration and of the applicant's relationship, a plot shall be
assigned and turned over with utmost dispatch, and in no case later than
three (3) working days from completion of the requirements, without any cost
to the family for the plot, its preparation, or the interment. Where the spouse
predeceases the Veteran, the plot allocated for the spouse shall be held for the
Veteran and shall revert to inventory if the Veteran is interred elsewhere.
Each plot shall be provided with a standardized marker at the expense of the
Program. ​
​
​     The Armed Forces of the Philippines shall render the appropriate
military funeral honors to the deceased Veteran in accordance with applicable
law and its regulations. Local government units are hereby authorized to
waive the fees for transfer and burial permits issued in connection with
interments under this Act.​
​
​      SEC. 11. Right of Election. — Participation in the Program is
voluntary. Nothing in this Act shall impair, condition, or diminish any
existing eligibility or right, under applicable law or regulations, to interment
at the Libingan ng mga Bayani, nor the right of a Veteran or of the next of kin
to elect interment in a family or private burial place or in any other place
authorized by law. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to confer, enlarge,
or restrict eligibility for interment at the Libingan ng mga Bayani. A Veteran
may, during his or her lifetime, record an election under this Act, which the
PVAO shall honor unless revoked in writing.​
​       ​
​     SEC. 12. Relation to Existing Benefits; Amendment of Republic Act
No. 6948. — The benefit granted under this Act is in addition to, and shall
not be deducted from or be a ground for the denial of, the burial flag under
Section 19 and the burial assistance under Section 20 of Republic Act No.
6948, as amended, or any other benefit to which a Veteran or the next of kin
is entitled. A new section is hereby inserted in Republic Act No. 6948, as
amended, to read as follows:​
        “SEC. 20-A. Interment Benefit. — In addition to the burial flag
        under Section 19 and the burial assistance under Section 20
        hereof, a veteran and the qualified surviving spouse of a veteran
        shall be entitled, upon death, to the allocation without cost of a
        perpetual interment right acquired by the Republic under the
        Veterans Burial Dignity Act, in accordance with that Act and the
        rules issued thereunder.”
        ​
​     SEC. 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against such appropriations of
the PVAO for the current year as are specifically authorized for burial benefits
or for veterans memorial and burial services, subject to existing budgeting,
accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations. Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to authorize the use or transfer of appropriations for any
other purpose, program, activity, or project. ​
​
    ​SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the PVAO, in consultation with the
Department of National Defense, the DHSUD, the Department of Budget and
Management, the Armed Forces of the Philippines, the Land Registration
Authority, and duly accredited veterans organizations, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary to implement this Act.​
​
 ​    SEC. 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.​
​
 ​    SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.​
​
​     SEC. 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.​
