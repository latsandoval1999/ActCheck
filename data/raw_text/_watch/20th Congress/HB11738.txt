                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


         In his Fifth State of the Nation Address, the President called for urgent action to lower
electricity costs, spotlighting hidden charges that weigh heavily on consumers, such as system loss
fees. He emphasized that Filipino families should not be forced to pay for power lost in the distribution
system, which is entirely out of their hands. This proposed measure answers that call, ensuring that
residential households and micro enterprises are no longer billed for electricity losses or related costs,
including VAT, on power they never actually used.
         Electricity powers every aspect of Filipino life, from lighting homes to fueling dreams of a
better future. Reliable and affordable electricity is the backbone of education, healthcare, work, and
daily comfort. It is equally essential to the survival and growth of micro enterprises, including
neighborhood sari-sari stores, small eateries, repair shops, market vendors, home-based businesses,
and other small livelihood ventures that serve as the lifeblood of the Philippine economy by generating
employment, supporting local communities, and driving grassroots economic activity. Yet, as
electricity costs keep climbing, millions of families and small business owners are forced to stretch
their budgets even thinner, choosing between keeping the lights on and meeting other basic needs or
sustaining their livelihoods.
         Under the present implementation of Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the “Electric
Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA),” allowable system losses may be recovered by
distribution utilities through electricity rates approved by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).
As a result, residential consumers are required to pay system loss charges included in their monthly
electricity bills even though a portion of the electricity accounted for by these charges was not actually
delivered to their homes and was not consumed by them. Likewise, many micro enterprises bear the
same burden, increasing their operating costs and reducing resources that could otherwise be used for
business expansion, job creation, and improved services.
          System loss refers to electricity that is generated, purchased, or transmitted but is not ultimately
delivered to end-users due to technical and non-technical factors within the electricity distribution
system. Technical losses arise from electricity dissipated through distribution lines, transformers, and
other electrical equipment. In contrast, non-technical losses may result from electricity pilferage,
illegal connections, meter-related irregularities, and other system inefficiencies. These losses are
primarily associated with the operation, management, and maintenance responsibilities of distribution
utilities and electric cooperatives.
       Based on prevailing system loss levels applied in electricity distribution, a system loss rate of
approximately five percent (5%) to eight percent (8%) means that for every one hundred (100)
kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity supplied into the distribution system, approximately five (5) to


                                                                                                            1
eight (8) kWh of electricity is lost before reaching consumers. Using an illustrative average electricity
cost of Twelve Pesos (₱12.00) per kWh, an eight percent (8%) system loss may represent
approximately eight (8) kWh of electricity not delivered for every one hundred (100) kWh supplied,
equivalent to approximately Ninety-Six Pesos (₱96.00) in electricity value.
        For a typical household using around two hundred kilowatt-hours each month, an eight percent
(8%) system loss means paying for sixteen kilowatt-hours of electricity that never arrives, costing
about One Hundred Ninety-Two Pesos (₱192.00) monthly or over Two Thousand Three Hundred
Pesos (₱2,300.00) each year. For a micro enterprise operating on thin profit margins, these unnecessary
charges likewise accumulate into significant operating expenses that could otherwise be invested in
inventory, equipment, employee wages, or business growth.
        Every peso spent on system loss charges is a peso taken away from food on the table, school
supplies, medicine, or daily transportation for Filipino families. For micro enterprises, every
unnecessary peso paid for electricity never received is a peso that could have supported business
operations, expanded services, created jobs, or strengthened local economic activity. With prices rising
and budgets stretched to the limit, asking consumers and small entrepreneurs to pay for electricity they
never received only adds to the unfair financial pressure they already face.
        Charging residential consumers and micro enterprises for system losses shifts the cost of power
grid inefficiencies onto consumers who have no control over these problems. Families and small
business owners should not be left footing the bill for outdated infrastructure, faulty equipment, or poor
maintenance that they cannot fix or prevent.
        This proposed measure intends to amend Republic Act No. 9136 by prohibiting the recovery
of system loss charges from residential household consumers and qualified micro enterprises, and by
ensuring that responsibility for system losses is placed on distribution utilities and electric cooperatives
that have direct control over the electricity distribution network. It further seeks to ensure that these
consumers are not charged VAT or other related costs on electricity that was never actually delivered
and consumed.
        Under this proposed reform, distribution utilities shall be required to improve system efficiency
through facility modernization, upgrading distribution equipment, reducing technical losses,
strengthening anti-electricity pilferage programs, implementing advanced monitoring technologies,
and complying with efficiency standards established by the Energy Regulatory Commission.
        This measure does not seek to undermine the financial viability of the power industry. Rather,
it promotes a fairer and more accountable system where residential consumers and micro enterprises
pay only for the electricity they actually receive and use. At the same time, utilities are encouraged to
invest in improvements that reduce losses and improve the reliability of electricity services.
        Taking system loss charges off the electricity bills of residential households and qualified
micro enterprises will immediately ease the financial burden on Filipino families and small
entrepreneurs. It will enable households to devote more of their income to essential needs while
allowing micro enterprises, the lifeblood of the Philippine economy, to reinvest savings into their
businesses, preserve jobs, expand operations, and strengthen local communities. This reform will also
promote greater accountability, efficiency, and transparency in the power sector, moving the country
closer to the goal of affordable electricity for every Filipino and a more resilient, inclusive economy.
        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

42   from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with the Energy

43   Regulatory Commission (ERC), shall establish and implement a National System Loss Reduction

44   Program to ensure the continuous improvement of electricity distribution efficiency and the

1    reduction of avoidable system losses. The program shall require distribution utilities and electric

2    cooperatives to comply with mandatory system loss reduction targets, submit regular monitoring

3    and performance reports, undertake modernization and upgrading of distribution facilities,

4    strengthen measures for the detection and prevention of electricity pilferage, and comply with

5    approved efficiency standards. Failure to meet such standards shall subject the concerned

6    distribution utilities and electric cooperatives to appropriate administrative penalties and

7    regulatory sanctions as may be prescribed by the ERC.

8           SECTION 8. Consumer Protection and Transparency. – All electricity bills issued to

9    residential consumers shall comply with transparency and consumer protection requirements. No

10   electricity bill shall contain system loss charges as a separate, indirect, or disguised billing

11   component. All remaining charges imposed upon residential consumers shall be clearly identified,

12   itemized, and explained in a manner that enables consumers to fully understand the basis and

13   computation of their electricity charges. The ERC shall ensure that electricity rate adjustments and

14   other billing components are disclosed in a transparent and accessible manner to all residential

15   consumers.

16          SECTION 9. Non-Residential Consumers. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to

17   prevent the Energy Regulatory Commission from establishing appropriate regulatory treatment of

18   system losses applicable to commercial, industrial, or other non-residential customer

19   classifications, provided that such treatment shall remain consistent with the principles of fairness,

20   efficiency, transparency, and consumer protection, and shall not undermine the objective of

21   ensuring accountability among power industry participants.

22          SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the

23   effectivity of this Act, the Energy Regulatory Commission, in coordination with the Department

24   of Energy, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation

25   of this Act, including mechanisms for monitoring compliance, enforcing system loss reduction

26   standards, and ensuring that residential consumers are protected from the imposition of system

27   loss charges.
