                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and
   promote the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake
   appropriate health human resource development responsive to the country’s
   needs and problems (Article XIII, Section 12).

         Pharmacists play a vital role in the health care system as stewards of
   safe, effective, and rational drug use. They are responsible for dispensing
   medications, ensuring proper dosage and administration, managing
   pharmaceutical supplies, and providing essential patient counseling. In public
   health settings, pharmacists are integral to the implementation of disease
   prevention and control programs, including vaccination efforts, antimicrobial
   stewardship, and the management of chronic illnesses.

          In hospitals and community health facilities, pharmacists contribute
   significantly to patient safety by preventing medication errors, monitoring
   adverse drug reactions, and ensuring the quality and availability of essential
   medicines. Their role has become increasingly critical amid the rising
   complexity of pharmacotherapy and the growing burden of both communicable
   and non-communicable diseases.

         Despite their indispensable contributions, pharmacists in government
   service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of compensation relative
   to the level of expertise, responsibility, and accountability required in their
   profession. The current salary grade does not adequately reflect the technical
   demands of pharmaceutical services nor the critical role pharmacists play in
   safeguarding public health.

          Moreover, the increasing global demand for pharmacists has contributed
   to the continued outmigration of Filipino health care professionals seeking
   better compensation and working conditions abroad. This trend adversely
   affects the country’s capacity to maintain an efficient and responsive healthcare
   system, particularly in ensuring the availability and proper management of
   essential medicines in public health facilities.

         This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government pharmacists from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15. The
   proposed adjustment aims to ensure equitable compensation, improve
   workforce retention, and align their remuneration with similarly situated
   healthcare professionals.
      Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold
decent work standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the
delivery of safe, accessible, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                                “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT PHARMACISTS FROM SALARY GRADE 11
            TO SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Government
Pharmacists’ Salary Upgrading Act of 2026. "

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government pharmacists in
order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public health sector,
consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality healthcare
services to all Filipinos.

     SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Pharmacists' Minimum Salary
Grade Level - The present minimum salary grade of government pharmacists
(Pharmacist I) shall be upgraded from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of pharmacists occupying higher positions shall be
correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all pharmacists employed in
public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and operated by
the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
