                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE



The Philippine archipelago, with its 36,289 kilometers of coastline, possesses one of the
most productive marine ecosystems in the world. However, this natural wealth faces an
existential threat. Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA, 2024)1 indicates
that the ﬁsheries sector’s contribution to the national GDP remains constrained by chronic
overﬁshing and habitat degradation. Scientiﬁc assessments, including the National Stock
Assessment Program (NSAP), have consistently shown that more than 80% of major ﬁsh
stocks in our municipal waters are currently overexploited. 2

This bill, the "Bantay-Dagat, Yaman-Lokal Act," seeks to operationalize the mandate of
Article X, Section 2 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution, which guarantees genuine and
meaningful local autonomy. While the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No.
7160) and the Philippine Fisheries Code (Republic Act No. 8550, as amended by
Republic Act No. 10654)3 delegate management powers to Local Government Units
(LGUs) within the 15-kilometer municipal water zone, practical enforcement is often
hampered by bureaucratic friction, overlapping national-local mandates, and a lack of
ﬁscal independence for marine conservation.

Legal and Constitutional Basis

    1. Constitutional Autonomy: Article X, Sections 2 and 6 of the 1987 Philippine
       Constitution empower LGUs to manage their resources and create their own
       sources of revenue.4
    2. Statutory Framework: RA 7160 mandates that the LGU is the primary entity for the
       delivery of basic services and environmental protection. 5 RA 10654 explicitly
       provides that LGUs have the authority to enact ordinances to protect municipal
       waters.6
    3. International Commitment: As a signatory to the United Nations Convention on
       the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982), the Philippines has an obligation to manage
       marine resources sustainably. 7 This bill mirrors global best practices in
       "Community-Based Coastal Resource Management" (CBCRM). 8

The Need for Reform

The "tragedy of the commons" continues to plague our municipal waters. Fragmented
enforcement, where LGUs lack the budget to sustain Bantay-Dagat operations, has led to
rampant Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) ﬁshing. By institutionalizing Resource
Rent—the economic surplus derived from the extraction of public aquatic resources—this
bill establishes a self-sustaining revenue stream for LGUs. This ensures that the wealth
generated by our seas is reinvested directly into protecting those resources.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Bantay-Dagat, Yaman-Lokal
 2   Act.”

 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

 4   (a) Assert Local Sovereignty: Uphold the constitutional mandate of local autonomy by
 5   affirming the exclusive, primary, and ministerial jurisdiction of Cities and Municipalities
 6   over their 15-kilometer municipal waters;

 7   (b) Operationalize the Trust Doctrine: Recognize municipal fishery and aquatic
 8   resources as a public trust and Renewable Natural Capital, possessing both intrinsic
 9   value and economic utility, and which must be managed by the LGU to ensure their
10   essential functions and capacity to regenerate;


                                                                                                   1
 1   (c) Uphold Social Justice and Food Sovereignty: Protect the preferential and tenure
 2   rights of municipal fisherfolk as a matter of fundamental social justice, recognizing
 3   them as primary stakeholders and rights-holders in the governance of their communal
 4   waters;

 5   (d) Institutionalize Human Rights-Based Governance (HRBA): Mandate a governance
 6   framework where the LGU acts as the primary duty-bearer, ensuring accountability,
 7   transparency, non-discrimination, and the active participation of fisherfolk as rights-
 8   holders;

 9   (e) Promote Gender Equality: Recognize women as independent economic actors and
10   rights-holders within the fisheries value chain, ensuring their equitable participation in
11   decision-making and access to resources;

12   (f) Integrate Science and Local Knowledge: Ensure that fisheries management is
13   science-based, ecosystem-oriented, and informed by local ecological knowledge,
14   consistent with the Philippine Ecosystem and Natural Capital Accounting System
15   (PENCAS);

16   (g) Maximize Fiscal Autonomy: Institutionalize transparent Resource Rent accounting,
17   ensuring that the economic surplus derived from the extraction of public aquatic
18   resources is captured and reinvested into the Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund
19   (MFSF) for ecological restoration and community support;

20   (h) Assert Enforcement Leadership: Strengthen Monitoring, Control, and Surveillance
21   (MCS) as a core LGU function to eradicate Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated Fishing
22   (IUUF), with national agencies serving only in a collaborative and supportive capacity;

23   (i) Regulate Extractive Development: Proactively mitigate and resolve resource-use
24   conflicts caused by large-scale commercial, tourism, and extractive activities—such as
25   reclamation, mining, and dredging—by subjecting them to strict LGU zoning, permitting,
26   and environmental safeguards; and

27   (j) Ensure Sustainable Co-Management: Promote the equitable use of municipal waters
28   through mandatory co-management mechanisms, dispute resolution, and the
29   imposition of proportionate, ordinance-based sanctions to ensure long-term
30   sustainability.

32   (a) Municipal Waters- include not only streams, lakes, inland bodies of water and tidal
33   waters within the municipality which are not included within the protected areas as
34   defined under Republic Act No. 7586 (The NIPAS Law), public forest, timber lands,
35   forest reserves or fishery reserves, or part of ancestral domain as defined under
36   Republic Act 8371 (IPRA), but also marine waters included between two (2) lines drawn

 1   perpendicular to the general coastline from points where the boundary lines of the
 2   municipality touch the sea at low tide and a third line parallel with the general coastline
 3   including offshore islands and fifteen (15) kilometers from such coastline, subject to
 4   the following rules:

 5      1. For cities or municipalities with generally straight coastlines, the distance shall
 6         be reckoned from the low-water line along the mainland shoreline;
 7      2. For cities or municipalities composed wholly or partly of islands, islets, or with
 8         irregular coastlines, the distance shall be reckoned from the coastline of the
 9         outermost island or islet, applying the archipelagic principle; and
10      3. Where two (2) municipalities are so situated on opposite shores that there is less
11         than thirty (30) kilometers of marine waters between them, the third line shall be
12         equally distant from the opposite shore of the respective municipalities.

13   This delineation is for fisheries governance and local administrative purposes and shall
14   not affect national maritime baselines.

15   (b) Municipal Fisherfolk refers to persons—including women and marginalized gender
16   groups—who are directly or indirectly engaged in the entire ﬁsheries value and supply
17   chain. This encompasses the taking, culturing, post-harvest handling, ﬁsh vending,
18   marketing, and further processing of ﬁshery or aquatic products. To be recognized
19   under this deﬁnition, such individuals must reside in the municipality or city and be duly
20   registered in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registry (FishR) as maintained by the local
21   government unit, pursuant to the inclusive standards set by the Bureau of Fisheries and
22   Aquatic Resources.

23   (c) Tenure Security refers to the legally protected, preferential, and continuing right of
24   municipal fisherfolk to access and use municipal waters, subject to conservation
25   measures and due process.

26   (d) Human Rights–Based Approach (HRBA) refers to governance guided by
27   participation, accountability, transparency, non-discrimination, empowerment, and
28   legality.

29   (e) Resource Rent refers to the economic surplus derived from the use of fishery and
30   aquatic resources beyond normal returns to labor and capital.

31   (f) Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management Council (FARMC) refers to the co-
32   management body established under existing fisheries laws.

33   (g) Resource-use conﬂict – refers to any activity of a resource user that impairs ﬁsh
34   stocks or habitats; displaces or undermines tenure security of municipal ﬁsherfolk;
35   extracts public resource rent without authorization; causes ecological degradation
36   affecting other lawful users and other analogous situations.

 7   SEC. 4. Exclusive Jurisdictional Authority and Governance Functions. The City or
 8   Municipal Government shall exercise primary and exclusive jurisdiction over the
 9   management, conservation, protection, utilization, and development of its municipal
10   waters (the 15-kilometer zone). This authority is absolute and shall not be diminished by
11   national administrative policies.

12   In the exercise of this jurisdiction, the LGU shall perform the following functions, in
13   mandatory consultation with the FARMC:

14   (a) Legislative Supremacy: Enact, update, and enforce local fisheries ordinances,
15   zoning plans, and conservation measures that are responsive to local ecological
16   conditions.

17   (b) Tenure and Access Management: Establish a registry of municipal fisherfolk and
18   implement a licensing system that prioritizes their preferential and tenure rights,
19   ensuring that women and men are recognized as independent rights-holders.

20   (c) Fisheries Conservation: Design and implement science-based conservation
21   programs, including the establishment of Marine Protected Areas (MPAs), the
22   declaration of closed seasons, and the limitation of fishing effort.

23      1. Enforcement and MCS: Exercise full enforcement authority, including the
24         deployment of Bantay-Dagat and local law enforcement for sea-borne patrols,
25         board-and-search operations, and the apprehension of violators.
26      2. Fiscal Sovereignty: Institutionalize a system of Resource Rent valuation to set
27         fees, licenses, and access charges. All revenues shall be deposited directly into
28         the Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund (MFSF).
29      3. Conflict Resolution: Manage and resolve all resource-use conflicts, including
30         large-scale commercial, tourism, and extractive activities (e.g., reclamation,
31         mining, dredging) affecting municipal waters.

32   SEC. 5. Mandatory Co-Management Process. To ensure governance is transparent and
33   participatory, the LGU shall observe the following co-management requirements:

14   SEC. 6. Tenure Security and Preferential Access Rights.

15   (a) Right to Preferential Access. — Registered municipal fisherfolk shall enjoy a
16   superior, preferential, and non-extinguishable right to access and utilize municipal
17   waters. This right shall take precedence over, and be protected against, the claims or
18   presence of non-resident commercial fishing operators, large-scale industrial projects,
19   or other extractive activities.

20   (b)Protection Against Arbitrary Displacement. — No municipal fisherfolk shall be
21   deprived of their traditional or customary access to municipal waters, except under the
22   following conditions:

23      1. Conservation Mandates: When temporary exclusion is strictly required for the
24         regeneration of fish stocks (e.g., closed seasons or MPA core zones);
25      2. Due Process: Provided that the limitation is supported by credible scientific data,
26         enacted through a public ordinance, and subject to prior notice and community
27         consultation;
28      3. Just Transition: If the limitation results in the permanent loss of livelihood, the
29         LGU must provide a "Just Transition Package," which may include financial
30         assistance, livelihood diversification training, or priority access to alternative
31         sustainable fishing grounds, funded by the MFSF.

32   (c) Independent Recognition of Rights-Holders. — Tenure documentation and
33   registration systems shall be gender-neutral and inclusive. Women engaged in all
34   stages of the fisheries value chain—including gleaning, post-harvest processing, and
35   marketing—shall be registered independently as full-fledged rights-holders, ensuring
36   they have the same access to government benefits, insurance, and resource-use
37   permits as their male counterparts.

38   (d) Stewardship Obligations. — The exercise of tenure rights is a stewardship mandate.
39   It is conditioned upon:

                                                                                               5
 1      1. Registration with the LGU;
 2      2. Compliance with local fishery ordinances and conservation measures; and
 3      3. Active participation in local resource management and the protection of
 4         municipal waters from illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing.

 5   SEC. 7. MPA Management and Inter-LGU Alliances. When managing MPAs or shared
 6   water bodies, the following applies:

 7   (a) Integrated Management: MPA Management Plans must be co-developed with local
 8   fisherfolk organizations. These plans may serve as collateral for conservation-related
 9   financing, as guided by DA-BFAR and financial institutions.

10   (b) Inter-LGU Fisheries Management Boards (ILFMBs): For bays, gulfs, or contiguous
11   waters, LGUs must form an ILFMB to unify licensing, enforcement, and zoning. This
12   ensures that the ecosystem is managed as a single, indivisible unit.

13   SEC. 8. Resource Rent-Based Fiscal Governance. To ensure the long-term financial
14   sustainability of the LGU’s maritime governance:

15   (a) Economic Valuation: Fees are not arbitrary; they are calculated based on the
16   Resource Rent (the economic surplus of the resource), ensuring equitable extraction.

17   (b) Fund Security: All revenues from licenses, fines, and fees shall be deposited directly
18   into the Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund (MFSF).

19   (c) The No-Reversion Rule: These funds are strictly earmarked for fisheries-related
20   expenses and shall not revert to the LGU's General Fund.

21   (d) Transparency: The LGU is mandated to publish an annual Fisheries Sustainability
22   Report, detailing all collections and expenditures for public audit.

23   SEC. 9. Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund (MFSF)

24   (a) Creation and Nature of Fund. — There is hereby created, in every coastal Local
25   Government Unit (LGU), a Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund (MFSF). This fund
26   shall be maintained as a special trust fund, separate and distinct from the LGU’s
27   General Fund. It shall be accounted for as a restricted fund, and all balances thereof
28   shall not revert to the General Fund at the end of the fiscal year, remaining available for
29   its mandated purposes until fully expended.

30   (b) Composition of the Fund. — The MFSF shall exclusively consist of the following:

31      1. All revenues collected from fisheries-related licenses, permits, and access
32         charges, including those calculated based on Resource Rent;


                                                                                                   6
 1      2. All administrative fines, penalties, and proceeds from the confiscation and sale
 2         of illicitly caught fish or prohibited fishing gear within municipal waters;
 3      3. Grants, donations, endowments, and technical assistance funds provided by
 4         national government agencies, international organizations, or private entities
 5         specifically for fisheries management; and
 6      4. Such other lawful revenues generated from the management and sustainable
 7         use of municipal waters.

 8   (c) Authorized Expenditures. — The MFSF shall be utilized exclusively for the
 9   management, protection, and development of municipal waters. Disbursements shall be
10   strictly limited to the following:

11      1. Enforcement: Procurement, maintenance, and operational expenses for
12         Monitoring, Control, and Surveillance (MCS) and Bantay-Dagat operations;
13      2. Conservation: Establishment, maintenance, and monitoring of Marine Protected
14         Areas (MPAs) and Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs);
15      3. Data and Registry: Development and maintenance of gender-disaggregated
16         fisheries databases, fisherfolk registration, and digital tenure documentation
17         systems;
18      4. Capacity Building: Training and institutional strengthening of the Fisheries and
19         Aquatic Resources Management Council (FARMC);
20      5. Resilience and Social Justice: Implementation of climate adaptation measures,
21         livelihood diversification projects, and just transition programs for municipal
22         fisherfolk affected by conservation-based restrictions.

23   (d) Transparency and Accountability. — The Local Treasurer shall submit a quarterly
24   report to the Sangguniang Bayan/Panlungsod and the local FARMC detailing all
25   collections and expenditures of the MFSF. This report shall be made publicly accessible
26   through the LGU’s official website and local bulletin boards. The use of the fund shall be
27   subject to the standard audit rules and regulations of the Commission on Audit (COA).

28   SEC. 10. Municipal Fisheries and Zoning Management Planning

29   (a) Mandatory Planning. — Every coastal Local Government Unit (LGU) shall formulate,
30   adopt, and implement a Comprehensive City/Municipal Fisheries and Zoning
31   Management Plan (CMFZMP). This plan shall serve as the primary legal framework for
32   all activities within the 15-kilometer municipal water zone.

33   (b) Core Components of the Plan. — The CMFZMP shall be developed using science-
34   based, precautionary, and climate-resilient approaches. It shall explicitly include:

35      1. Zoning Delineation: Clear mapping of commercial, municipal, aquaculture, and
36         conservation zones (MPAs);
37      2. Resource Assessment: Baseline data on fish stocks, critical habitats, and
38         biodiversity indicators;

                                                                                              7
 1      3. Climate Adaptation: Strategies for coastal resilience, including the protection of
 2         mangroves and seagrasses as natural storm barriers;
 3      4. Local Ecological Knowledge: Integration of documented traditional knowledge
 4         and practices of local fishing communities.

 5   (c) Validation and Participation. — The CMFZMP shall undergo a participatory
 6   validation process. Before adoption by the Sangguniang Bayan or Panlungsod, the draft
 7   plan must be:

 8      1. Subjected to at least three (3) community-level consultations with registered
 9         fisherfolk;
10      2. Reviewed and endorsed by the local Fisheries and Aquatic Resources
11         Management Council (FARMC); and
12      3. Harmonized with the plans of adjacent LGUs if the municipal waters are part of a
13         shared bay, gulf, or contiguous fishing ground.

14   (d) Planning Cycle and Updates. — The CMFZMP shall be a five-year rolling plan. The
15   LGU shall conduct a mid-term performance audit and an end-of-cycle review to adjust
16   management measures based on current stock assessments and changing climatic
17   conditions.

18   (e) Legal Primacy. — Upon adoption by ordinance, the CMFZMP shall be the governing
19   document for all national agencies and private entities operating within the municipal
20   waters. Any project or activity inconsistent with the CMFZMP shall be considered a
21   violation of local law.

29   (b) Mandatory Representation and Data. — To institutionalize inclusive and
30   intergenerational fisheries governance, LGUs shall:

31      1. Inclusive Registration: Implement a unified registration system that tracks and
32         documents the specific contributions and demographic profiles of both women
33         and youth in the sector.
34      2. Sectoral Representation in FARMC: Mandate the following minimum seat
35         allocations within the Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management Council
36         (FARMC) membership:

 1                I.   At least thirty percent (30%) shall be reserved for women;
 2               II.   At least ten percent (10%) shall be reserved for Fisherfolk Youth (aged
 3                     15–30);
 4              III.   Intentional support shall be provided for the appointment of women
 5                     and youth to executive and leadership positions to ensure their voices

 7      3. Disaggregated Reporting: Mandate the annual collection and public disclosure of
 8         sex- and age-disaggregated data in all fisheries management reports, resource

10      4. Inclusive Conflict Resolution: Ensure that all resource-use conflict resolution

13   SEC. 12. Regulation of Aquaculture in Municipal Waters

14   (a) Regulatory Supremacy. — Aquaculture is a regulated economic activity subject to
15   LGU jurisdiction, zoning, and environmental compliance. No aquaculture project may
16   commence without a permit issued by the LGU, which shall be granted only upon a
17   determination that the project is consistent with the Municipal Fisheries and Zoning
18   Management Plan (CMFZMP).

19   (b) Environmental and Social Safeguards. — Permitting for aquaculture shall be
20   governed by the following:

21      1. Ecological Carrying Capacity: Permits must strictly adhere to biomass density
22         limits, water quality standards, and waste discharge controls to prevent
23         eutrophication or habitat degradation;
24      2. Spatial Zoning: Aquaculture zones must be clearly demarcated to ensure they do
25         not obstruct navigation routes, encroach upon core municipal fishing grounds, or
26         compromise the integrity of Marine Protected Areas (MPAs);
27      3. Tenure Protection: Aquaculture operations shall not displace municipal
28         fisherfolk or traditional fishing activities. Any modification to land or water use
29         requires prior community consultation, due process, and a "Just Transition"
30         support plan funded by the operator;
31      4. Compliance and Remediation: Any aquaculture structure found to be abandoned,
32         non-compliant, or operating without a valid permit shall be subject to immediate
33         removal at the operator’s expense.

34   SEC. 13. Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) Fishing as Resource-Use Conflict

35   (a) Recognition as Infringement. — IUU Fishing (IUUF) within the 15-kilometer municipal
36   waters is hereby declared a formal Resource-Use Conflict. It is recognized as an act
37   that directly undermines the tenure security of municipal fisherfolk, threatens the


                                                                                             9
 1   ecological sustainability of the municipal ecosystem, and constitutes an unlawful
 2   extraction of the LGU’s Natural Capital.

 3   (b) Primary Response Mandate. — The LGU shall act as the first responder and lead
 4   authority in all IUUF matters within its waters, responsible for:

 5      1. Prevention and Detection: Regular patrols and the maintenance of a maritime
 6         monitoring system;
 7      2. Enforcement: Immediate de-escalation, apprehension of suspects, and seizure of
 8         illegal vessels or gear;
 9      3. Documentation: Proper processing of evidence for both administrative and
10         potential criminal proceedings.

11   (c) Support and Coordination. — While FARMCs shall assist in community-based
12   monitoring and impact assessments, the LGU shall initiate coordination with the
13   Department of Agriculture-Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DA-BFAR), the
14   Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), or other national agencies only when the IUUF involves
15   organized syndicates, foreign incursions, or transboundary activities that exceed local
16   enforcement capacity. In such instances, the LGU shall retain the right to be a lead
17   participant in the subsequent legal proceedings.

18   SEC. 14. Regulation of Extractive and Large-Scale Commercial Developments

19   (a) Characterization as Resource-Use Conflict. — Tourism, land reclamation, dredging,
20   mining, quarrying, land development, and any other large-scale commercial or extractive
21   activities proposed within or directly affecting municipal waters are hereby formally
22   classified as Resource-Use Conflicts. These activities are presumed to threaten the
23   integrity of the Renewable Natural Capital of the city or municipality if they result in the
24   impairment of essential fish habitats, the displacement of municipal fisherfolk, or the
25   obstruction of established tenure rights.

26   (b) Regulatory Authority and Veto Power. — The LGUs of Cities or Municipalities shall
27   exercise full regulatory authority over such activities. No project, regardless of national
28   government endorsement or permit, shall be allowed to commence within municipal
29   waters unless the LGU, through its Sangguniang Bayan or Panlungsod, has issued a
30   City/Municipal Clearance for Resource-Use, based on a rigorous environmental and
31   socio-economic impact assessment.

32   (c) Mandatory Procedural Requirements. — Before the LGU may consider any
33   application for such projects, the proponent must strictly comply with the following:

34      1. Full Disclosure: Proponents shall provide a complete, plain-language disclosure
35         of project plans, including potential impacts on fish stocks, water quality, and
36         fisherfolk livelihoods, to be made publicly accessible for at least thirty (30) days;


                                                                                               10
 1      2. Mandatory FARMC Concurrence: No permit, license, or zoning variance shall be
 2         approved without the formal, written endorsement of the Fisheries and Aquatic
 3         Resources Management Council (FARMC) after a series of public consultations;
 4      3. Tenure Safeguards: Proponents must submit an approved Fisherfolk

 7      4. Ecological Performance Bond: The proponent must post a performance bond in

10   (d) Prohibition. — In areas designated as critical habitats, spawning grounds, or core
11   zones of Marine Protected Areas (MPAs), the LGUs of Cities or Municipalities are
12   empowered to issue an absolute prohibition on all extractive and large-scale
13   commercial developments.

14   SEC. 15. Local Fisheries Adjudication and Sanctions

15   (a) Establishment of Local Fisheries Adjudication Board (LFAB). — Every coastal LGU
16   shall establish a Local Fisheries Adjudication Board (LFAB). This board shall serve as
17   the primary, non-adversarial, and participatory forum for resolving all fisheries-related
18   disputes and resource-use conflicts within municipal waters.

19   (b) Administrative Jurisdiction and Sanctions. — The LFAB shall have the authority to
20   hear and decide cases involving violations of local fisheries ordinances. Upon a finding
21   of liability, and following the requirements of due process, the LFAB may impose the
22   following administrative sanctions:

23      1. Cease-and-Desist Orders: Immediate stoppage of any activity infringing upon
24         the tenure rights of municipal fisherfolk or violating conservation measures;
25      2. Suspension or Revocation: Permanent or temporary cancellation of fishing
26         permits, aquaculture licenses, or access privileges;
27      3. Administrative Fines: Monetary penalties based on the schedule set by local
28         ordinance, which shall be commensurate to the economic benefit gained from
29         the violation (Resource Rent impact);
30      4. Confiscation: Seizure and forfeiture of prohibited fishing gears, illegally caught
31         aquatic products, and/or the vessels used in the commission of the violation, to
32         be disposed of in accordance with local ordinance.

33   (c) Referral for Criminal Prosecution. — Where a resource-use conflict involves
34   violence, intimidation, coercion, or the use of illegal fishing methods punishable under
35   RA 10654 (as amended) or the Revised Penal Code, the LFAB shall, within twenty-four
36   (24) hours, refer the matter to the appropriate law enforcement agency or the Office of
37   the Prosecutor for criminal filing.



                                                                                                 11
 1   (d) Due Process and Proportionality. — All proceedings before the LFAB shall be
 2   governed by the principles of:

 3      1. Due Process: The right to notice, the right to be heard, and the right to present
 4         evidence;
 5      2. Proportionality: The severity of the sanction shall be directly proportional to the
 6         gravity of the violation and the extent of the damage caused to the municipal
 7         waters or the tenure rights of the fisherfolk;
 8      3. Non-Adversarial Settlement: The LFAB shall prioritize mediation and restorative
 9         justice where appropriate, provided such settlements do not compromise the
10         ecological integrity of the municipal waters or the preferential rights of municipal
11         fisherfolk.

12   SECTION 16. Enforcement Against Violations of Tenure and Access Rights

13   (a) Prohibited Acts. — Any person or entity, whether natural or juridical, who performs
14   the following acts shall be liable for administrative sanctions, without prejudice to
15   criminal or civil liability under existing laws:

16      1. Encroachment: Repeated or unauthorized entry into demarcated municipal
17         fishing grounds, zones reserved for small-scale fisherfolk, or designated Marine
18         Protected Areas (MPAs);
19      2. Obstruction of Rights: Acts that willfully, fraudulently, or through gross
20         negligence result in the displacement, exclusion, or obstruction of the
21         preferential access rights of municipal fisherfolk;
22      3. Deceptive Practices: Use of false claims, misrepresentation, or fraudulent
23         documentation to secure access or exclude lawful users;
24      4. Coercion and Intimidation: Any act of intimidation, threats, or harassment
25         intended to prevent municipal fisherfolk from exercising their rights;
26      5. Non-Compliance: Refusal to obey lawful orders, cease-and-desist mandates, or
27         final agreements reached during mediation or conflict resolution;
28      6. Interference: Deliberate obstruction of government-authorized monitoring,
29         enforcement, or mediation processes, including the Bantay-Dagat.

30   (b) Administrative Sanctions and Due Process. — Upon a finding of liability by the Local
31   Fisheries Adjudication Board (LFAB), the LGU may impose any of the following
32   sanctions, singly or in combination:

33      1. Written Warning and Cease-and-Desist Order;
34      2. Suspension or Revocation of all fisheries licenses, permits, or access privileges;
35      3. Administrative Fines as defined by local ordinance, calibrated to the economic
36         value of the resource involved;
37      4. Temporary or Permanent Exclusion from specific fishing grounds or municipal
38         waters;


                                                                                               12
 1      5. Blacklisting: Disqualification from participating in any local government fisheries
 2         programs, subsidies, or preferential access rights for a period of up to five (5)
 3         years.

 4   (c) Aggravated Acts Involving Violence or Intimidation. — Any violation involving
 5   physical violence, destruction of property, or the use of weapons shall be classified as
 6   an Aggravated Violation. In such instances:

 7      1. Immediate Suspension: The LCE shall immediately issue a summary suspension
 8         of all permits held by the offender pending full investigation;
 9      2. Law Enforcement Referral: The LGU shall immediately coordinate with the
10         Philippine National Police (PNP) and the Maritime Group for immediate arrest
11         and filing of appropriate criminal charges;
12      3. Asset Seizure: Any vessel or equipment used in an aggravated violation shall be
13         subject to immediate administrative impoundment until final legal resolution.

14   (d) Liability of Juridical Persons. — Where the violation is committed by a corporation,
15   cooperative, or other juridical entity, the following shall be held liable:

16      1. Responsible Officers: The officers, board members, or managers who
17         authorized, tolerated, or knowingly ignored the prohibited conduct;
18      2. The Entity: The juridical person itself shall be subject to revocation of its
19         municipal business permit and permanent disqualification from accessing
20         municipal waters, if it is determined that the entity materially benefited from the
21         unlawful act.

22   SEC. 17. Operational Control and Enforcement Protocols

23   (a) Operational Supervision and Control. — The Local Chief Executive (LCE) of the City
24   or Municipality shall exercise direct operational supervision and control over the local
25   Philippine National Police (PNP) Maritime Unit, the Bantay-Dagat, and all other
26   authorized local enforcement personnel within the 15-kilometer municipal waters. The
27   LCE is designated as the primary authority responsible for the implementation and
28   enforcement of this Act, the Philippine Fisheries Code, and all validly enacted local
29   fishery ordinances.

30   (b) Mandated Coordination and Joint Enforcement. — To ensure the protection of
31   continuous marine ecosystems, coastal LGUs sharing common fishing grounds, bays,
32   or gulfs are authorized to establish an Inter-LGU Enforcement Network (ILEN). This
33   network shall:

34      1. Harmonize patrol schedules, licensing requirements, and enforcement protocols;
35      2. Facilitate the sharing of resources, personnel, and information; and
36      3. Execute joint enforcement operations against large-scale IUU fishing or cross-
37         boundary violators.
                                                                                                13
 1   (c) Support Role of National Agencies. — The Department of Agriculture – Bureau of
 2   Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DA-BFAR), the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), and
 3   other national law enforcement agencies shall perform a supportive, non-primary role
 4   within municipal waters. Their assistance shall be triggered as follows:

 5      1. Request-Based Intervention: National agencies shall conduct sea-borne patrols
 6         and apprehension activities within the 15km zone only upon the formal request
 7         of, or in active coordination with, the LCE, except in cases of urgent national
 8         security or active "hot pursuit";
 9      2. Technical and Logistical Support: Upon request, the DA-BFAR and other national
10         agencies are mandated to provide the LGU with technical equipment, maritime
11         vessels, legal expertise, and forensic evidence processing for fishery violations;
12      3. Non-Interference: National agencies shall not, through their own administrative
13         orders, restrict or diminish the LGU’s primary enforcement authority over
14         municipal waters.

15   (d) Protocol for Joint Operations. — All joint operations involving national and local
16   agencies shall be governed by a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) executed by the
17   LGU and the respective national agency, clearly defining the chain of command, which
18   shall remain vested in the LCE for operations occurring within the municipal jurisdiction.

19   SEC. 18. Expedited Prosecution of Fishery Law Violations

20   (a) Designation of Special Fisheries Prosecutors. — Within sixty (60) days from the
21   effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice (DOJ) shall designate a dedicated
22   Special Fisheries Prosecutor for every coastal province. These prosecutors shall have
23   the exclusive mandate to:

24      1. Prioritize the preliminary investigation and filing of criminal complaints for
25         violations of this Act, RA 10654, and related fishery laws;
26      2. Provide legal and technical assistance to LGU legal officers during the
27         preparation of affidavits and evidence; and
28      3. Represent the State in all judicial proceedings involving fishery law violations
29         committed within municipal waters.

30   (b) Protocol for Speedy Prosecution. — The DOJ, in coordination with the Department
31   of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the DA-BFAR, shall promulgate the
32   Uniform Guidelines for the Speedy Prosecution of Fishery Violations. These guidelines
33   shall include:

34      1. Direct Filing: A streamlined process where the sworn affidavits and physical
35         evidence collected by the Bantay-Dagat and local police are treated as sufficient
36         to establish probable cause;
37      2. Case Management: The establishment of a digital "Fishery Case Tracker"
38         accessible by both the LGU and the DOJ to monitor the status of every filed case;

                                                                                              14
 1      3. Witness Protection: Standardized measures for the protection of local
 2         witnesses, including community observers and Bantay-Dagat personnel, to
 3         ensure they are not intimidated during trial.

 4   (c) Institutional Capacity Building. — The DILG and DA-BFAR shall provide regular
 5   training for local prosecutors and LGU legal officers on the specific evidentiary
 6   requirements for maritime crimes, ensuring that cases are not dismissed due to
 7   technical procedural errors.

 8   (d) Periodic Reporting. — The DOJ shall submit an annual Fishery Prosecution Report
 9   to Congress, detailing the number of cases filed, the rate of conviction, and the reasons
10   for any dismissed cases, to ensure accountability and track the effectiveness of this
11   Act.

12   SEC. 19. Inter-LGU Ecosystem-Based Management

13   (a) Formation of Fisheries Management Alliances. — LGUs sharing common fishing
14   grounds, bays, gulfs, or contiguous bodies of water are hereby authorized and
15   encouraged to form Fisheries Management Alliances (FMAs). These alliances shall be
16   formalized through a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) or a joint ordinance.

17   (b) Scope of Cooperation. — An FMA shall have the authority to:

18      1. Uniform Regulation: Harmonize zoning, closed seasons, gear restrictions, and
19         catch limits to ensure ecosystem-wide management;
20      2. Shared Enforcement: Establish a common patrol fleet and shared
21         communication protocols for Bantay-Dagat units across municipal boundaries;
22      3. Joint Licensing: Develop a unified system for fishing permits to prevent "forum
23         shopping" by commercial operators seeking the weakest regulation;
24      4. Resource Sharing: Pool financial and technical resources to manage shared
25         Marine Protected Area (MPA) networks.

26   (c) Role of the Provincial Government. — Upon the request of the participating LGUs,
27   the Provincial Governor, through the Provincial Agriculture Office (PAGO), shall act as
28   the primary facilitator for the alliance. The Provincial Government shall:

29      1. Provide technical secretariat support and mediation services;
30      2. Include the Alliance’s collaborative programs in the Provincial Development and
31         Physical Framework Plan;
32      3. Allocate budgetary support in the Annual Investment Program (AIP) for shared
33         monitoring and enforcement equipment that benefits the participating
34         municipalities.

17   SEC. 21. Rule of Construction. Any ambiguity in the interpretation of the provisions of
18   this Act, or in the application of any local ordinance or national regulation concerning
19   municipal waters, shall be strictly construed in accordance with the following principles:

20   (a) Presumption of Autonomy: In cases of conflict between local ordinance and
21   national administrative directives, preference shall be given to the LGU's governance
22   authority, consistent with the constitutional mandate of local autonomy and the primary
23   jurisdiction established under this Act.

24   (b) Rights-Based Interpretation: Any doubt concerning the tenure security, preferential
25   access, or rights of municipal fisherfolk shall be resolved in their favor, consistent with
26   the State's duty to promote social justice and food sovereignty.

27   (c ) Sustainability Mandate: Provisions concerning the management of natural capital
28   and Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) shall be interpreted to prioritize the ecological
29   integrity and regenerative capacity of the ecosystem over commercial or extractive
30   utility.

31   (d) Gender-Inclusive Application: Interpretations of governance, registry, and resource
32   rent allocation shall be guided by the principle of gender equality, ensuring the
33   recognition of women as independent economic actors.

34   (e) Pro-Sustainability Precaution: Where scientific uncertainty exists regarding the
35   impact of a proposed development or resource-use activity, the Precautionary Principle


                                                                                                16
 1   shall be applied, favoring the protection of the municipal waters and the long-term
 2   sustainability of fisheries over immediate economic gain.

 3   SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)

 4   (a) Oversight Committee. — Within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, the
 5   Joint Oversight Committee on Municipal Fisheries Governance shall be constituted to
 6   formulate the IRR. The Committee shall be composed of the following:

 7      1. Chairperson: The Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local
 8         Government (DILG), to ensure the protection of local autonomy;
 9      2. Co-Chairperson: A representative of the League of Municipalities of the
10         Philippines (LMP) and the League of Cities of the Philippines (LCP), to represent
11         the interests of the LGUs;
12      3. Members:

13                I.   The Secretary of the Department of Agriculture (DA);
14               II.   The Director of the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR);
15              III.   The Secretary of the Department of Justice (DOJ);
16              IV.    Three (3) representatives from national federations of municipal
17                     fisherfolk organizations, ensuring that at least one (1) representative is
18                     a woman; and
19               V.    One (1) representative from the scientific community specializing in
20                     marine ecology and resource economics.

21   (b) Mandated Timeline. — The Committee shall promulgate the IRR within ninety (90)
22   days from the date of the effectivity of this Act. The IRR shall include, but not be limited
23   to:

24      1. Standardized Resource Rent Formulas: A methodology for calculating fees
25         based on economic surplus;
26      2. Enforcement Protocols: Clear guidelines on the chain of command during joint
27         operations between the LGU and national enforcement agencies;
28      3. Tenure Documentation Standards: Uniform templates for the gender-responsive
29         registration of fisherfolk;
30      4. MFSF Guidelines: Manuals for the local accounting and transparent
31         management of the Municipal Fisheries Sustainability Fund.

32   (c) Consultation Requirement. — The drafting process for the IRR shall be subject to a
33   National Public Consultation involving coastal LGUs, regional FARMCs, and fisherfolk
34   cooperatives. No IRR shall be considered valid unless it has been subjected to at least
35   three (3) regional public hearings.

36   (d) Non-Diminution Clause. — The rules and regulations promulgated under this Act
37   shall not, under any circumstances, diminish the exclusive jurisdiction of the LGU over
