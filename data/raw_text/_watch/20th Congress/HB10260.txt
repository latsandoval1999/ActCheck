                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Access to court documents is a core function of court administration, ensuring that
records of judicial proceedings are properly issued, reproduced, and certified in a manner
that is consistent, reliable, and accessible to requesting parties. These documents form
part of the official record of the judiciary and are routinely required for legal,
administrative, and documentary purposes, including compliance with court processes
and other lawful transactions.

        In practice, however, the absence of a uniform and clearly regulated fee structure
for the issuance, reproduction, and certification of court documents has resulted in
variation in charges across different courts and offices. This lack of standardization may
lead to administrative inconsistency, uncertainty in public service delivery, and
inefficiencies in the processing of requests for court records.

       This measure seeks to address these concerns by establishing a standardized and
reasonable fee structure applicable to all courts within the Philippine judicial system,
thereby promoting uniformity in the delivery of court document services and ensuring
predictable and transparent administrative procedures.

       The bill likewise incorporates a defined exemption mechanism for Indigenous
Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs), consistent with Republic Act No.
8371, otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997. This exemption is
intended as an administrative accommodation within court document services,
implemented through a clear verification process, without altering existing rules on
judicial proceedings or court jurisdiction.

      By institutionalizing standardized fees alongside a clearly defined exemption
framework, this measure strengthens administrative consistency within the judiciary,
improves the efficiency of court record services, and clarifies the parameters for the
issuance, reproduction, and certification of judicial documents.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
     STANDARDIZING THE FEES FOR THE ISSUANCE, REPRODUCTION, AND
     CERTIFICATION OF COURT DOCUMENTS, AND GRANTING FEE EXEMPTION TO
     INDIGENOUS PEOPLES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title - This Act shall be known as the “Court Documents Fee
 2   Standardization and Indigenous Peoples Exemption Act.”
 3
 4         SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   ensure that access to court documents is fair, transparent, uniform, and not unduly
 6   burdened by unreasonable or inconsistent fees.
 7
 8         The State recognizes that court records are essential to the protection and
 9   enforcement of rights and the proper administration of justice.
10
11         Accordingly, the State shall:
12
13         (a) ensure the adoption of a uniform and standardized system of fees for the
14         issuance, reproduction, and certification of court documents across all courts;
15
16         (b) prevent the imposition of excessive, arbitrary, or inconsistent charges for
17         substantially similar court document services; and
18
 1         (c) ensure that Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs),
 2         upon proper verification, are exempt from the payment of such fees in recognition
 3         of their distinct circumstances under Republic Act No. 8371.
 4
 5         SECTION 3. Definition of Terms - For purposes of this Act, the following terms
 6   are hereby defined:
 7
 8         (a) Court – refers to all courts within the Philippine judicial system, including the
 9         Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Sandiganbayan, Court of Tax Appeals, Regional
10         Trial Courts, Metropolitan Trial Courts, Municipal Trial Courts in Cities, Municipal
11         Trial Courts, Municipal Circuit Trial Courts, Shari’ah Courts, and such other courts
12         as may be established by law;
13
14         (b) Court Document – refers to any official record, pleading, order, decision,
15         resolution, transcript, certification, or other judicial document issued, filed, or
16         maintained by a court in connection with any judicial proceeding, whether civil,
17         criminal, or special proceedings;
18
19         (c) Certification – refers to the official attestation issued by an authorized court
20         officer that a document is a true, correct, and faithful copy of the original record
21         on file with the court;
22
23         (d) Reproduction – refers to the copying, printing, scanning, digital conversion, or
24         duplication of court documents, whether in physical or electronic form;
25
26         (e) Issuance – refers to the official release, furnishing, or provision of court
27         documents to a requesting party upon request and compliance with applicable
28         requirements;
29
30         (f) Requesting Party – refers to any natural or juridical person who requests the
31         issuance, reproduction, or certification of court documents for lawful purposes;
32
33         (g)   Indigenous    Peoples (IP)     –   refers    to    Indigenous Cultural
34         Communities/Indigenous Peoples as defined under Republic Act No. 8371,
35         otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997;
36
37         (h) Qualified Beneficiary – refers to any Indigenous Peoples individual whose
38         membership in an Indigenous Cultural Community has been duly certified,
39         recognized, or verified by the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)
40         in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations;
41
42         (i) National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) – refers to the national
43         government agency created under Republic Act No. 8371 mandated to protect and
44         promote the rights and welfare of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous
 1          Peoples, including the issuance or recognition of certifications confirming
 2          Indigenous Peoples membership for purposes of this Act;
 3
 4          (j) Court Records Unit or Office – refers to the designated office or personnel within
 5          a court responsible for the safekeeping, management, and release of court
 6          documents and judicial records;
 7
 8          (k) Standard Fee – refers to the uniform and fixed amount authorized under this
 9          Act for the issuance, reproduction, or certification of court documents, exclusive
10          of any prohibited additional charges;
11
12          (l) Judicial Proceeding – refers to any civil, criminal, special, or administrative
13          matter heard or decided by a court of competent jurisdiction.
14
15          SECTION 4. Coverage - This Act shall apply to all courts within the Philippine
16   judicial system, including the Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Sandiganbayan, Court of
17   Tax Appeals, Regional Trial Courts, first-level courts, Shari’ah Courts, and such other
18   courts as may be created by law.
19
20           This Act shall govern all requests made to any court for the issuance, reproduction,
21   certification, authentication, or release of court documents, whether in physical or
22   electronic form, and whether requested for judicial, quasi-judicial, administrative,
23   academic, employment-related, professional, personal, or other lawful purposes.
24
25          This Act shall apply to all stages of request and processing of court documents,
26   including but not limited to retrieval from archives, duplication, certification, and official
27   release by the appropriate court office or personnel.
28
29          The coverage of this Act shall apply regardless of the status of the requesting
30   party, provided that exemptions under this Act, including those granted to Indigenous
31   Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs), shall be applied in accordance with
32   its provisions.
33
34         SECTION 5. Standardized Fee Structure - All courts within the Philippine judicial
35   system shall adopt and strictly implement a uniform fee structure for the issuance,
36   reproduction, and certification of court documents covered under this Act.
37
38         The fee for reproduction of court documents shall be fixed at the rate of Two Pesos
39   (₱2.00) per page, or such amount as may be adjusted in accordance with law by the
40   Supreme Court of the Philippines or the competent authority exercising rule -making
41   powers over the judiciary.
42
43         No court shall impose or collect any amount beyond the standardized fees
44   expressly provided under this Act, whether directly or indirectly. This prohibition shall
 1   include, but not be limited to, processing fees, administrative fees, convenience fees,
 2   system fees, archival fees, or any similar or analogous charges, regardless of
 3   nomenclature or form.
 4
 5         All fees shall be uniform and shall apply consistently across all courts, offices, and
 6   personnel involved in the issuance, reproduction, and certification of court documents.
 7
 8           SECTION 6. Exemption of Indigenous People - All Qualified Beneficiaries who are
 9   Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) shall be fully exempt
10   from the payment of any and all fees, charges, or costs for the issuance, reproduction,
11   certification, authentication, or release of court documents covered under this Act.
12
13          The exemption shall apply only upon proper establishment of Indigenous Peoples
14   status through any of the following:
15
16          (a) a certification issued by the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)
17          confirming membership in a recognized Indigenous Cultural Community;
18
19          (b) inclusion in an official NCIP registry or database of ICCs/IPs beneficiaries; or
20
21          (c) such other official document or proof of recognition issued or validated by the
22          NCIP in accordance with its rules and regulations.
23
24           For purposes of implementation, courts shall not require any additional
25   certification, endorsement, or clearance beyond those issued or recognized by the NCIP,
26   except for reasonable verification of identity through a valid government-issued
27   identification card or equivalent official document.
28
29         The exemption shall be applied at the time of request or application for court
30   documents, and shall not require prior approval from any court officer beyond verification
31   of NCIP-issued or NCIP-recognized documentation.
32
33          SECTION 7. Documents Covered - This Act shall cover all court documents,
34   records, and judicial issuances maintained, issued, or kept by any court within the
35   Philippine judicial system, whether in physical, electronic, or archived form, and whether
36   existing at the time of enactment or created thereafter.
37
38          The coverage shall include, but shall not be limited to, the following:
39
40          (a) Judicial Decisions and Issuances - Decisions, final and interlocutory orders,
41          resolutions, minute resolutions, and other judicial issuances resolving incidents or
42          cases;
43
 1          (b) Pleadings and Filed Submissions - Complaints, informations, answers, petitions,
 2          motions, replies, memoranda, manifestations, affidavits, and other similar
 3          submissions filed in court;
 4
 5          (c) Records and Case Materials - Case dockets, index cards, case summaries,
 6          records of proceedings, exhibits formally offered and admitted, and entries of
 7          judgment;
 8
 9          (d) Transcripts and Hearing Records - Transcripts of stenographic notes, audio or
10          video recordings of proceedings where officially maintained, and certified
11          summaries of hearings;
12
13          (e) Certified and Authenticated Documents - Certified true copies of any court
14          record, authenticated records, and official reproductions of documents on file with
15          the court;
16
17          (f) Court Certifications and Administrative Issuances - Certifications issued by court
18          offices, including certifications of pending cases, no pending case, finality of
19          judgment, record verification, and similar attestations;
20
21          (g) Processes and Judicial Writs - Writs of execution, summons, subpoenas,
22          notices, orders to produce, writs of attachment, garnishment, and other court-
23          issued processes;
24
25          (h) Electronic Court Records - Digitized records, electronic filings, e-court
26          documents, and other court records maintained in electronic case management
27          systems; and
28
29          (i) Other Analogous Judicial Records - Any other document, record, or issuance of
30          similar nature maintained, issued, or recognized by a court in connection with

32
33           The enumeration under this Section shall be interpreted as illustrative and not
34   restrictive, and shall be construed in favor of a broad and comprehensive coverage of all
35   judicial records subject to issuance, reproduction, or certification under this Act.
36
37          SECTION 8. Excluded Fees - The standardized fee structure prescribed under this
38   Act shall apply exclusively to the issuance, reproduction, certification, authentication, and
39   release of court documents. It shall not apply to, nor be construed to affect, the following
40   fees, costs, or charges imposed under existing laws, rules of procedure, or court
41   issuances:
42
43          (a) Filing Fees - fees required for the commencement or initiation of actions,
44          proceedings, or special cases before any court;
 1
 2          (b) Docket and Court Appearance Fees - fees associated with case docketing,
 3          calendaring, or appearance-related charges as may be required under court rules;
 4
 5          (c) Legal Research Fund Fees - statutory fees intended for the Legal Research
 6          Fund or similar judicial funds authorized by law;
 7
 8          (d) Alternative Dispute Resolution Fees - fees related to mediation, judicial dispute
 9          resolution, arbitration, or other court-annexed or court-referred dispute resolution
10          processes;
11
12          (e) Sheriff’s and Enforcement Fees - fees related to service of summons, writs,
13          notices, levies, executions, or other enforcement of court processes;
14
15          (f) Bail, Bonds, and Related Security Charges - including bail bonds, property
16          bonds, or other forms of security required for provisional liberty or court orders;
17
18          (g) Other Judicial Fees Unrelated to Court Documents - any other fees, charges,
19          or assessments imposed by courts that are not directly connected to the issuance,
20          reproduction, certification, authentication, or release of court documents.
21
22          For the avoidance of doubt, no provision of this Act shall be interpreted to reduce,
23   modify, or eliminate any court fees or charges not expressly covered by its scope, except
24   those relating strictly to court document services as defined herein.

26         SECTION 9. Duties of Courts - All courts shall:
27         All courts within the Philippine judicial system shall be responsible for the effective
28   implementation of this Act and shall have the following duties:
29
30          (a) Transparency of Fees and Procedures - Ensure the clear, visible, and
31          continuous posting of standardized fee schedules, documentary requirements,
32          processing timelines, and step-by-step procedures for the issuance, reproduction,
33          and certification of court documents, including posting in official court premises
34          and, where applicable, official websites or electronic platforms;
35
36          (b) Efficient Processing of Requests - Process all requests for court documents
37          within reasonable and defined time periods to be prescribed in the Implementing
38          Rules and Regulations, taking into account the nature, volume, and availability of
39          records, and ensuring that delays are not caused by unjustifiable administrative
40          inefficiency;
41
42          (c) Designation of Responsible Officers or Units - Designate a specific office, unit,
43          or authorized court personnel responsible for the receipt, tracking, processing, and
 1          release of court documents, as well as the proper implementation of fee collection
 2          and exemption mechanisms under this Act;
 3
 4          (d) Standardized Application of Fees - Ensure strict and uniform application of the
 5          standardized fee structure prescribed under this Act across all branches, stations,
 6          and offices, without variation or discretionary adjustment unless expressly
 7          authorized by law or competent authority;
 8
 9          (e) Implementation of Indigenous Peoples Fee Exemption - Recognize and
10          implement      the    fee    exemption    granted    to   Indigenous     Cultural
11          Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) under this Act, ensuring that qualified
12          beneficiaries are not required to pay any covered fees upon presentation of valid
13          NCIP-issued or NCIP-recognized documentation;
14
15          (f) Records Management and Accountability - Maintain accurate records of all
16          requests for court documents, including those availing of exemptions, for purposes
17          of audit, monitoring, and compliance review, in accordance with rules to be issued
18          by the Supreme Court; and
19
20          (g) Non-Discrimination in Access to Services - Ensure that no requesting party is
21          denied, delayed, or subjected to additional requirements, treatment, or procedures
22          beyond those prescribed by law and the Implementing Rules and Regulations.
23
24          SECTION 10. Implementing Agencies - The Supreme Court of the Philippines,

27   circulars, guidelines, and operational procedures for its effective implementation across
28   all courts.
29
30          For    purposes     of    verifying    the    status   of   Indigenous     Cultural

33   Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), which shall serve as the competent authority for the issuance,
34   validation, and recognition of certifications of Indigenous Peoples membership.
35
36         All courts under the judicial system shall comply with and implement the rules,
37   standards, and mechanisms issued pursuant to this Act.

41   acts:
42
 1          (a) impose, demand, collect, or receive any fee, charge, cost, or consideration in
 2          excess of, or not authorized under, the standardized fee structure prescribed in
 3          this Act;
 4
 5          (b) refuse, deny, or fail to process, receive, or act upon any request for the
 6          issuance, reproduction, certification, or authentication of court documents on the

 8
 9          (c) delay, withhold, suspend, or unreasonably defer the release or processing of

12

16

20
21          (f) engage in any act, scheme, practice, or omission intended to circumvent,
22          frustrate, or defeat the rights, objectives, or intent of this Act.
23
24           SECTION 12. Penalties - Any court, court personnel, officer, or person acting on
25   behalf of the judiciary who violates any provision of this Act shall be held administratively
26   liable and shall be subject to disciplinary action in accordance with applicable civil service
27   laws, the Rules of Court, and the internal rules and procedures of the Supreme Court.
28
29          The penalties that may be imposed, after due notice and hearing, shall include
30   reprimand, suspension, fine, demotion, or dismissal from service, as may be warranted
31   by the gravity of the offense. In determining the appropriate penalty, due consideration
32   shall be given to the nature of the violation, the frequency or repetition of the offense,
33   the presence of bad faith, fraud, gross negligence, or willful intent, and the extent of
34   prejudice caused to the requesting party or qualified beneficiary.
35
36          This shall be without prejudice to the filing of civil, criminal, or other administrative
37   actions that may arise under existing laws, rules of court, or other applicable regulations
38   arising from the same act or omission.
