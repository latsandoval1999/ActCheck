                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Natural and human-induced calamities repeatedly inflict heavy loss of life,
property damage, and prolonged disruption of basic services and local economies. In
their wake, families and enterprises face severe cash flow stress from lost income and
destroyed assets, even as tax obligations remain due and enforceable. This mismatch
slows recovery and delays the return of normal economic activity.

       This measure establishes a predictable system of tax relief in areas placed under a
state of calamity. It authorizes time-bound and clearly defined interventions that ease
immediate burdens while preserving government revenues in the medium term,
namely:

   1. Extension of deadlines for filing returns and paying national taxes;
   2. Suspension of penalties, surcharges, and interest during the relief period;
   3. Deferment or partial waiver of tax obligations when warranted by the extent of
      damage and loss;
   4. Suspension of the assessment and collection of real property taxes in affected
      localities for one fiscal year, with payments to resume afterward in staggered
      installments.

       To support private relief and rehabilitation efforts, the bill also grants an
automatic exemption from donor’s tax for donations intended for calamity victims,
subject to a ceiling that not more than ten percent of the donated amount may be used
for administrative expenses.

       The policy goal is simple and practical: give disaster-stricken communities
immediate fiscal breathing room so they can rebuild livelihoods, restore services, and
restart local commerce, while setting clear rules for the resumption of obligations once
minimal stability returns.

      By institutionalizing timely and targeted tax relief, the State affirms its duty to
advance social justice, protect vulnerable sectors, and strengthen economic resiliency
through partnership with local governments and the private sector.

      In view of the foregoing, the early passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

              AN ACT PROVIDING TAX RELIEF IN AREAS UNDER
                         A STATE OF CALAMITY

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Tax Relief in Calamity
Areas Act”.

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to adopt a holistic,
comprehensive, integrated, and proactive approach to lessen the socioeconomic and
environmental impacts of disasters and to promote the participation of all sectors at all
levels, consistent with Republic Act No. 10121. Fiscal measures under this Act shall
provide timely relief that preserves livelihoods, maintains enterprise viability, and
supports recovery while safeguarding the revenue base.

       SECTION 3. Definitions. For purposes of this Act:

       a. Calamity refers to a condition involving mass casualty and or major damage
          to property, disruption of the means of livelihood and of normal life in
          affected areas due to natural or human-induced hazard, consistent with
          Republic Act No. 10121.

       b. Covered Area refers to any province, city, or municipality placed under a
          state of calamity by the President, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
          Management Council, or the proper Sanggunian in accordance with law.

       c. Covered Period refers to the period of one fiscal year counted from the date
          the state of calamity takes effect in the Covered Area, unless earlier lifted
          under Section 5.

       d. Covered Taxpayers refers to individuals, estates, trusts, corporations,
          partnerships, and other juridical persons whose residence, principal place of
          business, factory, plantation, branch, or fixed place of activity is within a
          Covered Area, and who are directly affected by the Calamity.

       e. Additions to the Tax refer to interest, surcharges, and penalties under the
          National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

       f. Real Property Tax (RPT) refers to the basic real property tax and any
          additional ad valorem tax on idle lands or a special education fund levy that
          may be imposed by local government units in accordance with law.

       g. Local Business Tax (LBT) refers to the taxes imposed by local government
          units on businesses, manufacturers, wholesalers, exporters, retailers,
          contractors, and financial institutions pursuant to Republic Act No. 7160,
          otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991.
       h.​ Amusement Tax refers to the percentage tax collected by local government
           units from proprietors, lessees, or operators of theaters, cinemas, concert
           halls, circuses, boxing stadia, and other places of amusement pursuant to the
           Local Government Code of 1991.

        SECTION 4. Triggering Event and Coverage. Upon a declaration of a state of
calamity in a locality, the tax relief in Sections 6 to 9 shall automatically apply in that
locality to Covered Taxpayers for the Covered Period. The Department of Finance and
the Bureau of Internal Revenue shall publish a list of Covered Areas, the effectivity date
for each locality, and a simple guide to availment within fifteen days of the declaration.

       SECTION 5. Early Lifting. If the state of calamity is lifted before the end of the
Covered Period, the Secretary of Finance may shorten the relief period for good cause
after consultation with the local government unit concerned, provided that no relief
already validly availed shall be clawed back.

      SECTION 6. Relief for National Internal Revenue Taxes. For Covered Taxpayers
during the Covered Period:

       a.​ Filing and Payment Extensions. Deadlines for filing returns and paying internal
           revenue taxes that fall due during the Covered Period are extended to the last
           day of the month following the end of the Covered Period.

       b.​ Installment Payment. Amounts due for returns and assessments that fell due
           during the Covered Period may be paid in equal monthly installments over
           six months starting on the first day after the extension in paragraph a,
           without Additions to the Tax during the installment schedule, provided the
           taxpayer complies with the schedule

       c.​ Suspension of Additions to the Tax. Additions to the Tax shall not accrue on
           liabilities that fell due during the Covered Period, from the original due date
           until the end of the extension or installment schedule under this Section

       d.​ Abatement for Reasonable Cause. The Commissioner of Internal Revenue shall
           abate Additions to the Tax incurred because records were lost or access to
           facilities was prevented by the Calamity, upon submission of reasonable
           alternative proof as provided in Section 10

       e.​ Suspension of Audit and Enforcement. Field audits, distraint, levy, and similar
           enforcement actions within Covered Areas are suspended during the Covered
           Period, except in cases of prima facie fraud or to prevent imminent loss of
           revenue. Prescriptive periods and protest or appeal periods are tolled during
           the Covered Period

      SECTION 7. Clarification for Withholding Agents and VAT. During the Covered
Period:

       a.​ Withholding agents shall continue to withhold and remit taxes to the extent
           practicable. Late remittances resulting from direct Calamity impact may
           qualify for abatement of Additions to the Tax under Section 6 (d).

       b.​ The treatment of value-added tax, output tax, and input tax under existing
           law shall continue. Filing and payment relief under Section 6 applies to VAT
           returns and remittances that fall due during the Covered Period.

       SECTION 8. Real Property Tax Relief. Within each Covered Area:

       a.​ Suspension. Assessment and collection of RPT is suspended for one fiscal year
           beginning on the date of the declaration of the state of calamity
        b.​ Resumption and Installments. After the suspension, payment shall resume and
            may be settled in three equal staggered installments payable on the last day of
            the third, sixth, and ninth month following resumption

        c.​ No Interest and Surcharges. No interest or surcharges shall accrue on RPT
            liabilities that were suspended and paid in accordance with paragraph b

        d.​ Reduction or Waiver of RPT for Severe Disruption. Local government units,
            through their respective Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang
            Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan, are authorized to enact ordinances
            granting further reductions, partial waivers, or total exemptions of real
            property taxes for covered taxpayers suffering catastrophic physical or
            financial destruction.

        e.​ Coordination. The Department of the Interior and Local Government and the
            Bureau of Local Government Finance shall issue joint guidelines to local
            government units on uniform computation, billing, and collection following
            this Section.

        SECTION 9. Local Business Tax and Amusement Tax Relief. Within each Covered
Area:

      a. Suspension and Extension of Deadlines. The assessment, collection, and filing
deadlines for local business taxes and amusement taxes due from Covered Taxpayers
during the Covered Period are suspended and extended for a period of one fiscal year
beginning on the date of the declaration of the state of calamity.

       b. Resumption and Installments. After the suspension period, payment of the
suspended local business taxes and amusement taxes may be settled in three equal
staggered installments payable on the last day of the third, sixth, and ninth month
following resumption.

       c. Waiver of Interest, Surcharges, and Penalties. No interest, surcharges, or penalties
shall accrue on local business tax and amusement tax liabilities that were suspended
and paid in accordance with this Section.

       d. Reduction or Waiver of LBT for Severe Disruption. Local government units,
through their respective Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, or
Sangguniang Bayan, are authorized to enact ordinances granting further reductions,
partial waivers, or total exemptions of local business taxes and amusement taxes for
businesses suffering catastrophic physical or financial destruction.

       e. Joint Guidelines. The Department of the Interior and Local Government and the
Bureau of Local Government Finance shall issue supplemental guidelines to assist local
government units in the uniform implementation, adjustment, and collection schedules
of local business and amusement taxes under this Section.

       SECTION 10. Donor’s Tax Exemption for Calamity Donations. Any donation or
contribution made through or in the name of any duly organized entity intended
exclusively for the benefit of victims in a Covered Area is exempt from the donor’s tax.
The exemption:​

        a.​ Applies automatically without accreditation, prior approval, or certification
            as a condition for availment.

        b.​ Is subject to the limitation that not more than ten percent of the donated
            amount may be used by the donee for administrative expenses.
      c.​ Requires the donee to keep records of receipts and disbursements for audit
          purposes, using reasonable alternative documentation where primary records
          were lost due to the Calamity, as provided in Section 10.

       SECTION 11. Alternative Documentation and Safe Harbor. To facilitate compliance
where primary records were lost or damaged due to the Calamity:​
​
       a. The Bureau of Internal Revenue shall accept reasonable secondary evidence,
such as bank statements, electronic invoices, supplier certifications, insurance adjuster
reports, and sworn declarations with supporting photos or geotagged records​

      b. Taxpayers who adopt the Bureau of Internal Revenue safe harbor schedules
and templates issued under this Act shall be deemed to have substantially complied in
good faith, subject to post relief verification

       SECTION 12. Anti-Fraud Safeguards. Nothing in this Act condones tax evasion or
prevents the investigation and prosecution of fraud. Relief may be denied or withdrawn
in cases of willful misrepresentation, sham transactions, or diversion of donations for
private benefit.

        SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Issuances. Within fifteen days from
effectivity, the Department of Finance, the Bureau of Internal Revenue, the Department
of the Interior and Local Government, and the Bureau of Local Government Finance
shall issue the necessary rules, revenue regulations, and circulars to implement this Act,
including model notices, sample computations, and a public list of Covered Areas and
effectivity dates.​

       SECTION 14. Relation to Other Laws. This Act supplements the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, and relevant local government tax laws. In case of
conflict, this Act shall prevail with respect to Covered Areas and the Covered Period.

      SECTION 15. Separability. If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, orders, rules, regulations, or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 17. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two newspapers of general circulation in the
Philippines.

Approved.
