                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Rapid advancements in digital technology have transformed the way young Filipinos
learn, communicate, and interact. While digital platforms provide valuable
opportunities for education and connectivity, excessive and unregulated use of social
media and online platforms has also contributed to growing concerns involving anxiety,
depression, cyberbullying, social isolation, sleep disruption, and other mental health
challenges among the youth.

The Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health of the
people and to instill health consciousness among them. This responsibility includes
safeguarding the mental and emotional well-being of children and young people,
especially in the face of emerging digital risks that affect their development and overall
welfare.

While Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,”
established a national framework for mental health services, there remains a need for
stronger school-based mental health programs and initiatives that specifically address
the effects of excessive digital exposure on students and young individuals.

Therefore, this bill seeks to institutionalize a National Youth Mental Health and Digital
Wellbeing Program that will strengthen mental health services in schools, promote
responsible digital engagement, establish early intervention and counseling
mechanisms, and encourage healthy offline social interaction among the youth.

Ultimately, this bill aims to foster a healthier, safer, and more balanced digital
environment for Filipino students while promoting emotional resilience, responsible
technology use, and accessible mental health support systems.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE NATIONAL YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH AND
     DIGITAL WELLBEING PROGRAM, PROVIDING MENTAL HEALTH
    PROFESSIONALS IN SCHOOLS, REGULATING HARMFUL DIGITAL
 PRACTICES, AND STRENGTHENING EARLY INTERVENTION FOR FILIPINO
          STUDENTS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Mental Health and Digital Wellbeing
for Youth Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the mental,
emotional, and psychological health of the Filipino youth. Recognizing the rising
incidence of depression, anxiety, self-harm, and digital addiction among students, the
State shall institutionalize responsive, school-based mental health services and
regulate the harmful effects of digital platforms on the well-being of minors.
SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act seeks to:
   a. Ensure access to early, preventive, and non-stigmatizing mental health support
      in all public secondary schools and State Universities and Colleges (SUCs);
   b. Deploy trained mental health professionals to educational institutions
      nationwide;
   c. Regulate youth-targeted digital platforms to prevent algorithmic harm and
      addictive design;
   d. Promote digital literacy and emotional resilience among students; and
   e. Create a nationwide platform for youth mental health support through
      technology.
SEC. 4. School-based Mental Health Services. – The Department of Education
(DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall implement
comprehensive mental health services in all public secondary schools and SUCs,
including:
   a. Deployment of at least one licensed mental health professional (e.g.
      psychologist, guidance counselor, or psychiatric nurse) per high school and
      university campus within three (3) years from the effectivity of this Act;
   b. Annual mental health screenings and wellness profiling for students;
   c. Creation of safe spaces for counseling, peer support, and emotional
      processing;
   d. Implementation of a confidential and non-punitive referral system for at-risk
      students; and
   e. Mandatory training programs for teachers and staff on mental health first aid,
      trauma-informed approaches, and empathy-based discipline.
SEC. 5. Digital Detox Week. – All schools shall observe an annual “Digital Detox
Week,” a week-long series of activities promoting offline connections, creative
expression, nature engagement, and digital self-regulation.
DepEd and CHED shall integrate Digital Detox Week into the immediately succeeding
official school calendar after the effectivity of this Act.
SEC. 6. Report on Youth Mental Health and Digital Behavior. – DepEd, CHED, and
the Department of Health (DOH) shall publish an annual report on Youth Mental Health
and Digital Behavior, covering:
   a. Trends in depression, anxiety, digital usage, and suicide risk among students;
   b. Regional disparities and emerging mental health issues; and
   c. Policy recommendations and accountability measures for digital platforms.
SEC. 7. Appropriations. – The necessary funds for the implementation of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA). The DepEd, CHED,
DOH, and the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)
shall coordinate to ensure adequate and timely release of funds. Local government
units (LGUs) may also allocate funds to support this Act within their jurisdiction.
SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DepEd, CHED, and DICT shall convene a Joint Task
Force to draft the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).
SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.
SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, and rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
