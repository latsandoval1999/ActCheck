                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of the Philippines’ most important
natural assets. Stretching from Cagayan in the north to Quezon in the south,
it forms a vast interconnected landscape of forests, watersheds, rivers,
biodiversity areas, and coastal ecosystems. Its ecological functions extend far
beyond the mountain range itself, providing essential services that sustain
communities, agriculture, water systems, infrastructure, livelihoods, and
economic activities in both upland and downstream areas.

The Sierra Madre likewise serves as a critical natural defense against
typhoons, flooding, landslides, storm surges, drought, and other climate-
related hazards. Its forests, watersheds, wetlands, riparian areas, ecological
corridors, and coastal ecosystems regulate water flows, store and sequester
carbon, support biodiversity, and contribute to the resilience of communities
and critical infrastructure. The protection and restoration of the Sierra Madre
are therefore not merely matters of environmental conservation, but matters
of national development, public safety, water security, climate resilience, and
national security.

Despite its importance, the Sierra Madre remains vulnerable to continuing
degradation arising from illegal logging, destructive extraction, incompatible
land conversion, habitat fragmentation, biodiversity loss, and other activities
that undermine ecological integrity. Existing governmental mandates and
management arrangements, while important, operate through different
agencies, jurisdictions, plans, and regulatory systems. Such fragmentation
makes it difficult to manage the Sierra Madre as one interconnected ecological
system and to consistently account for impacts that cross administrative
boundaries.




                                                                               1
This proposed measure seeks to address this institutional and ecological gap
by establishing the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA) as a
government-owned or -controlled corporation with an original charter,
attached to the Office of the President for policy coordination and
administrative supervision. The Authority is envisioned as the lead policy,
planning, coordinating, and regulatory body for range-wide ecological
governance, while preserving the lawful mandates of the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), local government units,
Protected Area Management Boards, the National Commission on Indigenous
Peoples, and other competent institutions.

At the center of the proposed framework is the Sierra Madre Ecological
Integrity and Sustainable Development Master Plan, a comprehensive,
science-based, geospatially explicit, 25-year plan that will establish ecological
limits, management zones, no-go areas, permitted and prohibited uses,
restoration priorities, measurable outcomes, investment requirements, and
implementation responsibilities. The Master Plan will provide a common
framework for harmonizing national, regional, local, ancestral-domain, and
sectoral plans affecting the Sierra Madre.

The measure adopts a landscape-scale and ridge-to-reef approach to
conservation. Rather than treating forests, watersheds, rivers, biodiversity
areas, and coastal ecosystems as isolated units, the proposed framework
recognizes their ecological and hydrological interdependence. It provides for
the identification and protection of ecological corridors, riparian buffers,
critical habitats, key biodiversity areas, and other connectivity systems
necessary to maintain biodiversity and ecosystem resilience.

The bill further advances a Nature-Based Solutions-First policy. Government
interventions     addressing   flooding,  landslides,   erosion,   watershed
degradation, water insecurity, coastal hazards, and climate risks will be
required to consider the feasibility of nature-based, green, or green-gray
solutions before relying solely on conventional infrastructure. The proposed
approach recognizes natural ecosystems as functional infrastructure capable
of providing long-term disaster-risk reduction, water-security, and climate-
adaptation benefits.

Recognizing that conservation cannot be sustained without viable
communities and economic incentives, the proposed measure promotes
sustainable livelihoods, including agroforestry, ecotourism, and sustainable
non-timber forest products. It also provides avenues for Indigenous Peoples,
upland communities, and lawful tenure holders to participate in conservation
and restoration activities and benefit from verified ecological outcomes.

A significant innovation of the measure is the establishment of a Sierra Madre
Nature Credit System. The system is intended to create a transparent and
independently verified mechanism through which measurable protection,
restoration, biodiversity, watershed, water-security, and climate-resilience
outcomes may be recognized and financed. Covered enterprises that




                                                                               2
materially depend upon Sierra Madre ecosystem services may be required to
satisfy Nature Credit Purchase Obligations, thereby helping align economic
activity with the ecological services upon which such activity depends. The
system expressly does not authorize prohibited activities or substitute for
environmental, tenure, protected-area, or Indigenous Peoples safeguards.

To support the long-term implementation of the law, the bill establishes a
Sierra Madre Fund and provides for lawful and transparent financing
mechanisms, including national government appropriations, certain
environmental charges, penalties and restoration recoveries when legally
assignable, grants and technical assistance, and other lawful sources. The
Fund is limited to the Authority’s statutory public-good and market-
governance functions and is subject to government accounting, procurement,
auditing, transparency, and disclosure requirements.

The measure also strengthens accountability by establishing ecological
standards, monitoring systems, restoration obligations, environmental
liability mechanisms, and stronger prohibitions against destructive activities.
It prohibits, among others, unauthorized land conversion, destructive
extractive activities, introduction of invasive species, unauthorized
commencement of covered works, continuation of activities despite lawful
orders, and the use of false compliance documents.

Equally important, the proposed framework recognizes the constitutional and
statutory rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples and
local communities. Their customary laws, ancestral domains, lawful tenure,
participation, benefit-sharing, and Free and Prior Informed Consent, where
applicable, shall be respected and protected. The measure likewise seeks to
ensure that conservation does not become a burden imposed upon
communities, but an opportunity for them to become active partners and
beneficiaries in the protection and restoration of the Sierra Madre.

Through a dedicated Authority, a unified long-term Master Plan, binding
ecological safeguards, modern monitoring and accountability mechanisms,
sustainable livelihood opportunities, and innovative conservation financing,
this measure seeks to establish a coherent and enduring framework for
protecting the Sierra Madre while allowing development that is consistent with
its ecological limits.

Protecting the Sierra Madre is not merely an obligation to conserve what
remains. It is an investment in the security, resilience, and prosperity of the
Filipino people and in the ecological inheritance of generations yet to come.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

28   Sec. 5. Creation of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority. There is
29   hereby created the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA),
30   hereinafter referred to as the Authority, as a government-owned or -controlled
31   corporation with an original charter under Republic Act No. 10149, vested
32   with the corporate and regulatory powers expressly granted by this Act. It
33   shall be attached to the Office of the President for policy coordination and
34   administrative supervision, and shall be subject to the oversight of the
35   Governance Commission for GOCCs and other applicable laws. For policy and
36   technical alignment, the Authority shall closely coordinate with the
37   Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and other
38   relevant national agencies. The Authority shall be the lead policy, planning,
39   coordinating, and regulatory body, and the range-wide ecological governance
40   and nature-market authority, tasked with managing the Sierra Madre
41   landscape as a whole.

28   resilience.

30   Recognizing its function as one of the country’s longest natural barrier against

32   protection as a matter of national survival and public safety.

 1   Sec. 13. Delineation of Powers and Functions. The Authority shall operate
 2   as a coordinative and integrative body, and shall not duplicate or supersede
 3   the existing mandates of national agencies, LGUs, Protected Area
 4   Management Boards (PAMBs), or Environmental Impact Committees (EICs)
 5   established under existing laws, rules, and regulations. It shall not issue an
 6   ECC, forestland tenure instrument, protected-area permit, NCIP certification,
 7   FPIC, CLUP, zoning ordinance, local business permit, or other permit, license,
 8   or clearance that is under the legal jurisdiction of the DENR, Environmental
 9   Management Bureau (EMB), other agencies, or local government units. It
10   shall not exercise customary governance or determine ownership of ancestral
11   domains. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish, modify, or
12   supplant the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) System and the
13   functions of the Environmental Impact Committee (EIC) in the evaluation of
14   projects requiring an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC). Likewise,
15   it shall not override LGU autonomy, except to the extent necessary to enforce
16   consistency with national environmental laws and the Sierra Madre Master
17   Plan. Instead, the Authority’s role is to harmonize policies, set scientific
18   standards, integrate plans, provide technical and scientific inputs, where
19   appropriate, to the EIA process, and coordinate enforcement across
20   jurisdictions, thereby enhancing a unified, ecosystem-based management of
21   the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

24   created by this Act, including adoption and enforcement of the Sierra Madre
25   Master Plan and ecological floors; issuance of Sierra Madre Master Plan
26   Conformity Certificates; operational ecosystem and sector-dependency
27   accounting; nature-market methodologies and safeguards; project and
28   developer registration; issuance and retirement of Sierra Madre Nature
29   Credits; assessment of Nature Credit Purchase Obligations; maintenance of
30   the Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry; ecological-loss and restoration
31   assessment; and market-integrity enforcement.

21   of this Act, the Authority shall formulate, adopt, and commence the
22   implementation of a Sierra Madre Master Plan covering a period of twenty-five
23   (25) years. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall serve as the comprehensive,
24   science-based, ecosystem-oriented, and climate-resilient blueprint for the
25   conservation, protection, restoration, and sustainable development of the
26   Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
27
28   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall integrate, among others, land use and
29   development planning; biodiversity conservation; watershed and ridge-to-reef
30   ecosystem management; climate change adaptation and mitigation; disaster
31   risk reduction and management; sustainable livelihoods; infrastructure
32   compatibility; and the traditional ecological knowledge, customary
33   governance systems, and sustainable resource management practices of
34   ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371, otherwise known as the "Indigenous
35   Peoples' Rights Act of 1997." The Authority shall likewise recognize ICCAs and
36   support indigenous co-management approaches consistent with applicable
37   laws.

28   Sec. 24. Landscape Zoning System. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall
29   establish a Landscape Zoning System that operationalizes the statutory
30   classes under this Act and shall be spatially organized into the following broad
31   management groupings: (a) Core Zone; (b) Sustainable Use Zone; (c) Buffer
32   Zone; and (d) Special Management Zone. The zoning system shall guide
33   conservation, protection, restoration, sustainable resource use, settlement,
34   development, and disaster-risk management throughout the Sierra Madre
35   Mountain Range.
36

38   Strict Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones; (2) Ecological Corridor
39   and Connectivity Zones; (3) Watershed and Catchment Protection Zones; (4)
40   Restoration and Recovery Zones; (5) Sustainable Production and Community
41   Stewardship Zones; and (6) Existing Settlement and Compatible Development
42   Zones. The Sierra Madre Master Plan may create subzones only within these
43   classes and for the purposes stated in this Act. The Master Plan shall likewise

 4   Sec. 28. Sierra Madre Environmental Enforcement System (SMEES). An
 5   integrated enforcement system is hereby established for the Sierra Madre,
 6   institutionalizing a whole-of-government, whole-of-society approach. The
 7   SMEES shall coordinate national oversight with regional operations and
 8   community-based actions to prevent, detect, and prosecute environmental
 9   crimes and illegal resource activities in the range.
10
11   Sec. 29. Structure of SMEES. The SMEES shall consist of:
12
13       (a) National Coordinating Council. Chaired by DENR, this council
14           provides strategic command. Members include heads of PNP, AFP,
15           National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), Department of Justice (DOJ),
16           and Bureau of Customs (BOC). The council formulates national
17           enforcement strategies, ensures inter-agency coordination, allocates
18           resources, and issues directives for operations in the Sierra Madre;
19       (b) Regional Enforcement Task Forces. Established in key Sierra
20           Madre regions, each task force includes DENR field officers, PNP,
21           including maritime and environmental units, specialized AFP units,
22           and LGU representatives. These task forces plan and execute joint
23           enforcement raids, intelligence sharing, and rapid responses to
24           violations within their jurisdictions, guided by the council’s
25           strategies; and
26       (c) Local Rapid Response Units (LRRUs). At the barangay or
27           municipality level, community-based teams are organized composed
28           of forest rangers, barangay officials, and trained representatives of
29           Indigenous Peoples. LRRUs act as frontline defenders: monitoring
30           forests, reporting illegal activities, and conducting initial patrols
31           under the oversight of the regional task forces.
32
33   Sec. 30. Anti-Illegal Logging Task Force. A specialized Anti-Illegal Logging
34   Task Force (AILTF) is created under the SMEES. This unit focuses on timber
35   syndicates and trafficking networks. Key activities include: intelligence
36   surveillance to identify culprits; permanent and mobile checkpoints on
37   logging routes; mobile ground patrols and aerial reconnaissance of forests;
38   coordination with courts for expedited search warrants; and maintaining a
39   shared database of offenders, seized logs, and enforcement outcomes.
40   Successful models like interagency aids have shown that coordinated action
41   is essential to stop chainsaw mills before they move stolen timber out of the
42   mountains.

16   Sec. 79. Periodic Congressional Review. This Act shall be subject to a
17   comprehensive review by Congress every five (5) years from its effectivity,
18   based on independently verified evidence, to assess its effectiveness, impact
19   on biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, and governance efficiency.
20
21   The review shall determine whether:
22     (a) The Sierra Madre Master Plan and Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
23         Certificates control covered decisions;
24     (b) Ecological limits and conflict rules are applied consistently by agencies,
25         LGUs, PAMBs, and other institutions;
26     (c) Ecosystem accounts identify service flows, beneficiaries, economic
27         contribution, and public costs;
28     (d) Statutory Nature Credit Purchase Obligations create predictable
29         demand and mobilize private capital;
30     (e) Protection, restoration, and stewardship have become financially and
31         economically more competitive with conversion and extraction;
32     (f) Obligated buyers comply without disproportionate burdens or evasion;
33     (g) Actual damage is restored and environmental costs are recovered;
34     (h) Outcomes are real, additional where required, durable, and free from
35         double counting;
36     (i) Revenues follow tenurial instruments, customary rights, and verified
37         stewardship; and
38     (j) Biodiversity, connectivity, water security, climate resilience, and
39         disaster-risk indicators improve against the baseline. The Authority
40         shall commission an independent evaluation at least six (6) months
41         before each review.
