 AN ACT ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE AND CITIZEN-CENTRIC
 NATIONAL CYBERCRIME AND SCAM PREVENTION AND RESPONSE
  FRAMEWORK, AMENDING RA 10175, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Cybercrime has evolved into a direct threat to public trust, economic stability, and
national security. While existing laws provided an initial foundation, they are no
longer adequate to confront the speed, scale, sophistication, and transnational
character of modern cybercrime. This measure introduces targeted reforms to restore
control over cyberspace and protect the public and the digital economy.

First, it strengthens penal protections against acts that undermine trust in digital
systems, including the malicious refusal to surrender lawfully required data, the
solicitation of cybercrime, and large-scale offenses amounting to economic
sabotage. These provisions recognize that cybercrime today is not merely individual
misconduct, but organized, strategic, and economically destructive.

Second, the measure establishes a stakeholder-centric enforcement framework
through the creation of a National Scam Hub, prioritizing the immediate disruption
of scam operations and the rapid mitigation and recovery of losses via a tightly
integrated, multi-stakeholder response. The co-location of investigators, banks, e-
wallet operators, and relevant agencies enables swift, coordinated action before
formal complaint processing, significantly enhancing victim protection and
investigative efficiency. The measure further liberalizes complaint procedures by
empowering service providers and law enforcement officers to initiate cases when
victims are unable to do so — particularly in cross-border and online contexts where
delay breeds impunity.

Third, it modernizes the legal framework for digital evidence custody and forensic
use, aligning the law with contemporary investigative realities and global best
practices while safeguarding due process.

Fourth, it updates and harmonizes existing laws to reflect present-day technologies
and criminal structures, including recognizing organized cybercrime groups,
expanding the concept of ICT systems to cover IoT and converged platforms, and
adopting operationally relevant definitions that reflect how cybercrime is actually
committed and facilitated.

Finally, the measure creates a cohesive national response architecture,
institutionalizing a National Anti-Cybercrime Strategic Plan, and creating a National
Cybercrime Prevention and Investigation Agency to unify enforcement and
intelligence under a single, accountable authority.

Together, these reforms shift the country from a fragmented, reactive system to a
decisive, coordinated, and technology-driven defense of the public, the economy, and
trust in the digital space.

                                           CHAPTER I
                               PRELIMINARY PROVISION


Section 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Cybercrime Act of 2026″.


Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of information
and communications industries such as content production, telecommunications,
broadcasting electronic commerce, and data processing, in the nation’s overall social
and economic development. The State also recognizes the importance of providing an
environment conducive to the development, acceleration, and rational application and
exploitation of information and communications technology (ICT) to attain free, easy,
and intelligible access to exchange and/or delivery of information; and the need to
protect and safeguard the integrity of computer, computer and communications
systems, networks, and databases, and the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of
information and data stored therein, from all forms of misuse, abuse, and illegal
access by making punishable under the law such conduct or conducts. In this light, the
State shall adopt sufficient powers to effectively prevent and combat such offenses by
facilitating their detection, investigation, and prosecution at both the domestic and
international levels, and by providing arrangements for fast and reliable international
cooperation.


IT IS THE POLICY OF THE STATE TO RECOGNIZE THE INDISPENSABLE
ROLE OF ICT, INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED TO TELECOMMUNICATIONS,
BROADCASTING, ELECTRONIC COMMERCE, CONTENT PRODUCTION,
AND DATA PROCESSING, IN ADVANCING THE NATION’S SOCIAL,
ECONOMIC, POLITICAL, AND CULTURAL DEVELOPMENT. THE STATE
SHALL PROMOTE AN ENVIRONMENT THAT IS CONDUCIVE TO THE
RESPONSIBLE GROWTH, INNOVATION, AND RATIONAL APPLICATION OF
ICT, THEREBY ENSURING FREE, EQUITABLE, AND INTELLIGIBLE ACCESS
TO THE EXCHANGE AND DELIVERY OF INFORMATION FOR ALL.


THE STATE LIKEWISE AFFIRMS THE NECESSITY OF PROTECTING AND
SAFEGUARDING THE CONFIDENTIALITY, INTEGRITY, AVAILABILITY,
AUTHENTICITY, NON​            -REPUDIATION, PRIVACY, AND SECURITY OF
COMPUTER SYSTEMS, COMMUNICATIONS NETWORKS, AND DATABASES.
TO THIS END, THE STATE SHALL PENALIZE ALL FORMS OF MISUSE,
ABUSE, AND ILLEGAL ACCESS THAT UNDERMINE THE TRUST AND
STABILITY OF THE ICT ENVIRONMENT.


THE STATE FURTHER RECOGNIZES THAT THE MISUSE OF ICT AS A TOOL
FOR CRIMINAL ACTIVITY ENABLES OFFENDERS TO OPERATE WITH
UNPRECEDENTED            SPEED,     REACH,       AND     ANONYMITY,          THEREBY
MAGNIFYING THE DESTRUCTIVE IMPACT OF TRADITIONAL CRIMES AND
EMPOWERING          ORGANIZED CRIME             GROUPS OR SYNDICATES                 TO
VICTIMIZE LARGE SEGMENTS OF THE POPULATION SIMULTANEOUSLY.
IN ORDER TO




​
SAFEGUARD PUBLIC ORDER, NATIONAL SECURITY, AND ECONOMIC
STABILITY, THE COMMISSION OF CYBERCRIME OFFENSES AS DEFINED
UNDER THIS ACT SHALL BE DEEMED AN ACT OF ECONOMIC SABOTAGE,
WARRANTING THE SEVEREST PENALTIES ALLOWABLE UNDER LAW TO
ENSURE DETERRENCE AND ACCOUNTABILITY.


IN PURSUIT OF THIS POLICY, THE STATE SHALL ADOPT AND EXERCISE
SUFFICIENT POWERS TO EFFECTIVELY PREVENT, DETECT, INVESTIGATE,
AND PROSECUTE CYBERCRIME OFFENSES, WHETHER COMMITTED
DOMESTICALLY OR TRANSNATIONALLY, AND SHALL PROVIDE FOR
FAST, RELIABLE, AND SECURE MECHANISMS OF INTERNATIONAL
COOPERATION, CONSISTENT AT ALL TIMES WITH THE CONSTITUTIONAL
SAFEGUARDS OF DUE PROCESS, THE RIGHT TO PRIVACY, AND
PROTECTION AGAINST UNREASONABLE SEARCHES AND SEIZURES.


Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined as follows:


(a)​   Access refers to the instruction, communication with, storing data in,
retrieving data from, or otherwise making use of any resources of a computer system
or communication network


(b)​   Alteration refers to the modification or change, in form or substance, of an
existing computer data or program.


(c)​   Communication refers to the transmission of information through ICT media,
including voice, video and other forms of data.


(d)​   Computer refers to an electronic, magnetic, optical, electrochemical, or other
data processing or communications device, or grouping of such devices, capable of
performing logical, arithmetic, routing, or storage functions and which includes any
storage facility or equipment or communications facility or equipment directly related
to or operating in conjunction with such device. It covers any type of computer device

cause a computer system to perform a function and includes electronic documents
and/or electronic data messages whether stored in local computer systems or online.


(f)​    Computer program refers to a set of instructions executed by the computer to
achieve intended results.


(g)​    Computer system refers to any device or group of interconnected or related
devices, one or more of which, pursuant to a program, performs automated processing
of data. It covers any type of device with data processing capabilities including, but
not limited to, computers and mobile phones. The device consisting of hardware and

                                      CHAPTER II
                                 PUNISHABLE ACTS
Section 4. Cybercrime Offenses. – The following acts constitute the offense of
cybercrime punishable under this Act:


(a)​   Offenses       against      the     confidentiality,     integrity,     availability,
NON-REPUDIATION,           AUTHENTICATION,            PRIVACY, AND SAFETY                of
computer data and systems:


       (1)​    Illegal Access. – The access to the whole or any part of AN ICT
       system without right. ILLEGAL ACCESS SHALL INCLUDE THE
       INFRINGEMENT OF SECURITY MEASURES WITH THE INTENT OF
       OBTAINING ELECTRONIC DATA WITHOUT RIGHT FROM THE
       OWNER OR OTHER DISHONEST OR CRIMINAL INTENT OR IN
       RELATION TO AN ICT SYSTEM THAT IS CONNECTED TO ANOTHER
       ICT SYSTEM.
       (2)​    Illegal Interception. – INTENTIONALLY AND WITHOUT RIGHT,
       the interception, made by technical means, of non-public transmission of
       ELECTRONIC DATA TO, from or within AN ICT system.
       (3)​    Data Interference. — The intentional alteration, INPUTTING,
       damaging, deletion or deterioration of computer data, electronic document, or
       electronic data message, without right, including the introduction or




​
    transmission of MALWARE, REGARDLESS OF WHO CONTROLS OR
    OWNS THE COMPUTER DATA.
    (4)​   System Interference. — The intentional alteration, INTERFERENCE
    IN WHOLE OR IN PART, of the functioning of a AN ICT TECHNOLOGY
    SYSTEM by inputting, transmitting, damaging, deleting, deteriorating,
    altering or suppressing without right or authority, including the introduction or
    transmission of MALWARE TO THE ICT SYSTEM.


    (5)​   Misuse of Devices. –
           (i)​    The    use,    production,   sale,   procurement,    importation,
           distribution, or otherwise making available, without right, of:
                   (aa) A device OR ANY HARDWARE COMPONENT,
                   including a computer program, designed or adapted primarily
                   for the purpose of committing any of the offenses under this
                   Act; or
                   (bb) A computer password, SYSTEM AND NETWORK
                   CREDENTIALS, access code, APPLICATION, MALWARE or
                   similar data, or ANY HARDWARE COMPONENT by which
                   the whole or any part of a computer system is capable of being
                   accessed with THE intent that it be used for the purpose of
                   committing any of the offenses under this Act.


           (ii)​   The MERE possession of an item referred to in paragraphs
           5(i)(aa) or (bb) above EITHER USED IN, OR with intent to use said
           devices for the purpose of committing any of the offenses under this
           section: PROVIDED, THAT, THERE IS A PRESUMPTION AND
           PRIMA FACIE EVIDENCE OF INTENT TO USE SAID DEVICES
           BY MERE POSSESSION, CONTROL, OR CUSTODY BY A
           NATURAL OR JURIDICAL PERSON WHOSE BUSINESS OR
           EMPLOYMENT DOES NOT LAWFULLY DEAL WITH THE USE,
           PRODUCTION,               MANUFACTURE,                PROCUREMENT,
           IMPORTATION, OR DISTRIBUTION THEREOF, OR NOT IN THE
           ORDINARY COURSE OF THE POSSESSOR’S TRADE OR
           BUSINESS.

       (6)​   Cyber-squatting. – The acquisition of a domain name over the internet

              (i)​     Similar, identical, or confusingly similar to an existing

              (ii)​    Identical or in any way similar with the name of a person other

       COMPUTER SYSTEM. – ANY PERSON WHO MALICIOUSLY AND

       THE FORM OF PERSONAL INFORMATION, SENSITIVE PERSONAL

              (c)​     THE COMMISSION OF ARTICLES 315, 316, 317 AND 318

          CYBERSEX-RELATED SCAM OPERATIONS SHALL BE LIABLE AS A

(2)​      Libel. — The unlawful or prohibited acts of libel as defined in Article 355 of

other similar means which may be devised in the future.


Section 5. Other Offenses. — The following acts shall also constitute an offense:


(a)​      Aiding or Abetting in the Commission of Cybercrime. – Any person who
willfully abets or aids in the commission of, OR INFLUENCES OR DECEIVES
ANOTHER TO COMMIT any of the offenses enumerated in this Act shall be held
liable.


(b)​      Attempt in the Commission of Cybercrime. — Any person who willfully
attempts to commit any of the offenses enumerated in this Act shall be held liable.




​
(c)​   SOLICITATION TO COMMIT CYBERCRIME. – ANY PERSON WHO
WILFULLY PROPOSES, SOLICITS, OR INDUCES ANOTHER PERSON TO
COMMIT ANY OF THE OFFENSES UNDER THIS ACT, SHALL BE HELD
LIABLE.


SECTION 6. USE OF ICT FOR ALL OTHER CRIMES AND IMPOSABLE
PENALTY. – ALL CRIMES, NOT MENTIONED ABOVE, DEFINED AND
PENALIZED BY THE REVISED PENAL CODE, AS AMENDED, AND SPECIAL
LAWS, IF COMMITTED BY, THROUGH AND WITH THE USE OF ICTS SHALL
BE COVERED BY THE RELEVANT PROVISIONS OF THIS ACT: PROVIDED,
THAT THE PENALTY TO BE IMPOSED SHALL BE ONE (1) DEGREE HIGHER
THAN THAT PROVIDED FOR BY THE REVISED PENAL CODE, AS
AMENDED, AND SPECIAL LAWS, AS THE CASE MAY BE: PROVIDED,
FURTHER, THAT THE PROSECUTION OF THE OFFENSES UNDER THIS ACT
MAY BE INITIATED BY ANY OFFENDED PARTY OR ANY AFFECTED
SERVICE PROVIDER.


SECTION 7. CYBERCRIMES CONSTITUTING ECONOMIC SABOTAGE. –
ANY PERSON WHO COMMITS CYBERCRIME WHEN SUCH ACTS RESULT
IN SERIOUS ECONOMIC HARM EXCEEDING ONE HUNDRED MILLION
PESOS (PHP 100,000,000.00) OR WHEN THEY TARGET OR DISRUPT
CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE SHALL BE DEEMED TO HAVE COMMITTED
AN ACT OF ECONOMIC SABOTAGE.


ECONOMIC     SABOTAGE   SHALL   ALSO   BE   APPRECIATED   IF   THE
CYBERCRIME IS COMMITTED UNDER ANY OF THE FOLLOWING
CIRCUMSTANCES:


       1.​   BY A GROUP OF THREE (3) OR MORE PERSONS CONSPIRING
       OR CONFEDERATING WITH ONE ANOTHER (ORGANIZED CRIME
       GROUP);
       2.​   AGAINST THREE (3) OR MORE PERSONS, INDIVIDUALLY OR
       COLLECTIVELY;




​
       3.​    USING A MASS MAILER, AUTOMATED SERVICE, SOFTWARE,
       OR ANY OTHER SCALABLE DELIVERY METHOD, INCLUDING
       SOCIAL ENGINEERING OR SIMILAR FRAUDULENT SCHEMES; OR
       4.​    THROUGH HUMAN TRAFFICKING.


Section 8. Liability under Other Laws. — A prosecution under this Act shall be
without prejudice to any liability for violation of any provision of the Revised Penal
Code, as amended, or special laws.


                                     CHAPTER III
                                     PENALTIES


SECTION 9. PENALTIES. — Any person found guilty of any of the punishable acts
enumerated in Sections 4(a) and 4(b) of this Act shall be punished with imprisonment
of prision mayor or a fine of at least Two hundred thousand pesos (Php 200,000.00)
up to a maximum amount commensurate to the damage incurred, or both.


Any person found guilty of the punishable act under Section 4(a)(5) shall be punished
with imprisonment of prision mayor or a fine of not more than Five hundred thousand
pesos (Php 500,000.00), or both.


ANY PERSON FOUND GUILTY OF ANY OF THE PUNISHABLE ACTS
ENUMERATED IN SECTION 4(C)(1) OF THIS ACT SHALL BE PUNISHED
WITH IMPRISONMENT OF PRISON MAYOR OR A FINE OF AT LEAST TWO
HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (PHP200,000.00) BUT NOT EXCEEDING ONE
MILLION PESOS (PHP1,000,000.00) OR BOTH.


Any person found guilty of any of the punishable acts enumerated in Section 4(c)(2)
of this Act, COMMITTED THROUGH A COMPUTER SYSTEM, shall be punished
with the penalty one (1) degree higher than that provided for in the RA 11930, ALSO
KNOWN AS THE ANTI-ONLINE SEXUAL ABUSE OR EXPLOITATION OF
CHILDREN (OSAEC) AND ANTI-CHILD SEXUAL ABUSE OR EXPLOITATION
MATERIALS (CSAEM) ACT.




​
Any person found guilty of any of the punishable acts enumerated in Section 5 shall
be punished with imprisonment one (1) degree lower than that of the prescribed
penalty for the offense or a fine of at least One hundred thousand pesos (Php
100,000.00) but not exceeding Five hundred thousand pesos (Php 500,000.00) or
both.


ACTS OF ECONOMIC SABOTAGE AS SET OUT IN SECTION 7 SHALL BE
PUNISHABLE BY LIFE IMPRISONMENT AND A FINE TWICE THE VALUE
OR THE TOTAL HARM OR LOSSES INVOLVED, WITHOUT PREJUDICE TO
PROSECUTION UNDER OTHER APPLICABLE LAWS.


Section 10. Corporate Liability. — When any of the punishable acts herein defined
are committed on behalf of or for the benefit of a juridical person, by a natural person
acting either individually or as part of an organ of the juridical person, who has a
leading position within, based on: (a) a power of representation of the juridical person
provided the act committed falls within the scope of such authority; (b) an authority to
take decisions on behalf of the juridical person: Provided, That the act committed falls
within the scope of such authority; or (c) an authority to exercise control within the
juridical person, the juridical person shall be held liable for a fine equivalent to at
least double the fines imposable UP TO A MAXIMUM OF FIFTEEN MILLION
PESOS (PHP 15,000,000.00).


If the commission of any of the punishable acts herein defined was made possible due
to the lack of supervision or control by a natural person referred to and described in
the preceding paragraph, for the benefit of that juridical person by a natural person
acting under its authority, the juridical person shall be held liable for a fine equivalent
to at least double the fines imposable in SECTION 8 up to a maximum of TEN
MILLION PESOS (PHP10,000,000.00).


The liability imposed on the juridical person shall be without prejudice to the criminal
liability of the natural person who has committed the offense.

BANKS, TELECOMMUNICATIONS COMPANIES, ONLINE PLATFORMS,
FINANCIAL   TECHNOLOGY COMPANIES, OPERATORS OF PAYMENT
SYSTEMS,    AND   OTHER    STAKEHOLDERS. THESE    STAKEHOLDERS,
FOLLOWING BEST STANDARDS AND PRACTICES, SHALL CO-LOCATE IN
THE NASH FACILITY TO RENDER THE ACTIONS AND THEIR RESULTS
IMMEDIATE AND MORE EFFECTIVE.


THE NACC MAY ISSUE THE NECESSARY CIRCULARS OR ORDERS TO
OPERATIONALIZE THE NASH TAKING INTO ACCOUNT GLOBAL BEST
PRACTICES   WITHIN   THE    FRAMEWORK   OF   RA   12010,   OR THE
ANTI-FINANCIAL ACCOUNT AND SCAMMING ACT.


Section 18. REGULATORY AUTHORITY OVER SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS
AND OVER-THE-TOP APPLICATIONS TO PREVENT AND SUPPRESS
CYBERCRIME. — THE NACC IS HEREBY AUTHORIZED AND EMPOWERED
TO PROMULGATE RULES, REGULATIONS, AND STANDARDS GOVERNING
THE OPERATIONS OF SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS, OVER-THE-TOP (OTT)
APPLICATIONS, AND OTHER DIGITAL INTERMEDIARIES OPERATING IN
THE PHILIPPINES, FOR THE PURPOSE OF PREVENTING, DETECTING,
DISRUPTING, AND SUPPRESSING CYBERCRIME, INCLUDING ONLINE
SCAMS, FRAUD, IDENTITY THEFT, AND OTHER CYBER-ENABLED
OFFENSES.




​
IN THE EXERCISE OF THIS AUTHORITY, THE NACC SHALL BE GUIDED BY
THE FOLLOWING STANDARDS:


     1.​   PUBLIC PROTECTION AND DIGITAL TRUST. — RULES SHALL
     BE DIRECTED TOWARD THE PROTECTION OF THE PUBLIC, THE
     PRESERVATION OF TRUST IN DIGITAL SYSTEMS, AND THE
     INTEGRITY OF THE DIGITAL ECONOMY.
     2.​   RISK-BASED    AND   PROPORTIONATE      REGULATION.    —
     REGULATORY        MEASURES   SHALL      BE      PROPORTIONATE,
     TECHNOLOGY-NEUTRAL,       AND     RISK-BASED,    TAKING   INTO
     ACCOUNT THE SIZE, REACH, AND FUNCTIONAL ROLE OF THE
     PLATFORM OR APPLICATION IN ENABLING OR MITIGATING
     CYBERCRIME.
     3.​   PREVENTION, DISRUPTION, AND RAPID RESPONSE. —
     REGULATIONS SHALL PRIORITIZE EARLY DETECTION, SWIFT
     DISRUPTION, AND TIMELY RESPONSE TO CYBERCRIME THREATS,
     INCLUDING OBLIGATIONS RELATING TO NOTICE-AND-ACTION
     MECHANISMS, CONTENT MODERATION FOR ILLEGAL ACTIVITIES,
     AND COOPERATION IN EMERGENCY TAKEDOWN OR BLOCKING
     PROCEDURES.
     4.​   ACCOUNTABILITY AND COOPERATION. — SOCIAL MEDIA
     PLATFORMS AND OTT APPLICATIONS SHALL BE REQUIRED TO
     MAINTAIN CLEAR POINTS OF CONTACT, COOPERATE WITH
     LAWFUL REQUESTS OF COMPETENT AUTHORITIES, PRESERVE
     RELEVANT DATA SUBJECT TO DUE PROCESS, AND IMPLEMENT
     REASONABLE SAFEGUARDS AGAINST THE MISUSE OF THEIR
     SERVICES FOR CRIMINAL ACTIVITY.
     5.​   DUE   PROCESS AND FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS. — ALL
     REGULATORY MEASURES SHALL RESPECT CONSTITUTIONAL
     RIGHTS, INCLUDING FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION, PRIVACY, AND
     DUE    PROCESS,     AND   SHALL     ENSURE      TRANSPARENCY,
     ACCOUNTABILITY, AND AVENUES FOR REDRESS.




​
     6.​    INTER-AGENCY          COORDINATION          AND   INTERNATIONAL
     ALIGNMENT.        —     RULES        SHALL     BE       FORMULATED       IN
     COORDINATION WITH RELEVANT GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND,
     WHERE        APPROPRIATE,       ALIGNED        WITH      INTERNATIONAL
     STANDARDS, TREATIES, AND BEST PRACTICES TO WHICH THE
     PHILIPPINES IS A PARTY.
     7.​    CONSULTATIVE AND ADAPTIVE GOVERNANCE. — THE
     NACC      SHALL       ADOPT    A     CONSULTATIVE         APPROACH       IN
     RULE-MAKING, ENGAGING STAKEHOLDERS FROM GOVERNMENT,
     INDUSTRY, CIVIL SOCIETY, AND THE TECHNICAL COMMUNITY,
     AND SHALL PERIODICALLY REVIEW AND UPDATE REGULATIONS
     IN LIGHT OF EVOLVING TECHNOLOGIES AND CYBERCRIME
     TRENDS.


RULES PROMULGATED UNDER THIS SECTION SHALL BE WITHOUT
PREJUDICE    TO     EXISTING       REGULATORY           POWERS    OF    OTHER
GOVERNMENT        AGENCIES     AND      SHALL      BE    HARMONIZED         WITH
APPLICABLE     LAWS,       INCLUDING       THOSE        ON    DATA     PRIVACY,
TELECOMMUNICATIONS, COMPETITION, AND ELECTRONIC COMMERCE.


SECTION 19. INITIATING THE COMPLAINT. – NOTWITHSTANDING ANY
EXISTING LAWS AND PROCEDURES, IN VIEW OF THE NATURE OF
CYBERCRIME, COMPLAINTS ON ANY OFFENSE PUNISHABLE UNDER
THIS ACT MAY BE FILED BY THE FOLLOWING:


     (1)​   PRIVATE COMPLAINANT; OR
     (2)​   ANY LAW ENFORCEMENT OFFICER IN CASES WHERE THE
     PRIVATE      COMPLAINANT        IS    LOCATED        OUTSIDE      OF    THE
     PHILIPPINES     AND     IS    UNABLE     TO    APPEAR       BEFORE     THE
     PROSECUTOR’S OFFICE OR THE COURT.


PRIVATE COMPLAINANT INCLUDES ALL THE SERVICE PROVIDERS AS
DEFINED IN SECTION 3(r), AS AMENDED, INCLUDING THOSE PROVIDING
BUSINESS PROCESS OUTSOURCING AND SIMILAR SERVICES. FILING




​
MADE UNDER THIS SUBSECTION (1) SHALL NOT REQUIRE ANY
PREREQUISITE ACTION INCLUDING THAT THE SAME BE PRECEDED BY A
REPORT OR COMPLAINT FILED WITH A LAW ENFORCEMENT AGENCY.


Section 20. Real-Time Collection of Traffic Data. — Law enforcement authorities,
with due cause, UPON SECURING A COURT WARRANT, MAY shall be
authorized to collect or record by technical or electronic means traffic data in
real-time associated with specified communications transmitted by means of a
computer system.


Traffic data refer only to the communication’s origin, destination, route, time, date,
size, duration, or type of underlying service, but not content, nor identities.


All other data to be collected or seized or disclosed require a court warrant.


Service providers are required to cooperate and assist law enforcement authorities in
the collection or recording of the above-stated information.


The court warrant required under this section shall only be issued or granted upon
written application and the examination under oath or affirmation of the applicant and
the witnesses he may produce and the showing: (1) that there are reasonable grounds
to believe that any of the crimes enumerated hereinabove has been committed, or is
being committed, or is about to be committed: (2) that there are reasonable grounds


to believe that evidence that will be obtained is essential to the conviction of any
person for, or to the solution of, or to the prevention of, any such crimes; and (3) that
there are no other means readily available for obtaining such evidence.


Section 21. Preservation of Computer Data. — The integrity of traffic data and
subscriber information relating to communication services provided by a service
provider shall be preserved for a minimum period of six (6) months from the date of
the transaction. Content data shall be similarly preserved for six (6) months from the
date of receipt of the order from law enforcement authorities requiring its
preservation.




​
Law enforcement authorities may order a one-time extension for another six (6)
months: Provided, That once computer data preserved, transmitted or stored by a
service provider is used as evidence in a case, the mere furnishing to such service
provider of the transmittal document to the Office of the Prosecutor shall be deemed a
notification to preserve the computer data until the termination of the case.


The service provider ordered to preserve computer data shall keep confidential the
order and its compliance.


Section 22. Disclosure of Computer Data. — Law enforcement authorities,
THROUGH A SUBPOENA OR upon securing a court warrant, shall issue an order
requiring any person or service provider, OR INTERNET INTERMEDIARIES,
INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED TO SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS,
MESSAGING CHANNELS, OR WEBSITES, to disclose or submit subscriber’s
information, traffic data, CONTENT DATA, or relevant data in his/its possession or
control within seventy- two (72) hours from receipt of the order in relation to a valid
complaint officially docketed and assigned for investigation BEFORE THE LAW
ENFORCEMENT AGENCY OR AUTHORITY and the disclosure is necessary and
relevant for the purpose of investigation.


Section 23. Search, Seizure and Examination of Computer Data. — Where a search
and seizure warrant is properly issued, the law enforcement authorities shall likewise
have the following powers and duties:


       Within the time period specified in the warrant, to conduct interception, as
       defined in this Act, and:


       (a)​    To secure a computer system or a computer data storage medium;
       (b)​    To make and retain a copy of those computer data secured;
       (c)​    To maintain the integrity of the relevant stored computer data;
       (d)​    To conduct forensic analysis or examination of the computer data
       storage medium; and
       (e)​    To render inaccessible or remove those computer data in the accessed
       computer or computer and communications network.




​
Pursuant thereof, the law enforcement authorities may order any person who has
knowledge about the functioning of the computer system and the measures to protect
and preserve the computer data therein to provide, as is reasonable, the necessary
information, to enable the undertaking of the search, seizure and examination.


Law enforcement authorities may request for an extension of time to complete the
examination of the computer data storage medium and to make a return thereon but in
no case for a period longer than thirty (30) days from date of approval by the court.


SECTION 24. APPLICATION OF CYBERCRIME WARRANTS. – The cybercrime
warrants required under this Act shall only be issued or granted upon written
application and the examination under oath or affirmation of the applicant and the
witnesses he may produce and the showing: (1) that there are reasonable grounds to
believe that any of the crimes enumerated hereinabove has been committed, or is
being committed, or is about to be committed: (2) that there are reasonable grounds to
believe that evidence that will be obtained is essential to the conviction of any person
for, or to the solution of, or to the prevention of, any such crimes; and (3) that there
are no other means readily available for obtaining such evidence.


SECTION 25. CUSTODY, PRESERVATION, AND STORAGE OF COMPUTER
DATA. — ALL COMPUTER DATA, INCLUDING CONTENT AND TRAFFIC
DATA, LAWFULLY OBTAINED, EXAMINED, OR SEIZED PURSUANT TO A
VALID WARRANT SHALL, WITHIN FORTY-EIGHT (48) HOURS AFTER THE
EXPIRATION OF THE PERIOD FIXED THEREIN, BE SECURELY PRESERVED
AND     DEPOSITED       WITH THE COURT              OR, WHERE         TECHNICALLY
NECESSARY,        STORED       IN   AN     ACCREDITED         DIGITAL      EVIDENCE
REPOSITORY UNDER THE CONTROL OF THE COURT OR A DESIGNATED
JUDICIAL CUSTODIAN, IN A MANNER THAT ENSURES THE INTEGRITY,
AUTHENTICITY, CONFIDENTIALITY, AND ADMISSIBILITY OF SUCH DATA.


THE DEPOSIT SHALL BE ACCOMPANIED BY A SWORN AFFIDAVIT OF THE
LAW ENFORCEMENT AUTHORITY EXECUTING THE WARRANT, STATING
THE DATES AND TIMES COVERED BY THE EXAMINATION, THE IDENTITY




​
OF THE OFFICERS WHO ACCESSED OR HANDLED THE DATA, THE
METHODS OF EXTRACTION AND PRESERVATION EMPLOYED, THE HASH
VALUES OR OTHER INTEGRITY VERIFICATION MEASURES USED, AND
SUCH OTHER RELEVANT INFORMATION AS MAY BE REQUIRED TO
ESTABLISH AN UNBROKEN CHAIN OF CUSTODY.


WHERE THE VOLUME, FORMAT, OR TECHNICAL NATURE OF THE
COMPUTER DATA MAKES PHYSICAL SUBMISSION IMPRACTICABLE, THE
COURT       MAY      AUTHORIZE         SECURE        ELECTRONIC         SUBMISSION,
ENCRYPTED STORAGE, OR RETENTION IN A CERTIFIED DIGITAL
FORENSIC FACILITY, SUBJECT TO STRICT ACCESS CONTROLS, AUDIT
LOGS, AND INTEGRITY-PRESERVATION MECHANISMS IN ACCORDANCE
WITH INTERNATIONALLY ACCEPTED DIGITAL EVIDENCE HANDLING
STANDARDS.


LAW ENFORCEMENT AUTHORITIES SHALL BE AUTHORIZED TO RETAIN
AND USE FORENSIC COPIES OF THE COMPUTER DATA STRICTLY FOR
PURPOSES OF INVESTIGATION, PROSECUTION, COURT PROCEEDINGS,
AND OFFICIAL RECORD-KEEPING, PROVIDED THAT SUCH COPIES
REMAIN       CONFIDENTIAL,         ACCESS-RESTRICTED,            AND      PROTECTED
AGAINST ALTERATION, DELETION, OR UNAUTHORIZED DISCLOSURE.
ANY     ACCESS,      DUPLICATION, ANALYSIS, OR TRANSFER OF THE
COMPUTER DATA SHALL BE PROPERLY DOCUMENTED AND SUBJECT TO
JUDICIAL OVERSIGHT TO ENSURE THE CONTINUED INTEGRITY AND
EVIDENTIARY VALUE OF THE DIGITAL EVIDENCE.


Section 26. Exclusionary Rule. — Any evidence procured without a valid warrant or
beyond the authority of the same shall be inadmissible for any proceeding before any
court or tribunal.


Section 23. Restricting or Blocking Access to Computer Data. — When a computer
data is prima facie found to be in violation of the provisions of this Act, the DOJ shall
issue an order to restrict or block access to such computer data..




​
SECTION 27. CONFIDENTIALITY IN HANDLING THIRD-PARTY DATA OR
INFORMATION           DURING         CYBERCRIME           INVESTIGATIONS            AND
PROCEEDINGS.         –   AN     ENTITY      ENGAGED         IN THE COLLECTION,
PROCESSING, HANDLING, STORAGE, OR TRANSMISSION OF PERSONAL,
SENSITIVE, AND PROPRIETARY INFORMATION BELONGING TO A THIRD
PARTY,     INCLUDING        BUT     NOT     LIMITED TO         BUSINESS PROCESS
OUTSOURCING (BPO) COMPANIES AND INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY (IT)
SERVICE PROVIDERS, SHALL OBSERVE STRICT CONFIDENTIALITY OVER
SUCH INFORMATION, EVEN IN THE EVENT THAT SUCH DATA BECOMES
RELEVANT TO A CYBERCRIME INVESTIGATION OR LEGAL PROCEEDING.


Section 28. Non-compliance. — Failure to comply with the provisions of Chapter IV
hereof, specifically the orders from law enforcement authorities, shall be punished
with imprisonment of prision correccional in its maximum period or a fine of THREE
HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (PHP300,00.00) or both, for each and every non-
compliance with an order issued by law enforcement authorities WITHOUT
PREJUDICE TO THE PROVISIONS OF PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 1829.


                                     CHAPTER V
                                   JURISDICTION


Section 29. Jurisdiction. — The Regional Trial Court shall have jurisdiction over any
violation of the provisions of this Act, including any violation committed by a
Filipino national, regardless of the place of commission. Jurisdiction shall lie if any of
the elements was committed within the Philippines or committed with the use of any
computer system wholly or partly situated in the country, or when by such
commission any damage is caused to a natural or juridical person who, at the time the
offense was committed, was in the Philippines.




There shall be designated special cybercrime courts manned by specially trained
judges to handle cybercrime cases.

Section 30. General Principles Relating to International Cooperation. — All
relevant international instruments on international cooperation in criminal matters,
arrangements agreed on the basis of uniform or reciprocal legislation, and domestic
laws, to the widest extent possible for the purposes of investigations or proceedings
concerning criminal offenses related to computer systems and data, or for the
collection of evidence in electronic form of a criminal, offense shall be given full
force and effect.

Section 31. Department of Justice (DOJ). — There is hereby created an Office of
Cybercrime within the DOJ designated as the central authority in all matters related to
international mutual LEGAL assistance and extradition.

SECTION 32. COOPERATION WITH FOREIGN LAW ENFORCEMENT
AGENCIES IN CYBERCRIME INVESTIGATION. — RECOGNIZING THE

COORDINATION WITH PNP AND NBI, SHALL ENDEAVOR TO ESTABLISH
COOPERATION ARRANGEMENTS WITH FOREIGN LAW ENFORCEMENT
AGENCIES FOR FASTER EXCHANGE OF INFORMATION, INTELLIGENCE,
BEST PRACTICES, AND JOINT INVESTIGATIONS ON VIOLATIONS OF THIS
ACT.

    NCPIA’S ORGANIZATIONAL FRAMEWORK AND FINAL PROVISIONS


SECTION 33. REGIONAL OFFICES OF NCPIA. – THE NCPIA MAY
ESTABLISH, OPERATE, AND MAINTAIN A REGIONAL OFFICE IN THE
DIFFERENT ADMINISTRATIVE REGIONS OF THE COUNTRY AS THE NEED
ARISES, SUBJECT TO APPROVAL OF NACC AND CONCURRENCE OF
DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT (DBM). EACH REGIONAL
OFFICE SHALL BE HEADED BY A REGIONAL DIRECTOR, WHO MAY BE
ASSISTED BY ONE (1) ASSISTANT REGIONAL DIRECTOR. THE REGIONAL
DIRECTOR AND ASSISTANT REGIONAL DIRECTOR SHALL BE APPOINTED
BY THE PRESIDENT. THE REGIONAL OFFICES SHALL ENSURE THE




​
PERFORMANCE OF THE FUNCTIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE
NCPIA IN THEIR RESPECTIVE ADMINISTRATIVE REGIONS.

SECTION 34. COMPENSATION AND OTHER EMOLUMENTS FOR NCPIA
OFFICIALS AND PERSONNEL. – TO ENSURE THAT THE NCPIA WILL
ATTRACT THE NECESSARY EXPERTISE AND TALENTS GIVEN ITS HIGHLY
TECHNICAL MANDATE, THE COMPENSATION AND OTHER EMOLUMENTS
OF ITS OFFICIALS AND PERSONNEL SHALL BE EXEMPTED FROM THE
COVERAGE OF RA 6758, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “SALARY
STANDARDIZATION ACT,” AS AMENDED. FOR THIS PURPOSE, THEIR
SALARIES AND EMOLUMENTS SHALL BE SET BASED ON AN OBJECTIVE
CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM, TAKING INTO CONSIDERATION THE HIGHLY
SPECIALIZED   NATURE   OF,   IMPORTANCE,   AND RESPONSIBILITIES
ATTACHED TO THE RESPECTIVE POSITIONS, AND SHALL BE SUBMITTED
TO THE PRESIDENT OF THE PHILIPPINES FOR HIS APPROVAL.

SECTION 35. ABOLITION OF CICC AND TRANSFER OF PERSONNEL. –
THE CICC IS HEREBY ABOLISHED, ITS APPLICABLE FUNDS AND
APPROPRIATIONS, RECORDS, EQUIPMENT, PROPERTY, AND PERSONNEL
ARE TRANSFERRED TO THE NCPIA.

SECTION 36. SEPARATION AND RETIREMENT FROM SERVICE. –
EMPLOYEES WHO ARE SEPARATED FROM SERVICE WITHIN SIX (6)
MONTHS FROM THE EFFECTIVITY OF THIS ACT AS A RESULT OF THE
ABOLITION OF CICC UNDER THE PROVISIONS OF THIS ACT SHALL
RECEIVE SEPARATION BENEFITS TO WHICH THEY MAY BE ENTITLED.

SECTION 37. STRUCTURE AND STAFFING PATTERN. – SUBJECT TO THE
APPROVAL OF THE DBM AND THE OFFICE OF THE PRESIDENT, THE
NCPIA SHALL DETERMINE ITS ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE AND
CREATE NEW DIVISIONS OR UNITS AS IT MAY DEEM NECESSARY, AND
SHALL APPOINT OFFICERS AND EMPLOYEES OF THE NCPIA IN
ACCORDANCE     WITH    THE   CIVIL   SERVICE   LAW,   RULES,   AND
REGULATIONS. THE NAME OF THE POSITIONS AND DESCRIPTION OF
QUALIFICATION STANDARDS RELATING TO THE NEW TABLE OF




​
ORGANIZATION SHALL TAKE INTO ACCOUNT THE TECHNICAL NATURE
THEREOF INCLUDING THE REASONABLE COMPENSATION LEVEL AS
PREVAILING IN THE SECTOR, AMONG OTHERS.

SECTION 38. TRANSITION PERIOD. – THE TRANSFER OF ASSETS, FUNDS,
EQUIPMENT, PROPERTIES, TRANSACTIONS, AND PERSONNEL OF CICC,
AND THE FORMULATION OF THE INTERNAL ORGANIC STRUCTURE,
STAFFING PATTERN, OPERATING SYSTEM, AND REVISED BUDGET OF
NCPIA, SHALL BE COMPLETED WITHIN TWO (2) YEARS FROM THE
EFFECTIVITY OF THIS ACT, DURING WHICH TIME, THE EXISTING
PERSONNEL SHALL CONTINUE TO ASSUME THEIR POSTS IN HOLDOVER
CAPACITIES UNTIL NEW APPOINTMENTS ARE ISSUED: PROVIDED, THAT
AFTER THE ABOLITION OF CICC AS SPECIFIED IN SECTION 34 OF THIS
ACT, THE NCPIA, IN COORDINATION WITH THE DBM, SHALL DETERMINE
AND CREATE NEW POSITIONS AS PRESCRIBED IN SECTION 36 OF THIS
ACT: PROVIDED, FURTHER, THAT REFERENCE TO NCPIA, PARTICULARLY
IN THE FORMULATION OF IRR, SHALL REFER TO THE HOLD-OVER
OFFICIALS FROM CICC.

Section 39. Appropriations. — THE AMOUNT OF AT LEAST NINE HUNDRED
EIGHTY MILLION PESOS (PHP 980,000,000.00) SHALL BE APPROPRIATED
ANNUALLY FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS ACT.

THEREAFTER, SUCH AMOUNTS NECESSARY FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION
OF THIS ACT SHALL BE INCLUDED IN THE ANNUAL GENERAL
APPROPRIATIONS ACT (GAA).

SECTION 40. ANNUAL REPORT. – THE NCPIA, THROUGH NACC, SHALL
SUBMIT TO THE OFFICE OF THE PRESIDENT, THE SENATE AND THE
HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES, WITHIN THE FIRST QUARTER OF THE
SUCCEEDING YEAR, AN ANNUAL REPORT RELATING TO THE PROGRESS
OF THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS ACT.


SECTION 41. IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS. — THE DICT,
NCPIA, NTC, PNP, NBI, BSP, DTI, DOJ-OOC AND AMLC SHALL FORMULATE




​
THE NECESSARY RULES AND REGULATIONS WITHIN NINETY (90) DAYS
FROM APPROVAL OF THIS ACT, FOR ITS EFFECTIVE IMPLEMENTATION.


SECTION 42. MANDATORY REVIEW BY THE REORGANIZATION AND
REVIEW COMMISSION. – FIVE (5) YEARS AFTER THE EFFECTIVITY OF
THIS    ACT, OR AS THE NEED ARISES, THE PRESIDENT SHALL
CONSTITUTE A REORGANIZATION AND REVIEW COMMISSION WHICH
SHALL     CONDUCT       A        SYSTEMATIC       EVALUATION      OF    THE
ACCOMPLISHMENTS AND IMPACT OF THIS ACT, AS WELL AS THE
PERFORMANCE AND ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE OF NCPIA, FOR
PURPOSES     OF   DETERMINING        REMEDIAL      LEGISLATION.    IN   THE
CONDUCT OF THIS SYSTEMATIC EVALUATION, THE COMMISSION SHALL
SPEARHEAD     AND     FACILITATE       THE    HOLDING      OF     IN-DEPTH
CONSULTATIONS WITH GOVERNMENT AGENCIES, LAW ENFORCEMENT
UNITS, INDUSTRY STAKEHOLDERS SUCH AS SERVICE PROVIDERS, ICT
AND BPO SECTORS, CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS, AND OTHER
RELEVANT     PARTNERS       TO    ENSURE     AN    EVIDENCE-BASED       AND
DATA-BACKED ANALYSIS RESPONSIVE TO EVOLVING CYBERCRIME
THREATS. THE EVALUATION REPORT SHALL BE RELEASED AND MADE
AVAILABLE TO STAKEHOLDERS AND THE PUBLIC.

THE COMMISSION SHALL BE COMPOSED OF THE OFFICE OF THE
EXECUTIVE SECRETARY, SECRETARY OF THE DICT, THE DIRECTOR
GENERAL OF NCPIA, DIRECTOR OF THE NBI, CHIEF OF THE PNP, AND
THE SECRETARY OF DBM.

SECTION 43. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE. — IF ANY PROVISION OF THIS ACT
IS HELD INVALID, THE OTHER PROVISIONS NOT AFFECTED SHALL
REMAIN IN FULL FORCE AND EFFECT.

SECTION 44. REPEALING CLAUSE. — ALL LAWS, EXECUTIVE ORDERS,
ISSUANCES,    RULES     AND       REGULATIONS,      OR   PARTS    THEREOF
INCONSISTENT WITH THE PROVISIONS OF THIS ACT ARE REPEALED OR
MODIFIED ACCORDINGLY.
