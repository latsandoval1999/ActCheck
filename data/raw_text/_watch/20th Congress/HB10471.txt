                            AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING THE FILIPINO FAMILY SERVICE CENTER (FFSC) IN ALL
 LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS, PROVIDING AN INTEGRATED, ONE-STOP,
   FAMILY-CENTERED DELIVERY OF HEALTH, SOCIAL PROTECTION,
   EDUCATION, LIVELIHOOD, AND CIVIL REGISTRATION SERVICES,
   APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Access to essential public services remains uneven across the country,
particularly at the level of local government units (LGUs), which serve as the
primary frontline providers of social, health, and community-based services.
Data from national agencies, including the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) and the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), demonstrate both the scale of service delivery efforts and the continuing
gaps in access that necessitate institutional reform.

        On one hand, DSWD data show that millions of Filipinos rely on
government programs, with over 4.5 million beneficiaries served under major
livelihood interventions, and hundreds of thousands of individuals accessing
social services annually. However, ongoing beneficiary identification and
validation efforts indicate that many eligible individuals remain unreached or
underserved, reflecting persistent barriers to access.

        At the local level, DILG data highlight structural disparities in service
delivery capacity among LGUs. As of 2023, only 28.75% of LGUs (493 out of
1,714) passed the Seal of Good Local Governance (SGLG), with the majority
falling short in key service-related areas such as social protection, disaster
preparedness, and financial administration. This indicates that a significant
number of LGUs face systemic challenges that directly affect the availability,
quality, and accessibility of public services.

       Moreover, while efforts toward digitalization are underway, access
remains uneven. Only about 16.9% of barangays (approximately 7,083) have
access to digital service systems such as the Barangay Information Management
System (BIMS), despite over 65% being oriented on such systems. This gap
shows that a large proportion of communities still rely on manual and
fragmented processes, resulting in inefficiencies, delays, and limited reach.

       At the same time, high compliance rates in transparency measures—such
as 97.7% of barangays complying with the Full Disclosure Policy—demonstrate
that LGUs are capable of meeting governance standards when systems are clear,
supported, and institutionalized. This suggests that with the appropriate legal
framework and coordinated support, improvements in service accessibility are
achievable.

       Taken together, these data reveal a critical gap: while government
programs are in place and continue to expand, access to these services remains
inconsistent due to uneven local capacity, limited digital infrastructure, and
fragmented delivery systems.

        The proposed Pamilyang Matatag – Bagong Bayan ng Mamamayan (PM-
BBM) Act is intended to directly address these gaps by strengthening local service
delivery systems through streamlining processes and expanding both physical
and digital access points to ensure that services reach all intended beneficiaries.”
Guided by the vision of “Pamilyang Pilipino, Konektado sa Lahat ng Serbisyo,”
the measure institutionalizes a whole-of-government and whole-of-society
approach to public service.

      By institutionalizing reforms that enhance efficiency, inclusivity, and
responsiveness, the measure aims to ensure that all individuals—particularly
those in underserved and remote communities—can fully benefit from
government programs and services.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING THE FILIPINO FAMILY SERVICE CENTER (FFSC) IN ALL
 LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS, PROVIDING AN INTEGRATED, ONE-STOP,
   FAMILY-CENTERED DELIVERY OF HEALTH, SOCIAL PROTECTION,
   EDUCATION, LIVELIHOOD, AND CIVIL REGISTRATION SERVICES,
   APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Pamilyang Matatag-
Bagong Bayan ng Mamamayan Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to:
    a) Adopt a family-centered approach in the planning and delivery of
    government services;
    b) Ensure accessible, equitable, and integrated service delivery across
    sectors;
    c) Strengthen preventive health care, primary care access, social
    protection, and human development outcomes;
    d) Eliminate fragmentation and inefficiencies in public service delivery
    through convergence mechanisms; and
    e) Empower Local Government Units (LGUs) as frontline implementers of
    holistic family welfare systems.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
      a) Filipino Family Service Center (FFSC) – A centralized, LGU-based, one-
      stop physical hub integrating essential government services for families;

      b) Family Unit – A group of individuals related by blood, marriage,
      adoption, or cohabitation, recognized as a single beneficiary unit for
      purposes of service delivery;

      c) Integrated Services – Coordinated delivery of programs and interventions
      across multiple government sectors, including health, social welfare,
      education, livelihood, and civil registration;

      d) Case Management System – A structured process of assessing,
      planning, coordinating, monitoring, and evaluating services to meet the
      comprehensive needs of a family over time;

      e) Convergence – The alignment and coordination of programs, resources,
      and services across government agencies at the national and local levels.

SECTION 4. Establishment of Filipino Family Service Centers. – All
provinces, cities, and municipalities shall establish at least one (1) Filipino
Family Service Center (FFSC).

Barangays may establish satellite centers or mobile service units, subject to the
capacity and resources of the LGU.

SECTION 5. Scope of Services. – Each FFSC shall provide, at a minimum, the
following integrated services:

   a) Health Services
   • Preventive health screenings, including maternal, newborn, child, and
      non-communicable diseases
   • Immunization and nutrition services
   • Health promotion and counseling
   • Referral and coordination with primary care providers and higher-level
      facilities

   b) Social Protection Services
   • Enrollment and facilitation of social welfare programs
   • Assistance for senior citizens, persons with disabilities, and vulnerable
      sectors
   • Conditional cash transfer and other subsidy program support
   c) Education Support Services
   • Early childhood care and development coordination
   • Alternative learning system (ALS) facilitation
   • Scholarship and student assistance linkage


   d) Livelihood and Employment Services
   • Skills training and capacity-building referrals
   • Job matching and employment facilitation
   • Access to livelihood and microenterprise programs


   e) Civil Registration and Identity Services
   • Assistance in birth, marriage, and death registration
   • Support for national identification and civil documentation
   • Records access and correction facilitation


SECTION 6. Family-Centered Case Management System – Each FFSC shall
implement a family-centered case management system, which shall:

      a) Maintain unified and secure family records;
      b) Identify risks, vulnerabilities, and service needs;
      c) Track delivery of services across sectors;
      d) Enable timely and proactive interventions; and
      e) Ensure continuity of care and support for families.

SECTION 7. Institutional Arrangements.

a) The Local Government Unit (LGU) shall be primarily responsible for the
establishment, management, and operation of FFSCs;

b) The following national government agencies shall provide technical assistance,
program support, and service integration:
    • Department of Health (DOH)
    • Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
    • Department of Education (DepEd)
    • Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)
    • Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)
    • Other relevant agencies as may be determined


c) Each LGU shall establish a Local Convergence Council to ensure coordination
among agencies and programs.
SECTION 8. Integration with Digital Systems. FFSCs shall be integrated with
national and local digital systems to:

a) Facilitate data sharing and interoperability;
b) Reduce duplication of records and processes;
c) Improve service efficiency and accessibility; and
d) Support evidence-based planning and monitoring.

SECTION 9. Staffing and Capacity Development. LGUs shall ensure adequate
staffing of FFSCs, which may include social workers, health personnel, case
managers, and administrative and support staff.

The national government shall provide training, technical assistance, and
capacity-building programs.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. The initial implementation of this Act shall be
charged against the current appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH),
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of
Education (DepEd), Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Philippine
Statistics Authority (PSA), and other participating agencies, consistent with their
respective mandates.

Local Government Units (LGUs) shall fund the establishment, operation, and
maintenance of the Filipino Family Service Centers (FFSCs) from their annual
budgets, including the General Fund and the 20% Development Fund, subject
to existing budgeting rules.

Thereafter, funding for the continued implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) and in LGU budgets.
The national government may provide financial assistance or grants to support
LGU implementation, and may also mobilize grants, donations, and official
development assistance in accordance with applicable laws.

SECTION 11. Monitoring and Evaluation. A national monitoring and
evaluation framework shall be established to assess:

a) Access to and utilization of services;
b) Health, education, and social protection outcomes;
c) Efficiency of integrated service delivery; and
d) Overall impact on family well-being.

An annual report shall be submitted to Congress.
SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the concerned agencies, in coordination with
LGUs, shall promulgate the necessary Implementing Rules and Regulations
(IRR).
SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act shall be
held unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions hereof which are not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
