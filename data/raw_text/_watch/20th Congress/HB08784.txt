                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


        This bill seeks to establish the Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team (HEART)
under the Department of Health (DOH), a reserve corps of trained health professionals and
volunteers who can be mobilized to augment the country’s healthcare workforce during public
health emergencies and other health crises.

         The COVID-19 pandemic exposed significant gaps in the capacity of the Philippine health
system to rapidly respond to large-scale emergencies. Hospitals, healthcare facilities, and frontline
workers were often overwhelmed by sudden surges in demand for medical services. These
challenges underscored the urgent need for a more agile, responsive, and adequately staffed health
system capable of scaling up quickly during crises. As the country continues to face threats from
pandemics, natural disasters, and other unforeseen public health emergencies, it is imperative to
institutionalize mechanisms that can provide immediate reinforcement to the healthcare workforce.

        Anchored on Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution, which mandates the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people, this proposed measure seeks to strengthen the
country’s emergency health response capacity. It also supports the attainment of Sustainable
Development Goal 3, which calls for ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being for all at all
ages.

       The HEART system is designed to complement, not replace, the existing healthcare
workforce. It establishes a reserve force composed of highly skilled health professionals and trained
volunteers who can be readily mobilized during public health emergencies and other health threats.
Through this mechanism, the government will be better prepared to address sudden increases in
demand for healthcare services while maintaining continuity of essential health operations.

        To ensure effective implementation, the bill creates the HEART Board, which will oversee
the organization, training, mobilization, and deployment of the auxiliary team. The Board will be
chaired by the DOH and composed of representatives from key government institutions, including
the Department of the Interior and Local Government, Department of National Defense,
Commission on Higher Education, Professional Regulation Commission, Armed Forces of the
Philippines, Office of Civil Defense, and a representative from a recognized non-governmental
health professional organization.

      The HEART Board will coordinate closely with the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Council (NDRRMC) and the Philippine National Volunteer Service Coordinating
Agency (PNVSCA) to ensure proper alignment with existing disaster response and volunteer
mobilization systems. It will also encourage the participation of government-owned and controlled
corporations, local government units, civil society organizations, and the private sector to
strengthen a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach to emergency health response.

        Among the key features of the bill are the establishment of a national registry of volunteers,
the creation of mobilization centers, and the implementation of regular training programs to ensure
the readiness of members of the HEART system. The measure also provides legal protection,
emergency insurance coverage, and appropriate incentives for volunteers who render service during
emergencies. In addition, it establishes mechanisms for deployment, reporting, and demobilization
once the emergency situation has subsided.

       This measure has been identified as a priority legislation by the administration. It was
endorsed by the Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) as part of the
Common Legislative Agenda during the Nineteenth Congress and was highlighted by Ferdinand
Marcos Jr. in his first State of the Nation Address. The proposal is also supported by Ferdinand
Martin Romualdez as one of its principal sponsors, underscoring the urgency of strengthening the
country’s health emergency response system.

        The establishment of the HEART system is a crucial step toward reinforcing the nation’s
healthcare capacity during times of crisis. By institutionalizing a trained and organized reserve
force, the country can respond more effectively to public health emergencies while fostering a
culture of preparedness, volunteerism, and national resilience.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

             AN ACT CREATING THE HEALTH EMERGENCY AUXILIARY
         REINFORCEMENT TEAM UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND
                      APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “ Health Emergency Auxiliary
2    Reinforcement Team (HEART) Act”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article II, Section 15 of the Constitution enjoins the
4    State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
5    among them. Article XIII, Section 11 also provides for the adoption of an integrated and
6    comprehensive approach to health development. The State further reiterates its commitment to
7    the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 3, which calls on the State to
8    ensure healthy lives and promote the well-being of Filipinos at all ages.

9           The State recognizes the need for a reserve force of highly skilled and health-oriented

10   professionals and volunteers who can be mobilized to maintain the capacity to meet surges in

11   the demand for additional manpower in the country’s healthcare system and provide assistance

12   as needed in times of health crises.

13          Pursuant to this, it is the policy of the State to:

14          a. Prioritize the health of its citizens whether in the country or overseas by maintaining

15              a strong and resilient healthcare system at all times that will immediately,

16              efficiently, and effectively address all potential and actual public health

17              emergencies; and

1    b. Promote the spirit of “Bayanihan” and enhance the capacity of the nation to expand

2       its human health resources in times of disasters, public health emergencies and

3       health threats of both national and local scale through the mobilization of the Health

4       Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team.

5    SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

6    a. Allied health professionals refer to professions who are involved with the delivery

7       of health or related services such as the identification, evaluation and prevention of

8       diseases and disorders, dietary and nutrition services, and rehabilitation and health

9       systems management. Allied health professionals include, among others, dental

10      hygienists, diagnostic medical sonographers, dietitians, medical technologists,

11      occupational therapists, physical therapists, radiographers, respiratory therapists,

12      and speech language pathologists;

13   b. Bayanihan refers to the value of communal unity through helping others to achieve

14      a certain goal without expecting anything in return;

15   c. Deployment refers to the actual movement of workforce to the assigned area within

16      a prescribed period;

17   d. Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team refers to a group of individual

18      volunteers who are in the field of medicine, nursing, medical technology, and other

19      allied health-related fields who are ready to be deployed to complement the health

20      workforce in the event of a public health emergency and health threat;

21   e. Health threat refers to a condition, agent, or incident that greatly impacts the health

22      that leads to disease, accident, injury, and loss of life;

23   f. Mobilization refers to the process and procedures for activating, assembling, and

24      transporting the needed health workforce to respond to a public health emergency

25      and health threat;

3       mobilization, coordinate their mission order and mobilization-related issues and

4       concerns, and submit their report upon receipt of the order for demobilization;

5    h. Public health emergency refers to an occurrence that poses an imminent threat of

3            SEC. 4. Scope. – This Act provides for the organization of the Health Emergency

4    Auxiliary Reinforcement Team, hereinafter referred to as the HEART, the development of

5    policies, plans, guidelines, and the implementation of actions pertaining to the mobilization,

6    services, and protection of the HEART in times of disasters, public health emergencies and

7    health threats.

8            SEC. 5. Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team. – There is hereby

9    established a HEART under the Department of Health (DOH) to augment the health workforce

10   in times of disasters, public health emergencies and health threats. The HEART shall be

11   composed of the following individuals who shall voluntarily register with the DOH:

12           a. Licensed professionals in the fields of medicine, nursing, medical technology, and

13               other allied health fields including those who are retired and those who are no

14               longer practicing their professions in a health facility setting. All health and allied

15               health professional organizations may be tapped for the engagement of

16               professionals who are not affiliated in any healthcare facility;

17           b. Graduates of medicine and allied health courses and medical students who have

18               completed a one-year medical internship and who have not yet been issued licenses

19               to practice their respective professions, but due to the need for their services may

20               be issued a limited and special authorization to render medical services during

21               public health emergencies or health threats pursuant to Republic Act No. 2382,

22               otherwise known as the Medical Act of 1959 or as may be amended hereafter; and

23           c. Other health support workforce, including barangay health workers, whose services

24               are necessary in times of disasters, public health emergencies and health threats.

1       SEC. 6. The Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team Promotion Program. –

2    There is hereby established the Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team Promotion

3    Program, which shall refer to the comprehensive set of objectives, targets, strategies and

4    activities for the promotion, recruitment and selection of a reserve force of highly skilled and

12      SEC. 7. Creation of the HEART Board. – A HEART Board, hereinafter referred to as the

13   Board, is hereby created to ensure efficient and effective mobilization of the HEART. It shall

14   be chaired by the Department of Health (DOH) and composed of representatives of the

15   following, as members:

16      a. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

17      b. Department of National Defense (DND);

18      c. Commission on Higher Education (CHED);

19      d. Professional Regulatory Commission (PRC);

20      e. Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP);

21      f. Office of Civil Defense (OCD); and

22      g. One representative from a non-governmental health professional organization.

23      The members of the Board shall designate a representative, with a rank not lower than

24   Assistant Secretary or its equivalent, to represent their respective offices in the Board:

2    agency.

3       The AFP shall designate a representative from the Office of the Surgeon General with a

4    rank not lower than Colonel as its Board representative.

5       The representative from a non-governmental health professional organization shall have a

6    two (2)-year term of office. The Board shall select the representative based on the guidelines

7    it shall promulgate: Provided, That the non-governmental health organization is national in

8    scope and has a good track record in providing emergency health services.

9       The Board shall establish close coordination with the National Disaster Risk Reduction

10   and Management Council and the Philippine National Volunteer Service Coordinating

11   Agency. It may call upon any government office or instrumentality, including government-

12   owned or controlled corporations and local government units (LGUs), as necessary. The Board

13   may likewise closely coordinate and collaborate with non-governmental health organizations

14   and the private sector, especially those with actual programs in the delivery of emergency

15   health services.

16      SEC. 8. Powers and Functions of the Board. – The Board shall have the following powers

17   and functions:

18      a. Define and develop the organization, management, mobilization, demobilization, and

19          reporting mechanisms, policies, and guidelines for the HEART;

20      b. Oversee the effective and efficient functioning of the HEART in responding to public

21          health emergencies and health threats;

22      c. Coordinate with concerned national government agencies and other stakeholders in

23          responding to public health emergencies and health threats; and

24      d. Conduct regular meetings every quarter or as often as may be necessary in times of

25          disasters, during public health emergencies or in the occurrence of health threats.

1       SEC. 9. Creation of a HEART Unit. – A HEART Unit shall be created by the DOH which

2    shall serve as the secretariat of the Board. It shall oversee program implementation, including

3    the maintenance of the database or information system of the HEART.

4       The DOH shall determine the organizational structure and staffing pattern of the HEART

5    unit subject to the evaluation and approval of the Department of Budget and Management

6    (DBM) and in accordance with the civil service laws, rules and regulations.

7       The regional counterparts of the HEART Unit shall likewise be allocated additional staff

8    and personnel to ensure the grassroots implementation of this Act.

9       All LGUs shall ensure that a HEART Unit shall be operationalized under their respective

10   Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office which shall act as mobilization centers

11   in times of disasters, public health emergencies or health threats.

12      SEC. 10. Registration and Training. – The Board shall prescribe a comprehensive

13   guideline for the recruitment and selection of a HEART volunteer.

14      The Board shall also prescribe a training program through written, practical, and simulation

15   activities on various aspects of health emergency management and response in different health

16   emergency scenarios. To this end, all volunteers must undergo:

17      a. Basic training on disaster and health emergency response; and

18      b. Skill enhancement activities with relevant agencies in both public and private sectors.

19      Completion of the basic training or acceptable substitute or equivalent certification shall

20   be a requisite for admission to the HEART.

21      SEC. 11. Registry and Accounting of Members. – HEART Volunteers shall be issued with

22   individual registration numbers which shall serve as their identification during deployment.

23      A registry or database that contains the names of the volunteers, their registration numbers,

24   address, contact details and other personal information as may be necessary, shall be




                                                                                                    7
1    maintained and regularly updated in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise

2    known as the Data Privacy Act.

3       The HEART registry or database shall be integrated or linked to existing registries such as

4    the National Health Workforce Registry pursuant to Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known

5    as the Universal Health Care Act, Section 25 (c).

6       All volunteers covered under this Act are required to update their addresses and contact

7    details on file through the registry system or database as necessary.

8       SEC. 12. Mobilization. – The Board shall approve the prompt mobilization of the HEART

9    upon receipt of a request from municipal and city mayors and provincial governors in

10   connection with a public health emergency, threats to public health, or a disaster, whether of

11   local or national scale. The HEART may be mobilized partially or in full, as may be necessary.

12      The Board shall promulgate a mechanism that shall ensure efficient implementation of

13   deployment, assignment areas, deployment periods, how deployment orders are communicated

14   to each volunteer, and to which mobilization center the volunteers will report, taking into

15   consideration Section 13 on “Accreditation, Mobilization, and Protection of Disaster

16   Volunteers and National Service Reserve Corps, CSOs, and the Private Sector” of Republic

17   Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management

18   Act of 2010.

19      SEC. 13. Deployment Order. – The President of the Philippines, upon the recommendation

20   of the HEART Board through a resolution, may order the nationwide mobilization of the

21   HEART to augment the health workforce in case of a declaration of a state of public health

22   emergency, state of calamity, and other threats to public health.

23      Orders of deployment sent to the volunteers’ addresses and through the contact details on

24   file in the registry system or database shall be sufficient notice for purposes of deployment.

1       SEC. 14. Mobilization Centers. – Mobilization Centers shall be established in each

2    province as needed where volunteers shall report to once deployment is ordered.

3       A mobilization center may be any establishment that can adequately house the volunteers,

4    their equipment and supplies during the period of deployment, including a multi-purpose hall,

5    gymnasium, and other similar structures.

6       The Board shall, in consultation with the local executives, prescribe the location of the

7    mobilization centers. The concerned municipal and city mayors and provincial governors shall

8    disseminate information on the location of mobilization centers to the widest extent possible.

9       SEC. 15. Essential Emergency Commodities. – The Board shall ensure that the minimum

10   essential individual and organizational commodities and medical equipment and supplies shall

11   be procured, stored, and managed to enhance the rapid transition to readiness required for

12   deployment of HEART teams in the shortest possible time.

13      The Board shall ensure and maintain its capacity to scale up the procurement of these

14   equipment and supplies as needed during the period of deployment.

15      SEC. 16. Incentives. – In recognition and appreciation of the HEART volunteers’ service

16   rendered during the period of actual mobilization, implementing agencies may provide

17   volunteers with an allowance as may be determined by the Department of Health (DOH), in

18   coordination with the Department of Budget and Management, subject to availability of funds,

19   and other existing civil service, budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and regulations.

20   Notwithstanding the foregoing, nothing in this Act shall be construed as creating an employer-

21   employee relationship between the government and HEART volunteers.

22      SEC. 17. Legal Liability. – No volunteer shall be held liable for the death or injury to any

23   person or for the loss of, or damage to the property of any person where such death, injury,

24   loss, or damage was proximately caused by the circumstance of an actual public health

25   emergency or its subsequent conditions.


                                                                                                   9
1       This Section shall not preclude liability for civil damages because of gross negligence,

2    recklessness, or willful misconduct.

3       SEC. 18. Public Health Emergency Insurance. – The DOH is mandated to secure an

4    insurance policy that would insure HEART volunteers from injury, death, damage to, or loss

5    of property, and for any legal liability asserted against or incurred by the volunteer arising from

6    the lawful performance of their duty.

7       SEC. 19. Termination of Deployment or Demobilization. – Upon the declaration of the

8    President, or as determined by the Board that the need for the HEART is no longer necessary,

9    or upon the expiration of the deployment or end of mission there being no approved extension

10   thereof, volunteers who are deployed pursuant to the declaration of a public calamity, a public

11   health emergency or health threat shall be discharged from the performance of HEART duties.

12      Upon order of the demobilization of the HEART, the deployed volunteers shall be reverted

13   to inactive status. Mobilization Centers shall ensure reports on the mission are duly submitted

14   and the certificate of services rendered pertaining to the deployment are promptly issued.

15      SEC. 20. Annual Report. – The HEART Unit shall prepare and submit an annual report to

16   the Office of the President, through the DOH Secretary, containing the list of accomplishments,

17   status of the operations, and program implementation of the HEART.

18      SEC. 21. Appropriations. – The funds necessary for the initial implementation of this Act

19   shall be charged against the available funds of the implementing agencies comprising the

20   HEART Board. Thereafter, the funding shall be included in the budget of the DOH under the

21   annual General Appropriations Act.

22      SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the

23   effectivity of this Act, the HEART Board shall, in consultation with other stakeholders,

24   promulgate the necessary guidelines for the effective implementation of this Act.
