                       AN ACT
DEFINING AND PENALIZING ELDERLY ABUSE AS A PUBLIC
CRIME, PROVIDING PROTECTIONS TO ELDERLY VICTIM-
SURVIVORS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to define and penalize elderly abuse as a public crime,
recognizing that protecting the elderly is both a duty of the State and a shared
responsibility of the community. It provides clear definitions of prohibited acts,
prescribes corresponding penalties, and establishes protection orders and
remedies to prevent further harm. It also delineates the duties of barangay
officials, law enforcers, healthcare providers, prosecutors, and courts in
addressing cases of elder abuse.

       According to the World Health Organization (WHO), one in six older
persons worldwide has experienced some form of abuse 1. In the Philippines, a
2004 community study revealed that about 40% of older persons in urban poor
areas had experienced abuse2, often committed by family members or caregivers.
Alarmingly, only 2% of cases were reported to authorities, underscoring the
hidden and underreported nature of the problem due to shame, fear, and the lack
of accessible reporting and support mechanisms. Experts believe that the actual
figures are much higher.

          Through this measure, the State reaffirms its constitutional mandate to
   promote the welfare and protect the rights of the elderly and upholds the
   constitutional guarantee of the right to life, liberty, and security of all Filipinos,
   regardless of age. It strengthens institutional mechanisms for prevention,
   protection, and redress, closes existing policy gaps, and ensures that senior
   citizens live their remaining years in dignity, security, and respect.

                       AN ACT
DEFINING AND PENALIZING ELDERLY ABUSE AS A PUBLIC
CRIME, PROVIDING PROTECTIONS TO ELDERLY VICTIM-
SURVIVORS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:



SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Elder Abuse
Act.”



SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Enshrined in Article XV, Section 4 of
1987 Philippine Constitution is the duty of the State to design programs of social
security for the elderly. Additionally, Article XIII, Section 11 provides for the
State to ensure accessible health and social services, giving priority to the needs
of the elderly alongside other vulnerable sectors. To fulfill these mandates, the
State shall eliminate all forms of senior citizen abuse, penalize violators, and
adopt programs for prevention, deterrence, and crisis intervention.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For the purposes of this Act, the following
definitions shall apply:

   a. Elderly refers to any individual aged sixty (60) and above;

   b. Elderly abuse refers to a single or repeated act, or the lack of appropriate
      action, occurring within any relationship where there is an expectation of
      trust, which causes harm or distress to an elderly individual including, but
      not limited to, physical, sexual, psychological or emotional abuse, material
      or financial exploitation, and neglect.



SECTION 4. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts of violence against the
elderly are declared unlawful and therefore prohibited:

   a. Physical Abuse refers to acts committed with the intent to cause bodily
      harm or physical pain to an elderly individual. It includes striking,
      slapping, beating, pushing, kicking, burning, the inappropriate use of
      physical restraints or medication, force-feeding, or any act resulting in
      physical injuries such as bruises, sprains, welts, abrasions, or fractures;

   b. Sexual Abuse refers to any non-consensual act that is sexual in nature,
      committed against an elderly individual. It includes rape, acts of
      lasciviousness, coerced nudity, unwanted sexual touching, sexually
      explicit photographing, sexual coercion, or forcing the victim into
      prostitution or other sexual acts.

   c. Psychological or Emotional Abuse refers to acts or omissions that cause or
      are likely to cause mental or emotional suffering to an elderly individual.
      It includes verbal assaults, threats, humiliation, ridicule, intimidation, or
      other similar acts that result in or contribute to emotional anguish, whether
      or not accompanied by physical abuse;
   d. Material, Financial, or Economic Abuse refers to the unauthorized or
      improper use of an elderly individual’s resources, property, or assets for
      another person’s monetary or personal gain, or any act that deprives the
      elderly of rightful access to or control over their finances or possessions. It
      includes financial exploitation, theft, coercion or deception in signing legal
      documents, misuse of property or funds, and other acts of economic
      manipulation or deprivation.

   e. Neglect refers to the failure or refusal, whether intentional or unintentional,
      to provide the basic needs of an elderly individual, including food, shelter,
      healthcare, protection, or companionship. It includes the deliberate
      withholding or inadequate provision of items or services essential to daily
      living, particularly for those who are physically dependent.



SECTION 5. Public Crime. – Elder abuse shall be considered as a public offense
which may be prosecuted upon the filing a complaint by any citizen having
personal knowledge of the circumstances involving the commission of the crime.



SECTION 6. Penalties. – The prohibited acts outlined hereof shall be punished
according to the following rules:

   a. Acts falling under Sec. 4 (a) and (b) shall be punished in accordance with
      the provisions of the Revised Penal Code (RPC);

   b. Acts falling under Sec. 4 (c) shall be punished by prision mayor;

   c. Acts falling under Sec. 4 (d) which are covered in the RPC shall be
      punished in accordance with the Code. However, acts not found in the RPC
      such as the unauthorized, or improper use of the resources of an elderly,
      depriving an elderly of rightful access to, or use of, benefits, resources,
      belongings, or assets, misuse of property or money, or forced signing of
      legal documents shall be punished by prision correccional; and

   d. Acts falling under Sec. 4(e) shall be punished by prision mayor.

The penalty provided shall be imposed in its maximum period if:

   a. The offender-perpetrator has been previously convicted under this Act or
      is a habitual delinquent;
   b. The offender is a descendant, collateral relative, family relative up to the
      2nd degree of consanguinity or affinity or guardian;
   c. The offender is an owner-operator, manager, or employee of a privately-
      operated elderly facility; and
   d. The offender is a public official, staff, or employee of a government-
      operated residential home; provided, that said public official staff or
      employee may be subjected to suspension and/or termination according to
      administrative disciplinary procedures.



SECTION 7. Prohibited Defense. – Being under the influence of alcohol, illicit
drugs, or any other mind-altering substance shall not be a defense under this Act.



SECTION 8. Persons Intervening Exempt from Liability. – Any person who
responds or intervenes with force or violence that is greater than necessary to
ensure the safety of the victim shall not be liable for any criminal, civil, or
administrative case resulting therefrom.



SECTION 9. Venue. – The Regional Trial Court designated as a Family Court
shall have original and exclusive jurisdiction over cases of violence against the
elderly under this law. In the absence of such court in the place where the offense
was committed, the case shall be filed in the Regional Trial Court where the crime
or any of its elements was committed, at the option of the complainant.



SECTION 10. Protection Orders. – A protection order is an order issued under
this Act for the purpose of preventing further acts of violence against the elderly
and granting other necessary relief. The relief granted under a protection order
serves the purpose of safeguarding the victim from further harm, minimizing any
disruption in the victim's daily life, and facilitating the opportunity and ability of
the victim to independently regain control over his/her life. The provisions of the
protection order shall be enforced by law enforcement agencies. The protection
orders that may be issued under this Act are the Barangay Protection Order
(BPO), Temporary Protection Order (TPO) and Permanent Protection Order
(PРО).

The protection orders that may be issued under this Act shall include any, some
or all of the following reliefs:

   a. Prohibition of the offender from threatening to commit or committing,
       personally or through another, any of the acts mentioned in Section 4 of
       this Act;
   b. Prohibition of the offender from harassing, annoying, telephoning,
       contacting or otherwise communicating with the petitioner, directly or
       indirectly;
   c. Removal and exclusion of the offender from the residence of the petitioner,
       regardless of ownership of the residence, either temporarily for the purpose
       of protecting the petitioner, or permanently where no property rights are
       violated, and if offender must remove personal effects from the residence,
   the court shall direct a law enforcement agent to accompany the offender
   in gathering their things and escort offender from the residence;
d. Directing the offender to stay away from petitioner and designated family
   or household member at a distance specified by the court, and to stay away
   from the residence or any specified place frequented by the petitioner and
   any designated family or household member;
e. Directing lawful possession and use by petitioner of an automobile and
   other essential personal effects, regardless of ownership, and directing the
   appropriate law enforcement officer to accompany the petitioner to the
   residence of the parties to ensure that the petitioner is safely restored to the
   possession of the automobile and other essential personal effects, or to
   supervise the petitioner's or offender’s removal of personal belongings;
f. Prohibition of the offender from any use or possession of any firearm or
   deadly weapon and order them to surrender the same to the court for
   appropriate disposition by the court, including revocation of license and
   disqualification to apply for any license to use or possess a firearm. If the
   offender is a law enforcement agent, the court shall order the offender to
   surrender their firearm and shall direct the appropriate authority to
   investigate on the offender and take appropriate action on matter;
g. Restitution for actual damages caused by the violence inflicted, including,
   but not limited to, property damage, medical expenses, childcare expenses
   and loss of income;
h. Directing the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) or
   any appropriate agency to provide services that the petitioner may need;
   and
i. Provision of such other forms of relief as the court deems necessary to
   protect and provide for the safety of the petitioner and any designated
   family or household member, provided petitioner and any designated
   family or household member consents to such relief.
The issuance of a BPO or the pendency of an application for BPO shall not
preclude a petitioner from applying for, or the court from granting a TPO or PPO.




SECTION 11. Legal Representation of Petitioners for Protection Order. – If
the elderly victim requests, in the application for a protection order, the
appointment of counsel due to lack of economic means to hire a counsel de parte,
the court shall immediately direct the Public Attorney’s Office (PAO) to represent
the petitioner in the hearing on the application.

If the PAO determines that the applicant has sufficient means to engage the
services of a counsel de parte, it shall facilitate the legal representation of the
petitioner by a counsel de parte. The lack of access to personal or family
resources by the applicant, such as when such resources are controlled or withheld
by the perpetrator, shall qualify the petitioner for legal representation by the PAO.

However, a private counsel offering free legal service shall not be barred from
representing the petitioner.




SECTION 12. Barangay Protection Orders (BPOs). – Barangay Protection
Orders (BPOs) refer to the protection order issued by the Punong Barangay
ordering the perpetrator to desist from committing acts constituting physical or
sexual abuse under Section 4 of this Act.

A Punong Barangay who receives an application for a BPO shall issue the
protection order to the applicant on the date of filing after ex parte determination
of the basis of the application.

If the Punong Barangay is unavailable to act on the application for a BPO, the
application shall be acted upon by any available Barangay Kagawad. If the BPO
is issued by a Barangay Kagawad, the order must be accompanied by an
attestation by the Barangay Kagawad that the Punong Barangay was unavailable
at the time for the issuance of the BPO.

BPOs shall be effective for fifteen (15) days from the date of issuance.
Immediately after the issuance of an ex parte BPO, the Punong Barangay or
Barangay Kagawad shall personally serve a copy of the same on the respondent,
or direct any barangay official to effect its personal service.

The parties may be accompanied by a non-lawyer advocate in any proceeding
before the Punong Barangay.




SECTION 13. Temporary Protection Orders (TPOs). – Temporary Protection
Orders (TPOs) refer to protection orders issued by the court on the date of filing
of the application after ex parte determination that such order should be issued.

A court may grant in a TPO any, some, or all of the reliefs provided under this
Act. The TPO shall be effective for thirty (30) days from issuance.

The court shall schedule a hearing on the issuance of a Permanent Protection
Order (PPO) prior to or on the date of the expiration of the TPO.

The court shall order the immediate personal service of the TPO on the
respondent by the court sheriff, who may obtain the assistance of law enforcement
officers or barangay officials in effecting such service.

The TPO shall include a notice of the date of hearing on the merits of the issuance
of a PPO.




SECTION 14. Permanent Protection Orders (PPOs). – Permanent Protection
Orders (PPOs) refer to protection orders issued by the court after notice and
hearing.

The respondent’s non-appearance despite proper notice, or his lack of a lawyer,
or the non-availability of his lawyer, shall not be a ground for the rescheduling or
postponement of the hearing on the merits of the issuance of a PPO. If the
respondent appears without counsel on the date of the hearing, the court shall
appoint a lawyer for the respondent and immediately proceed with the hearing.

In case the respondent fails to appear despite proper notice, the court shall allow
ex parte presentation of evidence by the applicant and render judgment on the
basis of the evidence presented. The court shall allow the introduction of any
history of abusive conduct of the respondent, even if the same was not directed
against the applicant or the person for whom the application is made.

The court shall, to the extent possible, conduct the hearing on the merits of the
issuance of a PPO in one (1) day. Where the court is unable to complete the
hearing within one (1) day and the Temporary Protection Order (TPO) is due to
expire, the court shall continuously extend or renew the TPO for a period of thirty
(30) days at a time until final judgment is issued. The extended or renewed TPO
may be modified by the court as may be necessary to address the needs of the
applicant.

The court may grant any, some, or all of the reliefs provided under this Act in a
PPO. A PPO shall remain effective until revoked by the court upon application
of the person in whose favor the order was issued. The court shall ensure
immediate personal service of the PPO upon the respondent.

The court shall not deny the issuance of a protection order on the basis of the
lapse of time between the act of abuse and the filing of the application.

Regardless of the conviction or acquittal of the respondent, the court must
determine whether or not the PPO shall become final. Even in the case of a
dismissal, a PPO shall be granted unless there is a clear showing that the act from
which the order may arise did not exist.




SECTION 15. Violation of Protection Orders. – A complaint for a violation of
a Barangay Protection Order (BPO) issued under this Act shall be filed directly
with any Municipal Trial Court (MTC), Metropolitan Trial Court (MeTC), or
Municipal Circuit Trial Court (MCTC) that has territorial jurisdiction over the
barangay that issued the BPO.

Violation of a BPO shall be punishable by imprisonment of thirty (30) days,
without prejudice to the filing of any other criminal or civil action that the
offended party may pursue for any of the acts committed.

A judgment for violation of a BPO may be appealed in accordance with the Rules
of Court. During trial and upon judgment, the trial court may, motu proprio, issue
a protection order as it deems necessary, without need of an application from the
offended party.

Violation of any provision of a Temporary Protection Order (TPO) or Permanent
Protection Order (PPO) issued under this Act shall constitute contempt of court,
punishable under Rule 71 of the Rules of Court, without prejudice to the filing of
any other criminal or civil action that the offended party may initiate for any of
the acts committed.




SECTION 16. Establishment of a Senior Citizens Help Desk. – Every barangay
shall establish a Senior Citizen Help Desk and corresponding hotline number
which shall provide immediate assistance to the victims of elder abuse. The
Senior Citizen Help Desk may consist of representatives of the Senior Citizens
who are residents of the barangay or members of a local Senior Citizen
organization designated and authorized by the Barangay Council or Chairperson.
In addition, police stations across the country shall have a Senior Citizen Desk
which shall attend to, and act on, complaints/cases covered by this Act.



SECTION 17. Duties of Prosecutors/Court Personnel. – Prosecutors and court
personnel should observe the following duties when dealing with victims under
this Act:

   a. Communicate with the victim in a language understood by the elder victim;
      and
   b. Inform the victim of their rights including legal remedies available and
      procedure, and privileges for indigent litigants.



SECTION 18. Duties of Barangay Officials and Law Enforcers. – Barangay
officials and law enforcers shall have the following duties:

   a. Respond immediately to a call for help or request for assistance or
      protection of the victim by entering the necessary whether or not a
      protection order has been issued and ensure the safety of the victim;
   b. Confiscate any deadly weapon in the possession of the perpetrator or
      within plain view;
   c. Transport or escort the victim to a safe place of their choice or to a clinic
      or hospital;
   d. Assist the victim in removing personal belongings from the house;
   e. Assist the barangay officials and other government officers and employees
      who respond to a call for help;
   f. Ensure the enforcement of the Protection Orders issued by the Punong
      Barangay or the courts;
   g. Arrest the suspected perpetrator without a warrant when any of the acts of
      violence defined by this Act is occurring, or when they have personal
      knowledge that any act of abuse has just been committed, and there is
      imminent danger to the life or limb of the victim as defined in this Act; and
   h. Immediately report the call for assessment or assistance of the DSWD,
      Social Welfare Department of LGUs or accredited non-government
      organizations (NGOs).

Any barangay official or law enforcer who fails to report the incident shall be
liable for a fine not exceeding Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP10,000.00) or whenever
applicable criminal, civil or administrative liability.




SECTION 19. Healthcare Provider Response to Abuse. – Any healthcare
provider, including, but not limited to, an attending physician, nurse, clinician,
barangay health worker, therapist or counselor who suspects abuse or has been
informed by the victim of violence shall:

   a. Properly document any of the victim's               physical, emotional or
      psychological injuries;
   b. Properly record any of victim's suspicions, observations and circumstances
      of the examination or visit;
   c. Automatically provide the victim free of charge a medical certificate
      concerning the examination or visit;
   d. Safeguard the records and make them available to the victim upon request
      at actual cost; and
   e. Provide the victim immediate and adequate notice of rights and remedies
      provided under this Act, and services available to them.
SECTION 20. Duties of Other Government Agencies and LGUs. – Other
government agencies and LGUs shall establish programs such as, but not limited
to, education and information campaigns and seminars or symposia on the nature,
causes, incidence and consequences of such violence particularly towards
educating the public on its social impacts.

It shall be the duty of the concerned government agencies and LGUs to ensure
the sustained education and training of their officers and personnel on the
prevention of violence against women and their children under the Act.

The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) of every LGU shall include
services addressing the violence against elderly. All suspected cases must be
reported in accordance with the existing laws. OSCA, in coordination with the
Local Social Welfare and Development Office, shall also maintain its own
documentation and monitoring systems of the cases.




SECTION 21. Rights of Victims. – In addition to their rights under existing
laws, victims of violence against the elderly shall have the following rights:

   a. To be treated with respect and dignity;
   b. To avail of legal assistance from the PAO of the Department of Justice
      (DOJ) or any public legal assistance office;
   c. To be entitled to support services form the DSWD and LGUs;
   d. To be entitled to all legal remedies and support as provided for under the
      Family Code; and
   e. To be informed of their rights and the services available to them including
      their right to apply for a protection order.
SECTION 22. Mandatory Programs and Services for Victims. – The DSWD,
in coordination with LGUs, shall provide the victims temporary shelters, provide
counseling, psycho-social services and/or, recovery, rehabilitation programs and
livelihood assistance.

Furthermore, the DSWD, in coordination with LGUs and accredited NGOs, shall
establish and maintain shelters or temporary homes that will provide a safe,
secure, and accessible refuge for elderly victims of abuse.

   a. Such shelters shall provide short-term and long-term accommodation,
      food, clothing, medical care, counseling, and rehabilitation services;
   b. Each region shall have at least one (1) government-operated shelter or
      partner facility designated for elderly victims of violence;
   c. LGUs are mandated to include in their annual investment plans the
      allocation for the operation and maintenance of shelters for elderly victims;
      and
   d. Priority shall be given to victims who are indigent, abandoned, or have no
      immediate family or household members to provide support.

The DOH shall provide medical assistance to victims.



SECTION 23. Damages. – Mandatory Programs and Services for Victims. –
Any victim of violence under this Act shall be entitled actual, compensatory,
moral and exemplary damages.



SECTION 24. Training of Persons Involved in Responding to Cases of Elder
Abuse. – All government agencies, instrumentalities, and LGUs involved in
responding to cases of violence against the elderly shall be required to undergo
education and capacity-building programs to acquaint them with:
   a. The nature, extent, and causes of abuse against senior citizens;
   b. The legal rights of, and remedies available to, victims of Senior Citizen
      Abuse;
   c. The services and facilities available to victims or survivors;
   d. The legal duties imposed on police officers and barangay officials to make
      arrests and to offer protection and assistance; and
   e. Techniques and approaches for handling incidents of elder abuse that
      minimize the likelihood of injury to the officer and promote the safety,
      dignity, and well-being of the elderly victim-survivor.

The Philippine National Police (PNP), in coordination with the National
Commission of Senior Citizens (NCSC), the DSWD, and the LGUs, shall
establish an education and training program for police officers, barangay
officials, and other first responders to ensure that they are properly equipped to
handle cases of abuse against senior citizens in a gender-sensitive, rights-based,
and age-appropriate manner to cases of elder abuse.




SECTION 25. Counseling and Treatment of Offenders. – The DSWD shall
provide rehabilitative counseling and treatment to perpetrators towards learning
constructive ways of coping with anger and emotional outbursts and reforming
their ways. When necessary, the offender shall be ordered by the Court to submit
to psychiatric treatment or confinement.



SECTION 26. Confidentiality. – All records pertaining to cases of violence
against the elderly including those in the barangay shall be confidential and all
public officers and employees and public or private clinics/hospitals shall respect
the right to privacy of the victim. Any person who shall make public any relevant
or identifying information about the case or the victims shall be liable to the
contempt power of the court and shall suffer the penalty of one (1) year
imprisonment and a fine of not more than Five Hundred Thousand pesos (PhP
500, 000.00).

SECTION 27. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the effective
implementation of the provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).

NCSC, DSWD, DOJ, and LGUs may utilize their respective program funds and
other available appropriations to support and implement services, programs, and
interventions for victims of Senior Citizen Abuse, consistent with the objectives
of this Act.




SECTION 28. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within six (6) months
from the approval of this Act, the NCSC, in coordination with the DOJ, DSWD,
DOH, Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the PNP, and
in consultation with concerned stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.



SECTION 29. Suppletory Application. – For purposes of this Act, the Revised
Penal Code and other applicable laws, shall have suppletory application.



SECTION 30. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the
other provisions hereof.
SECTION 31. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders and
rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.



SECTION 32. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
