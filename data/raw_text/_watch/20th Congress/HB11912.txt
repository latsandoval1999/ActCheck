                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Despite the enactment of landmark healthcare reforms such as Republic Act

and geographically disadvantaged provinces remain burdened by inadequate
healthcare infrastructure, shortages of healthcare workers, limited medical
equipment and medicines, weak referral systems, and insufficient local fiscal
capacity to support essential healthcare services.

      Many low-income provinces are unable to adequately finance primary
healthcare, maternal and child health programs, disease prevention initiatives,
emergency response systems, nutrition interventions, and public hospitals due to
limited local revenues and competing development priorities. As a result, healthcare
disparities between wealthier and poorer provinces continue to widen,

 disproportionately affecting indigent families, rural communities, indigenous
 peoples, geographically isolated populations, and other vulnerable sectors.

       The Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health
 of the people and adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health
 development. Equity in healthcare financing is therefore essential to achieving
 genuine universal healthcare and ensuring that the quality of health services
 available to Filipinos does not depend on the wealth or income classification of their
 province.

         This proposed bill seeks to establish a dedicated annual equity-based health
 financing mechanism that shall provide additional and targeted support to the
 poorest provinces in the country. The measure aims to strengthen local health
 systems, improve access to essential healthcare services, address regional healthcare
 inequalities, support underserved and disadvantaged communities, and ensure that
 all Filipinos have equitable access to quality healthcare regardless of geographic or
 economic conditions.

        The bill institutionalizes a transparent, needs-based, and performance-
 informed allocation system that prioritizes provinces with the greatest healthcare
 deficits and highest poverty incidence while ensuring accountability, measurable
 outcomes, and alignment with national health priorities.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                      AN ACT

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title.         This Act shall be known as the              .      .
Fund for Poor Provinces Act.


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.            It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure equitable access to quality, affordable, and accessible healthcare
services for all Filipinos, particularly those residing in poor, underserved,
geographically isolated, and disadvantaged provinces. The State shall adopt equity-



                                                                                        1
based healthcare financing mechanisms to reduce regional disparities in health
outcomes and strengthen local health systems consistent with the Universal Health
Care Act and the constitutional right to health.


      SECTION 3. Objectives.         This Act aims to:
      (a). Reduce inequalities in healthcare access and outcomes among provinces;
      (b). Provide sustainable and equity-based healthcare financing support to poor
      provinces;
      (c). Strengthen local healthcare systems and service delivery capacity;
      (d). Improve access to essential healthcare services, medicines, equipment,
      and healthcare personnel;
      (e). Enhance maternal, child, nutrition, preventive, and primary healthcare
      programs;
      (f). Support healthcare infrastructure modernization in underserved areas; and
      (g). Promote accountability, transparency, and performance-based health
      investments.


      SECTION 4. Definition of Terms.          For purposes of this Act:
      (a). Health Equity Fund refers to the annual equity-based financial allocation
      established under this Act for poor provinces;
      (b). Poor Provinces refer to provinces identified by the Philippine Statistics
      Authority (PSA), National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA),
      and Department of Health (DOH) based on poverty incidence, healthcare
      access deficits, healthcare workforce shortages, and related socioeconomic
      indicators;




                                                                                  2
      (c). Essential Health Services refer to basic and priority healthcare services
      including primary care, maternal and child health, immunization, nutrition,
      communicable and non-communicable disease prevention, emergency care,
      and other healthcare services identified by the DOH;
      (d). Healthcare Access Deficit refers to gaps in healthcare facilities,
      personnel, equipment, medicines, and service accessibility within a province.


      SECTION 5. Establishment of the Health Equity Fund.            There is hereby
established a Health Equity Fund (HEF), which shall serve as a dedicated annual
funding mechanism for the poorest provinces to improve access to essential
healthcare services and strengthen local health systems.
      The Fund shall be administered by the Department of Health (DOH) in
coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), PhilHealth,
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other relevant
agencies. The Fund shall be utilized exclusively for healthcare programs,
infrastructure, workforce support, medicines, equipment, digital health systems,
emergency preparedness, and other health-related interventions authorized under
this Act.


      SECTION 6. Identification of Beneficiary Provinces.        The Department of
Health (DOH), in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA),
Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Department of
Finance (DOF), and Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), shall
develop a transparent and evidence-based framework for identifying beneficiary
provinces eligible to receive allocations from the Health Equity Fund.
      The identification process shall consider factors including:



                                                                                  3
      (a). Poverty incidence and income classification;
      (b). Healthcare workforce shortages;
      (c). Hospital and healthcare facility deficits;
      (d). Maternal and infant mortality rates;
      (e). Malnutrition prevalence;
      (f). Geographic isolation and disaster vulnerability;
      (g). Disease burden and healthcare access limitations; and
      (h). Fiscal capacity of local government units.
      The list of beneficiary provinces shall be reviewed and updated every three
(3) years or as may be necessary.


      SECTION 7. Allocation Formula and Distribution.           The allocation of the
Health Equity Fund shall be based on an equity-oriented formula developed by the
Department of Health (DOH) and approved by the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM). The formula shall prioritize provinces with the greatest
healthcare needs, highest poverty levels, weakest healthcare infrastructure, and
lowest fiscal capacity.
      Fund allocation shall consider population size, health outcome indicators,
healthcare access deficits, geographic challenges, disaster risks, and existing local
healthcare resources to ensure fair and needs-based distribution. With, at least
seventy percent (70%) of the Fund shall be allocated directly for frontline healthcare
service improvement, healthcare personnel support, medicines and vaccines,
primary healthcare programs, and healthcare infrastructure development.


      SECTION 8. Authorized Uses of the Fund.           The Health Equity Fund may
be used for:



                                                                                    4
      (a). Construction, rehabilitation, and upgrading of public healthcare facilities;
      (b). Procurement of medicines, vaccines, laboratory supplies, and medical
      equipment;
      (c). Hiring, deployment, and retention incentives for healthcare workers;
      (d). Mobile clinics and outreach healthcare services;
      (e). Maternal, child health, and nutrition programs;
      (f). Digital health and telemedicine initiatives;
      (g). Emergency preparedness and disaster response healthcare systems;
      (h). Health promotion and disease prevention programs; and
      (i). Other healthcare interventions approved by the Department of Health
      consistent with this Act.


      SECTION 9. Local Health Equity Plans.           The beneficiary provinces shall
prepare and submit Local Health Equity Plans outlining healthcare priorities,
implementation strategies, target outcomes, and proposed utilization of the Fund.

      The Department of Health (DOH) shall provide technical assistance to ensure
that such plans are aligned with national healthcare priorities, local health needs, and
principles of accountability and sustainability.

      SECTION 10. Accountability and Transparency Mechanisms.                       All
allocations and expenditures under this Act shall be subject to existing government
accounting, auditing, procurement, and anti-corruption laws and regulations.


      Beneficiary provinces shall maintain publicly accessible reports on fund
utilization, healthcare outcomes, procurement activities, and implementation status.
The Department of Health (DOH) and Commission on Audit (COA) shall conduct



                                                                                      5
periodic monitoring, audits, and evaluations to ensure proper utilization of the Fund.
Any misuse, diversion, or irregular utilization of funds under this Act shall subject
responsible officials to appropriate administrative, civil, and criminal liabilities
under existing laws.


      SECTION 11. Role of the Department of Health.              The Department of
Health (DOH) shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act and shall:
      (a). Administer and oversee the Health Equity Fund;
      (b). Develop allocation criteria and healthcare equity indicators;
      (c). Identify beneficiary provinces;
      (d). Provide technical and institutional support to local governments;
      (e). Conduct monitoring, audits, and performance evaluations;
      (f). Establish healthcare outcome benchmarks and reporting standards; and
      (g). Submit annual reports to Congress and the Office of the President
      regarding implementation status, fund utilization, and healthcare outcomes.


      SECTION 12. Inter-Agency Coordination. The Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
PhilHealth, Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev),
Department of Finance (DOF), and other relevant agencies shall coordinate with the
Department of Health (DOH) in implementing this Act.


      Local government units shall ensure that Health Equity Fund allocations
supplement, and do not replace, existing local healthcare expenditures and
commitments.




                                                                                    6
      SECTION 13. Appropriations.           The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Health (DOH). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be
included annually in the General Appropriations Act.


      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations.            Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in
coordination with concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of
this Act.


      SECTION 15. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


      SECTION 17. Effectivity.      This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
