                          AN ACT
AMENDING THE GROUNDS FOR LEGAL SEPARATION UNDER ARTICLE 55
           OF THE FAMILY CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is a State Party to the Convention on the Elimination of All
Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), which obligates signatory
States to adopt appropriate legislative and policy measures to eliminate
discrimination against women in all matters relating to marriage and family
relations. In particular, CEDAW General Recommendation No. 19 and General
Recommendation No. 35 recognize gender-based violence as a form of
discrimination and urge States to review and strengthen domestic laws to ensure
that victims of violence within intimate and family relationships have access to
effective legal remedies and protection.

       The Family Code of the Philippines (Executive Order No. 209, s. 1987) was
enacted more than three decades ago. Since its enactment, the Philippine legal
framework addressing violence within the family has evolved considerably.
Congress enacted Republic Act No. 9262, or the Anti-Violence Against Women
and Their Children Act of 2004, which recognizes multiple forms of violence in
intimate relationships, including physical, sexual, psychological, and economic
abuse. In recent years, reports from the Philippine Commission on Women (PCW)
and the Philippine National Police (PNP) have continued to show a significant
number of reported cases of violence against women and intimate partner abuse.
These developments highlight the continuing need for legal mechanisms that
allow victim-survivors to protect themselves and seek relief from abusive marital
relationships.

      Despite these legislative and policy advances, Article 55 of the Family
Code, which enumerates the grounds for legal separation, continues to require
“repeated physical violence or grossly abusive conduct” before abuse may justify
legal separation. This standard is restrictive and fails to reflect the broader
understanding of domestic violence now recognized under existing laws. By
requiring repeated acts of physical violence or conduct already considered
grossly abusive, the current provision effectively forces victim-survivors to
endure prolonged harm before they may avail themselves of legal relief. Moreover,
the existing language inadequately captures other forms of abuse, such as
psychological, emotional, and sexual violence, which frequently occur within the
private sphere of the household.

      The proposed measure therefore, seeks to update and harmonize the
grounds for legal separation under Article 55 of the Family Code with the
principles embodied in more recent legislation, particularly Republic Act No.
9262, by recognizing abusive conduct and violence, including psychological and
sexual abuse, as sufficient grounds for legal separation without requiring
repeated physical harm. This amendment does not conflict with existing laws
but instead aligns the Family Code with the State’s broader framework for
protecting victims of domestic violence and abuse.

       While the proposed amendment applies equally to both spouses regardless
of gender, it is widely acknowledged that women remain disproportionately
affected by domestic and intimate partner violence. Updating the grounds for
legal separation will therefore strengthen legal protection for victim-survivors
while remaining consistent with the State’s constitutional mandate under Article
II, Section 14 of the 1987 Constitution, which recognizes the role of women in
nation-building and ensures the fundamental equality of women and men before
the law.

      By removing outdated limitations and recognizing the realities of abuse
within marriage, this measure reinforces the State’s commitment to safeguarding
the dignity, safety, and well-being of individuals within the family.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representative of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Grounds for Legal Separation — Article 55 of E.O. No. 209,
otherwise known as “Family Code of the Philippines” is hereby amended to read
as follows:

      "Article 55. A petition for legal separation may be filed on any of the
      following grounds;

      (1) [Repeated physical] ANY FORM OF GENDER BASED DOMESTIC
      violence, or [grossly] ABUSIVE CONDUCT directed against the
      petitioner, a common child, or a child of the petitioner;
      X X X."

      Section 2. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, and issuances, or
portions thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       Section 3. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
