                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act. No. 7394, or the Consumer Act of the Philippines, has stood as
the country’s principal consumer protection statute since 1992. It was designed for a
predominantly physical, retail-based economy. In the more than three decades since
its enactment, Philippine commerce has been transformed by e-commerce, digital
marketplaces, subscription-based services, and cross-border online transactions —
developments the Act did not originally contemplate.

       While Republic Act No. 11967 (the Internet Transactions Act) and Republic
Act No. 11765 (the Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act) have
partially addressed these gaps, consumer protection in the Philippines remains
fragmented across statutes and agencies, redress mechanisms remain slow and
inaccessible for small-value claims, penalties have not kept pace with inflation or the
scale of modern commercial harm, and there is no unified, low-cost venue for
consumers to pursue collective or individual complaints.

      This bill amends RA 7394 to:

     1. Clarify the liability of online marketplaces and digital platforms for
        products and services sold through them;

     2. Establish an accessible, low-cost Consumer Redress Mechanism for small-
        value claims;

     3. Provide a clear collective redress (class complaint) procedure for consumer
        harms affecting multiple persons;
     4. Update and index penalties to inflation, and strengthen enforcement powers
        of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);

     5. Close the product recall gap by making the sale of recalled products a
        distinct offense;

     6. Regulate subscription traps, forced auto-renewal, and dark patterns in
        digital transactions, including a "click-to-cancel" requirement;

     7. Establish an Inter-Agency Consumer Protection Coordinating Council to
        harmonize enforcement across DTI, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP),
        the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the National
        Telecommunications Commission (NTC), and other sector regulators; and

     8. Strengthen enforcement against foreign merchants and platforms targeting
        Filipino consumers.

       This measure builds on the consumer protection gains of the Internet
Transactions Act and the Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act
by closing the gaps that remain in the core Consumer Act itself, so that every Filipino
consumer — whether transacting in a public market or on a mobile application —
has equal access to protection and redress.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
  STRENGTHENING CONSUMER PROTECTION IN THE PHILIPPINES BY
   AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7394, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
 "CONSUMER ACT OF THE PHILIPPINES," TO EXTEND PROTECTION TO
    ONLINE AND DIGITAL TRANSACTIONS, ESTABLISH ACCESSIBLE
REDRESS MECHANISMS, STRENGTHEN ENFORCEMENT, AND FOR OTHER
                          PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Enhanced Consumer
Protection Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article 2 of Republic Act No. 7394 is hereby
amended to read as follows:

     “ART. 2. Declaration of Basic Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
     the interests of the consumer, promote the general welfare, and establish
     standards of conduct for business and industry, whether such business or
     industry operates through physical, online, or digital means, and whether
     the seller, supplier, manufacturer, or service provider is domestic or
     foreign, provided that the transaction targets or affects consumers in the
     Philippines. Towards this end, the State shall recognize the eight (8) basic
     consumer rights, namely: the right to basic needs, the right to safety, the
     right to information, the right to choose, the right to redress, the right to
     consumer education, the right to representation, and the right to a healthy

          accessible, affordable, and timely.”

     SEC. 3. De nition of Terms. – Article 4 of Republic Act No. 7394 is hereby amended
     to add the following de nitions:

          (bb) Online Marketplace refers to any website, application, or digital
              platform that enables third-party sellers, suppliers, or service
              providers to offer, advertise, or sell consumer products or
              services to consumers in the Philippines, regardless of whether
              the operator of the platform is the seller of record.

          (cc) Online Marketplace Operator refers to any natural or juridical
              person, whether domestic or foreign, that owns, operates, or
              controls an online marketplace.

          (dd) Digital Dark Pattern refers to any user interface or user
             experience design, in a website or application, that is intended or
             has the effect of subverting or impairing a consumer’s autonomy,
             decision-making, or choice, including but not limited to designs
             that make it substantially more dif cult to cancel a service than
             to subscribe to it, or that obscure material terms, fees, or
             conditions of a transaction.

          (ee) Recalled Product refers to any consumer product that a
              manufacturer, distributor, importer, or seller, or a government
              regulatory agency, has publicly announced or ordered to be
              withdrawn from the market due to a defect, hazard, or non-
              compliance with applicable safety or quality standards.

          (ff) Cross-Border Merchant refers to a seller, supplier, or online
               marketplace operator not established in the Philippines that
               targets or maintains substantial commercial interaction with
               consumers located in the Philippines.

     SEC. 4. Online Marketplace Liability. – A new Article 98-A is hereby inserted into
     Title III, Chapter V of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

          “ARTICLE 98-A. Liability of Online Marketplace Operators. – (a) An
          online marketplace operator shall be jointly and severally liable with a
          third-party seller for any violation of this Act involving a product or
          service sold through its platform where:

          SEC 5. Consumer Redress Mechanism. – A new Article 159-A is hereby inserted into
          Title V, Chapter III of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

               “ARTICLE 159-A. Consumer Redress Mechanism. – (a) There is hereby
               established, within the Department, a Consumer Redress Mechanism to
               provide consumers with an accessible, expeditious, and low-cost venue
               for resolving consumer complaints not exceeding Four Hundred
               Thousand Pesos (₱400,000.00) in claimed value, without prejudice to a
               consumer’s right to pursue any other remedy available under law.

               (b) Proceedings under this Article shall be conducted through mediation
               in the rst instance, and, failing settlement, through summary
               adjudication by a duly designated Consumer Arbiter. No formal rules of
               evidence or procedure applicable in regular courts shall be strictly
               applied, and legal representation shall not be required.

SEC. 6. Collective Redress for Consumer Complaints. – A new Article 159-B is
hereby inserted into Title V, Chapter III of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

       “ARTICLE 159-B. Collective Consumer Complaints. – (a) Where a
       violation of this Act affects, or is likely to affect, twenty (20) or more
       consumers arising from substantially the same act or practice of a
       respondent, any of the affected consumers, an accredited consumer
       organization, or the Department motu proprio, may le a collective
       consumer complaint before the Consumer Redress Mechanism
       established under Article 165-A of this Act, notwithstanding the
       individual claim value of any single consumer.

       (b) The Department shall promulgate rules governing notice to affected
       consumers, opt-in or opt-out procedures, and the distribution of any
       monetary award or restitution arising from a collective consumer
       complaint.

       (c) Nothing in this Article shall be construed to limit the right of
       consumers to avail of class suits under the Rules of Court.”

SEC. 8. Sale of Recalled Products Prohibited. – A new Article 98-B is hereby
inserted into Title III, Chapter V of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

      “ARTICLE 98-B. Prohibition on the Sale of Recalled Products. – (a) It shall
      be unlawful for any manufacturer, distributor, importer, seller, or online
      marketplace operator to sell, offer for sale, or continue to list for sale, any
      consumer product that has been recalled by a government regulatory
      agency or voluntarily recalled by its manufacturer or importer, from the
      date such recall is publicly announced or ordered.

      (b) The Department, in coordination with the concerned regulatory
      agency, shall maintain a publicly accessible registry of recalled products.

      (c) Any person who violates this Article shall be subject to the penalties
      provided under Article 60 of this Act, without prejudice to product
      liability claims under existing law.”

     “ARTICLE 52-A. Protection Against Unfair Digital Practices. — (a) Any
     seller or supplier offering a subscription, membership, or recurring-billing
     service to consumers shall:

     (1) clearly and conspicuously disclose the cost, frequency, and terms of
         the recurring charge, and the terms of auto-renewal, before the

     (2) obtain a consumer’s af rmative, unambiguous consent to any
         recurring charge, separate from consent to any other term of the
         transaction; and

     (3) provide a mechanism for cancellation that is at least as simple as the
         mechanism by which the consumer subscribed, and which does not
         require the consumer to contact customer service by phone or in
         person if the subscription was entered into online.

     (b) The use of digital dark patterns, as de ned under Article 4 of this Act,
     in connection with a consumer transaction is hereby declared an unfair
     and unconscionable sales act or practice under Chapter I, Title III, Book II
     of this Act.

     (c) The Department shall issue implementing rules further de ning
     prohibited dark patterns, in consultation with the Department of
     Information and Communications Technology and the National Privacy
     Commission.”

     Coordinating Council, to be chaired by the Secretary of Trade and
     Industry, and composed of representatives from the Bangko Sentral ng
     Pilipinas, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the Insurance
     Commission, the National Telecommunications Commission, the
     Department of Information and Communications Technology, the
     National Privacy Commission, the Food and Drug Administration, and
     the Department of Agriculture.

SEC. 11. Enforcement Against Cross-Border Merchants. – A new Article 98-C is
hereby inserted into Title III, Chapter V of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

     “ARTICLE 98-C. Cross-Border Enforcement. – (a) A cross-border merchant
     that targets or maintains substantial commercial interaction with
     consumers in the Philippines shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the
     Department and the provisions of this Act to the same extent as a
     merchant established in the Philippines.

     (b) The Department may require an online marketplace operator to restrict
     or delist a cross-border merchant found, after due process, to have
     violated this Act and to have failed or refused to satisfy a judgment, order,
     or award rendered under this Act.

     (c) The Department shall enter into cooperative arrangements with
     foreign consumer protection authorities for the exchange of information
     and mutual enforcement assistance, consistent with existing treaties and
     Philippine law.”

SEC. 12. Consumer Education. – A new Article 154-A is hereby inserted into Title V,
Chapter II of Republic Act No. 7394, to read as follows:

     “The Department shall integrate digital and e-commerce consumer
     literacy – including recognition of online scams, dark patterns, and the
     proper use of the Consumer Redress Mechanism – into its consumer
     education programs and into the consumer education curricula of the
     Department of Education and the Commission on Higher Education.”
