                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 15 of Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that the
State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people, while Section 11 of
Article XIII directs the State to adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to
health development and to make essential health services available at affordable
cost, with priority to the underprivileged and sick. In fulfillment of these mandates,

      International evidence reinforces these concerns. Several studies found
substantial disparities in the availability, affordability, and pricing of anticancer
medicines in low- and middle-income countries, and also reported a significant
burden of cancer-related financial toxicity in these settings.5 The ESMO Global

medicine availability, accessibility, and out-of-pocket costs, particularly in lower-
income settings. These findings indicate that effective cancer policy requires not
only statutory coverage but also reliable medicine supply, geographic accessibility,
financial protection, and coordinated delivery systems.6

       Recognizing these local and international findings, this proposed legislation
seeks to strengthen Republic Act No. 11215 by improving the delivery and
accessibility of cancer and supportive-palliative medicines. It strengthens the Cancer
Assistance Fund through complementary assistance and coordination with
PhilHealth and other government programs, integrates CAF processing into the
existing Malasakit Center mechanism, and institutionalizes the Cancer and
Supportive-Palliative Medicines Access Program (CSPMAP) as a mechanism for

        SEC. 21. PhilHealth Benefits for Cancer.        The Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation shall expand its benefit packages to include primary
care screening, detection, diagnosis, treatment assistance, supportive care,

      The development or expansion of any PhilHealth benefit shall go

        SEC. 23. Cancer and Related Supportive Care Medicines.               The
DOH, and other concerned government agencies shall implement reforms
supporting early access to essential medicines, innovative medicines and
health technologies, to ensure highest possible chance of survival among
people with cancer. The reforms include facilitating quick access to drugs for
compassionate use and developing a more responsive system for effectively
addressing emergency cases.

      The FDA shall create a dedicated and streamlined process, not
exceeding one (1) year, for the licensing of innovator and generic cancer
medication, subject to appropriate quality checks and compliance with
minimum standards, such as, but not limited to, being approved and used for
cancer treatment in other countries.

      The Cancer and Supportive-Palliative Medicines Access Program
(CSPMAP) implemented by the Department of Health is hereby
institutionalized as a national mechanism for facilitating access to cancer and
supportive-palliative medicines.

      The DOH shall maintain and expand a geographically distributed
network of CSPMAP access sites. The DOH shall ensure at least one (1)
designated CSPMAP access site in each administrative region and shall
designate additional access sites based on cancer burden, population, distance
from existing access sites, transportation barriers, health facility capacity, and
other relevant health system factors.

      The DOH shall prioritize the designation or establishment of access
sites in underserved and geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.


                                                                                6
      A DOH hospital or other public health facility may serve as a CSPMAP
access site upon compliance with standards prescribed by the DOH for
medicine storage, dispensing, personnel, patient navigation, inventory
management, referral, reporting, and continuity of care.

      The DOH shall establish standard procedures for medicine dispensing,
inventory monitoring, stock forecasting, replenishment, redistribution, and
reporting across CSPMAP access sites.

      The DOH shall establish a continuity-of-care mechanism to facilitate
the referral of a patient to another CSPMAP access site when the required
medicine is temporarily unavailable at the designated access site.

      The DOH shall regularly review and update the list of cancer and
supportive-palliative medicines made available through CSPMAP based on
evidence-based treatment guidelines, Health Technology Assessment, safety
and efficacy requirements, and applicable pharmaceutical regulations.

      The designation of a facility as a CSPMAP access site shall not replace
