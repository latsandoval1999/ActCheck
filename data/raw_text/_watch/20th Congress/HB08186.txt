                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The current setup of food markets and “bagsakans” in the Philippines is characterized by a
mix of wholesale and retail operations that often lack clear delineations. Intermediaries buy
produce from farmers, which they subsequently sell in a non-standardized and largely
unregulated manner. This results in inefficiencies such as low profit margins for farmers and
fisher folk, high transaction costs, inconsistent pricing, and increased food prices for
consumers. To date, the concept of Bagsakan Centers has not been well-defined nor
regulated, leading to a lack of transparency and accountability, making it difficult to ensure the
consistent availability, quality, and safety of food products.

The urgent need to address the country’s food security issues through the systematic
streamlining of its food distribution process has long been evident. Establishing a national food
hub will play a crucial role in shortening the supply chain from producer to consumer by
effectively transferring market signals and information between parties, thus overcoming the
limitations of traditional commodity supply chains. Implementing a food hub model will not only
standardize logistics but also ensure the efficient movement of food products, thereby
enhancing overall efficiency. International models have already set benchmarks for market
efficiency and established protocols and policies for food handling, providing valuable insights
for implementation.

Aligned with the President's commitment to food security and inclusivity as articulated in his
8-Point Socioeconomic Agenda, a national food hub will play a pivotal role in addressing these
pressing issues. By centralizing food distribution and implementing efficient logistics, a
national food hub will contribute to the broader goal of enhancing food security and promoting
economic development across the Philippines.

Reaffirming his commitment to food security and inclusivity, the President stated in his
Opening Remarks during the World Economic Forum Panel Discussion on Moving Towards
Nutrition Security held in Davos, Switzerland in January 18, 2023:

“Food security remains at the forefront of our national agenda. Anchored on our vision for a
prosperous, resilient, and secure Philippines by Year 2040, the overarching goals of this
administration are to build an inclusive society where no one is hungry; where Filipinos love
long and healthy lives; and, where they are provided with an environment built upon trust and
security.”

This initiative is further reinforced by the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028, which calls
for the modernization of agribusiness and agriculture, digitization, maintenance of low and
stable food and overall price points, promotion of a dynamic food innovation system,
improvement of connectivity among food trading markets, support for farmers and fisher folk,
promotion of sustainable practices, infrastructure investments, and the development of public-
private partnerships. Additionally, strategic frameworks such as the Philippine Food Chain
Logistics Masterplan (PFCLM) 2023–2033, the Fisheries Modernization and Industrialization
Plan 2021–2030, and the Three-Year Food Logistics Action Agenda 2023–2026 emphasize
the government's commitment to implementing effective, modernized, and sustainable
nationwide food chain logistics systems to promote economic growth while guaranteeing food
accessibility, availability, and affordability for every Filipino.

This further underscores the necessity for a national food hub that will not only serve as a
backbone and example of the planned logistics improvement for the country, but will also
enhance research and quality control, warehousing, food processing, international shipping,
marketing services, and trading for local and foreign markets.

The Clark National Food Hub (Clark NFH) is designed to achieve these objectives for the
Filipino people. Additionally, the Clark National Food Hub aims to be the leading source of
quality Halal food outside Mindanao, ensuring food inclusivity for Muslim Filipinos and
residents nationwide.

The Clark NFH will revolutionize the food distribution system in the Philippines by exploring
the establishment of additional food hubs outside of Metro Manila, with Clark, Pampanga
identified as a strategic location in North Luzon.

Central Luzon, known as the Rice Granary of the Philippines, is one of the largest producers
of the country's food staples, playing a vital role in the Philippine economy. Its accessibility to
Metro Manila and consistent ranking among the nation's fastest-growing regions further
underscores its substantial contribution to the national GDP.

Located at the heart of Central Luzon, the Clark Freeport Zone emerges as the ideal site for
the proposed national food hub. Situated within the Clark Civil Aviation Complex, the
establishment of the Clark NFH leverages its exceptional connectivity, owing to its close
proximity to the newly developed terminal of the Clark International Airport (CRK). It boasts
seamless access to major road networks, including the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX) to
the south and the Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway (TPLEX) to the north. Moreover,
its strategic location facilitates efficient transportation via the Subic-Clark-Tarlac Expressway
(SCTEX), linking it directly to the Subic seaport. Furthermore, the Clark NFH shows promising
potential for connectivity via rail, specifically through the North-South Commuter Railway
(NSCR) and the planned Subic-Clark Railway (SCR), thus bolstering its accessibility and
logistical capabilities.

Aligned with Clark's vision as the Philippines' next economic center, the Clark NFH will be a
key contributor to broader regional development initiatives, solidifying Clark's pivotal role as a
catalyst for national growth.

Thus, through this bill, the Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) will harness the
opportunity presented by establishing a 60-hectare Clark NFH inside the Clark Civil Aviation
Complex. This will support the national government’s vision to make the Philippines a leading
agricultural resource hub in the region and the world.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE CLARK NATIONAL FOOD HUB, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The Clark National Food Hub Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote food
security and inclusivity through the unimpeded trade and flow of agricultural products and
agro-food products by facilitating seamless logistics and transport distribution.
         The State shall enable farmers, fisher folk, livestock and poultry raisers, producers,
and cooperatives to sell their products at a higher profit, and provide wholesalers, institutional
buyers, and end consumers access to quality agricultural products and agro-food products at
lower prices.
         Toward this end, a comprehensive program for the creation, management, and
operation of the Clark National Food Hub with a strategic nationwide trading network is hereby
established.
         Through the Clark National Food Hub, the State shall also facilitate higher mobility of
locally produced and available agricultural products and agro-food products across the entire
country at a reduced cost, alongside the export of high-value crops to international markets,
thereby increasing the profitability and global competitiveness of Filipino farmers.
         The establishment of the Clark National Food Hub shall likewise ensure availability of
quality, affordable Halal food outside Mindanao for Muslim Filipinos and residents across the
country, as well as foreign nationals residing or in transit in the country.
         The State shall also provide and allocate resources for efficient research and quality
control, compliance with international food safety and regulations, cold storage systems,
warehousing, food processing and packaging, international shipping, marketing services and
trading for local and foreign markets.
         Finally, the State remains firm in its commitment to enhance the institutional and
human capacities of key government agencies in enforcing regulations and enhance
consumer awareness on the safety and quality of food products sold in the Philippine market.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act,

(a)    Agricultural product refers to products that result from agricultural activities which can
be raw or processed goods that come from farming, livestock, forestry, and fishing, i.e. goods
that come from plants, animals, and microorganisms. The term shall be synonymous with and
can be used interchangeably with “agricultural commodities”, “agricultural produce”, “agro
products”, and “agro produce,” and includes crops, livestock, poultry, and dairy.

(b)    Agro-food product refers to products that belong to a category of agricultural products
that encompass all food items that are made for human and animal consumption, such as
animal feed, farmed fish, and honey, and such other food made from agricultural products.

(c)   Assembly market refers to rural or town markets, normally situated on main highways
connecting rural-urban centers and near local transportation interchange points.

(d)  Clark Civil Aviation Complex (CCAC) refers to the 2,367-hectare property operated,
managed and maintained by the Clark International Airport Corporation.

(e)    Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) refers to the wholly-owned subsidiary of
the Clark Development Corporation which was incorporated in 1994 and created primarily to
develop, operate, manage and maintain the CCAC within the Clark Freeport Zone in the
province of Pampanga.

(f)     Clark National Food Hub refers to the agricultural product and agro-food terminal and
trading hub or the facility in the CCAC where farmers, first and second-level cooperatives,
from rural areas or directly from farms, can offer their products for sale directly to wholesalers
and direct customers, including interested foreign buyers and importers, in the food industry
like hotels, restaurants, and catering services, thus eliminating any middleman in the sale of
agricultural and agro-food products.

(g)     Cold storage refers to an insulated facility designed to maintain an artificially generated
temperature or range of temperatures used to store temperature-sensitive and perishable
agricultural commodities or food items to preserve food quality, ensure food safety, and
prevent deterioration or decay.

(h)     Halal food refers to any food products that are lawful or permissible to consume under
Shari’ah law and as defined in the Quran. Also refers to food and non-food products that are
lawful consumption in accordance to Shari’ah law.

(i)    Hub-and-spoke refers to a system of distribution comprised of interrelated units
(spokes) converging into a main central point (hub). The main hub, such as the Clark National
Food Hub, is where the goods are stocked, stored, sold, packaged, or traded, and the spokes
represent the various distribution and logistics centers that facilitate the transportation,
handling, and delivery of goods from the suppliers to end users.

(j)   Intermodal transportation hub refers to a place or facility used in the transportation of
goods and persons using two or more modes of transportation such as truck, rail, sea, and
air.

(k)   Non-perishable agricultural commodities refer to food products that are processed or
packaged for prolonged storage.

(l)     Pass-through fees refer to taxes, fees, charges, and other impositions upon goods
carried into or out of, or passing through the territorial jurisdiction of local government units in
the guise of a toll, charges for wharfage, or other taxes, fees, or charges in any form upon
agricultural products, goods, or merchandise.

(m)     Perishable agricultural commodities refer to agricultural products that, because of their
inherent quantities or dependence upon conditions of soil or weather, mature, decompose,
decay, or deteriorate and undergo material changes of form and quality that render them
unsuitable for the use for which they were produced. May also refer to any food that may spoil
or otherwise become unfit for human consumption because of its nature, type or physical
condition.

(n)    Public-Private Partnership (PPP) refers to a contractual arrangement between a
government implementing agency and a private partner to finance, design, construct, operate,
and maintain, or any combination or variation thereof, infrastructure or development projects
and services which are typically provided by the public sector, where each party shares in the
associated risks, and where the investment recovery of the Private Partner is linked to
performance.

(o)   Ripening room refers to a facility where environmental conditions, such as
temperature, relative humidity, and concentration of ethylene, oxygen, and carbon dioxide,
may be modified to control the ripening process of fruits and vegetables.

(p)     Sorting and grading line refers to a manual, mechanical, or fully-automated system used
to determine the quality and to sort food products into categories on the basis of weight, size,
shape, surface, color, firmness, and other qualities.

(q)    Throughput capacity refers to the volume of cargo discharged and loaded at a Clark
National Food Hub over a given period.

SEC. 4. Establishment of the Clark National Food Hub. – The Clark International Airport
Corporation (CIAC), as the lead implementing agency and governing body, shall establish,
manage, and operate the Clark National Food Hub, consistent with the following policies,
guidelines, and standards:

(a)     The Clark National Food Hub shall be built on a property with total area of
approximately sixty (60) hectares with an expansion site of approximately twelve (12) hectares
within the Clark Civil Aviation Complex (CCAC). It shall be developed into and operated as a
self-sustaining, commercial, industrial, trading, agro-industrial, and logistics hub which
provides among others, aviation-related logistics activities within the CCAC in order to promote
food security, contribute to national economic growth, and effectively encourage and attract
legitimate and productive foreign and local investments and market transactions;

(b)     The Clark National Food Hub shall provide adequate facilities and support
infrastructure such as an intermodal transportation hub, parking space, warehouse, ripening
room, cold storage, sorting and grading line, or similar facilities;

(c)     The Clark National Food Hub shall be fully integrated into the transportation and
mobility system of the CCAC and the greater Luzon area;

(d)     To contribute to the development of special trading markets for Halal food production
pursuant to Republic Act No. 10817, or the “Philippine Halal Export Development and
Promotion Act of 2016”, the Clark National Food Hub shall be the primary trade hub catering
to Halal food producers and investors outside Mindanao, making Halal food more accessible
to the Muslim population residing in the rest of the Philippines.

(e)      The CIAC is hereby authorized to transfer the management and operation of the Clark
National Food Hub to a qualified member of the private sector through any of, but not limited
to, the following modes:

       i.      Design, Build, Finance, Operate, and Maintain (DBFOM) arrangement, where
       the infrastructure or facilities shall be designed, constructed, financed operated and
       maintained by a private entity which shall be entitled to remuneration for a specified
       period from the end users of the project through fares or tolls, or from the CIAC in the
       form of availability payments. At the end of the period, operating control is transferred
       back to the CIAC.
       ii.    Hybrid Public-Private Partnership (PPP), where the infrastructure or facilities shall
       be constructed by the CIAC through public funding, while the private entity shall
       undertake the operation and maintenance thereof;

       iii.    Joint Venture between the private entity and the CIAC, where the private entity
       shall possess and control majority ownership of the infrastructure or facilities; or

       iv.     Any other PPP mode as defined and allowed under existing Philippine laws,
       rules, and regulations.

SEC. 5. Implementing Agency. – The CIAC shall implement, operate, and manage the Clark
National Food Hub.

As the implementing agency, CIAC shall:

(a)    Secure the necessary funding and support for the implementation of the projects of the
Clark National Food Hub;

(b)     Regulate the use by logistics providers, suppliers, and food producers of the Clark
National Food Hub and issue the permit therefor. The CIAC is thus authorized to collect a
permit fee for such authority to do business in the Clark National Food Hub;

(c)    Ensure adherence in the implementation of the project with all relevant and applicable
national and international standards and best practices for the optimum operation of the Clark
National Food Hub;

(d)     Implement policies on food transport and logistics within the Clark National Food Hub
Project to ensure safe, economical, and efficient air travel;

(e)      Perform such other related functions as may be authorized by the President of the
Philippines in the development and operation of the Clark National Food Hub at every stage
of its implementation.

SEC. 6. Nationwide Hub-and-Spoke Trading Network. – The Department of Agriculture
(DA) and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall establish backward and forward
linkages with the Clark National Food Hub through the creation of an integrated nationwide
hub-and-spoke trading network, with the Clark National Food Hub as the hub or central point.
This network will facilitate the movement of food products from assembly markets of
agricultural and agro-food commodities to end users, including exporters, food processing
plants, wholesalers, traders, retail markets, and restaurants.

SEC. 7. Food Safety and Quality Standards. – To strengthen the implementation of measures
in managing risks and addressing food safety and quality standards, including product
uniformity, packaging and handling, the Clark National Food Hub shall pioneer the promotion
of food labeling and world-class food safety measures in the country, and shall ensure
mandatory and strict compliance to existing national policies and standards in food handling,
storage, and trading, as determined by the appropriate regulatory agencies.
        These measures shall include the traceability of products to support targeted
regulations and regulatory actions to specific sources of risks, and initiatives to harmonize and
streamline regulatory services and strictly impose quality standards to ensure safe and quality
products, facilitate trade, and promote ease of doing business.
        Primarily, these objectives shall be achieved through:

(a)    The establishment of first border inspection facilities, laboratories, and container yards
for purposes of inspection and examination of containerized poultry, animal products, fish, and
other agricultural commodities, ensuring the safety and quality standards of the products
preventing the proliferation of agricultural smuggling and reducing the risk of the agricultural
commodities from pests, other infestations, and contamination; and
(b)     The creation of cold storage facilities that enable the preservation of examined and
inspected poultry, animal products, fish, and other agricultural commodities that have passed
the quality assurance and quality control procedures ensuring food safety and quality
standards prior to shipping or distribution.

SEC. 8. Green Energy and Waste Management. – The CIAC shall promote the use of green
energy, characterized as any energy type that is generated from natural resources, such as
sunlight, wind or water, for the Clark National Food Hub and other facilities that will support
the hub.
        Moreover, the CIAC shall ensure that proper waste management, including but not
limited to reduction, conversion and proper disposal of waste, to address environmental
sustainability.

SEC. 9. Promotion of Agro Processing and Value-Adding Activities within the Clark
Freeport Zone (CFZ). – The CIAC and the Clark Development Corporation (CDC) shall
promote value-adding activities and encourage investments in agro-food processing to
complement and support the Clark National Food Hub and food security thrust of the
government.

SEC. 10. Development of an Agriculture Production Base in Luzon. – The DA, in
cooperation with agriculture state universities and colleges (ASUCs), shall collaborate in the
development of an agriculture production base in Luzon to ensure regular and sustainable
supply of agricultural commodities for the Clark National Food Hub.

SEC. 11. Work Plan. – The CIAC, as the implementing agency, shall develop the following:
(a)      A pattern of cooperation and mutual assistance with National Government Agencies
such as the DA, DTI, and Department of Transportation (DOTr), non-governmental
organizations, cooperatives, traders unions, and the private sector, which shall maximize free
market, people empowerment and participatory approaches to program formulation,
implementation and monitoring, including a plan for bringing the program down to the farmers,
fisher folk, farmer and fisher folk cooperatives, producers, traders, and wholesalers by utilizing
available personnel and facilities of the Clark National Food Hub; and
(b)      A schedule of short-term, medium-term, and long-term targets on research and
development, marketing, trade promotion initiatives, and capacity building, among others.

SEC. 12. Whole-of-Nation Approach; Inter-Agency Collaboration. – The CIAC, together
and in close coordination with key agencies such as the DA, DTI, DOTr, National Economic
and Development Authority (NEDA), Department of Health (DOH), Department of Science
and Technology (DOST), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and
Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Center shall collaborate in identifying, strengthening, and
implementing the strategic and harmonized Work Plan and such other policies, regulations,
and standards which may be deemed appropriate under this Act.
       Furthermore, the Clark National Food Hub shall be the central hub of agricultural and
agro-food commodities that shall establish a link towards several end-users.

SEC. 13. The Clark National Food Hub Technical Working Group (CNFH-TWG). – There
is hereby constituted a Clark National Food Hub Technical Working Group (CNFH-TWG)
which shall be composed of the heads of the following agencies or organizations or their duly
authorized representatives who shall at least be third-ranking officers thereof:
       (a) CIAC;
       (b) DA;
        (c) DTI;
        (d) NEDA;
        (e) DOTr;
        (f) DOH;
        (g) DOST;
        (h) DILG;
        (i) PPP Center;
        (j) One representative from a nationally recognized agricultural product or agri-food
            product producers organization; and
        (k) One representative from a nationally recognized local business and industry
            association.

The President of the CIAC shall act as the Chairperson of the CNFH-TWG, and the
Secretaries of the DA and DTI shall act as the Vice-Chairpersons thereof. The appropriate
office of the CIAC, as the latter may designate, shall serve as the Secretariat of the CNFH-
TWG.
        The CNFH-TWG shall be the primary policy planning and coordinating body of the
government in ensuring the proper functioning of the Clark National Food Hub. As such, the
CNFH-TWG shall formulate, provide, and prescribe strategic policies in support of the
implementation, management, and operations of the Clark National Food Hub.

SEC. 14. Stakeholder Consultations. – The CIAC, in coordination with the rest of the CNFH-
TWG, shall conduct regular consultations with and among the project stakeholders and
beneficiaries on the planning, implementation, and evaluation of the Clark National Food Hub.

SEC. 15. Funding. – The amount of Three Billion Pesos (₱3,000,000,000) is hereby
appropriated for the initial year of implementation of this Act.
         Thereafter, the amount of such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the
budget of the CIAC in the annual General Appropriations Act for the implementation and
personnel requirements, monitoring and evaluation of the concession agreement, carrying out
of capacity building programs, and further sustainability initiatives for this project, including but
not limited to, the construction of access roads and utility corridors, materials recovery facility,
logistics equipment necessary for the efficient operation of the Clark National Food Hub,
development of information and communications technology (ICT) systems that support online
or digital transactions, including electronic auctions, and other essential or useful utility
infrastructure within and connected to the project site, including access and connectivity to the
airport and seaport.
         In addition, the establishment and operation of the terminal and trading centers will be
made with the available participatory funding from the national government.

SEC. 16. Tax Incentives. The provisions of existing laws, rules, and regulations to the
contrary notwithstanding, no national and local taxes shall be imposed on registered and/or
accredited logistics providers, business enterprises, traders, and investors within the Clark
National Food Hub.
        In lieu of said taxes, a five percent (5%) tax on gross income earned shall be paid by
all registered and/or accredited logistics providers, business enterprises, traders, and
investors within the Clark National Food Hub and shall be directly remitted as follows: three
percent (3%) to the National Government; one percent (1%) to the Treasurer’s Office of the
Municipality of Mabalacat, Province of Pampanga; and one percent (1%) to the CIAC.
        To ensure free flow or movement of goods and capital equipment within, into, and
exported out of the Clark National Food Hub, tax and duty-free importation of raw materials
and capital equipment shall be provided to registered and/or accredited logistics providers,
business enterprises, traders, and investors involved in and operating within the Clark National
Food Hub.
        However, the exportation or removal of goods from the territory of the Clark National
Food Hub to the other parts of the Philippine territory, except to Subic Bay Freeport Zone,
shall be subject to customs duties and taxes under the Republic Act No. 10863, or the
“Customs Modernization and Tariff Act (CMTA)”, and other relevant tax laws of the Philippines.
        The governing body, implementing agency, and principal investment promotion agency
(IPA) of the Clark National Food Hub shall be the CIAC. The CDC shall be the IPA for purposes
of the grant and administration of fiscal incentives only.

SEC. 17. Regulation and Accreditation of Logistic Providers, Suppliers, and Food
Producers. – Consistent with the authority of CIAC to authorize and enter into contracts with
private corporations, associations, or entities to operate or manage other businesses and
activities inside the CCAC, the nationwide trading network supporting the Clark National Food
Hub shall be exclusively comprised of logistics providers, suppliers, assembly markets,
wholesalers and importers, food traders, buyers, including retailers, food processors,
importers, farmers, fisher folk, and cooperatives registered with and accredited by the CIAC,
through appropriate public-private partnership arrangements and investment schemes, as
capable of servicing the operational requirements, or compliant with the food security and
safety standards implemented in the Clark National Food Hub.
         The CIAC shall likewise ensure that such standards, regulation, and accreditation
policies issued by appropriate government agencies are upheld.

SEC. 18. Congressional Oversight Committee. – A Congressional Oversight Committee,
hereinafter referred to as the Clark National Food Hub Congressional Oversight
Committee (CNFH-COC), shall be constituted to monitor and ensure the proper
implementation of this Act. It shall be composed of five (5) members from the Senate, which
shall include the Chairpersons of the Committee on Trade, Commerce and Entrepreneurship;
Agriculture, Food and Agrarian Reform; and Finance, and five (5) members from the House
of Representatives, which shall include the Chairperson of the Committees on Trade and
Industry; Agriculture and Food; and Appropriations.
         The Chairpersons of the Senate Committee on Trade, Commerce and
Entrepreneurship, and the House of Representatives Committee on Trade and Industry shall
jointly chair the CNFH-COC.
         The CNFH-COC shall be deactivated after five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred eighty (180) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the CIAC, assisted by the CNFH-TWG and upon consultations with
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement this
Act.

SEC. 20. Supervision and Control. – For purposes of policy direction and coordination, the
CIAC shall be under the direct supervision and control of the Office of the President of the
Philippines, through the Bases Conversion and Development Authority.

SEC. 21. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional,
the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of other provisions hereof.

SEC. 22. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 23. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
