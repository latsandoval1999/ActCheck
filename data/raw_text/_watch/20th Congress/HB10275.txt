                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Every Filipino worker knows the value of honest labor. Each peso earned
represents time away from family, daily sacrifice, and the hope of building a better
life. Yet for many middle-income earners, the present tax system takes a significant
portion of their income even before they are able to meet the basic needs of their
households.
        The current individual income tax structure exempts only the first Two
Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 250,000.00) of annual taxable income. This
threshold was introduced under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion
(TRAIN) Law, which took effect in 2018. While this reform provided much-needed
relief at the time, the real value of the PhP 250,000.00 exemption has since been
substantially eroded by inflation.
       Using the 2018-based Consumer Price Index as reference, the purchasing
power of the peso has fallen to approximately Seventy-Five Centavos (PhP 0.75) as
of the latest available 2026 data. In practical terms, the same basket of goods and
services that could be purchased for PhP 250,000.00 in 2018 would require
approximately PhP 333,000.00 today. Conversely, the present PhP 250,000.00 tax-
free threshold now carries only about PhP 187,500.00 in 2018 purchasing power.
Thus, without any legislative adjustment, inflation has effectively reduced the real
value of the tax exemption by about Twenty-Five Percent (25%).
        This means that the tax system has become heavier on workers not because
Congress imposed a new tax, but because the tax-free threshold has remained fixed
while the cost of living has continued to rise. Food, fuel, transportation, rent,
utilities, education, and health care have become more expensive, while the
amount of income protected from taxation has stayed the same.
       This situation has created an unfair burden on the working and middle
class. Many workers receive salary increases only to find that their additional
income is reduced by taxes and inflation. This phenomenon, commonly known as
bracket creep, weakens the purchasing power of Filipino families and undermines
the purpose of wage increases and promotions.
      This bill seeks to provide meaningful and structural tax relief by increasing
the annual tax-free income threshold of individual taxpayers to One Million Pesos
(PhP 1,000,000.00) through a phased and fiscally responsible schedule. Under this
measure, the tax-free threshold shall be increased as follows:
      PhP 400,000.00 beginning taxable year 2026;


                                         1
      PhP 800,000.00 beginning taxable year 2027; and
      PhP 1,000,000.00 beginning taxable year 2028 and thereafter.
      This measure is inspired by the principle that ordinary Filipinos should be
allowed to keep more of what they earn. Thus, the proposed Act is aptly titled "Tax-
Free First Million Act." It reflects the simple but powerful idea that the fruits of
honest work should first serve the needs of Filipino families.
       The bill likewise increases the tax-exempt ceiling for thirteenth month pay
and other benefits from Ninety Thousand Pesos (PhP 90,000.00) to One Hundred
Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 150,000.00), thereby allowing employees to enjoy more
of the benefits intended for them.
       To prevent the same inequity from recurring in the future, this measure
provides for periodic inflation-based adjustment of income tax thresholds and
relevant exemptions. This ensures that the tax system remains responsive to actual
economic conditions and does not silently impose a higher burden on workers
merely because of inflation.
        At the same time, the bill recognizes the need to preserve government
revenues for essential public services. Accordingly, it directs the Department of
Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue to implement a Tax Relief and
Revenue Integrity Plan focused on digitalization, risk-based audits, improved
collection efficiency, taxpayer assistance, enforcement against tax evasion, and
stronger compliance from high-risk and high-capacity taxpayers.
      This bill does not seek to weaken the revenue base of the State. Rather, it
seeks to make the tax system fairer by giving relief to compliant workers and
ordinary income earners while strengthening enforcement against non-compliance.
       By increasing take-home pay, protecting family income, and promoting a
more equitable tax system, this measure will help improve household welfare,
stimulate domestic consumption, encourage savings, and restore fairness for
millions of Filipino taxpayers.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
INCREASING THE ANNUAL INCOME TAX EXEMPTION THRESHOLD OF
INDIVIDUAL TAXPAYERS TO ONE MILLION PESOS (P1,000,000.00) THROUGH
A PHASED AND INDEXED TAX RELIEF SCHEDULE, INCREASING THE TAX-
EXEMPT CEILING FOR THIRTEENTH MONTH PAY AND OTHER BENEFITS,
STRENGTHENING    TAX  ADMINISTRATION    TO   PRESERVE     REVENUE
ADEQUACY, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 24 AND 32 OF THE
NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES


      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Tax-Free First
Million Act."
       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote a fair, progressive, efficient, and responsive tax system that
protects the purchasing power of Filipino families, supports the working and
middle class, encourages productivity, and ensures that tax relief is accompanied
by responsible revenue administration.
       Toward this end, the State shall increase the tax-free income threshold of
individual taxpayers in a phased and fiscally responsible manner, periodically
adjust income tax thresholds to reflect inflation, and strengthen tax administration,
compliance, and enforcement to preserve revenue adequacy for essential public
services.

      SEC. 5. Increase in the Tax-Exempt Ceiling for Thirteenth Month Pay
and Other Benefits. — Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:
        "(e) Thirteenth Month Pay and Other Benefits. - Gross benefits received by
officials and employees of public and private entities: Provided, That the total
exclusion under this subparagraph shall not exceed One Hundred Fifty Thousand
Pesos (PhP 150,000.00), which amount shall be subject to adjustment under
Section 6 of the Tax-Free First Million Act."
       SEC. 6. Inflation Adjustment and Prevention of Bracket Creep. —
Beginning taxable year 2031, and every three (3) years thereafter, the Secretary of
Finance, upon recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue and using
the latest official Consumer Price Index data of the Philippine Statistics Authority,
shall adjust upward the income thresholds under Section 24(A)(2)(a), the applicable
threshold under Section 24(A)(2)(b), and the tax-exempt ceiling under Section
32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
       The adjustment shall be based on cumulative inflation for the immediately
preceding three (3) calendar years. The adjusted income tax thresholds shall be
rounded to the nearest Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP 10,000.00), while the adjusted
tax-exempt ceiling for thirteenth month pay and other benefits shall be rounded to
the nearest Five Thousand Pesos (PhP 5,000.00).
        No adjustment under this section shall result in a reduction of any threshold
or ceiling.
       The Department of Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue shall
publish the adjusted thresholds not later than 31 October of the year preceding
their effectivity.
      SEC. 7. Tax Relief and Revenue Integrity Plan. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance and the Bureau of
Internal Revenue shall submit to the Committees on Ways and Means of both
Houses of Congress a Tax Relief and Revenue Integrity Plan to support the
implementation of this Act.
      The Plan shall include, but shall not be limited to:
       (a) full digitalization of taxpayer registration, filing, payment, invoicing,
receipting, audit selection, and taxpayer services;
       (b) expansion of electronic invoicing and lawful data matching, subject to
existing laws on data privacy, taxpayer rights, and due process;
      (c) risk-based audits focused on high-risk, high-capacity, and non-compliant
taxpayers, rather than compliant compensation income earners;
      (d) strengthened collection of delinquent accounts, enforcement against tax
evasion, and prosecution of fraudulent schemes;
      (e) improved coordination and lawful data-sharing            among    relevant
government agencies for tax compliance purposes;
      (f) periodic review of tax expenditures, leakages, and enforcement gaps;

       (g) policy recommendations on international tax cooperation, base erosion,

treaties and international commitments; and
      (h) recommended legislation, if necessary, to strengthen access to financial

administrative due process, and protection of taxpayer rights.
       SEC. 8. Withholding Tax Transition; Credit or Refund of Excess
Withholding. — For taxable year 2026, employers and withholding agents shall
apply the revised withholding tax tables within sixty (60) days from the issuance of
the implementing rules and regulations.
      Any excess tax withheld by reason of the transition to the new tax schedule
shall be credited against succeeding withholding tax liabilities of the same
employee or taxpayer, or refunded through the year-end adjustment or other
procedures prescribed by the Bureau of Internal Revenue.
      SEC. 9. Taxpayer Information and Assistance. — The Bureau of Internal
Revenue shall conduct a nationwide taxpayer information campaign explaining the
new tax thresholds, withholding rules, optional eight percent (8%) tax treatment,
treatment of mixed-income earners, and the procedures for crediting or refunding
excess withholding.
       SEC. 10. Congressional Oversight. — The Department of Finance and the
Bureau of Internal Revenue shall submit to the Committees on Ways and Means of
both Houses of Congress an annual report on the implementation of this Act,
including its impact on taxpayers, revenue collections, compliance rates,
withholding adjustments, refunds or credits, and revenue integrity measures
undertaken.
       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon recommendation of
the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
      SEC. 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SEC. 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
