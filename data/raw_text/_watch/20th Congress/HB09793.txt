                          AN ACT
ENSURING THE AVAILABILITY OF MEDICINES AND PROCUREMENT OF
MEDICAL EQUIPMENT IN ALL PUBLIC HOSPITALS THROUGH THE
UTILIZATION OF THE MEDICAL ASSISTANCE TO INDIGENT AND
FINANCIALLY INCAPACITATED PATIENTS (MAIFIP) FUND, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Universal Medical Access
and Equipment Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote and protect
the right to health of every Filipino by ensuring universal access to essential medicines
and quality healthcare services. In line with this, the State shall mandate the
Department of Health (DOH) to provide adequate medicines and procure essential
medical equipment for all government hospitals using the Medical Assistance to
Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP) Fund.

       SEC 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Essential medicines refer to the list of medicines identified by the DOH
       as essential for the treatment of prevalent diseases and health conditions in the
       Philippines, as well as medicines included in the Philippine National
       Formulary;

          b. Essential medical equipment refer to X-ray machines, ultrasound
       machines, ECG machines, and other diagnostic equipment deemed necessary
       by the DOH for basic diagnostic services in public hospitals;




                                              1
         c. Public hospitals refer to all hospitals owned and operated by the
      national government, including, but not limited to, national hospitals, regional
      hospitals, provincial hospitals, city hospitals, and municipal hospitals; and

         d. MAIFIP Fund refers to the Medical Assistance to Indigent and
      Financially Incapacitated Patients Fund.

      SEC 4. Availability of Essential Medicines. – The DOH shall ensure the continuous
and adequate supply of essential medicines in all public hospitals nationwide. To
achieve this, the following shall be implemented:

         a. The DOH shall establish a streamlined procurement process for
      essential medicines, ensuring competitive pricing and timely delivery through
      bulk purchasing and strategic partnerships with pharmaceutical providers.
      Preference shall be given to locally manufactured medicines: Provided, That
      they meet international quality and safety standards;

         b. All public hospitals shall implement efficient inventory management
      systems to prevent stockouts and wastage of medicines. The DOH shall
      provide technical assistance and training to hospital personnel on inventory
      management best practices;

         c. Public hospitals shall maintain proper storage of medicines that adhere
      to pharmacopeial standards, including the mandatory use of climate-
      controlled environments to monitor temperature and humidity, ensuring the
      long-term efficacy and safety of all medical stocks; and

         d. The DOH shall develop guidelines for the efficient and equitable
      dispensing of medicines to patients in public hospitals, prioritizing indigent
      and financially incapacitated patients.

       SEC 5. Provision of Essential Medical Equipment. – The DOH shall ensure that all
public hospitals are equipped with the following essential medical equipment,
including, but not limited to:

         a. Radiographic equipment, specifically at least one (1) X-ray machine
      capable of performing basic radiographic examinations;

         b. Ultrasonography tools, specifically at least one (1) ultrasound machine
      capable of performing basic imaging procedures;

          c. Electrocardiogram (ECG) units, specifically at least one ECG machine
      to facilitate cardiac monitoring and diagnosing; and

         d. Other specialized equipment as determined by a DOH-led needs
      assessment, which shall prioritize procurement based on hospital capacity,



                                           2
      regional health demands, and the needs of the patient population served,
      especially indigent and financially incapacitated patients.

       SEC 6. Funding. – The procurement of essential medicines and medical
equipment under this Act shall be funded primarily through the MAIFIP Fund,
subject to the following provisions:

         a. The DOH shall exclusively reserve at least twenty-five percent (25%) of
      the annual MAIFIP Fund for the procurement of essential medicines and
      medical equipment as mandated by this Act. This allocation shall be prioritized
      and protected from other uses.

         b. The National Government shall include supplemental funding in the
      annual General Appropriations Act to augment the MAIFIP allocation and
      ensure the long-term sustainability of the program.

         c. The DOH is authorized to explore other funding sources, including
      grants and donations, to further support the objectives of this Act.

       SEC 7. MAIFIP Fund Management and Transparency. – The DOH shall develop
clear and transparent guidelines for the utilization of the MAIFIP fund for the
purposes of this Act, ensuring accountability and preventing misuse of funds.

       Implement a robust monitoring system to track the utilization of MAIFIP funds
for the procurement of medicines and equipment and to assess the impact of this Act
on access to healthcare for indigent and financially incapacitated patients. Publish
regular reports on the utilization of the MAIFIP fund, including details on the
procurement of medicines and equipment, the number of patients served, and the
overall impact on public health.

        SEC 8. Implementation and Monitoring. – The DOH shall be the lead
implementing agency for this Act. It shall develop implementing rules and
regulations within sixty (60) days of the effectivity of this Act. Conduct regular
monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of this Act to ensure its
effectiveness. Submit an annual report to Congress on the progress of the
implementation of this Act, including data on the availability of medicines and
equipment in public hospitals, the utilization of funds, the number of indigent and
financially incapacitated patients served, and any challenges encountered.

        SEC 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

       SEC 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules and
regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act, are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.
