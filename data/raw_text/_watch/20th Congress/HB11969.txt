                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish the Upland Sustainable Agri-Forestry Development
(USAD) Program in all provinces, cities, and municipalities with upland
communities. The program is a comprehensive, convergence-based framework to
reduce poverty among the country’s most marginalized upland dwellers,
particularly farmers and Indigenous Peoples.

       Despite gains in national poverty reduction, the benefits of growth have not
reached the mountains and hillsides where many of the country’s poorest families
live. The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that in 2023, the basic sectors with
the highest poverty incidence were Indigenous Peoples at thirty-two point four
percent (32.4%), fisherfolk at twenty-seven point four percent (27.4%), and farmers at
twenty-seven percent (27%).1 In effect, about one (1) in every three (3) Indigenous
Peoples, fisherfolk, and farmers belongs to the poorest segment of the population.
These figures fall hardest on upland communities, where Indigenous Peoples make
up the majority of dwellers and where most of the uplands form part of their
ancestral domains and traditional livelihoods.

       This measure institutionalizes a clear structure of accountability, from the
Local Chief Executive down to field-level USAD Teams. It requires CBMS-based
targeting and social preparation through Participatory Rapid Appraisal. It calls for
sustained technical assistance across the entire value chain, from soil analysis and
land preparation to processing, marketing, and financial management. It also sets
defined phases for project establishment, maintenance, and marketing, and
establishes a measurable graduation standard.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING   THE   UPLAND    SUSTAINABLE AGRI–FORESTRY
DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM IN UPLAND AGRICULTURAL LANDS AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

                                      ARTICLE I

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Upland Sustainable
Agri-Forestry Development (USAD) Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
free the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate social services,
a rising standard of living and an improved quality of life. Toward this end, the State
shall adopt a comprehensive and holistic approach in poverty alleviation wherein
every Filipino is able to meet the basic needs of food, nutrition, environmental
sanitation, shelter, education, and income security.

      It is also the policy of the State to protect and promote the right of the people
to a balanced and healthful ecology. The State shall implement projects and
programs geared toward economic growth and sustained national development in
harmony with environmental conservation and ecological balance.

      SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to reduce poverty incidence in the uplands
and specifically aims to:

          a. ﻿Employ a convergence approach in alleviating poverty in the most
       marginalized sectors in the country, particularly the upland and indigenous
       communities;​

          b. ﻿Create and increase economic opportunities for upland communities
       especially for those who make a living out of farming;​
   c.​ ﻿Protect and conserve the natural resources by providing alternative
sources of livelihood for people in upland communities;​

   d.​ Involve the indigenous people communities into mainstream through
comprehensive development programs;​

   e.​ Empower the individuals and communities for sustainability through
holistic development approach that addresses the multi–dimensionality of
poverty;​

  f.​ Optimize agricultural        productivity       on   idle   lands   owned by
marginalized landowners.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   a.​ ﻿Agricultural land refers to land devoted principally to the raising of
crops such as rice, corn, sugar cane, tobacco, coconut, or to pasturing,
dairying, inland fishery, salt–making and others, including forest lands;​

   b.​ ﻿Convergence approach refers to multi-stakeholder and multi-agency
cooperation in sprouting sustainable economic development;​

   c.​ ﻿Custodian refers to the designated authority and/or accountable
person over the non-livelihood infrastructures;​

   d.​ ﻿Enrollees refer to upland dwellers which are identified as recipients of
the USAD Program;​

   e.​ ﻿Home and Backyard Small Business refers to small income-generating
business entity run from someone’s home or backyard which may include,
but not limited, to baking, pastry–making, weaving, knitting, dressmaking,
and tailoring, and others;​

   f.​ ﻿Participatory Rapid Appraisal (PRA) an interactive data collection
process conducted at a community level or with a specific community group
of interest. PRA is applied at the design, early implementation, and
evaluation phases of a project cycle;​

    g.​ ﻿Social Preparation refers to the conduct of preliminary social analysis
of the community where organizers engage in institution preparations, area
selection, and social investigation/integration. It also refers to a series of
pre–implementation activities involving individuals and communities
intended for the proper and effective discharge or execution of the USAD
Program such as but not limited to:​

         i.​ Educating and informing the enrollees and their respective
       communities on the poverty situation and other relevant data that will
             contextualize their participation in the program;​

              ii.​ The conduct of PRA and evaluation, if any, of the Barangay
             Development Plan; and​

              iii.​ The assessment of the absorptive capacity of the communities
             and enrollees based on the availability of labor and/or land;​

         h.​ ﻿Upland refers to areas comprising: (1) marginal lands with slopes of
      eighteen percent (18%) or higher, (2) lands within mountainous zones, and (3)
      lands within terrain classified as hilly to mountainous;​

         i.​ ﻿Upland Sustainable Agri–Forestry Development (USAD) refers to a
      comprehensive development framework aimed at alleviating poverty in the
      upland communities by way of a convergence approach, providing livelihood
      programs, non-livelihood and infrastructure projects, and conducting
      continuous education and training relative thereto.

      SEC. 5. Coverage. – This Act shall apply and cover provinces, cities, and
municipalities with upland communities.

                                  ARTICLE II
          Structure, Composition, and Operation of the USAD Program

      SEC. 6. Creation of USAD Coordinating Unit. – There is hereby created an
USAD Coordinating Unit in every province, city, and municipality which shall be
under the direct supervision of the Local Chief Executive. It shall organize an USAD
Coordinating Team for the effective implementation of the USAD Program. The
Provincial USAD Coordinating Unit shall serve as a lead unit to municipal
counterparts, hereby referred to as a Municipal USAD Coordinating Unit.

      SEC. 7. Composition of USAD Coordinating Unit. – The USAD Coordinating
Unit shall be composed of a Program Coordinator, USAD Teams and their respective
team members.

      SEC. 8. Functions. – The function of the USAD Coordinating Unit are as
follows:​

         a.​ The USAD Program Coordinator shall be the head of the
      Provincial/Municipal/City USAD units who shall be mandated to prepare
      and capacitate individuals and communities for the productive and beneficial
      absorption of the government’s development programs. They shall coordinate
      with the Program Management Team and the Implementing Offices for the
      programs, projects, and activities (PPAs) for an integrated intervention
      approach on any given target community for development. They shall
      perform the following functions:​
        i.​ Require Implementing Offices the submission of a detailed work
      and financial plan for USAD areas, along with the initial impact
      assessments report;​

       ii.​ ﻿Require updates on all programs, projects and activities under
      USAD Programs and recommend measures based on the
      implementation procedure from the Implementing Offices;​

       iii.​ For municipal USAD, coordinate with the Municipal Local Chief
      Executive, municipal or the barangay focal person if deemed
      necessary;​

       iv.​ Coordinate with other national agencies relative to USAD
      convergence programs;​

        v.​ Participate in the planning process of any program and project
      aimed at the reduction of poverty condition of the province;​

       vi.​    Supervise the USAD teams;​

      vii.​ ﻿Coordinate with the program management team to ensure
      success of the PPAs;​

      viii.​   Conduct meetings with Program Team Leaders;​

       ix.​ Conduct validation of submitted reports and present progress
      report to the province/city/municipal government in the
      implementation process of the USAD Program;​

        x.​ Provide updates for the Provincial Governor/City/Municipal
      Mayor and make recommendations for constant improvement in the
      implementation process of the USAD Program; and​

       xi.​ Perform any functions as required by the Provincial Governor/
      City/Municipal Mayors relative to the implementation of USAD
      Programs;​

   b.​ For a provincial USAD Unit, one (1) USAD Team shall be assigned for
every municipality or component city enrolled to USAD. Each USAD Team
shall be composed of a team leader and its respective members.

               The functions of a team leader are as follows:​

         i.​   ﻿Prepares the action plan;​

        ii.​   ﻿Leads the conduct of USAD activities in the field;​
             iii.​ ﻿Conducts monitoring and submits reports on the progress of the
            implementation of the PPAs;​

             iv.​ ﻿Coordinates with the City/Municipal/Barangay Local
            Government Unit (LGU) and USAD focal person for the efficient
            implementation of the PPAs;​

              v.​ ﻿Attends regular meetings and updates the program coordinator
            on the progress of USAD programs in their respective area of
            assignments;​

             vi.​ ﻿Submits to the program coordinator a written report on the
            validated report from the field.​

      The team members shall perform the following functions:

            i. Conduct social preparation and capacity development of enrollees
            and communities;

            ﻿ii. Participate in the team planning process in the conduct of field
             implementation, monitoring and reporting of programs, projects and
             activities in their respective area of assignments;

            iii. Conduct close monitoring and reporting on every stage of the
            implementation processes;

            iv. Provide updated information to guide the Implementing Office for a
            timely intervention and/or technical assistance to USAD Program
            enrollees; and

               v. Submit to the team leader validated reports from the field.

      SEC. 9. Implementing Offices. – The Implementing Offices shall propose,
formulate, and implement PPAs on the target and identified areas having high
poverty incidence. It shall be composed of the following offices:​

         a.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Agriculture Office (PAO/CAO/MAO);​

         b.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Veterinary Office (PVO/CVO/MVO);​

         c.​ Welfare and Social Development Office (PSWDO/CSWDO/MSWDO);​

         d.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Engineering Office (PEO/CEO/MEO);​

         e.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Health Office (PHO/CHO/MHO);​

         f.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Environment and          Natural   Resources
      Offices (PENRO/CENRO/MENRO);​
         g.​ Provincial/City/Municipal         Employment     Services     Offices
      (PESO/CESO/MESO).

       The Implementing Offices are hereby mandated to perform the following
functions:

        a.​ ﻿Implement and manage their respective programs or projects identified
      under USAD;​

        b.​ ﻿Provide technical support to the USAD Program especially to the
      USAD Teams in assisting enrollees to ensure successful implementation of the
      Program;​

        c.​ Prepare and provide necessary data to the program coordinator which
      will be used by the USAD Teams in monitoring and validating;​

        d.​ ﻿Prepare and provide a progress report on each implementation phase
      and a final report at the end of each fiscal year; and​

        e.​ ﻿Participate in the USAD general meetings.

      SEC. 10. Management Team. – There is hereby created a Program Management
Team (PMT) who shall serve as the overseer of the USAD Program Units’ activities.
The PMT shall be comprised of the Head of Office of the following offices:​

         a.​ Bids and Awards Offices (BAC);​

         b.​ Provincial/City/Municipal General Services Office
             (PGSO/CGSO/MGSO);​

         c.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Accounting Office
             (PadMO/CadMO/MadMO);​

         d.​ Provincial/City/Municipal Human Resource and Management Office
             (PHRMO/CHRMO/MHRMO);​

         e.​ Internal Audit Offices (IAOs), Information Management Offices
             (IMOs), Provincial/City/Municipal Treasury Office (PTO/CTO/MTO);
             and​

         f.​ A representative of the municipal and barangay councils.

      The Municipal Management Team shall also be created which shall closely
coordinate with the PMT.

      It shall be mandatory that the PMT is represented in every progress report
presentation. The PMT shall hold meetings as often as necessary and shall be
attended by the members sitting in ex–officio capacity.
       It shall, thereafter, provide policy support, review and recommend courses of
action and policies, provide feedback to policy makers, project developers, examine
the objectives of the proposed programs, evaluate the progress and achievement
reports, and perform other functions deemed necessary to aid the program
coordinator in ensuring the success of the PPAs under USAD.

       SEC. 11. Municipal/City USAD Units. – The Municipal/City Government shall
organize a Municipal/City USAD Unit which shall work under close coordination
with the Provincial USAD Program Unit. Its PPAs shall be consistent with those of
the Provincial USAD Unit.

       SEC. 12. Personnel, Members and Staff. – Only non–ex–officio personnel,
members, and staff of the USAD Program Units, Program Management Team, and
the Program Implementing Office shall be entitled to remunerations to be
determined by the concerned LGUs taking into account their actual duties and
responsibilities. It may be increased as per yearly reviews on the merits based on
their productivity.

                                    ARTICLE III
                            Project Establishment Phase

       SEC. 13. Selection of Barangays. – Barangays shall be selected and ranked
according to poverty incidence based on the results of the Community-Based
Monitoring System (CBMS). Among those selected barangays, priority shall be given
to those with an existing Barangay Development Plan.

      SEC. 14. Non–livelihood and Livelihood Projects. – The Program shall serve as an
avenue for infrastructure, social, and non–livelihood projects and livelihood
programs for upland residents towards comprehensive intervention and holistic
development framework.

         a.​ Non–livelihood projects shall cover health, education, cultural and
      sports programs. Infrastructure projects shall include barangay health units,
      day care centers, sanitation and water facilities.​

         b.​ For livelihood projects, the USAD Program Unit shall determine the
      priority commodities and other support crops, home, and backyard small
      business, fishery, livestock and poultry suitable for the livelihood projects.​

         c.​ The Implementing Offices shall issue the guidelines for the projects
      and project proposal. Non–compliance to the guidelines may result in
      automatic rejection without prejudice to its reversal upon compliance of the
      guidelines within the prescribed schedule.

       SEC. 15. Selection of Enrollees. – Selection of enrollees shall be made for the
livelihood projects only. The prospective enrollee shall come from the list of
households with income below poverty threshold provided that they are able to
meet the requirements of the project such as labor, time, and/or land.

       All prospective enrollees must present a barangay certification attesting that
he/she is a resident of the barangay at least six (6) months prior to the application to
the Program validated through the CBMS. Enrollees not listed in CBMS must register
with the system prior to acceptance to the Program. The Implementing Office may
also require additional requirements as may be necessary in the enrollment of the
Program.

        For agricultural development programs and projects, the Implementing Office
shall require a confirmation and validation from the Register of Deeds as to land
status.

      SEC. 16. Duties and Responsibilities of Enrollees. – Upon agreeing with the terms
and conditions of the Program, the enrollees shall comply with all requirements of
the USA’s projects, programs and activities which include but not limited to:

          a.​ ﻿Attending capacity development trainings;​

           b.​ Strictly observe guidelines and modules provided by the technical staff
       or expert resource person of the Implementing Office;​

          c.​ ﻿Provide timely and necessary feedback and/or report to the USAD
       Team on the progress of the projects under their care.

        SEC. 17. Procurement of Materials. – The procurement of materials shall be
prioritized and in accordance with existing procurement laws. The procurement
process shall likewise take into consideration the farms' schedule, timely field
implementation to avoid delays as much as possible.

       SEC. 18. Social Preparation and Capacity Development of Enrollees. – The USAD
Provincial, City, and Municipal Team, in coordination with the Implementing
Offices, shall conduct a PRA to identify community needs, assist in the crafting of a
Barangay Development Plan if in case such is not updated, and hold program
orientation to barangay councils, enrollees and any interested member/s of the
community.

       The USAD Coordinating Unit shall also assist in the organization of the
enrollees who shall have a set of officers, approved policy and/or by–laws. This
organization shall be registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

      In case of an existing registered organization, the Implementing Office shall
review the organization’s constitutions and by–laws to determine its compatibility
with the project’s objectives. Non–member enrollees shall be enlisted with the
organization.
       It shall be mandatory to conduct capacity development training which shall
be attended by the enrollees who shall be assessed after. Appropriate assessment
tools shall be developed for that purpose.

       SEC. 19. Establishment of Livelihood Projects. – For agricultural farm projects, the
following activities shall be undertaken:

           a.​ ﻿The concerned agricultural extension worker and agriculturist shall
       assist the enrollees in getting a soil sample from their respective farm site for
       laboratory analysis to determine its suitability for farming and appropriate
       crops;​

          b.​ Lay–outing of the farm and geo–tagging;​

          c.​ ﻿The timely delivery of planting materials, seedlings, and other
       necessary material inputs shall be offered to the concerned enrollees by the
       Implementing Offices, along with the USAD Teams. The guidelines for the
       proper distribution shall be strictly observed; and​

           d.​ ﻿The USAD team shall, along with the Implementing Offices and its
       technical staff, monitor and assist enrollees, if needed, in the implementation
       activities such as but not limited to land preparation, planting, trimming, and
       application of fertilizer.

       Technical assistance shall be made available to enrollees from their
corresponding Implementing Office all throughout the process from land
preparation, planting, harvesting, farm maintenance, value–adding, processing,
marketing, and financial management.

        For non–agricultural and projects, the Implementing Offices along with the
USAD Teams shall conduct ocular inspections of the sites to assess its suitability to
the intended project, program and activities. The same shall deliver to the enrollees
all material inputs and the kits needed for the approved projects, programs, and
activities.

       SEC. 20. Establishment of Non–livelihood and Infrastructure Projects. – The
assigned custodian shall submit a quarterly maintenance report to the USAD unit
which shall coordinate with the Implementing Office for timely and necessary
assistance and feedback. The delivery of the projects shall be done in accordance
with the approved implementation procedure.

        The Implementing Office shall likewise supervise the project’s delivery and
installation. It shall also conduct capacity development training for the assigned
custodian while the projects are still in progress and involve the same in the
formulation of the maintenance plan.
       The assigned custodian shall submit a quarterly maintenance report to
concerned Implementing Offices on which the latter shall provide necessary
assistance and feedback.

        SEC. 21. Project Maintenance Phase. – The reports submitted by the enrollees to
the USAD Team shall be validated and verified by the Implementing Office in the
field after which a meeting shall be called wherein the Implementing Office shall
make necessary recommendations and take the necessary actions concerning the
report. The USAD Team shall ensure that recommendations are properly carried out
in the field.

       It shall be mandatory for the Implementing Offices, in coordination with the
USAD Unit, to conduct continuing training and education of the enrollees, taking
into consideration issues and concerns in the field.

       SEC. 22. Project Marketing Phase. – The Implementing Office shall conduct an
inventory of areas with agriculture production and organize the enrollees into
associations in preparation for the marketing phase. Capacity building on
value–adding commodities and market matching for possible buyers shall also be
made.

      SEC. 23. Graduation of Enrollees. – An enrollee is deemed to have graduated
from the program if their household earned an income above the prevailing poverty
threshold as reflected by their respective Progress Report Card created for that
purpose.

       The graduated enrollee is hereby granted the option to continually attend the
training and education conducted under the USAD Program.

      SEC. 24. Reportorial Requirements. – It shall be mandatory for every USAD
Team to submit monthly progress reports to the Program Coordinator. The latter
shall provide updates to the Provincial Governor/City/Municipal Mayor
accordingly.

       A midyear assessment report shall be conducted by the USAD Program Unit
taking into consideration the progress of the PPAs in each barangay, the general
status of the USAD Program and other issues and concerns in the implementation
phase.

      The annual report shall cover the barangay status prior to the implementation
of the projects vis–à–vis progress of the USAD projects and its impact on the
communities.

       SEC. 25. Development Program Harmonization. – All national line agencies, civil
society organizations, and other institutions that implement socioeconomic
development programs shall harmonize and align policies, plans and programs for a
sustainable USAD implementation.
                                     ARTICLE III
                               Miscellaneous Provisions

       SEC. 26. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provision of
this Act shall be charged against the General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 27. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), in
coordination with the Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Agrarian
Reform (DAR), Department of Health (DOH), Department of Environment and
Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and Technical Educational Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), shall formulate the rules and regulations to fully
implement the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 28. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 29. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 30. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
