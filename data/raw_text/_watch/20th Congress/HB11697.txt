EXPLANATORY NOTE

The fisheries sector is of great importance to the Philippines in terms of food security,
employment, and economic development. Yet a great many fisherfolk in the Philippines, especially
those living in coastal and geographically isolated areas, do not have access to cold storage, ice-
making machines, fish landing facilities, processing equipment, and other necessary post-harvest
infrastructure. Because of these shortcomings, fish spoilage occurs, post-harvest losses take place,
the prices at which the fish are sold are lower, and their income is therefore reduced.

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority, fisheries production in 2025 at current
prices amounted to P328.65 billion, highlighting the economic significance of the sector. Yet, even
though it makes this contribution, numerous fishing communities do not have the facilities required
to preserve the quality of their catch and to get the most value from it.

The Fisherfolk Cold-Chain and Post-Harvest Loss Reduction Program is required to be
institutionalized by the Department of Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Fisheries and
Aquatic Resources (BFAR), under Section 59 of Republic Act No. 8550, known as the "Philippine
Fisheries Code of 1998", which provides for the establishment of post-harvest facilities for fishing
communities.

The Act in question is intended to guarantee universal and equitable access for all eligible
fisherfolk across the country to post-harvest assistance by offering cold-chain facilities, ice-
making equipment, fish landing centres, storage and processing facilities, and technical support,
with a priority being given to municipal and small-scale fisherfolk as well as to underserved coastal
communities.

The government's aim with this measure is to cut down on post-harvest losses, improve the
quality of fishery products, raise the income of fisherfolk, enhance food security, and promote
sustainable development within fishing communities all over the country.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

TRINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

AN ACT

MANDATING THE DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE, THROUGH THE BUREAU
OF FISHERIES AND AQUATIC RESOURCES, TO INSTITUTIONALIZE A
UNIVERSAL COLD-CHAIN AND POST-HARVEST FACILITY SUPPORT PROGRAM
FOR FISHERFOLK AND FISHING COMMUNITIES NATIONWIDE,
STRENGTHENING SECTION 59 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8550, OTHERWISE KNOWN
AS THE “PHILIPPINE FISHERIES CODE OF 1998,” AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fisherfolk Cold-Chain and

Post-Harvest Loss Reduction Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the welfare of
Filipino fisherfolk, reduce post-harvest losses, strengthen food security, and promote equitable

access to cold-chain and post-harvest facilities in fishing communities nationwide.

SEC. 3. Establishment of the Program. — The Department of Agriculture, through BFAR,
shall establish and institutionalize the Fisherfolk Cold-Chain and Post-Harvest Loss Reduction
Program to provide eligible fisherfolk and fishing communities with appropriate post-harvest

facilities, equipment, and technical assistance.

SEC. 4. Coverage and Assistance. — The Program shall cover eligible municipal and

small-scale fisherfolk, fisherfolk associations, cooperatives, and qualified fishing community

organizations nationwide. Assistance shall include ice-making machines, cold storage, refrigerated
transport, fish landing facilities, fish handling and processing equipment, preservation and

packaging facilities, and related technical training.

SEC. 5. Universal Access and Priority Beneficiaries. — BFAR shall ensure equitable
opportunities for eligible fisherfolk in all regions to access the Program. Priority shall be given to
low-income fisherfolk, underserved coastal communities, and areas with significant post-harvest
losses or inadequate facilities. Assistance shall be allocated through transparent, needs-based

criteria and subject to available appropriations.

SEC. 6. Implementation and Coordination. — BFAR shall implement the Program in
coordination with local government units, the Philippine Fisheries Development Authority,
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Management Councils, fisherfolk organizations, cooperatives,

and other concerned agencies, consistent with Section 59 of Republic Act No. 8550.

SEC, 7. Monitoring and Accountability. — BFAR shall monitor the implementation,
utilization, and maintenance of facilities under the Program and submit an annual report to
Congress on beneficiaries, projects, budget utilization, and results achieved. All expenditures shall

be subject to applicable auditing and accountability laws.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, through BFAR, shall promulgate the necessary

implementing rules and regulations in consultation with concerned agencies, LGUs, and fisherfolk

organizations.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DA and BFAR, subject to applicable
laws. Thereafter, funding for the Program shall be included in the annual General Appropriations

Act.

SEC. 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional

or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 12, Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
