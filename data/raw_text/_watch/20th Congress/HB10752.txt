                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Autism, or Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), is a lifelong, complex neurodevelopmental
condition caused by differences in how the human brain functions1. Social communications and
interaction challenges, as well as restricted and repetitive behaviors, are primarily seen in persons with
ASD2.

        The Philippines still does not have an official and available registry of autism prevalence.
Recent estimates indicate that approximately one (1) in every one hundred (100) Filipinos may be on
the autism spectrum, translating to roughly 1.2 million Filipinos living with autism, including an
estimated 350,000 Filipino children with ASD3. Despite these rising numbers, autism care in the
Philippines is still inaccessible and expensive to most Filipino families.

        Support systems are very much inadequate and unevenly distributed in the country. For many
low-income households, especially those in underserved and geographically isolated communities,
access to developmental specialists, diagnostic services, and intervention programs is out of financial
reach. In 2022, it was cited that the Philippines has 99 board-certified developmental-behavioral
paediatricians, of whom more than half (52%) are situated in the National Capital Region4. These
compel families from provinces to shoulder significant transportation and accommodation expenses
merely to access consultations and therapy.

        The financial burden associated with autism care is substantial and, in many cases,
heartbreaking to household stability. The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) jointly conducted a study that concluded that
raising a child with a disability requires expenditures approximately forty percent (40%) to eighty
percent (80%) higher than raising a typically developing child. Moreover, poverty rates among
households caring for children with disabilities are fifty percent (50%) higher compared to other
households. The same study also showed that only one (1) out of five (5) children with disabilities
possesses a Persons with Disability (PWD) identification card, limiting access to vital government

         According to a Philippine study published in Acta Medica Philippina, the average cost of
interventions, such as therapy, education, medication, and developmental monitoring, is about
Php38,868 during the first year of care, or about twenty-one percent (21%) of the monthly expenses of
a household earning Php15,000. The total cost of diagnostic evaluations for autism ranges from
Php1,356 to Php44,634. The same study further revealed that approximately forty-two percent (42%)
of diagnosed children received no intervention after diagnosis, largely due to financial and accessibility
barriers6.

       Hindi lamang ito mga numero o datos. Ito ay ang nakapanglulumong realidad ng mga
magulang at primary caregivers na isinasakripisyo ang kanilang oras at panahon para lamang alagaan
ang kanilang mga mahal sa buhay na may ASD. Ang kabawasan, lalo na ang kawalan ng oras, sa
paghahanapbuhay ang s’yang nagpapalugmok sa kanila sa kahirapan.

         And despite the passage of landmark legislation such as Republic Act No. 7277 or the Magna
Carta for Persons with Disability, Republic Act No. 11223 or the Universal Health Care Act, Republic
Act No. 11036 or the Mental Health Act, and Republic Act No. 11650 or the Inclusive Education Act,
there is still no comprehensive and coordinated national framework specifically devoted to autism care
across the life course, from early childhood through adulthood.

        Sa pamamagitan ng pagbabalangkas at pagtatag ng National Autism Care Plan at pagsaayos
ng isang dedicated inter-agency and multi-sectoral mechanism sa ilalim ng National Council on
Disability Affairs (NCDA), ang panukalang batas na ito ay hindi lamang tutugon sa mga isyu sa
kalusugan… ito ay may hangaring solusyunan ang mga isyu sa kahirapan, panlipunang proteksyon at
inklusyong pang-ekonomiya.

        By investing in early intervention, strengthening inclusive educational systems, expanding
access to therapies, and creating networks for skills training, livelihood, and employment, the State
empowers persons with autism to become more independent, self-reliant, and productive members of
society. Equally important, this measure enables parents and caregivers to remain economically
productive while reducing the long-term financial burden borne by families and local communities.

        The heart of this proposed legislation affirms the State’s obligation to ensure that access to
autism care is not determined by geography or financial capacity, but made available to every Filipino
family in need. The National Autism Care Plan advances the welfare of neurodivergent Filipinos while
contributing to broader national goals of inclusive growth, family resilience, and poverty reduction.

           In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
      INSTITUTING A NATIONAL AUTISM CARE PLAN, ESTABLISHING A SUB-
     COMMITTEE ON AUTISM UNDER THE NATIONAL COUNCIL ON DISABILITY
                 AFFAIRS, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "National Autism Care
 2   Act".

 3           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
 4   the right to health of the people to full, healthy, and holistic development. To this end, the
 5   State shall promote an environment conducive to the development of persons with autism to
 6   become, to the best of their potentials, independent, self-reliant, and productive members of
 7   society. Consistent with the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD),
 8   the Magna Carta for Persons with Disability (RA 7277, as amended), the Universal Health
 9   Care Act (RA 11223), and the Mental Health Act (RA 11036), the State shall ensure non-
10   discrimination, accessibility, reasonable accommodation, and support across the lifespan of
11   autistic persons, from early childhood through older adulthood.

12           Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition; abilities and support needs change
13   across the life course. WHO notes that some autistic people live independently while others
14   require life-long care and support, and that autism affects education and employment

                                                                                                  3
 1   opportunities – domains that extend well beyond childhood. Legally, the Convention on the
 2   Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) protects the rights of all persons with disabilities.
 3   There is no age limit, and it requires the State to ensure accessibility, non-discrimination, and
 4   reasonable accommodation throughout life; the Philippines’ Universal Health Care Act (RA
 5   11223) similarly guarantees access to care for all Filipinos across the life course.

 6          The State commits itself to the establishment of institutional and social mechanisms
 7   to support children and adult persons with autism and their families, develop a clear and
 8   consistent pathway to early detection and diagnosis, provide easy access to quality health care,
 9   education, and employment opportunities such that persons with autism may live
10   independently within the community, to minimize or completely eradicate discrimination, and
11   to enable the private sector to extend relevant services that will complement the government’s
12   programs. The State recognizes and respects the preferences and differences of the members
13   of the community, including the use of identity-first or person-first language.

14         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

15         a. Autism or Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) refers to a neurodevelopmental condition
16            characterized by challenges with communication, social skills, restrictive and
17            repetitive behaviours, as well as by unique strengths, as defined in the latest version
18            of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders. This constitutes a
19            diverse group of neurodevelopmental conditions and for clinical coding and benefits,
20            DSM-5-TR and/or ICD-11 case definitions and codes shall apply, as updated by the
21            DOH and PhilHealth.” (ICD-11 is the global standard; DSM-5-TR is widely used in
22            Philippine practice);

23         b. Diagnosis refers to necessary assessments, including standardized, validated
24            developmental and autism-specific tools administered by a multidisciplinary team
25            (e.g., developmental-behavioral pediatrician or child psychiatrist/psychologist; and
26            as appropriate, speech-language therapist and occupational therapist and
27            occupational);

28         c. Disability refers to 1) a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one
29            (1) or more psychological, physiological, or anatomical function of an individual or
30            activities of such individual; 2) a record of such an impairment; or 3) regarded as
31            having such an impairment;


                                                                                                     2
 1        d. Epidemiology refers to the study and analysis of the distribution and determinants of
 2           health conditions in defined populations;

 3        e. Habilitation refers to an integrated approach to physical, psychological, cultural,
 4           spiritual, educational, or vocational, measures that create conditions for individuals
 5           to attain the highest possible level of functional ability and quality of life;

 6        f. Inclusive Education refers to the process of addressing and responding to the
 7           diversity of needs of all learners by moving towards the end-goal of full participation,
 8           presence, and achievement in learning cultures and communities, which involves
 9           accommodation, modification, adaptation, and individualization in content,
10           approaches, structures, and strategies. It involves equitable opportunities for learners
11           with disabilities to receive educational services, and the needed support and related
12           services with their age-appropriate peer groups and, as far as practicable, in such
13           schools or Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs) nearest their homes or
14           residences, in order to prepare them to live as fully functional members of society;

15        g. Intervention refers to the interceding action undertaken to help improve the autism
16           prognosis. Intervention in autism may take the form of, medical, pharmacological,
17           therapeutic, educational, and non-traditional habilitative care, and all other support
18           services;

19        h. Rehabilitation refers to a set of interventions designed to optimize functioning and
20           reduce disability in individuals with health conditions in interaction with their
21           environment; and

22        i. Service providers refer to any person or entity that provides a service to the public.
23           This can include businesses, government agencies, and non-profit organizations.

24        SEC. 4. Inter-Agency and Multi-Sectoral Mechanism. – A Sub-Committee on Autism,
25   herein referred to as the Sub-Committee, is hereby created, under the National Council on
26   Disability Affairs (NCDA) which shall serve as an inter-agency and multi-sectoral policy-
27   making and coordinative mechanism to develop and implement a sustainable National Autism
28   Care Plan (NACP).
