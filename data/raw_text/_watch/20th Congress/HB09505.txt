                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Efficient logistics systems and modern trade facilitation mechanisms are
critical drivers of economic growth, industrialization, investment promotion, and
export competitiveness. In an increasingly interconnected and technology-driven
global economy, the ability of a country to move goods efficiently across borders
significantly influences its attractiveness to investors, its participation in global
value chains, and the competitiveness of its domestic industries.

      Despite the strategic importance of exports to national development, the
Philippines continues to face persistent logistics and supply chain inefficiencies that
increase the cost of doing business and weaken the competitiveness of Philippine
goods in international markets. Exporters continue to encounter fragmented
regulatory processes, duplicative documentary requirements, port congestion,
prolonged cargo dwell times, inconsistent border procedures, and insufficient digital
                                                                                        1
 interoperability    among     government       agencies.    These     inefficiencies
 disproportionately affect micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs),
 agricultural exporters, and emerging industries seeking access to global markets.

        This proposed measure seeks to establish a comprehensive national policy
 framework for export logistics competitiveness by institutionalizing coordinated
 trade facilitation reforms, digital interoperability among government agencies,
 modern customs and border processing systems, performance-based logistics
 standards, and infrastructure alignment for export development. The bill likewise
 establishes a National Export Logistics Competitiveness Council (NELCC) to
 ensure whole-of-government coordination and measurable implementation of export
 logistics reforms.

       The measure further promotes the establishment of a Single Electronic Export
 Window to enable end-to-end paperless export processing, reduce administrative
 burdens, improve transparency, and streamline interactions between exporters and
 government agencies. It likewise institutionalizes risk-based inspections, trusted
 exporter programs, logistics performance monitoring systems, and public
 transparency mechanisms to improve efficiency and accountability in export
 processing.

       By establishing a modern, integrated, transparent, and technology-driven
 export logistics system, the State affirms its commitment to building a more resilient,
 innovation-oriented, and globally competitive Philippine economy capable of
 adapting to the evolving demands of international trade and supply chain
 development.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Export
Logistics Competitiveness Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote industrialization, economic growth, trade facilitation, and the global


                                                                               1
competitiveness of Philippine exports by establishing an efficient, integrated,
modern, resilient, and technology-driven export logistics and supply chain system.
The State recognizes that efficient logistics systems, interoperable digital trade
infrastructure, streamlined border processes, and reliable transportation networks are
essential to national development, food security, investment promotion, regional
integration, and participation in global value chains.


Towards this end, the State shall adopt measures to reduce export logistics costs,
eliminate unnecessary regulatory burdens, modernize customs and trade facilitation
systems, improve port and transport efficiency, promote interoperability among
government agencies, and strengthen public-private coordination in logistics and
export development. The State shall likewise promote innovation, digital
transformation, transparency, accountability, and infrastructure alignment to ensure
that Philippine goods remain competitive in international markets.


SEC. 3. Objectives. - This Act seeks to:


(a) Reduce total export logistics costs by at least thirty percent (30%) within five (5)
years from the effectivity of this Act;


(b) Streamline, harmonize, and digitize export documentation, inspection, payment,
and clearance procedures;


(c) Establish interoperable and paperless export processing systems across
government agencies;




                                                                                      2
(d) Reduce cargo dwell time, port congestion, and administrative delays affecting
exporters;


(e) Promote risk-based, intelligence-driven, and technology-enabled customs and
inspection systems;


(f) Enhance transparency, predictability, and accountability in export logistics and
border processing;


(g) Improve logistics connectivity between production centers, economic zones,
airports, seaports, and international gateways;


(h) Strengthen the competitiveness of micro, small, and medium enterprises
(MSMEs) engaged in export activities; and


(i) Promote innovation, digital transformation, and sustainable logistics
development.


SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:


(a) “Export Logistics Costs” refer to all costs associated with the storage, handling,
documentation, inspection, customs clearance, transportation, warehousing, port
usage, and shipment of export goods;




                                                                                    3
(b) “Single Electronic Export Window” refers to a unified digital platform through
which exporters may submit, process, track, and receive approvals for all export-
related permits, certifications, payments, and clearances;


(c) “Interoperability” refers to the capability of information systems, agencies, and
digital platforms to securely exchange and utilize shared data in real time;


(d) “Risk-Based Inspection” refers to an inspection framework that utilizes data
analytics, intelligence systems, compliance history, and risk profiling to determine
the level of inspection required for export shipments;


(e) “Cargo Dwell Time” refers to the length of time cargo remains within ports,
terminals, or customs facilities from arrival until release or loading;


(f) “Trusted Exporter Program” refers to a government-recognized compliance
program granting expedited processing privileges to exporters with established
records of regulatory compliance;


(g) “Logistics Service Providers” refer to entities engaged in freight forwarding,
warehousing, trucking, shipping, customs brokerage, cargo handling, cold chain
operations, and related logistics activities;


(h) “Trade Facilitation” refers to measures that simplify, modernize, and harmonize
export and import processes to reduce trade costs and improve efficiency.




                                                                                   4
SEC. 5. Creation of the National Export Logistics Competitiveness Council
(NELCC). - There is hereby created a National Export Logistics Competitiveness
Council (NELCC), hereinafter referred to as the “Council,” which shall serve as the
primary inter-agency body responsible for the implementation and coordination of
this Act.


The Council shall be chaired by the Secretary of Trade and Industry, with the
following as members:


(a) Secretary of Finance;
(b) Secretary of Transportation;
(c) Secretary of Agriculture;
(d) Secretary of Information and Communications Technology;
(e) Director General of the National Economic and Development Authority;
(f) Commissioner of Customs;
(g) General Manager of the Philippine Ports Authority;
(h) Representative from the Philippine Competition Commission;
(i) Representatives from exporters’ associations;
(j) Representatives from logistics and supply chain industry associations; and
(k) Such other agencies or stakeholders as the Council may determine necessary.


SEC. 6. Powers and Functions of the Council. - The Council shall:


(a) Formulate and oversee the implementation of a National Export Logistics
Competitiveness Roadmap;




                                                                                  5
(b) Establish measurable annual targets for reducing export logistics costs and
improving trade facilitation indicators;


(c)   Harmonize     export-related   regulatory    requirements,    procedures,    and
documentation among government agencies;


(d) Identify and remove administrative, regulatory, and operational bottlenecks
affecting exporters;


(e) Monitor compliance of agencies with the interoperability and streamlining
requirements of this Act;


(f) Recommend policy, fiscal, administrative, and regulatory reforms to improve
export competitiveness;


(g) Coordinate with the private sector, exporters, logistics providers, port operators,
and international development partners;


(h) Establish national benchmarks and performance indicators for export logistics
efficiency; and


(i) Submit annual reports to Congress and the Office of the President.


SEC. 7. Establishment of the Single Electronic Export Window. - Within two (2)
years from the effectivity of this Act, all government agencies involved in export
processing, including the Bureau of Customs (BOC), Philippine Ports Authority


                                                                                     6
(PPA), Food and Drug Administration (FDA), Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI),
Bureau of Animal Industry (BAI), and other relevant agencies, shall fully integrate
their export-related systems into a Single Electronic Export Window.


The system shall provide for:


(a) End-to-end paperless export processing;
(b) Electronic submission and approval of permits and certifications;
(c) Real-time cargo tracking and monitoring;
(d) Electronic payment systems;
(e) Secure data sharing and interoperability among agencies;
(f) Use of digital signatures and electronic documents; and
(g) Automated application tracking and status notifications.


No agency shall require the resubmission of documents already available within the
integrated system.


SEC. 8. Service Delivery Standards and Trade Facilitation Measures. - The
Council, in coordination with concerned agencies, shall establish maximum
processing periods for export permits, inspections, customs clearances, cargo
releases, and related procedures.


All agencies shall adopt:
(a) Risk-based and intelligence-driven inspection systems;
(b) Expedited processing mechanisms for compliant exporters;
(c) Green lane processing systems;


                                                                                 7
(d) Electronic scheduling systems for inspections and cargo handling; and
(e) Automated customs and border processing systems.


Failure to act on applications within prescribed periods, without valid legal or
technical justification, shall subject responsible officials to administrative review in
accordance with civil service and anti-red tape laws.


SEC. 9. Trusted Exporter and Green Lane Programs. - The Bureau of Customs,
in coordination with the Council, shall establish a Trusted Exporter Program
granting expedited processing, reduced inspection rates, priority cargo handling, and
simplified procedures for exporters with strong compliance records.


The program shall prioritize:
(a) MSME exporters;
(b) Agricultural exporters;
(c) Strategic manufacturing exporters; and
(d) Exporters utilizing digital compliance systems.


SEC. 10. Port Efficiency and Logistics Performance Standards. - The Philippine
Ports Authority, Bureau of Customs, and relevant port operators shall establish and
publish performance indicators, including:


(a) Cargo dwell time;
(b) Truck turnaround time;
(c) Customs processing time;
(d) Cargo release efficiency;


                                                                                      8
(e) Inspection rates;
(f) Port congestion levels; and
(g) Logistics cost indicators.


Such performance metrics shall be publicly accessible through a National Logistics
Performance Dashboard.


SEC. 11. Infrastructure Alignment and Logistics Corridors. - The Department
of Transportation (DOTr), Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH),
Philippine Ports Authority (PPA), Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines
(CAAP), and other relevant agencies shall prioritize infrastructure projects that
improve connectivity between production centers, economic zones, logistics hubs,
airports, seaports, and export gateways.


The government may designate strategic Export Logistics Corridors to support high-
value agricultural, manufacturing, and industrial export sectors.


SEC. 12. MSME Export Support and Capacity Building. - The Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with relevant agencies and the private
sector, shall implement training, technical assistance, and digital capacity-building
programs for MSMEs engaged in export activities.


Such programs shall include:
(a) Export documentation and compliance;
(b) Digital trade systems;
(c) Logistics optimization;


                                                                                   9
(d) International standards compliance; and
(e) Market access facilitation.


SEC. 13. Competition and Fair Trade Safeguards. - The Philippine Competition
Commission shall monitor anti-competitive practices, abuse of dominant position,
unreasonable pricing, collusion, or restrictive arrangements within the logistics,
warehousing, cargo handling, and port services sectors that may adversely affect
export competitiveness.


SEC. 14. Logistics Innovation and Modernization Incentives. - Subject to
existing laws, rules, and regulations, the government may provide fiscal or non-fiscal
incentives for investments in:


(a) Smart logistics systems;
(b) Cold chain infrastructure;
(c) Green logistics technologies;
(d) Automated warehousing systems;
(e) Digital trade infrastructure;
(f) AI-driven supply chain management systems; and
(g) Export-oriented logistics modernization initiatives.


SEC. 15. Data Governance and Transparency. - The Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), in coordination with the Council, shall collect, analyze, and
publish export logistics performance data, including logistics cost components, trade
processing timelines, port efficiency metrics, and supply chain performance
indicators.


                                                                                   10
All agencies covered by this Act shall comply with national data governance,
cybersecurity, and data privacy standards.


SEC. 16. Annual Reporting and Congressional Review. - The Council shall
submit an annual report to Congress detailing:


(a) Progress toward export logistics cost reduction targets;
(b) Implementation status of digital integration measures;
(c) Agency compliance with this Act;
(d) Logistics performance indicators;
(e) Identified bottlenecks and corrective actions; and
(f) Recommendations for further legislative or administrative reforms.


Congress shall conduct a comprehensive review of this Act every five (5) years.


SEC. 17. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.


The government may likewise receive grants, technical assistance, and development
support from domestic or international sources, subject to existing laws, rules, and
regulations.




                                                                                  11
SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Trade and Industry, in
coordination with the member agencies of the Council and relevant stakeholders,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act.


SEC. 19. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SEC. 21. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
