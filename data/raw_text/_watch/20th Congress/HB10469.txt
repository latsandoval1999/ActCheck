                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article VI, Section 28 of the Constitution mandates that taxation shall be uniform and
equitable, and that Congress shall evolve a progressive system of taxation.

       On 2018, Republic Act No. 10963 or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN)
Law significantly eased the tax burden on Filipino workers by exempting from income tax
individuals earning up to PHP250,000 annually. The tax schedule may have been a relief at the time,
but persistent inflation and the rising cost of basic necessities have substantially eroded the
purchasing power of Filipino households. As a result, the current tax-exempt threshold finds itself
incongruent to present-day economic realities.

        While the costs of food, transportation, housing, healthcare, and other basic necessities kept
rising, wage increases have barely served only to keep pace with inflation rather than improve
workers' real purchasing power. In April 2026, the inflation rate in the country reached a staggering
7.2%, driven largely by compounding oil shocks that seem to persist due to conflicts outside the
country’s control and surging food and transport prices. Consequently, many low- and middle-
income earners continue to pay taxes on income that are increasingly consumed by these very daily
living expenses, despite experiencing little to no meaningful improvement in their actual financial
capacity.

         This representation then holds the view that there is an urgent need to realign the country's
individual income tax schedule to ensure that it remains equitable, progressive, and responsive to
the rising cost of living.

        This bill seeks to restore the fairness and progressivity of the individual income tax system
by amending the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, increasing the tax-exempt
annual income threshold from PHP250,000 to PHP350,000 and correspondingly adjusting the
graduated income tax schedule. The proposed amendment will allow Filipino workers to retain more
of their hard-earned income, strengthen household purchasing power, and stimulate domestic
consumption.

        At a time when Filipino families continue to face elevated living costs, tax policy must adapt
to economic realities. Increasing the income tax exemption threshold is simply a prudent, equitable,
and timely reform that promotes tax fairness, protects the purchasing power of workers, and affirms
the State's commitment to a progressive tax system that taxes citizens according to their true capacity
to pay.
    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
