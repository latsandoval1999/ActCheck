AN ACT CREATING A CONGRESSIONAL COMMISSION ON ROAD MOBILITY
 TO REVIEW, ASSESS, AND EVALUATE THE STATE OF MOBILITY IN THE
 PHILIPPINES AND RECOMMEND INNOVATIVE AND TARGETED POLICY
    REFORMS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
                          PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mobility is essential to the daily lives of Filipinos. Integrated, accessible, and efficient
transportation options are indispensable in enabling citizens to reach places of
employment, education, commerce, health and community services, leisure (including
public open spaces and parks), and cultural activities. Conversely, the absence of
adequate transport options restricts access to opportunities and undermines social
and economic participation.

Mobility outcomes, however, are not determined by transport services alone. They are
fundamentally shaped by land-use patterns and the design of the built environment,
which influence travel demand, distances, accessibility, safety and the viability of
public and active transport.

Despite this, conventional transport planning – which has historically prioritized faster
transportation, particularly car-based transportation – has often been pursued by the
Philippine government at the expense of other modes of transportation, particularly,
public transport, walking and cycling. This approach has reflected a focus on transport
inputs, such as vehicle movement and capacity of roads to allow vehicle movement,
rather than mobility outcomes, including accessibility, safety, efficiency, and equity in
the movement of people.

For instance, in Metro Manila, while only an estimated 12% of the population owns
private cars, a disproportionately large share of road space is allocated to car-centric
movement.1 Meanwhile, vehicle sales continue to rise. In 2025, total vehicle sales
reached 491,395 units, representing a 3.7% increase from the 473,842 units recorded
in 2024, according to joint data released by the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers
of the Philippines, Inc. (CAMPI) and the Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA).2

This car-dependent model also reinforces the country’s heavy reliance on diesel and
gasoline, which makes transport systems vulnerable to the volatility of fuel prices and
supply disruptions, as seen in the 2026 fuel crisis as a result of the Iran war.

The resulting structural imbalance in road space allocation, when combined with
dispersed land-use patterns, long travel distances, and built environments that do not
support walking, cycling, and public transport access, have contributed to worsening
traffic congestion, inequitable mobility outcomes, unsafe conditions for vulnerable
road users, especially children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, pedestrians, and
cyclists, including heat-related illnesses and risks.

The consequences of these structural imbalances are reflected in road safety outcomes.
Road crashes remain among the leading causes of mortality in the Philippines,3 and cost
the country an estimated USD 11 billion in 2021, equivalent to roughly 3% of the
country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in addition to healthcare expenditure of 5.9%
of GDP in the same year.4

These challenges are encountered across the various government agencies involved
in transport planning, infrastructure development, traffic management, environmental
protection, housing, and land-use regulation — functions which collectively shape the
built environment within which mobility occurs. These agencies include, but are not
limited to, the Department of Transportation (DOTr), Department of Public Works and
Highways (DPWH), Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), Land
Transportation Office (LTO), Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development (DHSUD), Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DepDev), Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), local
government units, and other concerned entities. Gaps in coordination among these
agencies, overlapping or unclear mandates, and inconsistent standards governing
infrastructure, roads, and the hierarchy of road users remain persistent challenges.
These constraints hinder the development of a coherent, integrated, and people-
centered mobility system in the Philippines.

Recognizing that mobility challenges are systemic and rooted not only in transport
operations but also in infrastructure planning, land-use decisions, environmental

governance, and institutional frameworks, and other mechanisms related to mobility
systems. While various reforms have been introduced across agencies and levels of
government, these efforts have often proceeded in a fragmented manner, without the
benefit of a dedicated effort within Congress to undertake a comprehensive
assessment of the mobility sector and to recommend coherent, evidence-based, and
forward-looking legislative and policy interventions.

Accordingly, this measure seeks to create a Congressional Commission on Road
Mobility (MOBICOMM). The proposed Commission shall serve as a specialized, cross-
sectoral body tasked to review, assess, and evaluate the state of public transport and
active transport in the Philippines, and to formulate innovative, targeted, measurable,
and time-bound policy and legislative reforms.

The establishment of MOBICOMM will provide Congress with an institutional platform
to:
      i.examine structural and systemic issues affecting mobility;
     ii.strengthen policy coherence and inter-agency coordination to promote public
        and active transport;
    iii.evaluate infrastructure, safety, and accessibility standards;
    iv.promote a people-centered and safety-first mobility framework; and
     v.guide long-term reforms consistent with national development goals and
        international commitments.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Congressional
 2   Commission on Mobility (MOBICOMM) Act.”

 4   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the declared policy of the State to protect
 5   and promote the right of all people to safe, accessible, inclusive, affordable,
 6   sustainable, and reliable mobility.
 7
 8   The State hereby recognizes that roads, road-based transportation systems, and
 9   complementary transport modes are essential public goods that directly affect human
10   dignity, socio-economic equality, inclusive growth, environmental sustainability, public
11   health, and the fulfillment of human rights. The State shall ensure that mobility
12   systems and the built environment shall be planned to maximize accessibility rather
13   than merely increase vehicle movement or road capacity.
14
15   The State shall ensure that mobility-related policies, plans, programs, and investments
16   uphold related constitutional rights, including the rights to life, health, a healthy
17   environment, safety, equality, and development. The State shall pursue related transit-
18   oriented development policies that enhance intermodal connectivity, last-mile
19   accessibility, universal design, and seamless mobility across transport modes, and
 1   avoid land use and built environment patterns that create unnecessary travel distances
 2   and structural dependence on private motor vehicles.
 3
 4   The State further recognizes the need to adopt a people-centered hierarchy of road
 5   users, consistent with national development policies, where pedestrians and cyclists
 6   are accorded the highest priority, followed by public transport and other transport
 7   modes. The State hereby recognizes that active transport shall is a fundamental
 8   component of the mobility system, requiring safe, continuous, and accessible
 9   infrastructure within the built environment.
10
11   The State affirms the rights of nature and the rights of the people to a balanced and
12   healthful ecology, and shall ensure that mobility systems incorporate and maximize
13   the provision of nature-based solutions including native trees and the restoration of
14   our urban forests, amid extreme heat and rain brought about by the climate crisis.
15
16   The State affirms the complementary roles of national and local governments, the
17   public and private sectors, communities, and transport workers in fostering people-
18   centered, safe, and sustainable mobility systems.

20   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
21
22   (a)   Active Transport – shall refer to the modes of transportation that require or
23         primarily involve human physical activity, including walking, cycling, and the use
24         of mobility assistive devices;
25
26   (b)   Built Environment – shall refer to all human-made or -modified physical
27         components of communities, including land development patterns, transport
28         networks, public spaces, utilities, infrastructure, and other structures.
29
30   (c)   Mobility – shall refer to the ability of persons, as enabled or constrained by the
31         built environment, transport systems, and public infrastructure planned,
32         designed, regulated, and managed by government, to move safely, efficiently,
33         affordably, inclusively, and sustainably, and to access opportunities, services, and
34         destinations necessary for personal, social, and economic well-being.
35
36   (d)   Mobility System – shall refer to framework of roads, streets, transport services,
37         public spaces, land-use structures, policies, regulations, and institutional
38         arrangements planned, designed, regulated, and managed by government,
39         which together enable or constrain the ability of persons and goods to move and
40         access destinations.
41
42   SEC. 4. State Principles on Mobility. — The State recognizes that mobility
43   systems, public transport, active transport, roads, and the built environment shall be
44   planned, designed, constructed, regulated, and managed in accordance with the
45   following principles:
46
47   (a) People-Centered. — Mobility policies, programs, and the design of roads and the
48   built environment shall prioritize the safe, equitable, and dignified movement of
 1   persons, particularly vulnerable road users, including pedestrians, cyclists, children,
 2   older persons, and persons with disabilities, while addressing imbalances and
 3   inequities arising from car-oriented planning approaches and allocation of public
 4   safety.
 5
 6   (b) Multimodal and Integrated. — Mobility systems and the built environment shall be
 7   designed within an integrated and balanced transport network comprising public
 8   transport, active transport, freight and logistics, and private motor vehicles.
 9
10   (c) Accessible and Inclusive. — The planning and design of mobility systems and the
11   built environment shall prioritize access of people to destinations, services, and
12   opportunities, rather than solely increasing vehicle movement or road capacity.
13   Mobility systems and the built environment shall be designed or redesigned to
14   eliminate physical, economic, and social barriers and ensure universal accessibility and
15   universal design, particularly for persons with disabilities, older persons, children, and
16   other vulnerable users, in roads, streets, sidewalks, public spaces, terminals, vehicles,
17   and related infrastructure.
18
19   (d) Sustainable and Climate-Resilient. — Mobility planning, infrastructure investments,
20   and the development of the built environment shall minimize environmental impacts,
21   promote resource efficiency, support cleaner transport technologies, and strengthen
22   resilience to climate-related risks.
23
24   (e) Safe and Health-Promoting. — Mobility systems and the built environment shall
25   adopt a safety-first approach consistent with the Safe System framework, and be
26   designed to reduce road crash fatalities and serious injuries, and promote public health
27   through safe and supportive conditions for active transport, including walking and
28   cycling.
29
30   (f) Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions - Mobility systems and built
31   environment shall prioritize the provision of green infrastructure, including the
32   provision of native trees and the restoration of our urban forests, amid extreme heat
33   and rain brought about by the climate crisis.
34
35   SEC. 5. Creation of the Commission. — There is hereby created a Congressional
36   Commission on Mobility (MobiComm), hereinafter referred to as the Commission, to
37   undertake a comprehensive national review and evaluation of the performance of the
38   Philippine road-based transport, road infrastructure and related built environment
39   policies and practices, for the purpose of recommending transformative, concrete, and
40   targeted reforms in governance, standards, planning, regulation, investment, and
41   implementation, with a view to achieving safe, accessible, inclusive, affordable,
42   efficient, and sustainable mobility outcomes for all.

31   Section 14. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
