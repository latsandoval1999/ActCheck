                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to protect Filipino farmers from usurious lending practices by
imposing a maximum effective interest rate of six percent (6%) per annum on
agricultural loans, mandating the restructuring of existing agricultural debts under
fair terms, and strengthening the regulatory authority of the Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas (BSP) over agricultural credit allocation.

       Despite their vital role in ensuring food security, many Filipino farmers
continue to face limited access to formal credit facilities, forcing them to rely on
informal lenders that impose exploitative interest rates that can reach as high as
twenty percent (20%) to thirty percent (30%) monthly. The Philippine Statistics
Authority confirm that farmers remain among the poorest sectors in 2021, with
consistently high poverty incidence compared to other groups1. At the national level,
about 18.1% of Filipinos lived below the poverty threshold, reflecting persistent
economic vulnerability in rural communities2. Studies from the World Bank
emphasize that limited access to credit, lack of farm inputs, and weak market access
continue to constrain rural incomes and productivity3. Meanwhile, research from the
Philippine Institute for Development Studies highlights that access to formal credit
remains uneven and insufficient, leaving many farmers dependent on informal and
often exploitative lending arrangements4. While the government has implemented
various agricultural financing programs, such as the release of approximately Five

        This proposed measure addresses these systemic challenges through the
establishment of a clear legal ceiling on interest rates, effectively removing the space
for usurious practices to persist. It likewise provides for the automatic restructuring
of existing loans to align outstanding obligations with the prescribed interest cap
rate, thereby extending immediate relief to heavily indebted farmers.

       The bill further directs the BSP and the Monetary Board to determine
appropriate loanable allocations for the agricultural sector in order to encourage
participation in agricultural financing and improve farmers’ access to formal credit
mechanisms.

        More importantly, this measure affirms the State’s duty to protect farmers
from exploitation and to promote a fair, accessible, and responsive agricultural credit
system. Farmers, who toil under the harshest conditions to ensure food security,
should not remain at the mercy of predatory lenders who profit from their
vulnerability. The urgency of this bill cannot be overstated: every planting season
delayed because of debt, every harvest diminished due to unjust interest, marks a
step backward for national development. The passage of this measure affirms that
the dignity of Filipino farmers deserves protection, their burdens demand relief, and
their future calls for decisive action.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROHIBITING USURIOUS LENDING TO FARMERS, APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFORE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Usury Against Farmers
Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article II, Sec. 9 of the 1987 Constitution states
that:

               “The State shall promote a just and dynamic social order that will
        ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation and free the people from
        poverty through policies that provide adequate social services, promote full
        employment, a rising standard of living, and an improved quality of life for
        all.”

        Towards this end, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the
agricultural sector from systemic exploitation. It shall commit to a proactive stance
in monitoring and dismantling usurious structures, replacing them with
institutionalized credit reforms that uphold the dignity and economic well-being of
the Filipino farmer.

       SEC. 3. Application of Interest Rates. – Interest on loans to farmers whose
landholding does not exceed seven (7) hectares, whether with collateral or without,
shall not exceed an effective interest rate (EIR) of six per cent (6%) per annum. The
same rate shall likewise apply to forbearance of any money, good or credit, and in
judgments. Any figure above this shall be considered usurious and illegal.

       SEC. 4. Restructuring the Balance of Existing Loans. – Upon the effectivity of this
Act, all existing loans shall be automatically restructured at an EIR not exceeding six
percent (5%) per annum.

      SEC. 5. Determination of Loanable Amounts. – The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas
and the Monetary Board shall determine the amount, or the percentage of loanable
amounts to farmers in the portfolio of banks, public or private.

       SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Finance shall
formulate the necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement the
provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days of its effectivity.

       SEC. 7. Penalty. – Any natural person, not a government official or employee,
who violates any provision of this Act shall be punished with imprisonment of one
(1) year and/or a fine not exceeding One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00).

       Any government employee or official who violates any provision of this Act
shall be punished with imprisonment of two (2) years and/or a fine not exceeding
Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00).

       Any juridical person who violates any provision of this Act shall be punished
with a fine of Five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00). Its directors and/or
officers may also be punished with imprisonment of six (6) months to one (1) year if
found liable of a violation under this Act.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All acts, decrees, letters of instruction, issuances,
orders, or parts thereof inconsistent with any provision of this Act are hereby
deemed repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the rest of the provisions shall remain in force and effect.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in two (2) newspapers of general circulation, or in the Official Gazette.
