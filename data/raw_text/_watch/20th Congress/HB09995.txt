                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        In recent years, the cost of air travel has become a major financial strain for millions
of Filipinos, including our Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs), MSME entrepreneurs, and
middle-class families. While competition among commercial airlines has supposedly
democratized air travel through advertised "low-cost" base fares, consumers are
systematically subjected to a deceptive practice known as "drip pricing" or the accumulation
of "junk fees."

         Under current airline booking systems, consumers are enticed by low advertised ticket
prices, only to discover a cascade of mandatory hidden charges added sequentially
throughout the digital checkout process. These surprise surcharges—ranging from arbitrary
web administration fees, mandatory booking system fees, hidden fuel adjustments, and
pre-checked optional add-ons like insurance or seat selection traps—frequently double or
triple the final cost of the ticket.

      This deceptive pricing model actively distorts market competition and violates the
fundamental rights of consumers to clear, honest, and upfront information.

       This measure, the "All-In Airline Ticket Price Transparency Act," is an original,
standalone bill designed to protect the purchasing power of Filipino travelers. It mandates
that any domestic or international airline operating within the Philippines must display an
"All-In" price—inclusive of all taxes, surcharges, and non-optional fees at the very first point
of consumer interaction.

        By eliminating predatory hidden costs and forcing absolute price transparency, this
bill restores integrity to the aviation consumer market, protects hard-working citizens from
predatory corporate pricing, and fosters a fairer domestic travel industry.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT MANDATING ABSOLUTE PRICE TRANSPARENCY IN COMMERCIAL
  AVIATION TICKETING, PROHIBITING THE IMPOSITION OF HIDDEN FEES
 AND SURCHARGES, AND PROVIDING STRICT PENALTIES FOR DECEPTIVE
                      DRIP-PRICING PRACTICES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “The All-In Airline Ticket Price
Transparency Act.”

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of air transport
in national development and the integration of global Filipino communities. It is the policy of
the State to protect consumers against deceptive, unfair, and unconscionable sales practices.
To this end, the State shall enforce strict price transparency in the commercial aviation sector,
ensuring that consumers are provided with the complete, final cost of air travel at the onset of
any commercial transaction.

       Section 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

        (a) All-In Price refers to the total, comprehensive financial cost required to purchase
an air ticket, explicitly inclusive of the base fare, government taxes, fuel surcharges, airport
operational fees, web administration fees, and any other mandatory cost.

        (b) Drip Pricing refers to the deceptive practice of incrementally revealing fees,
surcharges, or costs throughout a digital or physical checkout process, resulting in a final
price higher than the initially advertised rate.

       (c) Junk Fees refer to arbitrary, non-essential administrative or service fees tacked
onto a transaction that do not represent a distinct, tangible service chosen freely by the
consumer.

        Section 4. Mandate of Absolute "All-In" Price Display. – Any commercial airline,
digital booking platform, travel agency, or third-party aggregators operating or selling flights
within the territory of the Philippines shall be strictly required to display the full All-In Price
of an air ticket in all print, digital, broadcast, and social media advertisements, as well as
upon the initial search query on any digital booking interface.

        At no point in the customer journey shall an airline display a "base fare" or "naked
price" that excludes mandatory operational or administrative overhead costs.

       Section 5. Prohibited Ticketing Practices. – The following commercial practices are
hereby declared unlawful:

        (a) The use of pre-checked digital boxes that automatically opt the consumer into
paying for non-mandatory add-ons such as travel insurance, baggage allowance upgrades, or
seat selections;

        (b) The charging of distinct "Web Administration Fees" or "Digital Processing Fees"
for purchases made online through the airline’s own primary digital platforms; and

        (c) Altering the price of a selected ticket while it is actively held in a digital cart
during a standard checkout session, except in cases of verified session expiration exceeding
ten (10) minutes.

       Section 6. Penalties and Fines. – Any airline or ticketing platform found violating
the provisions of this Act shall be penalized by the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB), in
coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), with the following
administrative fines:

       (a) First Offense: A fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP 500,000);

       (b) Second Offense: A fine of One Million Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP
1,200,000); and

       (c) Subsequent Offenses: A fine of Three Million Pesos (PHP 3,000,000) per violation
incident, alongside a temporary suspension of the operator's digital or physical sales
clearance for up to thirty (30) days.

        Section 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) and the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI) shall jointly promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
implementation.

        Section 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected shall continue to be in full force and
effect.

        Section 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

       Section 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
