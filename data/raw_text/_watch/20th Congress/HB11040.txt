                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

For many people struggling with substance use, the hardest part of recovery is not
only overcoming addiction but also overcoming the judgment of others. Negative
labels and stereotypes can lead to discrimination, shame, and rejection, making it
harder for recovering individuals to seek help and rebuild their lives. While
accountability remains important, recovery should also give people a chance to
change. A person who has completed rehabilitation should not be de ined forever by
his or her past.

Recovery does not end after rehabilitation. For many recovering individuals, the next
challenge is inding a job, earning a living, and becoming productive and independent
members of society. Without skills, livelihood opportunities, and support, they may
struggle to rebuild their lives and become vulnerable to returning to harmful
circumstances. The State must therefore provide them with a real opportunity to start
again.

This measure seeks to institutionalize Technical-Vocational Education and Training
(TVET) and livelihood programs speci ically for rehabilitated drug dependents. These
programs will help them gain practical skills, improve their chances of inding decent
employment, and pursue small businesses or other livelihood opportunities. By

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in
coordination with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), shall
institutionalize the provision of technical-vocational education and training (TVET)
and livelihood programs designed speci ically for rehabilitated drug dependents. The
TVET and livelihood programs shall focus on competitive and employable skills that
can be acquired and developed by rehabilitated drug dependents to further enhance
their ability to ind gainful employment and entrepreneurial livelihood opportunities.

The DOLE shall provide incentives to companies which shall employ rehabilitated
drug dependents who have undergone the TVET and livelihood programs mandated
under this Act.

SECTION 2. The Director General of TESDA shall immediately include in the
Authority’s programs the implementation of this Act, the funding of which shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the TESDA shall, in coordination with the DOLE, formulate
the rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 4. Separability Clause. — If any part, section or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such
declaration shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
