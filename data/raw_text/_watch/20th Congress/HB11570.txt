                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution directs the State to adopt an integrated and comprehensive
approach to health development and to give priority to the needs of, among others, the
disabled and children (Article XIII, Section 11). It further mandates the establishment of a
special agency for persons with disability for their rehabilitation, self-development, and self-
reliance, and their integration into the mainstream of society (Article XIII, Section 13). The
Philippines, as a State Party to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with
Disabilities, has likewise committed to ensure the full and equal enjoyment of all human
rights by persons with disability.
        Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition that
affects how a person communicates, interacts with others, and experiences the world.
Advocacy groups such as the Autism Society Philippines have long estimated that more
than a million Filipinos may be on the autism spectrum, yet the country still lacks a
dedicated national framework to guide how government responds to their needs. Families
commonly face late or costly diagnosis, a shortage of developmental specialists outside
major urban centers, expensive and hard-to-access therapies, limited inclusive learning
environments, and few pathways to meaningful work once formal schooling ends.
        Existing laws provide important but general protections. Republic Act No. 7277, or
the Magna Carta for Persons with Disability, as amended, sets out the rights and privileges
of persons with disability; Republic Act No. 11228 provides for mandatory PhilHealth
coverage of all persons with disability; and Republic Act No. 11650 institutes a policy of
inclusion and services for learners with disabilities. None of these, however, establishes a
single, coordinated plan that ties together screening, intervention, education, employment,
and social protection specifically for persons with ASD across the life course.
        This bill seeks to close that gap. It mandates the formulation of a National Plan of
Action for Autism Spectrum Disorder, to be led by the National Council on Disability
Affairs (NCDA) through existing inter-agency mechanisms and in consultation with local
government units, civil society, autism self-advocates, and parents’ groups. It assigns clear
responsibilities: the Department of Health for integrating ASD screening, referral, and
diagnostic protocols into primary and maternal and child health care; the Department of
Health and PhilHealth for the progressive availability of evidence-based interventions in

public facilities; the Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education, and
the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority for inclusive education and
transition pathways; the Department of Labor and Employment and the Civil Service
Commission for equal and supported employment; and the Department of Social Welfare
and Development and local government units for social protection and community-based
support. The bill also declares discrimination against persons with ASD unlawful, requires
sustained public awareness campaigns, and provides for funding through the General
Appropriations Act.
         This measure adopts as its reference Senate Bill No. 1822, entitled “An Act
Instituting a National Plan of Action for the Care, Support and Inclusion of Persons with
Autism Spectrum Disorder and Appropriating Funds Therefor,” the substitute bill
recommended for approval of the Senate of the Twentieth Congress. Filing a counterpart
measure in the House of Representatives is intended to facilitate the timely enactment of
this legislation and its harmonization with the Senate version.
         Local government units are often the first point of contact for families of children
with ASD, whether through barangay health centers, child development programs, or local
social welfare offices. A national plan that equips and connects these frontline services with
national agencies will allow every Filipino with ASD, wherever he or she lives, to receive
timely support and to take part in community life with dignity.
         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
     INSTITUTING A NATIONAL PLAN OF ACTION FOR THE CARE, SUPPORT,
     AND INCLUSION OF PERSONS WITH AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, AND
                    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

1           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
2    Congress assembled:

3           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Autism Care,
4    Support, and Inclusion Act.”

5           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes persons with Autism
6    Spectrum Disorder (ASD) as persons with disability. It shall protect and uphold their rights,
7    dignity, and inherent worth, and shall promote their full inclusion, participation, and well-
8    being in society.

9           To this end, the State shall adopt responsive, inclusive, and well-coordinated
10   policies, programs, and services that guarantee persons with ASD and their families access
11   to early detection and screening, timely intervention, quality health care, inclusive
12   education, employment opportunities, and appropriate social support.

13          The State shall likewise cultivate an environment free from stigma and
14   discrimination, where persons with ASD can attain holistic development, independence,
15   self-reliance, and productive participation in community life in accordance with their
16   individual capacities and aspirations.

17          Further, the State shall strengthen institutional and community-based mechanisms,
18   advance multi-sectoral collaboration, and encourage the participation of civil society and
19   the private sector in complementing government efforts, consistent with this Act and other
20   applicable laws.


                                                  3
 1           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
 2   mean:

 3           (a) Autism or Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) – a neurodevelopmental condition
 4           marked by persistent difficulties in social communication and interaction, together
 5           with restricted or repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities, as defined in
 6           the latest edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders
 7           (DSM);

 8           (b) Early Detection and Screening – community-based or facility-based activities
 9           conducted by trained health personnel to identify early signs of ASD and to facilitate
10           timely referral for appropriate assessment;

11           (c) Inclusive Education – the process of addressing and responding to the diverse
12           needs of all learners by ensuring their full participation, presence, and achievement
13           in learning environments, consistent with Republic Act No. 11650, including the
14           provision of reasonable accommodation and learning support;

15           (d) Intervention Services – evidence-based medical, therapeutic, educational,
16           habilitative, and other support services designed to improve the functioning,
17           development, and quality of life of persons with ASD;

18           (e) NCDA – the National Council on Disability Affairs; and

19           (f) Persons with ASD – individuals who have been clinically diagnosed with ASD.

20           SEC. 4. National Plan of Action for Autism Spectrum Disorder. – A National Plan
21   of Action for Autism Spectrum Disorder, hereinafter referred to as the “Plan,” shall be
22   formulated. The Plan shall set out coordinated policies, programs, projects, services, and
23   activities that ensure access by persons with ASD and their families to early detection and
24   screening, timely intervention, quality health care, inclusive education, employment
25   opportunities, and appropriate social support. The Plan shall be periodically reviewed and
26   updated to respond to emerging needs and developments, and its components shall be
27   progressively mainstreamed into the plans, programs, and budgets of the relevant
28   government agencies and sectors.

29           SEC. 5. Inter-Agency and Multi-Sectoral Coordination for Planning and
30   Implementation. – The NCDA shall lead the formulation of the Plan in coordination with




                                                     4
 1   the concerned national government agencies, local government units (LGUs), civil society
 2   organizations, autism self-advocates, parents’ groups, and other stakeholders.

 3           The NCDA shall likewise advocate for, coordinate, oversee, and assess the
 4   implementation of this Act and the Plan through existing inter-agency mechanisms.

 5           The NCDA, through its Executive Director, shall provide secretariat support and
 6   assistance in the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the Plan.

 7           SEC. 6. Early Detection, Screening, and Diagnosis. – The Department of Health
 8   (DOH) shall incorporate protocols for the detection, screening, referral, and diagnosis of
 9   ASD into primary health care, maternal and child health programs, and other appropriate
10   health services. The DOH shall also develop standard clinical guidelines and training
11   programs for health professionals and health workers.

12           SEC. 7. Health and Intervention Services. – The DOH and the Philippine Health
13   Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), in coordination with other concerned agencies, shall
14   ensure the progressive availability of evidence-based ASD-related interventions and
15   therapies in public health facilities, and shall establish functional referral networks across
16   all levels of care.

17           SEC. 8. Inclusive and Appropriate Education. – The Department of Education
18   (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and the Technical Education and
19   Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in coordination with educational institutions, shall
20   strengthen inclusive education and training programs for learners with ASD, provide
21   reasonable accommodation and learning support, and promote transition pathways toward
22   employment or independent living.

23           SEC. 9. Employment and Livelihood. – The Department of Labor and Employment
24   (DOLE), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), and other relevant government agencies, in
25   coordination with the private sector, shall promote equal employment opportunities, skills
26   development, and supported employment programs for persons with ASD, consistent with
27   existing labor and disability laws.

28           SEC. 10. Social Protection and Community-Based Support. – The Department of
29   Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and the LGUs shall include persons with ASD
30   in existing social protection, assistance, and community-based support programs, as well as
31   in family education and counseling services: Provided, That the DSWD and the LGUs shall



                                                   5
 1   develop social welfare programs and services responsive to the emerging needs of persons
 2   with ASD, their families, and carers.

 3           SEC. 11. Information, Education, and Advocacy. – The NCDA, in coordination
 4   with relevant national government agencies, civil society organizations, and the private
 5   sector, shall lead sustained information and education campaigns to promote public
 6   awareness, understanding, and acceptance of persons with ASD.

 7           SEC. 12. Prohibited Acts. – No person, entity, or government agency shall
 8   discriminate against, deny, or impede the rights of persons with ASD, including, but not
 9   limited to, their rights to dignity, health care, inclusive education, employment, livelihood,
10   social protection, and participation in decisions affecting their welfare.

11           SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
12   this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies.
13   Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
14   included in the annual General Appropriations Act: Provided, That the Department of
15   Budget and Management, in coordination with the NCDA, the DOH, and other relevant
16   government agencies, shall determine the annual appropriations for the implementation of
17   this Act and the Plan.

18           SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
19   effectivity of this Act, the NCDA, in consultation with the DOH, PhilHealth, DepEd,
20   CHED, TESDA, DOLE, CSC, DSWD, LGUs, other concerned government agencies, civil
21   society organizations, autism self-advocates, parents’ groups, and other stakeholders, shall
22   promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

23           SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
24   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to
25   be in full force and effect.

26           SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
27   regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
28   amended, or modified accordingly.

29           SEC. 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
30   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

31           Approved,
