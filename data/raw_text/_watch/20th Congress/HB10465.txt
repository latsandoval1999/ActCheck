                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

         In his State of the Nation Address (SONA), the President issued an urgent and
unequivocal directive to lower the country's power rates, declaring: "At kung
pagbawas ng presyo ang usapan, sa aking palagay ay panahon na para tanggalin na natin
ang system loss na pinapasa sa consumer.” Emphasizing that ordinary Filipinos are not
at fault for these inefficiencies, the President noted that it is unjust to make them pay
for it, ultimately demanding the immediate amendment of the EPIRA to prohibit the
charging of system losses and the corresponding Value-Added Tax (VAT) against
consumers.

       Answering this urgent presidential mandate, this measure addresses the cost
of electricity in the Philippines, which remains a significant consideration for both
household budgets and the global competitiveness of local industries. As the nation
seeks sustainable ways to lower energy costs, a reevaluation of the components that
make up the monthly electricity bill, specifically the charging of system losses and its
VAT, is necessary and long overdue.

       System losses refer to the power lost during the transmission and distribution
of electricity due to technical and non-technical factors. Under the current legal
framework—specifically Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832 and Section 43(f) of
Republic Act No. 9136 (EPIRA)—distribution utilities and rural electric cooperatives
are authorized to recover a certain percentage of these losses from consumers.

        However, standard economic principles generally dictate that operational
losses—whether due to technical inefficiencies, infrastructure wear and tear, or
pilferage—should be absorbed by an enterprise as part of its standard operational
risks. Shifting the financial burden of system losses entirely to consumers diminishes
the natural financial incentive for distribution utilities to continuously invest in
modernizing their grid infrastructure and improving operational efficiency.
Furthermore, applying a consumption tax (VAT) on electricity that was lost before
reaching the end-user requires legislative correction.

       To align the power sector with best business practices and lower electricity
rates for the public, this measure introduces a comprehensive framework to
restructure the treatment of system losses:

   1. For Private Distribution Utilities: It mandates the abolition of the system loss
      charges. Private utilities are expected to absorb these operational risks as
      overhead, which will inherently incentivize immediate upgrades to their
      infrastructure and the adoption of better loss-mitigation technologies.

   2. For Rural Electric Cooperatives (RECs): Recognizing the distinct financial
      structures of RECs and the critical importance of continuous rural
      electrification, this bill institutes an accelerated transition. It initially reduces
      their recoverable system loss cap to a maximum of five percent (5%), with a
      scheduled phase-out to zero percent (0%) within one (1) year.

   3. VAT Exemption: While the system loss charges is slated for abolition, this bill
      explicitly amends the National Internal Revenue Code to exempt any such
      charge from VAT. This is a dual strategy: it provides immediate tax relief to
      consumers in REC franchise areas during the one-year phase-out period, and
      it establishes a legal safeguard in the tax code against any future attempts to
      tax embedded operational losses.

   4. Prohibition on Indirect Recovery: To ensure the intent of this policy is
      realized, the bill tasks the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) to strictly
      monitor and prevent the indirect recovery of system losses through
      adjustments to other bill components or new administrative fees.

     By internalizing these operational risks, this legislation aims to protect the
consuming public, systematically lower energy costs, and foster a more efficient,
modern, and reliable power distribution sector.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                              AN ACT
 PROHIBITING THE DIRECT OR INDIRECT RECOVERY OF DISTRIBUTION
 SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES FROM ELECTRICITY CONSUMERS, REDUCING
 AND EVENTUALLY PHASING OUT THE CAP OF RECOVERABLE SYSTEM
   LOSSES OF RURAL ELECTRIC COOPERATIVES, EXEMPTING SYSTEM
 LOSSES FROM THE VALUE-ADDED TAX, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
    REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832, AS AMENDED, REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT
OF 2001,” SECTION 109 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF
            1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “System Loss Charges
Abolition Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
the public interest as it is affected by the rates and services of electric utilities.
Towards this end, the State shall ensure transparent, fair, and affordable prices of
electricity, protect consumers from unjust charges, and demand full operational and
economic efficiency from all distribution utilities and electric cooperatives. The State
recognizes that consumers should not be penalized for electricity lost due to
inefficiencies, technical failures, or pilferage within the distribution system.

SEC. 3. Abolition of System Loss Charges for Private Distribution Utilities. – The
direct or indirect recovery of system loss charges, whether technical or non-technical,
by private distribution utilities from their captive and contestable markets is hereby

               absolutely prohibited. Private distribution utilities shall bear the costs of their
               respective system losses as part of their operational risk and business overhead.

               SEC. 4. Reduction and Phase-out of System Loss Cap for Rural Electric
               Cooperatives. – To ensure a just transition and prevent the disruption of rural
               electri cation, the cap of recoverable system losses for Rural Electric Cooperatives
               (RECs) is hereby reduced to a maximum of ve percent (5%) upon the effectivity of
               this Act.

                       One (1) year after the effectivity of this Act, the direct or indirect recovery of
               system loss charges by RECs shall be absolutely prohibited, matching the standard
               set for private distribution utilities in Section 3 of this Act.

               SEC. 5. System Modernization Support for Rural Electric Cooperatives. – To
               facilitate the accelerated one-year phase-out mandated in Section 4, the National
               Electri cation Administration (NEA) is hereby directed to allocate targeted nancial
               and technical assistance to quali ed RECs.

                     This assistance shall be strictly utilized for capital expenditures aimed at
               immediately reducing technical system loss, including but not limited to the
               upgrading of distribution lines, replacement of inef cient transformers, and
               implementation of smart grid technologies. The initial funding for this support shall
               be sourced from the unprogrammed funds of the National Treasury and the existing
               subsidies allocated to the NEA under the General Appropriations Act.

               SEC. 6. Prohibition on Circumvention. – Any attempt by a distribution utility or
               electric cooperative to indirectly recover system loss charges by adjusting other
               components of the electricity bill, introducing new administrative fees, or utilizing
               any pass-through mechanisms or cost-recovery schemes that produce the same
               result as a system loss charge is strictly prohibited. The Energy Regulatory
               Commission (ERC) shall strictly monitor and ensure compliance with this provision.

               SEC. 7. Penalties. – Any distribution utility, rural electric cooperative, or their
               respective of cers or directors found violating the provisions of this Act shall be
               subject to mandatory refunds to consumers, substantial nes, regulatory sanctions,
               and potential suspension or revocation of operating authority, as determined by the
               ERC.
