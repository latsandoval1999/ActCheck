                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Cumulative net-metered capacity stood at 157 megawatts as of May
2025, the yield of twelve years of the programme. Independent analysis of
market generation data suggests that roughly 600 megawatts of rooftop solar
were added in the twelve months to April 2026 alone. Households are now
installing generation faster than the approval system can process it, and the
greater part of that capacity sits outside the statutory scheme altogether. The
framework no longer describes the industry it governs.
       This bill closes that gap through a single classification rule, drawn from
the Electric Power Industry Reform Act, which ties regulatory obligation to sale
into the market: an end-user who generates power for personal consumption is
not a generation company. The operative architecture follows from that
premise. Qualified non-exporting systems require no prior approval and no
certificate of compliance. Distribution utilities may not demand technical
studies, meter replacement, agreements or added charges. Notification follows
installation rather than preceding it. And homeowners' associations, developers
and property managers may not prohibit installations on property the end-user
owns or exclusively controls.
       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT PROMOTING AND SAFEGUARDING THE INSTALLATION AND
OPERATION OF BEHIND-THE-METER ELECTRICITY GENERATION AND
ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEMS FOR PERSONAL CONSUMPTION, AMENDING
FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE “ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001”, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sariling
Kuryente Act”.
       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure the
reliable, secure, affordable and environmentally sustainable supply of
electricity, to accelerate the development of renewable energy resources, and to
protect the interest of the consuming public.
       SEC. 3. Definitions. — For purposes of this Act:
  (a) “Behind-the-meter system” refers to a generation facility, an energy
       storage facility, or both, located on the premises of an end-user and
       connected on the end-user’s side of the metering point.
  (b) “Distribution utility” refers to any electric distribution utility as defined
       in Republic Act No. 9136, including electric cooperatives.
  (c) “Export-limiting device” refers to equipment, certified under standards
       recognized by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), that prevents
       the flow of electricity from a behind-the-meter system into the
       distribution system, including reverse-power relays, zero-export
       controllers and equivalent means.
  (d) “Non-exporting system” refers to a behind-the-meter system equipped
       with a certified export-limiting device.
  (e)​ “Personal consumption” refers to the use of electricity by the end-user
        on the premises where the system is located, and includes use by
        members of the end-user’s household or by the end-user’s own business
        operations on those premises, but does not include the sale, resale or
        supply of electricity to any other person.
   (f)​ “Exporting system” refers to a behind-the-meter system that delivers
        electricity into the distribution system under the net metering
        programme established by Republic Act No. 9513, the distributed energy
        resources programme, or any successor mechanism.
        SEC. 4. Self-Generation Not a Generation Business. — An end-user who
owns or operates a behind-the-meter system solely for personal consumption
shall not, by reason thereof, be considered a generation company, a supplier,
an aggregator or a person engaged in the generation business under Republic
Act No. 9136, and shall not be required to secure a certificate of compliance, a
license, a franchise or any other authorization from the ERC in respect of such
system.
        Section 6 and the related provisions of Republic Act No. 9136 are hereby
amended accordingly.
        SEC. 5. No Prior Approval for Non-Exporting Systems. — The installation
and energization of a non-exporting system shall not require the prior approval
of the distribution utility or of the ERC.
        No distribution utility shall require, as a condition to the installation,
energization or continued operation of a non-exporting system:
   (a)​ any prior authorization, clearance or permit from the utility;
   (b)​ any additional technical, impact or interconnection study;
   (c)​ the replacement, reconfiguration or upgrading of the existing meter;
   (d)​ the execution of an interconnection, net metering or similar agreement;
        or
   (e)​ the payment of any fee, deposit, bond, insurance premium or charge not
        imposed on other end-users of the same customer class.
        SEC. 6. Notification After Energization. — Within thirty (30) days from
energization, the end-user or the end-user’s installer shall notify the
distribution utility of the existence of the non-exporting system, using a
standard form prescribed by the Department of Energy (DOE), stating the
system capacity, the protection and export-limiting devices installed, the
certification thereof, and the name and accreditation of the installer.
Notification shall be free of charge, may be made electronically, and shall not
be a condition to the operation of the system. Failure to notify shall subject the
end-user to an administrative fine of not more than Five thousand pesos
(P5,000.00), which shall not be a ground for disconnection of electric service.
        SEC. 7. Inspection and Correction. — The distribution utility may inspect
a non-exporting system within thirty (30) days from notification, upon
reasonable prior notice and at no cost to the end-user.
        The utility may require correction only of a demonstrated failure to
comply with a standard referred to in Section 8, stated in writing and
identifying the specific standard and deficiency. The end-user may contest the
finding before the ERC, which shall resolve the matter within thirty (30) days.
        SEC. 8. Safety Standards Not Affected. — The exemptions granted under
this Act are exemptions from economic and market regulation only.
        Nothing in this Act shall be construed to exempt any behind-the-meter
system, or any person installing, owning or operating one, from:
   (a)​ the Philippine Electrical Code;
   (b)​ the National Building Code and its implementing rules and regulations;
   (c)​ the requirements of local building officials and electrical inspectors;
   (d)​ the Philippine Distribution Code and the Philippine Grid Code, as
        applicable; or
   (e)​ any anti-islanding, reverse-power protection, equipment certification, fire
        safety, energy storage handling or installer accreditation standard
        prescribed by the ERC, the DOE or the Bureau of Fire Protection.
        A behind-the-meter system found, after inspection, to be capable of
energizing a de-energized portion of the distribution system shall be
disconnected from the premises wiring until corrected. Disconnection under
this paragraph shall not affect the end-user’s supply of electricity from the
distribution utility.
        SEC. 9. Homeowners’ Associations, Developers and Property Managers.
— No homeowners’ association, subdivision developer, condominium
corporation, property manager, lessor or similar entity shall prohibit, or impose
an unreasonable restriction upon, the installation or operation of a
behind-the-meter system on property owned or exclusively controlled by the
end-user.
        Such entities may prescribe reasonable rules addressed to structural
loading, fire safety, public safety, the integrity of shared or common facilities,
and the appearance of installations visible from common areas, provided that
such rules are published in advance, are applied uniformly, and do not have
the effect of substantially increasing the cost of, or substantially decreasing the
efficiency of, the system.
A rule, covenant, deed restriction or contractual stipulation contrary to this
Section shall be void. The Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development shall hear and resolve disputes under this Section within sixty
(60) days.
        SEC. 10. Exporting Systems; Deemed Approval. — For an exporting
system, the distribution utility shall act on a complete application within ten
(10) working days from receipt.
        Failure to act within that period shall be deemed approval of the
application, and the distribution utility shall install the necessary metering
facility within fifteen (15) working days thereafter.
        A distribution utility that denies an application shall state the technical
grounds in writing within the same period. The denial shall be appealable to
the ERC, which shall resolve the appeal within thirty (30) days.
        SEC. 11. Local Government Permits. — Local government units shall
adopt the standard application forms prescribed by the DOE and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, shall issue electrical permits
for behind-the-meter systems within three (3) working days and certificates of
final electrical inspection within seven (7) working days from receipt of a
complete application, and shall not impose any requirement not contained in
the standard form.
        Failure to act within the prescribed periods shall be deemed approval
upon presentation of proof of filing and of payment of the prescribed fees,
without prejudice to the administrative liability of the responsible official under
existing law.
        SEC. 12. Non-Diminution of Net Metering and Related Rights. — Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to diminish any right, benefit or entitlement
available to an end-user under Republic Act No. 9513, the net metering rules of
the ERC, or any DOE policy extending renewable energy certificate ownership,
multi-site or aggregate net metering, or similar benefits to qualified end-users.
        An end-user who operates a non-exporting system under this Act may
subsequently apply to export under Section 10 without penalty or
re-registration of the entire system.
        SEC. 13. Promotion of Behind-the-Meter Systems. — The DOE shall
promote the wider adoption of behind-the-meter generation and energy storage,
and shall:
  (a)​ identify and remove national and local administrative barriers to
       installation;
  (b)​ in coordination with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the Development
       Bank of the Philippines, the Land Bank of the Philippines and other
       financial institutions, improve access to financing, including on-bill and
       cooperative-based financing arrangements suited to low- and
       middle-income households;
  (c)​ maintain and publish a register of accredited installers and of certified
       equipment, including certified export-limiting devices; and
  (d)​ conduct public information campaigns on system safety, maintenance,
       and the rights of end-users under this Act.
       SEC. 14. Technical Assistance to Electric Cooperatives. — The National
Electrification Administration, in coordination with the DOE and the ERC,
shall provide electric cooperatives with the technical assistance, training,
personnel support and equipment necessary to discharge the notification,
inspection and application-processing functions under this Act within the
periods prescribed.
       SEC. 15. Existing Issuances. — Circulars, resolutions, joint
memorandum circulars and other issuances of the DOE, the ERC and other
agencies that are consistent with this Act shall remain in force until amended
or revoked. Where such an issuance prescribes a period shorter than, or a
requirement less burdensome than, that provided in this Act, the issuance
shall prevail.
       SEC. 16. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — It shall be unlawful for a
distribution utility, or any of its officers or employees, to refuse, delay or
condition the supply of electricity to an end-user by reason of the installation
or operation of a behind-the-meter system in accordance with this Act, or to
impose any charge, requirement, classification or tariff prohibited under
Sections 5 and 10.
       Violation shall subject the distribution utility to a fine of not less than
One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) nor more than One million pesos
(P1,000,000.00) for each offense, imposed by the ERC after notice and hearing,
without prejudice to the administrative liability of the responsible officer and to
the restoration of service and refund of any charge collected in violation of this
Act.
       SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOE and the ERC shall jointly promulgate
the implementing rules and regulations, in consultation with the Department
of the Interior and Local Government, the National Electrification
Administration, distribution utilities, electric cooperatives, consumer
organizations and the renewable energy industry.
      SEC. 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue in full force and effect.
      SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modified accordingly.
      SEC. 20. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
