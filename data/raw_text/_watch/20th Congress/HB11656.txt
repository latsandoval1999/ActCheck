                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippine health care system continues to face persistent challenges in
ensuring accessible, affordable, and culturally responsive health services for all
Filipinos. Despite advances in modern medicine, a significant portion of the
population continues to rely on traditional and complementary medicine as part of
their health care practices.

       Globally, the World Health Organization (WHO) reports that 88% of the
population adopts traditional medicine for preventive, curative, or rehabilitative
purposes (Agoot, 2023). In the Philippines, 43% of adult Filipinos have sought
complementary and alternative medicine, particularly the services of manghihilot or
bone setter and partera and albularyo or herbalist (Ladia, et al., 2018). These
figures highlight the continuing relevance and demand for traditional and
complementary health modalities.

        Republic Act No. 8423, otherwise known as the “Traditional and Alternative
Medicine Act of 1997,” was enacted to promote and advocate the use of traditional
and alternative health care. However, nearly three decades since its passage,
significant gaps remain in terms of regulatory capacity, integration into the broader
health system, and alignment with current health frameworks such as the
Universal Health Care (UHC) Act.

       With growing preference on herbal formulations over synthetic medicine
underscores the necessity to introduce regulation of herbal medicines to ensure
quality, safety, and efficacy (Sachan, Vishnoi, & Kumar, 2016). The lack of
standardized regulation and clinical validation mechanisms for traditional and
complementary medicine may results in inconsistent quality, safety concerns, and
limited integration into formal health systems. Moreover, under the Global Survey
on Traditional Medicine conducted by the WHO, member States are assisted to
research, integrate, and regulate traditional medicine in their national health
systems (Zhao & Terry, 2023).
       This bill seeks to address gaps in the traditional and complementary
medicine in the country by reorganizing the Philippine Institute of Traditional and
Alternative Health Care (PITAHC) into the Traditional, Complementary, and
Integrative Medicine Administration (TRACIMA), strengthening its mandate as the
lead policy, regulatory, and research body.

      To achieve these objectives, TRACIMA shall be responsible to conduct
research and development, validation, integration, regulation, consumer protection,
and protection of indigenous knowledge systems of traditional and complementary
health care practices. Through this measure, TRACIMA can establish the minimum
standard of health care based on validated medical data.

       Finally, the bill strengthens inter-agency coordination and health technology
assessment (HTA) mechanisms to ensure that only safe, effective, and cost-efficient
practices are adopted within the national health system allowing coverage and
inclusion in the health packages of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth) under the UHC.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought to
modernize the country’s approach to traditional and complementary medicine,
ensure public safety, and fully harness its potential as a complementary pillar of a
responsive and inclusive health care system.

Philippines in the Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. The Philippine Institute of Traditional and Alternative Health
Care (PITAHC) is hereby renamed as the Traditional, Complementary, Integrative
Medicine Administration (TRACIMA).
      SEC. 2. This Act shall be known as the “Traditional, Complementary, and
Integrative Medicine Administration (TRACIMA) Act.”
      SEC. 3. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to improve the quality
and delivery of healthcare services to the Filipino people through the development,
regulation and integration of traditional and complementary medicine into the
national health care delivery system, consistent with the Universal Health Care
(UHC) Act framework of automatic population coverage, comprehensive service
coverage, and equitable financial coverage.
      The State shall ensure that traditional, complementary, and integrative
medicine (TCIM) are evidence-informed, safe, effective, cost-efficient, and subject to
regulatory oversight, and shall protect the public from unsafe, fraudulent, or
unverified health practices and products.
      The State shall likewise protect and uphold the right of indigenous peoples
over their knowledge of traditional medicine consistent with existing laws on
intellectual property and indigenous peoples’ rights.
      SEC. 4. This Act shall have the following objectives:
      (a) To encourage scientific research, development, and clinical validation of
traditional and complementary health care modalities;
      (b) To establish licensing, accreditation, and regulatory systems for
traditional and complementary medicine practitioners, institutions, and products;
      (c) To promote the integration of validated traditional and complementary
health care modalities into the primary health care system and universal health
care framework;
      (d) To establish standards on safety, quality, labeling, advertising, and
promotion to ensure consumer protection; and
      (e) To strengthen inter-agency coordination in the regulation and promotion
of traditional and complementary medicine modalities.

       SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against available appropriations and
savings of the PITAHC, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and auditing rules
and regulations. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA).
       SEC. 14. Monitoring and Reporting. – The TRACIMA shall submit to the
President of the Philippines and to Congress an annual report detailing the
implementation of this Act, not later than March 31 of the following year, including
but not limited to:
       (a)    The number of registered and licensed practitioners and entities;
       (b)   The status of regulatory and licensing activities;
       (c)   Programs and initiatives undertaken to promote safe and effective
       integrative health care;
       (d)   Enforcement and compliance activities; and
       (e)    Recommendations for policy or legislative action.
       SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. – (a) The PITAHC Director General and
Deputy Director General(s) shall serve as the TRACIMA Director General and
Deputy Director General(s), respectively. The incumbent officials and employees
affected by the reorganization shall be transferred, as far as practicable, to the
appropriate unit in TRACIMA as determined by the Director General. There shall be
no demotion in ranks and positions and no diminution in salaries, benefits
allowances, and emoluments of all PITAHC personnel transferred to TRACIMA. All
positions, powers, functions and duties together with the facilities, equipment,
supplies, records, files, appropriations, and funds of the PITAHC shall be
transferred to the TRACIMA.
       (b) Existing practitioners and entities practicing TCIM shall be given a period
of two (2) years to comply with the requirements of this Act.
       SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Board shall, in consultation with other agencies
and stakeholders, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.
       SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts of this Act which are
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
       SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, letters of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof,
which are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
       SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
