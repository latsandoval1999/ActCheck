                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE


Early childhood is the most critical stage of human development. Scientific research consistently
shows that the first five (5) years of life represent a period of rapid brain growth, during which
early experiences shape cognitive ability, emotional regulation, language acquisition, and social
functioning. When developmental delays or disabilities are identified and addressed early,
children are far more likely to achieve improved educational outcomes, stronger adaptive skills,
and greater long-term productivity. Conversely, delayed identification often results in more
complex interventions later in life and imposes higher social, educational, and economic costs.

Despite this evidence, developmental screening in the Philippines remains inconsistent and
fragmented. Many children are only assessed when they begin formal schooling, at which point
developmental challenges may already be pronounced. Access to specialists is also severely
limited. There are only a small number of developmental pediatricians serving millions of Filipino
children, and most of them are concentrated in major urban centers. Families in rural,
geographically isolated, and disadvantaged communities frequently face long waiting times, high
costs of therapy services, and significant barriers to diagnosis and intervention.

Moreover, after developmental concerns are identified, services are often dispersed across
multiple agencies—including the Department of Health, the Department of Education, the
Department of Social Welfare and Development, the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), and local government units—without a fully integrated national pathway linking
screening, diagnosis, therapy, and educational support. This fragmentation results in missed
opportunities for early intervention and limits the effectiveness of existing public programs.

Local initiatives have already demonstrated that integrated early detection and intervention
systems are both feasible and effective. Some local government units have implemented
community-based developmental screening and referral programs that connect families to health,
therapy, and educational services. These initiatives show that when developmental risks are
identified early and families receive timely support, outcomes improve significantly. The
challenge is not the lack of workable models but the absence of a coherent national framework
that can standardize and scale these efforts.

This bill seeks to address these systemic gaps by establishing a National Early Detection and
Intervention Framework that institutionalizes routine developmental screening during early
childhood and creates a structured referral pathway for children with developmental concerns.
The measure integrates health, education, and social protection systems to ensure that children
identified through screening are efficiently linked to diagnosis, intervention services, and
inclusive education support.
The bill further promotes equitable access to developmental services by strengthening inter-
agency coordination, expanding telehealth support for underserved areas, and encouraging the
development of a broader workforce of specialists and allied professionals. It also directs
PhilHealth to expand coverage for developmental screening and early intervention therapies,
while ensuring that indigent families receive appropriate financial support through existing social
protection mechanisms.

By institutionalizing early detection and strengthening the continuum of care from screening to
intervention and school readiness, this measure transforms fragmented efforts into a unified
national system. Ultimately, investing in early childhood development is not merely a health
intervention—it is a strategic investment in human capital, educational equity, and the long-term
productivity of the Filipino people.



In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                   AN ACT
         INSTITUTING A NATIONAL EARLY DETECTION AND INTERVENTION
         FRAMEWORK FOR CHILDREN WITH DEVELOPMENTAL DELAYS AND
          DISABILITIES, ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL REFERRAL PATHWAY,
      STRENGTHENING INTER-AGENCY COORDINATION, EXPANDING EQUITABLE
        ACCESS TO DEVELOPMENTAL SERVICES, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                                  THEREFOR


     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Early Detection
 2   and Intervention Act.”

 3          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
 4   and promote the right of every child to optimal development from birth, recognizing early
 5   childhood, particularly from zero (0) to five (5) years of age, as a critical period for neurological,
 6   cognitive, social, and emotional growth. The State shall ensure equitable access to developmental
 7   services nationwide, with proportionately greater support extended to indigent and vulnerable
 8   families. In this respect, it shall strengthen coordination among the Department of Health (DOH),
 9   Department of Education (DepEd), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
10   Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), the Department of the Interior and Local
11   Government (DILG), and local government units (LGUs).
12
13          Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize universal, standardized, and routine
14   developmental screening within early childhood health services and establish an integrated and
15   coordinated system for developmental screening, diagnostic evaluation, early intervention, and
16   school readiness support. It shall likewise promote the expansion of national workforce capacity
17   necessary to deliver timely, accessible, and effective developmental services and establish a
18   National Developmental Health Workforce Expansion Program to increase the number and
19   equitable distribution of developmental pediatricians and allied developmental specialists
20   throughout the country.
21
22      Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
23
24      (a) “Child” refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age, with priority focus on
25          children aged zero (0) to five (5) years for purposes of early detection and intervention.
26
27      (b) “Developmental Screening” refers to the administration of standardized and validated
28          tools at defined developmental milestones to identify children at risk of developmental
29          delay or disability.
30
31      (c) “Early Intervention Services” refer to evidence-based therapeutic, educational, behavioral,
32          and family-centered services designed to address identified developmental delays or
33          disabilities.
 1
 2      (d) “National Early Detection and Intervention Framework” provides for a comprehensive,
 3          integrated, inter-agency, multi-sectoral, and community-based approach to detect and
 4          address developmental concerns of children. It ensures continuity of care from screening
 5          to diagnosis, intervention, and school readiness support.
 6
 7      (e) “National Early Detection and Intervention Plan” refers to the document to be formulated
 8          by the Inter-Agency Council on Early Detection and Intervention and implemented by
 9          various stakeholders, that sets out goals and specific objectives for the early detection of
10          developmental concerns of children, referral pathway and intervention measures to
11          address the same. It shall contain the delivery of services for developmental problems,
12          beginning from community-based developmental screening and progressing, when
13          necessary, to specialized diagnostic evaluation, therapeutic intervention, and educational
14          support.
15
16      (f) “National Referral Pathway System” refers to the integrated and coordinated national
17          system that guides the identification, assessment and referral of children with suspected
18          or confirmed developmental delays or disabilities.
19
20      (g) “Regional Developmental Hub” refers to a designated provincial or regional facility
21          equipped with multidisciplinary capacity to conduct secondary assessments and
22          coordinate intervention services for certain cases.
23
24      (h) “Telehealth” refers to the use of telecommunications and information technology to
25          provide access to health assessment, diagnosis, intervention, consultation, supervision,
26          and information across distance.
27
28          Sec. 4. Institutionalization of Universal Developmental Screening. – Developmental
29   screening is hereby institutionalized as a routine component of primary child health services
30   nationwide. The DOH shall adopt standardized and validated developmental screening tools and
31   develop a National Special Needs Screening and Assessment Toolkit. It shall likewise integrate
32   developmental screening into existing child health programs and establish national protocols for
33   documentation, referral, and follow-up.

34           The DOH, in coordination with LGUs, shall designate accredited Developmental
35   Screening Centers in every municipality or city. These centers may include Rural Health Units,
36   selected Barangay Health Stations, government hospitals, or other accredited facilities capable of
37   administering standardized screening tools and maintaining referral linkages.

38          Training and capacity-building for primary health personnel including physicians, nurses,
39   midwives, and barangay health workers shall be undertaken by the DOH in order to ensure that
40   they are properly equipped to perform their assigned duties and responsibilities. Structured
41   multiplier training programs shall likewise be conducted for Early Childhood Care and
42   Development (ECCD) teachers, Child Development Center teachers, daycare workers, Special
43   Education teachers, and other community-based service providers to enable them to administer
44   preliminary screening tools and identify developmental concerns.

45           Developmental screening shall not by itself constitute a medical diagnosis but shall serve
46   as an early identification mechanism for referral to appropriate services.

47           Sec. 5. National Early Detection and Referral Pathway System. – There is hereby
48   established a National Referral Pathway System (NRPS) for developmental concerns. It shall
49   ensure continuity of care from screening to diagnosis, intervention, and school readiness support.
50
51          Task-sharing, inter-facility coordination, resource pooling, telehealth utilization, and
52   equitable geographic access shall be the guiding principles of the NRP. LGU-based therapy
53   centers or accredited service providers capable of delivering speech therapy, occupational
54   therapy, behavioral therapy, psychological services, and other evidence-based interventions shall
55   be included in the NRPS. Pooling of personnel and resources may be undertaken through
 1   memoranda of agreement, service contracting, cross-appointment arrangements, or other lawful
 2   mechanisms consistent with civil service and procurement laws.
 3
 4          The DepEd, in coordination with the DOH, shall establish standardized medical-
 5   educational evaluation protocols prior to placement in Special Education (SPED) programs,
 6   ensuring that educational classification is supported by appropriate developmental assessment.
 7
 8          Sec. 6. Telehealth and Digital Systems Integration. – The DOH, in coordination
 9   with relevant agencies, shall develop and institutionalize telehealth systems to support
10   consultation, supervision, and service delivery in underserved areas. Subject to applicable data
11   privacy and confidentiality laws, child health records related to developmental screening and
12   intervention shall be digitally integrated and aligned, where feasible, with school readiness
13   indicators under the Department of Education.

14         Sec. 7. Financing and Equity Mechanisms. – The Philippine Health Insurance
15   Corporation (PhilHealth) shall expand its benefit packages to include coverage for:

16           a.   Developmental screening;
17           b.   Diagnostic developmental assessments;
18           c.   Speech therapy;
19           d.   Occupational therapy;
20           e.   Behavioral therapy;
21           f.   Physical rehabilitation services;
22           g.   Psychological services and developmental evaluation;
23           h.   Assistive technologies; and
24           i.   Periodic developmental reassessment.

25           The framework established under this Act shall apply universally to all children, without
26   discrimination, while ensuring equity-based allocation of public subsidies. Indigent families, as
27   determined under existing national targeting systems and social protection mechanisms
28   administered by the Department of Social Welfare and Development, shall be entitled to full
29   subsidy for approved early intervention services; transportation or ancillary support, where
30   necessary; and linkage with appropriate conditional support programs to promote consistent
31   participation in intervention plans.

32          The DSWD may provide targeted assistance for indigent families with certified children
33   with developmental disabilities undergoing intervention.

36   Intervention (IAC-EDI). The Council shall be chaired by the Secretary of the DOH.

16          Sec. 10. Workforce Development and Capacity Building. – The Department of
17   Health, in coordination with the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Department of
18   Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall develop and
19   implement programs to expand the national workforce for developmental services.

20          These programs shall include, among others, scholarship grants and training incentives;

25   create permanent plantilla positions for developmental pediatricians, therapists, psychologists,
26   and allied professionals in regional developmental hubs, DOH hospitals, and accredited LGU-
27   based developmental service centers.
