                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Article II, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution provides for the State’s commitment
to promoting full employment and a rising standard of living. This is further affirmed by
Article XIII, Section 3 which declares the State’s mandate to promote full employment
and equality of employment of all. Additionally, Article XIV, Section 2(1) provides that,
“[t]he State shall [e]stablish, maintain, and support a complete, adequate, and
integrated system of education relevant to the needs of the people and society.”

        In recent years, the State has taken significant steps towards ensuring equal
access to quality education at all levels, including tertiary education, through the
enactment of landmark legislation such as Republic Act No. 10931, or the “Universal
Access to Quality Tertiary Education (UAQTE) Act,” and the continuous filings of, and
deliberations on amendatory measures to strengthen existing laws. However, despite
these efforts, unemployment and underemployment remain persistent problems facing
Filipino graduates after completing their senior high school or college education.

       According to CHED Chairperson Shirley Agrupis, the June 2025 Labor Force
Survey by the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) showed a 2.6% increase
in the number of unemployed college graduates from 35.6% in December 2024.1

Additionally, “even when job opportunities exist, there is a persistent mismatch
between available jobs and graduates’ skills.”2

        In the January 2025 nationwide job fairs held by DOLE, only 3,364 out of 25,876
job seekers were hired and the majority were placed in “low-skilled roles that do not
require a college degree.”3 Based on a recent study, an estimated 40% of Filipinos
face educational-employment mismatches, where over 1 out of 3 Filipinos have an
educational attainment way beyond their job requirements.4 This job mismatch
problem underscores allocative inefficiency, given the greater public investment in
education, and more importantly, the “wage penalties” that millions of Filipino workers
suffer instead of reaping the full benefits that tertiary education promised.

       This bill seeks to establish Job Placement Offices (JPOs) in all public senior
high schools, state universities and colleges (SUCs), and local universities and
colleges (LUCs) to provide greater access for students transitioning from education to
employment. A Tripartite National Industry-Academe Council (NIAC) shall be created
to strengthen collaboration and coordination between the government, education, and
industry sectors. Local Industry-Academe Councils (LIACs) shall be institutionalized
in every province and highly urbanized city to guide local institutions and the JPOs.
The Philippine Labor Market Information System (PhilLMIS) shall be established within
the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) to harmonize labor market data
and guide policy. Lastly, the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) shall be
reinforced as the national reference for classifying qualifications and formalizes the
Philippine Skills Framework (PSF) as the tool for defining job-specific competencies.

       In strengthening the linkages between the government, education, and industry
sectors, providing greater access to career guidance for students transitioning from
education to employment, and establishing a system of harmonizing labor market
data, qualifications, and competencies, this bill does not merely intend to create new
instrumentalities, but to establish the steps in bridging the current education-
employment gap. This is essential for the Filipino people, especially the youth, to attain
an educational system where every Filipino has an opportunity to fully use their skills
to maximize their income and professional potential.

Aquino’s and Pangilinan’s bills.

 4        SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “School-to-
 5   Employment Program (STEP) Act.”

 7           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote full,
 8   productive, and decent employment for all Filipinos, especially the youth. The State
 9   shall strengthen institutional mechanisms that align education and training with labor
10   market needs, address jobs-skills mismatch, and facilitate smooth school-to-work
11   transitions through multi-sectoral collaboration among the government, industry, and
12   the academe.

36             (e) Coordinate certification and assessment for trainees and graduates

38             (f) Conduct labor market information dissemination and career coaching to
39                 guide students and reduce job search costs;

24          SEC. 19. Establishment of the Philippine Labor Market Information System
25   (PhilLMIS). – There is hereby established a comprehensive and integrated Philippine
26   Labor Market Information System (PhilLMIS) to be housed within the DOLE in close

30           SEC. 20. Objectives. – PhilLMIS shall serve as the central labor market
31   intelligence platform of the government and shall have the following objectives:
32              a) Collect, analyze, and disseminate real-time and forward-looking labor
33                  market data, including supply-demand trends, skills gaps, wage levels,

35              b) Identify critical skills shortages and surpluses across sectors and
36                  geographic areas;
37              c) Provide actionable insights for curriculum design, program offerings, and
38                  training alignment in education institutions and training centers;
39              d) Support career guidance and job matching services for jobseekers,
40                  including students and graduates;
41              e) Inform government workforce development policies, education planning,
42                  and investment promotion strategies;
43              f) Empower employers and enterprises with reliable labor data to support
44                  hiring, reskilling, and training decisions.

12                                        TITLE VII
13            NATIONAL FRAMEWORKS FOR SKILLS AND QUALIFICATIONS
14
15          SEC. 26. Reinforcement and Promotion of the Philippine Qualifications
16   Framework (PQF). – The Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF), as established
17   by Executive Order No. 83, s. 2012, is hereby reinforced as the national system for
18   classifying qualifications according to defined levels based on knowledge, skills, and
19   values.
20              a) All educational and training institutions, including those supervised by
21                 the DepEd, CHED, and TESDA, shall ensure that their programs and
22                 curricula are aligned with the relevant PQF levels and descriptors, and
23                 that learning outcomes are clearly defined in accordance with these
24                 standards.
25              b) The PQF National Coordinating Council (PQF-NCC) shall be
26                 strengthened with enhanced mandates and resources to actively
27                 promote, monitor, and ensure consistent implementation of the PQF
28                 across all sectors and institutions.
29              c) Mechanisms for the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) and recognition
30                 of non-formal and informal learning shall be standardized and
31                 streamlined across all PQF levels, allowing individuals to earn formal
32                 qualifications based on verified skills, competencies, and experience.
33
34           SEC. 27. Establishment and Institutionalization of the Philippine Skills
35   Framework (PSF). – The Philippine Skills Framework (PSF) is hereby established and
36   institutionalized as a complementary national reference tool outlining the specific
37   technical, transversal, and behavioral competencies required across various
38   industries and occupations.
39
40             a) The PSF shall be developed, maintained, and regularly updated by
41                TESDA, in close collaboration with DOLE, DTI, CHED, DepEd, industry
42                associations, and professional regulatory boards.
43             b) The PSF shall serve the following purposes:


                                              10
 1                   i.    Curriculum Development – Guide the design and revision of
 2                         curricula and training programs in DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and
 3                         other training institutions based on industry needs;
 4                  ii.    Skills Assessment and Certification – Provide the basis for
 5                         competency-based assessment, credentialing, and national
 6                         certifications;
 7                  iii.   Career Guidance – Inform students and jobseekers about
 8                         qualifications and competencies required for various career
 9                         pathways;
10                 iv.     Job Matching – Improve the accuracy of employment matching
11                         by linking job roles with verified skills;
12                  v.     Talent Development – Enable employers to identify workforce
13                         skill gaps and design appropriate training programs;
14                 vi.     International Benchmarking – Facilitate the alignment and mutual
15                         recognition of skills and qualifications with international partners.
16
17           SEC. 28. Digital Integration and Interoperability. – The PQF and PSF shall be
18   digitally integrated to allow for seamless navigation between qualifications and their
19   corresponding skills sets. A centralized digital platform shall be developed and
20   maintained to:
21               a) Host up-to-date information on the PQF and PSF;
22               b) Provide access to training providers, certified assessment centers, and
23                  competency standards; and
24               c) Serve as a registry of certified individuals and qualifications.
25
26         This platform shall be interoperable with the Philippine Labor Market
27   Information System (PhilLMIS) to support real-time skills tracking and planning.
28
29         SEC. 29. Industry-Led Development of the PSF. – Industry Sector Skills
30   Councils (SSCs) shall be formally established under TESDA and empowered to lead
31   the development and periodic updating of the PSF for their respective sectors. The
32   development of the PSF shall be evidence-based and data-driven, utilizing labor
33   market intelligence from PhilLMIS, national employment projections, and consultations
34   with employers, workers, educators, and other stakeholders.
35
36          SEC. 30. Promotion and Awareness. – A sustained national campaign shall be
37   launched to promote widespread awareness and adoption of the PQF and PSF among
38   learners, educators, guidance counselors, employers, government agencies, and the
39   general public.
40
41          DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and DOLE shall develop coordinated strategies for

 1          SEC. 31. International Recognition of Philippine Qualifications and Skills. – The
 2   Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), in coordination with DOLE, CHED, and TESDA,
 3   shall pursue bilateral and multilateral agreements to support the international
 4   recognition of Philippine qualifications and skills frameworks, particularly in countries
 5   with significant overseas Filipino worker (OFW) populations.
 6
 7          These agreements shall be designed to:
 8             a) Strengthen labor mobility and protect Filipino workers abroad;
 9             b) Ensure mutual recognition of skills and certifications;
10             c) Align local qualifications with international benchmarks and standards.

13                                    FINAL PROVISIONS
14
15          SEC. 32. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement this Act shall
16   be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Agencies and institutions may
17   also mobilize public-private partnerships and donor support to enhance JPO, NIAC
18   and LIAC functions.
19
20          SEC. 33. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
21   the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with CHED, DOLE, TESDA, DTI,
22   and DILG, shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations (IRR).
23
24          SEC. 34. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is declared
25   invalid or unconstitutional, the sections or provisions not affected thereby shall
26   continue to be in full force and effect.
27
28         SEC. 35. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive and
29   administrative orders, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts thereof
30   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
31   accordingly.
32
33          SEC. 36. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
34   publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation in the
35   Philippines.
