                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy include a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth, a sustained increase in the goods and services
produced by the nation, and expanding productivity as a means of raising the
quality of life. Consistent with these objectives, Republic Act No. 8435,

Research further shows that Philippine agricultural value chains continue to face

capacity of producers to participate in higher-value activities.1

       Recent Philippine and international studies likewise demonstrate that
logistics and post-harvest infrastructure are critical to agricultural
competitiveness. Philippine value-chain research identifies weaknesses in the
linkages among production, processing, logistics, and marketing, while
commodity-level assessments emphasize the need for targeted investments based
on specific value-chain and geographic constraints.2 International evidence
similarly shows that inadequate storage, transportation, cooling, and distribution
systems contribute significantly to food losses, particularly for perishable
products, and that reducing these losses requires coordinated interventions across
the food supply chain.3
       These findings indicate that the policy challenge is not simply the lack of
agricultural infrastructure. Rather, there is a need for a systematic and evidence-
based mechanism to determine which agricultural logistics projects should be
prioritized, where they should be located, which commodities and value chains
they should serve, and how they should be connected to existing investment and
project-development processes. While Republic Act No. 11966, otherwise known

general framework for PPP development, the proposed measure seeks to
complement this framework by establishing a sector-specific process for
identifying and prioritizing strategic agricultural logistics projects based on
production, post-harvest, market, value-chain, infrastructure, and project-
readiness considerations.

      This bill establishes a Priority Agricultural Logistics Project Framework
under the Department of Agriculture and provides for a rolling Priority
Agricultural Logistics Project List that will serve as a sectoral input to
government investment programs and the existing PPP processes of appropriate
implementing agencies. The measure does not create a separate PPP system,
approval process, or project-development fund, but instead strengthens
coordination among existing institutions and directs attention toward projects
with the greatest potential to reduce post-harvest losses, improve market
connectivity, lower logistics costs, strengthen agricultural value chains, and
enhance the competitiveness and welfare of farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and
agricultural enterprises.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. ± It is the policy of the State to promote an
efficient, integrated, resilient, and market-responsive agricultural logistics system
that reduces post-harvest losses, improves the movement and handling of
agricultural and fishery products, strengthens agricultural value chains, and
expands access to domestic and international markets.

      To achieve this policy, the State shall establish a sector-specific framework
for the systematic identification and prioritization of strategic agricultural
logistics projects and their integration into appropriate government investment
and public-private partnership (PPP) processes.

      The State shall ensure that priority agricultural logistics projects respond
to significant agricultural production, post-harvest, logistics, and market-access
constraints and are consistent with national, regional, local, and sectoral
development and investment plans.

      SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. ± This Act shall apply to agricultural
logistics infrastructure and development projects identified and prioritized under
this Act that may be implemented through a PPP or another appropriate
government financing or procurement modality.

      Covered projects may include:

      (a) aggregation and consolidation facilities;

      (b) warehouses and post-harvest facilities;

      (c) cold-chain facilities and systems;

      (d) processing and value-adding facilities;

      (e) agricultural and fishery markets, trading posts, and distribution
      facilities;

      (f) food hubs and integrated agricultural logistics facilities;

      (g) public fish ports and related storage and processing facilities;
                                                                                  2
      (h) agrilogistics systems; and

      (i) other infrastructure and development projects that directly support the
      efficient movement, handling, storage, processing, distribution, or
      marketing of agricultural and fishery products.

      The identification or prioritization of a project under this Act shall not
constitute project approval, authority to undertake a PPP, or authority to
commence procurement.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. ± As used in this Act:

      (a) Agricultural Logistics Project refers to an infrastructure or
      development project that facilitates the aggregation, handling, storage,
      processing, distribution, transport, trading, or marketing of agricultural and
      fishery products and inputs;

      (b) Priority Agricultural Logistics Project refers to an Agricultural
      Logistics Project identified and prioritized by the Department of
      Agriculture (DA) pursuant to this Act;

      (c) Priority Agricultural Logistics Project List refers to the rolling list of
      Priority Agricultural Logistics Projects established and maintained by the
      DA pursuant to this Act;

      (d) Implementing Agency refers to an entity authorized to undertake a PPP
      Project under Republic Act No. 11966; and

      (e) Project Development and Monitoring Facility refers to the facility
      administered by the Public-Private Partnership Center (PPP Center) for
      project development and related advisory and support services for PPP
      Projects.

                                                                                  3
      SEC. 5. Priority Agricultural Logistics Project Framework. ± The DA
shall establish and maintain a Priority Agricultural Logistics Project Framework
for the identification, assessment, prioritization, and endorsement of strategic
Agricultural Logistics Projects.

      The Framework shall:

      (a) establish sector-specific criteria for determining agricultural logistics
      priorities;

      (b) identify agricultural production areas and value chains with significant
      logistics constraints;

      (c) establish and maintain a rolling Priority List;

      (d) provide a process for endorsing Priority Agricultural Logistics Projects
      to the appropriate Implementing Agencies and the PPP Center for further
      development; and

      (e) provide for coordination with national, regional, local, and sectoral
      development and investment planning processes.

      The DA shall periodically update the Priority List based on agricultural
production, market conditions, logistics requirements, project readiness,
government priorities, and other relevant circumstances.

      The Priority List shall serve as a sectoral input in the preparation and
updating of the appropriate investment programs and Lists of PPP Projects of
concerned Implementing Agencies under Republic Act No. 11966 and its
implementing rules and regulations.

      SEC. 6. Criteria for Prioritization. ± In identifying and prioritizing
Agricultural Logistics Projects, the DA shall consider the following:
                                                                                 4
(a) Agricultural production and commodity importance, including
production volume, economic value, strategic importance, and geographic
concentration;

(b) Post-harvest losses and logistics constraints, including deficiencies in
aggregation, storage, cold-chain facilities, processing, transportation,
distribution, and market access;

(c) Value-chain connectivity, including the capacity of the project to link
production areas with aggregation, storage, processing, distribution, and
domestic or international markets;

(d) Benefits to farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and agricultural
enterprises, including improved market access, reduced logistics costs,
improved marketing opportunities, and reduced post-harvest losses;

(e) Market demand and commercial potential, including the potential for
sustainable private-sector participation;

(f) Geographic and infrastructure connectivity, including linkages with
production areas, transportation networks, ports, markets, food terminals,
and other relevant infrastructure;

(g) Project readiness, including site availability, institutional arrangements,
technical conditions, and other factors affecting project development;

(h) Economic, financial, and social benefits, including the project's
potential contribution to regional development and food-system resilience;

(i) Climate resilience and environmental sustainability, consistent with
applicable laws, rules, and regulations; and



                                                                             5
      (j) Consistency with national, regional, local, and sectoral development and
      investment plans.

      In applying the foregoing criteria, the DA shall give due consideration to
projects that address critical logistics gaps affecting an agricultural value chain,
production area, or group of strategically important commodities.

      SEC. 7. Identification and Submission of Projects. ± A national
government agency, government-owned or controlled corporation, local
government unit (LGU), cooperative, farmers' or fisherfolk's organization,
private-sector entity, or other relevant stakeholder may submit an Agricultural
Logistics Project to the DA for consideration under the Framework.

      The DA shall establish procedures for the submission, screening,
assessment, and prioritization of proposed projects.

      In assessing a proposed project, the DA shall determine whether the
      project:

      (a) addresses a documented agricultural logistics constraint;

      (b) satisfies the prioritization criteria under Section 6; and

      (c) is consistent with applicable development and investment plans.

      The DA shall coordinate with the concerned Implementing Agency when
a proposed project may be developed through a PPP or another appropriate
financing or procurement modality.

      SEC. 8. Endorsement for Project Development. ± A Priority Agricultural
Logistics Project that the DA determines to be suitable for further PPP
development may be endorsed to the appropriate Implementing Agency and the
PPP Center.
                                                                                  6
      The endorsement shall contain, to the extent available:

      (a) the project description and location;

      (b) the agricultural commodities and value chains to be served;

      (c) the logistics problem or development objective to be addressed;

      (d) the basis for the project's prioritization;

      (e) preliminary information on project cost, demand, and expected
      benefits; and

      (f) other information necessary for project development.

      The endorsement under this section shall not alter the authority of the
Implementing Agency, the PPP Center, or the appropriate approving body under
Republic Act No. 11966.

      SEC. 9. Integration into Government Investment and PPP Processes. ±
Priority Agricultural Logistics Projects shall be considered for inclusion in the
appropriate government investment programs and Lists of PPP Projects of
concerned Implementing Agencies, subject to the requirements of applicable
laws, rules, and regulations.

      A Priority Agricultural Logistics Project proposed for PPP implementation
shall be developed, assessed, approved, procured, awarded, and implemented in
accordance with Republic Act No. 11966 and its implementing rules and
regulations.

      The PPP Center may provide technical assistance for project development,
including feasibility studies, project structuring, financial and economic analysis,


                                                                                  7
risk assessment, value-for-money analysis, market sounding, transaction advisory
services, and other activities authorized under Republic Act No. 11966.

      Eligible projects may be considered for assistance under the PDMF,
subject to applicable rules and the availability of resources.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as creating a separate PPP approval,
procurement, or project-development system.

      SEC. 10. Coordination with National and Subnational Planning. ± The
DA shall coordinate with the Department of Economy, Planning and
Development (DEPDev), the PPP Center, regional development councils,
concerned LGUs, and other relevant government agencies to facilitate the
integration of Priority Agricultural Logistics Projects into applicable
development and investment planning processes.

      The DA shall provide relevant agricultural and logistics information
necessary for the assessment and prioritization of projects under national and
subnational development and investment plans.

      Nothing in this section shall modify the planning, investment
programming, project appraisal, or oversight functions assigned by law to
DEPDev, the Economy and Development Council, regional development
councils, LGUs, or other government agencies.

      SEC. 11. Participation of Local Government Units. ± The DA shall
coordinate with concerned LGUs in identifying and prioritizing Agricultural
Logistics Projects within their respective jurisdictions.

      An LGU may submit a project for consideration under this Act and may

      SEC. 12. Monitoring, Information, and Transparency. ± The DA shall
maintain and periodically update the Priority List.

      The DA shall make available to the public, through its official website or
other appropriate means, non-confidential information concerning Priority
Agricultural Logistics Projects, including:

      (a) the project name and location;

      (b) the agricultural commodities or value chains to be served;

      (c) the principal logistics constraints addressed;

      (d) the basis for prioritization;

      (e) the estimated project cost, when available;

      (f) the status of project development; and

      (g) other information that may be prescribed in the implementing rules and
      regulations.

      The disclosure of project studies, PPP contracts, and other project
documents shall remain subject to Republic Act No. 11966 and laws governing
confidential, proprietary, personal, and other protected information.

      The DA shall periodically evaluate the implementation of the Framework

      SEC. 13. Administrative Support and Funding. ± The DA and other
concerned government agencies shall implement this Act using their existing
organizational structures, personnel, programs, and appropriations, subject to
existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, procurement, and other applicable laws,
rules, and regulations.

      No separate department, bureau, office, or government corporation shall
be created by virtue of this Act.

      The amounts necessary for the implementation of this Act shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act, subject to the usual budgetary
requirements and procedures.

      SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. ± Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Agriculture, in coordination with
the PPP Center and DEPDev, and in consultation with concerned government
agencies, LGUs, farmers' and fisherfolk's organizations, cooperatives, private-
sector representatives, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary to implement this Act.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall provide for:

      (a) procedures for submitting and assessing proposed projects;

      (b) procedures for applying the prioritization criteria under Section 6;

      (c) procedures for establishing and updating the Priority List;

      (d) procedures for endorsing projects to Implementing Agencies and the
      PPP Center;

      SEC. 15. Separability Clause. ± If any provision of this Act is declared

in full force and effect.

      SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. ± All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act
are repealed or modified accordingly.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, amend, or modify Republic
Act No. 11966, Republic Act No. 8435, Republic Act No. 7160, or any other law
governing public-private partnerships, agricultural infrastructure, national and
local development planning, local government powers, taxation, procurement, or
related matters, except to the extent expressly provided by a subsequent law.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. ± This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
