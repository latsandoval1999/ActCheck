                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Amidst a changing climate and worsening natural disasters, early warning
may sometimes spell the difference between safety, disaster, and even death. In
the Philippines’ case, where typhoons after typhoons batter communities and
send local economies to a halt, a warning system that advises early and in logical
anticipation of impending disruption may allow for proper reaction in the part of
citizens, institutions, schools, and the private sector, among others.

       Additionally, the poly-crises we are facing due to climate change and global
warming, present a complex tapestry of natural disruptions that have found our
disaster-response lacking. We are always playing catch-up. And we are still stuck
with post-disaster action rather than pre-disaster preparedness. The poly-crises
comes in multiple forms. It can be drought in one area, torrential rain in another.
It can also be haze in some parts of the country, and unhealthy temperatures in
others. What we need now, is to revamp and modernize our disaster warning
systems so that we can plan, mitigate damage and loss, ease the burden of
adaptation put on the individual, and overall, help with how the individual
Filipino deals with a changing environment.

      Ours is an archipelago that is varied, storied, rich, and diverse. All the
more reason to prepare and preserve our islands through more rigorous systems,
innovative approaches, and maximizing the institutions that already exist. It is
high time to consolidate and deliver early warning systems. It is high time we
adapt before it is too late.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

      AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE PHILIPPINE MULTI-HAZARD EARLY
 WARNING NETWORK, MANDATING A COMMON ALERTING STANDARD,
PRESCRIBING AUTOMATIC AND LOCALIZED RULES ON THE SUSPENSION
OF CLASSES AND WORK, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT
        NO. 10639, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Early Warning
for Every Filipino Act”.

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
the life and property of every person within its territory from natural and human-
induced hazards, and to uphold the right of the people to information on matters
of public concern. An early warning discharges that duty only when it reaches
the person at risk, in time, in a language that person understands, and in terms
that say what to do.

      SECTION 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

      (a) Alert refers to a warning, advisory, bulletin, signal, or other notice of
          an actual, imminent, or forecast hazard, issued by an Issuing
          Authority;
      (b) Action Ladder refers to the five-level scale of prescribed public action
          under Section 5 of this Act;
      (c) Cell Broadcast refers to the technology by which a message is
          transmitted to all mobile devices within a defined geographic area
          simultaneously, without reference to any subscriber list, mobile
          number, or other personal information, and without regard to the load
          or data balance of the recipient;
      (d) Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) refers to the open, non-proprietary,
          machine-readable data standard for emergency alerting recognized by
          the International Telecommunication Union and the World
          Meteorological Organization, as adopted for Philippine use under
          Section 5 of this Act;
      (e) Issuing Authority refers to a government agency or instrumentality
          authorized by law to issue an Alert, namely PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, the
          Mines and Geosciences Bureau (MGB), the Office of Civil Defense
          (OCD), and such other agencies as the National Disaster Risk
          Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) may designate; and,
          within their respective jurisdictions and as to local hazards, local chief
          executives acting through their Local Disaster Risk Reduction and
          Management Offices;
      (f) Network refers to the Philippine Multi-Hazard Early Warning Network
          under Section 4 of this Act;
      (g) Relaying Entity refers to a public telecommunications entity,
          broadcast station, cable or satellite television operator, internet service
          provider, operator of a public transport terminal, seaport, or airport,
          operator of a public assembly place, mall, or market, and such other
          entity as may be required under Section 6 of this Act to relay an Alert;
          and
      (h) Warning Suppression refers to the act of preventing, delaying, altering,
          downgrading, or withholding the issuance or delivery of an Alert,
          otherwise than on technical or scientific grounds stated in writing by
          the Issuing Authority.

       SECTION 4. The Philippine Multi-Hazard Early Warning Network. – There
is hereby established the Philippine Multi-Hazard Early Warning Network, which
shall consist of the Issuing Authorities, the Relaying Entities, the local
government units, and the institutional participants enumerated in this Section,
operating under the common obligations imposed by this Act.

      The Network shall be convened by the OCD in its existing capacity as
secretariat and implementing arm of the NDRRMC under Section 9 of Republic
Act No. 10121, and shall be composed of:

       (a) PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, MGB, and the Advanced Science and
           Technology Institute (ASTI) of the Department of Science and
           Technology (DOST), in their existing mandates under Republic Act No.
           10692, Executive Order No. 128 (s. 1987), and related law;
       (b) The OCD, the Department of the Interior and Local Government
           (DILG), the Department of Information and Communications
           Technology (DICT), the National Telecommunications Commission
           (NTC), the Department of Education (DepEd), the Department of Labor
           and Employment (DOLE), the Department of Transportation, the
           Department of Public Works and Highways, and the National Mapping
           and Resource Information Authority;
       (c) All provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays, acting through
           their Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Offices and
           Committees under Section 12 of Republic Act No. 10121; and
       (d) As institutional participants, the University of the Philippines
           Resilience Institute and its NOAH Center, state universities and
           colleges and other higher education institutions operating hazard
           monitoring or forecasting facilities, the Philippine Red Cross, and
           accredited civil society organizations, people's organizations, and
           volunteer groups engaged in community-based early warning.

      Each member of the Network shall discharge the obligations imposed by
this Act within its existing mandate, personnel complement, and appropriations.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to transfer, diminish, or supersede the
scientific and technical authority of any Issuing Authority over the Alerts it
issues.

      SECTION 5. Common Alerting Standard, the National Alert Gateway, and
the Action Ladder.

      Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, every Alert issued by an
Issuing Authority shall be issued in the Common Alerting Protocol, in a
Philippine profile to be adopted jointly by the DICT, the DOST, and the OCD in
consultation with the Issuing Authorities. The profile shall be open, non-
proprietary, and published, and shall provide for the identification of affected
areas by polygon and, at minimum, by barangay.

       There shall be a single National Alert Gateway, operated by the OCD
through its existing National Operations Center with the technical support of the
DICT, through which all Alerts shall be published and from which all Relaying
Entities shall draw. The Gateway shall authenticate the origin of every Alert,
distribute it simultaneously to all channels, operate on a twenty-four-hour basis
with redundant power, connectivity, and geographically separate failover, and
remain operable when commercial networks fail. The Gateway shall not filter,
edit, delay, or withhold an Alert issued by an Issuing Authority.

       All Alerts, and the observational data on which they are based including
the rain gauge, water level, weather station, buoy, and seismic feeds of the
DOST-ASTI, PAGASA, and PHIVOLCS networks and of local government units
shall be public data, published in near real time, without fee, in machine-
readable form, and through a documented public application programming
interface. The Issuing Authorities shall not enter into any contract that restricts
the publication of such data or conditions public access upon a proprietary
platform, and any such stipulation in an existing contract is void as contrary to
public policy.

       The Gateway shall maintain a public log recording, for every Alert, the time
of issuance, the issuing authority, the areas covered, the Action Ladder level, the
channels through which it was distributed, and the time of confirmed delivery or
the reason for non-delivery reported by each Relaying Entity. The log shall be
published within forty-eight (48) hours and shall be permanently accessible.

       Every Alert shall state, in addition to its technical designation, one of the
following levels and the action it requires in accordance with the Action Ladder
to be developed.

      Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the Issuing Authorities
shall jointly publish the correspondence between each of their technical
warnings and the Action Ladder.

       SECTION 6. Local, Community-Based, School-Based, and Indigenous Early
Warning. – Every province, city, and municipality identified by the Issuing
Authorities as high-hazard shall, within three (3) years from the effectivity of this
Act, establish and maintain a local hazard monitoring and warning capability
appropriate to its hazard profile, which may include automated rain gauges,
water level sensors, weather stations, and localized nowcasting, and which shall
be operated by the operations and warning personnel of its Local Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Office under Section 12 of Republic Act No. 10121.
Local data shall be transmitted to the Gateway in the common standard and
shall be public under Section 5 of this Act.

       The DOST, through PAGASA and ASTI, shall provide technical
specifications, calibration, and training, and shall not require the use of any
single supplier or proprietary platform. Local government units are encouraged
to adopt, and the DOST and the DILG shall promote, interoperable local warning
platforms of demonstrated effectiveness, including those developed by local
government units and by Philippine academic institutions.

       The State recognizes community-based and indigenous early warning
systems as part of the Network. Indigenous cultural communities and
indigenous peoples shall have the right to maintain their own warning practices,
and the same shall be integrated into local contingency plans with their free and
prior informed consent in accordance with Republic Act No. 8371.

      Observations reported by residents, community volunteers, Accredited
Community Disaster Volunteers, and indigenous warning keepers shall be
received by the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office,
acknowledged, and where corroborated, transmitted to the Gateway. No person
reporting an observed hazard in good faith shall be liable for error.
       The OCD, the DILG, and the DepEd shall conduct a nationwide
simultaneous drill at least twice each year, which shall include an end-to-end
test of the Gateway and of every required channel, and the results of which shall
be published, including the channels that failed.

       SECTION 7. Research, Technology Transfer, and Domestic Capability. –
The DOST, in coordination with the OCD and the Commission on Higher
Education, shall support and adopt Philippine-developed early warning
technologies, including impact-based forecasting that identifies affected
barangays and populations in advance, low-cost sensor networks, earthquake
early warning, and locally developed alerting platforms. Research funded in
whole or in part by public funds shall produce data, models, and source code
that are publicly available and licensed for use by any government agency or
local government unit without royalty.

       The DOST, the Department of Trade and Industry, and the Board of
Investments shall promote the domestic design, manufacture, calibration, and
repair of hazard monitoring instruments, and procurement under this Act shall
give preference to domestically produced instruments of equivalent specification,
consistent with Republic Act No. 12009 and the rules of the Government
Procurement Policy Board.

      Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, PHIVOLCS shall submit
to Congress a plan and costing for a national earthquake early warning system,
including the automatic actuation of alarms, the interruption of broadcasts, and
the automatic braking or slowing of rail systems upon detection.

      SECTION 8. Funding. – The obligations of the Issuing Authorities and
other national agencies under this Act shall be funded from their respective
annual appropriations, and for PAGASA, from the modernization program under
Republic Act No. 10692.

      The establishment and operation of the Gateway shall be charged to the
appropriations of the OCD and the DICT and shall thereafter be carried as a
distinct line item in the General Appropriations Act. Local early warning
capability under Section 6 of this Act is a preparedness expenditure chargeable
against the seventy percent (70%) component of the Local Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Fund under Section 21 of Republic Act No. 10121.
Nothing in this Act shall authorize a charge against the Quick Response Fund.

      There shall be a National Early Warning Subsidy, administered by the
OCD from funds appropriated for the purpose, for the acquisition, installation,
and maintenance of monitoring and warning equipment in fourth, fifth, and sixth
class provinces, cities, and municipalities under Republic Act No. 11964, and in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas. Releases shall be published
quarterly by local government unit and by purpose.
       The costs of cell broadcast capability and of alert transmission shall be
borne by the public telecommunications entities concerned as a condition of
their franchises and shall not be passed on to consumers.

      SECTION 9. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. – The following acts are
prohibited:

      (a) Issuing, transmitting, or causing the transmission of a false Alert, or of
          a simulated Alert not identified as a drill;
      (b) Warning Suppression by any public officer or by any officer or employee
          of a Relaying Entity;
      (c) Failing or refusing, without justifiable cause, to relay an Alert
          designated Level 3 or higher, or failing to establish the cell broadcast
          capability required under Section 6 of this Act within the period
          prescribed;
      (d) Using the Gateway, the cell broadcast channel, or any alerting channel
          established under this Act for any partisan political, campaign,
          promotional, or commercial purpose, or including in an Alert the name,
          image, or voice of any public official or candidate except where
          operationally indispensable;
      (e) Charging a consumer, directly or indirectly, for the receipt of an Alert;
      (f) Requiring a worker or learner to report, or penalizing a worker or
          learner for not reporting, in violation of Section 8 of this Act, or making
          a deduction prohibited by that Section; and
      (g) Entering into or enforcing any contract or stipulation restricting the
          publication of data required to be public under Section 5 of this Act.

       Any person who commits an act under paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (d), or (g)
shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not less than Five hundred
thousand pesos (P500,000.00) but not more than Five million pesos
(P5,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more
than six (6) years, or both, at the discretion of the court. Any person who commits
an act under paragraph (e) or (f) shall be punished by a fine of not less than One
hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos
(P1,000,000.00), without prejudice to the payment of the amount withheld, with
legal interest.

      No civil, criminal, or administrative liability shall attach to an Issuing
Authority, a public officer, a Relaying Entity, a local official, or a volunteer for an
Alert issued or relayed in good faith on the basis of available technical
information, notwithstanding that the forecast hazard does not occur or occurs
otherwise than as forecast.

      SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the OCD, in coordination with
the DOST, PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, the DICT, the NTC, the DILG, the DepEd, the
Commission on Higher Education, the DOLE, the National Council on Disability
Affairs, and the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples, and in consultation
with the leagues of local government units, the Relaying Entities, the academic
institutions participating in the Network, and accredited civil society and
volunteer organizations, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to
implement this Act.

       SECTION 11. Reporting. – The OCD shall submit to the President and to
both Houses of Congress, not later than the thirty-first (31st) day of March of
each year, a report stating the number of Alerts issued and the confirmed
delivery rate of each by channel and by region; the barangays that remain
unreached; the local government units without the capability required under
Section 7 of this Act; the results of drills; the learning days lost as reported by
the DepEd; and every instance of non-compliance, naming the entity concerned.

       SECTION 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 14. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
