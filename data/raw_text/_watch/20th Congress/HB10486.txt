                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Children today are growing up in a digital environment where social media,
online games, and other online platforms have become an integral part of their daily
lives. While these technologies offer opportunities for learning, they also expose minors
to serious risks.

       Despite this, there is currently no comprehensive Philippine law requiring
digital platforms to implement uniform age verification systems or subjecting
them to meaningful accountability for failing to protect minors. Many platforms
continue to rely solely on self-declared birth dates, allowing underage users to easily
circumvent age restrictions.

        This measure seeks to fill this gap by empowering the Movie and Television
Review and Classification Board (MTRCB) to establish age classifications and child
safety standards for digital platforms, require reasonable age verification mechanisms,
and hold platform operators and developers accountable for violations, while ensuring
full respect for the constitutional rights to privacy, due process, freedom of expression,
and access to information. It likewise preserves the primary responsibility of parents
and legal guardians in guiding and supervising their children's online activities.

       By requiring digital platforms to exercise greater responsibility in protecting
Filipino children, this bill promotes a safer digital environment where innovation and
technology can flourish without compromising the welfare and best interests of the
youth.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING SAFEGUARDS FOR THE USE OF ONLINE
GAMES, SOCIAL MEDIA, AND DIGITAL PLATFORMS BY MINORS
THROUGH     THE   MOVIE AND     TELEVISION REVIEW AND
CLASSIFICATION BOARD (MTRCB), PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR
VIOLATIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Digital Platforms
Accountability Act of 2026”.

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to:
(a) Protect children from online exploitation, harmful digital content, excessive exposure
    to addictive technologies, and other digital risks;
(b) Promote responsible innovation while ensuring that technology companies exercise
    reasonable care in protecting minors;
(c) Hold digital platform operators accountable for failures in implementing effective
    child safety mechanisms;
(d) Ensure that online services accessible within the Philippines adopt reasonable and
    effective safeguards to prevent unauthorized access by minors; and
(e) Promote responsible use of digital technology while respecting the constitutional
    rights to freedom of expression, privacy, due process, and access to information.

        Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
(a) Digital Platform refers to any online service accessible through the internet,
    including social media platforms, online games, gaming services, live-streaming
    platforms, virtual worlds, messaging applications with public interactive features,
    and similar digital services.
(b) Platform Operator refers to any person or entity that owns, develops, controls,
    manages, or operates a Digital Platform.
(c) Developer refers to any natural or juridical person responsible for creating or
    publishing a Digital Platform.
(d) Minor refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age.
(e) Age Verification refers to reasonable and reliable methods of determining whether
    a user satisfies the minimum age prescribed under this Act.
(f) Philippine User refers to any user physically located within Philippine territory.

       Section 4. Additional Powers and Functions of the MTRCB. – In addition to
its powers under existing laws, the Movie and Television Review and Classification
Board (MTRCB) shall have the following powers and functions for purposes of this
Act:

    (a) Determine the minimum age appropriate for access to various categories of
        Digital Platforms;
    (b) Classify online games, social media platforms, and other Digital Platforms
        according to age appropriateness and risk level;
    (c) Prescribe mandatory child safety standards applicable to Digital Platforms;
    (d) Establish standards and guidelines for age verification and age assurance
        systems;
    (e) Conduct compliance audits and investigations of Platform Operators and
        Developers;
    (f) Issue compliance certifications and age classifications required under this Act;
    (g) Order corrective measures and impose administrative sanctions for violations of
        this Act;
    (h) Receive, investigate, and resolve complaints involving violations of this Act;
    (i) Coordinate with the Department of Information and Communications
        Technology (DICT), Department of Justice (DOJ), Department of Social
        Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Education (DepEd),
        Department of Health (DOH), National Privacy Commission (NPC), National
        Youth Commission (NYC), and other relevant government agencies in the
        implementation of this Act;
    (j) Coordinate with foreign regulators and international organizations for the
        enforcement of this Act against foreign Platform Operators and Developers; and
    (k) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary to effectively implement the
        provisions of this Act.

       Section 5. Mandatory Age Classification. – Every Digital Platform accessible
within the Philippines shall secure an Age Classification and Compliance Certification
from the MTRCB before offering its services to Philippine Users.

          The classification shall indicate:
          • minimum age;
          • parental supervision requirements;
          • content advisories;
          • risk level;
          • interactive features;
         •   gambling-like mechanics;
         •   monetization systems.

      Section 6. Platform Obligations. – Every Platform Operator shall:
   (a) implement effective age verification systems;
   (b) prevent creation of accounts by underaged users;
   (c) disable accounts found to belong to underaged users;
   (d) implement parental controls;
   (e) maintain audit logs;
   (f) promptly investigate and resolve reports of underage accounts; and
   (g) submit annual compliance reports to the MTRCB.

      Section 7. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any Platform Operator or
Developer to:
   (a) knowingly permit minors below the prescribed age to create or maintain
       accounts;
   (b) rely solely on self-declared birth dates where more reliable age assurance
       methods are reasonably available;
   (c) intentionally design systems to circumvent age restrictions;
   (d) fail to remove underage accounts after notice;
   (e) fail to verify and resolve a report within fourteen (14) days; and
   (f) advertise prohibited platforms to minors.

      Section 8. Foreign Digital Platforms. – Any foreign Platform Operator or
Developer offering services to Philippine users shall:
   (a) designate and maintain an authorized Philippine representative empowered to
       receive legal processes, notices, subpoenas, and administrative orders;
   (b) register with the MTRCB before making its services available in the Philippines;
   (c) submit to Philippine administrative jurisdiction with respect to matters arising
       under this Act;
   (d) comply with lawful orders issued by the MTRCB and Philippine courts.
   (a) Failure to designate a Philippine representative shall constitute grounds for
       enforcement measures under this Act.

        Section 9. Enforcement Against Foreign Entities. – Where a foreign Platform
Operator or Developer refuses to comply with this Act, the MTRCB may recommend
to the proper agencies, after due notice and hearing, one or more of the following:
   (a) suspension or removal of the application from digital application marketplaces
       operating in the Philippines;
   (b) suspension of local advertising services;
   (c) suspension of monetization through Philippine payment gateways and financial
       service providers;
   (d) blocking or restriction of access within Philippine jurisdiction, subject to
       appropriate judicial authorization when required by law;
   (e) coordination with foreign regulatory authorities under applicable treaties or
       reciprocal enforcement arrangements; and
   (f) institution of civil, criminal, or administrative proceedings against the operator,
       its local representative, or both, where authorized by law.

The foregoing remedies shall be without prejudice to other actions available under
existing laws.

       Section 10. Administrative Fines. – The MTRCB may impose administrative
fines as follows:
    First offense: ₱5,000,000
    Second offense: ₱10,000,000
    Third offense: ₱25,000,000
    Subsequent offenses: up to five percent (5%) of the platform's gross annual
    Philippine-generated revenue, or ₱100,000,000, whichever is higher.

Each underage account knowingly permitted to remain active after valid notice may be
treated as a separate violation.

       Section 11. Criminal Liability. – Any responsible officer who willfully
authorizes or directs systematic violations of this Act shall, upon conviction, be
punished by:
          (a) imprisonment of six (6) years to twelve (12) years;
          (b) a fine of not less than ₱10,000,000 but not exceeding ₱50,000,000; or
          (c) both, at the discretion of the court.

Where the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible directors, officers, or managing
employees who knowingly participated in or authorized the violation shall be held
liable.

        Section 12. Non-Diminution of Other Liabilities and Parental Responsibility.
– The liability of a Platform Operator, Developer, or any other person under this Act
shall be without prejudice to the civil, criminal, or administrative liability of any other
person under existing laws for acts or omissions arising from the same incident. Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to diminish, impair, or relieve the parental authority and
responsibility of parents or legal guardians over their minor children as provided under
the Family Code of the Philippines and other applicable laws.

      Section 13. Coordination with the National Privacy Commission. – The
MTRCB and the National Privacy Commission shall jointly formulate standards
governing age verification and age assurance systems to ensure that only the minimum
personal data necessary is collected, appropriate data privacy and security safeguards
are implemented, privacy-enhancing technologies are adopted whenever practicable,
and all processing of personal data complies with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012," and its implementing rules and regulations.

       Section 14. Citizen Complaints. – Parents, legal guardians, schools, government
agencies, or any concerned citizen may file complaints before the MTRCB regarding
violations of this Act.

       Section 15. Rule-making Authority. – Within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the MTRCB, in coordination with the DICT, DSWD,
DOJ, NPC, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary implementing
rules and regulations.

       Section 16. Appropriations.
The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against
the current appropriations of the MTRCB. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        Section 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall continue
to be in full force and effect.
       Section 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, and administrative
orders or parts thereof inconsistent with any provision of this Act are hereby repealed
or amended accordingly.
       Section 19. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
