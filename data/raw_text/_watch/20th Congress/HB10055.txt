                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Section 1, Article XIV of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides that
“The State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education
at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible
to all.” Likewise, Section 10, Article XIV declares that “Science and technology
are essential for national development and progress. The State shall give priority
to research and development, invention, innovation, and their utilization.” These
provisions recognize that education and technology are inseparable drivers of
inclusive national progress.

In line with these constitutional mandates, the government must take deliberate
measures to ensure that higher education institutions (HEIs) are equipped to
respond to the realities of a rapidly changing, technology-driven world. The
COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the vulnerabilities of Philippine higher
education, as institutions struggled to transition to online and blended learning
due to inadequate infrastructure, uneven faculty training, and persistent digital
divides. These challenges revealed that digital transformation is no longer
optional, but essential to guarantee access, resilience, and competitiveness in
higher education.

The Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II), created
under Republic Act No. 11899, has explicitly identified digital transformation
and educational technologies as one of its priority reform areas in higher
education. According to its framework, HEIs must become “digitally connected,
innovative, and technologically capable, with infrastructure for digital
transformation” to meet the needs of the Fourth Industrial Revolution (EDCOM
II Priority Areas, 2023). EDCOM II also underscored the urgency of developing
digital literacy, strengthening faculty competencies, and building equitable access
to technology to ensure that all learners benefit from digital advances (EDCOM
II Year 2 Report, 2025).

This bill, therefore, seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive framework for the
Digital Higher Education Transformation Act, which shall strengthen the digital
readiness of higher education institutions by:

   • Upgrading digital infrastructure and connectivity in SUCs, LUCs, and
     private HEIs;
   • Building capacity of faculty, administrators, and students in digital literacy,
     pedagogy, and research;
   • Establishing national standards for online and blended learning, micro-
     credentials, and digital quality assurance;
   • Ensuring equitable access to digital education for underserved and
     disadvantaged learners; and
   • Aligning higher education with the digital economy and the
     recommendations of EDCOM II to make Philippine graduates globally
     competitive.

By passing this measure will empower HEIs to serve as hubs of innovation,
inclusion, and excellence, redefining their role in society and ensuring that no
Filipino learner is left behind in the digital era.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Digital Higher
Education Transformation Act”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote accessible, inclusive, and globally competitive higher education by
strengthening the digital readiness of higher education institutions (HEIs).
Pursuant to Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution, the State shall take
appropriate steps to make education accessible to all, while fostering innovation,
digital equity, and national development.

SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act shall pursue the following Objectives:
   a. Upgrade the digital infrastructure of HEIs to support online, blended, and
      flexible learning.
   b. Build the capacity of faculty, administrators, and students in digital
      literacy, teaching, and research;
   c. Develop national standards for digital learning, quality assurance, and
      technology-enabled education;
   d. Ensure equitable access to digital higher education for students from
      underserved and disadvantaged sectors;
   e. Operationalize the digital transformation agenda identified by the Second
      Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II).

SEC. 4. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall cover all higher education
institutions, whether publicly or privately operated, encompassing state
universities and colleges (SUCs), local universities and colleges (LUCs), as well
as duly recognized private HEIs.

SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, CHED, in consultation with DICT, DBM, DEPDEV,
SUCs, and representatives of private HEIs, shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for its effective implementation.

SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders or issuances or any part
thereof, which are inconsistent herewith, are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.
