                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The power of taxation is a fundamental pillar of governance, providing the State
with the resources necessary to fulfill its constitutional duties. However, this power
must be exercised with utmost fairness, accountability, and integrity. In recent years,
concerns have grown over the abuse of discretion by some revenue officers in the
issuance of excessive, arbitrary, or unjustified tax assessments. These practices not
only harm taxpayers—particularly small businesses and individuals with limited
means—but also erode public trust in the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and the
tax system as a whole.

      The "Revenue Assessment Integrity and Accountability Act" seeks to address
this systemic issue by instituting critical safeguards and mechanisms that promote
transparency, professional diligence, and institutional accountability in the audit and
assessment process of internal revenue taxes.

        The bill mandates the submission of sworn justification reports in cases where
the final tax collected is significantly lower than the original deficiency assessment. It
establishes a presumption of abuse or negligence in cases where such discrepancies
are not properly explained, or when court rulings drastically reduce the BIR's
assessments. These presumptions carry strong administrative sanctions, including
dismissal from service, forfeiture of benefits, and civil liability to reimburse taxpayers
for litigation costs incurred due to excessive or abusive assessments.

      Recognizing the collaborative nature of BIR audit and assessment procedures,
the bill imposes solidary liability on all officials whose names appear in the routing and
approval of flawed assessments—unless they can demonstrate that they objected or
were misled.

      To promote internal reform and protect integrity whistleblowers, the bill
introduces whistleblower protections and reward mechanisms for BIR employees and
taxpayer representatives who expose systemic abuse. Furthermore, to ensure
impartiality in oversight, it establishes an Independent Revenue Assessment Review

Panel under the Civil Service Commission—not the BIR—to evaluate justification
reports and recommend sanctions.

      This proposed measure does not aim to hinder the government’s revenue
collection mandate, but to professionalize and rationalize it. By holding accountable
those who weaponize tax assessments for harassment, coercion, or personal gain,
the State affirms its commitment to a fair, credible, and corruption-free tax system.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                 AN ACT
     PENALIZING THE ABUSE OF DISCRETION BY REVENUE OFFICERS IN THE
     ISSUANCE OF UNREASONABLE TAX ASSESSMENTS, MANDATING
     JUSTIFICATION OF COMPROMISE PAYMENTS SIGNIFICANTLY LOWER THAN
     STATED DEFICIENCIES, AND PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR GROSS
     NEGLIGENCE AND PROFESSIONAL MISCONDUCT, AND FOR OTHER
     PURPOSES


          Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Revenue
 2   Assessment Integrity and Accountability Act.”
 3
 4         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to ensure fairness,
 5   accountability, and professionalism in tax administration. The government shall deter
 6   the abuse of discretion by revenue officers in the issuance of tax assessments and
 7   promote public trust in the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).
 8
 9         SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all revenue officers, examiners,
10   assessment personnel, and BIR officials involved in the audit, investigation, and
11   assessment of internal revenue taxes.
12
13           SEC. 4. Mandatory Justification of Unsettled Assessment Discrepancies.
14   – There should be a mandatory justification of unsettled assessment discrepancy by
15   a BIR official or employee in case a Notice of Discrepancy or Final Assessment Notice
16   (FAN) is issued indicating a deficiency tax liability exceeding three (3) times the actual
17   tax ultimately paid or compromised. In such case, the concerned revenue officer and
18   his/her recommending or approving officials shall submit a sworn reconciliation and
19   justification report within thirty (30) days from receipt of the final payment, explaining:
20
21          a. The basis of the original assessment,
22          b. The computations and data used,
                                             Page 3 of 5
 1          c. The legal grounds for reducing the amount, and
 2          d. The rationale for the final settlement or collection.
 3
 4           SEC. 5. Presumption of Abuse and Administrative Sanction. – If no
 5   sufficient justification is provided under Section 4, or if the justification is found to be
 6   false, misleading, or patently lacking in factual or legal basis, the revenue officer and
 7   all involved personnel in the docket routing process shall be presumed to have
 8   committed:
 9           a. Gross misconduct,
10           b. Grave abuse of authority, and/or
11           c. Conduct prejudicial to the best interest of the service.
12
13          The BIR shall automatically initiate administrative proceedings against the
14   erring official or employee, which may result in:
15          a. Dismissal from the service,
16          b. Forfeiture of retirement and other terminal benefits, and/or
17          c. Perpetual disqualification from public office.
18
19         SEC. 6. Liability for Harassment Through Excessive Assessments. – If a
20   taxpayer is forced to litigate a tax assessment and the final judgment of the Court of
21   Tax Appeals (CTA), later affirmed by the Supreme Court, reduces the assessed tax
22   by more than seventy percent (70%), the revenue officer and team involved shall be
23   presumed liable for:
24         a. Gross negligence,
25         b. Gross ignorance of the law, and/or
26         c. Lack of professional diligence.
27
28         As such, the concerned revenue officer and team shall be administratively
29   charged and, if found liable, shall:
30         a. Reimburse the taxpayer for reasonable litigation costs, including attorney’s
31            fees, court filing expenses, and other related expenses; and/or
32         b. Be subject to dismissal and forfeiture of benefits as provided in Section 5.
33
34            SEC. 7. Accountability of BIR Teams. – All officials and personnel whose
35   initials, signatures, or approvals appear in the audit docket, recommendation forms, or
36   memoranda routing the assessment for approval shall be solidarily liable under
37   Sections 5 and 6, unless they can demonstrate they:
38            a. Raised a written objection to the assessment or recommendation, or
39            b. Were misled by falsified or omitted documents.
40
41         SEC. 8. Whistleblower Protection. – Any BIR employee or taxpayer
42   representative who voluntarily discloses facts or documents showing systematic
43   abuse or unjustified assessments under this Act shall be entitled to:
44         a. Protection from administrative retaliation,
45         b. Confidentiality of identity, and
46         c. A reward not exceeding five percent (5%) of any public funds recovered or
47             refunded as a result of the disclosure.
48
49         SEC. 9. Creation of an Independent Revenue Assessment Review Panel.
50   – To ensure objectivity and avoid undue influence or internal interference, a Revenue

19          No member of the Panel may be removed or replaced during the pendency of

23          SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The BIR, in coordination
