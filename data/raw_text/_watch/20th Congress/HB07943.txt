                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The healthcare system in the Philippines is complex and fragmented, forcing
patients to seek medical and social services from different providers. This burden
often falls on the patients themselves, particularly those from marginalized groups
who may lack the resources or knowledge to navigate the system. As a result, many
suffer from medical errors, higher costs, and failure to continue treatment, which
worsen existing health inequalities.

      This bill aims to improve healthcare access by strengthening the current
regulatory framework and introducing measures to promote integrated care and
equitable services.

      Specifically, it proposes the institutionalization of the Community-Based
Health Navigator as a key figure in the referral system, ensuring better coordination
between healthcare and social services. By localizing these services in communities,

                                                                                      1
 patients can access care and information more easily. The measure also prioritizes
 support for vulnerable groups with specific needs, thereby reducing inequalities.

        The role of the Community-Based Health Navigator has been proven effective
 in other countries and in small-scale initiatives in the Philippines led by non-
 government organizations. These efforts have shown positive results in improving
 patient experiences and advancing integrated care. However, their sustainability has
 been limited due to lack of formal recognition and funding. This bill addresses these
 gaps by formally establishing and supporting the Community-Based Health
 Navigator system nationwide.

 Thus, the proposed measures seek to;

  1. To formalize the Community-Based Health Navigator and its function in the
     provision of integrated healthcare in the Philippines, and strengthen its role,
     scope, and program implementation.
  2. To establish a Patient Navigation Department for each municipal/city health
     office.
  3. To deploy a Community-Based Health Navigator to each barangay health center
     and strengthen access to primary healthcare services.
  4. To enact a shared health IT system storing all health care institutions and social
     services nationally and locally to mainstream all health aids and programs.
  5. To maximize the potential of the workforce by instituting standard guidelines,
     training curriculum, and certification pathways.
  6. To evaluate the effectiveness of its function and implementation through a sunset
     review.

       In the interest of safeguarding the well-being of our people, immediate
 passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Community-Based
Health Navigator Act.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State,
in accordance with Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution, “to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among


                                                                                 1
them”. To attain this, the State affirms its duty to uphold accessible, equitable, and
people-centered healthcare experienced at the local level.


Considering this, it is asserted that the role of the community-based health navigator
in addressing the complex and fragmented healthcare system is crucial.
Correspondingly, it will facilitate integrated and equitable care among vulnerable
populations. Through front-lining the referral care system and bridging the
community to clinical and social services, it affirms the provision of integrated
healthcare. Aligned with the purpose of RA 11223, otherwise known as the
“Universal Health Care Act,” the establishment of community-based health
navigators shall reinforce the primary health care and service delivery networks.


The purpose of this Act is as follows:


   A. To formalize the Community-Based Health Navigator and its function in the
      provision of integrated healthcare in the Philippines, and strengthen its role,
      scope, and program implementation.
   B. To improve timely access to health and reinforce the care continuum from
      prevention, early detection, diagnosis, treatment, and survivorship.
   C. To promote an efficient and effective referral system and personalized care
      among patients      in the community, especially         the chronic-ill and
      multimorbidity patients in need of continuum care.
   D. To address the fragmented healthcare system and the complexity of
      transitional care among patients.
   E. To minimize health inequalities and barriers by assisting marginalized
      populations, commonly with low socio-economic status.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following
definitions shall apply:


   a. Community-based Health Navigator is a trained frontline worker responsible
      for assisting and guiding patients in accessing primary health care services,
      social services, and other appropriate referrals, with the end view of ensuring
      quality and integrated health care
   b. Integrated Care refers to a person-centered care carried through
      multidisciplinary care by coordinating care providers.
   c. Person-Centered Care refers to the transcending disease-focused care and
      encompasses the quality of life of patients among the major considerations.
   d. Primary Health Care (PHC) refers to the health care institution accessible in
      the community.
   e. Referral System refers to the structured process of directing patients to
      higher-level facilities and care.


SECTION 4. Establishment of Patient Navigation Unit– There is hereby
established, under the administration of DOH, a Patient Navigation Unit/Division as
a standalone and formal unit within the municipality/city health office. It shall:


      a) Address the fragmented healthcare systems by being a frontline of care
      transition and referral, health education, and patient follow-up.
      b) Registered patients with chronic illness and identification of vulnerable
      populations and health determinants in the community.

      c) Established a shared health IT system and data restoring all health care
      institutions and social services locally and nationally to be mainstreamed to
      all deployed health navigators in the barangay.
      d) A sufficient number of Community-Based Health Navigator positions shall
      be created in consideration of their deployment to barangay health centers and
      their population size. Minimum standard shall be determined by the

      e) The Patient Navigation Unit shall be headed by a qualified Healthcare

SECTION 5. Function and Organizational Placement of Community-Based
Health Navigators. – Subject to the guidelines set by the Department of Health
(DOH), generally community health navigators (CHNs) shall be at the frontline of
care in assisting patients in accessing transitional care and social services through a
systematic referral system, particularly among vulnerable populations such patients
with chronic and comorbid conditions, low socioeconomic status, PWD, elderly,
pregnant women, etc. They too shall facilitate coordination to link patients to
resources, assist home visitation and medical adherence and commitment to
medication, and champion outreach and health information campaigns. This duty
shall be executed through tight coordination with barangay health workers (BHWs),
nurses, doctors, and social services institutions.


The health navigation department in each city/municipality shall be an authorized
body, in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH) and the Local

Government Unit (LGU), to determine the number of deployed health navigators in
each barangay, mainly considering the population size. The deployed health
navigator shall be stationed at the barangay health center to assist with
telecommuting referrals and rotate to the home visitation program if needed.


SECTION 6. Recruitment and Training. – The Department of Health (DOH),
together with the Civil Service Commission (CSC), TESDA, academic institutions,
and other related agencies, shall adopt a comprehensive national standard for Patient
Navigation. It shall institute its core function, ethical principles, and scope of
responsibilities that are aligned with global best practices and contextualized in the
Philippine health system. Sequentially, the mentioned bodies shall constitute
standard guidelines, training curriculum, and certification pathways to ensure that
all navigators, both clinical and non-clinical, meet the minimum competency.


Generally, Health Navigators shall be recruited from the local barangay where they
will be deployed, with a minimum residency requirement of two (2) years to ensure
community trust and familiarity. Candidates must have completed at least two years
of college education and demonstrate functional literacy and basic computer
proficiency. Priority shall be given to applicants with educational or professional
backgrounds in health, social work, or community organizing, but not limited to.


The DOH, in coordination with academic institutions, civil society organizations,
and LGUs, shall establish volunteer programs and community-based internships to
train individuals as lay Health Navigators. Such pathways shall serve both as
community service opportunities and as preparatory experience for formal
engagement in the health workforce.

The Civil Service Commission (CSC) shall issue guidelines on the grant of special

SECTION 7. Salary Scale – The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall establish a career progression and
corresponding salary scale for Community-based Health Navigators assigned in
cities and municipalities, subject to the position classification and compensation
system under Republic Act No. 6758, otherwise known as the “Position
Classification and Compensation Act of 1989.” The minimum entry level for Health
Navigators shall not be lower than Salary Grade 7 to 9, to ensure proper classification
and                           prevent                            undercompensation.


SECTION 8. Appropriations — The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the Department of Health budget in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

Act, or as the need arises, the House Committee on Health, Senate Committee on
Health and Demography shall conduct a sunset review. For purposes of this Act, the
term “sunset review” shall mean a systematic evaluation by the House Committee
on Health, Senate Committee on Health and Demography on implementing

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR) – Within ninety (90)
days from the approval of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in consultation
with the DBM, CSC, LGU, and other concerned stakeholders, shall formulate and
issue the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of this Act.


SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of
this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
