                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act (“R.A.”) No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code (1991)1,
as amended by R.A. 8553 (1998)2, was enacted to give full meaning to the constitutional
policy of local autonomy enshrined in Article X, Section 2 of the Constitution (1987),
which mandates that “the territorial and political subdivisions shall enjoy local
autonomy.”3 This autonomy is the cornerstone of a decentralized system of governance
intended to make local government units (“LGUs”) self-reliant, participatory, and
accountable in promoting the general welfare of their constituents.

Over the years, however, the implementation of the Local Government Code has revealed
lingering tensions between national oversight and local independence. These constraints
often manifest in the form of excessive review, pre-approval, or substitution of judgment
by national offices over local administrative determinations, particularly in
organizational structuring, staffing, budgeting, and fiscal management.

The Supreme Court, in Mandanas v. Ochoa (2018) reaffirmed that genuine
decentralization demands both fiscal and administrative empowerment of LGUs. Fiscal
autonomy, in particular, must be accompanied by the concomitant capacity of local
governments to independently determine and implement their programs and structures
in accordance with their unique service requirements4. Thus, decentralization cannot be
meaningful unless LGUs are given real authority, discretion, and responsibility to
manage their affairs free from undue interference by national agencies.

This bill seeks to further strengthen the autonomy of LGUs through a more rationalized
system of decentralization by amending specific provisions of Republic Act No. 7160. It

By explicitly limiting the power of national agencies to restrain or substitute the judgment
of LGUs, this measure reinforces the constitutional guarantee of local autonomy and
operationalizes the principle of subsidiarity, that matters ought to be handled by the
smallest, lowest, or least centralized competent authority.

Further, this bill aligns with the State’s policy under Section 2(c) of the Local Government
Code itself, which declares that it shall be the continuing policy of the State to ensure the
accountability of local governments while providing them with genuine and meaningful
self-governance. It also complements the objectives of the Supreme Court’s Mandanas-
Garcia ruling and the Executive’s subsequent full devolution initiatives under Executive
Order No. 138 (s. 2021)5, which seek to strengthen local capacities to deliver devolved
services effectively.

Ultimately, this proposed measure reaffirms the principle that autonomy is not mere
delegation but recognition of inherent local competence. It seeks to liberate local
governance from bureaucratic impediments that frustrate efficiency and innovation,
thereby enabling LGUs to respond more effectively to the needs and aspirations of their
constituents.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACТ TO FURTHER STRENGTHEN LOCAL AUTONOMY THROUGH A MORE
    RATIONALIZED SYSTEM OF DECENTRALIZATION AMENDING FOR THE
    PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NUMBER 7160, AS AMENDED, OTHERWISE
    KNOWN AS THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Declaration of State Policies and Principles. – It is hereby declared a
policy of the State to ensure local autonomy of its territorial and political subdivisions
consistent with decentralization inherent in local autonomy thereby ensuring local
administrative determination without undue restrictions.

       SEC. 2. Interpretation. – The provisions of this Act shall be interpreted in favor of
local autonomy and to ensure the independent administrative determination of local
government units free from any control or regulation of national agencies such that the
national government is not given the power to restrain or substitute the judgement of
local government units with its own.
