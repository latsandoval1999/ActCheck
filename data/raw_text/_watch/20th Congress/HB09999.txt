                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

         According to the World Bank's Learning Poverty estimates, approximately nine out of
ten Filipino children aged ten are unable to read and understand a simple text appropriate for
their age, highlighting a serious challenge in foundational learning.1 At the same time,
educational and health experts point out that modern youth face unprecedented mental health
pressures. Today, teachers in face-to-face classrooms are constantly dealing with a noticeable
spike in student anxiety, stress, and attention issues, which directly ruins a student's focus and
ability to learn.

        The Philippines continues to confront a dual challenge of learning loss and declining
student well-being. National and international assessments have demonstrated significant
gaps in foundational literacy and numeracy, while educators increasingly report difficulties in
learner engagement, concentration, and emotional regulation. These factors are interrelated
and directly affect classroom performance and long-term educational outcomes.

        International experience demonstrates that strengthening academic instruction alone is
insufficient to address learning recovery. Educational systems in jurisdictions such as
Singapore, the United Kingdom, and Australia have increasingly integrated evidence-based
social-emotional and classroom wellness strategies into daily instruction to improve learners'
focus, resilience, and readiness to learn. Studies likewise show that structured
social-emotional learning interventions are associated with improvements in academic
achievement, classroom behavior, and student well-being.

         Recognizing these developments, this measure establishes a National Classroom
Wellness and Learning Recovery Program under the Department of Education. Rather than
creating another academic subject or extending instructional hours, the Program
institutionalizes practical, evidence-based classroom strategies that may be integrated into
existing teaching practices while simultaneously strengthening foundational literacy and
numeracy.

       By combining learning recovery with learner well-being, the proposed measure seeks
to improve educational outcomes, reduce learning gaps, and promote the holistic
development of every Filipino learner.

  AN ACT STRENGTHENING FOUNDATIONAL LITERACY AND NUMERACY
 THROUGH THE INTEGRATION OF DAILY MENTAL WELLNESS AND FOCUS
      ACTIVITIES IN BASIC EDUCATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Youth Mental Health and
Foundational Learning Integration Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take appropriate
steps to make such education accessible to all. Pursuant to Article XIV of the 1987 Philippine
Constitution, the State shall ensure that all educational institutions foster the holistic,
intellectual, and total well-being of the learner.

       Concurrently, the State recognizes its duty under Article II, Section 15 of the
Constitution to protect and promote the right to health of the people, which inherently
includes the mental health and emotional resilience of Filipino youth.

        Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize an integrated approach to basic
education that recognizes academic success and mental wellness as mutually reinforcing
components of human development. The State shall adopt evidence-based, school-based
strategies that simultaneously correct learning deficits, foster emotional resilience, and
maximize the learner's cognitive readiness to learn.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

        (a) Foundational Literacy refers to the ability of a learner to read, comprehend,
interpret, and communicate effectively at a level appropriate to the learner's age and grade.

       (b) Foundational Numeracy refers to the ability of a learner to understand and apply
basic mathematical concepts and operations necessary for everyday life and continued
learning.
        (c) Classroom Wellness Activities refer to brief, age-appropriate, evidence-based
educational practices designed to improve learners' attention, emotional regulation, resilience,
stress management, and readiness to learn.

       (d) Learning Recovery Interventions refer to instructional strategies, remedial
programs, and support mechanisms intended to address learning gaps and improve
foundational competencies.

       (e) Department refers to the Department of Education (DepEd).

      SEC. 4. National Classroom Wellness and Learning Recovery Framework. – The
Department shall establish and implement a National Classroom Wellness and Learning
Recovery Program in all public elementary and secondary schools.

       The Program shall promote learner well-being, strengthen foundational literacy and
numeracy, improve classroom engagement, and support learning recovery through
evidence-based educational strategies integrated into regular classroom instruction.

       For this purpose, the Department shall develop standards, guidelines, instructional
materials, and implementation frameworks for schools.

        The Program may include, but shall not be limited to:
        (a) attention and focus exercises;
        (b) mindfulness and breathing activities;
        (c) brief movement or stretching activities;
        (d) age-appropriate social-emotional learning activities;
        (e) positive reflection and resilience-building practices;
        (f) cooperative learning and peer engagement activities; and
        (g) other evidence-based interventions that promote learner well-being and readiness
to learn.

        Implementation shall utilize existing instructional time and classroom routines and
shall not require the creation of a separate academic subject or additional instructional hours.

       SEC. 5. Strengthening Foundational Literacy and Numeracy. – The Department, in
alignment and coordination with existing national learning recovery initiatives under
Republic Act No. 12028, otherwise known as the Academic Recovery and Accessible
Learning (ARAL) Program Act, shall strengthen foundational literacy and numeracy by:

       (a) conducting periodic diagnostic assessments to identify learning gaps;
       (b) implementing targeted learning recovery and remediation programs;
       (c) developing evidence-based instructional materials;
       (d) providing differentiated instruction for struggling learners; and
       (e) continuously monitoring learner progress and outcomes.

       SEC. 6. Teacher Professional Development. – The Department shall provide
continuing professional development programs for teachers and school personnel on:

       (a) evidence-based classroom wellness strategies;
       (b) social-emotional learning;
       (c) foundational literacy and numeracy interventions;
       (d) classroom management that promotes learner engagement;
       (e)strategies to integrate these activities into classroom instruction while minimizing
additional administrative and reporting burdens on teachers; and
       (f) implementation of the Program established under this Act.​

      SEC. 7. Development of Standards and Learning Resources. – The Department
shall develop age-appropriate standards, teaching guides, resource materials, and
implementation tools necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

       In developing such standards, the Department may consult educational institutions,
child development experts, mental health professionals, and other relevant stakeholders.

       SEC. 8. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Annual Report to Congress- – The
Department shall establish a monitoring and evaluation system to assess the implementation
and effectiveness of the Program.

       Such evaluation shall include indicators relating to:
       (a) foundational literacy outcomes;
       (b) foundational numeracy outcomes;
       (c) learner engagement and attendance;
       (d) implementation status across schools; and
       (e) such other performance indicators as the Department may determine.

      The Department shall submit an annual report to Congress on the implementation and
outcomes of the Program, together with recommendations for policy and program
improvements.

       SEC. 9. Inter-Agency Coordination.– The Department may coordinate with the
Department of Health, state universities and colleges, local government units, professional
organizations, and other public and private stakeholders to promote evidence-based
implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other portions which are not affected thereby shall continue in
full force and effect.
       SEC. 14. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
