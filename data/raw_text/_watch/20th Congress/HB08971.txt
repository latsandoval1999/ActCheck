                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to incentivize registered physicians who provide free
medical services to indigent patients. It grants them tax credits to be deducted
from their annual income tax due, subject to the limitations and conditions
provided under Republic Act no. 8424, otherwise known as the “National
Internal Revenue Code of 1997”, as amended.

      The COVID-19 pandemic taught us the indispensable role of the
medical profession in maintaining national stability. However, the Second
Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) reported that as of
early 2026, the Philippines only records 21.2 healthcare workers for every ten
thousand (10,000) people1. This is less than half the World Health
Organization’s recommended doctor-to-population ratio of ten (10) doctors
per ten thousand (10,000) patients. Physicians who dedicate their time and
resources to treat patients who fall below the official poverty threshold often
absorb the operational costs of consultations and procedures themselves.
However, despite their critical contribution, limited incentives exist to provide
them economic relief, and to recognize their voluntary services.

       This proposed measure grants a tax credit of up to ten percent (10%) of
the registered physician’s annual income tax due to further encourage their
charitable reach. It also mandates a rigorous verification process to ensure
fiscal integrity and transparency. It ensures that the tax credits granted are
commensurate with the actual medical services rendered, and the
requirement of strict documentation is required to prevent fraudulent claims.

                           AN ACT
GIVING TAX CREDIT TO REGISTERED PHYSICIANS PROVIDING PRO
BONO MEDICAL SERVICES TO INDIGENT PATIENTS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Physician Pro Bono
Services Incentives Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them. Further, the State shall adopt an integrated and comprehensive
approach to health development which shall endeavor to make essential goods,
health and other social services available to all the people at affordable cost. Towards
this end, the State shall prioritize the needs of the underprivileged, including the
sick, elderly, persons with disabilities, women, and children, and shall endeavor to
provide accessible and affordable medical care to indigent patients.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Indigent Patient refers to a person who has no visible means of income
       or whose income falls below the official poverty threshold, as identified by
       the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development, and/or cannot
       afford in a sustained manner to provide one’s minimum basic needs of food,
       health, education, housing, and other essential amenities; and

          b. Pro Bono Services refer to medical services rendered by a duly licensed
       and registered physician to an indigent patient without any form of
       compensation or remuneration.

       SEC. 4. Incentive to Registered Physicians rendering Pro Bono Services to Indigent
Patients. – Registered physicians rendering pro bono services to indigent patients
shall be entitled to a tax credit to be deducted from their income tax due, subject to
the limitations and conditions provided under Republic Act (RA) No. 8424,

otherwise known as the “National Internal Revenue Code of 1997”, as amended. The
tax credit shall not exceed ten percent (10%) of the total income tax due of the
physician for the taxable year.

       The grant of the tax credit shall be subject to verification and validation by the
Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), based on guidelines developed in coordination
with the Department of Health (DOH). The DOH, in consultation with the
Philippine Medical Association (PMA), shall develop standards for determining the
nature, scope, and documentation of pro bono services.

        SEC. 5. Valuation of Pro Bono Services. – The value of the pro bono services shall
be based on standardized rates to be determined by the DOH, taking into account
prevailing rates for similar medical services in the locality and Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) case rates. Such valuation shall be subject to
review and audit by the BIR to ensure accuracy and compliance with existing laws,
rules, and regulations.

       SEC. 6. Documentation and Compliance Requirements. – To avail of the tax credit
under this Act, registered physicians shall submit appropriate documentation,
including:

         a.​ Certificate of Indigency of the patient issued by the barangay or the
       Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office;

          b.​ Medical records or service reports detailing the nature and extent of
       services rendered; and

         c.​ A sworn statement attesting that no compensation or remuneration
       was received for the services rendered.

​      No tax credit shall be granted for services that are reimbursed, compensated,
or otherwise covered under any government program, including those administered
by PhilHealth.

​      SEC. 7. Penalties. – Any person who falsely claims or fraudulently avails of the
tax credit provided under this Act, including the submission of falsified documents
or misrepresentation of pro bono services rendered, shall, upon conviction, be subject
to the penalties provided under RA 8424, otherwise known as the “National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997”, as amended, without prejudice to the imposition of
administrative sanctions under existing laws, rules, and regulations:

​       Provided, That any tax credit wrongfully or fraudulently claimed shall be
subject to restitution, including the payment of the amount unlawfully claimed,
surcharges, interest, and other penalties as may be imposed under existing tax laws
and regulations; Provided, further, That if the offender is a registered physician, such
act shall constitute grounds for administrative disciplinary action by the
Professional Regulation Commission, which may include suspension or revocation
of license.

      SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – BIR, in coordination with the
DOH, and in consultation with the Philippine Medical Association, shall promulgate
the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act
within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

       SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – Should any provision of this Act or any part
thereof be declared invalid, the other provisions, so far as they are separable from
the invalid ones, shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, orders, issuances, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

     SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
