                                EXPLANATORY NOTE



      The Philippines faces a critical food security crisis and persistent poverty among
farmers and fisherfolk. A recent Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey (March 2025)
indicates a near-doubling in involuntary hunger, surging from 14.2% in 2024 to 27.2%
in 2025.   Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data (2023) shows that farmers and
fisherfolk are the poorest sectors, with poverty incidence estimates at 27.0% and
27.4%, respectively.


    Agricultural cooperatives are crucial to addressing these issues. Data from the
Cooperative Development Authority (CDA) shows that cooperatives are a vital part of
the economy, but many agricultural cooperatives struggle with fragmentation and
limited resources. Studies from organizations like the Food and Agriculture
Organization (FAO) consistently show that farmers who are members of cooperatives
have better access to credit, modern technology, and markets. These organizations
enable economies of scale by allowing small-scale farmers to collectively purchase
inputs and sell their produce, increasing their income and reducing their costs.
    This bill aims to strengthen agricultural cooperatives as key partners for national
food security and rural development. It seeks to:


     (a) Provide funding and resources to boost the productivity and sustainability of
agricultural cooperatives;
     (b) Encourage clustering and consolidation to achieve economies of scale, and
improve competitiveness of agricultural cooperatives;
    (c) Promote modern, climate-resilient farming practices and technologies;
    (d) Enhance access to credit, knowledge, and collective marketing through
cooperative networks; and
    (e) Build the capacity of smallholder farmers through improved extension services
and training.


       It is respectfully acknowledged that a similar measure has been filed by Senator
Risa Hontiveros. This bill draws on and complements said measure, sharing the
common objective of advancing the development, promotion, and protection of
agricultural cooperatives through the creation of a dedicated bureau.


       Empowering these cooperatives can create a more resilient agricultural sector,
reduce poverty, and ensure food self-sufficiency for millions of Filipinos. The
immediate passage of this bill is therefore earnestly sought.

 4      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Agricultural Cooperatives

 6       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State, in line
 7   with the Constitution, that expanding productivity is the key to raising the quality of
 8   life for all especially the underprivileged, together with a sustained increase in the
 9   amount of goods and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people.
10       In line with this, the State recognizes the need to hasten the growth, integration,
11   and modernization of the agricultural sector in order to meet the rising dilemma of
12   food insecurity, which disproportionately affects the marginalized sectors of society.
13       The State further recognizes the indispensable need to encourage the
14   participation of women and youth as a primary force In the government's drive to
15   empower the agricultural sector. Therefore, the State shall focus on establishing a
16   structure and network that institutionalizes support, capacity-building, and industry
17   growth for agricultural cooperatives.
 1      Similarly, it is the declared policy of the State to foster the creation and growth of
 2   cooperatives as a practical vehicle for promoting self-reliance and harnessing people
 3   power towards the attainment of economic development and social justice.
 4      Towards this end, the Government and all of its branches, subdivisions,
 5   instrumentalities, and agencies shall ensure the provision of technical guidance,
 6   financial assistance, and other relevant services to enable farmers, fisherfolk, and
 7   other agricultural workers to organize themselves into self-sustaining cooperatives
 8   that work together to strengthen the Philippine agricultural industry.

11       Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - In so far as they are used in this Act, the following
12   terms shall mean:
13      a. Agribusiness - refers to a range of activities that occur in the agricultural value
14        chain utilized in modem food production, including but not limited to farming,
15        forestry, fishing operations, as well as seed supply, agrichemicals, related
16        machineries, processing, storage, marketing, distribution, and sales, whether
17        wholesale or retail;
18     b. Agricultural Cooperative - refers to a type of cooperative comprising farmers,
19        fisherfolk, agrarian reform beneficiaries, dairy farmers, community forestry
20        farmers, aquaculture farmers, and other individuals engaged in agricultural
21        activities. These individuals voluntarily organized themselves into an agricultural-
22        based economic enterprise, including subsidiary agricultural cooperatives formed
23        by existing multi-purpose cooperatives and multi-purpose cooperatives whose
24        primary business activity is at least fifty percent (50%) relatal to agricultural
25        production, agro-industry, agri-business, or services related to agricultural
26        production. Agricultural activities encompassed by the cooperative include the
27        production, processing, agrologists, and distribution of agricultural, fishery, and
28        forestry inputs and products, whether primary, derived, or processed. The
29        cooperative also provides a range of services aimed at improving the livelihood,
30        income, and overall welfare of its members. These services include savings and
31        credit, marketing, agricultural extension, farm education, agri-tourism, digital
32        agriculture, and other allied services related to climate resiliency and safety nets;
 1   c. Asset Development - refers to the growth and improvement, according to
 2     targeted goals and objectives, of key instruments and resources for agricultural
 3     operations, such as, but not limited to, land, buildings, which may Include
 4     residences, warehouses, or barns, irrigation systems, fences, and trees;
 5   d. Build Scale - refers to the growth or expansion of agribusiness in a proportional
 6     and profitable manner;
 7   e. Cooperative - refers to an autonomous and duly registered association of
 8     persons, with a common bond of interest, who have voluntarily joined together
 9     to achieve their social, economic, and cultural needs and aspirations by making
10     equitable contributions to the capital required, patronizing their products and
11     services, and accepting a fair share of the risks and benefits of the undertaking
12     in accordance with universally accepted cooperative principles;
13   f. Clustering - refers to the process by which multiple agribusinesses, each involved
14     In different stages of the agricultural value chain, combine their operations into
15     a single hub. This collaboration aims to enhance operational efficiency,
16     streamline processes, and maximize profits through integrated efforts;
17   g. Consolidation - refers to a process by which fragmented and scattered
18     landholdings are reorganized or restructured into larger, more contiguous plot
19     or plots. This can be done through voluntary reallocation, sales, land exchanges
20     or swaps, lease, and other means.
21   h. Criteria for Assessment and Ratings - refers to a set of standards in determining
22     that the performance of an agricultural cooperative, which includes, among
23     others, enterprise management, adherence to cooperative principles, and
24     product or service quality;
25   i. Family Farming - refers to an agribusiness operation that is managed and
26     operated by a family unit, related by consanguinity or affinity, whether nuclear
27     or extended, and which predominantly relies on the labor of the members of
28     such family for its operations. The family and the farm, therefore, are inextricably
29     linked, and co-evolve with each other;
30   j. Local Development Hub (LDH) - refers to a farming zone or transport
31     infrastructure, which is generally far from formal markets. An LDH serves around
32     five hundred (500) to one thousand (1,000) farmers, and links them to buyers.
 1         which may be, among others, traders, processors, and export companies, among
 2         others.
 3      k. National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC) - refers to a federation
 4         of agricultural cooperatives that are engaged in agribusiness and financial
 5         business for the benefit of the members through its subsidiaries.
 6      I. Wholesale Market - refers to a market, owned by the government and managed
 7         by the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC) through its
 8         subsidiary/ies, where a trader buys farm produce from the farmers, through the
 9         agricultural cooperatives, in bulk and re-sells the goods to business houses or
10         retailers to further sell the goods to end consumers.
11      m. Cooperative Climate Resilience Fund - refers to a grant-making mechanism that
12         aims to ensure that agricultural cooperatives and their farmer-members are
13         protected from and resilient to the impact of climate change.
14       Sec. 4. Registration and Supervision. - Agricultural Cooperatives shall be governed
15   by the general law on cooperatives, except as otherwise provided herein. In
16   connection with this, agricultural cooperatives must be registered with the
17   Cooperatives Development Authority (CDA) in order to be entitled to the privileges
18   and exemptions extended to cooperatives under Republic Act (RA) No. 9520,
19   otherwise known as the Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008.
20       An agricultural cooperative shall file its application for registration with the CDA
21   regional office that has jurisdiction over its area of operation. No fees shall be assessed
22   or charged to any agricultural cooperative by any government entity, national or local,
23   at any stage of its organization and registration process.
24       This notwithstanding, as the agency with the mandate to promote agricultural
25   development by providing policy framework, public investments, and support services,
26   the Department of Agriculture (DA) shall be the regulatory agency exercising primary
27   supervision over the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC). The
28   Federation shall exercise supervision and control over its member-primary agricultural
29   cooperatives.
30        Sec. 5. Capital Requirements ofAgricultural Cooperatives. - The authorized share
31   capital of an agricultural cooperative shall be stated in its Articles of Cooperation. Prior
32   to its registration, at least twenty-five percent (25%) of the authorized share capital
 1   shall be subscribed to by the members, and at least twenty five percent (25%) of the
 2   subscribed share capital shall be paid-up: Provided, That the paid-up capitalization
 3   requirement for primary cooperatives shall not be less than Fifteen Thousand Pesos
 4   (P15,000.00).
 5        Sec. 6. Cooperative Business Activities and Allied Services. - A duly registered
 6   agricultural cooperative may engage in all or any of the following activities:
 7        a. Raising and/or culture of plants, high-value crops, animals, fish, poultry,
 8          livestock, other agri-products as source of food, feed, and fiber, and other agri-
 9          fishery or non-food commodities;
10       b. Production of salt;
11       c. Community and social forestry;
12       d. Provision of savings and credit facilities for marginal farmers and fishermen;
13       e. Facilitating the procurement and distribution of farm inputs/implements for its
14       members;
15       f. Processing, marketing, and sale of members agri-products or produce;
16       g. Installation, maintenance and management of irrigation systems, water
17          impounding, and other agricultural facilities; and
18       h. Other allied services such as, but not limited to, warehousing, cold storage and
19       other post-harvest services, hauling, transport of members' products or produce,
20       and such other similar business services and activities that will reduce cost or add
21       value.
22       Sec. 7. Bond of Membership. - The bond of membership of agricultural
23   cooperatives shall be any of the following:
24       a. Residential - members are farmers, farm workers, or fisherfolk who are actually
25   and physically residing within the area of operation;
26       b. Occupational - members are those who are engaged in raising or culture of
27   plants or animals, or are part of the technical and scientific communities of the
28   agriculture sector;
29      c. Associational - members are from registered associations, groups, clubs, cultural
30   and other similar aggrupation of farmers, farm workers, and fisherfolk; and
31       d. Institutional - members are employees, workers, or officers of a particular
32   Institution who are undertaking or will undertake agricultural projects. Provided, that
 1   at least fifty-one per centum (51%) of its members are duly registered with the
 2   Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA).

 4                      BENEFITS OF AGRICULTURAL COOPERATIVES
 5       Sec. 8. Tax Treatment of Agricultural Cooperatives that Transact with Non-
 6   members. - Notwithstanding any law or regulation to the contrary, duly registered
 7   agricultural cooperatives that transact with both members and nonmembers, and with
 8   reserve funds of One Hundred Million Pesos (P100,000,000.00) or below shall be
 9   exempt from all national Internal revenue taxes, fees, and charges of whatever name
10   and nature.
11       On the other hand, if such agricultural cooperatives have reserve funds of greater
12   than One Hundred Million Pesos (P100,000,000.00), it shall pay the following taxes on
13   their transactions with non-members:
14       a. Income Tax on Income derived from transactions with non-members: Provided,
15         That the same tax is not consequently imposed on interest individually received
16         by members. The tax base for all cooperatives liable to pay income tax shall be
17         the income directly related from the business transactions with non-members
18         after deducting the amounts for the statutory funds;
19       b. Value-Added Tax (VAT) on transactions with non-members: Provided, however,
20         that duly registered agricultural cooperatives shall be exempt from the
21         imposition of VAT, on the following:
22         1. Sales by agricultural cooperatives duly registered with the DA to their
23            members as well as sale of their produce, whether in its original state or
24            processed form, to non-members; their importation of direct farm inputs,
25            machineries and equipment, including spare parts thereof, to be used
26            directly and exclusively in the production and/or processing of their produce,
27            and their resale to other agricultural cooperatives;
28         2. Gross receipts from lending activities towards its members or other
29            agricultural cooperatives;
30
31            Provided, That the exempt transactions in the foregoing shall include sales
32            made by agricultural cooperatives duly registered with the DA organized and
 1            operated by their members to undertake the production and processing of
 2            raw materials or of goods produced by their members into finished or process
 3            products for sale by the cooperatives to their members and non-members:
 4            Provided, however, that any processed product or its derivative arising from
 5            the raw materials produced by their members, sold in the name and for the
 6            account of the cooperative shall be deemed a product of the cooperative and
 7            Its members: Provided, further. That at least twenty five per centum (25%)
 8            of the net income of the cooperatives is returned to the members in the form
 9            of interest and/or patronage refunds.
10       c. All sales of goods and/or services rendered for non-members shall be subject
11         to the applicable percentage taxes; and
12       d. All other taxes unless otherwise provided herein.
13       Donations to duly accredited charitable, research and educational institutions and
14   reinvestment to socio-economic projects within the area of operation of the
15   cooperative may be tax deductible.
16       Sec. 9. Tax Treatment of Agricultural Cooperatives that Transact with Members
17   Only. - Duly registered agricultural cooperatives under this Act which do not transact
18   any business with non-members or the general public shall not be subject to any
19   taxes, fees, and charges Imposed under the internal revenue laws and other tax laws.
20        Sec. 10. Other Benefits and Privileges of Agricultural Cooperatives. - An
21   agricultural cooperative shall enjoy the following benefits and privileges:
22       a. Taxes, Duties, and Liens:
23          1. All benefits, privileges, and tax treatments of cooperatives as defined and
24            enumerated under RA 9520 and other relevant laws shall apply to agricultural
25            cooperatives;
26          2. Agricultural cooperatives shall be exempt from customs duties, advance sales
27            or compensating taxes on their Importation and local purchase of
28            machineries, equipment, accessories, batteries, and spare parts used by
29            them: Provided, That all tax- and duty-free imports and local purchases shall
30            not be sold nor the beneficial ownership thereof be transferred to any person
31            until after five (5) years, otherwise, the cooperative and the transferee or
 1       assignee shall be solidarity liable to pay twice the amount of the imposed tax
 2       and/or duties; and
 3    3. All real properties owned, and those directly used for the operations,
 4       management, and maintenance of agricultural cooperatives shall be exempt
 5       from the payment of real property taxes Imposed under existing laws.
 6   b. Trainings and Conferences - All trainings and conferences for the organization,
 7      registration, management and operations of an agricultural cooperative,
 8      rendered by the appropriate government agency, shall be given free of
 9      charge, including costs of venue, training materials, speakers and resource
10      persons. The CDOs shall be primarily responsible for these trainings and
11      conferences within their respective localities.
12   c. Consolidation - Agricultural cooperatives may consolidate agricultural lands
13      owned by its members within in a contiguous area for purposes of agricultural
14      development.
15   d. Negotiated Procurement by National and Local Government Agencies. To
16      promote and support agricultural cooperatives, national and local government
17     agencies shall negotiate the procurement of agricultural products and other
18     agricultural commodities from registered and accredited agricultural
19     cooperatives. Negotiated procurement of these products and commodities
20     shall follow RA No. 12009, its implementing rules and regulations, and
21     applicable guidelines issued by the Government Procurement Policy Board;
22      Provided that the procurement of said products and commodities are
23      necessary for the performance of the mandates of the procuring entities.
24   e. Loans and Grants:
25      1. All loans applied for by agricultural cooperatives in any government owned
26        or-controlled banks or government financial Institutions shall enjoy
27        preferential treatment as to the requirements, rates, securities, and
28        collaterals; and
29     2. The DA shall coordinate with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to lower
30        the requirements for all loans applied for and granted to agricultural
31        cooperatives.
 1         f. strengthening Program for Agricultural Cooperatives - the government
 2             through the DA and CDA shall introduce a program aimed at strengthening
 3             agricultural cooperatives through mergers and consolidations. This initiative,
 4             part of the government's broader effort to enhance the stability and
 5             resilience of the agriculture sector, offers income support incentives to
 6             encourage smaller agricultural cooperatives and farmers associations to
 7             consolidate that can lead to improved operational efficiencies, reducing costs
 8             and improving service delivery for agricultural cooperatives. These may
 9             include grants or subsidies that help build business scale and improve capital
10             base, operational synergies, broader service offerings, and risk mitigation.
11       Sec. 11. Organization as a Multi-Purpose Cooperative - Notwithstanding any law
12   to the contrary, an agricultural cooperative may be organized as a multi-purpose
13   cooperative from its registration with the CDA: Provided, that it has complied with the
14   minimum requirements for an agricultural cooperative as prescribed by the CDA Rules
15   and Regulations.
16       Sec. 12. Pre-Cooperative Agricultural Organization. - Prior to registration with the
17   CDA, a group of natural persons intending to form an agriculture cooperative may
18   voluntarily form a Pre-Cooperative Agricultural Organization (PCAO) that shall have its
19   own juridical personality to undertake activities that shall determine the viability of the
20   creation of an agricultural cooperative. The PCAO must be registered as such with the
21   Cooperative Development Offices (CDOs) of the appropriate LGU where the PCAO
22   intends to operate. The Bureau may assist and endorse PCAOs to CDA for registration.
23        The DA and CDA, in coordination with the CDOs, shall assist PCAOs so that they
24   can formally organize as agricultural cooperatives; Provided, that should the PCAO
25   decide to formally organize into a cooperative, it must comply with the requirements
26   under the law. Any contract executed by the PCAO prior to its registration as an
27   agricultural cooperative shall remain valid and binding between the parties insofar as
28   it redounds to the benefit of the cooperative.
29        The DA and CDA shall jointly issue guidelines on the mechanisms for PCAOs.

11       Sec. 36. Powers and Functions of the Council. - The Council shall exercise the

15          ecological agricultural practices, access to resources and benefits, market
16          opportunities, and capacity-building, among others;
17        b. Formulate the Food Security Master Plan, in coordination with the Bureau;
18        c. Provide an integrated framework in order to optimize all government resources
19          for the modernization and empowerment it of the sector;
20        d. Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations for the development of the tangible
21          assets, such as machineries, granaries, equipment, and lands, among others,
22          of agricultural cooperatives;
23        e. Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations, in coordination with the appropriate
24          agencies, to ensure the rights and welfare of agricultural cooperatives and
25          their members, especially women, youth, and family farmers, as well as their
26          preferential treatment in government procurement as enshrined under the
27          law;
28        f. Formulate policies for the development of sustainable financial reform in the
29          agricultural cooperative sector, including the creation of a government
30          financial institution that will specifically cater to the sector, in coordination
31          with the relevant departments and agencies;

11       Sec. 37. Policies Fostering Indusivity and Sustainability. - Recognizing the
12   contributions of women and youth to the agricultural sector, as well as the need to
13   improve indusivity, the Council shall prioritize policies that seek to improve women
14   empowerment and youth participation in the sector, and which shall foster
15   sustainability and further opportunities for economic growth.
16       Further, the Council shall prioritize the implementation of policies that enhance
17   biodiversity, sustainability, and agroecology, and the development of innovative
18   farming techniques and practices.

23   to provide funds to defray the cost of reasonable travel expenses, in addition to the
24   per diem, as may be necessary.
