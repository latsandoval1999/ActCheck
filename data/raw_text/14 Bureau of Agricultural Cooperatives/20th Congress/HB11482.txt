                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to face the twin challenges of food insecurity and
persistent poverty among farmers and fisherfolk, the very people who ensure that
food reaches every Filipino table. The March 2025 Social Weather Stations (SWS)
survey revealed a troubling increase in involuntary hunger, rising from 14.2 percent
in 2024 to 27.2 percent in 2025. It is a painful irony that those who produce the
country’s food are often the ones who experience hunger and deprivation the most.

       Agricultural cooperatives have long served as an essential mechanism for
empowering farmers and fisherfolk, improving productivity, and strengthening rural
communities. According to the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA),
cooperatives play a significant role in the Philippine economy. However, many
agricultural cooperatives remain fragmented, underfunded, and constrained by limited
access to markets, financing, and modern technologies. Studies conducted by the
Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) consistently demonstrate that cooperative
members enjoy greater access to credit, improved farming technologies, and wider
market opportunities. By enabling small-scale producers to pool resources, purchase
inputs collectively, and market their products more effectively, cooperatives help
reduce production costs, increase incomes, and improve overall competitiveness.

11          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. In so far as they are used in this Act, the
12   following terms shall mean:
13
14         a) Agribusiness – refers to a range of activities that occur in the agriculture
15            value chain utilized in modern food production, including but not limited to
16            farming, forestry, fishing operations, as well as seed supply, agrichemicals,
17            related machineries, processing, storage, marketing, distribution, and sales,
18            whether wholesale or retail;
19
20         b) Agricultural Cooperative – refers to a type of cooperative comprising
21            farmers, fisherfolk, agrarian reform beneficiaries, fairy farmers, community
22            forestry farmers, aquaculture farmers, and other individuals engaged in
23            agricultural activities. These individuals voluntarily organized themselves
24            into an agricultural-based economic enterprise, including subsidiary
25            agricultural cooperatives formed by existing multi-purpose cooperatives and
26            multi-purpose cooperatives whose primary business activity is at least fifty
27            percent (50%) related to agricultural production, agro-industry, agri-
28            business, or services related to agricultural production. Agricultural activities
29            encompassed by the cooperatives include the production, processing,
30            agrologist, and distribution of agricultural, fishery, and forestry inputs and
31            products, whether primary, derived, or processed. The cooperative also
32            provides a range of services aimed at improving the livelihood, income, and
33            overall welfare of its members. These services include savings and credit,
34            marketing, agricultural extension, farm education, agri-tourism, digital
35            agriculture and other allied services related to climate resiliency and safety
36            nets;
37
38         c) Asset Development – refers to the growth and improvement, according to
39            targeted goals and objectives, of key instruments and resources for
40            agricultural operations, such as, but not limited to, land, buildings, which
41            may include residences, warehouses, or barns, irrigation systems, fences,
42            and trees;
43
44         d) Build Scale – refers to the growth or expansion of agribusiness in a
45            proportional and profitable manner;
46
47         e) Cooperative – refers to an autonomous and duly registered association of
48            persons, with a common bond of interest, who have voluntarily joined

                                                2
 1      together to achieve their social, economic, and cultural needs and
 2      aspirations by making equitable contributions to the capital required,
 3      patronizing their products and services, and accepting a fair share of the
 4      risks and benefits of the undertaking in accordance with universally
 5      accepted cooperative principles;
 6
 7   f) Clustering – refers to the process by which multiple agribusinesses, each
 8      involved in different stages of the agricultural value chain, combine their
 9      operations into a single hub. This collaboration aims to enhance operational
10      efficiency, streamline processes, and maximize profits through integrated
11      efforts;
12
13   g) Consolidation – refers to a process by which fragmented and scattered
14      landholdings are reorganized or restructured into larger, more contiguous
15      pot or plots. This can be done through voluntary reallocation, sales, land
16      exchanges, or swaps, lease, and other means.
17
18   h) Criteria for Assessment and Ratings – refers to a set of standards in
19      determining the performance of an agricultural cooperative, which includes,
20      among others, enterprise management, adherence to cooperative
21      principles, and product or service quality.
22
23   i) Family Farming – refers to an agribusiness operation that is managed and
24      operated by a family unit, related by consanguinity or affinity, whether
25      nuclear or extended, and which predominantly relies on the labor of the
26      members of such family for its operations. The family and the farm,
27      therefore, are inextricably linked, and co-evolve with each other;
28
29   j) Local Development Hub (LDH) – refers to a farming zone or transport
30      infrastructure, which is generally far from formal markets. An LDH serves
31      around five hundred (500) to one thousand (1,000) farmers, and links them
32      to buyers, which may be, among others, traders, processors, and export
33      companies, among others.
34
35   k) National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC) – refers to a
36      federation of agricultural cooperatives that are engaged in agribusiness and
37      financial business for the benefit of the members through its subsidiaries.
38
39   l) Wholesale Market – refers to a market, owned by the government and
40      managed by the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC)
41      through its subsidiary/ies, where a trader buys farm produce from the
42      farmers, through the agricultural cooperatives, in bulk and re-sells the goods
43      to business houses or retailers to further sell the goods to end consumers.
44
45   m) Cooperative Climate Resilience Fund – refers to a grant-making mechanism
46      that aims to ensure that agricultural cooperatives and their farmer-members
47      are protected from and resilient to the impact of climate change.

13          Sec. 24. Creation of the Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives. The DA
14   shall establish a Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives (hereafter, the "Bureau"), which
15   shall be the primary coordinating, implementing, and administrative body responsible
16   for matters relating to agricultural cooperatives, including, but not limited to,
17   monitoring their performance and implementing plans and programs for their growth
18   and development. The Bureau shall be headed by a qualified Director, and its
19   organizational and staffing pattern shall be determined by the DA, in accordance with
20   the rules set forth by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).
21
22         Sec. 25. Powers and Functions of the Bureau of Agricultural
23   Cooperatives. The Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives shall exercise the following
24   powers and functions:
25
26         a) Collaborate and coordinate with various government agencies, institutions,
27            and units for the development and implementation of projects and plans for
28            agricultural cooperatives;
29         b) Promote the use of digital platforms and innovative technologies for the
30            growth and development of agricultural cooperatives, in coordination with
31            the relevant agencies;
32         c) Consolidate and study all relevant information regarding agricultural
33            cooperatives that shall be used in the formulation of policies and programs
34            for the sector;
35         d) Develop advocacy programs for capacity-building and further education of
36            members of agricultural cooperatives, with a particular focus on women and
37            youth;
38         e) Provide technical assistance to agricultural cooperatives;
39         f) In partnership with the NFAC, establish Local Development Hubs (LDHs) In
40            order to foster cooperation, collaboration, and mutual support among
41            agricultural cooperatives, in coordination with the CDA and LGUS;
42         g) Promote agricultural cooperative dusters and assist in the creation thereof,
43            as a means to forge strategic partnerships among cooperatives; and
44         h) Monitor and evaluate the performance of agricultural cooperatives
45            according to an established assessment and ratings system.
46
47            Sec. 26. Sustainability Mapping. Within one (1) year from the effectivity
48   of this Act, the Bureau shall undertake a comprehensive nationwide survey in order to

15           Sec. 36. Powers and Functions of the Council. The Council shall exercise
16   the following powers and functions:
17           a) Formulate and issue policies for the growth and development of the
18              agricultural cooperative sector, which shall encompass adoption of
19              innovative and ecological agricultural practices, access to resources and
20              benefits, market opportunities, and capacity-building, among others;
21           b) Formulate the Food Security Master Plan, in coordination with the Bureau;
22           c) Provide an integrated framework in order to optimize all government
23              resources for the modernization and empowerment it of the sector;
24           d) Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations for the development of the tangible
25              assets, such as machineries, granaries, equipment, and lands, among
26              others, of agricultural cooperatives;
27           e) Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations, in coordination with the appropriate
28              agencies, to ensure the rights and welfare of agricultural cooperatives and
29              their members, especially women, youth, and family farmers, as well as
30              their preferential treatment in government procurement as enshrined under
31              the law;
32           f) Formulate policies for the development of sustainable financial reform in the
33              agricultural cooperative sector, including the creation of a government
34              financial institution that will specifically cater to the sector, in coordination
35              with the relevant departments and agencies;
36           g) Develop policies and guidelines for the institutionalization of Cooperative
37              Resilience Mechanism (CRM) that will aid agricultural to adapt to and
38              recover from the effects of climate change or other unforeseen
39              circumstances. The CRM shall embody a holistic approach, with provisions
40              for funding assistance, addressing production and distribution issues, and
41              use of new or innovative technologies, production technologies using
42              renewable energies, regenerative and sustainable agriculture, among
43              others; and
44           h) Advocate for policy reforms and programs that enhance social protection of
45              the agricultural cooperatives and their members, including access to
46              healthcare, insurance, and retirement benefits, among others.
