     IMPLEMENTING MECHANISMS, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE
     NATIONAL LAND USE COMMISSION, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
     THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1                                          CHAPTER I
 2                               INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS
 3
 4           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Land Use
 5   Act”.
 6           SEC. 2. Declaration of Principles and Policies. – All lands of the public

 7   domain, waters, minerals, coal, petroleum, and other mineral oils, all forces of

 8   potential energy, fisheries, forests or timber, wildlife, flora and fauna, and other

 9   natural resources are owned by the State. With the exception of agricultural lands, all

10   other natural resources shall not be alienated.

11           Pursuant to the constitutional provision or mandate, it is the policy of the State

12   to provide for a rational, holistic, and just allocation, utilization, management, and

13   development of the country’s land to ensure their optimum use to promote

14   sustainable socioeconomic development and ecological protection.

15           The State recognizes the need for rational, optimal and sustainable

16   settlements    development,     consistent   with   the   principles   of   environmental

17   management and equitable access to land and security.

18           To this end, the State institutionalizes land use and physical planning as

19   mechanisms for identifying, determining, and evaluating appropriate land use and

20   allocation patterns that promote and ensure:

21           (a)    Sustainable management and utilization of natural resources;




                                                                                             2
 1         (b)    Maintenance and preservation of environmental integrity and stability,

 2   specifically, the perpetual protection of permanent forests and watershed and

 3   ecosystem services for the attainment of food, water and energy sufficiency;

 4         (c)    Disaster risk-reduction and climate change resiliency;

 5         (d)    Protection of prime agricultural lands for food security in basic

 6   commodities with emphasis on self-sufficiency in rice and corn;

 7         (e)    Water security through sustainable development and management of

 8   water resources towards water security;

 9         (f)    Provision of adequate and efficient settlements, transportation and

10   infrastructure development in support of urban, peri-urban, and rural linkages

11   towards regional development and inclusive growth;

12         (g)    Harmony between the rights and the varied interests of every Filipino

13   within the framework of people empowerment, decentralization, social justice, and

14   equity;

15         (h)    Respect for and protection of the sustainable traditional resource rights

16   of the Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs or IPs) to their

17   ancestral domains to ensure their economic, social, and cultural well-being as well

18   as recognition of the applicability of customary laws and sustainable traditional

19   resource use and management, knowledge, and practices in ancestral domains in

20   compliance with free and prior informed consent of ICCs or IPs;

21         (i)    Protection of the rights of basic sectors to equitable access to the

22   country’s land;

23         (j)    Protection, preservation, and development of the country’s historical,

24   cultural, archaeological, and built heritage resources for the deeper understanding of

25   our history and culture as a people;

26         (k)    Attainment of energy security and self-sufficiency;



                                                                                         3
 1           (l)   A whole-of-society and whole-of-government orientation, through which

 2   thrusts and strategies espoused by different actors and sectors are heard and

 3   brought together to achieve complex and interrelated goals, is encouraged and

 4   adopted as a basic parameter in achieving efficiency in land use and allocation,

 5   within the framework of ecological development and equity;

 6           (m)   Promotion of the integrated approach to land use and urban transport

 7   planning to effectively manage growth in urban areas and ensure that the capacity of

 8   transport facilities can accommodate the demand for movement of passengers and

 9   goods to, from, and within the centers of socioeconomic activities;

10           (n)   Vertical and horizontal linkages or spatial strategies and the potential

11   for co-management and inter-locality cooperation across territorial jurisdictions and

12   government levels, highlighting the imperative for a ridge-to-reef and ecosystem-

13   based approach to land and water resource management and governance; and

14           (o)   Data and evidence-informed management and governance of land and

15   water resources, in line with the place-based aspirations and shared values of the

16   community.

17           It is also the policy of the State to ensure that local government units (LGUs)

18   share with the national government the responsibility of managing and maintaining

19   ecological balance within their territorial jurisdiction as stated in the 1987 Constitution

20   and Republic Act (RA) No. 7160, as amended, otherwise known as the “Local

21   Government Code of 1991.” Guided by the principle that the use of land bears a

22   social function and that all economic agents contribute to the common good,

23   landowners, land tenure holders, in the case of forestlands, be it an individual,

24   communal, corporate or group, is held responsible for developing and conserving

25   their lands, thereby making their lands productive and supportive of environmental

26   stability.



                                                                                              4
 1          SEC. 3. Scope. – This Act applies to all lands and natural resources in the

 2   country, whether public, private, government-owned or in the possession of any

 3   person, whether natural or juridical, and serves as guide for the rational, holistic and

 4   just allocation, utilization, development, and management of the land and water

 5   resources in the country, including such activities that bear impact on those

 6   resources.

 7          SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

 8          (a)    Agricultural land refers to land of public domain which has been

 9   classified as such and is devoted to agricultural activity as defined under RA 6657,

10   as amended, otherwise known as the “Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law of

11   1988,” and not further classified for residential, commercial or industrial use and

12   such other uses as may be provided by law;

13          (b)    Agricultural land use conversion refers to the act or process of

14   changing the current physical use of a piece of agricultural land into some other use

15   not comprising an agricultural activity as defined under RA 6657, as approved

16   exclusively by the Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR) in an order of conversion;

17          (c)    Alienable and Disposable (A&D) lands refer to lands of the public

18   domain which have been delineated, classified, and certified as open and available

19   for disposition under existing laws;

20          (d)    Ancestral domains refer to all areas generally belonging to indigenous

21   cultural communities or indigenous peoples as defined in RA 8371, otherwise known

22   as “The Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997”;

23          (e)    Archaeological site refers to any place, whether above or underground,

24   underwater or at sea level, containing fossils, artifacts and other cultural, geological,

25   botanical, zoological materials which depict and document culturally relevant

26   paleontological, prehistoric or historic events;



                                                                                            5
 1          (f)    Basic sector refers to a group of people not referring to any organized

 2   groups, such as but not limited to women, youth, elderly, differently abled, peasants

 3   and fisherfolk;

 4          (g)    Coastal area or zone refers to a band of dry land and the adjacent

 5   ocean space, water and submerged land, in which terrestrial processes and uses

 6   directly affect oceanic processes and uses and vice versa, the geographic extent of

 7   which includes areas within a landward limit of one (1) kilometer from the shoreline

 8   at high tide to include mangrove swamps, brackish water ponds, nipa swamps,

 9   estuarine rivers, sandy beaches and other areas within a seaward limit of two

10   hundred (200) meters isobaths to include coral reefs, algal flats, seagrass beds, and

11   other soft-bottom areas;

12          (h)    Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) refers to a plan embodying a

13   set of policies accompanied by maps and similar illustrations that serves as a policy

14   guide for determining the future use of lands and natural resources within the

15   territorial jurisdiction of the LGU, including municipal waters with due consideration to

16   the biogeographic region within which the locality belongs, representing the

17   community desired pattern of population distribution and proposes future allocation

18   of land and resources to various activities including the processes and criteria

19   employed in such determination of allocation of land and resources for a long-term

20   management of the local territory covering a minimum period of twelve (12) years,

21   reviewable not earlier than every six (6) years;

22          (i)    Critical habitats refer to areas outside protected areas as defined in RA

23   7586, otherwise known as the “National Integrated Protected Areas System Act of

24   1992,” that are known habitats of threatened species and designated as such based

25   on scientific data taking into consideration species endemicity or richness, presence

26   of human-made pressures or threats to the survival of wildlife living in the area;



                                                                                            6
 1          (j)   Critical watershed refers to a drainage area of a river system

 2   supporting existing and proposed hydroelectric power, irrigation works or existing

 3   water facilities needing immediate protection and rehabilitation to minimize erosion

 4   and improve water yield;

 5          (k)   Cultural heritage refers to the totality of cultural properties, whether

 6   natural or human made, preserved and developed through time and passed on for

 7   posterity;

 8          (l)   Cultural property refers to all products of human creativity by which a

 9   people and a nation reveal their identity, including churches, mosques and other

10   places of religious worship, schools and natural history specimens and sites,

11   whether public or privately-owned, movable or immovable, and tangible or intangible;

12          (n)   Customary laws refer to a body of written or unwritten rules, usages,

13   customs and practices traditionally and continually recognized, accepted, and

14   observed by respective ICCs or IPs, consistent with RA 8371;

15          (o)   Development plan refers to the activities and measures that the

16   national government or LGUs intend to implement over a specified period of time to

17   achieve the defined set of development goals identified in the national or local

18   physical framework plans integrating socio-economic, financial, fiscal, legal and

19   legislative institutional and sectoral plans of the national government or any of its

20   instrumentalities or a particular LGU, consistent with the objectives identified in

21   spatial plans, such as land use plans or physical framework plans including an

 6                    PHYSICAL FRAMEWORK AND LAND USE PLAN

 7          SEC. 5. National Physical Framework Plan. – The National Land Use

 8   Commission (NLUC) created under Section 18 herein shall draft the National

 9   Physical Framework Plan (NPFP), which shall have a timeframe of thirty (30) years,

10   with regular review and updating every ten (10) years. The NPFP, which guides the

11   planning and management of the country's land and other physical resources at the

12   national and sub-national levels, shall indicate broad spatial directions and policy

13   guidelines on settlements development, production land use, protection land use,

14   social services and utilities, transmission line corridor, and transportation and

15   communication.

16          The Regional Physical Framework Plans (RPFPs), Provincial Physical

17   Framework and Development Plans (PPFDPs), and CLUPs shall cover the physical

18   development of their respective territories and shall be consistent with the NPFP:

19   Provided, That the integration and harmonization of the physical framework plans at

20   all levels shall be iterative to ensure that the concerns of both top and bottom levels

21   of government are considered. The physical and land use plans prepared at all

22   levels shall have internal consistency, specifically on, but not limited to, the

23   development, management, and conservation of forestlands, watersheds, and all

24   terrestrial, aquatic, and marine ecosystems found within a given territory and the

25   linkages of the major land use categories to ensure their complementation in the

26   utilization, development, management, protection, and conservation of all natural



                                                                                          19
 1   resources. The NLUC shall issue procedural guidelines which shall guide the

 2   formulation and integration or harmonization of land use plans at all levels: Provided

 3   further, That the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev),

 4   DENR, Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD),

 5   Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other agencies will

 6   continue to issue specific guidelines on the planning process and content of the

 7   plans consistent with the NLUC guidelines and their respective agency mandates.

 8          SEC. 6. Regional Physical Framework Plans. – In consultation with

 9   concerned sectors, the Regional Land Use Policy Council (RLUPC) shall define the

14   spatially-based and area-focused policies, consistent with those in the national

15   framework for physical planning, to guide detailed physical, socio-economic,

16   sectoral, and investment planning. The RPFP shall have a planning period of twelve

17   (12) years to be updated every six (6) years or when deemed necessary.

16                                        CHAPTER III

17                       FRAMEWORK FOR LAND USE PLANNING

18          SEC. 12. Basic Objective of Land Use or Physical Framework Planning. –

19   The primary objective of a physical framework plan is to describe existing land uses

20   and to identify an integrated physical framework, including an overall vision, policies

21   on social and economic development and environmental protection and supporting

22   transportation and other infrastructure facilities, that shall define future development.

23   Its output includes the identification of programs, projects and activities needed to

24   achieve the desired development, without compromising environmental and

25   ecological stability, and which shall serve as inputs to the investment program. In the




                                                                                           23
 1   case of the CLUP, it is also intended to provide specific development and building

 2   guidelines and parameters that can be implemented through a zoning ordinance.

 3         SEC. 13. Priorities in Land Use Allocation and Planning. – Using the R2R

 4   or WEM approach in projecting spatial allocation for different land uses, the LGUs

 5   shall first exclude areas under protection land use in Section 17 (a) hereof, national

 6   parks, energy resource lands, and prime agricultural lands to ensure ecological

 7   integrity, energy supply, and to promote food security. Areas with prior rights and

 8   those with site-specific resources for basic services such as water and indigenous

 9   energy resources, shall also be excluded. All land use activities inside the excluded

10   areas that are not consistent with the general purposes of the excluded areas shall

11   be assessed, regulated or modified to ensure that these activities do not compromise

12   the general purposes of the excluded areas. Spatial allocation and planning shall

13   then proceed in accordance with Sections 16 and 17 hereof with priorities given to

14   integrated watershed management areas, socialized housing sites, fisherfolk

15   settlement in coastal areas, and waste disposal sites.

16         SEC. 14. Approach to Land Use and Physical Framework Planning. –

17   The R2R or WEM approach shall be used in land use and physical framework

18   planning to promote a holistic, integrated, equitable and inclusive development, and

19   a flourishing environment, watersheds, and ecosystems. It shall include a description

20   of existing land uses and land use trends, as well as the physical, demographic,

21   economic, social, political, environmental, and ecological conditions that have

22   shaped and are likely to shape future land use and physical developments. It shall

23   then derive a land use plan or physical framework by matching, reconciling or

24   integrating the demand for land with the supply of land in a holistic manner to

 2   and activities, and development controls to guide future development. The land use

 3   plan or physical framework shall be holistic in nature and not emanate from specific

 4   individual or corporation which, in essence, constitutes a spot zoning.

 5          SEC. 15. Adoption of Multiple Uses of Land Resources. – The primary and

 6   alternative uses of a specific unit of land resource based on its capability to support

 7   certain uses sustainably shall be determined and evaluated prior to any decision for

 8   the assignment of its use. Areas feasible for sustainable land resource use may be

 9   considered multiple-use zones wherein settlements, tourism, agriculture, forestry,

10   agro-forestry, and extraction activities and other income-generating or livelihood

11   activities may be allowed by concerned agencies: Provided, That multiple uses of

12   land resources shall be excluded in areas identified as protection land use: Provided,

13   further, That such land uses shall maximize the socioeconomic benefits without

14   compromising the desired levels of ecosystem services within a ridge to reef unit:

15   Provided, finally, That such land uses shall be in accordance with priorities in land

16   use allocation and planning and that no reclassification shall be allowed.

17          SEC. 16. Basic Land Use Planning Considerations. – In determining the

18   various land uses, the people and their productive activities and the need for

19   functional open spaces and preservation areas as well as the various

20   interrelationships of the physical characteristics of the land and other elements

21   therein shall be considered, including the following:

22         (a)     Availability of natural resources including indigenous energy resources

23   for energy security and self-sufficiency;

24         (b)     Geology, geomorphology, current and emerging geologic climate-

25   related hazards and risks, soil, vegetative cover, demography, and slope;




                                                                                         25
 1          (c)    Economic, environmental, cultural, social, and related development

 2   activities;

 3          (d)    Existing government policies on land and natural resources allocation,

 4   utilization, management, and disposition;

 5          (e)    Technological changes or advancement;

 6          (f)    Existing customary rights for the protection of indigenous peoples and

 7   vulnerable groups including women, rural and urban poor;

 8          (g)    Desired levels of ecosystem services like water quality and quantity,

 9   soil stability, biodiversity, agricultural and fishery productivity, among others; and

10          (h)    Inventory of prior and existing rights, to harmonize determination of

11   land use.

 5   shall be guided by the following:

 6         (a)    Areas vegetated with mangrove species shall be preserved for

 7   mangrove protection and shall not be converted to other uses;

 8         (b)    Areas which meet all accepted criteria on elevation, soil type, soil

 9   depth, topography, supply for successful fishpond development, and are not

10   identified as mangrove protection areas, shall be utilized for aquaculture purposes;

11         (c)    Areas sub-classified as mangrove and still suitable for use as such, or

12   due to environmental conditions need to be preserved as mangrove, but is devoid of

13   mangrove stands shall not be converted to other uses. The DENR shall ensure that

14   these lands shall be reforested within a given period of time;

15         (d)    Areas accessible to the sea and identified for fisherfolk settlement and

16   housing shall be allocated to traditional fisherfolk who are inhabitants of the coastal

17   communities and members of legitimate fisherfolk organizations or holders of

18   stewardship lease contracts or titles to ancestral domains or any form of property

19   right arrangements who participate in coastal resource management initiatives,

20   subject to the usual census procedures of the DHSUD.

21         (e)    Areas which do not fall under (a) to (d) above may be devoted to

22   recreational or tourism purposes: Provided, That such undertaking will not result in

23   environmental degradation and displacement of small fishers;

24         (f)    Areas which are considered as traditional fishing grounds shall be used

25   primarily for such purpose;




                                                                                            42
 1          (g)       Areas which have been allocated for small infrastructure needed by

 2   fisherfolk shall be allowed;

 3          (h)       Areas which form part of foreshore lands as defined in this Act

 4   including those which are under lease agreements or arrangements shall undergo

 5   zoning and evaluation to determine their boundaries and actual sizes and

 6   corresponding uses: Provided, That vested rights under the lease agreements or

 7   arrangements shall be respected. Evaluation of the said lands shall also consider the

 8   natural, geologic, and geomorphic conditions of the coast; and


 9          (i)       The free and prior informed consent of the concerned ICCs or IPs and

10   the necessary certification from the NCIP shall be secured before any allocation and

11   utilization of lands within the coastal zones that are found to be within ancestral

12   lands or domains.

OBJECTIVE:

      •    To establish a policy that will serve as guide for the rational, holistic and
           equitable allocation, utilization, management, and development of our land and
           water resources including activities that significantly impacts these resources as
           well as the parameters that will direct physical framework and land use planning
           at the national and sub-national levels.


KEY PROVISIONS:

  •       Provides the national framework for the development of the country's lands to
          ensure their optimum use, consistent with the principle of sustainable
          development.

  •       Creates the National Land Use Commission (NLUC) under the Office of the
          President which shall act as the highest policy making body on land use and
          resolve land use policy conflicts between or among agencies, branches or levels
          of government.

  •       Establishes the composition of NLUC with the Commissioner as Chairperson,
          Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development as Vice-
          Chairperson and the Secretaries of the Department of Environment and Natural
          Resources, Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
          (DHSUD), Department of Agriculture, Department of Agrarian Reform, Department
          of Public Works and Highways, and Department of the Interior and Local
          Government, a representative from the local government units, the Chairperson
          of the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples, and Commissioner of the
          Bureau of Internal Revenue as Ex-Officio Members.

  •       Requires the creation of the positions of Commissioner, which has the rank of a
          Cabinet Secretary, and two Deputy Commissioners with the rank of
          Undersecretary.

  •       Creates the National Land Use Office (NLUO) under the Office of the President
          which shall serve as the technical secretariat of the NLUC.

  •       Converts the Environmental, Land Use and Urban Planning and Development
          Bureau (ELUPDB) under DHSUD into a line bureau that shall serve as the NLUO
          and designates the Director of the ELUPDB as Executive Director.

                                                                                                2
   •   Provides mandatory consultations from the regional, provincial, and city or
       municipal levels.

   •   Harmonizes existing rules and regulations governing the allocation, utilization,
       development, and management of the land and water resources in the country.

   •   Addresses the vital issues of land conversion, climate change, the completion of a
       national database and geo-hazard mapping; and disaster risk reduction and
       management.

   •   Mandates the NLUC to come up with a system of incentives and awards to LGUs
       that regularly updates their comprehensive land use plan.

   •   Provides sanctions and penalties for violations.
