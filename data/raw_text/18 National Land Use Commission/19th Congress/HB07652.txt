                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE


       All lands of the public domain, waters, minerals, coal, petroleum, mineral oils, all
forces of potential energy, fisheries, forests or timber, wildlife, fora and fauna, and other
natural resources are owned by the State. With the exception of agricultural lands, all
other natural resources shall not be alienated. Thus, there is a need for rational, optimal
and   sustainable   settlements    development,     consistent   with   the   principles   of
environmental management and equitable access to land and security.


       This bill proposes to provide the national framework for a rational, holistic, and
just allocation, utilization, management, and development of the country's land to ensure
their optimum use, consistent with the principle of sustainable development. Moreover,
the bill will include mandatory consultations from the regional, provincial, city and
municipal level. Overall, the goal of this proposed legislation is to harmonize existing
plans, rules and regulations governing the allocation, utilization, development and
management of land resources.


       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly urged.

11                          INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS

12         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Land

13   Use Act”.

14         SEC. 2. Declaration of Principles and Policies. – All lands of the public

15   domain, waters, minerals, coal, petroleum, and other mineral oils, all forces of

16   potential energy, fisheries, forests or timber, wildlife, fora and fauna, and other
 1   natural resources are owned by the State. With the exception of agricultural lands,

 2   all other natural resources shall not be alienated.

 3         Pursuant to the constitutional provision or mandate, it is the policy of the

 4   State to provide for a rational, holistic, and just allocation, utilization,

 5   management, and development of the country's land to ensure their optimum use,

 6   consistent with the principle of sustainable development.

 7         The State recognizes the need for rational, optimal and sustainable

 8   settlements development, consistent with the principles of environmental

 9   management and equitable access to land and security.

10         Toward this end, the State institutionalizes land use and physical planning as

11   mechanisms for identifying, determining, and evaluating appropriate land use and

12   allocation patterns that promote and ensure:

13         (a)    Sustainable management and utilization of natural resources;

14         (b)    Maintenance and preservation of environmental integrity and stability

15   specifically the perpetual protection of permanent forests and watershed for the

16   attainment of food, water and energy sufficiency;

17         (c)    Disaster risk-reduction and climate change resiliency;

18         (d)    Protection of prime agricultural lands for food security in basic

19   commodities with emphasis on self-sufficiency in rice and corn;




                                                                                       2
 1         (e)    Water security through sustainable development and management of

 2   water resources towards water security;

 3         (f)    Settlements, transportation and infrastructure development in support

 4   of urban, rural and regional development and inclusive growth;

 5         (g)    Harmony between the rights and the varied interests of every Filipino

 6   within the framework of people empowerment, decentralization, social justice, and

 7   equity;

 8         (h)    Respect for and protection of the sustainable traditional resource

 9   rights of the Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) to

10   their ancestral domains to ensure their economic, social, and cultural well-being as

11   well as recognition of the applicability of customary laws and sustainable

13   domains in compliance with free and prior informed consent of ICC/IPs;

14         (i)    Protection of the rights of basic sectors to equitable access to the

15   country's land;

17   cultural and built cultural heritage and resources for the deeper understanding of

18   our history and culture as a people;

19         (k)    Attainment of energy security and self-sufficiency;

 1         (l)    Market orientation where the interplay of market forces and fair trade

 2   within the framework of ecological development and equity consideration is

 3   encouraged and adopted as the basic parameter in achieving efficiency in land use

 4   and allocation; and

 6   planning to effectively manage growth in urban areas and ensure that the capacity

 7   of transport facilities can accommodate the demand for movement of passengers

 9         It is also the policy of the State to ensure that local government units (LGUs)

10   share with the national government the responsibility of managing and maintaining

11   ecological balance within their territorial jurisdiction as stated in the 1987

15   good, landowners, land tenure holders, in the case of forestlands, be it an

16   individual, communal, corporate or group is held responsible for developing and

17   conserving their lands thereby making their lands productive and supportive of

18   environmental stability.

 5                                      CHAPTER III

 6                    FRAMEWORK FOR LAND USE PLANNING

 7         SEC. 11. Basic Objective of Land Use or Physical Framework Planning. –

 8   The primary objective of a physical framework plan is to describe existing land

 9   uses and to identify an integrating physical framework, including an overall vision,

10   policies on social and economic development and environmental protection and

11   supporting transportation and other infrastructure facilities, that will define future

12   development. Its output includes the identification of programs, projects and

13   activities needed to achieve the desired development, and which will serve as

14   inputs to the investment program. In the case of the CLUP, it is also intended to

15   provide specific development and building guidelines and parameters that will be

16   implemented through a zoning ordinance.

17         SEC. 12. Priorities in Land Use Allocation and Planning. – In projecting

18   spatial allocation for different land uses, the LGUs shall first exclude areas under

19   protection land use of Section 16 (a) hereof, national parks, energy resource lands,

20   and prime agricultural lands to ensure ecological integrity, energy supply, and



                                                                                        27
 1   promote food security. Areas with prior rights and those with site-specific

 2   resources for basic services, such as but not limited to, water and indigenous

 3   energy resources, shall also be excluded. Spatial allocation and planning shall then

 4   proceed in accordance with Sections 15 and 16 hereof with priorities given to

 5   integrated watershed management areas, socialized housing sites, fisherfolk

 6   settlement in coastal areas, and waste disposal sites.

 7         SEC. 13. Approach to Land Use and Physical Framework Planning. – In

 8   general, the land use or physical framework planning process shall include a

 9   description of existing land uses and land use trends, as well as the physical,

10   demographic, economic, social and environmental conditions that have shaped and

11   are likely to shape future land use and physical developments. It shall then derive a

12   land use plan or physical framework by matching, reconciling or integrating the

13   demand for land with the supply of land. The resulting land use plan or physical

14   framework shall be the basis for identifying necessary policies, programs, projects

15   and activities, and development controls to guide future development. The land use

16   plan or physical framework must be holistic in nature and not emanate from

17   specific individual or corporation which, in essence, constitutes as spot zoning.

18         SEC. 14. Adoption of Multiple Uses of Land Resources. – The primary and

19   alternative uses of a specific land resource shall be determined and evaluated prior

20   to any decision for the assignment of its use. Areas feasible for sustainable land



                                                                                         28
 1   resource use may be considered multiple-use zones wherein settlements , tourism,

 2   agriculture, forestry, agro-forestry and extraction activities and other income-

 3   generating or livelihood activities may be allowed: Provided, That multiple uses of

 4   land resources shall be excluded in areas identified as protection land use:

 5   Provided, further, That such land resources shall be in accordance with priorities in

 6   land use allocation and planning and that no reclassification shall be allowed.

 7          SEC. 15. Basic Land Use Planning Considerations. – In determining the

 8   various land uses, the people and their productive activities and the need for

 9   functional open spaces and preservation areas as well as the various

10   interrelationships of the physical characteristics of the land and other elements

11   therein shall be considered, among others, the following:

12          (a)    Availability of natural resources including indigenous energy

13   resources for energy security and self-sufficiency;

14          (b)    Geology, geomorphology, geologic hazard, climate, soil, vegetative

15   cover, demography and slope;

16          (c)    Economic, environmental, cultural, social and related development

17   activities;

18          (d)    Existing government policies on land and natural resources allocation,

19   utilization, management and disposition;

20          (e)    Technological changes or advancement;



                                                                                       29
 1         (f)    Existing customary rights for the protection of indigenous peoples and

 2   vulnerable groups including women and urban poor; and

 3         (g)    Inventory of prior and existing rights, to harmonize determination of

 4   land use.

 5         SEC. 16. Categories of Land Uses for Planning Purposes. – In determining

 6   and defining the national, regional and provincial framework plans, and CLUPs,

 7   land uses shall be grouped into four major functional uses as follows:

 8         (a)    Protection land use wherein the use of land primarily for food, water

 9   and energy security, rehabilitation, conservation, and protection purposes to

10   promote the country’s ecological and life-support systems. Planning for protection

11   land use intends to achieve food self-sufficiency in rice and corn, water and energy

12   security, environmental stability and ecological integrity, ensure a balance between

13   resource use and the preservation of some areas with environmental, aesthetic,

14   educational, cultural, heritage and historical significance, and protect people and

15   human- made structures from the ill-effects of natural hazards. All lands, either

16   public or private, under protection land use shall be permanently conserved,

17   preserved, rehabilitated and protected from all other land uses, disposition,

18   intrusion, utilization and development that is not consistent from its existing uses.

19   Areas under this category also include those covered by R.A. 7586, otherwise

20   known as the “NIPAS Act”, as amended, and other coastal and marine protected



                                                                                       30
 1   areas, those areas outside NIPAS but nonetheless require protection because of

 2   their outstanding physical and aesthetic features, anthropological significance, and

 3   biological diversity, and those areas prone to natural hazards, such as but not

 4   limited to the following:

 5              (1)     Old-growth forests and mossy;

 6              (2)     Areas with more than 50% in slope gradient;

 7              (3)     Mangrove and fish sanctuaries, pursuant to R.A. No. 8435, or

 8         the “Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act of 1997”, and R.A. No.

 9         8550, or “The Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998”;

10              (4)     Buffer zones or strips/easements, pursuant to Presidential

11         Decree (P.D.) No. 705, amending P.D. No. 389, otherwise known as the

12         “Forestry Reform Code of the Philippines” and P.D. No. 1067, or the

13         “Water Code of the Philippines”;

14              (5)     Salvage zones along foreshore areas, freshwater swamps and

15         marshes, plazas and heritage or historic sites, pursuant to P.D. No. 2146;

16              (6)     Watersheds supporting domestic water supply;

17              (7)     Utility easement like transmission lines of power companies, oil

18         and gas facilities, cell sites, and domestic water lines;

19              (8)     Amenity areas or those with high aesthetic values, pursuant to

20         P.D. No. 2146;



                                                                                        31
 1               (9)    Natural and human-made areas/sites of cultural, historical and

 2         anthropological significance, which are declared as such by internationally

 3         recognized organizations and concerned national agencies;

 4               (10)   Critical habitats as provided in R.A. No. 9147, otherwise

 5         known as the “Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act”;

 6               (11)   Significant caves under R.A. No. 9072, otherwise known as the

 7         “National Caves and Cave Resources Management and Protection Act”; or

 8               (12)   Key biodiversity areas under Executive Order No. 578, Series

 9         of 2006, otherwise known as the “National Biodiversity Policy”.

10         (b)    Production Land Use wherein there is a direct and indirect utilization

11   of land resources for crop, fishery, livestock and poultry production, forestry,

                                                                                       32
 1   tourism development areas where productive activities may be undertaken to meet

 6   identification of the roles and functions of key urban centers, determination of

 7   relationships among settlement areas, and the provision of basic services and

 8   facilities to such settlement. The settlements development plan ensures for the

10   within and among settlements, allowing efficient movement of people and

13   health care, recreation, transportation and communication, sanitation, and basic

15         (d)    Infrastructure development includes the availability or supply of basic

 2         SEC. 61.     Strategic Transport Network. –       The national government,

 3   through the Department of Transportation, shall establish a strategic and

 4   sustainable multimodal transport network that provides connectivity within the

 5   national network of settlements. Such connectivity shall include linkages among

 6   existing and proposed priority urban centers, rural areas, production hubs and

 3         SEC. 70. Causing Irrigated Agricultural Lands to be Idle. – Any person

 4   or juridical entity who shall cause any irrigated lands whether contiguous or not,

 5   within the protected areas for agricultural development, to lie idle and

 6   unproductive for a period exceeding one (1) year, unless due to force majeure,

 7   shall be subject to an idle land tax equivalent to five percent (5%) of the value of

 8   the agricultural land as appearing in its real property tax declaration. In addition,

 9   the violator shall be required to put back such lands to productive agricultural use.

11   period of two (2) years, the land shall be subject to escheat proceedings.
