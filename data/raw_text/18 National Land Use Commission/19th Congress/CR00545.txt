26                                     CHAPTER I

27                         INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS

28         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National

29   Land Use Act”.

30         SEC. 2. Declaration of Principles and Policies. – All lands of the public

31   domain, waters, minerals, coal, petroleum, and other mineral oils, all forces of

32   potential energy, fisheries, forests or timber, wildlife, flora and fauna, and other




                                                                                       2
1    natural resources are owned by the State. With the exception of agricultural

2    lands, all other natural resources shall not be alienated.

3          Pursuant to the constitutional provision or mandate, it is the policy of the

4    State to provide for a rational, holistic, and just allocation, utilization,

5    management, and development of the country's land to ensure their optimum

6    use to promote sustainable socioeconomic development and ecological

7    protection.

8          The State recognizes the need for rational, optimal and sustainable

9    settlements development, consistent with the principles of environmental

10   management and equitable access to land and security.

11         Toward this end, the State institutionalizes land use and physical planning

12   as mechanisms for identifying, determining, and evaluating appropriate land use

13   and allocation patterns that promote and ensure:

14         (a)      Sustainable management and utilization of natural resources;

15         (b)      Maintenance and preservation of environmental integrity and

16   stability specifically the perpetual protection of permanent forests and

17   watershed and ecosystem services for the attainment of food, water and energy

18   sufficiency;

19         (c)      Disaster risk-reduction and climate change resiliency;

20         (d)      Protection of prime agricultural lands for food security in basic

21   commodities with emphasis on self-sufficiency in rice and corn;




                                                                                     3
1          (e)    Water security through sustainable development and management

2    of water resources towards water security;

3          (f)    Provision of adequate and efficient settlements, transportation and

4    infrastructure development in support of urban, peri-urban, and rural linkages

5    towards regional development and inclusive growth;

6          (g)    Harmony between the rights and the varied interests of every

7    Filipino within the framework of people empowerment, decentralization, social

8    justice, and equity;

9          (h)    Respect for and protection of the sustainable traditional resource

10   rights of the Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs)

11   to their ancestral domains to ensure their economic, social, and cultural well-

12   being as well as recognition of the applicability of customary laws and

13   sustainable traditional resource use and management, knowledge, and practices

14   in ancestral domains in compliance with free and prior informed consent of

15   ICC/IPs;

16         (i)    Protection of the rights of basic sectors to equitable access to the

17   country's land;

18         (j)    Protection, preservation, and development of the country’s

19   historical, cultural, archaeological, and built heritage resources for the deeper

20   understanding of our history and culture as a people;

21         (k)    Attainment of energy security and self-sufficiency;




                                                                                    4
1           (l)    A whole-of-society and whole-of-government orientation, through

2    which thrusts and strategies espoused by different actors and sectors are heard

3    and brought together to achieve complex and interrelated goals, is encouraged

4    and adopted as a basic parameter in achieving efficiency in land use and

5    allocation, within the framework of ecological development and equity;

6           (m)    Promotion of the integrated approach to land use and urban

7    transport planning to effectively manage growth in urban areas and ensure that

8    the capacity of transport facilities can accommodate the demand for movement

9    of passengers and goods to, from, and within the centers of socioeconomic

10   activities;

11          (n)    Vertical and horizontal linkages or spatial strategies and the

12   potential for co-management and inter-locality cooperation across territorial

13   jurisdictions and government levels, highlighting the imperative for a ridge-to-

14   reef and ecosystem-based approach to land and water resource management and

15   governance; and

16          (o)    Data and evidence-informed management and governance of land

17   and water resources, in line with the place-based aspirations and shared values

18   of the community.

19          It is also the policy of the State to ensure that local government units

20   (LGUs) share with the national government the responsibility of managing and

21   maintaining ecological balance within their territorial jurisdiction as stated in

22   the 1987 Constitution and Republic Act RA 7160, as amended, also known as


                                                                                    5
1    the “Local Government Code of 1991”. Guided by the principle that the use of

2    land bears a social function and that all economic agents contribute to the

3    common good, landowners, land tenure holders, in the case of forestlands, be it

4    an individual, communal, corporate or group, is held responsible for developing

5    and conserving their lands, thereby making their lands productive and

6    supportive of environmental stability.

7          SEC. 3. Scope. – This Act applies to all lands and natural resources in

8    the country, whether public, private, government-owned or in the possession of

9    any person, whether natural or juridical, and serves as guide for the rational,

10   holistic and just allocation, utilization, development and management of the

11   land and water resources in the country, including such activities that bear

12   impact on those resources.

13         SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

14         (a)    Agricultural land refers to land of public domain which has been

15   classified as such and is devoted to agricultural activity as defined under RA

16   6657, as amended, otherwise known as the “Comprehensive Agrarian Reform

17   Law of 1988,” and not further classified for residential, commercial or industrial

18   use and such other uses as may be provided by law;

19         (b)    Agricultural land use conversion refers to the act or process of

20   changing the current physical use of a piece of agricultural land into some other

21   use not comprising an agricultural activity as defined under RA 6657, as




                                                                                     6
1    approved exclusively by the Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR) in an order

2    of conversion;

3          (c)    Alienable and Disposable (A&D) lands refer to lands of the public

4    domain which have been delineated, classified, and certified as open and

5    available for disposition under existing laws.

6          (d)    Ancestral domains refer to all areas generally belonging to

7    indigenous cultural communities or indigenous peoples as defined in RA 8371,

8    otherwise known as “The Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997;”

9          (e)    Archaeological site refers to any place, whether above or

10   underground, underwater or at sea level, containing fossils, artifacts and other

11   cultural, geological, botanical, zoological materials which depict and document

12   culturally relevant paleontological, prehistoric or historic events;

13         (f)    Basic sector refers to a group of people not referring to any

14   organized groups, such as but not limited to women, youth, elderly, differently

15   abled, peasants and fisher folk;

16         (g)    Coastal area or zone refers to a band of dry land and the adjacent

17   ocean space, water and submerged land, in which terrestrial processes and uses

18   directly affect oceanic processes and uses and vice versa, the geographic extent

19   of which includes areas within a landward limit of one (1) kilometer from the

20   shoreline at high tide to include mangrove swamps, brackish water ponds, nipa

21   swamps, estuarine rivers, sandy beaches and other areas within a seaward limit




                                                                                   7
1    of two hundred (200) meters isobaths to include coral reefs, algal flats, seagrass

2    beds, and other soft-bottom areas;

3             (h)   Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) refers to a plan embodying

4    a set of polices accompanied by maps and similar illustrations that serves as a

5    policy guide for determining the future use of lands and natural resources within

6    the territorial jurisdiction of the LGU, including municipal waters with due

7    consideration to the biogeographic region within which the locality belongs,

8    representing the community desired pattern of population distribution and

9    proposes future allocation of land and resources to various activities including

10   the processes and criteria employed in such determination of allocation of land

11   and resources for a long-term management of the local territory covering a

12   minimum period of twelve (12) years, reviewable not earlier than every six (6)

13   years;

14            (i)   Comprehensive land use planning refers to the act of defining the

15   allocation, utilization, development and management of all lands and water

16   resources within a given territory or jurisdiction according to the inherent

17   characteristic and carrying capacities of the land itself and supportive of

18   sustainable    economic,     demographic,     socio-cultural   and    environmental

19   objectives as an aid to decision-making and legislation;

20            (j)   Critical habitats refer to areas outside protected areas as defined in

21   RA 7586, otherwise known as the “National Integrated Protected Areas System

22   Act of 1992”, that are known habitats of threatened species and designated as


                                                                                        8
1    such based on scientific data taking into consideration species endemicity or

2    richness, presence of human-made pressures or threats to the survival of wildlife

3    living in the area;

4          (k)    Critical watershed refers to a drainage area of a river system

5    supporting existing and proposed hydroelectric power, irrigation works or

6    existing water facilities needing immediate protection and rehabilitation to

7    minimize erosion and improve water yield;

8          (l)    Cultural heritage refers to the totality of cultural properties,

9    whether natural or human made, preserved and developed through time and

10   passed on for posterity;

11         (m)    Cultural property refers to all products of human creativity by

12   which a people and a nation reveal their identity, including churches, mosques

13   and other places of religious worship, schools and natural history specimens and

14   sites, whether public or privately-owned, movable or immovable, and tangible

15   or intangible;

16         (a)    Customary laws refer to a body of written or unwritten rules,

17   usages, customs and practices traditionally and continually recognized,

18   accepted, and observed by respective ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371;

19         (b)    Development plan refers to the activities and measures that the

20   national government or LGUs intend to implement over a specified period of

21   time to achieve the defined set of development goals identified in the national or

22   local physical framework plans integrating socio-economic, financial, fiscal,


                                                                                     9
1    legal and legislative institutional and sectoral plans of the national government

2    or any of its instrumentalities or a particular LGU, consistent with the objectives

3    identified in spatial plans, such as land use plans or physical framework plans

4    including an analysis of problems and resources, definition of goals and

5    objectives, policy guidelines, programs and projects, and an implementation

6    mechanism which defines the roles and contributions expected from the

7    government and the private sector;

8           (c)   Ecologically fragile areas refer to areas within critical watersheds,

9    brackish and freshwater wetlands, pasture lands, inland rivers and waterways

10   coastal and settlement areas, and croplands which require rehabilitation,

11   protection and whose continued unsustainable use may result in physical risks

12   and threats to life and property, public health and safety as well as adversely

13   affect the productivity of lowland agricultural areas and stability of the upland

14   system;

15          (d)   Ecotourism refers to a form of sustainable tourism within a natural

16   or cultural heritage area where community participation, protection and

17   management of natural resources, culture and indigenous knowledge and

18   practices, environmental education and ethics, as well as economic benefits are

19   fostered and pursued for the enrichment of host community and satisfaction of

20   visitors;

21          (e)    Energy resources refer to surface or subsurface substances that

22   serve as energy resources including traditionally mineral fuel deposits such as


                                                                                     10
1    coal petroleum, natural gas or renewable resources from geothermal, hydro

2    reservoirs or non-conventional sources such as ocean waves, solar, wind,

3    biomass, and other similar resources which serve the same purpose;

4          (f)    Energy resource lands refer to lands where naturally occurring or

5    indigenous energy resources exist in sufficient quantity or quality as to be

6    economically viable for exploration, development production, utilization and

7    distribution purposes;

8          (g)     Exhausted mineral resources refer to specific sites where the

9    mineral resources are no longer in sufficient quantity or quality to justify

10   additional expenditure for extraction or utilization as determined by the Mines

11   and Geosciences Bureau and approved by the Department of Environment and

12   Natural Resources (DENR);

13         (h)    Flood plain refers to relatively smooth valley floors adjacent to and

14   formed by rivers which overflow its banks at flood stages. The plain usually

16         (i)    Flood-prone areas refer to low lying areas usually adjacent to large

17   or active water bodies and which therefore experience regular or seasonal

18   inundation as a result of changes in the mean water level of these bodies or

19   because of land reclamation and other artificial interference with the natural

20   processes;

21         (j)    Foreshore land refers to a string of land margining a body of

22   water, the part of a seashore between the low-water line usually at the seaward

1    margin of a low tide terrace and the upper limit of wave wash at high tide

3          (k)    Forest refers to an ecosystem or an assemblage of ecosystems

6          (l)    Forest lands refer to one of the four classifications of land defined

15   volcanic activities, and geophysical processes like mass movements landslides,

16   surface collapses, debris and mud flow;

17         (n)    Geo-hazard prone areas refer to areas frequently affected and/or

18   are vulnerable or at risk to weather/climatic, hydrologic, geologic and other

20         (o)    Heritage zone refer to historical, anthropological, archaeological,

21   artistic geographical areas and settings that are culturally significant to the




                                                                                    12
1    country, as declared by the National Commission for Culture and the Arts

16         4. Other analogous activities.

9          SEC. 5. National Physical Framework Plan. – The National Land Use

10   Commission (NLUC) created under Section 17 herein shall draft the National

11   Physical Framework Plan (NPFP), which shall have a timeframe of thirty (30)

12   years, with regular review and updating every ten (10) years. The NPFP, which

13   guides the planning and management of the country's land and other physical

14   resources at the national and sub-national levels, shall indicate broad spatial

15   directions and policy guidelines on settlements development, production land

16   use, protection land use, social services and utilities, transmission line corridor,

17   and transportation and communication.

18         The Regional Physical Framework Plans (RPFPs), Provincial Physical

19   Framework and Development Plans (PPFDPs), and CLUPs shall cover the

20   physical development of their respective territories and shall be consistent with

21   the NPFP: Provided, That the integration and harmonization of the physical

22   framework plans at all levels shall be iterative to ensure that the concerns of

5    found within a given territory and the linkages of the major land use categories

2                   FRAMEWORK FOR LAND USE PLANNING

3          SEC. 11. Basic Objective of Land Use or Physical Framework

4    Planning. – The primary objective of a physical framework plan is to describe

5    existing land uses and to identify an integrated physical framework, including

6    an overall vision, policies on social and economic development and

7    environmental protection and supporting transportation and other infrastructure

8    facilities, that shall define future development. Its output includes the

9    identification of programs, projects and activities needed to achieve the desired

10   development, without compromising environmental and ecological stability,

11   and which shall serve as inputs to the investment program. In the case of the

12   CLUP, it is also intended to provide specific development and building

13   guidelines and parameters that be implemented through a zoning ordinance.

14         SEC. 12. Priorities in Land Use Allocation and Planning. – Using the

15   R2R/WEM approach in projecting spatial allocation for different land uses, the

16   LGUs shall first exclude areas under protection land use in Section 16 (a)

17   hereof, national parks, energy resource lands, and prime agricultural lands to

18   ensure ecological integrity, energy supply, and promote food security. Areas

19   with prior rights and those with site-specific resources for basic services, such

20   as but not limited to, water and indigenous energy resources, shall also be

21   excluded. All land use activities inside the excluded areas that are not consistent

22   with the general purposes of the excluded areas shall be assessed, regulated,


                                                                                     30
1    and/or modified to ensure that these activities do not compromise the general

2    purposes of the excluded areas. Spatial allocation and planning shall then

3    proceed in accordance with Sections 15 and 16 hereof with priorities given to

4    integrated watershed management areas, socialized housing sites, fisherfolk

5    settlement in coastal areas, and waste disposal sites.

6          SEC. 13. Approach to Land Use and Physical Framework Planning. –

7    The R2R/WEM approach shall be used in land use and physical framework

8    planning to promote a holistic, integrated, equitable and inclusive development,

9    and a flourishing environment, watersheds and ecosystems. It shall include a

10   description of existing land uses and land use trends, as well as the physical,

11   demographic, economic, social, political, environmental, and ecological

12   conditions that have shaped and are likely to shape future land use and physical

13   developments. It shall then derive a land use plan or physical framework by

14   matching, reconciling or integrating the demand for land with the supply of land

15   in a holistic manner to optimize socioeconomic development and minimize the

16   total impacts of the land use plan on watersheds and ecosystems. The resulting

17   land use plan or physical framework shall be the basis for identifying necessary

18   policies, programs, projects and activities, and development controls to guide

19   future development. The land use plan or physical framework shall be holistic

20   in nature and not emanate from specific individual or corporation which, in

21   essence, constitutes as spot zoning.

OBJECTIVE:

      •    To provide a policy for the rational, holistic and just allocation, utilization, management
           and development of our land resources including the parameters that will guide
           planning at the national and local levels.


KEY PROVISIONS:

  •       Provides the national framework for the development of the country's land to
          ensure their optimum use, consistent with the principle of sustainable
          development.

  •       Creates the National Land Use Commission (NLUC) under the Office of the
          President which shall act as the highest policy making body on land use and
          resolve land use policy conflicts between or among agencies, branches or levels
          of government.

  •       Establishes the composition of NLUC with the Commissioner as Chairperson,
          Secretary of National Economic Development Authority (NEDA) as Vice-
          Chairperson and the Secretaries of the Department of Environment and Natural
          Resources (DENR), Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
          (DHSUD), Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR),
          Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), and Department of Local
          Government (DILG), a representative from the local government units (LGUs),
          and the Commissioner of the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)
          as Ex-Officio Members.

  •       Requires the creation of a Commissioner, which has the rank of a Cabinet
          Secretary, and two (2) Deputy Commissioners with the rank of Undersecretary.

  •       Creates the National Land Use Office (NLUO) under the Office of the President
          which shall serve as the technical secretariat of the NLUC and headed by an
          Executive Director.

  •       Provides mandatory consultations from the regional, provincial, city and municipal
          level.

  •       Harmonizes existing rules and regulations governing the allocation, utilization,
          development and management of land resources.

   •   Addresses the vital issues of agrarian reform, climate change, the completion of a
       national database and geo-hazard mapping and disaster risk reduction and
       management.

   •   Mandates the NLUC to come out with a system of incentives and awards to LGUs
       that regularly updates their comprehensive land use plan (CLUP)

   •   Provides sanctions and penalties for violations.
