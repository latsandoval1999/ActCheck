                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

          The Constitution provides that: the State shall promote a just and dynamic
social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation and free
the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate social services,
promote full employment, a rising standard of living, and improved quality of life for
all (Sec. 9, Art. II); the State shall promote social justice in all phases of national
development (Sec. 10, Art. II); the State shall give priority to education, science and
technology, arts, culture, and sports to foster patriotism and nationalism, accelerate
social progress, and promote total human liberation and development (Sec. 17, Art. II);
the Congress shall give highest priority to the enactment of measures that reduce
social, economic, and political inequalities (Sec.1, Art. XIII); the State shall encourage
non-formal, informal, and indigenous learning systems, as well as self-learning,
independent, and out-of-school study programs particularly those that respond to
community needs (Sec 2[4], Art. XIV); and Science and technology are essential for
national development and progress. The State shall give priority to research and
development, invention, innovation, and their utilization; and to science and
technology education, training, and services. It shall support indigenous, appropriate,
and self-reliant scientific and technological capabilities, and their application to the
country’s productive systems and national life (Sec. 10, Art. XIV).
      In this regard, the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), which was created by virtue of Republic Act 7796, otherwise known as the
“Technical Education and Skills Development Act of 1994,” is the government agency
tasked to manage and supervise technical education and skills development in the
Philippines. TESDA’s vision is to be a transformational leader in the technical
education and skills development of the Filipino workforce, with a mission of setting
directions, promulgating relevant standards, and implementing programs geared



                                        Page 1 of 17
towards a quality-assured and inclusive technical education and skills development
and certification system.

      Under the bill, the proposed Department of Technical Education and Skills
Development (DTESD) shall be the primary policy formulation, planning,
coordinating, implementing, regulatory, and administrative entity of the government
for technical education and training programs. With its gargantuan task, it is believed
that by converting it into a Department, TESDA will be strengthened as an
implementing and regulatory institution of the technical and vocational sector. It will
put this Agency in a better position to fulfill its vision and mission, believing in
demonstrated competence, institutional integrity, personal commitment, a culture of
innovativeness, and a deep sense of nationalism.

    The early passage of this Bill, therefore, is earnestly requested.

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Department of
Technical Education and Skills Development (DTESD) Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
provide relevant, accessible, high-quality, and efficient technical education and skills
development in support of the development of high-quality Filipino middle-level
manpower responsive to and in accordance with Philippine development goals and
priorities. The State shall encourage active participation of private technical-
vocational institutions (TVIs) and private enterprises, being direct participants in and
immediate beneficiaries of a technically educated and trained skilled workforce, in
providing technical education services and training opportunities.

     education and skills development programs to attain international
     competitiveness;

b. Quality Middle-Level Manpower. - To focus technical education and skills
   development on meeting the changing demands for quality middle-level
   manpower and work environment, both in the national and international
   community;

     SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
a.    Apprenticeship refers to on-the-job practical training with compulsory related
      theoretical instructions involving a contract between an apprentice and an
      employer on an approved apprenticeable occupation.

b. Apprentice refers to a person undergoing training for an approved apprenticeable
   occupation during an apprenticeship agreement;


c.    Apprenticeship Agreement refers to a contract wherein a prospective employer
      binds himself to train the apprentice, who in turn accepts the terms of training for
      a recognized apprenticeable occupation, emphasizing the rights, duties, and
      responsibilities of each party.

d. Apprenticeable Occupation refers to an occupation officially endorsed by a
   tripartite body and approved as apprenticeable by the Department.

e.    Dual Training System refers to an instructional delivery system of technical and
      vocational education and training that combines in-plant training and in-school
      training based on a training plan collaboratively designed and implemented by
      an accredited dual system educational institution/training center and accredited
      dual system agricultural, industrial, and business establishments with prior
      notice and advice to the local government unit concerned. Under this system,
      said establishments and the educational institution share the responsibility of
      providing the trainee with the best possible job qualifications, the former
      essentially through practical training and the latter by securing an adequate level
      of specific, general, and occupation-related theoretical instruction. The word
      “dual” refers to the two parties providing instruction: the concept “system”
      means that the two instructing parties do not operate independently of one
      another, but rather coordinate their efforts.

f.    Ladderized education refers to the harmonization of all education and training
      mechanisms that allow students and workers to progress between technical,
      vocational, and higher education programs, or vice versa. It opens opportunities
      for career and educational advancement to students and workers. It creates a
      seamless and borderless education and training system that will allow transfers
      in terms of flexible entry and exit between technical-vocational and higher
      education programs in the post-secondary school educational system.

g. Leading Technology Center refers to centers that provide high-quality and efficient
   technical education, training, and certification in support of the development of
   high-quality Filipino manpower responsive to and in accordance with Philippine

                                         Page 5 of 17
     development goals and priorities.

h. Learner refers to persons hired as trainees in semi-skilled and other industrial
   occupations that are non-apprenticeable. Learnership programs must be
   approved by the Department.

i.   Levy Grant System refers to a legal contribution from participating employers who
     would be beneficiaries of the program (often as a percentage of the payroll), which
     is subsequently turned over or rebated to enterprises offering employee training
     programs.

j.   Middle-Level Manpower refers to those:

           i)    Who have acquired practical skills and knowledge through formal or
                 non-formal education and training equivalent to at least a secondary
                 education, but preferably at post-secondary education with a
                 corresponding degree or diploma; or

           ii)   Skilled workers who have become highly competent in their trade or
                 craft, as attested by the industry.

           iii) Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) refers to the national policy
                that describes the levels of educational qualifications and sets the
                standards for qualification outcomes. It is a quality-assured national
                system for the development, recognition, and award of qualifications
                based on standards of knowledge, skills, and values acquired in
                different ways and methods by learners and workers of a certain
                country.

           iv) Private Enterprise refers to an economic system under which property
               of all kinds can be privately owned and in which individuals, alone or
               in association with others, can embark on a business activity. This
               includes but is not limited to industrial, agricultural, or agro-industrial
               establishments engaged in the production, manufacturing, processing,
               repacking, or assembly of goods, including service-oriented
               enterprises.

           v) Skill refers to the acquired and practiced ability to carry out a task or
              job.

           vi) Skills Development refers to the process through which learners and
               workers are systematically provided with learning opportunities to
               acquire or upgrade, or both, their ability, knowledge, and behavior
               patterns required as qualifications for a job or range of jobs in a given
               occupational area.


                                         Page 6 of 17
          vii) Technical Education refers to the education process designed at upper
               secondary and lower tertiary levels, aligned with the levels of the PQF,
               with the pathways to higher education through the credit transfer
               system based on learning outcomes aimed at preparing technicians,
               technologists, para-professionals, and other categories of workers
               and/or entrepreneurs by providing them with a broad range of general
               education, theoretical, scientific and technological studies, and related
               job skills training.

          viii) Trade refers to any group of interrelated jobs or any occupation that is
                traditionally or officially recognized as a craft or artisan in nature,
                requiring specific qualifications that can be acquired through work
                experience and/or training;

          ix) Trainee refers to persons who are participants in a vocational,
              administrative, or technical training program for the purpose of
              acquiring and developing job-related skills;

          x) Trainer refers to persons who direct the practice of skills towards
             immediate improvement in some task;

          xi) User-Led” or “Market-Driven Strategy refers to a strategy that promotes
              strengthened linkages between educational/training institutions and
              industry to ensure that appropriate skills and knowledge are provided
              by the educational system;

       SEC. 5. Creation and Mandate of the Department of Technical Education and
Skills Development. – There is hereby created a Department of Technical Education
and Skills Development, hereinafter referred to as DTESD. The DTESD shall be the
primary policy formulation, planning, coordinating, implementing, regulatory, and
administrative entity of the government for technical education and skills
development programs.
      SEC. 6. Powers and Functions. – The DTESD shall have the following powers
and functions:
    a) Formulate a roadmap for Philippine technical education and skills
development and set the general directions for education and training policies and
standards that shall be embodied in a National Technical Education and Skills
Development Plan (NTESDP);

    b) Formulate and recommend national policies and guidelines and conduct in-
depth studies on all policy areas and options that will promote technical education
and skills development in consultation with the relevant stakeholders, and address
job mismatch between technical vocational education and training and industry
requirements;



                                      Page 7 of 17
    c) Assess, review, harmonize, and coordinate all technical education and skills
development policies and programs to ensure overall relevance, consistency,
responsiveness, effectiveness, and efficient implementation of the national policies;

    d) Organize and constitute various standing committees, subsidiary groups, or
technical working groups for efficient integration, coordination, and monitoring of
technical education and skills development programs at the national, regional, and
local levels;

    e) Enter into, make, execute, perform, and carry out domestic and foreign
contracts for the purpose of attaining its goals and objectives as provided for in this
Act, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations;

    f) Restructure the entire sub-sector consisting of all institutions and programs
involved in the promotion and development of middle-level manpower through
upgrading, merger, and/or phase-out following a user-led or market-driven strategy;

   g) Approve training standards and assessment packages as established,
conducted, and proven effective by private industries;

    h) Establish and administer a system of accreditation of both public and private
technical vocational institutions;

    i) Establish, develop, and support trainers’ training and/or programs;

   j) Impose reasonable fees and charges for such assessment and pieces of training
conducted and retain such earnings for its own use, subject to guidelines that it shall
promulgate and to government auditing rules and procedures;

    k) Allocate resources for the programs and projects it shall undertake pursuant to
the approved NTESDP, subject to pertinent laws, rules, and regulations;

    l) Determine and approve systematic funding schemes such as the levy and grant
scheme for technical education and skills development purposes;

    m) Create, when deemed necessary, an Advisory Committee that shall provide
expert and technical advice to the Secretary of the Department to be chosen from the
academe and the private sector. Provided, That in case the Advisory Committee is
created, the Department is authorized to set aside a portion of its appropriation for
the Committee’s operations;

     n) Formulate or review and recommend actions to concerned authorities on
proposed technical assistance programs and grants-in-aid for technical education and
skills development, including those which may be entered into between the
Government and other nations, including international or foreign organizations,
whether doing business in the Philippines or not;

                                      Page 8 of 17
   o) Promulgate rules and regulations for the full, effective, and efficient
implementation of its policies and related laws; and

    p) Perform such other functions as may be needed to achieve its goals and
objectives to the fullest and as may be provided by this Act.

    SEC. 7. Composition. - The DTESD shall be composed of the Offices of the
Department Secretary, the Undersecretaries, the Assistant Secretaries, and their
immediate support staff. It shall also have bureaus, as well as regional and provincial
or district offices. The Office of the Secretary shall be in the main office of the
Department.

      SEC. 9. Undersecretaries and Assistant Secretaries. - The DTESD Secretary shall
be assisted by three (3) Undersecretaries and three (3) Assistant Secretaries who shall
be appointed by the President upon recommendation of the DTESD Secretary:
Provided, that two (2) Undersecretaries and two (2) Assistant Secretaries shall be career
officers.

The Undersecretaries and Assistant Secretaries shall assist the Secretary in the
performance of his powers and functions under this Act in order to fully achieve the
goals and objectives of the DTESD. The Undersecretaries and Assistant Secretaries
shall be given specific areas of responsibility by the Secretary.

      SEC. 10. Qualifications and Appointments. – No Secretary, Undersecretary, or
Assistant Secretary of the DTESD shall be appointed unless he/she is a natural-born
Filipino citizen, a resident of the Philippines, of good moral character, of proven
integrity, a college degree graduate, a Civil Service eligible or its equivalent, and with
at least five (5) years of experience in technical education skills and development.

      SEC. 11. Bureaus, Services, and Personnel. – The bureaus which shall be under
the Office of the Secretary shall be headed by an Executive Director to be appointed
by the President upon the recommendation of the Secretary. The Executive Director
shall have the rank and emoluments of Director IV and shall have the same
qualifications as those of the Secretary, Undersecretary, and Assistant Secretary of the
DTESD.

      SEC. 12. Regional Office – There shall be established a Regional Office in each
region of the country to be headed by a Regional Director to be appointed by the
President upon the recommendation of the Secretary. A Regional Director shall have
the rank and emoluments of Director IV and shall have the same qualifications as
those of the Secretary, Undersecretary, and Assistant Secretary of the DTESD. The
Regional Directors shall have the following functions:

    a)   Direct t h e implementation of TESD programs, projects, and related
         activities in the region;

    b)   Develop and recommend to the Secretary for approval TESD programs for
         local- level implementation within set policies;

    c)   Disseminate policies to stakeholders;


                                       Page 10 of 17
    d) Manage TESD resources in the region;

    e)   Plan and implement policy inputs in the region;

    f)   Monitor and evaluate program implementation; and

    g)   Perform such other functions as may be directed by the Secretary or as may be
         provided by law, rules or regulations, or executive issuances.


     SEC. 13. The Provincial/District Offices.- The Provincial/District Office shall be
headed by a Provincial/District Director to be appointed by the Secretary of the
DTESD.


      A Provincial/District Director shall have the rank and emoluments of Director
III who shall be a natural-born Filipino citizen, a resident of the Province or District
where he is appointed, of good moral character, of proven integrity, a college degree

the following functions:

    a)   Serve as secretariat to the Provincial Technical Education and Skills

    b)   Provide technical assistance to the local government units for effective

    c)   Review and recommend to the Secretary priority technical education and

    d) Perform such other functions as may be authorized or directed by the
         Secretary,

      SEC. 14. Technical Education and Training Centers. - The DTESD shall absorb
and enhance existing government Technical Education and Training Centers,
including the Cordillera State Institute of Technical Education or CSITE, as provided

technical education and skills development centers for the purpose of promoting
technical education and skills development. This network shall include leading
training technology centers, training centers in vocational and technical schools,
technical institutes, polytechnic colleges, and all other duly accredited public and
private dual-system educational institutions. The technical education and skills
development centers shall be administered and operated under such rules and
regulations as may be established by the DTESD in accordance with the National

                                      Page 11 of 17
Technical Education and Skills Development Plan. These centers and schools shall
have the following functions:

    a)   Provide responsive formal and non-formal TESD programs;

    b)   Conduct applied research and development programs and provide extension
         services in coordination with Regional Offices (ROs)/Provincial Offices (POs);

    c)   Undertake entrepreneurship development program;

    d) Conduct outreach programs in coordination with ROs/POs;

    e)   Provide job linkages;

    f)   Provide assessment and certification;

    g)   Devise mechanisms for sustainable operation and development;

    h)   Monitor and evaluate program implementation; and

    i)   Perform such other functions as may be authorized by law, rules, or
         regulations, including executive issuances.

     SEC. 15. Structure and Staffing Pattern. - Subject to the approval of the DBM, the
DTESD, through the Office of the Secretary, shall determine its organizational
structure, staffing, pattern, qualification standards, and placement of personnel in the
DTESD who shall be appointed in accordance with the civil service law, rules, and
regulations.

     SEC. 16. Compliance with the Salary Standardization Law. - The compensation
and emoluments of the officials and employees of the DTESD shall be in accordance
with the salary standardization law and other applicable laws under the national
compensation and clarification plan.


     SEC. 17. Consultants and Technical Assistance, Publication and Research. -
In pursuance of its objectives, the DTESD is hereby authorized to set aside a
portion of its appropriation for the hiring of services of qualified consultants and
private organizations for research work and publication in the field of technical
education and training. It shall avail itself of the services of other agencies of the
Government as may be necessary or required.


    SEC. 18. Formulation of a Comprehensive Development Plan for Middle-Level
Manpower. - The DTESD shall formulate a comprehensive development plan for
middle manpower based on a national employment plan or policies for the optimum

                                      Page 12 of 17
allocation, development, and utilization of skilled workers for employment,
entrepreneurship, and technology development for economic and social growth. This
plan shall be updated periodically and submitted to the President for approval.
Thereafter, it shall be the plan for the technical education, training, and certification
for the entire country within the framework of the National Development Plan. The
DTESD may, through its various offices, call on public agencies, the private sector,
and academe for assistance in the full and efficient implementation of this
comprehensive plan.

The comprehensive plan shall provide for a reformed industry-based training
   program, including apprenticeship, dual training system, and other similar
   schemes intended to:

    a)   Promote maximum protection and welfare of the worker-trainee;

    b) Improve the quality, relevance, and social accountability of technical
         education and training;

    c)   Accelerate the employment generation effort of the government; and

    d) Expand the range of opportunities for upward social mobility of students
         beyond the traditional higher levels of formal education. All agencies
         receiving financial and technical assistance from the government shall be
         required to formulate their respective annual agency technical education and
         training plans and programs. The budget to support such plans and programs
         shall be subject to review and endorsement by the DTESD to the DBM. The
         DTESD shall evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness of an agency’s skills
         development program and schemes to make them conform to the quantitative
         and qualitative objectives of the national technical education and skills
         development plan.

       SEC. 19. Technical Education and Skills Development Committees. - The
DTESD shall establish Technical Education and Skills Development Committees for
the regional and local development councils to coordinate and monitor the delivery
of all training activities by the public and private sectors, and recommend policies,
programs and projects to better carry out the objectives of this Act. The composition
of these Committees shall be determined by the Secretary.

     SEC. 20. Enterprise-Based Training Program. - The DTESD shall promote
enterprise-based Training as the major mode of training rather than an employment
arrangement and shall see to it that it is not subject to abuse by unscrupulous entities.

     SEC. 21. Establishment and Administration of National Training Standards. -
There shall be national training standards to be established by DTESD-accredited
industry committees. The DTESD shall develop and implement a certification and
accreditation program in which private industry groups and trade associations are

                                       Page 13 of 17
accredited to conduct approved assessment packages. The local government units
shall promote these assessment packages in their respective areas in accordance with
the guidelines set by the DTESD.

The Secretary shall determine the occupational trades for mandatory certification. All
certificates relating to the national training standards and certification system shall be
issued by the DTESD through its Regional and Provincial Offices.

 In consonance with the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF), the DTESD shall
closely collaborate with the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) as regards the
assessment of technicians, master craftsmen, and technologists.

       SEC. 22. Administration of Training Programs. - The DTESD shall design and
administer training programs and schemes that will develop the capabilities of public
and private institutions to provide quality and cost-effective technical education and
skills development and related opportunities. Such training programs and schemes
shall include teachers’/trainers’ training in occupational trades and related fields of
employment and values development as an integral component of all technical
education and training programs.

     SEC. 23. Assistance to Employers and Organizations. - The DTESD shall assist
employers or organizations engaged in technical education and training schemes
designed to attain its objectives under the rules and regulations established for the
purpose.

      SEC. 24. Accreditation and Regulation of Technical Education and Skills
Development Programs. - All technical education and skills development programs
shall be duly accredited by the DTESD before it is offered to the public by any public
or private entity or non-government organization.
      Non-compliance with the accreditation requirements of the DTESD shall be a
basis for the suspension or cancellation of the entity’s license to operate issued by
competent authorities.

      SEC. 25. Coordination of All Technical Education and Skills Development
Schemes. – In order to integrate the national technical education and skills
development efforts, all technical education and skills development programs and
schemes as provided for in this Act shall comply with national standards as
determined by the DTESD, particularly those having to do with competency
standards. For this purpose, existing technical education and skills development
programs in the Government and in the private sector, specifically those wholly or
partly financed with government funds, shall be reported to the DTESD for
assessment and evaluation to ensure their efficiency and effectiveness.

      Non-compliance with these standards including reportorial requirements shall
be a disqualification from any form of government assistance in the field of technical
education and skills development.
                                       Page 14 of 17
      SEC. 26. Enterprise-based Partnerships and Linkages. - The DTESD shall
establish effective and efficient institutional arrangements with industry bodies and
such other organizations or associations to ensure direct participation of employers
and workers in the design and implementation of technical education and skills
development schemes, competency standardization and certification, and such other
functions in the fulfillment of the DTESD’s objectives.

       SEC. 27. Inclusive Training Opportunities. - The DTESD shall likewise design
and implement a delivery system for community- based technical education and
skills development opportunities for the marginalized and disadvantaged sectors of
the population, including the beneficiaries of Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program
(4Ps) as provided under Republic Act No. 11310 or the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino
Program Act, providing them with new tools of wealth creation and with the
capability to take on higher value-added gainful activities, and to share equitably in
productivity gains eventually leading to poverty alleviation and reduction.
     SEC. 28. Skills Competitions. - To promote quality technical education and skills
development in the country and with the view of participating in international skills
competitions, the DTESD, with the active participation of private industries, shall
organize and conduct annual National Skills competitions.

      The DTESD shall promulgate the necessary rules and guidelines for the effective
and efficient conduct of National Skills competitions and for the country’s
participation in international Skills competitions such as ASEAN and World Skills
Olympics.

      SEC. 29. The TESD Development Fund. - A Technical Education and Skills

     a) A one-time lump sum appropriation from the National Government;

     b) An annual contribution from the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration
        Fund, the amount of which shall be determined by the Overseas Workers
        Welfare Administration and based on the study on the financing capacity of
        the OWWA fund;

     c) Donations, grants, endowments, and other bequests or gifts, and any other
        income generated by the DTESD.

The Secretary shall be the administrator of the fund, and as such, shall formulate the

     a) Unless otherwise stipulated by the private donor, only earnings of private

       SEC. 30. Technical Assistance, Incentives, and Scholarship Grants. - The
DTESD shall adopt a system of providing technical assistance, incentives, and
scholarship grants which shall be designed to encourage public and private sector and
clientele participation in technical education and training activities along the priorities
identified by the National Development Plan/Agenda.

       SEC. 31. Placement Centers. - There shall be established a network of placement
centers or PLACERS in all the Provincial/District Offices to cater specifically to
the job placement of graduates of TESD institutions. These Centers shall be financed
and administered by the DTESD. In coordination with the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), these centers shall be part of the National Employment Service
Network of the DOLE.

       SEC. 32. Separation and Retirement from Service. - Employees who are
separated from service within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act as a result
of the abolition/reorganization under the provisions of this Act shall receive
separation benefits to which they may be entitled under existing laws, orders and
issuances: Provided, that those who are qualified to retire under existing retirement
laws shall be allowed to retire and receive retirement benefits to which they may be
entitled under applicable laws, orders, and issuances.

       SEC. 33. Transitory Provision. – The Technical Education and Skills
Development (TESDA) and the TESDA Board created under Republic Act No. 7796
are hereby abolished.

The TESDA Secretariat created under Sections 10-15 of R.A. 7796 shall comprise the
initial manpower complement of the DTESD, Provided, that, no employee shall be
terminated by virtue of this transfer. The laws and rules on government
reorganization as provided under Republic Act No. 6656, otherwise known as the
Reorganization Law, shall govern the transfer and reorganization process of the
DTESD.

The personnel of the TESDA shall, in a holdover capacity, continue to perform their

Those personnel whose positions are not included in the new staffing pattern or who

       SEC. 34. Appropriations. - The amount needed for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be taken from the current fiscal year’s appropriation of TESDA.
Thereafter, the amount needed for the operation and maintenance of the DTESD shall
be included in the General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- The DTESD, Department of
Education, Department of Labor and Employment, Department of Budget and
Management, and the Department of Finance shall, in consultation with other
government agencies and concerned stakeholders, promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations to effectively and efficiently carry out the provisions of this
Act: Provided, That the non-promulgation of such rules and regulations shall not
prevent the immediate implementation of this Act upon effectivity thereof.


       SEC. 36. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any portion or provision of
this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

       SEC. 37. Repealing Clause. - Republic Act No. 7796, otherwise known as the
“TESDA Act of 1994,” is hereby repealed. All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with
or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby deemed repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 38. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
